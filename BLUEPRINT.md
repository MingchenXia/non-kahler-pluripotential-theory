Upper bounded mass and positive lower mass are independent. The elliptic example has a balanced metric with finite upper mass and zero lower mass. Extended non-pluripolar measures, finite damping and envelopes therefore provide the general framework. A finite linear Gauduchon trace cannot be substituted for nonlinear mass integrability. Nor does representing a measure as an integrable density against a Monge–Ampère measure solve an equation for that measure.

For a Hermitian background, positive lower mass characterizes normalized solvability for every finite non-pluripolar measure. A fixed L1 density instead has an attained subsolution threshold with an exact Gauduchon formula. Under positive lower mass, this threshold is the equation scalar, and normalized greatest exponential solutions converge in capacity along their L1-convergent subsequences. Without that mass property, uniform first moments for one maximizing sequence and a genuine smaller-background envelope for the greatest normalized threshold subsolution give the corresponding exact equation.
