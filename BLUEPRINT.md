## Structure

The book has 33 active chapters and an appendix. Chapters 1–29 form the main sequence. Part XII separates the substantial mass-dependent theory into three chapters:

- Chapter 30, *Volumes under bounded mass*: finite singularity volumes, comparison, relative full mass and the conditional ideal-envelope volume characterization. Positive lower reference volume is imposed at the particular reverse implications that need it.
- Chapter 31, *Relative equations under positive lower mass*: semipositive big backgrounds and smaller-background model references. Positive lower mass is a standing assumption; bounded mass is not.
- Chapter 32, *Big backgrounds under bounded mass*: arbitrary smooth big backgrounds, bounded mass and a model reference of positive lower volume. The last section compares the equation regimes, each under its own explicit assumptions.

General extended-volume definitions, relative-envelope lemmas and measure representations remain in Chapters 12–13. Individual-density equations and mass-property characterizations remain in the main sequence, together with failure examples. A condition on a single density or model reference must not be confused with a uniform metric mass property. Geometric hypotheses that imply a mass property are stated where used.

The former Chapter 30, *A transcendental Nakamaye theorem*, is temporarily excluded at the user's request. Its three source files and review history are preserved. Active references promising its proof have been removed. The ordinary Bott–Chern loci and LCK chapters remain, now Chapters 28–29. Chapter 33 collects research questions. Historical audits retain their original numbering; `research/READING-GUIDE.md` gives the current route, and `research/editorial/mass-structure-map.json` records the relocation.

Upper bounded mass and positive lower mass are independent. The elliptic example has a balanced metric with finite upper mass and zero lower mass. Extended non-pluripolar measures, finite damping and envelopes therefore provide the general framework. A finite linear Gauduchon trace cannot be substituted for nonlinear mass integrability. Nor does representing a measure as an integrable density against a Monge–Ampère measure solve an equation for that measure.

For a Hermitian background, positive lower mass characterizes normalized solvability for every finite non-pluripolar measure. A fixed L1 density instead has an attained subsolution threshold with an exact Gauduchon formula. Under positive lower mass, this threshold is the equation scalar, and normalized greatest exponential solutions converge in capacity along their L1-convergent subsequences. Without that mass property, uniform first moments for one maximizing sequence and a genuine smaller-background envelope for the greatest normalized threshold subsolution give the corresponding exact equation. Root tails alone are not shown to supply these additional hypotheses.

Smooth strictly positive dual attainment, in dimension at least two, gives a smooth exact threshold equation, uniqueness and uniform convergence of the normalized exponential family. A smooth semipositive maximizer positive off a proper analytic set gives exactness, local smoothness, uniqueness modulo constants, and the stated trace/current convergence estimates. It does not automatically give global regularity or the smaller-background envelope needed for capacity convergence. Integrable-current attainment by itself gives no such regularity. These distinctions remain central to Chapter 26.

The normalization functionals in Chapter 14 have the established entropy comparisons, reference monotonicity and dual formulas. Functional convergence controls supporting probabilities in the stated regimes. In a closed background these functionals can retain only mass, so their pseudometric need not separate singularity types. Chapter 15 keeps ordinary and ideal envelopes distinct. Chapter 19 identifies the source topology on positive-mass curve ideal envelopes with total variation of their atomic data; a fixed positive lower mass gives completeness and analytic density, but not total boundedness. This does not construct the general Hermitian singularity metric.

Linear current continuity, exceptional-divisor formulas and a canonical ordinary type on one model do not imply nonlinear volume continuity or higher-dimensional trace realization on all models. The explicit elliptic current/envelope/section descent, boundary-volume calculations and primitive elliptic bundles are test cases for these questions.
