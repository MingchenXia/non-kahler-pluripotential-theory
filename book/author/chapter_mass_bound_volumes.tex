\chapter{Volumes under bounded mass}
\label{chap:mass-bound-volumes}

\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$ with bounded mass, and let $\theta$ be a big smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. We use the singularity volumes of \cref{def:singularity-volumes}.

The non-pluripolar mass of a potential in a closed background depends only on its ordinary singularity type. For a nonclosed background, equivalent potentials can have different masses. Upper and lower singularity volumes retain the order information needed for comparison. Bounded mass makes the upper volume finite; a positive lower volume prevents auxiliary envelopes from collapsing. Together these bounds turn a small upper-volume deficit into a large admissible stretch, relating equality of volumes to relative full mass and ordinary envelopes.

Comparison through a common maximum measures the separation of normalized models. On a sector with a uniform positive lower-volume bound, stretching controls three-point comparisons, monotone limits and convergence in capacity. The comparison itself need not satisfy the triangle inequality, even on a projective surface. At zero volume it can identify distinct normalized models, by \cref{ex:zero-volume-comparison-collapse}. Finite chains give a complete metric once reciprocal lower volume is included to exclude escape to zero volume. Analytic regularization identifies the ideal-envelope models among the resulting limits, while strict barriers allow these comparisons to persist under small changes of background.

On a curve, the polar part of the curvature determines the ordinary model, and the volume comparison is exactly total variation of polar measures. This description extends to the zero-volume boundary. Taking the atomic part gives the nearest ideal model; diffuse polar curvature measures the failure of analytic approximation. Area rescaling and smooth gauges then describe changes of background, including the collapse of all normalized potentials when the backgrounds converge uniformly to a form of zero area.

\section{Comparison of singularity volumes}
\label{sec:bounded-mass-volume-comparison}

Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta$. The estimates in \cref{prop:bmp-finite,thm:arbitrary-extended-ma} bound the mass of every $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential by $v_+(\Omega)$. Hence, for $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 0\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)<+\infty.
\]
If $\theta$ is closed, both volumes equal $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)$, by invariance of non-pluripolar mass under equivalent singularities in the closed theory \cite{SGPT}. For a non-closed background this equality can fail even at $u=0$, as \cref{ex:torus-mass} shows.

Volume increases when the background increases or the singularities decrease. The rooftop and maximum constructions preserve the required ordinary types; locality confines their errors to sets with vanishing mass.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:volume-monotonicity}\index{Singularity volume!monotonicity}
Let $\theta_1,\theta_2$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. Suppose $\theta_1$ is big, $\theta_1\leq\theta_2$, and $u_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i)$ satisfy $u_1\preceq u_2$. Then
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
 \leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Shift $u_1$ so that $u_1\leq u_2$. Choose $v_2\sim u_2$ in $\PSH(X,\theta_2)$ and, for real $t>0$, set
\[
 p_t=P_{\theta_1}(u_1+t,v_2).
\]
For $t>0$, a sufficiently low constant translate of $u_1$ is a competitor; also $p_t\leq u_1+t$. Thus $p_t\sim u_1$. The contact theorem, applied in each background on the corresponding contact part, and positivity of the mixed products give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(p_t)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{u_1\leq v_2-t\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
      +\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_2}(v_2).
\]
Indeed $p_t$ is also $\theta_2$-plurisubharmonic and is below $v_2$, so the contact inequality in that background applies. The first measure on the right has mass tending to zero: its indicators tend to zero off the pole sets, and the measure is finite and non-pluripolar. Integrate, let $t\to+\infty$, and then take the infimum over $v_2$.

For the upper volume, choose any $v_1\sim u_1$ in $\PSH(X,\theta_1)$ and put $w_t=\max(v_1,u_2-t)$. It is $\theta_2$-plurisubharmonic and $w_t\sim u_2$. On $D_t=\{v_1>u_2-t\}$, locality and mixed-product positivity imply
\[
 \mathbf1_{D_t}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(v_1)
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_t}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_2}(w_t).
\]
The sets $D_t$ exhaust the complement of a pluripolar set. Hence integration and passage to the limit give
$\int_X\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(v_1)\leq
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2)$.
Take the supremum over $v_1$.
\end{proof}

Adding a positive current also increases both volumes. For the lower volume, subtract its potential inside an envelope to recover a competitor in the original background.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:volume-addition}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $v\in\PSH(X,\eta)$ for a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ on $X$. Then, for either the lower or the upper volume,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_{\theta+\eta}(u+v).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The form $\theta+\eta$ is big, by adding the positive current $\eta+\ddc v$ to a strictly positive current in the class of $\theta$. For the upper volume, add $v$ to each competitor for $u$. Positivity of mixed products shows that the resulting mass is at least the original mass.

For the lower volume, choose $w\sim u+v$ in $\PSH(X,\theta+\eta)$, and let $p=P_\theta(w-v)$. Since $w-v=u+O(1)$ outside the pole sets, the envelope is genuine and $p\sim u$. The contact theorem says that its measure is carried by $D=\{p+v=w\}$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Furthermore $p+v\leq w$ everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. Thus
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_{\theta+\eta}(p+v)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_{\theta+\eta}(w).
\]
Integrating and taking the infimum over $w$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Volume deficits and relative full mass}

The upper-volume deficit measures how far one potential can be stretched away from another. The positive lower volume of the less singular potential prevents the stretching envelopes from collapsing. We use $Q_b$ and its competitor family from \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:volume-deficit-stretch}\index{Singularity volume!stretching estimate}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $u\preceq v$, and put
\[
 \delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(v)>0,
 \qquad D=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(v)
             -\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
If $b>1$ and $b^nD<\delta$, then $Q_b(u,v)$ is genuine.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adding constants to $u$ and $v$ only translates the stretching envelope. Normalize $v\leq0$, and write $V=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(v)$. Since $b^nD<\delta$, choose $w\sim u$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-volume-deficit}
 V-\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)<b^{-n}\delta.
\end{equation}
Translate $w$ so that $w\leq v$. For positive integers $j$, put
\[
 w_j=\max(w,v-j),\qquad h_j=Q_b(w_j,v).
\]
The competitor $v-bj$ and the defining inequality give $v-bj\leq h_j\leq v$. Thus $w_j,h_j\sim v$, and $h_j$ decreases. If its limit were $-\infty$, \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} would give $\sup_Xh_j\to-\infty$.

By \cref{thm:big-general-envelope-contact}, the measure of $h_j$ is carried by $C_j=\{b^{-1}h_j+(1-b^{-1})v=w_j\}$. Ordered contact and positivity of mixed products give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(h_j)
       \leq b^n\mathbf1_{C_j}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_j).
\]
\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Fix a positive integer $k$. For large $j>k$, collapse would imply $h_j\leq-bk$ everywhere. Since $v\leq0$, contact then implies $w_j\leq-k$, hence $w\leq-k$. Locality on $\{w>-k\}\subset\{w>v-j\}$ gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \delta\leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(h_j)
 &\leq b^n\int_{\{w\leq-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_j)\\
 &\leq b^n\left(V-\int_{\{w>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)\right).
 \end{split}
\]
Here the first inequality uses $h_j\sim v$, and the last uses $w_j\sim v$. Letting $k\to\infty$ contradicts \eqref{eq:relative-volume-deficit}, since the finite non-pluripolar measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)$ gives zero mass to the pole set. The limit $h$ is therefore genuine and satisfies $b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})v\leq w$. If $w\leq u+C$, then $h-bC\in\mathcal A_b(u,v)$, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:volume-deficit-lelong}\index{Lelong number!volume-deficit bound}
Fix a smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to X$. There is a finite constant $C_\pi$, depending only on $\pi$ and $\theta$, such that, for $u,v,\delta,D$ as in \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch},
\[
 0\leq\nu(u\circ\pi,z)-\nu(v\circ\pi,z)
 \leq C_\pi\min\left\{1,\left(\frac D\delta\right)^{1/n}\right\}
 \qquad(z\in Z).
\]
The same estimate holds for generic Lelong numbers along every prime divisor on $Z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:gauduchon-uniform-lelong} to the smooth background $\pi^*\theta$ on $Z$, and choose $C_\pi$ bounding all its Lelong numbers. Order gives the lower bound and the upper bound $C_\pi$, which proves the assertion when $D\geq\delta$.

If $D<\delta$, every $b>1$ with $b^nD<\delta$ admits $h\in\mathcal A_b(u,v)$ by the lemma. Pulling back the defining convex inequality and taking Lelong numbers gives
\[
 \nu(u\circ\pi,z)-\nu(v\circ\pi,z)
 \leq b^{-1}\bigl(\nu(h\circ\pi,z)-\nu(v\circ\pi,z)\bigr)
 \leq b^{-1}C_\pi.
\]
For $D>0$, let $b\uparrow(\delta/D)^{1/n}$; for $D=0$, let $b\to\infty$. Taking generic values proves the divisorial assertion.
\end{proof}

At zero deficit, every finite stretch is possible. Fix a reference $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:relative-volume-class}\index{Relative full-mass class}
Define
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\preceq\varphi,
 \ \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
   =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
Convex comparison turns the envelope condition back into equality of volumes.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-equivalence}
Suppose $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0$. Then
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi).
\]
Every member also satisfies
$\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First assume the envelope condition. For each $a\in(0,1)$ there is $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ such that $a\varphi+(1-a)h\leq u$. By \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity,prop:volume-addition} and homogeneity, for either volume,
\[
 a^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(a\varphi+(1-a)h)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\varphi).
\]
Let $a\to1$. This proves equality of both volumes in this direction, even without positive lower volume.

Conversely, equality of the upper volumes makes $D=0$ in \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch}, with $v=\varphi$. Every $Q_b(u,\varphi)$ is genuine, which is the envelope condition.
\end{proof}

\section{Domination and ordinary envelopes}

Passing to an ordinary envelope gives one volume inequality by monotonicity. For the reverse inequality, contact support and domination identify the relative obstacle limit. Positive lower volume makes the stretching envelopes in the domination argument genuine. Write $\mu_w=\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)$ for $w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:big-relative-domination}
Let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have positive lower volume, let $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$, $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $v\preceq\varphi$, and $0\leq c<1$. If
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mu_u\leq c\,\mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mu_v,
\]
then $u\geq v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $\ell=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0$ and $\mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$. Replace $v$ by $\max(u,v)$. Locality preserves the hypothesis and upward stability puts the new $v$ in $\mathcal E_\varphi$. Translate $u,v$ by the same constant so that $v\leq\varphi$, and then translate all three potentials so that $\varphi\leq0$. Set
\[
 q_b=Q_b(u,v),\qquad
 D_b=\{b^{-1}q_b+(1-b^{-1})v=u\}.
\]
\Cref{lem:relative-stretch-closure} gives $q_b\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $q_b\leq u$. Contact support and positivity of the mixed products give
\[
 b^{-n}\mu_{q_b}+(1-b^{-1})^n\mathbf1_{D_b}\mu_v
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_b}\mu_u.
\]
Here $\mu_{q_b}$ is supported on $D_b$. Choose $b$ so large that $(1-b^{-1})^n>c$. Subtracting finite measures on $D_b\cap\{u<v\}$ shows that $\mu_{q_b}$ vanishes there. Thus it is supported on $D_b\cap\{u=v\}\subset\{q_b=u\}$. Since $q_b\leq u$, ordered contact with coefficient one and \cref{thm:relative-equivalence} now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-domination-fixed-tail}
 \ell\leq\mu_{q_b}(X)\leq\mu_u(\{q_b=u\}).
\end{equation}

If $u<v$ anywhere, there is $\delta>0$ such that $\{u<v-\delta\}$ has positive smooth volume. Indeed, the contrary would give $u\geq v$ almost everywhere and then everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. On this set
$q_b\leq u-(b-1)\delta$. The family $q_b$ decreases in $b$ and is bounded above by $u$. A genuine limit could not be $-\infty$ on a set of positive smooth volume. Hence its limit is identically $-\infty$, and \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives $\sup_Xq_b\to-\infty$. The sets $\{q_b=u\}$ then have $\mu_u$-mass tending to zero by dominated convergence off the pole set of $u$. This contradicts \eqref{eq:big-domination-fixed-tail}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:big-relative-orders}
For $u,v$ as in \cref{thm:big-relative-domination} and $\lambda>0$,
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}\mu_u\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda v}\mu_v
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\geq v.
\]
If $\mu_u\leq c\mu_v$ on $X$ for a finite $c\geq0$, then $c\geq1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On $\{u<v-\delta\}$ the weighted inequality gives $\mu_u\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\delta}\mu_v$. Apply strict domination to $v-\delta$ and let $\delta\downarrow0$. If the second assertion failed, strict domination against every translate $v+A$ would contradict finiteness of $u,v$ at a common point.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-obstacle-contact}
Let $u,w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $P_\theta(u,w)$ genuine, and put
\[
 P_\theta[u](w)=\left(\lim_{C\to\infty}P_\theta(u+C,w)\right)^*.
\]
Then
\[
 \mu_{P_\theta[u](w)}
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{P_\theta[u](w)=w\}}\mu_w.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $p_C=P_\theta(u+C,w)$ for $C\geq0$ and $p=P_\theta[u](w)$. The envelopes increase, are bounded below by $p_0$ and above by $w$, and converge in capacity by \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}. Assigning ties in the contact set to $w$ gives
\[
 \mu_{p_C}\leq\mathbf1_{\{p_C=u+C<w\}}\mu_u
                    +\mathbf1_{\{p_C=w\}}\mu_w.
\]
The first measure has total mass at most
\[
 \varepsilon_C=\mu_u(\{u\leq\sup_Xw-C\})\longrightarrow0,
\]
because $\mu_u$ is finite and non-pluripolar. Testing against nonnegative continuous functions and applying \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} gives $\mu_p\leq\mu_w$.

The bounded quasi-continuous weight $\chi=\min(1,(w-p)_+)$, defined arbitrarily on the polar indeterminacy set, vanishes on $\{p_C=w\}$, since $p_C\leq p\leq w$. Thus $\int_X\chi\,\mu_{p_C}\leq\varepsilon_C$. Weighted lower semicontinuity gives $\int_X\chi\,\mu_p=0$, so $\mu_p$ is carried by $\{p=w\}$. This proves the asserted restriction of $\mu_w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}\index{Singularity volume!ordinary envelope}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $p=P_\theta[u]$,
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
       =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p).
\]
If this volume is positive, then also
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
       =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p),\qquad
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,p),\qquad
 P_\theta[p]=p.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)=0$, lower-volume equality follows from monotonicity. Suppose henceforth that it is positive. Normalize $u\leq0$ and choose any $w\sim p$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$. Fix $K\geq0$ with $p-K\leq w\leq p+K$. Since $u\leq p$, the rooftop $p_C=P_\theta(u+C,w)$ satisfies $u-K\leq p_C\leq u+C$ for each $C\geq0$. In particular it is genuine and equivalent to $u$. Its increasing canonical limit $r=P_\theta[u](w)$ satisfies
\[
 P_\theta(u+C+K,0)-K\leq p_C\leq w,
 \qquad p-K\leq r\leq w\leq p+K.
\]
Indeed, the leftmost potential lies below $u+C$ and below $p-K\leq w$, so is a rooftop competitor. Thus $r\sim p$, and \cref{prop:relative-elementary} puts $r$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,p)$.

By \cref{lem:relative-obstacle-contact}, $\mu_r$ vanishes on $\{r<w\}$. \Cref{thm:big-relative-domination}, with coefficient zero and reference $p$, gives $r\geq w$; hence $r=w$. Capacity convergence and lower semicontinuity now yield
\[
 \int_X\mu_w\leq\liminf_{C\to\infty}\int_X\mu_{p_C}
       \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
Taking the supremum over $w\sim p$ proves the upper-volume inequality in the required direction; the reverse inequality is monotonicity. \Cref{thm:relative-equivalence} then gives both relative-class membership and lower-volume equality. Finally \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality} gives $P_\theta[p]=P_\theta[u]=p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:full-mass-envelope-characterization}
If $\varphi=P_\theta[\varphi]$ has positive lower volume, then
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):P_\theta[u]=\varphi\}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first equality is \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}. Membership implies envelope equality by \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality}; the converse follows from \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}.
\end{proof}

\section{Strict barriers and background variation}
\label{sec:positive-volume-barriers}

Positive lower volume permits a small stretch away from any less singular potential, with a scale controlled by a common mass bound. Mixing the stretch with a strict potential gives a strict potential lying below the original one. The same mixed-mass bound controls the lower-volume error under a smooth background perturbation. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive-volume-stretch}
Let $u,r\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, with $u\leq r$, and assume
$\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)>0$. For a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta$, set
\[
 b_0=1+\frac{\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)}
                   {2^{n+1}n\,v_+(\Omega)}.
\]
Then $Q_b(u,r)$ is genuine for $1\leq b\leq b_0$, and
\[
 \lim_{b\downarrow1}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(Q_b(u,r))
 =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
\end{lemma}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Put $K=v_+(\Omega)$, so that $1<b_0\leq2$, and translate so that $u\leq r\leq0$. Suppose $Q_b(u,r)$ is not genuine for some $1<b\leq b_0$. Set
\[
 u_j=\max(u,r-j),\qquad v_j=u+(b-1)u_j,
 \qquad q_j=P_\theta(v_j-(b-1)r).
\]
Differences at common poles are interpreted through the corresponding convex inequality. Off the poles the obstacle lies between $u-(b-1)j$ and $u$. Hence $q_j$ is genuine and $q_j\sim u$. As $j$ increases it decreases. A genuine limit would be a competitor for $Q_b(u,r)$, so $\sup_Xq_j\to-\infty$.

Let $C_j=\{q_j+(b-1)r=v_j\}$. The contact theorem puts $\mu_{q_j}$ on $C_j$. The positive current with potential $q_j+(b-1)r$ in background $b\theta$ contains the current with potential $q_j$ in background $\theta$. Ordered contact in background $b\theta$ therefore gives
\[
 \mu_{q_j}\leq\mathbf1_{C_j}\bigl((\theta+\ddc u)+(b-1)(\theta+\ddc u_j)\bigr)^n.
\]
On $C_j$ we have $q_j=u+(b-1)u_j-(b-1)r\geq bu$. For any $k>0$ and then all sufficiently large $j$, $q_j\leq-bk$ everywhere, and consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-volume-stretch-tail}
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mu_{q_j}(X)
 \leq\int_{\{u\leq-k\}}\bigl((\theta+\ddc u)+(b-1)(\theta+\ddc u_j)\bigr)^n.
\end{equation}

For any $s,t\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $0\leq q\leq n$, positivity of the non-pluripolar mixed products and \cref{prop:bmp-finite} imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\binom nq\int_X(\theta+\ddc s)^{n-q}\wedge(\theta+\ddc t)^q\\
 &\quad\leq\binom nq\int_X(\Omega+\ddc s)^{n-q}\wedge(\Omega+\ddc t)^q\\
 &\quad\leq2^n\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\Omega((s+t)/2)\leq2^nK.
 \end{aligned}
\]
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Here the estimate for the possibly unbounded average follows by constant truncation in the positive background $\Omega$. All products in the display are positive. Expanding the last integrand in \eqref{eq:positive-volume-stretch-tail}, and using $(b-1)^q\leq b-1$ for $q\geq1$, gives
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mu_u(\{u\leq-k\})+n2^nK(b-1).
\]
The measure $\mu_u$ is finite and ignores its pole set. Let $k\to\infty$. The remaining error is at most half of $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ by the choice of $b_0$, a contradiction. Thus every stated stretch is genuine; at $b=1$ the envelope is $u$.

Put $h_0=Q_{b_0}(u,r)$ and, for $1<b\leq b_0$, $t=(b-1)/(b_0-1)$. The defining inequalities show that
\[
 (1-t)u+th_0\leq Q_b(u,r)\leq u.
\]
For $b<b_0$, addition, homogeneity and volume monotonicity give
\[
 (1-t)^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(Q_b(u,r))
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
Letting $b\downarrow1$ proves the limit. Undoing the common translation changes neither ordinary-type volume nor genuineness.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:positive-volume-strict-barrier}
If $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has positive lower volume, then there are $w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $\varepsilon>0$ with
\[
 w\leq u,\qquad \theta+\ddc w\geq\varepsilon\omega.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Translate $u$ so that $u\leq V_\theta$. Choose a strict potential $\rho\leq V_\theta$ with $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\delta\omega$, using bigness. For a genuine $h=Q_b(u,V_\theta)$ with $b>1$, put
\[
 w=b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})\rho.
\]
Its defining convex inequality gives $w\leq u$, and its current is at least $(1-b^{-1})\delta\omega$. Restore the translation of $u$ by the same translation of $w$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-volume-smaller-background}
Assume also that $\theta\geq0$. If $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and
$\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)>0$, then
$P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)$ is genuine for some $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:positive-volume-strict-barrier}, choose $w\leq u$ and $\delta>0$ with $\theta+\ddc w\geq\delta\omega$. Choose $C>0$ with $\theta\leq C\omega$, and $0<t<\min(1,\delta/C)$. Then
\[
 (1-t)\theta+\ddc w\geq(\delta-tC)\omega\geq0.
\]
Thus $w$ is a competitor for $P_{(1-t)\theta}(u)$.
\end{proof}

For model references, the positive-volume condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:li-model-condition}
 P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi,\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0.
\end{equation}
When $\theta\geq0$, the proposition turns this into the smaller-background condition \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}. If also $v_-(\theta)>0$, the converse follows from \cref{lem:smaller-background-mass-lower}: every potential equivalent to $\varphi$ admits a smaller-background competitor with the same $\varepsilon$, and hence
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)
 \geq(1-\varepsilon)^n v_-(\theta)>0.
\]
Without this additional lower bound, the smaller-background argument still gives individual positivity, as proved in \cref{sec:relative-envelope-room}.

A smooth perturbation changes the lower volume by at most a linear error. A strict lower potential gives a multiplicative comparison for both volumes.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison}
There is $C>0$, depending only on $\theta$ and $\omega$, such that, for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ with $\theta\leq\eta\leq\theta+\varepsilon\omega$, $0\leq\varepsilon\leq1$,
\[
 0\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\eta(u)
       -\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\leq C\varepsilon.
\]
If $u$ has positive lower volume, there is also $\delta>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon\geq0$ and $\theta\leq\eta\leq\theta+\varepsilon\omega$, either volume satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\eta(u)
 \leq(1+\varepsilon/\delta)^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u).
\]
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta,\omega$ and set $C=n2^n v_+(\Omega)$. For every $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $1\leq k\leq n$, positivity and the mixed-mass estimate in the proof of \cref{lem:positive-volume-stretch}, applied in background $\Omega$ to $v$ and zero, give
\[
 \binom nk\int_X(\theta+\ddc v)^{n-k}\wedge\omega^k
 \leq2^n v_+(\Omega).
\]
Expanding in the smooth positive perturbation and using $\varepsilon^k\leq\varepsilon$ yields
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\eta(v)
 \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}(v)
 \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)+C\varepsilon.
\]
Take the infimum over $v\sim u$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$. Each such $v$ is also an admissible competitor in background $\eta$, proving the upper bound for the lower volume. The lower bound is background monotonicity.

Now assume $u$ has positive lower volume. Choose $w\leq u$ with $\theta+\ddc w\geq\delta\omega$ by \cref{cor:positive-volume-strict-barrier}. The case $\varepsilon=0$ is immediate. For $t=\varepsilon/\delta>0$, the potential $tw$ belongs to $\PSH(X,t(\theta-\delta\omega))$. Addition and monotonicity give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{vol}_\eta(u)
 &\leq\operatorname{vol}_{\eta+t(\theta-\delta\omega)}(u+tw)\\
 &\leq\operatorname{vol}_{(1+t)\theta}((1+t)u)
 =(1+t)^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u).
 \end{split}
\]
Here $\eta+t(\theta-\delta\omega)\leq(1+t)\theta$ and $u+tw\leq(1+t)u$. The lower bound is background monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\section{Monotone limits}
\label{sec:mass-bound-monotone-limits}

Passing to a monotone limit must preserve the ordinary-envelope fixed point property as well as the required volume bounds. For increasing sequences, contact support identifies the limiting envelope before its volume is known. For decreasing models, a uniform small stretch retains a positive lower-volume bound. Normalized compactness then preserves the convex inequalities supplied by the deficit lemma; strict barriers allow the background to decrease at the same time.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:increasing-envelope-volume-continuity}
Suppose $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ increases and $u=(\lim_j u_j)^*\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has positive lower volume. Then
\[
 \left(\lim_jP_\theta[u_j]\right)^*=P_\theta[u],
 \qquad P_\theta[u_j]\longrightarrow P_\theta[u]
       \quad\text{in capacity},
\]
and
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u_j)\nearrow
       \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u_j)\nearrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
For every fixed smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to X$,
\[
 \sup_{z\in Z}\bigl|\nu(u_j\circ\pi,z)-\nu(u\circ\pi,z)\bigr|
       \longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Translate so that $u\leq0$, and set $p_j=P_\theta[u_j]$, $p=P_\theta[u]$ and $s=(\lim_jp_j)^*$. Then $u\leq s\leq p\leq V_\theta$. By \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, the lower volume of $p$ is positive and $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,p)$. Upward stability puts $s$ in the same class.

The ordinary envelope can use $V_\theta$ as obstacle, by \cref{def:singularity-envelope}. Hence \cref{lem:relative-obstacle-contact} gives
\[
 \mu_{p_j}\leq\mathbf1_{\{p_j=V_\theta\}}\mu_{V_\theta}.
\]
The fixed bounded quasi-continuous weight $\chi=\min(1,(p-s)_+)$, defined arbitrarily on the polar indeterminacy set, vanishes on $\{p_j=V_\theta\}$. Since $p_j\to s$ in capacity, \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} gives $\int_X\chi\,\mu_s=0$. Thus $\mu_s$ vanishes on $\{s<p\}$. Strict domination with reference $p$ and coefficient zero yields $s=p$.

Choose any $w\sim p$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $K\geq0$ with $p-K\leq w\leq p+K$. Define $r_j=P_\theta[u_j](w)$. The initial rooftop is genuine, since $u_j-K\leq w$. For $C\geq0$ the competitor comparison gives
\[
 P_\theta(u_j+C+K,0)-K
       \leq P_\theta(u_j+C,w)\leq p_j+K.
\]
The leftmost potential lies below $u_j+C$ and below $p_j-K\leq p-K\leq w$. For the upper bound, subtract $K$ from the middle term: it lies below zero and is more singular than $u_j$. Taking the increasing limit in $C$ gives
\[
 p_j-K\leq r_j\leq p_j+K,\qquad r_j\leq w.
\]
The $r_j$ increase to $r=(\lim_jr_j)^*$ in capacity, and $p-K\leq r\leq w\leq p+K$. Thus $r\sim p$. The contact lemma gives $\mu_{r_j}\leq\mathbf1_{\{r_j=w\}}\mu_w$. Applying weighted lower semicontinuity with $\min(1,(w-r)_+)$ shows that $\mu_r$ vanishes on $\{r<w\}$. Domination with reference $p$ gives $r=w$. Therefore
\[
 \int_X\mu_w\leq\liminf_j\int_X\mu_{r_j}
       \leq\lim_j\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j),
\]
where $r_j\sim p_j$ justifies the last inequality. Taking the supremum over $w\sim p$ and using volume monotonicity proves
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)\nearrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p).
\]

Put $\delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)>0$ and $D_j=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)-\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)$. Since $D_j\to0$, \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} supplies $a_j\to1$, $0<a_j<1$, and $h_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ such that
\[
 a_jp+(1-a_j)h_j\leq p_j
\]
for all sufficiently large $j$. Addition, homogeneity and monotonicity of the lower volume, together with \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, give
\[
 a_j^n\delta\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)
       =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u_j)\leq\delta.
\]
This proves lower-volume convergence and positivity for all large $j$. The same envelope theorem then identifies the upper volumes of $u_j$ and $p_j$, giving the asserted upper-volume limit. Finally \cref{prop:volume-deficit-lelong}, applied to $u_j\leq u$, gives the uniform Lelong-number convergence on each fixed model.
\end{proof}

For the decreasing results, let $\theta_j$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms with $\theta\leq\theta_{j+1}\leq\theta_j$ and $\theta_j\to\theta$ uniformly. The constant sequence is allowed. Normalizing the auxiliary potentials first proves continuity in a fixed background; corrected tail envelopes then handle the varying forms.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}\index{Model potential!decreasing limits}
Suppose $q_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ satisfies $q_j=P_{\theta_j}[q_j]$ and decreases to $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Then $P_\theta[q]=q$ and
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)\searrow
       \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q).
\]
If $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0$, then also
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)\searrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q),
\]
and, for every fixed smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to X$,
\[
 \sup_{z\in Z}\bigl|\nu(q_j\circ\pi,z)-\nu(q\circ\pi,z)\bigr|
       \longrightarrow0.
\]
In this case all generic divisorial Lelong numbers converge.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $q\leq0$, background and obstacle monotonicity give $q\leq P_\theta[q]\leq P_{\theta_j}[q]\leq q_j$. Letting $j\to\infty$ proves the fixed-point assertion. Set $\ell=\lim_j\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)$. Monotonicity gives $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)\leq\ell$. If $\ell=0$, the lower-volume assertion follows.

Suppose $\ell>0$. Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta_1$, put $K=v_+(\Omega)$, and choose
\[
 b=1+\frac{\ell}{2^{n+1}nK},\qquad a=1-b^{-1}.
\]
The uniform scale in \cref{lem:positive-volume-stretch} makes $Q_b(q_j,V_{\theta_j})$ genuine for every $j$. Put $h_j=P_{\theta_j}[Q_b(q_j,V_{\theta_j})]$. For each normalized competitor $s$ for this envelope, $aV_{\theta_j}+(1-a)s$ lies below zero and is more singular than $q_j$. Since $q_j=P_{\theta_j}[q_j]$, taking the envelope gives
\[
 aV_{\theta_j}+(1-a)h_j\leq q_j.
\]
The functions $h_j$ have supremum zero and a common curvature lower bound $-\Omega$. Normalized compactness and uniform background convergence give a subsequential $L^1$ limit $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Also $V_{\theta_j}\searrow V_\theta$: their limit is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic and lies between $V_\theta$ and zero. Passing to the limit in the convex inequality and using almost-everywhere order gives
\[
 aV_\theta+(1-a)h\leq q.
\]
Moreover, $v_-(\Omega)\geq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_1}(q_1)\geq\ell>0$ by volume monotonicity. Choose $\rho\leq V_\theta$ with $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\kappa\Omega$, $\kappa>0$. Addition applied to $0\in\PSH(X,\kappa\Omega)$ and $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta-\kappa\Omega)$ gives $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\rho)\geq\kappa^n v_-(\Omega)$. Monotonicity and addition in the displayed convex combination now yield
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)
 \geq a^n\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(V_\theta)
 \geq(a\kappa)^n v_-(\Omega)>0.
\]
Thus it remains to prove the volume limits when the limit has positive lower volume.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
First suppose $\theta_j=\theta$ for every $j$. Put
\[
 \delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0,
 \qquad L=\lim_j\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q_j),
 \qquad e_k=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q_k)-L.
\]
The limit exists by \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity} and bounded mass. After discarding finitely many indices, choose $b_k>1$ tending to infinity with $b_k^n e_k<\delta$, and set $a_k=1-b_k^{-1}$.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For every $j\geq k$, the volume deficit between $q_j$ and $q_k$ is at most $e_k$, whereas the lower volume of $q_k$ is at least $\delta$. Thus \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} supplies $h_{k,j}\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with
\[
 a_kq_k+(1-a_k)h_{k,j}\leq q_j.
\]
Combining normalized ordinary-envelope competitors, as in the proof of \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality}, gives
\[
 a_kq_k+(1-a_k)P_\theta[h_{k,j}]\leq q_j.
\]
The potentials $P_\theta[h_{k,j}]$ have supremum zero. For fixed $k$, normalized compactness gives a subsequential $L^1$ limit $h_k$ as $j\to\infty$. Passing to the limit and applying \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} gives
\[
 a_kq_k+(1-a_k)h_k\leq q.
\]
For either volume, homogeneity, addition and monotonicity now yield
\[
 a_k^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q_k)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta\bigl(a_kq_k+(1-a_k)h_k\bigr)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q_k).
\]
Letting $k\to\infty$ proves both volume limits in the fixed background.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
For the varying backgrounds, choose $w\leq q$ with $\theta+\ddc w\geq d\omega$, $d>0$, by \cref{cor:positive-volume-strict-barrier}. Choose $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ with $\varepsilon_j>0$ and $\theta_j\leq\theta+\varepsilon_j\omega$, and put
\[
 c_j=\frac d{d+\varepsilon_j},\qquad
 f_j=c_jq_j+(1-c_j)w,
\]
\[
 F_j=\left(\sup_{k\geq j}f_k\right)^*,\qquad
 r_j=P_\theta[F_j].
\]
The background of the displayed convex combination is bounded above by $\theta$:
\[
 c_j\theta_j+(1-c_j)(\theta-d\omega)\leq\theta.
\]
Thus $f_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Since $f_k\leq q_k\leq q_j$ for $k\geq j$, upper regularization gives $F_j\leq q_j$. Also $f_k\to q$ in $L^1$, so a subsequence converges almost everywhere and $q\leq F_j$ by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Background monotonicity now gives
\[
 q\leq r_j=P_\theta[F_j]\leq P_{\theta_j}[F_j]
       \leq P_{\theta_j}[q_j]=q_j.
\]
The $r_j$ decrease to $q$. Their lower volumes are positive, so \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} makes them ordinary model potentials. The fixed-background case just proved applies to $r_j$. Addition and background monotonicity also give, for either volume,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_{\theta_j}(q_j)
 \leq c_j^{-n}\operatorname{vol}_\theta(f_j)
 \leq c_j^{-n}\operatorname{vol}_\theta(r_j).
\]
The outer terms tend to the same limit. Monotonicity in both background and potential gives the stated decreasing convergence.

By \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison}, the volumes of the fixed potential $q$ in background $\theta_j$ tend to its volumes in background $\theta$. Hence the upper-volume deficit between $q$ and $q_j$, measured in $\theta_j$, tends to zero, while the lower volume of $q_j$ is bounded below by $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0$. Apply \cref{prop:volume-deficit-lelong} in each background. Its proof allows one common Lelong bound from $\pi^*\theta_1$, since $\theta_j\leq\theta_1$. This proves uniform convergence on each fixed model.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
For an arbitrary decreasing sequence, envelope convergence is exactly the missing upper-volume continuity.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:decreasing-envelope-volume-criterion}
Suppose $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ decreases to $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with positive lower volume. Then
\[
 P_{\theta_j}[u_j]\searrow P_\theta[u]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(u_j)\searrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
When these conditions hold, the lower volumes converge to that of $u$ in background $\theta$, and the Lelong numbers converge uniformly on every fixed smooth compact modification.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $u_j\leq0$ by a common translation. Set $p_j=P_{\theta_j}[u_j]$, $p=P_\theta[u]$ and $q=\lim_jp_j$. The envelope-volume theorem in background $\theta_j$ makes every $p_j$ a fixed point of positive lower volume. The envelopes decrease, and the uniform convergence of the backgrounds gives $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Since $u\leq p\leq q$, the preceding decreasing-model theorem applies and gives, for either volume,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)
       =\lim_j\operatorname{vol}_{\theta_j}(p_j)
       =\lim_j\operatorname{vol}_{\theta_j}(u_j),
 \qquad P_\theta[q]=q.
\]
If $q=p$, these equalities and \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} give both volume limits. Conversely, equality of the upper volumes of $u$ and $q$ puts $u$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,q)$. By \cref{cor:full-mass-envelope-characterization}, $P_\theta[u]=q$. For the Lelong assertion, \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison} makes the upper-volume deficit between $u$ and $u_j$ in background $\theta_j$ tend to zero. Apply \cref{prop:volume-deficit-lelong} with the common lower bound $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)>0$ and the uniform Lelong bound from $\pi^*\theta_1$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ordinary-envelope-background-limit}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(u)\searrow
       \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
If this limiting volume is positive, then also
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(u)\searrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),\qquad
 P_{\theta_j}[u]\searrow P_\theta[u].
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The volume limits follow from the two estimates in \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison}. In the positive-lower-volume case, apply \cref{prop:decreasing-envelope-volume-criterion} to the constant sequence $u_j=u$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
For the next corollary, let $Y\subset X$ be a connected compact complex submanifold of positive dimension, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $\nu(u,Y)=0$. Use the trace data and restricted approximants of \cref{chap:trace-data}. The majorant in a smaller background gives the trace envelope positive lower volume, so \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} applies on $Y$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:mass-bound-exact-trace}
Suppose $Y$ has bounded mass. Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega_0\geq\theta|_Y$ and a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq2\Omega_0$ with $v_-(\Omega)>0$. Then
\[
 \nu\bigl(\mathcal T_\Omega(u;Y)\circ\pi,E\bigr)=\tau_{Y,u}(E)
\]
for every prime divisor $E$ on every smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $T=\mathcal T_\Omega(u;Y)$ and $r=\dim Y$. The normalized majorant $v$ of \cref{prop:trace-profile-majorant} lies below $T$ and belongs to $\PSH(Y,\Omega/2)$. For every $w\sim T$ in $\PSH(Y,\Omega)$, a constant translate of $v$ lies below $w$. By \cref{lem:smaller-background-mass-lower},
\[
 \int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\Omega(w)\geq2^{-r}v_-(\Omega),
 \qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\Omega(T)
       \geq2^{-r}v_-(\Omega)>0.
\]
The restricted approximants belong to $\PSH(Y,\Omega)$ for all sufficiently large indices. By \cref{thm:restricted-analytic-envelope-limit}, their dyadic ordinary envelopes $q_j=P_\Omega[u_{2^j}|_Y]$ decrease to $T$. The decreasing-model theorem applies on $Y$ and gives
\[
 \nu(T\circ\pi,E)
 =\lim_j\nu(q_j\circ\pi,E)
 =\lim_j2^{-j}\operatorname{ord}_E\mathfrak a_{2^j}(u;Y)
 =\tau_{Y,u}(E),
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

\section{Cauchy convergence of model potentials}
\label{sec:model-volume-comparison}

For two potentials, the maximum gives a common upper bound. The sum of the two upper-volume deficits measures their separation after ordinary projection. A uniform positive lower-volume bound controls how this comparison changes when a third potential is introduced. This replaces the triangle inequality in the Cauchy argument below. Write $U(u)=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ and $M=U(V_\theta)$, and fix a smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$ for $L^1$ norms.

\begin{definition}\label{def:model-volume-comparison}\index{Model potential!volume comparison}
For $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, define
\[
 d_\theta(u,v)=2U(\max(u,v))-U(u)-U(v).
\]
For $\delta>0$, put
\[
 \mathcal M_\delta(\theta)=
 \{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):P_\theta[u]=u,
       \ \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\geq\delta\}.
\]
A sequence is $d_\theta$-Cauchy if $d_\theta(u_j,u_k)\to0$ as $j,k\to\infty$, and converges to $u$ for this comparison if $d_\theta(u_j,u)\to0$.
\end{definition}

The function $d_\theta$ is finite, nonnegative and symmetric. It depends only on the ordinary singularity types, since replacing either input by an equivalent one changes the maximum by a bounded amount. Monotonicity also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:volume-comparison-basic}
 |U(u)-U(v)|\leq d_\theta(u,v),\qquad
 u\leq v\ \Longrightarrow\ d_\theta(u,v)=U(v)-U(u).
\end{equation}

The positive-lower-volume restriction in the separation result below is necessary even in a closed positive background.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:zero-volume-comparison-collapse}\index{Model potential!failure of zero-volume separation}
In every complex dimension $n\geq2$, a positive closed background on a projective manifold has two normalized ordinary models $a\not\sim b$ with
\[
 P_\theta[a]_{\mathcal I}=b,\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(a)
 =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(b)=0,\qquad
 d_\theta(a,b)=0.
\]
For $n\geq3$, the same assertions hold for a positive nonclosed background with bounded mass.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Take $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{n-1}$ and the positive product background of \cref{ex:zero-volume-distinct-product-models}. Its models satisfy $a\leq b$ and $U(a)=U(b)=0$. Thus $\max(a,b)=b$, and the defining formula gives
\[
 d_\theta(a,b)=2U(b)-U(a)-U(b)=0.
\]
The product example proves the other assertions.
For the nonclosed case use \cref{ex:nonclosed-zero-volume-distinct-models}; its ordered models have the same zero upper volumes, so the displayed calculation applies unchanged.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model-volume-separation}
For potentials $u,v$ of positive lower volume,
\[
 d_\theta(u,v)=d_\theta(P_\theta[u],P_\theta[v]),\qquad
 d_\theta(u,v)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[v].
\]
There is $C_\theta>0$, depending on $\theta$ and $\mathrm{d}V$, such that, for $u,v\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$,
\[
 \|u-v\|_{L^1}\leq
 2C_\theta\min\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{2d_\theta(u,v)}\delta\right)^{1/n}\right\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Translate the inputs below zero, and put $p=P_\theta[u]$, $q=P_\theta[v]$, $m=\max(u,v)$ and $r=P_\theta[m]$. Envelope order gives
\[
 m\leq\max(p,q)\leq r.
\]
All these potentials have positive lower volume. The ordinary-envelope volume identity and monotonicity give $U(m)=U(\max(p,q))=U(r)$, while $U(u)=U(p)$ and $U(v)=U(q)$. This proves projection invariance. If $d_\theta(u,v)=0$, then $U(u)=U(v)=U(m)$. The relative full-mass equivalence and \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality} give $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[m]=P_\theta[v]$. Conversely, if $p=q$, projection invariance gives zero.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Now let $u,v\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ and set $s=d_\theta(u,v)$. Normalized compactness supplies $C_\theta$ bounding the $L^1$ norm of every $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential with supremum zero. If $s\geq\delta/2$, the asserted estimate follows from the two individual norm bounds. Suppose $0<s<\delta/2$ and put $t=(2s/\delta)^{1/n}$. The deficit from $u$ to $m$ is at most $s$, and the lower volume of $m$ is at least $\delta$. Thus \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch}, with $b=t^{-1}$, gives $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ such that
\[
 (1-t)m+th\leq u.
\]
For each normalized competitor for $P_\theta[h]$, the corresponding convex combination with $m$ lies below zero and is more singular than $u$. Since $u=P_\theta[u]$, taking the envelope gives
\[
 (1-t)m+tP_\theta[h]\leq u.
\]
Hence $\int_X(m-u)\,\mathrm{d}V\leq tC_\theta$, because $m\leq0$ and $P_\theta[h]$ has supremum zero. Applying the same argument to $v$ and adding gives the estimate. If $s=0$, use any $t\in(0,1)$ and let $t\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

The next bound supplies a uniform modulus for three-point comparisons. The triangle inequality itself fails, as \cref{ex:model-comparison-triangle-failure} shows.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model-volume-comparison-modulus}
For $u,v,w\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus}
 d_\theta(u,w)\leq d_\theta(u,v)+d_\theta(v,w)
 +2nM\min\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{2d_\theta(v,w)}\delta\right)^{1/n}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently every $d_\theta$-convergent sequence in $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ is $d_\theta$-Cauchy, and its limit is unique.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $s=d_\theta(v,w)$. If $s\geq\delta/2$, use $d_\theta(u,w)\leq2M$. If $0<s<\delta/2$, set $t=(2s/\delta)^{1/n}$. The deficit lemma supplies $h\leq v$ with
\[
 (1-t)\max(v,w)+th\leq v.
\]
Set $r=\max(u,v)$ and $z=\max(u,v,w)$. On the branch $z=\max(v,w)$ the displayed inequality applies; on the branch $z=u$, both $u$ and $h$ lie below $r$. Thus
\[
 (1-t)z+th\leq r.
\]
Addition, homogeneity and volume monotonicity give
\[
 U(z)-U(r)\leq\bigl(1-(1-t)^n\bigr)U(z)\leq nMt.
\]
Since $\max(u,w)\leq z$, it follows that
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\theta(u,w)
 &\leq2U(r)+2nMt-U(u)-U(w)\\
 &=d_\theta(u,v)+U(v)-U(w)+2nMt\\
 &\leq d_\theta(u,v)+s+2nMt.
 \end{split}
\]
For $s=0$, the same proof works with every $t\in(0,1)$; let $t\downarrow0$. This proves \eqref{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus}. Applying it with the proposed limit as the middle point shows that a convergent sequence is Cauchy. Applying it with a sequence member as the middle point and then using separation proves uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness}
Every $d_\theta$-Cauchy sequence in $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ converges for $d_\theta$ to a unique element of $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$. It also converges to that element in $L^1(X,\mathrm{d}V)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a subsequence $a_j$ of the given sequence such that
\[
 d_\theta(a_j,a_{j+1})<\delta\,2^{-(j+1)n}
 \qquad(j\geq1).
\]
For $k\geq0$, set
\[
 v_j^k=\max(a_j,\ldots,a_{j+k}),\qquad
 v_j=\left(\sup_{l\geq j}a_l\right)^*.
\]
For every $l\geq j$, the deficit lemma with $b=2^l$ gives $h_l\leq a_l$ satisfying
\[
 (1-2^{-l})\max(a_l,a_{l+1})+2^{-l}h_l\leq a_l.
\]
The maximum argument used above gives
\[
 (1-2^{-l})v_j^{l+1-j}+2^{-l}h_l\leq v_j^{l-j}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 U(v_j^{l+1-j})-U(v_j^{l-j})\leq nM2^{-l}.
\]
Summing from $l=j$ to $j+k-1$ and then applying increasing-volume continuity as $k\to\infty$ yields
\[
 0\leq U(v_j)-U(a_j)\leq nM2^{1-j}.
\]
Here $v_j$ is genuine and has lower volume at least $\delta$, because $a_j\leq v_j\leq0$.

Put $q_j=P_\theta[v_j]$. The ordinary-envelope volume theorem makes $q_j$ a fixed point with the same two volumes as $v_j$. The sequence decreases, and each $q_j$ has supremum zero. Normalized compactness therefore gives a genuine decreasing limit $q$. By \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity},
\[
 q=P_\theta[q],\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)
 =\lim_j\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q_j)\geq\delta,
 \qquad U(q_j)\longrightarrow U(q).
\]
Both $a_j$ and $q$ lie below $q_j$. Hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\theta(a_j,q)
 &\leq\bigl(U(q_j)-U(a_j)\bigr)+\bigl(U(q_j)-U(q)\bigr)\\
 &\leq nM2^{1-j}+U(q_j)-U(q)\longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
\]
To recover the whole sequence, choose a late subsequence member $a_j$ as the middle point in \eqref{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus}. The Cauchy assumption makes the first term uniformly small for all sufficiently late original members, while $d_\theta(a_j,q)$ makes the other terms small. Thus the original sequence converges to $q$. Uniqueness follows from the preceding proposition, and the $L^1$ estimate in \cref{prop:model-volume-separation} proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The closed lower-volume bound is essential for this completeness statement.

\begin{example}\label{ex:strict-volume-cutoff-incomplete}
On a compact Riemann surface with a smooth positive form $\theta$ of integral one, the ordinary envelope fixed points of mass strictly greater than $\delta$, for $0<\delta<1$, do not form a complete space for $d_\theta$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Fix a point $p$ and use the nonpositive Green potential $g_p$ from \cref{thm:curve-trace-realization}, so that $\ddc g_p=\delta_p-\theta$. For $0\leq a<1$, put $q_a=P_\theta[ag_p]$. The current of $ag_p$ is $a\delta_p+(1-a)\theta$, with non-pluripolar mass $1-a$. Since the background is closed, its two singularity volumes equal this mass. The ordinary-envelope volume theorem gives
\[
 q_a=P_\theta[q_a],\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q_a)=U(q_a)=1-a.
\]
The potentials $q_a$ decrease as $a$ increases. Therefore \eqref{eq:volume-comparison-basic} gives $d_\theta(q_a,q_b)=|a-b|$. Choose $a_j\uparrow1-\delta$ with $a_j<1-\delta$. The resulting sequence is Cauchy and every term has mass greater than $\delta$. Any $d_\theta$ limit has upper volume $\delta$ by \eqref{eq:volume-comparison-basic}. On a curve the two volumes coincide, so no such limit belongs to the strict-cutoff space.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\section{Rooftops and capacity convergence}
\label{sec:volume-rooftop-capacity}

Return to the fixed big background $\theta$ on $X$. A small upper-volume deficit also controls the common lower envelope of two potentials. For a finite family, the sum of the deficits must stay below the lower volume of a common upper bound. Applying this criterion after stretching gives increasing lower bounds for a suitable subsequence of models. Together with the decreasing upper bounds from the preceding section, they turn comparison convergence into capacity convergence.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finite-roof-volume-deficit}
Let $u_1,\ldots,u_N,w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, with $N\geq1$ and $u_i\preceq w$. If
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^N\bigl(U(w)-U(u_i)\bigr)
       <\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(w),
\]
then $P_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_N)$ is genuine.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Genuineness of a finite rooftop is unchanged by bounded changes of its obstacles. Choose $v_i\sim u_i$ whose masses satisfy
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^N\left(U(w)-\int_X\mu_{v_i}\right)
       <\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(w).
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Translate so that $v_i\leq w\leq0$, and put
\[
 v_{i,k}=\max(v_i,w-k),\qquad
 p_k=P_\theta(v_{1,k},\ldots,v_{N,k}).
\]
The common competitor $w-k$ gives $w-k\leq p_k\leq w$, so $p_k\sim w$. If the desired rooftop were not genuine, $p_k$ would decrease to $-\infty$, and normalized compactness would give $\sup_Xp_k\to-\infty$.

Fix $s>0$ and choose $k>s$ so large that $p_k\leq-s$. Iterating \cref{prop:rooftop-measure} and using locality on $\{v_i>-s\}$ gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(w)
 &\leq\int_X\mu_{p_k}
 \leq\sum_{i=1}^N\int_{\{v_{i,k}\leq-s\}}\mu_{v_{i,k}}\\
 &\leq\sum_{i=1}^N\left(U(w)-\int_{\{v_i>-s\}}\mu_{v_i}\right).
 \end{split}
\]
Here $v_{i,k}\sim w$, and $w-k<-s$ makes $\{v_{i,k}>-s\}=\{v_i>-s\}$. Let $s\to\infty$. The measures are finite and non-pluripolar, so the resulting inequality contradicts the choice of $v_i$.
\end{proof}

To control a rooftop's volume, first stretch each obstacle, then move both stretches a fixed fraction back toward their maximum. The softened stretches have a genuine common rooftop by the lemma.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:volume-rooftop-continuity}\index{Envelope!rooftop volume control}
Let $u_j,u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, with $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)>0$, and suppose $d_\theta(u_j,u)\to0$. For all sufficiently large $j$, the rooftop $p_j=P_\theta(u_j,u)$ is genuine, and
\[
 d_\theta(p_j,u)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)
       \longrightarrow\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $\delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$, $m_j=\max(u_j,u)$ and $s_j=d_\theta(u_j,u)$. After discarding finitely many indices, choose $b_j\to\infty$ with $b_j>1$ and $b_j^ns_j<\delta$. The deficits of both $u_j$ and $u$ from $m_j$ are at most $s_j$, while $m_j$ has lower volume at least $\delta$. Thus \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} makes
\[
 G_j=Q_{b_j}(u_j,m_j),\qquad H_j=Q_{b_j}(u,m_j)
\]
genuine. Choose $a\in(0,1)$ with $2nMa<\delta$, and set
\[
 g_j=(1-a)m_j+aG_j,\qquad h_j=(1-a)m_j+aH_j.
\]
These potentials lie below $m_j$. Addition and monotonicity give
\[
 2U(m_j)-U(g_j)-U(h_j)
 \leq2\bigl(1-(1-a)^n\bigr)U(m_j)
 \leq2nMa<\delta.
\]
The preceding lemma therefore gives a genuine $r_j=P_\theta(g_j,h_j)$. For large $j$, put $t_j=(ab_j)^{-1}<1$. The defining inequalities for $G_j,H_j$ show that
\[
 (1-t_j)m_j+t_jr_j\leq u_j,u.
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Hence $p_j$ is genuine. For either volume, addition and monotonicity give
\[
 (1-t_j)^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq(1-t_j)^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(m_j)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(p_j)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u).
\]
Since $t_j\to0$ and $p_j\leq u$, this proves both assertions.
\end{proof}

For ordinary models, the finite rooftops are again models. Their normalization prevents collapse when the number of obstacles tends to infinity.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}\index{Model potential!capacity convergence}
Suppose $u_j,u\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ and $d_\theta(u_j,u)\to0$. After passing to a subsequence and relabeling it, there are ordinary model potentials $v_j,w_j\in\mathcal M_{\delta/2}(\theta)$ such that
\[
 w_j\leq u_j\leq v_j,\qquad w_j\leq u\leq v_j,
\]
$v_j$ decreases to $u$, $w_j$ increases with $(\lim_jw_j)^*=u$, and
\[
 d_\theta(v_j,u)+d_\theta(w_j,u)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The original sequence converges to $u$ in capacity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For large $j$, put $r_j=P_\theta(u_j,u)$, using \cref{prop:volume-rooftop-continuity}. It is a model: envelope order gives
$r_j\leq P_\theta[r_j]\leq u_j,u$, so $P_\theta[r_j]$ is a competitor for the rooftop and equals $r_j$. The sequence $u_j$ is Cauchy by \cref{prop:model-volume-comparison-modulus}. Choose a subsequence so that
\[
 d_\theta(u_j,u_{j+1})<\delta\,2^{-(j+1)n},\qquad
 U(u)-U(r_j)<\delta\,4^{-(j+1)n}.
\]
The tail-maximum construction in the proof of \cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness} gives
\[
 v_j=P_\theta\left[\left(\sup_{l\geq j}u_l\right)^*\right]
       \searrow u,\qquad d_\theta(v_j,u)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed that construction gives the unique comparison limit, which here is $u$. Its upper models satisfy $v_j\geq u_j,u$ and have lower volume at least $\delta$.

For the lower bounds, the deficit lemma gives genuine potentials
\[
 H_l=Q_{4^l}(r_l,u),\qquad K_l=Q_{2^l}(r_l,u).
\]
Their defining inequalities give
\[
 (1-2^{-l})u+2^{-l}H_l\leq K_l\leq u,
 \qquad U(u)-U(K_l)\leq nM2^{-l}.
\]
Choose $j$ so large that $nM2^{1-j}<\delta$. For every $k\geq0$, the sum of the deficits of $K_j,\ldots,K_{j+k}$ is less than $\delta$, so \cref{lem:finite-roof-volume-deficit} makes their rooftop genuine. Since $r_l\leq u$, stretching decreases with its parameter; for $l\geq j$,
\[
 Q_{2^j}(r_l,u)\geq K_l.
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Thus $R_j^k=P_\theta(Q_{2^j}(r_j,u),\ldots,Q_{2^j}(r_{j+k},u))$ is genuine. The convex inequality for each obstacle gives
\[
 (1-2^{-j})u+2^{-j}R_j^k
       \leq w_j^k:=P_\theta(r_j,\ldots,r_{j+k})\leq u.
\]
For either volume,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-rooftop-volume-bound}
 (1-2^{-j})^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(w_j^k)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u).
\end{equation}
The same envelope-order argument used for $r_j$ shows that $w_j^k$ is a model. For fixed $j$ it decreases in $k$, with supremum zero. Normalized compactness gives a genuine limit $w_j$. The lower-volume conclusion of \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} passes the lower bound in \eqref{eq:tail-rooftop-volume-bound} to $w_j$. It is positive, so the theorem's upper-volume conclusion passes that bound as well.

The $w_j$ increase and satisfy $w_j\leq r_j\leq u_j,u$. After discarding finitely many indices, they have lower volume at least $\delta/2$, and \eqref{eq:tail-rooftop-volume-bound} gives $d_\theta(w_j,u)\leq nM2^{-j}$. By \cref{prop:model-volume-separation}, $w_j\to u$ in $L^1$, so $(\lim_jw_j)^*=u$. The two monotone sequences converge in capacity by \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}. Their pointwise bounds squeeze the selected $u_j$ to $u$ in capacity. Applying the construction to every subsequence proves capacity convergence for the original sequence.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
\section{Volumes of multiplier-ideal envelopes}
\label{sec:ideal-volume-approximation}

Analytic regularizations recover the ideal envelope, whose volume may exceed that of the original potential. Decreasing-model continuity computes this excess. A strict analytic potential absorbs the curvature error, so the same comparison can be made entirely in the fixed background. For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, set
\[
 \mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)
   =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}),\qquad
 \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)
   =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}).
\]
They depend only on the multiplier-ideal class of $u$ in the fixed background. By \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge} and gauge invariance of singularity volumes, they also depend only on the associated $\ddc$-class and the multiplier-ideal data, rather than on its smooth decomposition. Moreover, \cref{prop:p-below-i,thm:volume-monotonicity} give
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
The ideal envelope is an ordinary fixed point by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}. Its volumes are limits of the volumes of the dyadic analytic types.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dyadic-ideal-volume-limit}
Fix a Hermitian form $h$, and take the dyadic regularizations $a_j$ of $u$ in backgrounds $\theta_j=\theta+\epsilon_jh$ as in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}. Then
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(a_j)
       \searrow\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
If $\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)>0$, then also
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(a_j)
       \searrow\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:dyadic-analytic-envelope-limit}, the analytic ordinary models $q_j=P_{\theta_j}[a_j]$ decrease to $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Apply \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}. Since $q_j\sim a_j$ by \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, their two volumes agree term by term.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
Positive lower volume of the ideal envelope lets relative full mass detect when this limit also computes the upper volume of $u$.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:conditional-i-characterization}\index{Envelope!equality for all obstacles}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and put $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Suppose $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0$. Then the following are equivalent:
\[
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
      =\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
They are also equivalent to $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,q)$ and to
\[
 P_{\theta,F}[u]=P_{\theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
 \quad\text{for every smooth real }F.
\]
If they hold, the corresponding equality for the lower volume also holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If the envelopes agree, \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} gives equality of both volumes with those of $q$.

Conversely, upper-volume equality and $u\preceq q$ put $u$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,q)$. Since $q=P_\theta[q]$, \cref{cor:full-mass-envelope-characterization} gives $P_\theta[u]=q$.

The same corollary identifies zero-obstacle equality with membership in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,q)$. By \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection}, this implies equality for every smooth obstacle; taking $F=0$ gives the converse.
\end{proof}

Under the hypotheses of the proposition, \cref{cor:dyadic-goodness-criterion} also identifies equality of the upper volumes with convergence of the dyadic ordinary envelopes to $P_\theta[u]$. The next construction removes their background error while retaining the upper-volume limit. The corrected analytic types need not be ordered.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, put $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, and suppose $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0$. There are analytic $b_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $b_j\to u$ in $L^1$ such that the ordinary models $r_j=P_\theta[b_j]$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-big-analytic-comparison}
 d_\theta(r_j,q)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 d_\theta(r_j,u)\longrightarrow
 \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)
       -\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\end{equation}
Their lower volumes converge to $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)$, and $r_j\to q$ in $L^1$ and capacity. In particular $d_\theta(r_j,u)\to0$ if and only if $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a Hermitian form $h$. Bigness and \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input} supply an analytic $\rho\leq0$ and a rational $\kappa>0$ with $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\kappa h$. Choose the dyadic $a_j$ in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}, enlarging their errors to positive rational numbers $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$. Put
\[
 c_j=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\epsilon_j},\qquad
 b_j=c_ja_j+(1-c_j)\rho.
\]
Then $\theta+\ddc b_j\geq((1-c_j)\kappa-c_j\epsilon_j)h=0$. The coefficients are rational, so \cref{prop:analytic-type-calculus} gives analytic singularities. Since $a_j\to u$ in $L^1$ and $c_j\to1$, also $b_j\to u$ there.

Choose $A>0$ with $\theta\leq Ah$, and set
\[
 \widehat\theta_j=\theta+(1+A/\kappa)\epsilon_jh,
 \qquad t_j=P_{\widehat\theta_j}[a_j].
\]
These backgrounds decrease to $\theta$ and dominate $\theta+\epsilon_jh$. The dyadic envelope theorem therefore gives $t_j\searrow q$. Moreover
\[
 c_j\widehat\theta_j-\theta
  =(1-c_j)(Ah-\theta)+c_j\epsilon_jh\geq0.
\]
The analytic-envelope identity gives $r_j\sim b_j$. Thus $r_j/c_j$ is a normalized $\widehat\theta_j$-plurisubharmonic competitor more singular than $a_j$, since $\rho$ is bounded above. It follows that $r_j\leq c_jt_j$. Also $q\leq t_j\leq c_jt_j$.

For a lower bound, $q\sim_{\mathcal I}u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. Addition and scaling preserve this order by \cref{thm:local-i-inputs,lem:hermitian-i-calculus}. Thus $c_jq+(1-c_j)\rho\preceq_{\mathcal I}b_j$. This potential lies below zero, so envelope order and \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} give
\[
 c_jq+(1-c_j)\rho\leq P_\theta[b_j]_{\mathcal I}=r_j
       \leq c_jt_j.
\]
For either singularity volume, homogeneity, addition and monotonicity now yield
\[
 c_j^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(r_j)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\max(r_j,q))
 \leq c_j^n\operatorname{vol}_{\widehat\theta_j}(t_j).
\]
The last comparison uses both $\theta\leq c_j\widehat\theta_j$ and $\max(r_j,q)\leq c_jt_j$. By \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}, the outer terms tend to $\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)$. This proves convergence of both volumes and the first limit in \eqref{eq:fixed-big-analytic-comparison}. In particular, $r_j$ and $q$ have a common positive lower-volume bound after discarding finitely many indices. Their $L^1$ and capacity convergence follow from \cref{prop:model-volume-separation,thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}.

To prove the second limit, shift $u$ below $q$; the comparison is invariant under constants. Then
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(r_j)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\max(r_j,u))
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\max(r_j,q)).
\]
Both bounds tend to $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)$. Substitute in the definition of $d_\theta$. The last equivalence is \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization}.
\end{proof}

For a Hermitian background the simpler rescaling in \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density} gives decreasing analytic models, with the same limits by \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}. In a general big background, the strict correction above may introduce additional poles. The upper-volume comparison still detects exactly the ideal-envelope excess.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:analytic-model-volume-criterion}\index{Analytic singularity!volume approximation}
Let $p=P_\theta[p]$ have positive lower volume. Then $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$ if and only if there are analytic ordinary models $p_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $d_\theta(p_j,p)\to0$.

More generally, if $p_j=P_\theta[p_j]_{\mathcal I}$ and $d_\theta(p_j,p)\to0$, then $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$, apply \cref{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation} with $u=p$. For the converse it suffices to prove the more general assertion, because analytic ordinary models are ideal envelope fixed points.

Put $q=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$ and $\delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)>0$. Choose $s_j\in(0,1)$ tending to zero with $d_\theta(p_j,p)<\delta s_j^n$, after discarding finitely many indices. For $m_j=\max(p_j,p)$ one has
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(m_j)\geq\delta,
 \qquad U(m_j)-U(p_j)\leq d_\theta(p_j,p).
\]
By \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch}, there is $h_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $(1-s_j)m_j+s_jh_j\leq p_j$. Set $H_j=P_\theta[h_j]_{\mathcal I}$. Monotonicity, addition and scaling in \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations} give
\[
 (1-s_j)q+s_jH_j
 \leq P_\theta[(1-s_j)m_j+s_jh_j]_{\mathcal I}
 \leq P_\theta[p_j]_{\mathcal I}=p_j.
\]
Consequently $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)\geq(1-s_j)^n\delta$ by addition and volume monotonicity. The models $p_j,p$ eventually lie in $\mathcal M_{\delta/2}(\theta)$, so \cref{prop:model-volume-separation} gives $p_j\to p$ in $L^1$.

The $H_j$ have supremum zero. Normalized compactness bounds $\int_X(-H_j)\,\mathrm{d}V$ by a constant $C_\theta$. Since $q\leq0$, the displayed convex inequality gives
\[
 \int_X(q-p_j)_+\,\mathrm{d}V
       \leq s_j\int_X(-H_j)\,\mathrm{d}V
       \leq C_\theta s_j\longrightarrow0.
\]
Passing to the $L^1$ limit gives $q\leq p$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere. The opposite inequality holds by the envelope comparison, so $p=q$.
\end{proof}

Thus the positive-lower-volume ideal envelope models are precisely the limits of analytic ordinary models for $d_\theta$. On each $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ they form a closed set for this convergence and inherit Cauchy completeness from \cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness}. The approximants constructed above have a common positive lower-volume bound, which need not equal the lower volume of the limiting model.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:hermitian-i-characterization}
For a big nonclosed background on a manifold with bounded mass, find criteria for $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I})>0$ in terms of restricted multiplier ideals or divisorial orders. When this lower volume vanishes, which implications between envelope equality and upper-volume equality in \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization} remain valid?
\end{problem}

One implication fails: upper-volume equality with the ideal model does not force envelope equality at zero lower ideal volume. In \cref{ex:nonclosed-zero-volume-distinct-models}, the background is positive and nonclosed on a projective manifold with bounded mass, while $q=P_\Theta[a]_{\mathcal I}$ satisfies $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\Theta(a)=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\Theta(q)=0$ and $P_\Theta[a]\ne q$. The example does not decide the other implications asked for in \cref{qu:hermitian-i-characterization}.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\section{Model comparison under background changes}
\label{sec:model-background-comparison}

A smooth change of representative preserves the current but changes the zero obstacle used to normalize its model. Taking the ordinary envelope again gives an exact correspondence for the volume comparison. An increase of the background changes the volumes; a common positive lower-volume bound controls this error uniformly and allows analytic approximations to have vanishing curvature loss.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model-comparison-gauge}
Let $f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$ and $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$. For every $\delta>0$, the map
\[
 G_f:\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)\longrightarrow\mathcal M_\delta(\theta'),
 \qquad G_f(p)=P_{\theta'}[p-f],
\]
is a bijection with inverse $q\mapsto P_\theta[q+f]$. It preserves both singularity volumes, and
\[
 d_{\theta'}(G_f(p),G_f(q))=d_\theta(p,q).
\]
It takes analytic models to analytic models and ideal envelope models to ideal envelope models.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The map $u\mapsto u-f$ identifies the competitor currents defining the two volumes. Also $\max(u-f,v-f)=\max(u,v)-f$, so
\[
 d_{\theta'}(u-f,v-f)=d_\theta(u,v).
\]
By \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge}, $G_f(p)+f\sim p$. Thus $G_f(p)$ has the same volumes as $p$ and, by \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, is an ordinary model. Dependence of $d$ only on ordinary types proves the displayed identity. Taking the ordinary envelope of $G_f(p)+f\sim p$ gives $P_\theta[G_f(p)+f]=p$. The same argument with the two backgrounds interchanged proves bijectivity.

Smooth subtraction and bounded changes preserve analytic singularities. If $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$, \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge} also gives
\[
 P_{\theta'}[p-f]_{\mathcal I}\sim p-f\sim G_f(p).
\]
The ideal envelope is an ordinary model by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}. Ordinary models with the same ordinary type coincide, so it equals $G_f(p)$. The inverse map gives the converse.
\end{proof}

Fix a Hermitian form $h$. Choose a Hermitian $\Omega\geq\theta+2h$ and an analytic $\rho\leq0$ with $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\kappa h$, where $\kappa>0$ is rational. Such a $\rho$ exists by bigness and \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input}. Put $K=v_+(\Omega)$. For $\delta>0$, set
\[
 b_\delta=1+\frac{\delta}{2^{n+1}nK},\qquad
 \kappa_\delta=(1-b_\delta^{-1})\kappa.
\]
These constants will be fixed while the potentials and backgrounds vary.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:uniform-background-volume-comparison}
Let $\beta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form with $\theta\leq\beta\leq\theta+h$. Suppose $u\in\PSH(X,\beta)$ and $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\beta(u)\geq\delta>0$. For every smooth background $\gamma$ with $\beta\leq\gamma\leq\beta+\varepsilon h$, $0\leq\varepsilon\leq1$, either singularity volume satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\beta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\gamma(u)
 \leq(1+\varepsilon/\kappa_\delta)^n\operatorname{vol}_\beta(u).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $u\leq0$, so $u\leq V_\beta$, and note that $\rho\leq V_\beta$. Since $\Omega\geq\beta$ and $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\beta(u)\geq\delta$, \cref{lem:positive-volume-stretch} makes $H=Q_{b_\delta}(u,V_\beta)$ genuine. The potential
\[
 w=b_\delta^{-1}H+(1-b_\delta^{-1})\rho
\]
satisfies $w\leq u$ and $\beta+\ddc w\geq\kappa_\delta h$. The addition argument of \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison}, with $t=\varepsilon/\kappa_\delta$, therefore gives the upper bound. Its constants are independent of $\beta$ and $u$. The lower bound is volume monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:vanishing-background-comparison}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have lower volume at least $\delta>0$, and let $\eta$ be a smooth background with $\theta\leq\eta\leq\theta+\varepsilon h$, where $0\leq\varepsilon\leq1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vanishing-background-comparison}
 \left|d_\eta(P_\eta[u],P_\eta[v])-d_\theta(u,v)\right|
 \leq2K\left((1+\varepsilon/\kappa_\delta)^n-1\right).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The preceding proposition applies to $u$, $v$ and $m=\max(u,v)$, and their upper volumes in background $\theta$ are at most $K$. Thus, for $x=u,v,m$, the errors
\[
 e_x=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\eta(x)
       -\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(x)
\]
lie between zero and $K((1+\varepsilon/\kappa_\delta)^n-1)$. The difference $d_\eta(u,v)-d_\theta(u,v)$ is $2e_m-e_u-e_v$, whose absolute value is at most twice this bound. Finally \cref{prop:model-volume-separation}, in background $\eta$, identifies $d_\eta(u,v)$ with the projected comparison in \eqref{eq:vanishing-background-comparison}.
\end{proof}

In particular, for $p_j,p\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ and $\theta\leq\eta_j\leq\theta+\varepsilon_jh$ with $\varepsilon_j\to0$,
\[
 d_\theta(p_j,p)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d_{\eta_j}(P_{\eta_j}[p_j],P_{\eta_j}[p])\longrightarrow0.
\]
Monotonicity of the sequence of backgrounds is unnecessary for this estimate. The next result also allows the analytic approximants themselves to belong only to the larger backgrounds.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:analytic-approximation-curvature-error}
Let $p=P_\theta[p]$ have positive lower volume, and let $\eta_j$ be smooth backgrounds with $\theta\leq\eta_j\leq\theta+\varepsilon_jh$, where $\varepsilon_j\geq0$ tends to zero. Then $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$ if and only if there are analytic $a_j\in\PSH(X,\eta_j)$ with
\[
 d_{\eta_j}(a_j,p)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The $a_j$ may be required to be ordinary models in their respective backgrounds. Given any such analytic approximants, rational convex combinations with the fixed $\rho$ produce analytic $b_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ whose models satisfy
\[
 d_\theta(P_\theta[b_j],p)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad P_\theta[b_j]\longrightarrow p
       \quad\text{in }L^1\text{ and capacity}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$. By \cref{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation}, there are analytic ordinary models $r_j$ in background $\theta$ with $d_\theta(r_j,p)\to0$ and a common positive lower-volume bound. Applying \cref{cor:vanishing-background-comparison} gives $d_{\eta_j}(r_j,p)\to0$. One can also use $a_j=P_{\eta_j}[r_j]$, since the analytic-envelope identity preserves their ordinary types in background $\eta_j$.

Conversely, normalize the given $a_j$ below zero and put $m_j=\max(a_j,p)$ and $\delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)>0$. The basic comparison bound and the preceding uniform volume estimate give
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\eta_j}(a_j),\quad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\eta_j}(m_j)
       \longrightarrow\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p).
\]
Indeed the upper volume of $p$ in background $\eta_j$ tends to its upper volume in background $\theta$, and both displayed differences from that volume are bounded in absolute value by $d_{\eta_j}(a_j,p)$.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Enlarge the errors to positive rational numbers tending to zero, and put
\[
 c_j=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\varepsilon_j},\qquad
 b_j=c_ja_j+(1-c_j)\rho,\qquad r_j=P_\theta[b_j].
\]
As in \cref{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation}, the current of $b_j$ in background $\theta$ is positive and its singularities are analytic. Choose $A>0$ with $\theta\leq Ah$, and set
\[
 \widehat\theta_j=\theta+(1+A/\kappa)\varepsilon_jh,
 \qquad M_j=P_{\widehat\theta_j}[m_j].
\]
Then $\widehat\theta_j\geq\eta_j$ and $c_j\widehat\theta_j\geq\theta$. Since $r_j\sim b_j$, the potential $r_j/c_j$ is a nonpositive $\widehat\theta_j$-plurisubharmonic competitor more singular than $a_j$, hence more singular than $m_j$. Therefore $r_j\leq c_jM_j$. Also $p\leq M_j\leq c_jM_j$.

The lower volume of $m_j$ in background $\eta_j$ is at least $\delta$. For large $j$, \cref{prop:uniform-background-volume-comparison} applies with $\beta=\eta_j$, $\gamma=\widehat\theta_j$ and perturbation bound $(1+A/\kappa)\varepsilon_jh$. Consequently
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\widehat\theta_j}(m_j)
       \longrightarrow\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p).
\]
The ordinary-envelope theorem gives the same upper volume for $M_j$. On the other hand,
\[
 c_j\eta_j+(1-c_j)(\theta-\kappa h)\leq\theta.
\]
Addition and background monotonicity therefore bound the upper volume of $b_j$ below by $c_j^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\eta_j}(a_j)$. Since $r_j\sim b_j$, the two comparisons yield
\[
 \begin{split}
 c_j^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\eta_j}(a_j)
 &\leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(r_j)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\max(r_j,p))\\
 &\leq c_j^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\widehat\theta_j}(m_j).
 \end{split}
\]
Both outer terms tend to $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)$, proving $d_\theta(r_j,p)\to0$. The analytic criterion \cref{thm:analytic-model-volume-criterion} gives $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$.

For large $j$, \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} with $b=2$ supplies a convex lower bound for $r_j$ whose first term is $\frac12\max(r_j,p)$. Volume addition gives $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(r_j)\geq2^{-n}\delta$. Thus \cref{prop:model-volume-separation,thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence} give the asserted $L^1$ and capacity convergence of the models.
\end{proof}

The comparison is unchanged by bounded changes of the approximating potentials. The convergence of canonical models in the theorem therefore does not assert convergence of arbitrary analytic representatives. For two-sided background errors, the next section constructs admissible reference models in the varying backgrounds.

\section{Two-sided background perturbations}
\label{sec:two-sided-model-backgrounds}

A potential in the original background may cease to be admissible when the background decreases. Mixing it with a strict potential restores positivity, and ordinary projection gives a model in the new background. The volume comparison controls the error of this transport uniformly on a positive lower-volume sector. It also makes the limiting notion of approximation independent of the strict potential used in the correction.

To use the correction in a varying background, we first make the preceding estimate uniform for pairs of forms near $\theta$.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nearby-background-volume-comparison}
For every $\delta>0$ there are $C_\delta>0$ and $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, whenever smooth backgrounds satisfy
\[
 \theta-\varepsilon h\leq\beta\leq\gamma\leq\theta+\varepsilon h,
 \qquad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0,
\]
and $u\in\PSH(X,\beta)$ has lower volume at least $\delta$, either volume satisfies
\[
 0\leq\operatorname{vol}_\gamma(u)-\operatorname{vol}_\beta(u)
       \leq C_\delta\varepsilon.
\]
For two such potentials $u,v$, one also has
\[
 \left|d_\gamma(P_\gamma[u],P_\gamma[v])-d_\beta(u,v)\right|
       \leq2C_\delta\varepsilon.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\theta_0=\theta-\kappa h/2$ and $h_0=(1+\kappa/2)h$. The fixed $\rho$ is strict in $\theta_0$, so this background is big. If $\varepsilon\leq\min(\kappa/2,1)$, then $\theta_0\leq\beta\leq\theta_0+h_0$ and $\gamma\leq\beta+2\varepsilon h_0$. Apply \cref{prop:uniform-background-volume-comparison} with reference background $\theta_0$ and comparison form $h_0$. Its strict margin and dominant Hermitian form can be fixed independently of $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Their common upper mass bound turns the multiplicative estimate into the stated additive estimate for $\varepsilon\leq1/2$. Apply it to $u$, $v$ and $\max(u,v)$; the same three-error calculation as in \cref{cor:vanishing-background-comparison}, followed by ordinary projection, gives the second assertion.
\end{proof}

Choose $A>0$ with $\theta\leq Ah$ and put $B=2(1+A/\kappa)$. For $p\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $p\leq0$ and a smooth background $\eta$ with $\theta-\varepsilon h\leq\eta\leq\theta+\varepsilon h$, where $0<\varepsilon<\min(\kappa/2,1/B)$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-sided-model-transport}
 c_\varepsilon=1-\varepsilon/\kappa,\qquad
 T^\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}(p)
   =P_\eta[c_\varepsilon p+(1-c_\varepsilon)\rho],\qquad
 \widehat\theta_\varepsilon=\theta+B\varepsilon h.
\end{equation}
The mixed potential is $\eta$-plurisubharmonic, since its current is at least $\eta-\theta+\varepsilon h$.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:two-sided-model-transport}\index{Model potential!change of background}
For $p\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$, the transport in \eqref{eq:two-sided-model-transport} is an ordinary model in background $\eta$, of lower volume at least $c_\varepsilon^n\delta$. For either volume,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-sided-transport-volume}
 c_\varepsilon^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(p)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\eta(T^\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}(p))
 \leq c_\varepsilon^n\operatorname{vol}_{\widehat\theta_\varepsilon}(p).
\end{equation}
For $p,q\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-sided-comparison-error}
 \begin{split}
 &\left|d_\eta(T^\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}(p),T^\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}(q))
                  -c_\varepsilon^n d_\theta(p,q)\right|\\
 &\qquad\leq2c_\varepsilon^nK
       \left((1+B\varepsilon/\kappa_\delta)^n-1\right).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
For fixed $p$, these models tend to $p$ in $L^1$ and capacity as $\varepsilon\to0$. Two choices of strict analytic potential, rational margin and vanishing error bounds give $d_\eta$-comparison tending to zero between the transports of $p$ in any common sequence of admissible backgrounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $c=c_\varepsilon$, $s=1-c$, and $v=cp+s\rho$. Addition in background $\theta-s\kappa h\leq\eta$ gives the lower bound in \eqref{eq:two-sided-transport-volume} for $v$. Its lower volume is positive, so \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} gives the same two volumes for $T(p)=T^\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}(p)$.

The choices of $B$ and $c\geq1/2$ give
\[
 c\widehat\theta_\varepsilon-(\theta+\varepsilon h)
 \geq\varepsilon(cB-1-A/\kappa)h\geq0.
\]
Every nonpositive $\eta$-plurisubharmonic competitor $w\preceq v$ satisfies $w/c\in\PSH(X,\widehat\theta_\varepsilon)$ and $w/c\preceq p$, since $\rho\leq0$. Taking envelopes yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-sided-transport-bounds}
 cp+s\rho\leq T(p)\leq cP_{\widehat\theta_\varepsilon}[p].
\end{equation}
Volume monotonicity and the ordinary-envelope identity in the larger background prove \eqref{eq:two-sided-transport-volume}.

Let $m=\max(p,q)$. The same pointwise bounds give
\[
 cm+s\rho\leq\max(T(p),T(q))
       \leq cP_{\widehat\theta_\varepsilon}[m].
\]
Consequently the upper volumes of $T(p)$, $T(q)$ and their maximum lie between $c^n$ times the respective upper volumes of $p$, $q$, $m$ in backgrounds $\theta$ and $\widehat\theta_\varepsilon$. The lower volumes of all three original inputs are at least $\delta$, and their upper volumes are at most $K$. By \cref{prop:uniform-background-volume-comparison}, each of the three errors is nonnegative and at most $c^nK((1+B\varepsilon/\kappa_\delta)^n-1)$. This proves \eqref{eq:two-sided-comparison-error}.

For fixed $p$, \cref{cor:ordinary-envelope-background-limit,lem:monotone-psh-capacity} give $P_{\widehat\theta_\varepsilon}[p]\to p$ in $L^1$ and capacity as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Decreasing tail suprema handle arbitrary error sequences. Quasi-continuity and integrability give $cp+s\rho\to p$ in both senses. The bounds \eqref{eq:two-sided-transport-bounds} prove the asserted limits.

To compare two choices along a sequence $\eta_j$, denote their coefficients by $c_j^{(i)}$ and their upper backgrounds by $\theta+B_i\varepsilon_j^{(i)}h$, $i=0,1$. Put $c_j=\min_i c_j^{(i)}$ and $a_j=\max_i B_i\varepsilon_j^{(i)}$. Since the envelopes are nonpositive, both transported models lie below $c_jP_{\theta+a_jh}[p]$. Moreover $\eta_j\leq c_j(\theta+a_jh)$: use the previous form inequality for an index attaining the smaller coefficient. The upper volume of their maximum is therefore at most $c_j^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta+a_jh}(p)$, which tends to $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)$. Each individual upper volume is at least $(c_j^{(i)})^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)$ and has the same limit. Their $d_{\eta_j}$-comparison tends to zero.
\end{proof}

For $p_j,p\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$, \eqref{eq:two-sided-comparison-error} shows that $d_\theta(p_j,p)\to0$ if and only if the transported comparisons tend to zero along backgrounds with errors tending to zero. The next criterion permits approximants chosen directly in those backgrounds.

\enlargethispage{2\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:two-sided-analytic-criterion}
Let $p=P_\theta[p]$ have positive lower volume. Let smooth backgrounds $\eta_j$ satisfy $\theta-\varepsilon_jh\leq\eta_j\leq\theta+\varepsilon_jh$, where $0<\varepsilon_j<\min(\kappa/2,1/B)$ are rational and tend to zero. Put $p_j=T^\rho_{\eta_j,\varepsilon_j}(p)$. Then $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$ if and only if there are analytic $a_j\in\PSH(X,\eta_j)$ with
\[
 d_{\eta_j}(a_j,p_j)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The approximants may be required to be ordinary models. This convergence condition is independent of the fixed strict analytic potential and admissible vanishing error bounds used to transport $p$. Every such analytic approximation admits rational corrections in the original background whose ordinary models converge to $p$ for $d_\theta$, in $L^1$ and in capacity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$. Choose analytic ordinary models $r_j$ in background $\theta$ with $d_\theta(r_j,p)\to0$ by \cref{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation}. Their lower volumes have a common positive bound. The transports $a_j=T^\rho_{\eta_j,\varepsilon_j}(r_j)$ have analytic singularities: the convex coefficients are rational, and ordinary projection preserves the bounded-difference type of an analytic potential. The comparison estimate gives $d_{\eta_j}(a_j,p_j)\to0$.

Conversely, assume these analytic approximants exist, and put $\delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)$. For large $j$, the lower volume of $p_j$ is at least $2^{-n}\delta$. The upper-volume deficit from $a_j$ to $\max(a_j,p_j)$ tends to zero. Apply \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} with $b=2$ in background $\eta_j$; addition gives
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\eta_j}(a_j)\geq2^{-2n}\delta
\]
for all sufficiently large $j$.

Put $\beta_j=\theta+\varepsilon_jh$. \Cref{lem:nearby-background-volume-comparison} and projection invariance imply $d_{\beta_j}(a_j,p_j)\to0$. Also $d_{\beta_j}(p_j,p)\to0$. Indeed \eqref{eq:two-sided-transport-bounds} bounds $\max(p_j,p)$ above by $c_{\varepsilon_j}P_{\widehat\theta_{\varepsilon_j}}[p]$, and the form inequality in its proof also dominates $\beta_j$. The upper volume of this maximum in background $\beta_j$ is squeezed to $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)$, as are the upper volumes of $p_j$ and $p$.

Project the three inputs to ordinary models in background $\beta_j$. Their lower volumes have a common positive bound, and a fixed dominant Hermitian form bounds their upper volumes. The three-point estimate \eqref{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus} therefore gives $d_{\beta_j}(a_j,p)\to0$, with constants independent of $j$. Apply \cref{thm:analytic-approximation-curvature-error} to conclude that $p$ is an ideal envelope model and to obtain the stated corrections and convergence in the original background.

For another choice of transport, \cref{thm:two-sided-model-transport} gives comparison tending to zero between the two reference models in background $\eta_j$. The lower bound just proved and the same uniform three-point estimate transfer the convergence condition in both directions. This proves independence of the choice.
\end{proof}

\section{A complete metric on positive-volume models}
\label{sec:model-volume-metrization}

The three-point modulus controls convergence even though $d_\theta$ need not satisfy the triangle inequality. Intersecting divisors already show this on a projective surface. A complete metric can nevertheless be constructed from finite chains: lower-volume continuity controls the chain estimates locally, and the reciprocal lower volume prevents Cauchy sequences from escaping to the zero-volume boundary.

\begin{example}\label{ex:model-comparison-triangle-failure}\index{Model potential!failure of the triangle inequality}
On $\mathbf P^2$ with its Fubini--Study form $\theta$ normalized by $\int_{\mathbf P^2}\theta^2=1$, there are analytic ordinary models $p,q,r$ of positive lower volume such that
\[
 d_\theta(p,q)>d_\theta(p,r)+d_\theta(r,q).
\]
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Write $|Z|^2=|Z_0|^2+|Z_1|^2+|Z_2|^2$ in homogeneous coordinates. Fix a rational $a\in(0,1/2)$ and put
\[
 u=a\log\frac{|Z_0|^2}{|Z|^2},\qquad
 v=a\log\frac{|Z_1|^2}{|Z|^2},\qquad w=u+v.
\]
The corresponding currents have residual forms $(1-a)\theta$, $(1-a)\theta$ and $(1-2a)\theta$, respectively. The background is closed, so each singularity volume equals the non-pluripolar mass. Discarding the divisors gives
\[
 U(u)=U(v)=(1-a)^2,\qquad U(w)=(1-2a)^2.
\]
The maximum $\max(u,v)$ has the ordinary type of
\[
 z=a\log\frac{|Z_0|^2+|Z_1|^2}{|Z|^2}.
\]
Blow up the common point $[0:0:1]$. Denote the blowup by $\pi:Y\to\mathbf P^2$, its exceptional divisor by $E$, and the pencil map $[Z_0:Z_1]$ by $f:Y\to\mathbf P^1$. For the Fubini--Study form $\omega_1$ of mass one on $\mathbf P^1$, the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula gives
\[
 \pi^*\theta+\ddc(z\circ\pi)=a[E]+\beta,
 \qquad \beta=(1-a)\pi^*\theta+af^*\omega_1.
\]
Here $\beta$ is smooth and semipositive. Also
\[
 \int_Y(\pi^*\theta)^2=1,\qquad
 \int_Y\pi^*\theta\wedge f^*\omega_1=1,\qquad
 (f^*\omega_1)^2=0.
\]
The mixed integral is one because every fiber of the pencil maps to a line. By \cref{lem:bounded-residual-mass-polynomial} and change of variables off $E$,
\[
 U(\max(u,v))=\int_Y\beta^2=1-a^2.
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Since $w\leq u,v$, it follows that
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\theta(u,v)&=4a-4a^2,\\
 d_\theta(u,w)+d_\theta(w,v)&=4a-6a^2.
 \end{split}
\]
Set $p=P_\theta[u]$, $q=P_\theta[v]$ and $r=P_\theta[w]$. These models retain the analytic types by \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, and their lower volumes are at least $(1-2a)^2$. Projection invariance in \cref{prop:model-volume-separation} preserves the displayed comparisons, whose difference is $2a^2>0$.
\end{proof}

Return to the big background $\theta$ on the $n$-dimensional manifold $X$. Write $L(p)=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)$ and let
\[
 \mathcal M_+(\theta)=\bigcup_{\delta>0}\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)
\]
be the space of ordinary models of positive lower volume. We retain $U$ and $M$ from \cref{sec:model-volume-comparison}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model-lower-volume-modulus}
For $p,q\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-lower-volume-modulus}
 |L(p)-L(q)|\leq nM\min\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{2d_\theta(p,q)}\delta\right)^{1/n}\right\}.
\end{equation}
If $p_j,p\in\mathcal M_+(\theta)$ and $d_\theta(p_j,p)\to0$, then $L(p_j)\to L(p)>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $s=d_\theta(p,q)$ and $m=\max(p,q)$. If $0<s<\delta/2$, take $t=(2s/\delta)^{1/n}$. The upper-volume deficit from either $p$ or $q$ to $m$ is at most $s$, while $L(m)\geq\delta$. Thus \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} gives a potential $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with
\[
 (1-t)m+th\leq p.
\]
Volume addition and monotonicity imply $L(p)\geq(1-t)^nL(m)\geq(1-t)^nL(q)$. Interchanging $p$ and $q$ and using $L(p),L(q)\leq M$ proves the bound. If $s\geq\delta/2$, use $|L(p)-L(q)|\leq M$. If $s=0$, separation gives $p=q$.

For the last assertion, put $s_j=d_\theta(p_j,p)$. Whenever $0<s_j<L(p)/2$, the same stretching argument applied to $\max(p_j,p)$ gives
\[
 L(p_j)\geq(1-t_j)^nL(p),\qquad
 t_j=\left(\frac{2s_j}{L(p)}\right)^{1/n}.
\]
If $s_j=0$, then $p_j=p$. Hence $L(p_j)\geq L(p)/2$ for all sufficiently large $j$. Apply \eqref{eq:model-lower-volume-modulus} with this common positive bound.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:positive-model-complete-metric}\index{Model potential!complete metric}
There is a complete metric $\mathfrak d_\theta$ on $\mathcal M_+(\theta)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-model-metric-convergence}
 \mathfrak d_\theta(p_j,p)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_\theta(p_j,p)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
A sequence is $\mathfrak d_\theta$-Cauchy if and only if it is $d_\theta$-Cauchy and the sequence $1/L(p_j)$ is Cauchy in $\mathbf R$. On each $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$, the two comparisons are uniformly equivalent, and the restriction of $\mathfrak d_\theta$ is complete.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $C=1+M$ and $m(p)=L(p)/C$, so $0<m(p)<1$. Put $a_1=1$, $a_{k+1}=na_k+1$, and choose $0<\varepsilon_1<1$. Recursively choose $\varepsilon_{k+1}>0$ so small that, with $r_k=4^{a_{k+1}}\varepsilon_{k+1}$,
\[
 3\varepsilon_{k+1}<\varepsilon_k,\qquad
 3r_k+4nM\left(\frac{2r_k}{C}\right)^{1/n}<\varepsilon_k.
\]
Let $\mathcal U_0=\mathcal M_+(\theta)^2$ and, for $k\geq1$, set
\[
 \mathcal U_k=\left\{(p,q):
 \left|\frac1{m(p)}-\frac1{m(q)}\right|<\varepsilon_k,
 \quad d_\theta(p,q)<\varepsilon_k\min\{m(p),m(q)\}^{a_k}\right\}.
\]
These symmetric sets contain the diagonal, decrease with $k$, and have diagonal intersection. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-metric-triple-composition}
 \mathcal U_{k+1}\circ\mathcal U_{k+1}\circ\mathcal U_{k+1}
 \subset\mathcal U_k\qquad(k\geq0),
\end{equation}
where composition means joining successive pairs. For $k=0$ this is immediate. For $k\geq1$, suppose $(p_{i-1},p_i)\in\mathcal U_{k+1}$ for $i=1,2,3$, and put $m_* =\min_{0\leq i\leq3}m(p_i)$. The reciprocal differences along this chain have total less than $3\varepsilon_{k+1}<1$. In particular $m(p_i)\leq4m_*$ for every $i$. Each successive comparison is therefore less than
\[
 e=r_k m_*^{a_{k+1}}.
\]
All four models have lower volume at least $Cm_*$. Applying \eqref{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus} twice, with the last edge providing its error term, yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\theta(p_0,p_3)
 &\leq3e+4nM\left(\frac{2e}{Cm_*}\right)^{1/n}\\
 &\leq\left(3r_k+4nM\left(\frac{2r_k}{C}\right)^{1/n}\right)m_*^{a_k}\\
 &<\varepsilon_k\min\{m(p_0),m(p_3)\}^{a_k}.
 \end{split}
\]
Here $(a_{k+1}-1)/n=a_k$, $a_{k+1}\geq a_k$ and $m_*<1$. The endpoint reciprocal difference is less than $3\varepsilon_{k+1}<\varepsilon_k$, proving \eqref{eq:model-metric-triple-composition}.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-model-chain-metric}
 \mathfrak d_\theta(p,q)=\inf\sum_{i=1}^N2^{-k_i},
\end{equation}
where the infimum runs over finite chains $p=p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_N=q$ and integers $k_i\geq0$ with $(p_{i-1},p_i)\in\mathcal U_{k_i}$. A single edge in $\mathcal U_0$ gives $\mathfrak d_\theta\leq1$. Reversing and concatenating chains gives symmetry and the triangle inequality, while diagonal edges with arbitrarily large indices give $\mathfrak d_\theta(p,p)=0$.

We verify the chain bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-chain-entourage-bounds}
 \{\mathfrak d_\theta<2^{-(k+1)}\}
 \subset\mathcal U_k\subset\{\mathfrak d_\theta\leq2^{-k}\}
 \qquad(k\geq1).
\end{equation}
The second inclusion follows from a single edge. For the first, we prove by induction on the number of edges that every chain of cost less than $2^{-k}$ has endpoints in $\mathcal U_{k-1}$, simultaneously for all $k\geq1$. The one-edge case follows from nesting. For a chain of cost $A<2^{-k}$, choose $l\geq k$ with
\[
 2^{-(l+1)}\leq A<2^{-l}.
\]
Split the chain at the first edge at which the cumulative cost reaches $2^{-(l+1)}$. Both the preceding and the following subchains have cost less than $2^{-(l+1)}$. By induction their endpoint pairs lie in $\mathcal U_l$, with an empty subchain interpreted as a diagonal pair. Every edge cost is a power of two smaller than $2^{-l}$, so the middle edge lies in $\mathcal U_{l+1}\subset\mathcal U_l$. The triple-composition bound places the full endpoint pair in $\mathcal U_{l-1}\subset\mathcal U_{k-1}$. This proves the induction and \eqref{eq:model-chain-entourage-bounds}. Since the $\mathcal U_k$ have diagonal intersection, $\mathfrak d_\theta$ separates points.

The inclusions \eqref{eq:model-chain-entourage-bounds} identify convergence for $\mathfrak d_\theta$ with eventual membership in every $\mathcal U_k$. This implies $d_\theta$ convergence. Conversely, if $d_\theta(p_j,p)\to0$, then \cref{prop:model-lower-volume-modulus} gives $L(p_j)\to L(p)>0$. For each fixed $k$, both inequalities defining $\mathcal U_k$ hold eventually. This proves \eqref{eq:positive-model-metric-convergence}.

The same inclusions show that a $\mathfrak d_\theta$-Cauchy sequence is $d_\theta$-Cauchy and has Cauchy reciprocal lower volumes. Those reciprocals are bounded, so its tail lies in some $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$. By \cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness}, it converges there for $d_\theta$, hence for $\mathfrak d_\theta$. This proves completeness. Conversely, a $d_\theta$-Cauchy sequence with Cauchy reciprocal lower volumes has a common positive lower-volume bound on a tail. Both inequalities defining each $\mathcal U_k$ then hold uniformly on that tail, proving the stated Cauchy criterion.

On $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$, \eqref{eq:model-lower-volume-modulus} controls the reciprocal differences uniformly in terms of $d_\theta$, while $m(p)\geq\delta/C$ controls the second threshold in $\mathcal U_k$. Thus the comparisons are uniformly equivalent there. Lower-volume continuity makes $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ closed for $\mathfrak d_\theta$, so its restriction is complete.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:positive-ideal-metric-completion}
The ideal envelope models form a closed complete subspace of $(\mathcal M_+(\theta),\mathfrak d_\theta)$. Positive-volume analytic ordinary models are dense in this subspace. Equivalently, this subspace is the metric completion of the positive-volume analytic ordinary models with the restricted metric.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:analytic-model-volume-criterion}, a positive-lower-volume ordinary model limit of ideal models for $d_\theta$ is again an ideal model. The same theorem and \cref{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation} give analytic density. Apply \eqref{eq:positive-model-metric-convergence} and completeness of the ambient metric space.
\end{proof}

\section{Model potentials on curves}
\label{sec:curve-zero-volume-models}

Let $Y$ be a compact connected Riemann surface, let $\theta>0$ be smooth, and put $A=\int_Y\theta$.

The curvature carried by the pole set determines the ordinary envelope on a curve. Its atomic part determines the ideal envelope. Comparing these two measures describes both the zero-volume boundary and the failure of analytic approximation for diffuse polar singularities. Total variation retains this singularity data while controlling convergence of the model potentials in capacity.

Write $T_u=\theta+\ddc u$ and $\mu_u=\mathbf1_{\{u=-\infty\}}T_u$ for $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$. By \cref{sec:local-polar-parts}, $\mu_u=T_u^{\mathrm{pol}}$. The two singularity volumes coincide with
\[
 U(u)=\int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=A-\mu_u(Y).
\]
Use a fixed nonpositive Green kernel $G$ for $\eta=\theta/A$, as in \cref{sec:curve-trace-realization}, and write $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ for total variation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:curve-polar-model}
For a positive measure $\mu$ carried by a polar set with $\mu(Y)\leq A$, put
\[
 w_\mu(x)=\int_YG(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y),\qquad
 p_\mu=P_\theta[w_\mu].
\]
Then $p_\mu\sim w_\mu$, $\mu_{p_\mu}=\mu$ and $U(p_\mu)=A-\mu(Y)$. Every $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-polar-envelope}
 P_\theta[u]=p_{\mu_u}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mu\mapsto p_\mu$ is a bijection from these polar measures to all normalized ordinary models on $Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Green-kernel bounds give $w_\mu\in L^1(Y)$ and
\[
 T_{w_\mu}=\mu+(A-\mu(Y))\eta.
\]
In particular $w_\mu$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic and its polar curvature is $\mu$. For any $v\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-polar-domination}
 v\preceq w_\mu\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T_v\geq\mu.
\end{equation}
The forward implication follows from \cref{cor:polar-order-maximum}. Conversely, if $T_v\geq\mu$, the distribution $v-w_\mu$ has Hessian bounded below by $-(A-\mu(Y))\eta$. Its quasi-subharmonic representative is bounded above on $Y$. Almost-everywhere order between quasi-plurisubharmonic functions then gives $v\preceq w_\mu$.

Thus $p_\mu$ is the upper envelope of all $v\leq0$ with $T_v\geq\mu$. This family is closed under maxima by \eqref{eq:polar-maximum-measure}. Choquet selection and $L^1$ convergence of increasing maxima retain the current inequality $T_{p_\mu}\geq\mu$. Also $p_\mu\geq w_\mu-\sup_Yw_\mu$. Hence $b=p_\mu-w_\mu$ has a quasi-subharmonic representative bounded below, and compactness bounds it above. Therefore $p_\mu\sim w_\mu$. The positive measure
\[
 T_{p_\mu}-\mu=(A-\mu(Y))\eta+\ddc b
\]
charges no polar set because $b$ is bounded. This proves the assertions about $\mu_{p_\mu}$ and $U(p_\mu)$, and ordinary equivalence gives $P_\theta[p_\mu]=p_\mu$.

For arbitrary $u$, take $\mu=\mu_u$. Then $T_u\geq\mu$, so \eqref{eq:curve-polar-domination} gives $u\preceq p_\mu$, and $U(u)=U(p_\mu)$. If this mass is positive, \cref{cor:full-mass-envelope-characterization} gives \eqref{eq:curve-polar-envelope}. If it is zero, $\mu(Y)=A$ and $T_u=\mu=T_{w_\mu}$. Their difference is harmonic on $Y$ and hence constant, giving the same conclusion. Distinct polar measures give distinct models by the already proved identity $\mu_{p_\mu}=\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-ideal-volume-distance}\label{thm:curve-model-volume-distance}\index{Model potential!polar metric on a curve}
For every $u,v\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-model-volume-distance}
 d_\theta(u,v)=\|\mu_u-\mu_v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation}
In particular $d_\theta$ is a metric on all normalized ordinary models on $Y$. For the ideal models $q_D,q_E$ of \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-ideal-volume-distance}
 d_\theta(q_D,q_E)=\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:polar-maximum-measure}, the polar curvature of $\max(u,v)$ is $\mu_u\wedge\mu_v$. The one-dimensional volume formula therefore gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\theta(u,v)&=2U(\max(u,v))-U(u)-U(v)\\
 &=\mu_u(Y)+\mu_v(Y)-2(\mu_u\wedge\mu_v)(Y)\\
 &=\|\mu_u-\mu_v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
 \end{split}
\]
The classification in \cref{lem:curve-polar-model} proves separation on normalized ordinary models, and total variation supplies the triangle inequality. Ideal models have precisely the atomic polar measures, giving \eqref{eq:curve-ideal-volume-distance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:curve-zero-volume-ideal-completion}\label{cor:curve-model-completeness}
All normalized ordinary models on $Y$ form a complete geodesic metric space for $d_\theta$. The ideal models form a closed geodesically convex subspace, which is the metric completion of the positive-mass analytic ordinary models.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The positive polar measures of mass at most $A$ are closed in total variation. A total-variation limit retains positivity and the mass bound, and is carried by the countable union of polar carriers of the sequence. Completeness follows from that of finite signed measures. For polar measures $\mu,\nu$, the measures $\mu_t=(1-t)\mu+t\nu$ remain in this set and satisfy
\[
 \|\mu_t-\mu_s\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=|t-s|\,\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Their models give a constant-speed geodesic.

The atomic measures form a closed convex subset: a total-variation limit is carried by the countable union of the sets of atoms of the sequence. Finite restriction and rational approximation of the coefficients from below, with an arbitrarily small further decrease if needed, approximate every such measure by finite atomic measures of mass strictly less than $A$. Their models have analytic singularities by \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}. Apply \eqref{eq:curve-ideal-volume-distance}.
\end{proof}

The positive-mass part itself is incomplete for $d_\theta$. For a fixed $x\in Y$, the models of $tA\delta_x$, $t\uparrow1$, give a Cauchy family with a zero-mass limit. In contrast, the complete metric of \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric} makes this family non-Cauchy because its reciprocal lower volumes diverge. The two metrics induce the same topology on positive-mass models, but have different Cauchy sequences near the boundary.

The metric also controls convergence of the potentials at the zero-mass boundary. There the polar part exhausts the limiting curvature, so its total variation controls the full current.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence}
Let $p_j,p$ be normalized ordinary models on $Y$. If $d_\theta(p_j,p)\to0$, then $p_j\to p$ in $L^1$ and in capacity. If $U(p)=0$, then in addition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-zero-mass-current-variation}
 \|T_{p_j}-T_p\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq2d_\theta(p_j,p)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $\mu_j=\mu_{p_j}$ and $\mu=\mu_p$. By \eqref{eq:curve-model-volume-distance}, $\mu_j\to\mu$ in total variation. Thus $U(p_j)=A-\mu_j(Y)\to U(p)$. If $U(p)>0$, the models lie in a common $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ for all large $j$. Apply \cref{prop:model-volume-separation,thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}.

Suppose $U(p)=0$. Then $T_p=\mu$ and $R_j=T_{p_j}-\mu_j$ is positive of mass $A-\mu_j(Y)$. Consequently
\[
 \|T_{p_j}-T_p\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\|\mu_j-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+A-\mu_j(Y)
 \leq2\|\mu_j-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:curve-zero-mass-current-variation}. By normalized compactness, each subsequence has an $L^1$-convergent further subsequence with a supremum-zero limit $q$. The current identity gives $\ddc(q-p)=0$. Harmonic uniqueness and normalization give $q=p$, proving $L^1$ convergence of the full sequence. Apply \cref{lem:planar-variation-capacity} on finitely many nested coordinate discs. Both terms on its right tend to zero, and the local-global capacity comparison in \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity} gives the result.
\end{proof}

For normalized potentials, $T_{p_j}\rightharpoonup T_p$ is equivalent to $p_j\to p$ in $L^1$, by \cref{thm:gaud-trace-homeomorphism} and normalized compactness. It need not imply capacity convergence even when the sequence and limit are ideal models. Scaling the area-one construction in \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} gives zero-mass ideal models converging to zero in $L^1$. Capacity convergence would contradict \cref{thm:damped-continuity}, since their damped measures vanish and the limiting measure is $\theta$.

Capacity convergence in turn need not retain total variation of the polar part. Moving a single pole gives an example within the zero-mass boundary itself.

\begin{example}\label{ex:moving-pole-capacity-not-variation}
On $\mathbb P^1$ with the Fubini--Study form $\theta$ of area one, there are zero-mass ideal models $p_j,p$ with $p_j\to p$ in capacity but $d_\theta(p_j,p)=2$ for every $j$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
In the affine coordinate $z$, for $a\in\mathbb C$ put
\[
 p_a(z)=\log\frac{|z-a|^2}{(1+|z|^2)(1+|a|^2)}.
\]
This extends to $\mathbb P^1$, has supremum zero, and satisfies $T_{p_a}=\delta_a$. Hence it is the ideal model for $\delta_a$ by \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}. Choose $a_j\ne0$ tending to zero and set $p_j=p_{a_j}$, $p=p_0$. On the complement of any fixed disc about zero the convergence is uniform, including at infinity. The capacities of these discs tend to zero by \eqref{eq:global-capacity-tail} applied to $p_0$. Therefore $p_j\to p$ in capacity. Since $\|\delta_{a_j}-\delta_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=2$, \eqref{eq:curve-model-volume-distance} gives the asserted distance.
\end{proof}

On the general curve $Y$, write $\mu_u=D_u+\sigma_u$, where $D_u=\sum_{x\in Y}\nu(u,x)\delta_x$ is the atomic part and $\sigma_u$ has no atoms. Set $\tau(u)=\sigma_u(Y)=A-U(u)-D_u(Y)$, and let $\mathcal A_+(Y,\theta)$ denote the positive-mass analytic ordinary models.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect}\index{Envelope!nearest ideal model on a curve}\index{Analytic singularity!diffuse polar obstruction}
For every $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-analytic-distance-defect}
 \inf_{a\in\mathcal A_+(Y,\theta)}d_\theta(u,a)
 =d_\theta(u,P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I})=\tau(u).
\end{equation}
On normalized ordinary models, $p\mapsto P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$ is a $1$-Lipschitz retraction onto the ideal models, and gives the unique nearest ideal model. There are $a_j\in\mathcal A_+(Y,\theta)$ with $d_\theta(u,a_j)\to0$ if and only if $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The ideal envelope is $q_{D_u}$. For any atomic measure $E$ of mass at most $A$, the nonatomic measure $\sigma_u$ and the signed atomic measure $D_u-E$ are mutually singular. Thus \eqref{eq:curve-model-volume-distance} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-finite-analytic-comparison}
 d_\theta(u,q_E)=\tau(u)+\|D_u-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation}
The unique minimum among ideal models occurs at $E=D_u$. Density of the positive-mass analytic models in the ideal subspace gives \eqref{eq:curve-analytic-distance-defect}. Taking the atomic part contracts total variation, proving the Lipschitz assertion. Finally, \cref{lem:curve-polar-model} identifies $P_\theta[u]=q_{D_u}$ with $\mu_u=D_u$, equivalently $\tau(u)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-model}
There is an ordinary model $p$ on $Y$ with zero mass and trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight. It satisfies
\[
 P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}=0,\qquad
 \inf_{a\in\mathcal A_+(Y,\theta)}d_\theta(p,a)=A.
\]
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Choose a compact real interval inside a coordinate disc. Construct a binary Cantor set $K$ with $2^k$ pairwise disjoint level-$k$ intervals of length $r_k=\exp(-4^{k+1})$, each containing its two level-$(k+1)$ children. Let $\sigma$ be the probability measure assigning mass $2^{-k}$ to each level-$k$ interval. It has no atoms. Put $u(x)=A\int_YG(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)$, so $T_u=A\sigma$. On the chosen compact coordinate disc, $G(x,y)\leq\log|x-y|^2+C$ for a fixed $C$. For $x\in K$, integration over its level-$k$ interval, using $G\leq0$ on the complement, gives
\[
 u(x)\leq A2^{-k}(2\log r_k+C)\longrightarrow-\infty.
\]
Thus $u=-\infty$ on $K$, while it is smooth off $K$ by its current equation. Hence $K$ is polar, $\mu_u=A\sigma$ and $U(u)=0$. By \cref{lem:curve-polar-model}, $p=P_\theta[u]$ has the same polar curvature. Since this already has mass $A$, $T_p=T_u$ and $p=u-\sup_Yu$. Its Lelong numbers vanish, so all its multiplier ideals are trivial and its ideal envelope is zero. Finally $\tau(p)=A$, which gives the distance by \eqref{eq:curve-analytic-distance-defect}.
\end{proof}

\section{Background changes on curves}
\label{sec:curve-model-backgrounds}

Polar curvature separates the area of the background from the singularity data of a curve model. A smooth gauge reduces any positive-area background to a positive form. Keeping polar data fixed preserves distances on the common admissible range, while area rescaling identifies the entire model spaces. The gauge controls the normalized representatives. At zero area the whole space collapses to the unique normalized potential.

Keep the curve $Y$ of \cref{sec:curve-zero-volume-models} and fix a smooth positive form $\eta$ of integral one. In this section allow any smooth real background $\sigma$ with $A_\sigma=\int_Y\sigma\geq0$, and let $\mathcal M(\sigma)$ be its normalized ordinary model space. Use $\eta$ for $L^1$ norms and capacity. Put $U_\sigma(p)=\int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\sigma(p)$; at zero area the volume-comparison formula of \cref{def:model-volume-comparison} is identically zero.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}
Polar curvature identifies $\mathcal M(\sigma)$ with the positive polar measures of mass at most $A_\sigma$. Write $p_\sigma(\mu)$ for the model of $\mu$. Then
\[
 U_\sigma(p_\sigma(\mu))=A_\sigma-\mu(Y),\qquad
 d_\sigma(p_\sigma(\mu),p_\sigma(\nu))=\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\]
and ideal projection corresponds to taking the atomic part. The model space is complete and geodesic, and metric convergence implies $L^1$ and capacity convergence. If $A_\sigma=0$, its only member is the smooth potential $u_\sigma$ of \cref{lem:curve-background-area}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $A=A_\sigma>0$. By \cref{lem:curve-background-area}, write $\sigma=A\eta+\ddc h$. For $v\in\PSH(Y,A\eta)$, the ordinary-envelope inequalities of \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge} give
\[
 P_{A\eta}[v]+\min_Yh
 \leq P_\sigma[v-h]+h
 \leq P_{A\eta}[v]+\max_Yh.
\]
For a model $p$ in background $A\eta$, the potential $q=P_\sigma[p-h]$ therefore satisfies $q+h\sim p$. Ordinary-type invariance of the envelope gives $P_\sigma[q]=q$. Conversely, for $q\in\mathcal M(\sigma)$, the same bounds show that $P_{A\eta}[q+h]\sim q+h$, and taking the envelope back recovers $q$. Thus this construction is a bijection of the model spaces.

Smooth gauges preserve curvature measures, and ordinary equivalence preserves their polar parts by \cref{cor:polar-order-maximum}. The bijection consequently preserves polar curvature. It also preserves singularity volumes; subtracting the same smooth function commutes with maxima. The classification and distance formula in \cref{lem:curve-polar-model,thm:curve-model-volume-distance} now give the first assertions. The identical gauge bounds for ideal envelopes make the corresponding ideal projections ordinarily equivalent after transport. They are ordinary models by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}, so uniqueness in each ordinary type makes them equal. Thus the bijection commutes with ideal projection, which still takes the atomic part. Completeness and geodesics follow by the same identification with polar measures as in \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}.

For metric convergence to a model of positive mass, $\sigma$ is big because $\sigma+\ddc(-h)=A\eta>0$, and the converging polar masses give a common positive lower-volume bound. Apply \cref{prop:model-volume-separation,thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}. At a zero-mass limit the current estimate \eqref{eq:curve-zero-mass-current-variation} follows from the same polar decomposition. Normalized compactness and harmonic uniqueness give $L^1$ convergence; \cref{lem:planar-variation-capacity} then gives capacity convergence on a finite cover.

If $A_\sigma=0$, \cref{lem:curve-background-area} leaves only $u_\sigma$ after normalization. Its current vanishes, and both envelope operations fix it. All assertions reduce to the zero measure and the one-point metric space.
\end{proof}

The envelope-equality criterion also depends only on the polar data, including at zero area.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion}\index{Relative envelope class!arbitrary curve background}
For $u\in\PSH(Y,\sigma)$ put
\[
 q=P_\sigma[u]_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 \mu_u=\mathbf1_{\{u=-\infty\}}(\sigma+\ddc u),\qquad
 D_u=\sum_{y\in Y}\nu(u,y)\delta_y.
\]
The following are equivalent:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\sigma,q),\qquad \mu_u=D_u,\\
 P_\sigma[u]=P_\sigma[u]_{\mathcal I},\\
 P_{\sigma,F}[u]=P_{\sigma,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
       \quad\text{for every smooth real }F.
 \end{gathered}
\]
If $A_\sigma=0$, all four conditions hold for every admissible $u$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $A=A_\sigma>0$ and write $\sigma=A\eta+\ddc h$ as in \cref{lem:curve-background-area}. Put $v=u+h$ and $q_+=P_{A\eta}[v]_{\mathcal I}$. The smooth gauge preserves the curvature current and its pole set, hence both $\mu_u$ and $D_u$. By \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge}, $q+h\sim q_+$. Smooth-gauge transport and reference invariance in \cref{prop:relative-elementary} give
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\sigma,q)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,A\eta,q_+).
\]
By \cref{cor:curve-relative-envelope-criterion}, this is equivalent to $\mu_u=D_u$.

The gauge bounds for both zero-obstacle envelopes, \cref{cor:polar-order-maximum} and the positive-background classifications identify their polar curvatures with $\mu_u$ and $D_u$, respectively. Both envelopes are ordinary models, the ideal one by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}. The model classification in \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} therefore identifies their equality with $\mu_u=D_u$.

For a smooth real $F$, the competitor bijection $a\mapsto a+h$ gives exactly
\[
 P_{\sigma,F}[u]+h=P_{A\eta,F+h}[v],
\]
and the identical identity for the ideal envelopes. It preserves the obstacle bound, ordinary order and ideal equivalence; addition of $h$ commutes with upper regularization. Since $F\mapsto F+h$ is a bijection of smooth obstacles, \cref{cor:curve-relative-envelope-criterion} gives the remaining equivalence.

If $A_\sigma=0$, \cref{lem:curve-background-area} gives $u=-h+c$ and zero curvature. Thus $\mu_u=D_u=0$. All potentials in this background are smooth and ordinarily equivalent, so their ordinary and ideal competitor families below every obstacle coincide. They are nonempty after a constant translation. The ideal reference is ordinarily equivalent to $u$, and \cref{prop:relative-elementary} gives relative-envelope membership.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-background-homothety}\index{Model potential!curve background homothety}
Let $\theta_0,\theta_1$ have positive areas $A_i=A_{\theta_i}$ and put $\lambda=A_1/A_0$. The map
\[
 F_{10}:\mathcal M(\theta_0)\longrightarrow\mathcal M(\theta_1),
 \qquad F_{10}(p_{\theta_0}(\mu))=p_{\theta_1}(\lambda\mu),
\]
is a bijection and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-background-homothety}
 \begin{split}
 d_{\theta_1}(F_{10}(p),F_{10}(q))&=\lambda d_{\theta_0}(p,q),\\
 U_{\theta_1}(F_{10}(p))&=\lambda U_{\theta_0}(p),\\
 P_{\theta_1}[F_{10}(p)]_{\mathcal I}
 &=F_{10}(P_{\theta_0}[p]_{\mathcal I}).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
For a third smooth background $\theta_2$ of positive area, the same construction gives $F_{21}F_{10}=F_{20}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Multiplication by $\lambda$ is a bijection between the positive polar measures of mass at most $A_0$ and those of mass at most $A_1$. By \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}, it gives the asserted bijection, with distance
\[
 \|\lambda\mu-\lambda\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\lambda\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
The volume identity follows from $A_1-\lambda\mu(Y)=\lambda(A_0-\mu(Y))$. Taking the atomic part commutes with multiplication by $\lambda$, which gives the ideal-envelope identity. The area ratios multiply, proving the composition rule.
\end{proof}

For $A_\sigma>0$, the distances $d_\sigma/A_\sigma$ give isometric model spaces under these correspondences. The unscaled spaces have diameter $2A_\sigma$: this is an upper bound for the total variation of two positive measures of mass at most $A_\sigma$, and the models of $A_\sigma\delta_x$ and $A_\sigma\delta_y$, $x\ne y$, attain it. Consequently the full unscaled spaces can be isometric only when their areas agree.

Alternatively, $p_{\theta_0}(\mu)\mapsto p_{\theta_1}(\mu)$ is an isometry on the common range $\mu(Y)\leq\min(A_0,A_1)$. These two models have the same ordinary type. Indeed, their Green representatives have the same polar term, so their difference has smooth $\ddc$ and is smooth by elliptic regularity; \cref{lem:curve-polar-model,prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} identify the model with its Green representative, corrected by a smooth gauge, up to a bounded function. Under this correspondence the non-pluripolar volume changes by $A_1-A_0$.

The area-rescaling map preserves analytic models when $\lambda$ is rational, by the convention in \cref{sec:analytic-types}: on a curve their polar measures have finite support and rational coefficients. If $\lambda$ is irrational, an atom $a\delta_x$ with rational $0<a<A_0$ acquires an irrational Lelong number, so its image is not analytic in this convention. Ideal models are preserved for every $\lambda$ by \eqref{eq:curve-background-homothety}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve-background-obstacle-bound}
Choose a smooth real $h$ with $\theta_1=\lambda\theta_0+\ddc h$. For $p\in\mathcal M(\theta_0)$ and $q=F_{10}(p)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-background-obstacle-bound}
 \begin{gathered}
 q=P_{\theta_1}[\lambda p-h],\\
 \lambda p+\min_Yh\leq q+h\leq\lambda p+\max_Yh.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
If $U_{\theta_0}(p)=0$, then
\[
 q=\lambda p-h-\sup_Y(\lambda p-h).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The form $\theta_1-\lambda\theta_0$ has integral zero, so the scalar Poisson equation supplies $h$, as in \cref{lem:curve-background-area}. Write $p=p_{\theta_0}(\mu)$. Scaling the competitors in the ordinary envelope shows that $\lambda p$ is a normalized ordinary model for $\lambda\theta_0$. The smooth-gauge construction in \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} therefore makes $P_{\theta_1}[\lambda p-h]$ a model. The current of $\lambda p-h$ is $\lambda(\theta_0+\ddc p)$, so its polar measure is $\lambda\mu$. The construction preserves this measure, giving the first identity in \eqref{eq:curve-background-obstacle-bound}.

The function $\lambda p-h+\min_Yh$ is a competitor for $q$, giving the lower bound. If $v\leq0$ is any competitor, then $v+h-\max_Yh$ is $\lambda\theta_0$-plurisubharmonic, nonpositive and more singular than $\lambda p$. It is therefore at most $\lambda p$. Taking the upper envelope gives the upper bound. The inequalities hold at the poles as well and, off their common pole set, imply $|q-\lambda p|\leq\operatorname{osc}_Yh$.

At zero mass, the polar measures exhaust the currents of both $p$ and $q$. Hence $\ddc(q-\lambda p+h)=0$. Harmonic uniqueness and the supremum-zero normalization give the last formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:curve-varying-background-continuity}
Suppose smooth real forms $\theta_j$ of positive area converge uniformly to a smooth form $\theta_0$ of positive area. Put $\lambda_j=A_{\theta_j}/A_{\theta_0}$ and write $F_{j0}$ for the preceding correspondence. For every $t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-background-uniform-continuity}
 \begin{split}
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal M(\theta_0)}
       \|F_{j0}(p)-p\|_{L^1(Y)}&\longrightarrow0,\\
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal M(\theta_0)}
       \operatorname{Cap}_\eta(\{|F_{j0}(p)-p|>t\})&\longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
More generally, let $q_j\in\mathcal M(\theta_j)$ have polar measures $\nu_j$, and let $p=p_{\theta_0}(\mu)$. If $\lambda_j^{-1}\nu_j\to\mu$ in total variation, then $q_j\to p$ in $L^1$ and in capacity.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The mean-zero inverse of the self-adjoint scalar Laplacian for $\eta$ has a symmetric Green kernel $G$. Set
\[
 h_j(x)=\int_YG(x,y)\,(\theta_j-\lambda_j\theta_0)(y).
\]
The integrand form has integral zero, so $\ddc h_j=\theta_j-\lambda_j\theta_0$, and elliptic regularity makes $h_j$ smooth. Symmetry and the uniform $L^1$ bound from \cref{sec:curve-trace-realization} give
\[
 \|h_j\|_{L^\infty(Y)}
 \leq C\left\|\frac{\theta_j-\lambda_j\theta_0}{\eta}\right\|_{L^\infty(Y)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Here $C$ depends only on $\eta$. The symmetric kernel may be made nonpositive by subtracting a constant, which does not change $h_j$.

By \eqref{eq:curve-background-obstacle-bound}, outside the common poles,
\[
 |F_{j0}(p)-p|\leq\operatorname{osc}_Yh_j+|\lambda_j-1|\,|p|.
\]
Normalized compactness bounds $\|p\|_{L^1}$ uniformly over $\mathcal M(\theta_0)$, proving the first limit. Choose $C_0>0$ with $\theta_0\leq C_0\eta$. Then $p/C_0$ is normalized and $\eta$-plurisubharmonic, so the uniform sublevel estimate \eqref{eq:global-capacity-tail} proves the second limit. Pole sets have zero capacity.

For the last assertion put $p_j=F_{0j}(q_j)$. Its polar measure is $\lambda_j^{-1}\nu_j$, so \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} gives $p_j\to p$ in $L^1$ and capacity. The two uniform limits in \eqref{eq:curve-background-uniform-continuity}, applied to $p_j$, give $q_j-p_j\to0$ in the same senses. The triangle inequality for $L^1$ and subadditivity of capacity finish the proof.
\end{proof}

Finally suppose $\theta_j\to\theta_0$ uniformly, where $A_{\theta_j}\geq0$ and $A_{\theta_0}=0$. The limiting model space is $\{u_{\theta_0}\}$, and \cref{thm:curve-zero-area-collapse} gives uniform $L^1$ and capacity convergence to this point for all normalized potentials, hence for all models. The model spaces also collapse for their unscaled metrics: their diameters are $2A_{\theta_j}\to0$. At positive area, dividing the metric by the area instead retains the same polar-measure space as $\mathcal M(\eta)$ under \cref{thm:curve-background-homothety}. Thus the normalized metric retains singularity data that disappear in the limiting potentials.
