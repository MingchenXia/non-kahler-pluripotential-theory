\chapter{Volumes under bounded mass}
\label{chap:mass-bound-volumes}

For a nonclosed background, a potential's Monge--Ampère mass can vary within its ordinary singularity type. Upper and lower singularity volumes retain the comparisons needed for envelopes. Under bounded mass, upper-volume deficits control stretching, while a positive lower volume prevents collapse. This links relative full mass to ordinary envelopes and gives continuity along monotone families. On normalized models with a common positive lower-volume bound, comparison through maxima and rooftops yields Cauchy completeness and capacity convergence. Analytic regularization then characterizes the ideal envelope models among these limits.

Assume throughout this chapter that $X$ has bounded mass, and fix a big smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$. We use the volumes of \cref{def:singularity-volumes}.

\section{Comparison of singularity volumes}
\label{sec:bounded-mass-volume-comparison}

Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta$. The estimates in \cref{prop:bmp-finite,thm:arbitrary-extended-ma} bound the mass of every $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential by $v_+(\Omega)$. Hence, for $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 0\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)<+\infty.
\]
If $\theta$ is closed, both volumes equal $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)$, by invariance of non-pluripolar mass under equivalent singularities in the closed theory \cite{SGPT}. For a non-closed background this equality can fail even at $u=0$, as \cref{ex:torus-mass} shows.

Volume increases when the background increases or the singularities decrease. The rooftop and maximum constructions preserve the required ordinary types; locality confines their errors to sets with vanishing mass.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:volume-monotonicity}
Let $\theta_1,\theta_2$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. Suppose $\theta_1$ is big, $\theta_1\leq\theta_2$, and $u_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i)$ satisfy $u_1\preceq u_2$. Then
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
 \leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Shift $u_1$ so that $u_1\leq u_2$. Choose $v_2\simeq u_2$ in $\PSH(X,\theta_2)$ and, for real $t>0$, set
\[
 p_t=P_{\theta_1}(u_1+t,v_2).
\]
For $t>0$, a sufficiently low constant translate of $u_1$ is a competitor; also $p_t\leq u_1+t$. Thus $p_t\simeq u_1$. The contact theorem, applied in each background on the corresponding contact part, and positivity of the mixed products give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(p_t)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{u_1\leq v_2-t\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
      +\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_2}(v_2).
\]
Indeed $p_t$ is also $\theta_2$-plurisubharmonic and is below $v_2$, so the contact inequality in that background applies. The first measure on the right has mass tending to zero: its indicators tend to zero off the pole sets, and the measure is finite and non-pluripolar. Integrate, let $t\to+\infty$, and then take the infimum over $v_2$.

For the upper volume, choose any $v_1\simeq u_1$ in $\PSH(X,\theta_1)$ and put $w_t=\max(v_1,u_2-t)$. It is $\theta_2$-plurisubharmonic and $w_t\simeq u_2$. On $D_t=\{v_1>u_2-t\}$, locality and mixed-product positivity imply
\[
 \mathbf1_{D_t}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(v_1)
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_t}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_2}(w_t).
\]
The sets $D_t$ exhaust the complement of a pluripolar set. Hence integration and passage to the limit give
$\int_X\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(v_1)\leq
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2)$.
Take the supremum over $v_1$.
\end{proof}

Adding a positive current also increases both volumes. For the lower volume, subtract its potential inside an envelope to recover a competitor in the original background.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:volume-addition}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $v\in\PSH(X,\eta)$ for a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ on $X$. Then, for either the lower or the upper volume,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_{\theta+\eta}(u+v).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The form $\theta+\eta$ is big, by adding the positive current $\eta+\ddc v$ to a strictly positive current in the class of $\theta$. For the upper volume, add $v$ to each competitor for $u$. Positivity of mixed products shows that the resulting mass is at least the original mass.

For the lower volume, choose $w\simeq u+v$ in $\PSH(X,\theta+\eta)$, and let $p=P_\theta(w-v)$. Since $w-v=u+O(1)$ outside the pole sets, the envelope is genuine and $p\simeq u$. The contact theorem says that its measure is carried by $D=\{p+v=w\}$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Furthermore $p+v\leq w$ everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. Thus
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_{\theta+\eta}(p+v)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_{\theta+\eta}(w).
\]
Integrating and taking the infimum over $w$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Volume deficits and relative full mass}

The upper-volume deficit measures how far one potential can be stretched away from another. The positive lower volume of the less singular potential prevents the stretching envelopes from collapsing. We use $Q_b$ and its competitor family from \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:volume-deficit-stretch}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $u\preceq v$, and put
\[
 \delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(v)>0,
 \qquad D=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(v)
             -\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
If $b>1$ and $b^nD<\delta$, then $Q_b(u,v)$ is genuine.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adding constants to $u$ and $v$ only translates the stretching envelope. Normalize $v\leq0$, and write $V=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(v)$. Since $b^nD<\delta$, choose $w\simeq u$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-volume-deficit}
 V-\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)<b^{-n}\delta.
\end{equation}
Translate $w$ so that $w\leq v$. For positive integers $j$, put
\[
 w_j=\max(w,v-j),\qquad h_j=Q_b(w_j,v).
\]
The competitor $v-bj$ and the defining inequality give $v-bj\leq h_j\leq v$. Thus $w_j,h_j\simeq v$, and $h_j$ decreases. If its limit were $-\infty$, \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} would give $\sup_Xh_j\to-\infty$.

By \cref{thm:big-general-envelope-contact}, the measure of $h_j$ is carried by $C_j=\{b^{-1}h_j+(1-b^{-1})v=w_j\}$. Ordered contact and positivity of mixed products give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(h_j)
       \leq b^n\mathbf1_{C_j}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_j).
\]
Fix a positive integer $k$. For large $j>k$, collapse would imply $h_j\leq-bk$ everywhere. Since $v\leq0$, contact then implies $w_j\leq-k$, hence $w\leq-k$. Locality on $\{w>-k\}\subset\{w>v-j\}$ gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \delta\leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(h_j)
 &\leq b^n\int_{\{w\leq-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_j)\\
 &\leq b^n\left(V-\int_{\{w>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)\right).
 \end{split}
\]
Here the first inequality uses $h_j\simeq v$, and the last uses $w_j\simeq v$. Letting $k\to\infty$ contradicts \eqref{eq:relative-volume-deficit}, since the finite non-pluripolar measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)$ gives zero mass to the pole set. The limit $h$ is therefore genuine and satisfies $b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})v\leq w$. If $w\leq u+C$, then $h-bC\in\mathcal A_b(u,v)$, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:volume-deficit-lelong}
Fix a smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to X$. There is a finite constant $C_\pi$, depending only on $\pi$ and $\theta$, such that, for $u,v,\delta,D$ as in \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch},
\[
 0\leq\nu(u\circ\pi,z)-\nu(v\circ\pi,z)
 \leq C_\pi\min\left\{1,\left(\frac D\delta\right)^{1/n}\right\}
 \qquad(z\in Z).
\]
The same estimate holds for generic Lelong numbers along every prime divisor on $Z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:gauduchon-uniform-lelong} to the smooth background $\pi^*\theta$ on $Z$, and choose $C_\pi$ bounding all its Lelong numbers. Order gives the lower bound and the upper bound $C_\pi$, which proves the assertion when $D\geq\delta$.

If $D<\delta$, every $b>1$ with $b^nD<\delta$ admits $h\in\mathcal A_b(u,v)$ by the lemma. Pulling back the defining convex inequality and taking Lelong numbers gives
\[
 \nu(u\circ\pi,z)-\nu(v\circ\pi,z)
 \leq b^{-1}\bigl(\nu(h\circ\pi,z)-\nu(v\circ\pi,z)\bigr)
 \leq b^{-1}C_\pi.
\]
For $D>0$, let $b\uparrow(\delta/D)^{1/n}$; for $D=0$, let $b\to\infty$. Taking generic values proves the divisorial assertion.
\end{proof}

At zero deficit, every finite stretch is possible. Fix a reference $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:relative-volume-class}
Define
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\preceq\varphi,
 \ \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
   =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)\}.
\]
\end{definition}

Convex comparison turns the envelope condition back into equality of volumes.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-equivalence}
Suppose $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0$. Then
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi).
\]
Every member also satisfies
$\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First assume the envelope condition. For each $a\in(0,1)$ there is $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ such that $a\varphi+(1-a)h\leq u$. By \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity,prop:volume-addition} and homogeneity, for either volume,
\[
 a^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(a\varphi+(1-a)h)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\varphi).
\]
Let $a\to1$. This proves equality of both volumes in this direction, even without positive lower volume.

Conversely, equality of the upper volumes makes $D=0$ in \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch}, with $v=\varphi$. Every $Q_b(u,\varphi)$ is genuine, which is the envelope condition.
\end{proof}

\section{Domination and ordinary envelopes}

Positive lower volume prevents comparison envelopes from collapsing. This gives a domination principle and identifies the limit of relative obstacle envelopes, which in turn compares a potential's volumes with those of its ordinary envelope. Write $\mu_w=\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:big-relative-domination}
Let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have positive lower volume, let $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$, $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $v\preceq\varphi$, and $0\leq c<1$. If
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mu_u\leq c\,\mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mu_v,
\]
then $u\geq v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $\ell=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0$ and $\mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$. Replace $v$ by $\max(u,v)$. Locality preserves the hypothesis and upward stability puts the new $v$ in $\mathcal E_\varphi$. Translate $u,v$ by the same constant so that $v\leq\varphi$, and then translate all three potentials so that $\varphi\leq0$. Set
\[
 q_b=Q_b(u,v),\qquad
 D_b=\{b^{-1}q_b+(1-b^{-1})v=u\}.
\]
\Cref{lem:relative-stretch-closure} gives $q_b\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $q_b\leq u$. Contact support and positivity of the mixed products give
\[
 b^{-n}\mu_{q_b}+(1-b^{-1})^n\mathbf1_{D_b}\mu_v
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_b}\mu_u.
\]
Here $\mu_{q_b}$ is supported on $D_b$. Choose $b$ so large that $(1-b^{-1})^n>c$. Subtracting finite measures on $D_b\cap\{u<v\}$ shows that $\mu_{q_b}$ vanishes there. Thus it is supported on $D_b\cap\{u=v\}\subset\{q_b=u\}$. Since $q_b\leq u$, ordered contact with coefficient one and \cref{thm:relative-equivalence} now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-domination-fixed-tail}
 \ell\leq\mu_{q_b}(X)\leq\mu_u(\{q_b=u\}).
\end{equation}

If $u<v$ anywhere, there is $\delta>0$ such that $\{u<v-\delta\}$ has positive smooth volume. Indeed, the contrary would give $u\geq v$ almost everywhere and then everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. On this set
$q_b\leq u-(b-1)\delta$. The family $q_b$ decreases in $b$ and is bounded above by $u$. A genuine limit could not be $-\infty$ on a set of positive smooth volume. Hence its limit is identically $-\infty$, and \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives $\sup_Xq_b\to-\infty$. The sets $\{q_b=u\}$ then have $\mu_u$-mass tending to zero by dominated convergence off the pole set of $u$. This contradicts \eqref{eq:big-domination-fixed-tail}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:big-relative-orders}
For $u,v$ as in \cref{thm:big-relative-domination} and $\lambda>0$,
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}\mu_u\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda v}\mu_v
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\geq v.
\]
If $\mu_u\leq c\mu_v$ on $X$ for a finite $c\geq0$, then $c\geq1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On $\{u<v-\delta\}$ the weighted inequality gives $\mu_u\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\delta}\mu_v$. Apply strict domination to $v-\delta$ and let $\delta\downarrow0$. If the second assertion failed, strict domination against every translate $v+A$ would contradict finiteness of $u,v$ at a common point.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-obstacle-contact}
Let $u,w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $P_\theta(u,w)$ genuine, and put
\[
 P_\theta[u](w)=\left(\lim_{C\to\infty}P_\theta(u+C,w)\right)^*.
\]
Then
\[
 \mu_{P_\theta[u](w)}
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{P_\theta[u](w)=w\}}\mu_w.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $p_C=P_\theta(u+C,w)$ for $C\geq0$ and $p=P_\theta[u](w)$. The envelopes increase, are bounded below by $p_0$ and above by $w$, and converge in capacity by \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}. Assigning ties in the contact set to $w$ gives
\[
 \mu_{p_C}\leq\mathbf1_{\{p_C=u+C<w\}}\mu_u
                    +\mathbf1_{\{p_C=w\}}\mu_w.
\]
The first measure has total mass at most
\[
 \varepsilon_C=\mu_u(\{u\leq\sup_Xw-C\})\longrightarrow0,
\]
because $\mu_u$ is finite and non-pluripolar. Testing against nonnegative continuous functions and applying \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} gives $\mu_p\leq\mu_w$.

The bounded quasi-continuous weight $\chi=\min(1,(w-p)_+)$, defined arbitrarily on the polar indeterminacy set, vanishes on $\{p_C=w\}$, since $p_C\leq p\leq w$. Thus $\int_X\chi\,\mu_{p_C}\leq\varepsilon_C$. Weighted lower semicontinuity gives $\int_X\chi\,\mu_p=0$, so $\mu_p$ is carried by $\{p=w\}$. This proves the asserted restriction of $\mu_w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $p=P_\theta[u]$,
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
       =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p).
\]
If this volume is positive, then also
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
       =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p),\qquad
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,p),\qquad
 P_\theta[p]=p.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)=0$, lower-volume equality follows from monotonicity. Suppose henceforth that it is positive. Normalize $u\leq0$ and choose any $w\simeq p$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$. Fix $K\geq0$ with $p-K\leq w\leq p+K$. Since $u\leq p$, the rooftop $p_C=P_\theta(u+C,w)$ satisfies $u-K\leq p_C\leq u+C$ for each $C\geq0$. In particular it is genuine and equivalent to $u$. Its increasing canonical limit $r=P_\theta[u](w)$ satisfies
\[
 P_\theta(u+C+K,0)-K\leq p_C\leq w,
 \qquad p-K\leq r\leq w\leq p+K.
\]
Indeed, the leftmost potential lies below $u+C$ and below $p-K\leq w$, so is a rooftop competitor. Thus $r\simeq p$, and \cref{prop:relative-elementary} puts $r$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,p)$.

By \cref{lem:relative-obstacle-contact}, $\mu_r$ vanishes on $\{r<w\}$. \Cref{thm:big-relative-domination}, with coefficient zero and reference $p$, gives $r\geq w$; hence $r=w$. Capacity convergence and lower semicontinuity now yield
\[
 \int_X\mu_w\leq\liminf_{C\to\infty}\int_X\mu_{p_C}
       \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
Taking the supremum over $w\simeq p$ proves the upper-volume inequality in the required direction; the reverse inequality is monotonicity. \Cref{thm:relative-equivalence} then gives both relative-class membership and lower-volume equality. Finally \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality} gives $P_\theta[p]=P_\theta[u]=p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:full-mass-envelope-characterization}
If $\varphi=P_\theta[\varphi]$ has positive lower volume, then
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):P_\theta[u]=\varphi\}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first equality is \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}. Membership implies envelope equality by \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality}; the converse follows from \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}.
\end{proof}

\section{Strict barriers and background variation}
\label{sec:positive-volume-barriers}

Positive lower volume permits a small stretch away from any less singular potential, with a scale controlled by a common mass bound. Mixing the stretch with a strict potential gives a strict potential lying below the original one. The same mixed-mass bound controls the lower-volume error under a smooth background perturbation. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive-volume-stretch}
Let $u,r\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, with $u\leq r$, and assume
$\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)>0$. For a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta$, set
\[
 b_0=1+\frac{\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)}
                   {2^{n+1}n\,v_+(\Omega)}.
\]
Then $Q_b(u,r)$ is genuine for $1\leq b\leq b_0$, and
\[
 \lim_{b\downarrow1}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(Q_b(u,r))
 =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
\end{lemma}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Put $K=v_+(\Omega)$, so that $1<b_0\leq2$, and translate so that $u\leq r\leq0$. Suppose $Q_b(u,r)$ is not genuine for some $1<b\leq b_0$. Set
\[
 u_j=\max(u,r-j),\qquad v_j=u+(b-1)u_j,
 \qquad q_j=P_\theta(v_j-(b-1)r).
\]
Differences at common poles are interpreted through the corresponding convex inequality. Off the poles the obstacle lies between $u-(b-1)j$ and $u$. Hence $q_j$ is genuine and $q_j\simeq u$. As $j$ increases it decreases. A genuine limit would be a competitor for $Q_b(u,r)$, so $\sup_Xq_j\to-\infty$.

Let $C_j=\{q_j+(b-1)r=v_j\}$. The contact theorem puts $\mu_{q_j}$ on $C_j$. The positive current with potential $q_j+(b-1)r$ in background $b\theta$ contains the current with potential $q_j$ in background $\theta$. Ordered contact in background $b\theta$ therefore gives
\[
 \mu_{q_j}\leq\mathbf1_{C_j}\bigl((\theta+\ddc u)+(b-1)(\theta+\ddc u_j)\bigr)^n.
\]
On $C_j$ we have $q_j=u+(b-1)u_j-(b-1)r\geq bu$. For any $k>0$ and then all sufficiently large $j$, $q_j\leq-bk$ everywhere, and consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-volume-stretch-tail}
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mu_{q_j}(X)
 \leq\int_{\{u\leq-k\}}\bigl((\theta+\ddc u)+(b-1)(\theta+\ddc u_j)\bigr)^n.
\end{equation}

For any $s,t\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $0\leq q\leq n$, positivity of the non-pluripolar mixed products and \cref{prop:bmp-finite} imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\binom nq\int_X(\theta+\ddc s)^{n-q}\wedge(\theta+\ddc t)^q\\
 &\quad\leq\binom nq\int_X(\Omega+\ddc s)^{n-q}\wedge(\Omega+\ddc t)^q\\
 &\quad\leq2^n\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\Omega((s+t)/2)\leq2^nK.
 \end{aligned}
\]
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Here the estimate for the possibly unbounded average follows by constant truncation in the positive background $\Omega$. All products in the display are positive. Expanding the last integrand in \eqref{eq:positive-volume-stretch-tail}, and using $(b-1)^q\leq b-1$ for $q\geq1$, gives
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mu_u(\{u\leq-k\})+n2^nK(b-1).
\]
The measure $\mu_u$ is finite and ignores its pole set. Let $k\to\infty$. The remaining error is at most half of $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ by the choice of $b_0$, a contradiction. Thus every stated stretch is genuine; at $b=1$ the envelope is $u$.

Put $h_0=Q_{b_0}(u,r)$ and, for $1<b\leq b_0$, $t=(b-1)/(b_0-1)$. The defining inequalities show that
\[
 (1-t)u+th_0\leq Q_b(u,r)\leq u.
\]
For $b<b_0$, addition, homogeneity and volume monotonicity give
\[
 (1-t)^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(Q_b(u,r))
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
Letting $b\downarrow1$ proves the limit. Undoing the common translation changes neither ordinary-type volume nor genuineness.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:positive-volume-strict-barrier}
If $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has positive lower volume, then there are $w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $\varepsilon>0$ with
\[
 w\leq u,\qquad \theta+\ddc w\geq\varepsilon\omega.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Translate $u$ so that $u\leq V_\theta$. Choose a strict potential $\rho\leq V_\theta$ with $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\delta\omega$, using bigness. For a genuine $h=Q_b(u,V_\theta)$ with $b>1$, put
\[
 w=b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})\rho.
\]
Its defining convex inequality gives $w\leq u$, and its current is at least $(1-b^{-1})\delta\omega$. Restore the translation of $u$ by the same translation of $w$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-volume-smaller-background}
Assume also that $\theta\geq0$. If $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and
$\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)>0$, then
$P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)$ is genuine for some $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:positive-volume-strict-barrier}, choose $w\leq u$ and $\delta>0$ with $\theta+\ddc w\geq\delta\omega$. Choose $C>0$ with $\theta\leq C\omega$, and $0<t<\min(1,\delta/C)$. Then
\[
 (1-t)\theta+\ddc w\geq(\delta-tC)\omega\geq0.
\]
Thus $w$ is a competitor for $P_{(1-t)\theta}(u)$.
\end{proof}

For model references, the positive-volume condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:li-model-condition}
 P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi,\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0.
\end{equation}
When $\theta\geq0$, the proposition turns this into the smaller-background condition \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}. If also $v_-(\theta)>0$, the converse follows from \cref{lem:smaller-background-mass-lower}: every potential equivalent to $\varphi$ admits a smaller-background competitor with the same $\varepsilon$, and hence
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)
 \geq(1-\varepsilon)^n v_-(\theta)>0.
\]
Without this additional lower bound, the smaller-background argument still gives individual positivity, as proved in \cref{sec:relative-envelope-room}.

A smooth perturbation changes the lower volume by at most a linear error. A strict lower potential gives a multiplicative comparison for both volumes.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison}
There is $C>0$, depending only on $\theta$ and $\omega$, such that, for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ with $\theta\leq\eta\leq\theta+\varepsilon\omega$, $0\leq\varepsilon\leq1$,
\[
 0\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\eta(u)
       -\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\leq C\varepsilon.
\]
If $u$ has positive lower volume, there is also $\delta>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon\geq0$ and $\theta\leq\eta\leq\theta+\varepsilon\omega$, either volume satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\eta(u)
 \leq(1+\varepsilon/\delta)^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u).
\]
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta,\omega$ and set $C=n2^n v_+(\Omega)$. For every $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $1\leq k\leq n$, positivity and the mixed-mass estimate in the proof of \cref{lem:positive-volume-stretch}, applied in background $\Omega$ to $v$ and zero, give
\[
 \binom nk\int_X(\theta+\ddc v)^{n-k}\wedge\omega^k
 \leq2^n v_+(\Omega).
\]
Expanding in the smooth positive perturbation and using $\varepsilon^k\leq\varepsilon$ yields
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\eta(v)
 \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}(v)
 \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)+C\varepsilon.
\]
Take the infimum over $v\simeq u$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$. Each such $v$ is also an admissible competitor in background $\eta$, proving the upper bound for the lower volume. The lower bound is background monotonicity.

Now assume $u$ has positive lower volume. Choose $w\leq u$ with $\theta+\ddc w\geq\delta\omega$ by \cref{cor:positive-volume-strict-barrier}. The case $\varepsilon=0$ is immediate. For $t=\varepsilon/\delta>0$, the potential $tw$ belongs to $\PSH(X,t(\theta-\delta\omega))$. Addition and monotonicity give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{vol}_\eta(u)
 &\leq\operatorname{vol}_{\eta+t(\theta-\delta\omega)}(u+tw)\\
 &\leq\operatorname{vol}_{(1+t)\theta}((1+t)u)
 =(1+t)^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u).
 \end{split}
\]
Here $\eta+t(\theta-\delta\omega)\leq(1+t)\theta$ and $u+tw\leq(1+t)u$. The lower bound is background monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\section{Monotone limits}
\label{sec:mass-bound-monotone-limits}

For an increasing sequence, contact support identifies the limiting ordinary envelope before its volume is known. The upper-volume deficit then controls the lower volumes. For decreasing models, a uniform small stretch prevents a positive lower-volume bound from being lost at the limit. Normalized compactness preserves the convex inequalities supplied by the deficit lemma, while strict barriers reduce decreasing backgrounds to a fixed one.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:increasing-envelope-volume-continuity}
Suppose $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ increases and $u=(\lim_j u_j)^*\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has positive lower volume. Then
\[
 \left(\lim_jP_\theta[u_j]\right)^*=P_\theta[u],
 \qquad P_\theta[u_j]\longrightarrow P_\theta[u]
       \quad\text{in capacity},
\]
and
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u_j)\nearrow
       \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u_j)\nearrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
For every fixed smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to X$,
\[
 \sup_{z\in Z}\bigl|\nu(u_j\circ\pi,z)-\nu(u\circ\pi,z)\bigr|
       \longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Translate so that $u\leq0$, and set $p_j=P_\theta[u_j]$, $p=P_\theta[u]$ and $s=(\lim_jp_j)^*$. Then $u\leq s\leq p\leq V_\theta$. By \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, the lower volume of $p$ is positive and $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,p)$. Upward stability puts $s$ in the same class.

The ordinary envelope can use $V_\theta$ as obstacle, by \cref{def:singularity-envelope}. Hence \cref{lem:relative-obstacle-contact} gives
\[
 \mu_{p_j}\leq\mathbf1_{\{p_j=V_\theta\}}\mu_{V_\theta}.
\]
The fixed bounded quasi-continuous weight $\chi=\min(1,(p-s)_+)$, defined arbitrarily on the polar indeterminacy set, vanishes on $\{p_j=V_\theta\}$. Since $p_j\to s$ in capacity, \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} gives $\int_X\chi\,\mu_s=0$. Thus $\mu_s$ vanishes on $\{s<p\}$. Strict domination with reference $p$ and coefficient zero yields $s=p$.

Choose any $w\simeq p$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $K\geq0$ with $p-K\leq w\leq p+K$. Define $r_j=P_\theta[u_j](w)$. The initial rooftop is genuine, since $u_j-K\leq w$. For $C\geq0$ the competitor comparison gives
\[
 P_\theta(u_j+C+K,0)-K
       \leq P_\theta(u_j+C,w)\leq p_j+K.
\]
The leftmost potential lies below $u_j+C$ and below $p_j-K\leq p-K\leq w$. For the upper bound, subtract $K$ from the middle term: it lies below zero and is more singular than $u_j$. Taking the increasing limit in $C$ gives
\[
 p_j-K\leq r_j\leq p_j+K,\qquad r_j\leq w.
\]
The $r_j$ increase to $r=(\lim_jr_j)^*$ in capacity, and $p-K\leq r\leq w\leq p+K$. Thus $r\simeq p$. The contact lemma gives $\mu_{r_j}\leq\mathbf1_{\{r_j=w\}}\mu_w$. Applying weighted lower semicontinuity with $\min(1,(w-r)_+)$ shows that $\mu_r$ vanishes on $\{r<w\}$. Domination with reference $p$ gives $r=w$. Therefore
\[
 \int_X\mu_w\leq\liminf_j\int_X\mu_{r_j}
       \leq\lim_j\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j),
\]
where $r_j\simeq p_j$ justifies the last inequality. Taking the supremum over $w\simeq p$ and using volume monotonicity proves
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)\nearrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p).
\]

Put $\delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)>0$ and $D_j=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)-\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)$. Since $D_j\to0$, \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} supplies $a_j\to1$, $0<a_j<1$, and $h_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ such that
\[
 a_jp+(1-a_j)h_j\leq p_j
\]
for all sufficiently large $j$. Addition, homogeneity and monotonicity of the lower volume, together with \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, give
\[
 a_j^n\delta\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)
       =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u_j)\leq\delta.
\]
This proves lower-volume convergence and positivity for all large $j$. The same envelope theorem then identifies the upper volumes of $u_j$ and $p_j$, giving the asserted upper-volume limit. Finally \cref{prop:volume-deficit-lelong}, applied to $u_j\leq u$, gives the uniform Lelong-number convergence on each fixed model.
\end{proof}

For the decreasing results, let $\theta_j$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms with $\theta\leq\theta_{j+1}\leq\theta_j$ and $\theta_j\to\theta$ uniformly. The constant sequence is allowed. Normalizing the auxiliary potentials first proves continuity in a fixed background; corrected tail envelopes then handle the varying forms.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}
Suppose $q_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ satisfies $q_j=P_{\theta_j}[q_j]$ and decreases to $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Then $P_\theta[q]=q$ and
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)\searrow
       \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q).
\]
If $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0$, then also
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)\searrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q),
\]
and, for every fixed smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to X$,
\[
 \sup_{z\in Z}\bigl|\nu(q_j\circ\pi,z)-\nu(q\circ\pi,z)\bigr|
       \longrightarrow0.
\]
In this case all generic divisorial Lelong numbers converge.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $q\leq0$, background and obstacle monotonicity give $q\leq P_\theta[q]\leq P_{\theta_j}[q]\leq q_j$. Letting $j\to\infty$ proves the fixed-point assertion. Set $\ell=\lim_j\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)$. Monotonicity gives $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)\leq\ell$. If $\ell=0$, the lower-volume assertion follows.

Suppose $\ell>0$. Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta_1$, put $K=v_+(\Omega)$, and choose
\[
 b=1+\frac{\ell}{2^{n+1}nK},\qquad a=1-b^{-1}.
\]
The uniform scale in \cref{lem:positive-volume-stretch} makes $Q_b(q_j,V_{\theta_j})$ genuine for every $j$. Put $h_j=P_{\theta_j}[Q_b(q_j,V_{\theta_j})]$. For each normalized competitor $s$ for this envelope, $aV_{\theta_j}+(1-a)s$ lies below zero and is more singular than $q_j$. Since $q_j=P_{\theta_j}[q_j]$, taking the envelope gives
\[
 aV_{\theta_j}+(1-a)h_j\leq q_j.
\]
The functions $h_j$ have supremum zero and a common curvature lower bound $-\Omega$. Normalized compactness and uniform background convergence give a subsequential $L^1$ limit $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Also $V_{\theta_j}\searrow V_\theta$: their limit is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic and lies between $V_\theta$ and zero. Passing to the limit in the convex inequality and using almost-everywhere order gives
\[
 aV_\theta+(1-a)h\leq q.
\]
Moreover, $v_-(\Omega)\geq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_1}(q_1)\geq\ell>0$ by volume monotonicity. Choose $\rho\leq V_\theta$ with $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\kappa\Omega$, $\kappa>0$. Addition applied to $0\in\PSH(X,\kappa\Omega)$ and $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta-\kappa\Omega)$ gives $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\rho)\geq\kappa^n v_-(\Omega)$. Monotonicity and addition in the displayed convex combination now yield
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)
 \geq a^n\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(V_\theta)
 \geq(a\kappa)^n v_-(\Omega)>0.
\]
Thus it remains to prove the volume limits when the limit has positive lower volume.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
First suppose $\theta_j=\theta$ for every $j$. Put
\[
 \delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0,
 \qquad L=\lim_j\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q_j),
 \qquad e_k=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q_k)-L.
\]
The limit exists by \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity} and bounded mass. After discarding finitely many indices, choose $b_k>1$ tending to infinity with $b_k^n e_k<\delta$, and set $a_k=1-b_k^{-1}$.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For every $j\geq k$, the volume deficit between $q_j$ and $q_k$ is at most $e_k$, whereas the lower volume of $q_k$ is at least $\delta$. Thus \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} supplies $h_{k,j}\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with
\[
 a_kq_k+(1-a_k)h_{k,j}\leq q_j.
\]
Combining normalized ordinary-envelope competitors, as in the proof of \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality}, gives
\[
 a_kq_k+(1-a_k)P_\theta[h_{k,j}]\leq q_j.
\]
The potentials $P_\theta[h_{k,j}]$ have supremum zero. For fixed $k$, normalized compactness gives a subsequential $L^1$ limit $h_k$ as $j\to\infty$. Passing to the limit and applying \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} gives
\[
 a_kq_k+(1-a_k)h_k\leq q.
\]
For either volume, homogeneity, addition and monotonicity now yield
\[
 a_k^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q_k)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta\bigl(a_kq_k+(1-a_k)h_k\bigr)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q_k).
\]
Letting $k\to\infty$ proves both volume limits in the fixed background.

For the varying backgrounds, choose $w\leq q$ with $\theta+\ddc w\geq d\omega$, $d>0$, by \cref{cor:positive-volume-strict-barrier}. Choose $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ with $\varepsilon_j>0$ and $\theta_j\leq\theta+\varepsilon_j\omega$, and put
\[
 c_j=\frac d{d+\varepsilon_j},\qquad
 f_j=c_jq_j+(1-c_j)w,
\]
\[
 F_j=\left(\sup_{k\geq j}f_k\right)^*,\qquad
 r_j=P_\theta[F_j].
\]
The background of the displayed convex combination is bounded above by $\theta$:
\[
 c_j\theta_j+(1-c_j)(\theta-d\omega)\leq\theta.
\]
Thus $f_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Since $f_k\leq q_k\leq q_j$ for $k\geq j$, upper regularization gives $F_j\leq q_j$. Also $f_k\to q$ in $L^1$, so a subsequence converges almost everywhere and $q\leq F_j$ by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Background monotonicity now gives
\[
 q\leq r_j=P_\theta[F_j]\leq P_{\theta_j}[F_j]
       \leq P_{\theta_j}[q_j]=q_j.
\]
The $r_j$ decrease to $q$. Their lower volumes are positive, so \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} makes them ordinary model potentials. The fixed-background case just proved applies to $r_j$. Addition and background monotonicity also give, for either volume,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_{\theta_j}(q_j)
 \leq c_j^{-n}\operatorname{vol}_\theta(f_j)
 \leq c_j^{-n}\operatorname{vol}_\theta(r_j).
\]
The outer terms tend to the same limit. Monotonicity in both background and potential gives the stated decreasing convergence.

By \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison}, the volumes of the fixed potential $q$ in background $\theta_j$ tend to its volumes in background $\theta$. Hence the upper-volume deficit between $q$ and $q_j$, measured in $\theta_j$, tends to zero, while the lower volume of $q_j$ is bounded below by $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0$. Apply \cref{prop:volume-deficit-lelong} in each background. Its proof allows one common Lelong bound from $\pi^*\theta_1$, since $\theta_j\leq\theta_1$. This proves uniform convergence on each fixed model.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
For an arbitrary decreasing sequence, envelope convergence is exactly the missing upper-volume continuity.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:decreasing-envelope-volume-criterion}
Suppose $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ decreases to $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with positive lower volume. Then
\[
 P_{\theta_j}[u_j]\searrow P_\theta[u]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(u_j)\searrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
When these conditions hold, the lower volumes converge to that of $u$ in background $\theta$, and the Lelong numbers converge uniformly on every fixed smooth compact modification.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $u_j\leq0$ by a common translation. Set $p_j=P_{\theta_j}[u_j]$, $p=P_\theta[u]$ and $q=\lim_jp_j$. The envelope-volume theorem in background $\theta_j$ makes every $p_j$ a fixed point of positive lower volume. The envelopes decrease, and the uniform convergence of the backgrounds gives $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Since $u\leq p\leq q$, the preceding decreasing-model theorem applies and gives, for either volume,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)
       =\lim_j\operatorname{vol}_{\theta_j}(p_j)
       =\lim_j\operatorname{vol}_{\theta_j}(u_j),
 \qquad P_\theta[q]=q.
\]
If $q=p$, these equalities and \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} give both volume limits. Conversely, equality of the upper volumes of $u$ and $q$ puts $u$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,q)$. By \cref{cor:full-mass-envelope-characterization}, $P_\theta[u]=q$. For the Lelong assertion, \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison} makes the upper-volume deficit between $u$ and $u_j$ in background $\theta_j$ tend to zero. Apply \cref{prop:volume-deficit-lelong} with the common lower bound $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)>0$ and the uniform Lelong bound from $\pi^*\theta_1$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ordinary-envelope-background-limit}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(u)\searrow
       \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
If this limiting volume is positive, then also
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(u)\searrow
       \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),\qquad
 P_{\theta_j}[u]\searrow P_\theta[u].
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The volume limits follow from the two estimates in \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison}. In the positive-lower-volume case, apply \cref{prop:decreasing-envelope-volume-criterion} to the constant sequence $u_j=u$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
We apply \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} on the submanifold $Y$, returning to the ambient data of \cref{chap:trace-data}. The majorant in a smaller background gives the trace envelope positive lower volume.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:mass-bound-exact-trace}
Suppose $Y$ has bounded mass. Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega_0\geq\theta|_Y$ and a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq2\Omega_0$ with $v_-(\Omega)>0$. Then
\[
 \nu\bigl(\mathcal T_\Omega(u;Y)\circ\pi,E\bigr)=\tau_{Y,u}(E)
\]
for every prime divisor $E$ on every smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $T=\mathcal T_\Omega(u;Y)$ and $r=\dim Y$. The normalized majorant $v$ of \cref{prop:trace-profile-majorant} lies below $T$ and belongs to $\PSH(Y,\Omega/2)$. For every $w\simeq T$ in $\PSH(Y,\Omega)$, a constant translate of $v$ lies below $w$. By \cref{lem:smaller-background-mass-lower},
\[
 \int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\Omega(w)\geq2^{-r}v_-(\Omega),
 \qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\Omega(T)
       \geq2^{-r}v_-(\Omega)>0.
\]
The restricted approximants belong to $\PSH(Y,\Omega)$ for all sufficiently large indices. By \cref{thm:restricted-analytic-envelope-limit}, their dyadic ordinary envelopes $q_j=P_\Omega[u_{2^j}|_Y]$ decrease to $T$. The decreasing-model theorem applies on $Y$ and gives
\[
 \nu(T\circ\pi,E)
 =\lim_j\nu(q_j\circ\pi,E)
 =\lim_j2^{-j}\operatorname{ord}_E\mathfrak a_{2^j}(u;Y)
 =\tau_{Y,u}(E),
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

\section{Cauchy convergence of model potentials}
\label{sec:model-volume-comparison}

For two potentials, the maximum gives a common upper bound. The sum of the two upper-volume deficits measures their separation after ordinary projection. A uniform positive lower-volume bound controls how this comparison changes when a third potential is introduced. This replaces the triangle inequality in the Cauchy argument below. Write $U(u)=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ and $M=U(V_\theta)$, and fix a smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$ for $L^1$ norms.

\begin{definition}\label{def:model-volume-comparison}
For $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, define
\[
 d_\theta(u,v)=2U(\max(u,v))-U(u)-U(v).
\]
For $\delta>0$, put
\[
 \mathcal M_\delta(\theta)=
 \{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):P_\theta[u]=u,
       \ \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\geq\delta\}.
\]
A sequence is $d_\theta$-Cauchy if $d_\theta(u_j,u_k)\to0$ as $j,k\to\infty$, and converges to $u$ for this comparison if $d_\theta(u_j,u)\to0$.
\end{definition}

The function $d_\theta$ is finite, nonnegative and symmetric. It depends only on the ordinary singularity types, since replacing either input by an equivalent one changes the maximum by a bounded amount. Monotonicity also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:volume-comparison-basic}
 |U(u)-U(v)|\leq d_\theta(u,v),\qquad
 u\leq v\ \Longrightarrow\ d_\theta(u,v)=U(v)-U(u).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model-volume-separation}
For potentials $u,v$ of positive lower volume,
\[
 d_\theta(u,v)=d_\theta(P_\theta[u],P_\theta[v]),\qquad
 d_\theta(u,v)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[v].
\]
There is $C_\theta>0$, depending on $\theta$ and $\mathrm{d}V$, such that, for $u,v\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$,
\[
 \|u-v\|_{L^1}\leq
 2C_\theta\min\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{2d_\theta(u,v)}\delta\right)^{1/n}\right\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Translate the inputs below zero, and put $p=P_\theta[u]$, $q=P_\theta[v]$, $m=\max(u,v)$ and $r=P_\theta[m]$. Envelope order gives
\[
 m\leq\max(p,q)\leq r.
\]
All these potentials have positive lower volume. The ordinary-envelope volume identity and monotonicity give $U(m)=U(\max(p,q))=U(r)$, while $U(u)=U(p)$ and $U(v)=U(q)$. This proves projection invariance. If $d_\theta(u,v)=0$, then $U(u)=U(v)=U(m)$. The relative full-mass equivalence and \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality} give $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[m]=P_\theta[v]$. Conversely, if $p=q$, projection invariance gives zero.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Now let $u,v\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ and set $s=d_\theta(u,v)$. Normalized compactness supplies $C_\theta$ bounding the $L^1$ norm of every $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential with supremum zero. If $s\geq\delta/2$, the asserted estimate follows from the two individual norm bounds. Suppose $0<s<\delta/2$ and put $t=(2s/\delta)^{1/n}$. The deficit from $u$ to $m$ is at most $s$, and the lower volume of $m$ is at least $\delta$. Thus \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch}, with $b=t^{-1}$, gives $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ such that
\[
 (1-t)m+th\leq u.
\]
For each normalized competitor for $P_\theta[h]$, the corresponding convex combination with $m$ lies below zero and is more singular than $u$. Since $u=P_\theta[u]$, taking the envelope gives
\[
 (1-t)m+tP_\theta[h]\leq u.
\]
Hence $\int_X(m-u)\,\mathrm{d}V\leq tC_\theta$, because $m\leq0$ and $P_\theta[h]$ has supremum zero. Applying the same argument to $v$ and adding gives the estimate. If $s=0$, use any $t\in(0,1)$ and let $t\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

The next bound supplies a uniform modulus for three-point comparisons. It does not assert the triangle inequality for $d_\theta$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model-volume-comparison-modulus}
For $u,v,w\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus}
 d_\theta(u,w)\leq d_\theta(u,v)+d_\theta(v,w)
 +2nM\min\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{2d_\theta(v,w)}\delta\right)^{1/n}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently every $d_\theta$-convergent sequence in $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ is $d_\theta$-Cauchy, and its limit is unique.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $s=d_\theta(v,w)$. If $s\geq\delta/2$, use $d_\theta(u,w)\leq2M$. If $0<s<\delta/2$, set $t=(2s/\delta)^{1/n}$. The deficit lemma supplies $h\leq v$ with
\[
 (1-t)\max(v,w)+th\leq v.
\]
Set $r=\max(u,v)$ and $z=\max(u,v,w)$. On the branch $z=\max(v,w)$ the displayed inequality applies; on the branch $z=u$, both $u$ and $h$ lie below $r$. Thus
\[
 (1-t)z+th\leq r.
\]
Addition, homogeneity and volume monotonicity give
\[
 U(z)-U(r)\leq\bigl(1-(1-t)^n\bigr)U(z)\leq nMt.
\]
Since $\max(u,w)\leq z$, it follows that
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\theta(u,w)
 &\leq2U(r)+2nMt-U(u)-U(w)\\
 &=d_\theta(u,v)+U(v)-U(w)+2nMt\\
 &\leq d_\theta(u,v)+s+2nMt.
 \end{split}
\]
For $s=0$, the same proof works with every $t\in(0,1)$; let $t\downarrow0$. This proves \eqref{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus}. Applying it with the proposed limit as the middle point shows that a convergent sequence is Cauchy. Applying it with a sequence member as the middle point and then using separation proves uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness}
Every $d_\theta$-Cauchy sequence in $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ converges for $d_\theta$ to a unique element of $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$. It also converges to that element in $L^1(X,\mathrm{d}V)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a subsequence $a_j$ of the given sequence such that
\[
 d_\theta(a_j,a_{j+1})<\delta\,2^{-(j+1)n}
 \qquad(j\geq1).
\]
For $k\geq0$, set
\[
 v_j^k=\max(a_j,\ldots,a_{j+k}),\qquad
 v_j=\left(\sup_{l\geq j}a_l\right)^*.
\]
For every $l\geq j$, the deficit lemma with $b=2^l$ gives $h_l\leq a_l$ satisfying
\[
 (1-2^{-l})\max(a_l,a_{l+1})+2^{-l}h_l\leq a_l.
\]
The maximum argument used above gives
\[
 (1-2^{-l})v_j^{l+1-j}+2^{-l}h_l\leq v_j^{l-j}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 U(v_j^{l+1-j})-U(v_j^{l-j})\leq nM2^{-l}.
\]
Summing from $l=j$ to $j+k-1$ and then applying increasing-volume continuity as $k\to\infty$ yields
\[
 0\leq U(v_j)-U(a_j)\leq nM2^{1-j}.
\]
Here $v_j$ is genuine and has lower volume at least $\delta$, because $a_j\leq v_j\leq0$.

Put $q_j=P_\theta[v_j]$. The ordinary-envelope volume theorem makes $q_j$ a fixed point with the same two volumes as $v_j$. The sequence decreases, and each $q_j$ has supremum zero. Normalized compactness therefore gives a genuine decreasing limit $q$. By \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity},
\[
 q=P_\theta[q],\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)
 =\lim_j\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q_j)\geq\delta,
 \qquad U(q_j)\longrightarrow U(q).
\]
Both $a_j$ and $q$ lie below $q_j$. Hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\theta(a_j,q)
 &\leq\bigl(U(q_j)-U(a_j)\bigr)+\bigl(U(q_j)-U(q)\bigr)\\
 &\leq nM2^{1-j}+U(q_j)-U(q)\longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
\]
To recover the whole sequence, choose a late subsequence member $a_j$ as the middle point in \eqref{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus}. The Cauchy assumption makes the first term uniformly small for all sufficiently late original members, while $d_\theta(a_j,q)$ makes the other terms small. Thus the original sequence converges to $q$. Uniqueness follows from the preceding proposition, and the $L^1$ estimate in \cref{prop:model-volume-separation} proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The closed lower-volume bound is essential for this completeness statement.

\begin{example}\label{ex:strict-volume-cutoff-incomplete}
On a compact Riemann surface with a smooth positive form $\theta$ of integral one, the ordinary envelope fixed points of mass strictly greater than $\delta$, for $0<\delta<1$, do not form a complete space for $d_\theta$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Fix a point $p$ and use the nonpositive Green potential $g_p$ from \cref{thm:curve-trace-realization}, so that $\ddc g_p=\delta_p-\theta$. For $0\leq a<1$, put $q_a=P_\theta[ag_p]$. The current of $ag_p$ is $a\delta_p+(1-a)\theta$, with non-pluripolar mass $1-a$. Since the background is closed, its two singularity volumes equal this mass. The ordinary-envelope volume theorem gives
\[
 q_a=P_\theta[q_a],\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q_a)=U(q_a)=1-a.
\]
The potentials $q_a$ decrease as $a$ increases. Therefore \eqref{eq:volume-comparison-basic} gives $d_\theta(q_a,q_b)=|a-b|$. Choose $a_j\uparrow1-\delta$ with $a_j<1-\delta$. The resulting sequence is Cauchy and every term has mass greater than $\delta$. Any $d_\theta$ limit has upper volume $\delta$ by \eqref{eq:volume-comparison-basic}. On a curve the two volumes coincide, so no such limit belongs to the strict-cutoff space.
\end{proof}

\section{Rooftops and capacity convergence}
\label{sec:volume-rooftop-capacity}

A small upper-volume deficit also controls the common lower envelope of two potentials. For a finite family, the sum of the deficits must stay below the lower volume of a common upper bound. Applying this criterion after stretching gives increasing lower bounds for a suitable subsequence of models. Together with the decreasing upper bounds from the preceding section, they turn comparison convergence into capacity convergence.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finite-roof-volume-deficit}
Let $u_1,\ldots,u_N,w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, with $N\geq1$ and $u_i\preceq w$. If
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^N\bigl(U(w)-U(u_i)\bigr)
       <\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(w),
\]
then $P_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_N)$ is genuine.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Genuineness of a finite rooftop is unchanged by bounded changes of its obstacles. Choose $v_i\simeq u_i$ whose masses satisfy
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^N\left(U(w)-\int_X\mu_{v_i}\right)
       <\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(w).
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Translate so that $v_i\leq w\leq0$, and put
\[
 v_{i,k}=\max(v_i,w-k),\qquad
 p_k=P_\theta(v_{1,k},\ldots,v_{N,k}).
\]
The common competitor $w-k$ gives $w-k\leq p_k\leq w$, so $p_k\simeq w$. If the desired rooftop were not genuine, $p_k$ would decrease to $-\infty$, and normalized compactness would give $\sup_Xp_k\to-\infty$.

Fix $s>0$ and choose $k>s$ so large that $p_k\leq-s$. Iterating \cref{prop:rooftop-measure} and using locality on $\{v_i>-s\}$ gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(w)
 &\leq\int_X\mu_{p_k}
 \leq\sum_{i=1}^N\int_{\{v_{i,k}\leq-s\}}\mu_{v_{i,k}}\\
 &\leq\sum_{i=1}^N\left(U(w)-\int_{\{v_i>-s\}}\mu_{v_i}\right).
 \end{split}
\]
Here $v_{i,k}\simeq w$, and $w-k<-s$ makes $\{v_{i,k}>-s\}=\{v_i>-s\}$. Let $s\to\infty$. The measures are finite and non-pluripolar, so the resulting inequality contradicts the choice of $v_i$.
\end{proof}

To control a rooftop's volume, first stretch each obstacle, then move both stretches a fixed fraction back toward their maximum. The softened stretches have a genuine common rooftop by the lemma.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:volume-rooftop-continuity}
Let $u_j,u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, with $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)>0$, and suppose $d_\theta(u_j,u)\to0$. For all sufficiently large $j$, the rooftop $p_j=P_\theta(u_j,u)$ is genuine, and
\[
 d_\theta(p_j,u)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)
       \longrightarrow\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $\delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$, $m_j=\max(u_j,u)$ and $s_j=d_\theta(u_j,u)$. After discarding finitely many indices, choose $b_j\to\infty$ with $b_j>1$ and $b_j^ns_j<\delta$. The deficits of both $u_j$ and $u$ from $m_j$ are at most $s_j$, while $m_j$ has lower volume at least $\delta$. Thus \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} makes
\[
 G_j=Q_{b_j}(u_j,m_j),\qquad H_j=Q_{b_j}(u,m_j)
\]
genuine. Choose $a\in(0,1)$ with $2nMa<\delta$, and set
\[
 g_j=(1-a)m_j+aG_j,\qquad h_j=(1-a)m_j+aH_j.
\]
These potentials lie below $m_j$. Addition and monotonicity give
\[
 2U(m_j)-U(g_j)-U(h_j)
 \leq2\bigl(1-(1-a)^n\bigr)U(m_j)
 \leq2nMa<\delta.
\]
The preceding lemma therefore gives a genuine $r_j=P_\theta(g_j,h_j)$. For large $j$, put $t_j=(ab_j)^{-1}<1$. The defining inequalities for $G_j,H_j$ show that
\[
 (1-t_j)m_j+t_jr_j\leq u_j,u.
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Hence $p_j$ is genuine. For either volume, addition and monotonicity give
\[
 (1-t_j)^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq(1-t_j)^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(m_j)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(p_j)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u).
\]
Since $t_j\to0$ and $p_j\leq u$, this proves both assertions.
\end{proof}

For ordinary models, the finite rooftops are again models. Their normalization prevents collapse when the number of obstacles tends to infinity.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}
Suppose $u_j,u\in\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ and $d_\theta(u_j,u)\to0$. After passing to a subsequence and relabeling it, there are ordinary model potentials $v_j,w_j\in\mathcal M_{\delta/2}(\theta)$ such that
\[
 w_j\leq u_j\leq v_j,\qquad w_j\leq u\leq v_j,
\]
$v_j$ decreases to $u$, $w_j$ increases with $(\lim_jw_j)^*=u$, and
\[
 d_\theta(v_j,u)+d_\theta(w_j,u)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The original sequence converges to $u$ in capacity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For large $j$, put $r_j=P_\theta(u_j,u)$, using \cref{prop:volume-rooftop-continuity}. It is a model: envelope order gives
$r_j\leq P_\theta[r_j]\leq u_j,u$, so $P_\theta[r_j]$ is a competitor for the rooftop and equals $r_j$. The sequence $u_j$ is Cauchy by \cref{prop:model-volume-comparison-modulus}. Choose a subsequence so that
\[
 d_\theta(u_j,u_{j+1})<\delta\,2^{-(j+1)n},\qquad
 U(u)-U(r_j)<\delta\,4^{-(j+1)n}.
\]
The tail-maximum construction in the proof of \cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness} gives
\[
 v_j=P_\theta\left[\left(\sup_{l\geq j}u_l\right)^*\right]
       \searrow u,\qquad d_\theta(v_j,u)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed that construction gives the unique comparison limit, which here is $u$. Its upper models satisfy $v_j\geq u_j,u$ and have lower volume at least $\delta$.

For the lower bounds, the deficit lemma gives genuine potentials
\[
 H_l=Q_{4^l}(r_l,u),\qquad K_l=Q_{2^l}(r_l,u).
\]
Their defining inequalities give
\[
 (1-2^{-l})u+2^{-l}H_l\leq K_l\leq u,
 \qquad U(u)-U(K_l)\leq nM2^{-l}.
\]
Choose $j$ so large that $nM2^{1-j}<\delta$. For every $k\geq0$, the sum of the deficits of $K_j,\ldots,K_{j+k}$ is less than $\delta$, so \cref{lem:finite-roof-volume-deficit} makes their rooftop genuine. Since $r_l\leq u$, stretching decreases with its parameter; for $l\geq j$,
\[
 Q_{2^j}(r_l,u)\geq K_l.
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Thus $R_j^k=P_\theta(Q_{2^j}(r_j,u),\ldots,Q_{2^j}(r_{j+k},u))$ is genuine. The convex inequality for each obstacle gives
\[
 (1-2^{-j})u+2^{-j}R_j^k
       \leq w_j^k:=P_\theta(r_j,\ldots,r_{j+k})\leq u.
\]
For either volume,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-rooftop-volume-bound}
 (1-2^{-j})^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(w_j^k)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u).
\end{equation}
The same envelope-order argument used for $r_j$ shows that $w_j^k$ is a model. For fixed $j$ it decreases in $k$, with supremum zero. Normalized compactness gives a genuine limit $w_j$. The lower-volume conclusion of \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} passes the lower bound in \eqref{eq:tail-rooftop-volume-bound} to $w_j$. It is positive, so the theorem's upper-volume conclusion passes that bound as well.

The $w_j$ increase and satisfy $w_j\leq r_j\leq u_j,u$. After discarding finitely many indices, they have lower volume at least $\delta/2$, and \eqref{eq:tail-rooftop-volume-bound} gives $d_\theta(w_j,u)\leq nM2^{-j}$. By \cref{prop:model-volume-separation}, $w_j\to u$ in $L^1$, so $(\lim_jw_j)^*=u$. The two monotone sequences converge in capacity by \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}. Their pointwise bounds squeeze the selected $u_j$ to $u$ in capacity. Applying the construction to every subsequence proves capacity convergence for the original sequence.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
\section{Volumes of multiplier-ideal envelopes}
\label{sec:ideal-volume-approximation}

Analytic regularizations recover the ideal envelope, whose volume may exceed that of the original potential. Decreasing-model continuity computes this excess. A strict analytic potential absorbs the curvature error, so the same comparison can be made entirely in the fixed background. For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, set
\[
 \mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)
   =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}),\qquad
 \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)
   =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}).
\]
They depend only on the multiplier-ideal class of $u$ in the fixed background. By \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge} and gauge invariance of singularity volumes, they also depend only on the associated $\ddc$-class and the multiplier-ideal data, rather than on its smooth decomposition. Moreover, \cref{prop:p-below-i,thm:volume-monotonicity} give
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
The ideal envelope is an ordinary fixed point by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}. Its volumes are limits of the volumes of the dyadic analytic types.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dyadic-ideal-volume-limit}
For the regularizations $a_j$ and backgrounds $\theta_j=\theta+\epsilon_jh$ in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds},
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(a_j)
       \searrow\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
If $\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)>0$, then also
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(a_j)
       \searrow\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:dyadic-analytic-envelope-limit}, the analytic ordinary models $q_j=P_{\theta_j}[a_j]$ decrease to $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Apply \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}. Since $q_j\simeq a_j$ by \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, their two volumes agree term by term.
\end{proof}

Positive lower volume of the ideal envelope lets relative full mass detect when this limit also computes the upper volume of $u$.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:conditional-i-characterization}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and put $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Suppose $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0$. Then the following are equivalent:
\[
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
      =\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
If they hold, the corresponding equality for the lower volume also holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If the envelopes agree, \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} gives equality of both volumes with those of $q$.

Conversely, upper-volume equality and $u\preceq q$ put $u$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,q)$. Since $q=P_\theta[q]$, \cref{cor:full-mass-envelope-characterization} gives $P_\theta[u]=q$.
\end{proof}

Under the hypotheses of the proposition, \cref{cor:dyadic-goodness-criterion} also identifies equality of the upper volumes with convergence of the dyadic ordinary envelopes to $P_\theta[u]$. The next construction removes their background error while retaining the upper-volume limit. The corrected analytic types need not be ordered.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, put $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, and suppose $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0$. There are analytic $b_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $b_j\to u$ in $L^1$ such that the ordinary models $r_j=P_\theta[b_j]$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-big-analytic-comparison}
 d_\theta(r_j,q)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 d_\theta(r_j,u)\longrightarrow
 \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)
       -\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u).
\end{equation}
Their lower volumes converge to $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)$, and $r_j\to q$ in $L^1$ and capacity. In particular $d_\theta(r_j,u)\to0$ if and only if $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a Hermitian form $h$. Bigness and \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input} supply an analytic $\rho\leq0$ and a rational $\kappa>0$ with $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\kappa h$. Choose the dyadic $a_j$ in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}, enlarging their errors to positive rational numbers $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$. Put
\[
 c_j=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\epsilon_j},\qquad
 b_j=c_ja_j+(1-c_j)\rho.
\]
Then $\theta+\ddc b_j\geq((1-c_j)\kappa-c_j\epsilon_j)h=0$. The coefficients are rational, so \cref{prop:analytic-type-calculus} gives analytic singularities. Since $a_j\to u$ in $L^1$ and $c_j\to1$, also $b_j\to u$ there.

Choose $A>0$ with $\theta\leq Ah$, and set
\[
 \widehat\theta_j=\theta+(1+A/\kappa)\epsilon_jh,
 \qquad t_j=P_{\widehat\theta_j}[a_j].
\]
These backgrounds decrease to $\theta$ and dominate $\theta+\epsilon_jh$. The dyadic envelope theorem therefore gives $t_j\searrow q$. Moreover
\[
 c_j\widehat\theta_j-\theta
  =(1-c_j)(Ah-\theta)+c_j\epsilon_jh\geq0.
\]
The analytic-envelope identity gives $r_j\simeq b_j$. Thus $r_j/c_j$ is a normalized $\widehat\theta_j$-plurisubharmonic competitor more singular than $a_j$, since $\rho$ is bounded above. It follows that $r_j\leq c_jt_j$. Also $q\leq t_j\leq c_jt_j$.

For a lower bound, $q\sim_{\mathcal I}u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. Addition and scaling preserve this order by \cref{thm:local-i-inputs,lem:hermitian-i-calculus}. Thus $c_jq+(1-c_j)\rho\preceq_{\mathcal I}b_j$. This potential lies below zero, so envelope order and \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} give
\[
 c_jq+(1-c_j)\rho\leq P_\theta[b_j]_{\mathcal I}=r_j
       \leq c_jt_j.
\]
For either singularity volume, homogeneity, addition and monotonicity now yield
\[
 c_j^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(r_j)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\max(r_j,q))
 \leq c_j^n\operatorname{vol}_{\widehat\theta_j}(t_j).
\]
The last comparison uses both $\theta\leq c_j\widehat\theta_j$ and $\max(r_j,q)\leq c_jt_j$. By \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}, the outer terms tend to $\operatorname{vol}_\theta(q)$. This proves convergence of both volumes and the first limit in \eqref{eq:fixed-big-analytic-comparison}. In particular, $r_j$ and $q$ have a common positive lower-volume bound after discarding finitely many indices. Their $L^1$ and capacity convergence follow from \cref{prop:model-volume-separation,thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}.

To prove the second limit, shift $u$ below $q$; the comparison is invariant under constants. Then
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(r_j)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\max(r_j,u))
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\max(r_j,q)).
\]
Both bounds tend to $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)$. Substitute in the definition of $d_\theta$. The last equivalence is \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization}.
\end{proof}

For a Hermitian background the simpler rescaling in \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density} gives decreasing analytic models, with the same limits by \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}. In a general big background, the strict correction above may introduce additional poles. The upper-volume comparison still detects exactly the ideal-envelope excess.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:analytic-model-volume-criterion}
Let $p=P_\theta[p]$ have positive lower volume. Then $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$ if and only if there are analytic ordinary models $p_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $d_\theta(p_j,p)\to0$.

More generally, if $p_j=P_\theta[p_j]_{\mathcal I}$ and $d_\theta(p_j,p)\to0$, then $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$, apply \cref{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation} with $u=p$. For the converse it suffices to prove the more general assertion, because analytic ordinary models are ideal envelope fixed points.

Put $q=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$ and $\delta=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p)>0$. Choose $s_j\in(0,1)$ tending to zero with $d_\theta(p_j,p)<\delta s_j^n$, after discarding finitely many indices. For $m_j=\max(p_j,p)$ one has
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(m_j)\geq\delta,
 \qquad U(m_j)-U(p_j)\leq d_\theta(p_j,p).
\]
By \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch}, there is $h_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $(1-s_j)m_j+s_jh_j\leq p_j$. Set $H_j=P_\theta[h_j]_{\mathcal I}$. Monotonicity, addition and scaling in \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations} give
\[
 (1-s_j)q+s_jH_j
 \leq P_\theta[(1-s_j)m_j+s_jh_j]_{\mathcal I}
 \leq P_\theta[p_j]_{\mathcal I}=p_j.
\]
Consequently $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(p_j)\geq(1-s_j)^n\delta$ by addition and volume monotonicity. The models $p_j,p$ eventually lie in $\mathcal M_{\delta/2}(\theta)$, so \cref{prop:model-volume-separation} gives $p_j\to p$ in $L^1$.

The $H_j$ have supremum zero. Normalized compactness bounds $\int_X(-H_j)\,\mathrm{d}V$ by a constant $C_\theta$. Since $q\leq0$, the displayed convex inequality gives
\[
 \int_X(q-p_j)_+\,\mathrm{d}V
       \leq s_j\int_X(-H_j)\,\mathrm{d}V
       \leq C_\theta s_j\longrightarrow0.
\]
Passing to the $L^1$ limit gives $q\leq p$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere. The opposite inequality holds by the envelope comparison, so $p=q$.
\end{proof}

Thus the positive-lower-volume ideal envelope models are precisely the limits of analytic ordinary models for $d_\theta$. On each $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ they form a closed set for this convergence and inherit Cauchy completeness from \cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness}. The approximants constructed above have a common positive lower-volume bound, which need not equal the lower volume of the limiting model.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:hermitian-i-characterization}
For a big nonclosed background on a manifold with bounded mass, find criteria for $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I})>0$ in terms of restricted multiplier ideals or divisorial orders. When this lower volume vanishes, which implications between envelope equality and upper-volume equality in \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization} remain valid?
\end{problem}
