\chapter{Volumes under bounded mass}
\label{chap:mass-bound-volumes}

The upper and lower volumes in \cref{def:singularity-volumes} are defined as extended quantities in every smooth background. In this chapter $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$ with bounded mass, and the backgrounds are big smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. These assumptions give finite invariants and comparison under changes of singularities. Positive lower volume will be imposed separately where it is used. The envelope constructions of \cref{chap:envelopes} remain available without these mass assumptions.

\section{Comparison of singularity volumes}
\label{sec:bounded-mass-volume-comparison}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$ with bounded mass. Let $\theta$ be a big smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Use the upper and lower singularity volumes $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ and $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ from \cref{def:singularity-volumes}, obtained by taking the supremum and infimum of total mass over potentials ordinarily equivalent to $u$. The relation $u_1\preceq u_2$ means $u_1\leq u_2+C$ for a constant $C$.

In this setting the volumes are those of \cite[Definition 3.1]{BGL25}, expressed in potentials, and
\[
 0\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)<+\infty.
\]
The uniform finiteness at the right is the content of the bounded mass estimates, not merely the finiteness of each individual integral.

Under bounded mass and bigness, if $\theta$ is also closed, both volumes equal $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)$; this uses invariance of the non-pluripolar mass under equivalent singularities, recalled in \cite[Example 3.2]{BGL25}. For a non-closed background this equality can fail even at $u=0$, as \cref{ex:torus-mass} shows.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:volume-monotonicity}
Let $\theta_1,\theta_2$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. Suppose $\theta_1$ is big, $\theta_1\leq\theta_2$, and $u_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i)$ satisfy $u_1\preceq u_2$. On a manifold with bounded mass,
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
 \leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We reconstruct \cite[Proposition 3.7]{BGL25} in the big regime. Shift $u_1$ so that $u_1\leq u_2$. Choose $v_2\simeq u_2$ in $\PSH(X,\theta_2)$ and, for real $t>0$, set
\[
 p_t=P_{\theta_1}(u_1+t,v_2).
\]
For $t>0$, a sufficiently low constant translate of $u_1$ is a competitor; also $p_t\leq u_1+t$. Thus $p_t\simeq u_1$. The contact theorem, applied in each background on the corresponding contact part, and positivity of the mixed products give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(p_t)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{u_1\leq v_2-t\}}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(u_1)
      +\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_2}(v_2).
\]
Indeed $p_t$ is also $\theta_2$-plurisubharmonic and is below $v_2$, so the contact inequality in that background applies. The first measure on the right has mass tending to zero: its indicators tend to zero off the pole sets, and the measure is finite and non-pluripolar. Integrate, let $t\to+\infty$, and then take the infimum over $v_2$.

For the upper volume, choose any $v_1\simeq u_1$ in $\PSH(X,\theta_1)$ and put $w_t=\max(v_1,u_2-t)$. It is $\theta_2$-plurisubharmonic and $w_t\simeq u_2$. On $D_t=\{v_1>u_2-t\}$, locality and mixed-product positivity imply
\[
 \mathbf1_{D_t}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(v_1)
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_t}\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_2}(w_t).
\]
The sets $D_t$ exhaust the complement of a pluripolar set. Hence integration and passage to the limit give
$\int_X\mathrm{MA}_{\theta_1}(v_1)\leq
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_2}(u_2)$.
Take the supremum over $v_1$. This proof does not require $v_1$ to be bounded.
\end{proof}

The theorem orders the backgrounds and the potentials separately. These hypotheses should not be confused with ordering the two currents $\theta_i+\ddc u_i$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:volume-addition}
Assume bounded mass. Let $\theta$ be big, $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and $v\in\PSH(X,\eta)$ for a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ on $X$. Then, for either the lower or the upper volume,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_{\theta+\eta}(u+v).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The form $\theta+\eta$ is big, by adding the positive current $\eta+\ddc v$ to a strictly positive current in the class of $\theta$. For the upper volume, add $v$ to each competitor for $u$. Positivity of mixed products shows that the resulting mass is at least the original mass.

For the lower volume, choose $w\simeq u+v$ in $\PSH(X,\theta+\eta)$, and let $p=P_\theta(w-v)$. Since $w-v=u+O(1)$ outside the pole sets, the envelope is genuine and $p\simeq u$. The contact theorem says that its measure is carried by $D=\{p+v=w\}$. Furthermore $p+v\leq w$ everywhere, since this inequality holds off a pluripolar set between canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic functions. Thus
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_{\theta+\eta}(p+v)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_{\theta+\eta}(w).
\]
Integrating and taking the infimum over $w$ proves the assertion. This is the envelope proof of \cite[Proposition 2.5]{XL26}.
\end{proof}

\section{Relative full mass}

Retain the manifold $X$ of dimension $n$ with bounded mass and a big smooth real background $\theta$ from \cref{sec:bounded-mass-volume-comparison}. Fix a genuine reference $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and use the same upper and lower volumes. The envelope class $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$ is defined in \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}. For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and real $b>1$, write $Q_b(u,\varphi)=P_\theta(bu-(b-1)\varphi)$ for its stretching envelope, with the obstacle interpreted off the pole sets as in that definition.

\begin{definition}\label{def:relative-volume-class}
Define
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\preceq\varphi,
 \ \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
   =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)\}.
\]
\end{definition}

This is \cite[Definition 3.2]{XL26}. The next theorem compares it with the envelope class of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-equivalence}
Suppose $X$ has bounded mass, $\theta$ is big, and
$\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0$. Then
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi).
\]
Every member also satisfies
$\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First assume the envelope condition. For each $a\in(0,1)$ there is $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ such that $a\varphi+(1-a)h\leq u$. By \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity,prop:volume-addition} and homogeneity, for either volume,
\[
 a^n\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(a\varphi+(1-a)h)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(u)
 \leq\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\varphi).
\]
Let $a\to1$. This proves equality of both volumes in this direction, even without positive lower volume.

Conversely, suppose equality of the upper volumes, and fix $b>1$. Shift the potentials so that $u\leq\varphi\leq0$. For positive integers $j$, put
\[
 u_j=\max(u,\varphi-j),\qquad q_j=Q_b(u_j,\varphi).
\]
Each $q_j$ is genuine and equivalent to $\varphi$: the lower competitor $\varphi-bj$ and the defining inequality give
$\varphi-bj\leq q_j\leq\varphi$.
The sequence decreases. If its limit were $-\infty$, the compactness and Hartogs lemma for normalized quasi-plurisubharmonic functions would give $\sup_Xq_j\to-\infty$.

Set $C_j=\{b^{-1}q_j+(1-b^{-1})\varphi=u_j\}$. The contact theorem and positivity of mixed products give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(q_j)
 \leq b^n\mathbf1_{C_j}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_j).
\]
For a positive integer $k$, on $C_j\cap\{q_j\leq-bk\}$, the inequality $\varphi\leq0$ implies $u_j\leq-k$, hence $u\leq-k$. For $j>k$, locality on $\{u>-k\}\subset\{u>\varphi-j\}$ therefore yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_{\{q_j\leq-bk\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(q_j)
 &\leq b^n\int_{\{u\leq-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_j)\\
 &\leq b^n\left(
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)
 -\int_{\{u>-k\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)\right).
 \end{split}
\]
If $\sup_Xq_j\to-\infty$, the domain on the left is all of $X$ for large $j$. It follows, on letting $k\to+\infty$, that
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq b^n\left(
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)
 -\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)\right).
\]
The same collapse would hold with any $u'\simeq u$ in place of $u$: a competitor for either target gives one for the other by a constant translation. Repeating the argument and taking the supremum of $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u')$ gives
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq b^n\left(
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)
 -\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\right)=0,
\]
a contradiction. Thus $Q_b(u,\varphi)$ is genuine for every $b$. The argument is the full-volume case of \cite[Lemma 3.1]{XL26}; the proof uses bounded mass and the displayed positive lower volume, without a separate global positive volume hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\section{Model references with positive lower volume}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold with bounded mass, let $\theta$ be a big smooth real $(1,1)$-background and let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be genuine. Write $P_\theta[\varphi]$ for its ordinary singularity envelope and $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)$ for its lower singularity volume.

For a big, possibly non-semipositive form under bounded mass, Li uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:li-model-condition}
 P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi,\qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0.
\end{equation}
Under bounded mass, positive lower volume implies the smaller-background condition by \cite[Proposition 2.11]{ALS26}. In the latter assertion $\theta$ is semipositive and big, and the reference is an ordinary envelope fixed point. The smaller-background condition \eqref{eq:als-model-condition} does not itself assert a uniform positive lower mass. Its no-mass-bound consequences were proved in \cref{sec:relative-envelope-room}.

\section{Volumes of multiplier-ideal envelopes}

Retain a connected compact complex manifold $X$ with bounded mass and a big smooth real $(1,1)$-background $\theta$. Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and write $P_\theta[u]$ and $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ for its ordinary and multiplier-ideal singularity envelopes. Use the upper/lower volumes of \cref{def:singularity-volumes} and the envelope class of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}.

There is a natural pair of replacements under bounded mass. For big $\theta$, define
\[
 \mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)
   =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}),\qquad
 \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u)
   =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}).
\]
They are finite and depend only on the multiplier-ideal class of $u$ in the fixed background. By \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge} and gauge invariance of singularity volumes, they also depend only on the associated $\ddc$-class and the multiplier-ideal data, rather than on its smooth decomposition. Moreover, \cref{prop:p-below-i,thm:volume-monotonicity} give
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
These inequalities justify considering the two nonnegative gaps. They do not assert that either gap detects equality of envelopes without further hypotheses.

The following proposition gives a conditional volume characterization of equality of the ordinary and multiplier-ideal envelopes.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:conditional-i-characterization}
Assume bounded mass and a big background $\theta$. Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and put $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Suppose
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0,
 \qquad u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,P_\theta[u]).
\]
Then the following are equivalent:
\[
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
      =\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta}(u).
\]
If they hold, the corresponding equality for the lower volume also holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If the envelopes agree, the relative-class hypothesis puts $u$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,q)$. The proof of \cref{thm:relative-equivalence} gives equality of both volumes with those of $q$.

Conversely, equality of the upper volumes, together with $u\preceq q$ from \cref{prop:p-below-i}, puts $u$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,q)$. Positive lower volume and \cref{thm:relative-equivalence} imply membership in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,q)$. By \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality},
\[
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[q]=q,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

The ordinary model identity $P_\theta[q]=q$ used in this proof is automatic by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}. The remaining displayed hypotheses are substantive. Under these hypotheses, \cref{cor:dyadic-goodness-criterion} also identifies equality of the upper volumes with convergence of the dyadic ordinary envelopes to $P_\theta[u]$. The remaining issue is to find intrinsic hypotheses ensuring the required positive lower volume and relative-class membership.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:hermitian-i-characterization}
For big non-closed backgrounds on manifolds with bounded mass, what is the weakest positive-volume assumption under which the hypotheses of \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization} are automatic? Can that assumption be checked using only the ideal or divisorial data in the fixed background?
\end{problem}

This is a research question for the present manuscript, not a claim that every part is absent from the literature. In particular, the relative-envelope results in \cite{ALS26,XL26} already address parts of the modelization problem. The remaining comparison must preserve their precise hypotheses. A convergence defined through upper volumes should not be called a pseudometric until a triangle inequality has actually been proved.
