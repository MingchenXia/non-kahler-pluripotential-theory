\chapter{Partial kernels and quotient equilibrium}
\label{chap:partial-bergman}

\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $(L,h)$ be a holomorphic line bundle on $X$ with a smooth Hermitian metric.

Partial Bergman kernels use a singular weight to select global holomorphic sections and a continuous weight in their norm. When the selecting potential is plurisubharmonic relative to the curvature of $(L,h)$, the Bernstein--Markov estimate preserves the obstacle, while Lelong-number estimates on modifications preserve the multiplier-ideal constraint. Together they give the partial envelope as an upper bound. A matching lower bound requires global section approximation. With a K\"ahler modification and positive non-pluripolar mass, the closed theory also gives measure convergence; a Jacobian isometry transfers it to $X$.

Positivity against an unrelated Hermitian form permits different behavior. On a fibration, every section of a pulled-back line bundle descends even when the selecting potential does not. Moving a pole along a fiber can then preserve every kernel while changing the multiplier-ideal type. Point and divisor models exhibit this loss through explicit binomial tails. Section growth determines the normalization, and changing the integration volume separates the base equilibrium measure from the conditional fiber probabilities retained by its lift.

When vanishing orders grow sublinearly in the tensor degree, a weighted entire-function model gives a Gaussian transition on a shrinking scale. A uniform local norm comparison and a global $L^2$ correction show how far the same transition persists on a compact curve.

\section{The selected space and the continuous weight}\label{sec:partial-selected-space}\index{Bergman kernel!partial}

Fix a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi$ and a smooth positive probability measure $\nu$ on $X$. For each integer $m\geq1$ set
\[
 V_m=H^0\bigl(X,L^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\varphi)\bigr),
 \qquad N_m=\dim V_m.
\]
For a real $v\in C(X)$ define the norm
\[
 \|s\|_{m,v,\nu}^2=\int_X|s|_{h^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mv}\,\mathrm{d}\nu
 \quad(s\in V_m).
\]
The continuous weight is bounded, so the norm is finite on every global section. Its nondegeneracy follows from the strictly positive density and the identity theorem. This separation of the selecting weight from the norm follows \cite[Section 14.3]{SGPT}.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
Fix an integer $r\geq1$ for the normalization. Let $s_1,\ldots,s_{N_m}$ be an orthonormal basis of $V_m$ for this norm, and for $x\in X$ put
\[
 B_{m,v,\varphi,\nu}(x)
       =\mathrm{e}^{-mv(x)}\sum_j|s_j(x)|_{h^m}^2,
 \qquad
 \beta_m^{(r)}=\frac{r!}{m^r}B_{m,v,\varphi,\nu}\,\nu.
\]
Empty spaces give zero kernels and zero measures. Unitary invariance and the evaluation argument of \cref{prop:finite-bergman-identities} give
\[
 B_{m,v,\varphi,\nu}(x)
 =\sup_{\substack{s\in V_m\\\|s\|_{m,v,\nu}\leq1}}
       |s(x)|_{h^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mv(x)},
 \qquad
 \int_X\beta_m^{(r)}=\frac{r!N_m}{m^r}.
\]
The singular weight occurs only in $V_m$. In particular, ordering its multiplier ideals orders the kernels when $h,v,\nu$ are fixed, because it orders the spaces in the displayed supremum.

Replacing $h$ by $h\mathrm{e}^{-a}$ and $v$ by $v-a$, for smooth real $a$, leaves both the norm and the kernel measure unchanged.

\section{A local Bernstein--Markov estimate}
\label{sec:partial-bernstein-markov}

A Bernstein--Markov estimate compares the supremum norm with the integral norm up to an arbitrarily small exponential loss in the degree. The local submean inequality supplies it for a smooth positive volume and a continuous weight on the whole manifold.

For a compact set $K\subseteq X$ and $v\in C(K)$, write
\[
 \|s\|_{m,v,K}=\sup_K |s|_{h^m}\mathrm{e}^{-mv/2}.
\]
A probability measure $\mu$ on $K$ is Bernstein--Markov for $(K,v)$ if, for every $\epsilon>0$, there is $C_\epsilon$ such that
\[
 \|s\|_{m,v,K}\leq C_\epsilon \mathrm{e}^{\epsilon m}
          \left(\int_K|s|_{h^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mv}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/2}
\]
for all $m\geq1$ and all $s\in H^0(X,L^m)$. If $K$ is non-pluripolar, the supremum norm is nondegenerate: the zero set of a nonzero section is an analytic divisor, hence pluripolar. The Bernstein--Markov inequality then makes the integral norm nondegenerate as well. The same inequality holds on any selected subspace $V_m$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-full-bm}\index{Bernstein--Markov estimate}
Every smooth positive probability measure is Bernstein--Markov for $(X,v)$ when $v\in C(X)$, on an arbitrary compact complex manifold.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\nu$ be the smooth strictly positive probability measure under consideration. Choose finitely many nested coordinate charts with holomorphic frames for $L$. In a holomorphic frame $e$, write $|e|_h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\chi}$ and $s=f e^{\otimes m}$. Uniform continuity permits, for any $\epsilon>0$, a uniform small radius $r$ such that every point lies at the center of a coordinate ball of radius $r$ contained in one of the larger charts, and the oscillation of $\chi+v$ on that ball is at most $2\epsilon$. The radius and the volume comparison constants can be chosen uniformly using the finite covering.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
The submean inequality for the holomorphic function $f$ and the lower bound for the density of $\nu$ give
\[
 \begin{split}
 |s(x)|_{h^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mv(x)}
 &=|f(x)|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m(\chi+v)(x)}\\
 &\leq C_r \mathrm{e}^{2\epsilon m}
                 \int_{B(x,r)}|f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m(\chi+v)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\\
 &\leq C_r \mathrm{e}^{2\epsilon m}\|s\|_{m,v,\nu}^2.
 \end{split}
\]
Take the supremum over $x$ and then the square root.
\end{proof}

For a general weighted compact subset, the Bernstein--Markov property will be an explicit hypothesis.

\section{The determinant functional}
\label{sec:partial-determinant}

The logarithm of the Gram determinant integrates the partial kernel measure as the continuous weight varies. Orthogonal projection expresses its second derivative as a negative squared norm, giving concavity and a uniform bound in terms of the section dimension.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
Return to the full-manifold norm $\|\cdot\|_{m,v,\nu}$ of \cref{sec:partial-selected-space}, with a quasi-plurisubharmonic selecting weight $\varphi$. Fix $m$ with $N_m>0$ and a basis of $V_m$. Let $G_m(v)$ be its positive Hermitian Gram matrix in this norm. Define
\[
 \mathcal F_m^{(r)}(v)
 =-\frac{r!}{m^{r+1}}
       \log\frac{\det G_m(v)}{\det G_m(0)}.
\]
The quotient makes the definition independent of the chosen basis. Put $\mathcal F_m^{(r)}=0$ if $N_m=0$.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:partial-kernel-variation}\index{Determinant functional!partial kernels}
For $v,f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$,
\[
 \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\mathcal F_m^{(r)}(v+tf)
       =\int_X f\,\mathrm{d}\beta_m^{(r)}.
\]
For constants $c\in\mathbb R$ and weights $w\in C(X,\mathbb R)$, the functional is concave and satisfies
\[
 \mathcal F_m^{(r)}(v+c)=\mathcal F_m^{(r)}(v)
                         +\frac{r!N_m}{m^r}c,
 \qquad
 |\mathcal F_m^{(r)}(v)-\mathcal F_m^{(r)}(w)|
       \leq\frac{r!N_m}{m^r}\|v-w\|_\infty.
\]
If $N_m>0$, its second derivative along $v+tf$ is strictly negative unless $f$ is constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use a basis $s_1,\ldots,s_{N_m}$ orthonormal at $v$, so $G_m(v)=I$, the identity matrix of size $N_m$. Matrix indices $i,j$ below range from $1$ to $N_m$. Boundedness of the weights justifies differentiation under the integral. With pointwise inner products taken in $h^m$, put
\[
 A_{ij}=\int_X f\langle s_i,s_j\rangle_{h^m}\mathrm{e}^{-mv}\,\mathrm{d}\nu,
 \qquad
 C_{ij}=\int_X f^2\langle s_i,s_j\rangle_{h^m}\mathrm{e}^{-mv}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\]
Then $G'_m=-mA$ and $G''_m=m^2C$. The identity
$(\log\det G)'=\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}G')$ gives the first derivative. Differentiating it once more yields
\[
 \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2}\right|_{t=0}\mathcal F_m^{(r)}(v+tf)
 =-\frac{r!}{m^{r-1}}
       \left(\int_X f^2B_{m,v,\varphi,\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\nu
                     -\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^2\right).
\]
Let $P_v$ be the orthogonal projection of the full weighted $L^2$ space of measurable sections onto $V_m$. The quantity in parentheses equals
\[
 \sum_j\|(I-P_v)(fs_j)\|_{m,v,\nu}^2\geq0.
\]
This proves concavity. Multiplication of every Gram entry by $\mathrm{e}^{-mc}$ proves the translation rule. The first derivative and the total mass identity bound the derivative along the segment from $v$ to $w$, giving the Lipschitz estimate.

If the second derivative vanishes, choose $0\ne s\in V_m$ with $fs=\widetilde s\in V_m$ in $L^2$. Continuity and the positive density give equality everywhere. The real holomorphic quotient $\widetilde s/s=f$ is constant, say $c$, on a ball where $s\ne0$. The identity theorem gives $\widetilde s=cs$ on connected $X$, and continuity gives $f=c$ everywhere. The translation rule proves the converse.
\end{proof}

The derivative here is a partial kernel measure. The obstruction to a primitive for the raw Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere operator remains the one proved in \cref{chap:energy}.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:partial-kernel-zero-scale}
If $N_m=o(m^n)$, then $\beta_m^{(n)}\to0$ in total variation. Also $\mathcal F_m^{(n)}(v)\to0$ uniformly when $v$ ranges over a bounded subset of $C(X)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The measures are positive and their total masses are $n!N_m/m^n$. Use the Lipschitz estimate and $\mathcal F_m^{(n)}(0)=0$ for the second assertion.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For example, let $X$ be any connected compact complex manifold that is not Moishezon. By \cref{cor:non-moishezon-section-vanishing}, either every positive-degree section space of $L$ is zero, or $h^0(X,L^m)=O(m^d)$ for some $d<n$. Every selected subspace therefore satisfies \cref{cor:partial-kernel-zero-scale}. This applies in particular whenever $X$ fails the bounded mass property.

\section{Kernels on a fibration}\label{sec:partial-submersion}

Compact connected fibers make every section of a pulled-back line bundle descend. Choosing probability measures on the fibers makes this descent an isometry; the kernel measures then lift by fiber integration.

Let $\pi:X\to B$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion of connected compact complex manifolds, with connected fibers. Write $n=\dim X$ and $q=\dim B\geq1$. Let $A$ be a holomorphic line bundle on $B$ with smooth metric $h_A$, and choose a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\psi$ on $B$. Set
\[
 (L,h)=(\pi^*A,\pi^*h_A),\qquad \varphi=\pi^*\psi.
\]
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
For $y\in B$, write $X_y=\pi^{-1}(y)$. Choose a smoothly varying family of smooth strictly positive probability measures $\nu_y$ on these fibers. Such a family is obtained by restricting any smooth positive vertical volume density and dividing by its fiber integral. For $F\in C(X)$ set
\[
 \mathcal A F(y)=\int_{X_y}F\,\mathrm{d}\nu_y.
\]
The function $\mathcal A F$ is continuous. Indeed, a proper smooth submersion is locally a smooth fiber bundle; in a trivialization the smooth densities vary smoothly, and uniform continuity of $F$ on compact pieces gives continuity of the integrals. For a finite Borel measure $\mu$ on $B$, define its lift $\mathcal L\mu$ by
\[
 \int_X F\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal L\mu=\int_B\mathcal A F\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
\]
Positivity and the Riesz representation theorem give a finite measure with the same total mass as $\mu$. In particular, weak convergence of finite measures on the base implies weak convergence of their lifts.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:partial-kernel-submersion}
Pullback identifies
\[
 H^0\bigl(B,A^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\psi)\bigr)
       \simeq H^0\bigl(X,L^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\varphi)\bigr).
\]
Let $K\subseteq B$ be closed and non-pluripolar, let $w\in C(K)$, and let $\mu$ be a Bernstein--Markov probability measure for $(K,w)$ on $B$. Set $K_X=\pi^{-1}(K)$, $w_X=\pi^*w$ and $\mu_X=\mathcal L\mu$. The identified spaces have identical norms for these weighted sets and measures. Their partial kernel measures, normalized with the same integer $r$, satisfy
\[
 \beta_{X,m}^{(r)}=\mathcal L\bigl(\beta_{B,m}^{(r)}\bigr).
\]
The Bernstein--Markov inequality itself also transfers to the unweighted section spaces on $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every section of $\pi^*A^m$ descends. On the inverse image of a trivializing base chart, its coefficient is a holomorphic function and is constant on every compact connected fiber. Local holomorphic sections of the submersion show that the descended coefficient is holomorphic. These coefficients glue by the transition functions of $A^m$, giving the unweighted identification.

By \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback}, the upstairs multiplier ideal is the extension of $\mathcal I(m\psi)$. A descended coefficient belongs to this extended ideal exactly when its base coefficient belongs to $\mathcal I(m\psi)$: pullback gives one implication, and restriction to a local holomorphic section gives the other. This proves the selected-space identification.

Pointwise norms of corresponding sections agree and are constant along fibers. Since $\nu_y$ has mass one,
\[
 \int_{K_X}|\pi^*s|_{h^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mw_X}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_X
       =\int_K|s|_{h_A^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mw}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
\]
Their supremum norms also agree by surjectivity. Thus the Bernstein--Markov inequality transfers, and the integral norms are nondegenerate. Pulling back an orthonormal basis proves $B_{X,m}=\pi^*B_{B,m}$ on $K_X$. Integrating a continuous test function first along the fibers proves the measure identity. Kernels may be set to zero off their respective weighted sets.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\subsection{The lifted equilibrium measure}
\label{sec:partial-base-equilibrium}

On a K\"ahler base, the partial kernel measures converge to the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of the partial envelope. Continuity of fiber integration then gives a limit upstairs with the base-dimensional normalization.

Keep the submersion and weighted compact set $(K,w,\mu)$ above. Assume that $B$ is K\"ahler, put $\theta=c_1(A,h_A)$, and take $\psi\in\PSH(B,\theta)$ with positive non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere mass. Write $B_{B,m},B_{X,m}$ for the corresponding kernels, $\mu_X=\mathcal L\mu$, and
\[
 E=E_{\theta,K}[\psi]_{\mathcal I}(w).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:partial-kernel-quotient-limit}\index{Equilibrium measure!quotient}
Under these hypotheses,
\[
 \frac{q!}{m^q}B_{X,m}\,\mu_X
       \rightharpoonup\mathcal L\bigl(\mathrm{MA}_\theta(E)\bigr).
\]
If $n>q$, the measures with normalization $n!/m^n$ tend to zero in total variation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cite[Theorem~14.3.1]{SGPT} on the compact K\"ahler base, with its auxiliary twisting line bundle trivial. Its hypotheses are precisely the positive-mass potential $\psi$, the closed non-pluripolar set $K$, the continuous weight $w$, and the Bernstein--Markov probability $\mu$. It gives
\[
 \frac{q!}{m^q}B_{B,m}\,\mu
          \rightharpoonup\mathrm{MA}_\theta(E).
\]
\Cref{prop:partial-kernel-submersion} and continuity of the lifting operation give the asserted upstairs limit. In particular the $q$-normalized total masses are bounded. Multiplying them by $(n!/q!)m^{q-n}$ proves the last statement when $n>q$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\subsection{The Hopf equilibrium measure}

The vertical part of the Hopf metric converts the lifted base equilibrium measure into a Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere measure. Its coefficient records both the fiber probability and the top-product expansion.

Use $X_n$, $n\geq2$, its projection $\pi:X_n\to\mathbb P^{n-1}$ and the forms $\sigma,\alpha,\zeta,\omega$ of \cref{chap:hopf-reduction}. Take $A=\mathcal O(1)$ with curvature $\theta=\sigma$ and retain the selecting potential $\psi$ and weighted data $(K,w,\mu)$ above. On each elliptic fiber set $\nu_y=\zeta|_{X_y}/2$. For any base measure $\tau$ its lift is
\[
 \mathcal L\tau=\tfrac12\pi^*\tau\wedge\zeta
\]
in the precise fiber-integration notation of that chapter. Since $E\in\PSH(B,\sigma)$, \cref{thm:hopf-descended-hermitian-ma} identifies the limit as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-partial-equilibrium}
 \frac{(n-1)!}{m^{n-1}}B_{X_n,m}\,\mu_X
       \rightharpoonup
       \frac{1}{2n}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(\pi^*E).
\end{equation}
The factor $1/(2n)$ combines the fiber probability with the top-product coefficient in $\omega=\alpha+\zeta$. The closed curvature $\alpha$ has zero top product on these descended potentials; the vertical form supplies the nonzero Hermitian measure. For $n=3$ this gives a nonzero limit on a manifold failing BMP, with normalization $2/m^2$.

Every potential positive against the closed line-bundle curvature $k\alpha$, for a positive integer $k$, descends by \cref{thm:hopf-weighted-section-descent}. A potential positive only against $\omega$ can instead have an isolated pole. Its sections still descend, so the selected space records the pole through a condition on the base. We now determine that condition.
