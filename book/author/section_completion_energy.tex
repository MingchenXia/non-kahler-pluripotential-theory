\section{Symplectic completions and potential variations}
\label{sec:completion-energy}

The radial energy is built from the Monge--Amp\`ere one-form, and its first variation exhibits the torsion of the background. When a Hermitian form is the $(1,1)$-part of a closed real two-form, there is another natural energy: the squared $L^2$ norm of the smallest $(2,0)$-part needed to close it. In complex dimension four, adding a complex Hessian to the form changes this energy by minus the change of the volume. Its potential variations are therefore governed by the volume, and not by a primitive of the Monge--Amp\`ere one-form.

Throughout this section we assume $n=4$. A Hermitian form $\omega$ is \emph{Hermitian symplectic} if there is a smooth $(2,0)$-form $\rho$, called a \emph{completion} of $\omega$, such that
\[
 \partial\rho=0,\qquad \bar\partial\rho=-\partial\omega.
\]
Equivalently, the real two-form $\omega+\rho+\bar\rho$ is closed. Applying $\bar\partial$ to the second equation shows that $\omega$ is pluriclosed, meaning $\ddc\omega=0$. Two completions differ by a closed holomorphic $(2,0)$-form, so the completions of $\omega$ form an affine space over the finite-dimensional space of closed holomorphic $(2,0)$-forms. The quadratic norm
\[
 \|\rho\|_{L^2(\omega)}^2
   =\frac12\int_X\rho\wedge\bar\rho\wedge\omega^2
\]
is positive definite on $(2,0)$-forms, and minimizing it over this affine space gives a unique \emph{canonical completion} $\rho_\omega$. It is characterized by being orthogonal to every closed holomorphic $(2,0)$-form. Define the \emph{completion energy} and the volume of $\omega$ by
\[
 F(\omega)=\|\rho_\omega\|_{L^2(\omega)}^2,
 \qquad V(\omega)=\frac1{4!}\int_X\omega^4.
\]
\index{Hermitian symplectic metric}\index{Completion energy}\index{Symplectic completion!canonical}
The completion energy is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when $\omega$ is K\"ahler: a zero canonical completion forces $\partial\omega=0$, while a closed $\omega$ has the zero completion.

Since $\omega$ is pluriclosed, it defines an Aeppli cohomology class, whose real $(1,1)$-representatives are the forms $\omega+\partial\bar\tau+\bar\partial\tau$, with $\tau$ a smooth $(1,0)$-form. We call a real form $\psi=\partial\bar\tau+\bar\partial\tau$ an \emph{Aeppli variation}. If $\rho$ completes $\omega$ and $\omega+t\psi$ is positive for a real $t$, then $\omega+t\psi$ has the completion $\rho+t\partial\tau$. In particular, the positive representatives $\omega+\partial\bar\tau+\bar\partial\tau>0$ of the Aeppli class of $\omega$ are again Hermitian symplectic. Every positive potential variation $\omega+\ddc\varphi$ belongs to this class and has the same exterior derivative as $\omega$. We write $DF_\omega(\psi)$ for the derivative of $t\mapsto F(\omega+t\psi)$ at $t=0$.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:completion-first-variation}
Let $\omega$ be Hermitian symplectic, with canonical completion $\rho=\rho_\omega$. Every Aeppli variation $\psi=\partial\bar\tau+\bar\partial\tau$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completion-first-variation}
 DF_\omega(\psi)=\int_X\left(
  \operatorname{Re}(\partial\tau\wedge\bar\rho\wedge\omega^2)
  +\rho\wedge\bar\rho\wedge\psi\wedge\omega\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The canonical completion of $\omega+t\psi$ is $\rho+t\partial\tau-h(t)$, where $h(t)$ is a closed holomorphic $(2,0)$-form and $h(0)=0$. In a fixed basis of these holomorphic forms, the orthogonal projection defining $h(t)$ solves a positive definite Gram system whose coefficients are smooth in $t$. Since $\rho$ is orthogonal to closed holomorphic $(2,0)$-forms, the contribution of $h'(0)$ to the first derivative of the squared norm vanishes. We may therefore differentiate using the completion $\rho+t\partial\tau$. Differentiating its two factors and the factor $(\omega+t\psi)^2/2$ gives the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:completion-energy-volume}
Let $\omega$ be Hermitian symplectic. For every smooth real function $\varphi$ with $\omega_\varphi=\omega+\ddc\varphi>0$, one has $\rho_{\omega_\varphi}=\rho_\omega$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completion-energy-volume}
 F(\omega_\varphi)+V(\omega_\varphi)=F(\omega)+V(\omega).
\end{equation}
Moreover, $DF_\omega(\ddc\varphi)=0$ for every smooth real $\varphi$ if and only if $\ddc(\omega^3)=0$. Thus a critical point of $F$ in its Aeppli class is Gauduchon.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix any completion $\zeta$ of $\omega$ and put $\Theta_\zeta=\omega+\zeta+\bar\zeta$. Expansion by type gives
\[
 \frac1{4!}\int_X\Theta_\zeta^4
 =V(\omega)+\frac12\int_X\zeta\wedge\bar\zeta\wedge\omega^2
       +\frac14\int_X\zeta^2\wedge\bar\zeta^2.
\]
Since $\partial\omega_\varphi=\partial\omega$, the forms $\omega_\varphi$ and $\omega$ have the same completions. With $\zeta$ fixed, replacing $\omega$ by $\omega_\varphi$ changes the closed form $\Theta_\zeta$ by an exact form, so the integral on the left is unchanged; so is the last integral on the right, which does not involve $\omega$. Hence
\[
 \|\zeta\|_{L^2(\omega_\varphi)}^2
 -\|\zeta\|_{L^2(\omega)}^2=V(\omega)-V(\omega_\varphi).
\]
This difference is independent of $\zeta$, so the two norms have the same minimizer, which gives $\rho_{\omega_\varphi}=\rho_\omega$ and \eqref{eq:completion-energy-volume}. Differentiating at $t=0$ along $\omega+t\ddc\varphi$ and using \cref{lem:ibp}, we obtain
\[
 DF_\omega(\ddc\varphi)=-\frac16\int_X\varphi\ddc(\omega^3).
\]
Since $\varphi$ is arbitrary, this proves the remaining assertions.
\end{proof}

The \emph{positive index} of a real quadratic form on a real vector space is the supremum of the dimensions of the finite-dimensional subspaces on which it is positive definite; the \emph{negative index} is defined in the same way. Thus the positive index is infinite when the form is positive definite on subspaces of arbitrarily large finite dimension.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:completion-potential-hessian}\index{Completion energy!potential Hessian}
Let $\omega$ be Hermitian symplectic, and let $H_\omega$ be the Hessian of $F$ at $\omega$ along the affine space of real representatives of the Aeppli class of $\omega$, so that $H_\omega(\psi,\psi)$ is the second derivative of $t\mapsto F(\omega+t\psi)$ at $t=0$. If $\ddc(\omega^2)\ne0$, the restriction of $H_\omega$ to potential directions $\psi=\ddc\varphi$ has infinite positive and negative index. If $\ddc(\omega^2)=0$, then $F(\omega+\ddc\varphi)=F(\omega)$ whenever $\omega+\ddc\varphi>0$. Consequently, every local minimum of $F$ in an Aeppli class satisfies $\ddc(\omega^2)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the Hessian formula.} Pluriclosedness and Stokes' theorem give $\int_X\omega\wedge(\ddc\varphi)^3=\int_X(\ddc\varphi)^4=0$. Thus the volume and, by \eqref{eq:completion-energy-volume}, the energy along a potential line $t\mapsto\omega+t\ddc\varphi$ are polynomials of degree at most two in $t$. Differentiating twice and integrating by parts, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completion-potential-hessian}
 \begin{aligned}
 H_\omega(\ddc\varphi,\ddc\varphi)
 &=-\frac12\int_X\omega^2\wedge(\ddc\varphi)^2\\
 &=\frac12\int_X\ddc(\omega^2)\wedge\mathrm d\varphi\wedge\dc\varphi.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For the second equality, apply \cref{lem:ibp} once and then use the identity $\ddc(\varphi^2/2)=\varphi\ddc\varphi+\mathrm d\varphi\wedge\dc\varphi$ against the closed form $\ddc(\omega^2)$.

\emph{Step 2: both indices are infinite if $\ddc(\omega^2)\ne0$.} Put $\Psi=\ddc(\omega^2)$. At each point, $\xi\mapsto\Psi\wedge\mathrm i\xi\wedge\bar\xi$, divided by a positive volume form, is a Hermitian quadratic form in the $(1,0)$-covector $\xi$, and these quadratic forms determine $\Psi$. Their traces with respect to $\omega$ are positive multiples of the density $\Psi\wedge\omega$, whose integral vanishes by Stokes' theorem and $\ddc\omega=0$. If the quadratic forms took no strictly positive value, they would be negative semidefinite everywhere, with nonpositive traces of integral zero; the traces, and hence $\Psi$, would vanish identically. The same argument applies with the signs reversed. Therefore, if $\Psi\ne0$, the quadratic forms take a strictly positive value somewhere and a strictly negative value somewhere.

Fix one of the two signs. Choose holomorphic coordinates on a ball such that, with $x=\operatorname{Re}z_1$, the density $\Psi\wedge\mathrm dx\wedge\dc x$ has that sign on the ball; this is possible because $\mathrm dx\wedge\dc x$ is a positive multiple of $\mathrm i\,\mathrm dz_1\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_1$. For a nonzero real cutoff function $a$ supported in the ball, set $\varphi_N=a\cos(Nx)$. Formula \eqref{eq:completion-potential-hessian} gives
\[
 H_\omega(\ddc\varphi_N,\ddc\varphi_N)
 =\frac{N^2}{2}\int_Xa^2\sin^2(Nx)\Psi\wedge\mathrm dx\wedge\dc x+O(N),
\]
where the constant in $O(N)$ depends on $\Psi$, the chart and the cutoff, but not on $N$. By integration by parts in $x$, the integral tends to half the same integral without the factor $\sin^2(Nx)$. Hence the Hessian has the chosen sign for large $N$. For a positive integer $k$, apply this construction on $k$ disjoint smaller balls inside this ball. Since the integrand in \eqref{eq:completion-potential-hessian} is local, the resulting nonzero directions, which have disjoint supports, are linearly independent and pairwise orthogonal for the Hessian, so their span is a $k$-dimensional subspace on which $H_\omega$ is definite of the chosen sign. Repeating the argument with the opposite sign proves that both indices are infinite. Each finite-dimensional span admits sufficiently small variations preserving positivity.

\emph{Step 3: flatness if $\ddc(\omega^2)=0$.} If $\Psi=0$, \cref{lem:power-torsion} and pluriclosedness give $\ddc(\omega^3)=3\omega\wedge\Psi=0$. Both derivatives at $t=0$ of the quadratic polynomial $t\mapsto F(\omega+t\ddc\varphi)$ vanish, so it is constant on the interval of $t$ for which $\omega+t\ddc\varphi>0$. This interval contains $0$ and $1$ whenever $\omega+\ddc\varphi>0$. Finally, a local minimum cannot have a potential direction along which the Hessian is negative, so it satisfies $\ddc(\omega^2)=0$ by Step~2.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:completion-flat-invariant-mass}\index{Completion energy!invariant mass}
For a Hermitian symplectic form $\omega$ on $X$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F(\omega+\ddc\varphi)$ is independent of the smooth real function $\varphi$ with $\omega+\ddc\varphi>0$;
\item $\ddc(\omega^2)=0$;
\item $\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^4$ is independent of the bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function $u$.
\end{enumerate}
The common mass in the third condition is $24V(\omega)$. Every local minimum of $F$ in an Aeppli class has these properties.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:completion-energy-volume}, the energy is constant along smooth positive potential variations if and only if their volume is. Since $\ddc\omega=0$, \cref{lem:power-torsion,thm:mass-invariance-characterization} show that this smooth mass invariance is equivalent to $\ddc(\omega^2)=0$ and to mass invariance for bounded potentials. The zero potential gives the common mass. The last assertion follows from \cref{thm:completion-potential-hessian}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completion energy on products of complex tori}
\label{subsec:torus-completion-critical}

Products of two complex two-tori give explicit Hermitian symplectic forms in both regimes of \cref{thm:completion-potential-hessian}. Each factor carries a nonclosed form with an explicit completion. If both factors carry such a form, the product is critical for $F$ under every Aeppli variation although its potential Hessian is indefinite. If only one factor does, the energy is constant along potential variations but varies in the Aeppli class.

Consider the square complex two-torus $S=\mathbb C^2/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)^2$. Let $z_j=x_j+\mathrm i y_j$ be the coordinates on its cover and put $\eta=\mathrm i\sum_{j=1}^2\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$. For $0<s<1$, the periodic forms
\[
 v_s=\frac{s}{\pi}\mathrm e^{2\pi\mathrm i x_1}\mathrm dz_2,
 \qquad \alpha_s=\eta+\partial\bar v_s+\bar\partial v_s
\]
are well defined on $S$. The Hermitian matrix of $\alpha_s$ with respect to the forms $\mathrm i\,\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_k$ is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-s\mathrm e^{-2\pi\mathrm i x_1}\\
 -s\mathrm e^{2\pi\mathrm i x_1}&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
with eigenvalues $1-s$ and $1+s$, so $\alpha_s$ is a Hermitian form. It has the completion $\rho_s=\partial v_s=\mathrm i s\mathrm e^{2\pi\mathrm i x_1}\mathrm dz_1\wedge\mathrm dz_2$, and with $\mu_0=\eta^2/2$,
\[
 \frac{\alpha_s^2}{2}=(1-s^2)\mu_0,
 \qquad \rho_s\wedge\bar\rho_s=s^2\mu_0.
\]

\begin{example}\label{ex:torus-completion-critical}\index{Complex torus!nonclosed critical metric}\index{Completion energy!nonzero critical point}
On $X=S\times S$, let $\omega$ be the sum of the pullbacks of $\alpha_{1/\sqrt2}$ from the two factors. This form is Hermitian symplectic but not K\"ahler, and it is critical for $F$ under every Aeppli variation, including those with mixed factor components. If $V_0=\int_S\mu_0$, then $F(\omega)=V_0^2/2>0$. The potential Hessian of $F$ at $\omega$ has infinite positive and negative index, even though potential variations preserve $\mathrm d\omega$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Denote the two factor forms by $\alpha_i,\rho_i$, and put $\mu_i=\alpha_i^2/2$ and $\nu_i=\rho_i\wedge\bar\rho_i$; we suppress pullbacks from the notation. Every holomorphic form on a complex torus has constant coefficients. On each factor, the $L^2$ pairing of $\rho_i=\partial v_i$ with a holomorphic two-form is a multiple of their wedge pairing, which vanishes by Stokes' theorem. On the product, holomorphic two-forms pulled back from the first factor, those pulled back from the second factor and mixed ones are mutually pointwise orthogonal, and the pairing of $\rho_i$ with a form pulled back from the same factor is the vanishing pairing on that factor times the volume of the other factor. Thus $\rho=\rho_1+\rho_2$ is the canonical completion of $\omega$. Since $\mu_i=\nu_i$ is half the pullback of $\mu_0$, expansion gives $F(\omega)=\int_X(\nu_1\wedge\mu_2+\mu_1\wedge\nu_2)=V_0^2/2$.

Put $\Theta_i=\alpha_i+\rho_i+\bar\rho_i$ and let $\Theta=\Theta_1+\Theta_2$, which is closed. For an arbitrary real $(1,1)$-form $\psi$ and $(2,0)$-form $\sigma$ on $X$, expansion on the two factors gives
\[
 \operatorname{Re}(\sigma\wedge\bar\rho\wedge\omega^2)
   +\rho\wedge\bar\rho\wedge\psi\wedge\omega
 =\frac1{12}(\psi+\sigma+\bar\sigma)\wedge\Theta^3.
\]
Indeed, the mixed components of $\psi$ and $\sigma$ have odd degree on both factors and contribute zero. The pure components on the left give $(\psi+\sigma+\bar\sigma)\wedge(\Theta_1\wedge\mu_2+\Theta_2\wedge\mu_1)$, and since $\Theta_i^2=4\mu_i$, the cube on the right is twelve times the same complementary form. Apply \cref{lem:completion-first-variation} with $\psi=\partial\bar\tau+\bar\partial\tau$ and $\sigma=\partial\tau$. The derivative is $\frac1{12}\int_X\mathrm d(\tau+\bar\tau)\wedge\Theta^3=0$, since $\Theta$ is closed. This proves criticality under every Aeppli variation.

Each $\partial\alpha_i=-\bar\partial\rho_i$ is nonzero, so $\omega$ is not K\"ahler. In $\partial\omega\wedge\bar\partial\omega$, the terms coming from a single factor vanish for dimension reasons, while the two cross terms are nonzero and have different bidegrees on the factors, so they cannot cancel. Hence $\mathrm d\omega\wedge\dc\omega\ne0$, and since $\ddc\omega=0$, \cref{lem:power-torsion} gives $\ddc(\omega^2)\ne0$. It remains to apply \cref{thm:completion-potential-hessian}.
\end{proof}

If only one factor carries a nonclosed form, the energy becomes constant along potential variations, while the same explicit family shows that it still varies in the Aeppli class.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:torus-flat-nonstationary}\index{Completion energy!positive flat leaf}\index{Complex torus!invariant mass}
For $0<s<1$, let $\widehat\omega_s$ on $X=S\times S$ be the sum of the pullbacks of $\alpha_s$ from the first factor and of $\eta$ from the second, and set $V_0=\int_S\mu_0$. Then $\mathrm d\widehat\omega_s\ne0$ and
\[
 F(\widehat\omega_s)=s^2V_0^2,\qquad
 V(\widehat\omega_s)=(1-s^2)V_0^2.
\]
The energy is constant on all positive smooth potential variations of $\widehat\omega_s$, and every bounded $\widehat\omega_s$-plurisubharmonic function has mass $24(1-s^2)V_0^2$. The form $\widehat\omega_s$ is not a critical point of $F$ in its Aeppli class, and its Monge--Amp\`ere one-form has no primitive on any $C^2$-open neighborhood of zero.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The pullback of $\rho_s$ from the first factor is a completion of $\widehat\omega_s$. It is canonical: for the product metric it is pointwise orthogonal to the holomorphic two-forms pulled back from the second factor and to the mixed ones, and its pairing with those pulled back from the first factor vanishes by the Stokes argument in the proof of \cref{ex:torus-completion-critical}. The energy is therefore $\int_X\rho_s\wedge\bar\rho_s\wedge\eta^2/2=s^2V_0^2$. The volume is the product of the two surface volumes, which gives the second formula.

The exterior derivatives of $\widehat\omega_s$ come entirely from the first factor. Their wedge product $\mathrm d\widehat\omega_s\wedge\dc\widehat\omega_s$ vanishes for degree reasons, while $\ddc\widehat\omega_s=0$. Thus $\ddc(\widehat\omega_s^2)=0$ by \cref{lem:power-torsion}, and \cref{cor:completion-flat-invariant-mass} gives the constancy of the energy and the common bounded mass.

The forms $\widehat\omega_{(1+t)s}$ are positive for small $|t|$ and lie in the Aeppli class of $\widehat\omega_s$, since $v_{(1+t)s}=(1+t)v_s$. The derivative of their energy at $t=0$ is $2s^2V_0^2>0$, so $\widehat\omega_s$ is not a critical point in its Aeppli class. Finally, $\partial\alpha_s=-\bar\partial\rho_s\ne0$. Hence $\mathrm d\widehat\omega_s\ne0$, and \cref{thm:raw-primitive} shows that no primitive exists.
\end{proof}
