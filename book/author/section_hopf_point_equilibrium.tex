\section{A point pole on the Hopf manifold}
\label{sec:hopf-point-equilibrium}

On the Hopf manifold the comparison of \cref{sec:fiber-point-kernels} can be made completely explicit. A single point pole selects the homogeneous polynomials whose degree in affine coordinates centered at the image point is bounded below, and summing the squared norms of the normalized monomials expresses the kernel as a binomial tail. The exponential rate of this tail determines the limit of the Fubini--Study potentials, and its concentration determines the limit of the measures. The limit potential has a pole along the entire fiber through the original pole, which makes the loss of the point singularity explicit.

We keep the notation of \cref{sec:partial-hopf-equilibrium}, with $q=n-1$, so that $\pi\colon X_n\to\mathbb P^q$. Use $L=L_1=\pi^*\mathcal O(1)$ with its pulled-back Fubini--Study metric and the probability volume $\nu_X=\omega^n/(2n)$, which is the lift of $\sigma^q$ for the fiber probabilities $\zeta|_{X_y}/2$. Let $p\in X_n$ lie over $b=[1:0:\cdots:0]$. Suppose that $\varphi$ has a single logarithmic pole at $p$, with coefficient $0<a<1$, as in \eqref{eq:isolated-selecting-poles}, and is locally bounded elsewhere. The continuous weight is zero. In homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots:Z_q]$ on $\mathbb P^q$, set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 t([Z_0:\cdots:Z_q])=
       \frac{|Z_1|^2+\cdots+|Z_q|^2}{|Z_0|^2+\cdots+|Z_q|^2},\\
 r_m=\max\{\lfloor ma\rfloor-q,0\},
 \qquad D_m=\binom{m+q}{q}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Thus $t=0$ exactly at $b$. Define the function $e_a$ on $[0,1]$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-equilibrium-function}
 e_a(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 a\log(t/a)+(1-a)\log((1-t)/(1-a)),&0<t<a,\\
 0,&a\leq t\leq1,\\
 -\infty,&t=0,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and put $E_a=e_a\circ t$ on $\mathbb P^q$. For $q=1$ this is the function $\Gamma_a$ of \eqref{eq:pone-explicit-model}; here it arises from a point pole on the total space of a fibration. We write $B_{X_n,m}=B_{m,0,\varphi,\nu_X}$ for the partial Bergman kernel.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hopf-point-binomial-kernel}\index{Bergman kernel!point pole}
The kernel and the dimension of the selected spaces are
\[
 B_{X_n,m}=D_m\left(
          \sum_{j=r_m}^{m}\binom mj t^j(1-t)^{m-j}\right)\circ\pi,
 \qquad
 N_m=D_m-\binom{r_m+q-1}{q},
\]
where the last binomial coefficient is zero when $r_m=0$. In particular, all selected spaces are nonzero, and
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{q!N_m}{m^q}=1-a^q>0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $n=q+1$, \cref{prop:isolated-pole-section-descent} identifies the selected space with the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $m$ on $\mathbb C^{q+1}$ that vanish to order at least $r_m$ at $b$. For $\ell=(\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_q)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{q+1}$ write $|\ell|=\sum_i\ell_i$ and $Z^\ell=\prod_iZ_i^{\ell_i}$. In the affine chart $Z_0=1$, the vanishing order of $Z^\ell$ at $b$ is $\ell_1+\cdots+\ell_q$, so the selected space is spanned by the monomials $Z^\ell$ with $|\ell|=m$ and $\ell_1+\cdots+\ell_q\geq r_m$.

Distinct monomials are orthogonal, by invariance under the coordinate circle actions. Their squared norms with respect to $\sigma^q$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projective-monomial-norm}
 \int_{\mathbb P^q}\frac{|Z^\ell|^2}{|Z|^{2m}}\,\sigma^q
       =\frac{q!\,\ell_0!\cdots\ell_q!}{(m+q)!},
 \qquad |\ell|=m.
\end{equation}
To verify this, integrate $|z^\ell|^2\mathrm{e}^{-|z|^2}$ over $\mathbb C^{q+1}$ in two ways. As a product of one-variable integrals it equals $\pi^{q+1}\prod_i\ell_i!$, while in polar coordinates it is the average of $|z^\ell|^2$ over the unit sphere times a radial integral that contributes the factor $(m+q)!$. Dividing by the same computation for $\ell=0$ gives the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:projective-monomial-norm} as the normalized sphere average, and the pushforward of the normalized sphere measure to $\mathbb P^q$ is $\sigma^q$, since both are the probability measures invariant under the unitary group. This proves \eqref{eq:projective-monomial-norm}.

The reciprocal of the squared norm is $D_mm!/(\ell_0!\cdots\ell_q!)$. Summing first over the monomials with $\ell_1+\cdots+\ell_q=j$, the multinomial formula gives the contribution
\[
 D_m\binom mj t^j(1-t)^{m-j}
\]
to the kernel on $\mathbb P^q$. Since $\nu_X$ is the lift of $\sigma^q$, the norms and kernels lift exactly to $X_n$, which proves the kernel formula. There are $\binom{j+q-1}{q-1}$ monomials of affine degree $j$, and summing over the excluded degrees $0\leq j<r_m$ gives $\binom{r_m+q-1}{q}$. Since $0\leq r_m\leq m$ and $r_m/m\to a$, this proves the dimension formula, the nonvanishing and the limit.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hopf-point-potential-limit}
Let $U_m=m^{-1}\log B_{X_n,m}$ be the partial Fubini--Study potential for the zero weight. Then
\[
 U_m\longrightarrow E_a\circ\pi
\]
in $L^1(X_n)$, pointwise as extended-real functions, and locally uniformly off the fiber $\pi^{-1}(b)$. The normalized kernel measures converge in total variation:
\[
 \frac{q!}{m^q}B_{X_n,m}\,\nu_X
       \longrightarrow
       \mathbf1_{\{t\circ\pi>a\}}\nu_X.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $P_m(t)$ for the binomial sum in \cref{prop:hopf-point-binomial-kernel}. For fixed $0<t<1$, $P_m(t)$ is the probability that a binomial random variable with parameters $(m,t)$ is at least $r_m$. Its variance is $mt(1-t)$, so Chebyshev's inequality and $r_m/m\to a$ give
\[
 P_m(t)\longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}0,&t<a,\\1,&t>a.\end{cases}
\]
The pushforward of $\sigma^q$ under $t$ is $q t^{q-1}\,\mathrm{d}t$. To see this, note that in the affine chart the Fubini--Study measure has radial density
$2q r^{2q-1}(1+r^2)^{-q-1}\,\mathrm{d}r$, and $t=r^2/(1+r^2)$. In particular, the level set $\{t=a\}$ has measure zero. Since $0\leq P_m\leq1$ and $q!D_m/m^q\to1$, dominated convergence gives convergence of the densities in $L^1(\sigma^q)$, hence in $L^1(\nu_X)$ after lifting; this is the asserted convergence in total variation. The limit has mass $\int_a^1 qt^{q-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=1-a^q$.

To identify the limit of the potentials, put
\[
 H(s)=-s\log s-(1-s)\log(1-s),\qquad 0\leq s\leq1,
\]
with $0\log0=0$. Then
\[
 \frac{\mathrm{e}^{mH(j/m)}}{m+1}\leq\binom mj
                   \leq \mathrm{e}^{mH(j/m)}\quad(0\leq j\leq m).
\]
Indeed, $\binom mj\mathrm{e}^{-mH(j/m)}$ is the $j$th term of the binomial distribution with parameters $(m,j/m)$. It is at most one, and it is at least $1/(m+1)$ because, as the ratio of neighboring terms shows, it is a largest of the $m+1$ terms, which sum to one. For $j=0$ and $j=m$ it equals one.

The logarithm of a sum of at most $m+1$ nonnegative terms differs from the largest of their logarithms by at most $\log(m+1)$. Hence $m^{-1}\log P_m(t)$ differs by at most $\log(m+1)/m$ from
\[
 \max_{r_m\leq j\leq m}
 \left\{H(j/m)+(j/m)\log t+(1-j/m)\log(1-t)\right\}.
\]
On compact subintervals of $(0,1)$, uniform continuity and $r_m/m\to a$ show that these maxima converge uniformly to the maximum of the same expression over $a\leq s\leq1$. Maximizing in $s$, we find that this maximum is zero for $t\geq a$, attained at $s=t$, and equals the first expression in \eqref{eq:point-equilibrium-function} for $t<a$, attained at $s=a$. Thus the limit is $e_a(t)$, also at $t=a$.

The convergence is also uniform near $t=1$. Choose $c$ with $a<c<1$. The same variance estimate gives $P_m(t)\geq1-C/m$ uniformly for $c\leq t\leq1$ and large $m$, so $m^{-1}\log P_m\to0$ uniformly there. Together with the argument on compact subintervals and $m^{-1}\log D_m\to0$, this proves locally uniform convergence off $\{t=0\}$. At $t=0$ we have $r_m>0$ for large $m$, so $P_m(0)=0$ and the pointwise limit is $-\infty$.

Finally, $t^m\leq P_m(t)\leq1$, because the term $j=m$ occurs in every binomial sum. Since $m^{-1}\log D_m$ is nonnegative and bounded, it follows that $\log(t\circ\pi)\leq U_m\leq C$ off the fiber over $b$. In local submersion coordinates near this fiber, $\log(t\circ\pi)=\log|z|^2+O(1)$, which is integrable against a smooth volume. The pointwise convergence and these bounds give convergence in $L^1$, by dominated convergence.
\end{proof}

For rational $a$, the function $E_a$ is the partial envelope of a point pole on the base. Put $Z'=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_q)$ and $\psi_a=a\log t$ on $\mathbb P^q$. For $0<a<1$ this function is $\sigma$-plurisubharmonic. Indeed, away from $b$ we have $\sigma+\ddc\psi_a=(1-a)\sigma+a\sigma'$, where $\sigma'$ is the semipositive pullback of the Fubini--Study form under the projection $[Z_0:Z']\mapsto[Z']$ (and $\sigma'=0$ if $q=1$); near $b$, the function $\psi_a$ is $a\log|z|^2$ plus a smooth function, so the positivity extends across $b$.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hopf-point-envelope}
The function $E_a$ belongs to $\PSH(\mathbb P^q,\sigma)$, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\sigma(E_a)=\mathbf1_{\{t>a\}}\sigma^q,
 \qquad
 \mathbf1_{\{t\circ\pi>a\}}\nu_X
       =\frac1{2n}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(E_a\circ\pi).
\]
If $a$ is rational, then $E_a=E_{\sigma,\mathbb P^q}[\psi_a]_{\mathcal I}(0)$, and $E_a\circ\pi$ is the largest $u\in\PSH(X_n,\alpha)$ with $u\leq0$ and $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}\varphi$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the chart $Z_0=1$ put $z=Z'/Z_0$ and $R^2=a/(1-a)$. For $|z|<R$, the local potential of $\sigma+\ddc E_a$ is
\[
 \log(1+|z|^2)+E_a
       =a\log|z|^2-a\log a-(1-a)\log(1-a).
\]
Its complex Hessian is semipositive and, away from $z=0$, of rank $q-1$. For $|z|>R$ we have $E_a=0$, so the form is $\sigma$. The two expressions for $E_a$ have equal values and equal first derivatives on the sphere $|z|=R$, so they glue to a $C^{1,1}$ function near this sphere, whose distributional Hessian has no part carried by the sphere. Near $b$, $E_a$ has a logarithmic pole of coefficient $a$. This proves that $E_a$ is $\sigma$-plurisubharmonic and that $E_a\sim\psi_a$.

Off $b$ the local potentials of $\sigma+\ddc E_a$ are $C^{1,1}$. By \cref{prop:c11-products}, their Bedford--Taylor top products are given by the determinants of their complex Hessians almost everywhere. By the rank computation, this determinant vanishes where $t<a$, while for $t>a$ the measure is $\sigma^q$. The sphere $\{t=a\}$ has volume zero, and a $C^{1,1}$ local potential puts no mass on it. Finally, the non-pluripolar measure does not charge the point $b$. This proves the measure formula. Since $E_a\in\PSH(\mathbb P^q,\sigma)$, \cref{thm:hopf-descended-hermitian-ma} and the measure formula on the base give the identity on $X_n$.

Now let $a$ be rational. For the envelope identity, let $w\in\PSH(\mathbb P^q,\sigma)$ satisfy $w\leq0$ and $w\preceq_{\mathcal I}\psi_a$. Since $\psi_a$ has analytic singularities, \cref{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance} gives $w\leq\psi_a+C$. Fix a complex line through $0$ in the affine chart. If the restriction of $w+\log(1+|z|^2)$ to this line is identically $-\infty$, there is nothing to prove on the line; otherwise the restriction is subharmonic. On the punctured disk of radius $R$ in the line, subtract $a\log|z|^2$. The difference is subharmonic and, since $w\leq\psi_a+C$, bounded above near $0$, so it extends subharmonically across $0$. On the boundary circle it is at most $\log(1+R^2)-a\log R^2=-a\log a-(1-a)\log(1-a)$, because $w\leq0$. By the maximum principle and the formula for $\log(1+|z|^2)+E_a$ on $\{|z|<R\}$, $w\leq E_a$ on the disk. Every point with $|z|<R$ lies on such a line, and for $|z|\geq R$ we have $w\leq0=E_a$. Since $E_a$ is itself a competitor, it is the envelope. For the continuous obstacle zero, the quasi-everywhere and the pointwise obstacle conditions agree, as recalled in \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}.

For the assertion on $X_n$, every $\alpha$-plurisubharmonic function has the form $u=w\circ\pi$ with $w\in\PSH(\mathbb P^q,\sigma)$, by \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion}. Since $\varphi$ has analytic singularities, $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}\varphi$ implies $u\preceq\varphi$ by \cref{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance}. Restricting this inequality to $\tau=0$ in product coordinates $(z,\tau)$ at $p$ gives $w(z)\leq a\log|z|^2+C$, and hence $w\preceq\psi_a$ on $\mathbb P^q$. Conversely, if $w\preceq\psi_a$, then
\[
 w(z)\leq a\log|z|^2+C
             \leq a\log(|z|^2+|\tau|^2)+C
\]
near $p$. Away from $p$, the function $\varphi$ is locally bounded and $w$ is bounded above, so by compactness the inequality $w\circ\pi\leq\varphi+C$ holds globally. Thus $w\circ\pi\preceq\varphi$, which implies $w\circ\pi\preceq_{\mathcal I}\varphi$. Since \cref{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance} also applies on the base, the map $u=w\circ\pi\mapsto w$ identifies the competitors on $X_n$ with the competitors for $E_a$ considered above. Their largest members therefore correspond under pullback.
\end{proof}

For sufficiently small $a$, \cref{lem:small-isolated-log-poles} gives a function $\varphi\in\PSH(X_n,\omega)$ whose only pole is at $p$. In particular, \cref{thm:hopf-point-potential-limit} applies on the Hopf threefold $X_3$, which does not have the bounded mass property, and gives a nonzero limit measure with the normalization $2/m^2$. The limit potential $E_a\circ\pi$ has a pole along the whole elliptic fiber over $b$, whereas $\varphi$ is locally bounded at the other points of this fiber, so the multiplier ideals $\mathcal I(mE_a\circ\pi)$ are proper there for large $m$ while those of $\varphi$ are not. This makes explicit the loss of information described at the end of \cref{sec:fiber-point-kernels}.

\enlargethispage{2\baselineskip}
The limit measure also differs from the Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere measure of $\varphi$ itself. In the construction of \cref{lem:small-isolated-log-poles}, choose $\varphi$ to vanish outside a small neighborhood of $p$. A neighborhood of another point of the fiber over $b$ is then an open set outside this neighborhood on which $t\circ\pi<a$. There $\varphi=0$, so $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(\varphi)=\omega^n$ by locality, whereas the limit of the kernel measures vanishes. The identity in \cref{prop:hopf-point-envelope} concerns the envelope $E_a\circ\pi$, not the selecting potential $\varphi$.
