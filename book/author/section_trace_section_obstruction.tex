\section{Restricted sections and trace data}

A nonzero restricted section has the vanishing orders imposed by the image ideal. Removing finitely many of these orders leaves an effective divisor. As the section degree tends to infinity, normalized compactness gives a positive current in the residual class determined by the intrinsic trace data. Pairing this current with a Gauduchon metric yields the degree obstruction. Here $c_1$ denotes the real Bott--Chern first Chern class.

Let $Y\subset X$ be smooth, compact and connected, of positive dimension $r$, with $\nu(u,Y)=0$. Use the image ideals $\mathfrak a_m=\mathfrak a_m(u;Y)$ of \cref{sec:restricted-ideals} and put
\[
 V_m=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\mathcal H_m(u)\longrightarrow H^0(Y,L^m|_Y)\bigr).
\]
Thus $V_m\subseteq H^0(Y,L^m|_Y\otimes\mathfrak a_m)$; the right-hand space can contain sections that do not extend to $X$.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gauduchon-trace-section-obstruction}
Suppose $V_m\neq0$ for arbitrarily large integers $m$. Let $\pi:Z\to Y$ be any smooth compact modification, let $E_1,\ldots,E_q$ be distinct prime divisors on $Z$, and let $\gamma$ be a Gauduchon metric on $Z$. Then
\[
 \deg_\gamma(\pi^*(L|_Y))
 \geq\sum_{i=1}^q\tau_{Y,u}(E_i)\int_{E_i}\gamma^{r-1}.
\]
Moreover the real closed $(1,1)$ class
\[
 c_1(\pi^*(L|_Y))-
       \sum_{i=1}^q\tau_{Y,u}(E_i)c_1(\mathcal O_Z(E_i))
\]
is pseudoeffective: it admits a closed positive current.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nonzero $s_m\in V_m$ along an unbounded sequence of degrees, and put $b_i(m)=\operatorname{ord}_{E_i}\mathfrak a_m$. Its pullback is nonzero, and its local coefficients belong to the pulled-back image ideal. Thus
\[
 D_m:=\operatorname{div}(\pi^*s_m)-\sum_{i=1}^q b_i(m)E_i
 \geq0.
\]
Choose smooth metrics on $\mathcal O_Z(E_i)$ with curvatures $\beta_i$, and set
\[
 \Theta_m=\pi^*(\beta|_Y)-\sum_{i=1}^q\frac{b_i(m)}m\beta_i,
 \qquad
 \Theta=\pi^*(\beta|_Y)-\sum_{i=1}^q\tau_{Y,u}(E_i)\beta_i.
\]
Dividing by the canonical divisor sections gives a nonzero holomorphic section of $\pi^*(L^m|_Y)\otimes\mathcal O_Z(-\sum_i b_i(m)E_i)$ with divisor $D_m$. Equip this bundle with the induced quotient metric. The logarithm of the squared norm of this section, divided by $m$, is a global quasi-plurisubharmonic function $w_m$ satisfying the Poincar\'e--Lelong identity
\[
 \Theta_m+\ddc w_m=\frac1m[D_m]\geq0.
\]
The full integer trace limit in \cref{thm:intrinsic-trace-profile} gives $\Theta_m\to\Theta$ smoothly, also along the selected degrees. One Hermitian form $\Lambda$ on $Z$ dominates all $\Theta_m$. Normalize $\sup_Zw_m=0$ and apply \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} in $\PSH(Z,\Lambda)$ to obtain an $L^1$-convergent subsequence. Its limit $w$ satisfies
\[
 \Theta+\ddc w\geq0.
\]
Since $\Theta$ is closed, this positive current proves the asserted pseudoeffectivity in every dimension.

Finally, pair it with $\gamma^{r-1}$. Stokes removes $\ddc w$, while Poincar\'e--Lelong gives $\int_Z\beta_i\wedge\gamma^{r-1}=\int_{E_i}\gamma^{r-1}$. The resulting nonnegative pairing is exactly the stated degree inequality.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, a strict violation of one of these finite Gauduchon inequalities forces $V_m=0$ for all sufficiently large $m$. Otherwise an unbounded sequence of nonzero restricted spaces would contradict the theorem.

The necessary condition has no general converse. On the elliptic curve $E=\C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm{i}\mathbb Z)$, choose an irrational real number $a$ and the flat line bundle with multipliers $\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i} a}$ and $1$ on the two generators. Its constant metric has curvature zero. Take $u=0$ and $Y=X=E$, so every trace number is zero and the displayed pseudoeffectivity condition holds. Nevertheless every positive tensor power has no nonzero holomorphic section. To see this, a hypothetical section of a power has a zero divisor of degree zero and hence is nowhere zero. Its logarithmic norm is harmonic on $E$, and is constant. The lifted holomorphic function therefore has constant modulus and is constant on $\C$. The nontrivial multiplier $\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i} ma}$ forces that constant to be zero, a contradiction.
