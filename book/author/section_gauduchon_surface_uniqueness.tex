\section{Gauduchon metrics and the cofactor}
\label{sec:gauduchon-surface-uniqueness}

The weighted cofactor condition above concerns a particular pair of solutions. The unweighted integration identity shows how the derivatives of the equation background enter it. On a surface, a Gauduchon metric cancels the error for every smooth pair.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mixed-ddc-uniqueness}
Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, let $\omega$ be Hermitian, and let $u,v$ be smooth $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions with
\[
 A^n=B^n=f\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad
 A=\omega+\ddc u,\quad B=\omega+\ddc v,
\]
where $\mathrm{d}V$ is smooth and strictly positive. Put
\[
 w=u-v,\qquad
 T=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j\wedge B^{n-1-j}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-uniqueness-defect}
 \int_X \mathrm{d}w\wedge\dc w\wedge T
       =\frac12\int_X w^2\ddc T.
\end{equation}
If $\ddc T=0$ and $\{f>0\}$ is dense in $X$, then $u-v$ is constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The difference of powers factors as
\[
 0=A^n-B^n=\ddc w\wedge T.
\]
The product rule gives
\[
 \ddc(w^2)=2w\ddc w+2\mathrm{d}w\wedge\dc w.
\]
Integrating against the smooth form $T$ and applying Stokes twice yields
\[
 \int_X\ddc(w^2)\wedge T=\int_Xw^2\ddc T.
\]
Substitution proves \eqref{eq:mixed-uniqueness-defect}.

For the uniqueness conclusion, apply \cref{uni-thm:cofactor-intro}(1) with $h=1$. At every point with $f>0$, the form $B$ is positive definite, so the term $B^{n-1}$ makes the cofactor strictly positive. Its positivity locus is therefore dense, as required.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gauduchon-surface-uniqueness}
Let $X$ be a connected compact complex surface and let $\omega$ be a Gauduchon metric. If $f\geq0$ is smooth and its zero set has empty interior, then two smooth $\omega$-plurisubharmonic solutions, both normalized to have supremum zero, of
\[
 (\omega+\ddc u)^2=cf\,\mathrm{d}V
\]
with the same $c>0$ coincide.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In dimension two, $T=A+B$ and
\[
 \ddc T=2\ddc\omega=0,
\]
which is exactly the Gauduchon condition on a surface. Smooth nonnegative $f$ with zero set of empty interior is positive on a dense set. The proposition gives equality up to a constant; normalization removes that constant.
\end{proof}

This proof uses the Gauduchon condition on the equation background. The different argument in \cref{uni-thm:sobolev,uni-cor:dense-lowdim} will remove that condition on a surface, using a signed quadratic difference and Stokes. The conformal theorem itself cannot make that change: replacing the metric conformally changes the equation.

In dimension three, direct expansion gives
\[
 \ddc T=3\ddc(\omega^2)+3\ddc\omega\wedge\ddc(u+v).
\]
The Gauduchon condition cancels the first term but need not cancel the second. For a concrete check, on the complex three-torus write $e_j=\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z_j$, $x_j=\operatorname{Re}z_j$, and set
\[
 H=2+\cos(2\pi x_1),\qquad
 \omega=e_1+H e_2+H^{-1}e_3.
\]
The expansion
$\omega^2=2H e_1\wedge e_2+2H^{-1}e_1\wedge e_3+2e_2\wedge e_3$
has $\ddc\omega^2=0$: differentiating either variable coefficient adds a repeated $e_1$. Take $u=v=\delta\cos(2\pi x_2)$ with nonzero $\delta$ sufficiently small. Then $A=B>0$, but
\[
 \ddc T=6\ddc\omega\wedge\ddc u\ne0,
\]
because the $e_1\wedge e_3$ coefficient of $\ddc\omega$ and the $e_2$ coefficient of $\ddc u$ are both nonzero at $x_1=x_2=0$. This is a counterexample to automatic $\ddc$-closedness of $T$, not a nonuniqueness example. In general the right side of \eqref{eq:mixed-uniqueness-defect} is the integration error that must be controlled.
