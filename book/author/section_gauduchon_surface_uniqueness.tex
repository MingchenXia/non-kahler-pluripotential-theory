\subsection{The unweighted energy defect}
\label{sec:gauduchon-surface-uniqueness}

Integration against $w^2$ also measures the obstruction to using the unweighted cofactor, that is, the case $h=1$. For smooth potentials, the error is exactly the pairing of $w^2$ with $\ddc T$. On a surface, the Gauduchon condition makes this error vanish for every pair of potentials; in dimension three, $\ddc T$ contains an additional term depending on the potentials.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mixed-ddc-uniqueness}
Let $n\geq2$, and let $u,v$ be smooth $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions with $A^n=B^n=f\,\mathrm{d}V$ for a smooth function $f\geq0$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-uniqueness-defect}
 \int_X \mathrm{d}w\wedge\dc w\wedge T
       =\frac12\int_X w^2\ddc T.
\end{equation}
If $\ddc T=0$ and $\{f>0\}$ is dense in $X$, then $u-v$ is constant.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{uni-eq:linearization}, $\ddc w\wedge T=0$. Integrating \eqref{uni-eq:w2} against $T$, and applying Stokes' theorem twice to the left-hand side, yields
\[
 \int_X\ddc(w^2)\wedge T=\int_Xw^2\ddc T.
\]
Substitution proves \eqref{eq:mixed-uniqueness-defect}.

For the uniqueness assertion, apply \cref{uni-thm:cofactor-intro}(1) with $h=1$. At every point where $f>0$, the form $B$ is positive definite, so the term $B^{n-1}$ makes the cofactor $T$ strictly positive. The set where $T$ is strictly positive is therefore dense, as required.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gauduchon-surface-uniqueness}\index{Uniqueness!Gauduchon surface}
Assume $n=2$ and $\ddc\omega=0$. Let $f\geq0$ be smooth, with a zero set of empty interior, and let $c>0$. Then any two smooth $\omega$-plurisubharmonic solutions of
\[
 (\omega+\ddc u)^2=cf\,\mathrm{d}V
\]
normalized by $\sup_Xu=0$ coincide.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Here $T=A+B$ and $\ddc T=2\ddc\omega=0$. The set $\{f>0\}$ is dense, so the proposition, applied to the density $cf$, makes the difference of the two solutions constant, and the normalization makes this constant zero.
\end{proof}

In dimension three, direct expansion gives
\[
 \ddc T=3\ddc(\omega^2)+3\ddc\omega\wedge\ddc(u+v).
\]
The Gauduchon condition cancels the first term but need not cancel the second. For instance, take both potentials equal to the perturbation $sv$ in \cref{ex:gauduchon-persistence-torus}. The common form $A=B$ is positive and satisfies $\ddc(A^2)\ne0$, so $\ddc T=3\ddc(A^2)\ne0$. Thus even for two equal positive solution forms, the cofactor need not be $\ddc$-closed.
