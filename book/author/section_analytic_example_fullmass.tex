\section{Nonuniqueness in the minimal envelope class}

Uniqueness of the scalar is compatible with several normalized potentials. The smooth examples from \cref{uni-thm:analytic-example} lie on projective manifolds, where comparison with a K\"ahler form gives both positive lower mass and bounded upper mass. Their bounded potentials belong to the minimal envelope class.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:analytic-nonuniqueness-envelope-class}
In every complex dimension $n\geq3$, there is a projective manifold $X$, a smooth nonclosed Hermitian form $\omega$, and a nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$ such that
\[
 0<v_-(\omega)\leq v_+(\omega)<+\infty,
\]
but the equation
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)=c\nu,
 \qquad u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0),
 \qquad \sup_Xu=0,
\]
has at least two smooth solutions. Its only possible positive constant is $c=1$. Relative to a K\"ahler volume form on $X$, the measure $\nu$ has a real analytic density, positive off two connected smooth real hypersurfaces and vanishing quadratically along them.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the form and the two potentials of \cref{uni-thm:analytic-example}, and let $\Omega$ be the indicated K\"ahler form. Compactness gives $\varepsilon\Omega\leq\omega\leq C\Omega$ for positive constants $\varepsilon,C$. For every bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic $a$, positivity of bounded products and closed Stokes give
\[
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc a)^n
 \leq\int_X(C\Omega+\ddc a)^n
 =C^n\int_X\Omega^n.
\]
This proves bounded mass on $X$. Lower-volume monotonicity in \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity}, applied to the two zero potentials and the ordered forms $\varepsilon\Omega\leq\omega$, now gives
\[
 v_-(\omega)\geq v_-(\varepsilon\Omega)
 =\varepsilon^n\int_X\Omega^n>0.
\]
The same-type competitors of zero are exactly the bounded potentials, so these lower volumes are the stated infima.

The two smooth potentials belong to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0)$ by the bounded-potential observation in the proof of \cref{thm:general-measure-minimal}. Their extended measures equal the ordinary top powers. Their common measure $\nu$ is finite and ignores pluripolar sets. The construction gives $c=1$, while \cref{cor:relative-extended-exponential-order} makes this the unique possible positive scalar. The density and its zero set were computed in \cref{uni-thm:analytic-example}.
\end{proof}
