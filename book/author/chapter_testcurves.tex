\chapter{Test curves and Legendre duality}
\label{chap:hermitian-testcurves}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Fix $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$; thus $\varphi\leq0$ and $\sup_X\varphi=0$. Use a fixed smooth positive volume form for the $L^1(X)$ topology.

Legendre duality exchanges two maximality conditions. Each member of a test curve is fixed by its singularity envelope, while every finite segment of the corresponding ray is a Perron envelope. Replacing such a segment changes each inverse transform only by a bounded amount, so the envelope condition forces Perron maximality. Affine rooftop comparisons prove the converse. This argument uses convexity, the minimum principle and the Perron construction of \cref{chap:hermitian-rays}, and applies to nonclosed backgrounds as well as to K\"ahler forms.

We use the pointwise transforms of \cref{sec:psh-legendre}; the proofs follow the envelope arguments in \cite[section on the Ross--Witt Nystr\"om correspondence]{SGPT}.

\section{Envelope test curves and rays}

The parameter of a test curve measures increasing singularity. Its cutoff becomes the slope of the transformed ray, while its limit towards $-\infty$ gives the initial potential.

\begin{definition}\label{def:hermitian-envelope-testcurve}\index{Test curve}
An \emph{envelope test curve} with initial potential $\varphi$ consists of a real number $c$ and potentials $\Gamma_\tau\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ for $\tau<c$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\tau\mapsto\Gamma_\tau$ is pointwise concave and decreasing;
\item $P_\theta[\Gamma_\tau]=\Gamma_\tau$ for every $\tau<c$;
\item $(\sup_{\tau<c}\Gamma_\tau)^*=\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}
Its cutoff is $c$. We extend it by
\[
 \Gamma_c=\lim_{\tau\uparrow c}\Gamma_\tau,
 \qquad \Gamma_\tau\equiv-\infty\quad(\tau>c).
\]
\end{definition}

Each $\Gamma_\tau$ has supremum zero, so \cref{thm:normalized-compactness,cor:decreasing-limit} give $\Gamma_c\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:hermitian-envelope-ray}\index{Envelope ray}
An \emph{envelope ray} with initial potential $\varphi$ and slope $c$ is a subgeodesic $u_t$, $t>0$, such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $u_t\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$ as $t\downarrow0$, and every finite restriction, with $u_0=\varphi$, is a Perron segment;
\item $P_\theta[u_t]=\varphi$ for every $t>0$;
\item $\sup_Xu_t=ct$ for every $t>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Write $U_t$ for the forward transform \eqref{eq:hermitian-legendre-forward} and $\widehat u_\tau$ for the inverse transform \eqref{eq:hermitian-legendre-backward}. The forward transform is already a subgeodesic by \cref{thm:hermitian-legendre-usc}; its finite restrictions must still be shown to be Perron segments.

\section{Perron maximality}

The envelope normalization gives the transformed ray's slope and singularity envelope directly. To prove Perron maximality, replace the transform on a finite interval by its Perron segment. The estimate \eqref{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound} keeps each inverse transform in its original ordinary singularity type, where the envelope condition forces equality.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:testcurve-to-envelope-ray}\index{Legendre duality!test curves and rays}\index{Perron interpolation!Legendre correspondence}
The forward transform of an envelope test curve is an envelope ray with the same initial potential and cutoff slope. Moreover $U_t-ct$ decreases as $t$ increases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:hermitian-legendre-usc}, $U_t$ is a subgeodesic. The inequalities
\[
 \Gamma_\tau+t\tau\leq U_t\leq\varphi+ct
\]
give $\sup_XU_t\geq t\tau$ for each $\tau<c$ and $\sup_XU_t\leq ct$. Letting $\tau\uparrow c$ proves equality. The formula
\[
 U_t-ct=\sup_{\tau<c}\bigl(\Gamma_\tau-t(c-\tau)\bigr)
\]
proves decreasingness.

The order property of the singularity envelope gives
\[
 \Gamma_\tau=P_\theta[\Gamma_\tau]\leq P_\theta[U_t]
 \leq P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi.
\]
Taking the regularized supremum in $\tau$ proves $P_\theta[U_t]=\varphi$.

The local envelope theorem in \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} gives $\sup_{\tau<c}\Gamma_\tau=\varphi$ almost everywhere. At those points, the preceding bounds imply $U_t\to\varphi$ as $t\downarrow0$. For any fixed $\tau_0<c$ and $0<t\leq1$, they also give
\[
 \Gamma_{\tau_0}-|\tau_0|\leq U_t\leq\varphi+|c|.
\]
Both bounds are integrable, so dominated convergence proves the initial $L^1$ limit.

Fix $0\leq a<b$ and replace $U_t$ on $(a,b)$ by the Perron segment between $U_a,U_b$, using $U_0=\varphi$ when $a=0$. The restricted original path attains these endpoints, so \cref{prop:hermitian-perron} gives the replacement. Its joint boundary bounds permit gluing by \cref{lem:qpsh-gluing,prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}. The resulting subgeodesic $v_t$ dominates $U_t$ and equals it outside $(a,b)$. The endpoint chord on the replaced interval gives $v_t\leq ct$ everywhere.

For $\tau<c$, put $\chi_\tau=\inf_{t>0}(v_t-t\tau)$. The lower bound by $\Gamma_\tau$ and \cref{prop:hermitian-kiselman} give $\chi_\tau\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound}
 \Gamma_\tau\leq\chi_\tau
 \leq\Gamma_\tau+(b-a)(c-\tau).
\end{equation}
Indeed for $s>0$ with $s\in[a,b]$, the decreasingness of $U_t-ct$ gives
\[
 \chi_\tau\leq U_b-b\tau
 \leq U_s-s\tau+(b-s)(c-\tau).
\]
For $s$ outside the interval, use $v_s=U_s$ to obtain the same bound with $(b-a)(c-\tau)$. Taking the infimum over $s>0$ and using \cref{lem:testcurve-closed-fibers} proves \eqref{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound}.

Moreover $\chi_\tau\leq t(c-\tau)$ for every $t>0$, hence $\chi_\tau\leq0$. By \eqref{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound}, it has the ordinary type of $\Gamma_\tau$ and is therefore a competitor for $P_\theta[\Gamma_\tau]=\Gamma_\tau$. Thus $\chi_\tau=\Gamma_\tau$. Recovering $v_t$ by \cref{lem:halfline-legendre-duality} gives $v_t=U_t$, proving Perron maximality on every finite interval.
\end{proof}

\section{Recovering the singularity envelopes}

The minimum principle produces the inverse potentials, but their envelope property uses the whole ray. A rooftop under a translated ray is an admissible affine path on every interval $[0,M]$. Letting the interval length vary, and then shrinking the relative time, removes the rooftop's additive constant.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:envelope-ray-to-testcurve}\index{Legendre duality!inverse transform}
If $u_t$ is an envelope ray of slope $c$, its inverse transform is an envelope test curve with cutoff $c$ and initial potential $\varphi$. The forward and inverse transforms are mutually inverse bijections between the two classes in \cref{def:hermitian-envelope-testcurve,def:hermitian-envelope-ray}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:invariant-strip-convexity}, each vertical function $u_t(x)-ct$ is finite and convex, or identically $-\infty$. The upper bound by zero makes it decreasing. Since $\sup_X(u_t-ct)=0$, \cref{thm:normalized-compactness,cor:decreasing-limit} give
\[
 \widehat u_c=\inf_{t>0}(u_t-ct)\in\PSH(X,\theta).
\]
For $\tau<c$, we have $\widehat u_c\leq\widehat u_\tau\leq u_1-\tau$, so \cref{prop:hermitian-kiselman} gives $\widehat u_\tau\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Also
\[
 \widehat u_\tau\leq\inf_{t>0}t(c-\tau)=0
 \quad(\tau\leq c),
\]
while the same upper bound with $t\to\infty$ shows $\widehat u_\tau\equiv-\infty$ for $\tau>c$. Concavity and decreasingness in $\tau$ follow by taking an infimum of affine functions with slopes $-t$.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Fix $\tau<c$ and write $g=\widehat u_\tau$. The inequality $g\leq u_M-M\tau$, valid for every $M>0$, gives
\[
 P_\theta[g]\leq P_\theta[u_M]=\varphi.
\]
For $C>0$ put $q_C=P_\theta(g+C,0)$. It is genuine since $g\leq0$ is a competitor, and
\[
 g\leq q_C\leq P_\theta[g]\leq\varphi,
 \qquad q_C-C\leq g\leq u_M-M\tau.
\]
The affine path $q_C-Ct/M$ is admissible for $\varphi,u_M-M\tau$ on $[0,M]$. Adding $-t\tau$ gives a bijection of Perron competitor families by \cref{prop:hermitian-subgeodesic-calculus}, so the Perron segment for these endpoints is $u_t-t\tau$. For $0<s\leq1$ this yields
\[
 q_C-Cs\leq u_{sM}-sM\tau.
\]
Take the infimum in $M>0$ to get $q_C-Cs\leq g$, and then let $s\downarrow0$. Thus $q_C=g$ for every $C>0$. Taking its increasing envelope limit proves $P_\theta[g]=g$.

Let $\psi=(\sup_{\tau<c}\widehat u_\tau)^*\leq\varphi$. Monotonicity of $u_t-ct$ gives a pointwise limit at zero; an almost-everywhere convergent sequence from the $L^1$ endpoint condition identifies it with $\varphi$ almost everywhere. At these points, the endpoint identity in \cref{lem:halfline-legendre-duality} gives
\[
 \sup_{\tau<c}\inf_{t>0}(u_t(x)-t\tau)=\varphi(x).
\]
Therefore $\psi=\varphi$ by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.

As $\tau\uparrow c$, the transforms decrease to $\widehat u_c$: the infima over $t>0$ and $\tau<c$ commute. This gives the required cutoff convention. Finally, \cref{lem:halfline-legendre-duality} recovers $u_t$ from its inverse transforms, including at identically $-\infty$ vertical functions, and \cref{lem:testcurve-closed-fibers} recovers each test curve from its forward transform. Hence the correspondence is a bijection.
\end{proof}

\section{A logarithmic pole on the projective line}

Set $X=\mathbb P^1$ and $\varphi=0$. The envelope of a logarithmic pole is obtained by replacing part of a convex function by a tangent line. Its transform is a smooth ray whose bounded finite slices retain the pole through their long-time behavior. In the affine coordinate $z$, take
\[
 \theta=\ddc\log(1+|z|^2),\qquad
 g(z)=\log\frac{|z|^2}{1+|z|^2}.
\]
The form extends to $\mathbb P^1$ and has integral one. The potential $g$ has a logarithmic pole at zero and extends smoothly across infinity, with value zero there.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Put $H(\lambda)=-\lambda\log\lambda-(1-\lambda)\log(1-\lambda)$ for $0<\lambda<1$, with $H(0)=H(1)=0$. For $0<\lambda<1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pone-explicit-model}
 \Gamma_\lambda(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 \lambda\log|z|^2-\log(1+|z|^2)+H(\lambda),
       & |z|^2\leq\lambda/(1-\lambda),\\
 0,    & |z|^2\geq\lambda/(1-\lambda).
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Set $\Gamma_\lambda=0$ for $\lambda\leq0$, $\Gamma_1=g$, and $\Gamma_\lambda=-\infty$ for $\lambda>1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:pone-explicit-testcurve}\index{Projective line!logarithmic test curve}\index{Legendre duality!logarithmic pole}
This is an envelope test curve with initial potential zero and cutoff one. Its forward transform is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pone-explicit-ray}
 U_t(z)=\log\frac{1+\mathrm{e}^t|z|^2}{1+|z|^2},\qquad t>0.
\end{equation}
At $z=0$ its value is zero, and at infinity its value is $t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $0<\lambda<1$. For $s=\log|z|^2$ let $q_\lambda(s)=\Gamma_\lambda(z)+\log(1+\mathrm{e}^s)$. In the first region of \eqref{eq:pone-explicit-model} it is the affine function $\lambda s+H(\lambda)$, tangent to $\log(1+\mathrm{e}^s)$ at $s_\lambda=\log(\lambda/(1-\lambda))$. In the second region it is $\log(1+\mathrm{e}^s)$. Its derivative is continuous and nondecreasing at the joining point, so $q_\lambda$ is convex. By \cref{cor:radial-subharmonic-convexity}, $\Gamma_\lambda$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic away from zero. It extends across its logarithmic pole by \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}. It is zero near infinity and has ordinary type $\lambda g$.

To prove $\Gamma_\lambda=P_\theta[\lambda g]$, let $v\leq0$ be a competitor with $v\preceq\lambda g$. On the punctured disc $0<|z|^2<\lambda/(1-\lambda)$, the function
\[
 v-\Gamma_\lambda
 =v+\log(1+|z|^2)-\lambda\log|z|^2-H(\lambda)
\]
is subharmonic, since $v+\log(1+|z|^2)$ is subharmonic and $\log|z|^2$ is harmonic there. The singularity bound on $v$ makes this difference bounded above near zero, so it extends subharmonically across zero by \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}. Its upper boundary limit is at most zero, since $v\leq0$ and $\Gamma_\lambda=0$ on the boundary circle. The maximum principle gives $v\leq\Gamma_\lambda$ on the disc; at zero both have value $-\infty$. Outside the disc, the same inequality follows from $\Gamma_\lambda=0$. Thus every competitor lies below $\Gamma_\lambda$, which is itself a competitor. Ordinary-type invariance gives $P_\theta[\Gamma_\lambda]=\Gamma_\lambda$. Zero is also an envelope fixed point.

For a fixed $0<|z|<\infty$, put $p=|z|^2/(1+|z|^2)$. The function $\lambda\mapsto\Gamma_\lambda(z)$ is zero for $\lambda\leq p$. For $p<\lambda<1$ its derivative is
\[
 \log|z|^2+\log\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}.
\]
This starts at zero and decreases, proving concavity and decreasingness. At zero the curve is zero for $\lambda\leq0$ and $-\infty$ thereafter; at infinity it is zero for $\lambda\leq1$. These also satisfy the asserted conditions. Its limit towards $-\infty$ is zero, and its limit at one is $g$, proving that it is a test curve.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For finite nonzero $z$ and $t>0$, the maximum in the transform occurs in the second parameter region at
\[
 \lambda=\frac{\mathrm{e}^t|z|^2}{1+\mathrm{e}^t|z|^2}.
\]
Substitution gives \eqref{eq:pone-explicit-ray}. At zero only parameters $\lambda\leq0$ have finite values, giving zero; at infinity the supremum of $t\lambda$ for $\lambda<1$ is $t$. In the coordinate $w=1/z$, the formula is $\log((|w|^2+\mathrm e^t)/(|w|^2+1))$, so the ray is smooth in both charts.
\end{proof}

Each finite slice of this ray is bounded and has envelope zero, while its inverse transforms for $0<\lambda\leq1$ have logarithmic poles. At $z=0$, the parameters $\lambda\leq0$ determine the transform: omitting them would incorrectly give $U_t(0)=-\infty$.
