\chapter{Test curves and Legendre duality}
\label{chap:hermitian-testcurves}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Fix $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$, where $P_\theta[\cdot]$ is the ordinary singularity envelope of \cref{def:singularity-envelope}; thus $\varphi\leq0$ and $\sup_X\varphi=0$. A fixed smooth positive volume form defines the $L^1(X)$ topology.

Legendre duality in the parameter exchanges two maximality conditions: each member of a test curve is a fixed point of the singularity envelope, while every finite segment of the corresponding ray is a Perron segment in the sense of \cref{chap:hermitian-rays}. Replacing a finite segment of the ray by the Perron segment with the same endpoints changes each inverse transform only by a bounded amount, so the envelope condition forces Perron maximality. Conversely, comparison with affine paths built from obstacle envelopes shows that the inverse transforms of a ray of Perron segments are envelope fixed points. The argument uses only convexity, the minimum principle and the Perron construction, and therefore applies to nonclosed backgrounds as well as to K\"ahler forms.

The transforms are the pointwise Legendre transforms of \cref{sec:psh-legendre}, and the proofs follow the envelope arguments for the Ross--Witt Nystr\"om correspondence in \cite{SGPT}.

\section{Envelope test curves and rays}

The members of a test curve become more singular as the parameter increases. The cutoff of the curve becomes the slope of the transformed ray, while its limit as the parameter tends to $-\infty$ gives the initial potential.

\begin{definition}\label{def:hermitian-envelope-testcurve}\index{Test curve}
An \emph{envelope test curve} with initial potential $\varphi$ consists of a real number $c$ and potentials $\Gamma_\tau\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ for $\tau<c$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\tau\mapsto\Gamma_\tau$ is pointwise concave and decreasing;
\item $P_\theta[\Gamma_\tau]=\Gamma_\tau$ for every $\tau<c$;
\item $(\sup_{\tau<c}\Gamma_\tau)^*=\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}
Its cutoff is $c$. We extend it by
\[
 \Gamma_c=\lim_{\tau\uparrow c}\Gamma_\tau,
 \qquad \Gamma_\tau\equiv-\infty\quad(\tau>c).
\]
\end{definition}

Each $\Gamma_\tau$ is an envelope fixed point and hence has supremum zero, so \cref{thm:normalized-compactness,cor:decreasing-limit} give $\Gamma_c\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:hermitian-envelope-ray}\index{Envelope ray}
An \emph{envelope ray} with initial potential $\varphi$ and slope $c$ is a subgeodesic $u_t$, $t>0$, such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $u_t\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$ as $t\downarrow0$, and, with $u_0=\varphi$, the restriction of the ray to every interval $(a,b)$ with $0\leq a<b<\infty$ is the Perron segment from $u_a$ to $u_b$;
\item $P_\theta[u_t]=\varphi$ for every $t>0$;
\item $\sup_Xu_t=ct$ for every $t>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

For an envelope test curve, write $U_t$ for its forward transform \eqref{eq:hermitian-legendre-forward}, the pointwise supremum of $\Gamma_\tau+t\tau$ over $\tau<c$. For a family $(u_t)_{t>0}$, write $\widehat u_\tau$ for its inverse transform \eqref{eq:hermitian-legendre-backward}, the pointwise infimum of $u_t-t\tau$ over $t>0$. The forward transform is a subgeodesic by \cref{thm:hermitian-legendre-usc}; it remains to show that its finite segments are Perron segments.

\section{Perron maximality}

The slope and the singularity envelope of the transformed ray follow directly from the normalization of the test curve. To prove Perron maximality, we replace the transform on a finite interval by the Perron segment with the same endpoints. The estimate \eqref{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound} keeps each inverse transform in its original ordinary singularity type, where the envelope condition forces equality.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:testcurve-to-envelope-ray}\index{Legendre duality!test curves and rays}\index{Perron interpolation!Legendre correspondence}
The forward transform of an envelope test curve with cutoff $c$ is an envelope ray with the same initial potential and slope $c$. Moreover, $U_t-ct$ decreases as $t$ increases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:hermitian-legendre-usc}, $U_t$ is a subgeodesic. The inequalities
\[
 \Gamma_\tau+t\tau\leq U_t\leq\varphi+ct
\]
give $\sup_XU_t\geq t\tau$ for each $\tau<c$, since $\sup_X\Gamma_\tau=0$, and $\sup_XU_t\leq ct$. Letting $\tau\uparrow c$ proves $\sup_XU_t=ct$. The formula
\[
 U_t-ct=\sup_{\tau<c}\bigl(\Gamma_\tau-t(c-\tau)\bigr)
\]
shows that $U_t-ct$ is decreasing in $t$.

Since $\Gamma_\tau\preceq U_t\preceq\varphi$, monotonicity of the singularity envelope gives
\[
 \Gamma_\tau=P_\theta[\Gamma_\tau]\leq P_\theta[U_t]
 \leq P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi.
\]
Taking the regularized supremum in $\tau$ proves $P_\theta[U_t]=\varphi$.

The local envelope theorem in \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} gives $\sup_{\tau<c}\Gamma_\tau=\varphi$ almost everywhere, and at those points the bounds $\Gamma_\tau+t\tau\leq U_t\leq\varphi+ct$ imply $U_t\to\varphi$ as $t\downarrow0$. For any fixed $\tau_0<c$ and $0<t\leq1$, they also give
\[
 \Gamma_{\tau_0}-|\tau_0|\leq U_t\leq\varphi+|c|.
\]
Both bounds are integrable, so dominated convergence gives $U_t\to\varphi$ in $L^1$ as $t\downarrow0$.

It remains to prove Perron maximality. Fix $0\leq a<b$ and replace $U_t$ on $(a,b)$ by the Perron segment from $U_a$ to $U_b$, with $U_0=\varphi$ when $a=0$; it exists by \cref{prop:hermitian-perron}, because the restriction of $U_t$ itself runs between these endpoints. The joint upper limits of the Perron segment at $a$ and $b$ allow gluing by \cref{lem:qpsh-gluing,prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}. The resulting subgeodesic $v_t$ dominates $U_t$ and equals it outside $(a,b)$, where therefore $v_t\leq ct$. On $(a,b)$, the chord bound \eqref{eq:hermitian-chord-bound} together with $U_a\leq ca$ and $U_b\leq cb$ gives $v_t\leq ct$ as well.

For $\tau<c$, put $\chi_\tau=\inf_{t>0}(v_t-t\tau)$. Since $v_t-t\tau\geq U_t-t\tau\geq\Gamma_\tau$, \cref{prop:hermitian-kiselman} gives $\chi_\tau\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound}
 \Gamma_\tau\leq\chi_\tau
 \leq\Gamma_\tau+(b-a)(c-\tau).
\end{equation}
The first inequality was just noted. For the second, let $s>0$. If $s\in[a,b]$, then $v_b=U_b$, and since $U_t-ct$ is decreasing,
\[
 \chi_\tau\leq U_b-b\tau
 \leq U_s-s\tau+(b-s)(c-\tau).
\]
If $s\notin[a,b]$, then $\chi_\tau\leq v_s-s\tau=U_s-s\tau$. In both cases $\chi_\tau\leq U_s-s\tau+(b-a)(c-\tau)$. Taking the infimum over $s>0$ and using \cref{lem:testcurve-closed-fibers} proves \eqref{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound}.

Moreover, $v_t\leq ct$ gives $\chi_\tau\leq t(c-\tau)$ for every $t>0$, hence $\chi_\tau\leq0$. By \eqref{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound}, $\chi_\tau$ has the ordinary type of $\Gamma_\tau$, so it is a competitor for $P_\theta[\Gamma_\tau]=\Gamma_\tau$. Thus $\chi_\tau=\Gamma_\tau$. Recovering $v_t$ from its inverse transforms by \cref{lem:halfline-legendre-duality} gives $v_t=U_t$, which proves Perron maximality on every finite interval.
\end{proof}

\section{Recovering the singularity envelopes}

The minimum principle shows that the inverse transforms are $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, but their envelope property uses the whole ray. For an inverse transform $g$ and $C>0$, the envelope $P_\theta(g+C,0)$ of the obstacle $\min(g+C,0)$ gives an admissible affine path on every interval $[0,M]$, lying below the ray translated by an affine function of $t$. Letting the length $M$ vary, and then letting the relative time $t/M$ tend to zero, removes the additive constant $C$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:envelope-ray-to-testcurve}\index{Legendre duality!inverse transform}
If $u_t$ is an envelope ray of slope $c$ with initial potential $\varphi$, its inverse transform is an envelope test curve with cutoff $c$ and initial potential $\varphi$. The forward and inverse transforms are mutually inverse bijections between the envelope test curves and the envelope rays with initial potential $\varphi$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:invariant-strip-convexity}, for each $x$ the function $t\mapsto u_t(x)-ct$ is either finite and convex or identically $-\infty$. It is bounded above by zero, so it is decreasing. Since $\sup_X(u_t-ct)=0$, \cref{thm:normalized-compactness,cor:decreasing-limit} give
\[
 \widehat u_c=\inf_{t>0}(u_t-ct)\in\PSH(X,\theta).
\]
For $\tau<c$, we have $\widehat u_c\leq\widehat u_\tau\leq u_1-\tau$, so \cref{prop:hermitian-kiselman} gives $\widehat u_\tau\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Also
\[
 \widehat u_\tau\leq\inf_{t>0}t(c-\tau)=0
 \quad(\tau\leq c),
\]
while the bound $\widehat u_\tau\leq t(c-\tau)$ with $t\to\infty$ shows $\widehat u_\tau\equiv-\infty$ for $\tau>c$. Concavity and monotonicity in $\tau$ hold because $\widehat u_\tau$ is an infimum of affine functions of $\tau$ with negative slopes $-t$.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Fix $\tau<c$ and write $g=\widehat u_\tau$. The inequality $g\leq u_M-M\tau$, valid for every $M>0$, gives
\[
 P_\theta[g]\leq P_\theta[u_M]=\varphi.
\]
For $C>0$ put $q_C=P_\theta(g+C,0)$. Since $g\leq0$, the function $g$ is a competitor, so $q_C\not\equiv-\infty$, and
\[
 g\leq q_C\leq P_\theta[g]\leq\varphi,
 \qquad q_C-C\leq g\leq u_M-M\tau.
\]
The affine path $q_C-Ct/M$ is admissible for the endpoints $\varphi$ and $u_M-M\tau$ on $[0,M]$. Adding $-t\tau$ gives a bijection between the competitor families for the endpoints $\varphi,u_M$ and $\varphi,u_M-M\tau$, by \cref{prop:hermitian-subgeodesic-calculus}, so the Perron segment for the latter endpoints is $u_t-t\tau$. For $0<s\leq1$ this yields
\[
 q_C-Cs\leq u_{sM}-sM\tau.
\]
Taking the infimum over $M>0$ gives $q_C-Cs\leq g$, and letting $s\downarrow0$ gives $q_C\leq g$. Thus $q_C=g$ for every $C>0$, and letting $C\to\infty$ in the definition of $P_\theta[g]$ proves $P_\theta[g]=g$.

Let $\psi=(\sup_{\tau<c}\widehat u_\tau)^*$; since $\widehat u_\tau=P_\theta[\widehat u_\tau]\leq\varphi$, we have $\psi\leq\varphi$. Because $u_t-ct$ is decreasing in $t$, the limit of $u_t(x)$ as $t\downarrow0$ exists at every $x$, and an almost everywhere convergent sequence given by the $L^1$ convergence $u_t\to\varphi$ identifies it with $\varphi$ almost everywhere. At these points, the endpoint identity in \cref{lem:halfline-legendre-duality} gives
\[
 \sup_{\tau<c}\inf_{t>0}(u_t(x)-t\tau)=\varphi(x).
\]
Therefore $\psi=\varphi$ by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.

As $\tau\uparrow c$, the transforms decrease to $\widehat u_c$, since the infima over $t>0$ and over $\tau<c$ commute; this is the convention for the value at the cutoff. Finally, \cref{lem:halfline-legendre-duality} recovers $u_t$ from its inverse transforms, also at points where $t\mapsto u_t(x)$ is identically $-\infty$, and \cref{lem:testcurve-closed-fibers} recovers each test curve from its forward transform. Hence the correspondence is a bijection.
\end{proof}

\section{A logarithmic pole on the projective line}

Let $X=\mathbb P^1$ and $\varphi=0$. The envelope of a multiple of a logarithmic pole is obtained by replacing part of a convex function by a tangent line. The corresponding ray is smooth, and all its slices are bounded; the pole appears only in the behavior of the ray as $t\to\infty$. In the affine coordinate $z$, take
\[
 \theta=\ddc\log(1+|z|^2),\qquad
 g(z)=\log\frac{|z|^2}{1+|z|^2}.
\]
The form $\theta$ extends to the Fubini--Study form of $\mathbb P^1$, which has integral one. The potential $g$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, has a logarithmic pole at zero, and extends smoothly across infinity, with value zero there.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Put $H(\lambda)=-\lambda\log\lambda-(1-\lambda)\log(1-\lambda)$ for $0<\lambda<1$, with $H(0)=H(1)=0$. For $0<\lambda<1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pone-explicit-model}
 \Gamma_\lambda(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 \lambda\log|z|^2-\log(1+|z|^2)+H(\lambda),
       & |z|^2\leq\lambda/(1-\lambda),\\
 0,    & |z|^2\geq\lambda/(1-\lambda).
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Set $\Gamma_\lambda=0$ for $\lambda\leq0$, $\Gamma_1=g$, and $\Gamma_\lambda=-\infty$ for $\lambda>1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:pone-explicit-testcurve}\index{Projective line!logarithmic test curve}\index{Legendre duality!logarithmic pole}
The family $(\Gamma_\lambda)$ is an envelope test curve with initial potential zero and cutoff one. Its forward transform is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pone-explicit-ray}
 U_t(z)=\log\frac{1+\mathrm{e}^t|z|^2}{1+|z|^2},\qquad t>0.
\end{equation}
At $z=0$ its value is zero, and at infinity its value is $t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $0<\lambda<1$. For $s=\log|z|^2$, let $q_\lambda(s)=\Gamma_\lambda(z)+\log(1+\mathrm{e}^s)$. In the first region of \eqref{eq:pone-explicit-model} this is the affine function $\lambda s+H(\lambda)$, which is tangent to $\log(1+\mathrm{e}^s)$ at $s_\lambda=\log(\lambda/(1-\lambda))$, and in the second region it is $\log(1+\mathrm{e}^s)$. Its derivative is continuous at the junction and nondecreasing, so $q_\lambda$ is convex. By \cref{cor:radial-subharmonic-convexity}, $\Gamma_\lambda$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic away from zero, and it extends across its logarithmic pole by \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}. It vanishes near infinity and has the ordinary type of $\lambda g$.

To prove $\Gamma_\lambda=P_\theta[\lambda g]$, let $v\leq0$ be a competitor with $v\preceq\lambda g$. On the punctured disc $0<|z|^2<\lambda/(1-\lambda)$, the function
\[
 v-\Gamma_\lambda
 =v+\log(1+|z|^2)-\lambda\log|z|^2-H(\lambda)
\]
is subharmonic, since $v+\log(1+|z|^2)$ is subharmonic and $\log|z|^2$ is harmonic there. Since $v\preceq\lambda g$, this difference is bounded above near zero, so it extends subharmonically across zero by \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}. Its upper limits at the boundary circle are at most zero, since $v\leq0$ and $\Gamma_\lambda=0$ there. The maximum principle gives $v\leq\Gamma_\lambda$ on the disc (at zero both have the value $-\infty$), and outside the disc the same inequality follows from $\Gamma_\lambda=0$. Thus every competitor lies below $\Gamma_\lambda$, which is itself a competitor, so $\Gamma_\lambda=P_\theta[\lambda g]$. Since the envelope depends only on the ordinary type, also $P_\theta[\Gamma_\lambda]=\Gamma_\lambda$. The zero potential is an envelope fixed point as well.

For a fixed $0<|z|<\infty$, put $p=|z|^2/(1+|z|^2)$. The function $\lambda\mapsto\Gamma_\lambda(z)$ is zero for $\lambda\leq p$. For $p<\lambda<1$ its derivative is
\[
 \log|z|^2+\log\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}.
\]
It vanishes at $\lambda=p$ and decreases in $\lambda$, which proves that $\lambda\mapsto\Gamma_\lambda(z)$ is concave and decreasing. At $z=0$, the function is zero for $\lambda\leq0$ and $-\infty$ for $\lambda>0$; at $z=\infty$, it is zero for $\lambda\leq1$. These functions are concave and decreasing as well. Finally, $\sup_{\lambda<1}\Gamma_\lambda=0$, and the limit as $\lambda\uparrow1$ is $g$, so the family is an envelope test curve with cutoff one.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For finite nonzero $z$ and $t>0$, the supremum defining $U_t(z)$ is attained at a parameter $\lambda>p$, namely at
\[
 \lambda=\frac{\mathrm{e}^t|z|^2}{1+\mathrm{e}^t|z|^2}.
\]
and substituting this value into the first line of \eqref{eq:pone-explicit-model} gives \eqref{eq:pone-explicit-ray}. At $z=0$, only the parameters $\lambda\leq0$ give finite values, and the supremum is zero; at $z=\infty$, the supremum of $t\lambda$ over $\lambda<1$ is $t$. In the coordinate $w=1/z$, the formula reads $\log((|w|^2+\mathrm e^t)/(|w|^2+1))$, so the ray is smooth in both charts.
\end{proof}

Each slice of this ray is bounded and has envelope zero, whereas its inverse transforms for $0<\lambda\leq1$ have logarithmic poles. At $z=0$, the parameters $\lambda\leq0$ determine the transform: omitting them would give the wrong value $U_t(0)=-\infty$.
