\section{Normalization at exponent zero}

In this section the background is a Hermitian form $h$ with $v_-(h)>0$ and the reference is zero; thus $\theta=h$, $\varphi=0$, and $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ is the minimal envelope class. For nonzero $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, recall from \eqref{eq:individual-truncated-normalizer} that the subsolution threshold $b_h(f)$ is the limit of the normalizing constants of the truncations $\min(f,k)$; by \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}, it is the largest $a\geq0$ for which some $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)\geq af\,\mathrm{d}V$. We show that $b_h(f)$ is exactly the scalar of the normalized equation in the minimal envelope class: scalar comparison bounds every subsolution coefficient by this scalar. Dominated continuity also identifies the equation satisfied by every limit of normalized greatest exponential solutions; whether these limits coincide is a question about the potentials.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:positive-lower-mass-exact-threshold}\index{Normalization scalar!zero-exponent limit}
Let $h$ be a Hermitian form on $X$ with $v_-(h)>0$, and let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero. There is $v\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v)=b_h(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
The number $b_h(f)>0$ is the unique positive scalar for which this equation has a solution in that envelope class.

For $\lambda>0$, let $u_\lambda$ be the greatest solution of $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u_\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f\,\mathrm{d}V$ from \cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution}, and set $v_\lambda=u_\lambda-\sup_Xu_\lambda$. Each $u_\lambda$ belongs to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$. If $\lambda_j\downarrow0$ and $v_{\lambda_j}\to v$ in $L^1$, then the convergence holds in capacity, and $v$ belongs to the same envelope class and satisfies the equation above.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:general-measure-minimal}, applied with $\theta=h$, there are $c>0$ and $w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(w)=cf\,\mathrm{d}V$. If $s\in\PSH(X,h)$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(s)\geq af\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $a>0$, then
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(w)
       \leq\frac ca\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(s).
\]
Every $h$-plurisubharmonic $s$ satisfies $s\preceq0$, so the scalar assertion of \cref{cor:relative-extended-exponential-order}, with reference zero, gives $c/a\geq1$. Taking the maximum over all subsolution coefficients $a$, \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution} yields $b_h(f)\leq c$. The reverse inequality holds because $w$ itself is a subsolution with coefficient $c$. The same argument identifies the scalar of every solution in this envelope class with $b_h(f)$.

For $\lambda>0$, \cref{thm:general-measure-minimal} also gives a solution $s_\lambda$ of the exponential equation in the envelope class. Since $s_\lambda$ is in particular a subsolution, the greatest-solution property gives $s_\lambda\leq u_\lambda$. Upward stability (\cref{prop:relative-elementary}) places $u_\lambda$ and its translate $v_\lambda$ in that class.

Write $c_\lambda=\exp(\lambda\sup_Xu_\lambda)$. By \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential}, $c_\lambda\to b_h(f)>0$ and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v_\lambda)
       =c_\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda}f\,\mathrm{d}V
       \longrightarrow b_h(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V
       \quad\text{in total variation}.
\]
Since $v_\lambda\leq0$ and $c_\lambda$ stays bounded for small $\lambda$, these measures are dominated by one finite non-pluripolar measure $C f\,\mathrm{d}V$. Applying \cref{thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity} to the given $L^1$-convergent sequence $v_{\lambda_j}$, we obtain class membership, convergence in capacity and the equation for its limit.
\end{proof}

By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, every sequence $\lambda_j\downarrow0$ has a subsequence along which $v_{\lambda_j}$ converges in $L^1$. The corollary identifies the equation of every such limit; the examples below show that this equation alone need not determine the normalized potential.
