Parallel torsion gives a setting in which the integral constraint of a balanced trace becomes pointwise. Suppose $n\geq2$, write $J$ for the complex structure of $X$, let $g$ be a Hermitian metric on $X$, and let $D$ be a real connection on $TX$ that preserves $g$ and $J$ and has parallel torsion $T^D$:
\[
 Dg=0,\qquad DJ=0,\qquad DT^D=0,
 \qquad T^D(U,V)=D_UV-D_VU-[U,V].
\]
The balanced and pluriclosed metrics considered below need not equal $g$. Write
\[
 E_D^k=\{\alpha\in\Omega^k(X,\mathbb R):D\alpha=0\}
\]
for the space of $D$-parallel real $k$-forms. Since $X$ is connected, evaluation at one point is injective on this space, so it is finite-dimensional. Its complexification inherits the decomposition by bidegree because $DJ=0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:parallel-positive-projection}\index{Parallel torsion!positive projection}
There is a linear projection $S:\Omega^\bullet(X,\mathbb R)\to E_D^\bullet$ with $S\mathrm d=\mathrm dS$. Its complexification preserves bidegree and commutes with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$. It preserves strict positivity in bidegrees $(1,1)$ and $(n-1,n-1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the projection.} For a parallel form $\alpha$, the formula for exterior differentiation in terms of a connection expresses $\mathrm d\alpha$ as an algebraic contraction of $T^D$ with $\alpha$. Since $DT^D=0$, it follows that $\mathrm dE_D^k\subseteq E_D^{k+1}$. The Hodge star of $g$ is also parallel and maps $E_D^k$ onto $E_D^{2n-k}$. Consequently the pairing
\[
 E_D^k\times E_D^{2n-k}\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
 \qquad (\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto\int_X\alpha\wedge\beta
\]
is nondegenerate: pairing a nonzero $\alpha$ with $*_g\alpha$ gives its squared $L^2$ norm, which is positive. Hence every smooth real $k$-form $\alpha$ determines a unique $S_k\alpha\in E_D^k$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallel-projection-pairing}
 \int_X S_k\alpha\wedge\beta=\int_X\alpha\wedge\beta
 \qquad(\beta\in E_D^{2n-k}).
\end{equation}
This defines a projection onto $E_D^k$. For $\gamma\in E_D^{2n-k-1}$ we have $\mathrm d\gamma\in E_D^{2n-k}$, so Stokes' theorem gives
\[
 \int_X\mathrm dS_k\alpha\wedge\gamma
 =(-1)^{k+1}\int_X\alpha\wedge\mathrm d\gamma
 =\int_X S_{k+1}(\mathrm d\alpha)\wedge\gamma.
\]
Both forms being compared are parallel, so nondegeneracy proves $\mathrm dS_k\alpha=S_{k+1}\mathrm d\alpha$.

\emph{Step 2: positivity.} Fix $p\in X$. Let $H$ be the closure of the full $D$-holonomy group at $p$, a compact subgroup of the unitary group, with probability Haar measure $m_H$. For a path from $p$ to $x$, write $P_x:T_pX\to T_xX$ for parallel transport along it. The average
\[
 R_\alpha(x)=\int_H h^*(P_x^*\alpha_x)\,\mathrm dm_H(h)
\]
is independent of the path, since a change of path acts by an element of $H$. It is an $H$-invariant tensor at $p$ depending smoothly on $x$, because paths can be chosen smoothly on each coordinate neighborhood. Average it over $X$:
\[
 a_p=\frac1{\operatorname{Vol}_g(X)}
                \int_X R_\alpha(x)\,\mathrm dV_g(x).
\]
Being $H$-invariant, the tensor $a_p$ extends uniquely to a global parallel form $\widetilde S_k\alpha$. For parallel $\beta$, write $\alpha\wedge\beta=f\,\mathrm dV_g$. Since parallel transport is unitary and $\beta$ is invariant under holonomy, averaging gives
\[
 R_\alpha(x)\wedge\beta_p=f(x)\,\mathrm dV_{g,p}.
\]
Integrating this equality shows that $\widetilde S_k\alpha$ satisfies \eqref{eq:parallel-projection-pairing}; hence $\widetilde S_k=S_k$.

Every parallel transport and every holonomy element preserves reality, bidegree and the positive cones. A smooth strictly positive $(q,q)$-form, for $q=1$ or $n-1$, is bounded below by a positive multiple of the $q$th power of the fundamental form of $g$, uniformly on the compact manifold, and both probability averages preserve that lower bound. Therefore $S$ preserves strict positivity and bidegree. Comparing bidegrees in $S\mathrm d=\mathrm dS$ proves that $S$ commutes with $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$.
\end{proof}

The last step of the construction uses the fact, mentioned above, that a metric which is both balanced and pluriclosed is K\"ahler.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:balanced-pluriclosed-closed}
If a Hermitian form $\zeta$ satisfies $\mathrm d\zeta^{n-1}=0$ and $\ddc\zeta=0$, then $\mathrm d\zeta=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $n=2$, the balanced condition is the assertion $\mathrm d\zeta=0$. Suppose $n\geq3$ and set $\alpha=\partial\zeta$. Since $\zeta$ is balanced, $\alpha\wedge\zeta^{n-2}=0$, so $\alpha$ is a primitive $(2,1)$-form. Applying $\partial\bar\partial$ to the balanced power gives
\[
 0=\partial\bar\partial\zeta^{n-1}
  =(n-1)\partial\bar\partial\zeta\wedge\zeta^{n-2}
    +(n-1)(n-2)\alpha\wedge\bar\alpha\wedge\zeta^{n-3}.
\]
The first term vanishes because $\zeta$ is pluriclosed. For a primitive $(2,1)$-form, pointwise Hermitian linear algebra gives
\[
 -\mathrm i\,\alpha\wedge\bar\alpha\wedge
                 \frac{\zeta^{n-3}}{(n-3)!}
       =|\alpha|_\zeta^2\frac{\zeta^n}{n!}.
\]
Thus $\alpha=0$, and since $\zeta$ is real, also $\bar\partial\zeta=0$, so $\mathrm d\zeta=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:parallel-torsion-kahler}\index{Parallel torsion!Kahler metric@K\"ahler metric}\index{Balanced metric!parallel torsion}\index{Pluriclosed metric!parallel torsion}
Let $D$ be a connection on $TX$ as above, preserving $g$ and $J$ and with parallel torsion. If $X$ admits a balanced metric and a pluriclosed metric, then it admits a $D$-parallel K\"ahler metric.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\beta$ be a balanced metric and $\omega$ a pluriclosed metric. By \cref{lem:parallel-positive-projection}, $S(\beta^{n-1})$ is strictly positive, parallel and closed. It has a unique smooth positive $(n-1)$-st root $B$. Indeed, after the fixed normalization of wedge powers, the corresponding map on matrices is $G\mapsto C=(\det G)G^{-1}$, whose inverse is
\[
 G=(\det C)^{1/(n-1)}C^{-1}.
\]
By uniqueness the root is invariant under parallel transport. Thus $DB=0$ and $\mathrm dB^{n-1}=0$. Similarly, $P=S\omega$ is a parallel positive pluriclosed form.

Consider the finite-dimensional space of real parallel directions of exact type
\[
 \mathcal A_D=\{(\mathrm d\xi)^{1,1}:\xi\in E_D^1\}.
\]
Every such direction is $\partial\bar\partial$-closed, since
\[
 (\mathrm d\xi)^{1,1}=\partial\xi^{0,1}+\bar\partial\xi^{1,0}.
\]
For a parallel positive form $Q$, the volume functional has derivative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallel-volume-variation}
 \left.\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\right|_{t=0}
       \frac1{n!}\int_X(Q+t(\mathrm d\xi)^{1,1})^n
 =\frac1{(n-1)!}\int_X\xi\wedge\mathrm dQ^{n-1},
\end{equation}
because the $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$ parts of $\mathrm d\xi$ pair to zero with $Q^{n-1}$. Since $\mathrm dQ^{n-1}$ is parallel, nondegeneracy of the parallel pairing shows that all these derivatives vanish exactly when $Q$ is balanced.

Apply this to the balanced form $B$. For $a\in\mathcal A_D$, the function $\operatorname{tr}_B a$ is parallel and hence constant. Its integral against $B^n$ vanishes by \eqref{eq:parallel-volume-variation}, so it vanishes identically. Therefore every form in the positive affine slice
\[
 \mathcal P=\{Q\in P+\mathcal A_D:Q>0\}
\]
has the same pointwise trace $\operatorname{tr}_B Q=\operatorname{tr}_B P=:C>0$.

Evaluate the parallel forms at one point. The positive eigenvalues of $Q\in\mathcal P$ relative to $B$ sum to $C$, so they are bounded above by $C$. Thus $\mathcal P$ is bounded in its finite-dimensional affine space. Its closure is
\[
 \overline{\mathcal P}=\{Q\in P+\mathcal A_D:Q\geq0\},
\]
because $(1-t)Q+tP$ is positive for $0<t\leq1$ whenever $Q$ is semipositive; in particular, this closure is compact. Since both forms are parallel, the relative eigenvalues and the relative determinant are constant on $X$. Hence
\[
 \frac1{n!}\int_XQ^n
       =\det(B^{-1}Q)\frac1{n!}\int_XB^n.
\]
At a relative boundary point of $\overline{\mathcal P}$ the semipositive matrix is singular, so this volume is zero, whereas it is positive at $P$. A maximum of the volume on the compact set $\overline{\mathcal P}$ therefore lies in $\mathcal P$.

At this interior maximum all the derivatives in \eqref{eq:parallel-volume-variation} vanish, so the maximizing form is balanced; it is pluriclosed because it lies in $P+\mathcal A_D$. By \cref{lem:balanced-pluriclosed-closed} it is closed. Since it is positive and $D$-parallel, this proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

The trace constraint in this argument is pointwise because the metrics and the directions of variation are parallel. This additional structure makes the positive slice compact and the volume vanish on its boundary; for a general family, the conserved balanced trace gives no such control.
