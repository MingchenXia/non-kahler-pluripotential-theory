\chapter{Envelope systems over Hermitian perturbations}
\label{chap:hermitian-envelope-systems}

The non-Archimedean chapter of \cite{SGPT} starts with analytic test curves on compact K\"ahler manifolds. Its definition is not restricted to projective varieties: compatible $\mathcal I$-model curves are taken over positive closed perturbations of the background. The comparison with Berkovich metrics is a further, projective statement.

We develop a restricted part of this construction with arbitrary Hermitian perturbations. The curves will be constant sufficiently far to the left. This condition avoids assuming continuity of $\mathcal I$-modelization along an unrestricted increasing family. We obtain compatible systems, a canonical limit in the original background, and an exact criterion for recovering a system from that limit. No Monge--Amp\`ere mass enters these constructions. Their volume theory remains separate.

Throughout, $X$ is connected and compact, $\theta$ is smooth and real, and $\PSH(X,\theta)\neq\varnothing$. Fix real numbers $a<c$.

\section{Curves constant on a left half-line}

\begin{definition}\label{def:bounded-left-i-curve}
Let $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$ be the set of families $\Gamma=(\Gamma_\tau)_{\tau<c}$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every $\Gamma_\tau$ is a genuine $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential with $Q_\theta[\Gamma_\tau]=\Gamma_\tau$;
\item the family is pointwise concave and decreasing in $\tau$;
\item $\Gamma_\tau=\Gamma_a$ for $\tau\leq a$.
\end{enumerate}
The cutoff is $c$, with the extension $\Gamma_c=\lim_{\tau\uparrow c}\Gamma_\tau$ and $\Gamma_\tau=-\infty$ for $\tau>c$. The order on these curves is pointwise order of their finite slices.
\end{definition}

Every slice has supremum zero. The decreasing endpoint limit is genuine by normalized compactness, and it is also $Q_\theta$-fixed: order preservation gives
\[
 \Gamma_c\leq Q_\theta[\Gamma_c]\leq\Gamma_\tau
 \quad(\tau<c),
\]
so taking the infimum proves equality. The first inequality uses the normalized potential itself as a competitor.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-left-i-ray}
A curve in $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$ is an envelope test curve with initial potential $\varphi=\Gamma_a$. Its Legendre transform is a Perron envelope ray and satisfies
\[
 \varphi+at\leq U_t\leq\varphi+ct\qquad(t>0).
\]
In particular $U_t\simeq\varphi$ at each finite time.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A $Q_\theta$-fixed potential is $P_\theta$-fixed by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}. The regularized supremum of the curve is exactly $\Gamma_a$, so all the hypotheses of \cref{def:hermitian-envelope-testcurve} hold. Apply \cref{thm:testcurve-to-envelope-ray}. The term $\tau=a$ in the transform gives the lower bound, and $\Gamma_\tau\leq\varphi$, $\tau<c$, give the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Transition maps}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-i-curve-transition}
If $\beta\geq\theta$ are smooth real backgrounds, slicewise projection defines an injective map
\[
 Q_\beta:\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)\longrightarrow
                       \mathscr T_{a,c}(\beta).
\]
For $\beta\geq\beta'\geq\theta$, these maps satisfy
\[
 Q_\beta\bigl[Q_{\beta'}[\Gamma]\bigr]=Q_\beta[\Gamma].
\]
They preserve order.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The input slices are $\beta$-plurisubharmonic, so their projections are genuine and $Q_\beta$-fixed. Decreasingness, constancy for $\tau\leq a$, and order preservation follow from \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations}. To check concavity, put $\tau=(1-s)r+s t$ with $0<s<1$. That same proposition and the concavity of the original curve give
\[
 \begin{split}
 (1-s)Q_\beta[\Gamma_r]+sQ_\beta[\Gamma_t]
 &\leq Q_\beta[(1-s)\Gamma_r+s\Gamma_t]\\
 &\leq Q_\beta[\Gamma_\tau].
 \end{split}
\]
Here the first inequality follows by combining the scaling and addition formulas with backgrounds $(1-s)\beta$ and $s\beta$.

Each projection preserves the multiplier ideals at every weight. Thus the two sides of the transition identity are $Q_\beta$-fixed representatives of the same multiplier-ideal class, and are equal. If two projected curves agree, their original slices have the same multiplier ideals; since those original slices are $Q_\theta$-fixed, they agree as well. This proves injectivity. We have used uniqueness of a fixed representative within its background, without applying $Q_\theta$ to an inadmissible potential in a larger background.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
Let $\mathscr H(X)$ denote the set of smooth positive Hermitian forms on $X$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:hermitian-envelope-system}
An \emph{envelope system over $\theta$}, with parameters $a<c$, is a family
\[
 \boldsymbol\Gamma=(\Gamma^\eta)_{\eta\in\mathscr H(X)},
 \qquad \Gamma^\eta\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta+\eta),
\]
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-system-compatibility}
 \Gamma^\eta=Q_{\theta+\eta}[\Gamma^{\eta'}]
 \quad\hbox{whenever }\eta\geq\eta'.
\end{equation}
Write $\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)$ for this set, ordered componentwise. A system is \emph{realized in the background $\theta$} if it has the form
\[
 \mathsf E(\Gamma)^\eta=Q_{\theta+\eta}[\Gamma]
 \quad\hbox{for some }\Gamma\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta).
\]
\end{definition}

Equation \eqref{eq:hermitian-system-compatibility} specifies the inverse system without an ambiguity about the direction of the order. Its maps go from a smaller perturbation to a larger one. All components of a system have the same multiplier ideals at any fixed finite parameter: compare two of them with the component indexed by $\eta+\eta'$.

The set is nonempty. If $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the constant family $\Gamma_\tau=Q_\theta[u]$ for $\tau<c$, with the specified cutoff, lies in $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$. Its projections form an envelope system. The transition proposition also proves that $\mathsf E$ is injective.

\section{A canonical limit in the original background}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-system-retraction}
Fix any Hermitian form $h$. For every $\boldsymbol\Gamma\in\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)$ the limits
\[
 (\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau
 =\lim_{j\to\infty}\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau\qquad(\tau<c)
\]
exist as decreasing genuine potentials and define an element of $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$. They are independent of $h$. The maps satisfy
\[
 \mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id},
 \qquad \mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol\Gamma)
                       \leq\boldsymbol\Gamma.
\]
Both maps preserve order. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-system-order-adjunction}
 \mathsf E(\Gamma)\leq\boldsymbol\Gamma
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma\leq\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma.
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol\Gamma)$ is the greatest system below $\boldsymbol\Gamma$ that is realized in the original background.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\tau<c$ and put $q_j=\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau$. Compatibility and the fact that $q_{j+1}\leq0$ is a competitor for its larger-background envelope give $q_j\geq q_{j+1}$. The functions have supremum zero and lie in one fixed Hermitian lower-curvature class. Normalized compactness excludes collapse; equivalently, their nonempty compact maximum sets are nested, so their intersection is nonempty. The decreasing limit $q$ is genuine. Passing the distributional inequalities
\[
 \theta+h/j+\ddc q_j\geq0
\]
to the limit gives $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $\sup_Xq=0$.

We claim $Q_\theta[q]=q$. Since $q\leq q_j$, its multiplier ideals are contained in those of $q_j$. The potential $Q_\theta[q]$ belongs to the larger background $\theta+h/j$ and has the ideals of $q$. Hence order preservation there gives
\[
 Q_\theta[q]
 \leq Q_{\theta+h/j}[Q_\theta[q]]
 \leq Q_{\theta+h/j}[q_j]=q_j.
\]
Take the infimum in $j$ and use $Q_\theta[q]\geq q$ to prove the claim. Concavity, decreasingness and constancy on the left half-line pass pointwise to these limits. They therefore form a curve in $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$.

For every $\eta>0$ there is $j$ with $h/j\leq\eta$. Compatibility gives $\Gamma^\eta_\tau\geq\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau$. Consequently
\[
 (\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau
 =\inf_{\eta\in\mathscr H(X)}\Gamma^\eta_\tau,
\]
which proves independence of $h$. It also proves order preservation of $\mathsf R$.

Write $q=(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau$. Since $q\leq\Gamma^\eta_\tau$, order preservation gives
$Q_{\theta+\eta}[q]\leq\Gamma^\eta_\tau$.
This proves $\mathsf E\mathsf R\leq\mathrm{id}$.

Now start with a base curve $\Gamma$. Its slice $\Gamma_\tau$ is a competitor in every larger background, so
\[
 \Gamma_\tau\leq(\mathsf R\mathsf E\Gamma)_\tau
                  \leq Q_{\theta+\eta}[\Gamma_\tau]
 \quad(\eta>0).
\]
The outside terms have the same multiplier ideals. By the two ideal inclusions supplied by these inequalities, the middle term has those ideals as well. Both it and $\Gamma_\tau$ are $Q_\theta$-fixed, so they are equal. This proves $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$.

Order preservation of $\mathsf E$ follows from the transition proposition. Applying $\mathsf R$ proves the forward implication in \eqref{eq:hermitian-system-order-adjunction}. For the reverse implication apply $\mathsf E$ and use $\mathsf E\mathsf R\leq\mathrm{id}$. This equivalence proves the final maximality assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-system-realization-criterion}
A system is realized in $\theta$ if and only if, for every $\tau<c$, its limiting slice $(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau$ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to one, and hence every, slice $\Gamma^\eta_\tau$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a realized system this follows from $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$ and ideal preservation by each projection. Conversely the assumed equivalence and fixedness in the larger background give
\[
 Q_{\theta+\eta}[(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau]
                       =\Gamma^\eta_\tau.
\]
Thus the system equals $\mathsf E\mathsf R$ of itself.
\end{proof}

The criterion can be expressed entirely locally. By \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}, it asks that the Lelong numbers of the limiting slice and a component agree at every point on every local modification. The limiting slice lies below every component, so its Lelong numbers can only be larger. Equality is not automatic: \cref{sec:envelope-boundary-example} will exhibit an extra divisorial pole in the limit, even for a positive K\"ahler background.

\section{Smooth changes of representative}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-system-gauge}
If $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$ with $f$ smooth and real, the formulas
\[
 (\mathsf G_f\Gamma)_\tau=Q_{\theta'}[\Gamma_\tau-f],
 \qquad
 (\mathsf G_f\boldsymbol\Gamma)^\eta_\tau
                    =Q_{\theta'+\eta}[\Gamma^\eta_\tau-f]
\]
define bijections on the corresponding curve and system spaces. They satisfy the cocycle law and commute with $\mathsf E$ and $\mathsf R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtracting $f$ changes the background as indicated and preserves concavity. Slicewise projection preserves concavity by the same convex-combination argument as before. It preserves the cutoff and the constant left part. Every new slice has the multiplier ideals of the original one because $f$ is smooth. Applying the construction with $-f$ gives a $Q_\theta$-fixed slice with those same ideals, hence the original slice. This proves the inverse statement; the same uniqueness proves the cocycle law.

Compatibility of the system and commutation with $\mathsf E$ follow by comparing multiplier ideals in the common final background. For commutation with $\mathsf R$, the gauge estimate of \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge}, applied in each background $\theta+\eta$, gives
\[
 \Gamma^\eta_\tau+\min_Xf
 \leq (\mathsf G_f\boldsymbol\Gamma)^\eta_\tau+f
 \leq\Gamma^\eta_\tau+\max_Xf.
\]
The constants are independent of $\eta$. Pass to the cofinal decreasing limits. The new limit is ordinarily equivalent to the old limit minus $f$, hence has the same multiplier ideals. It is $Q_{\theta'}$-fixed by \cref{thm:hermitian-system-retraction}. It must therefore equal $Q_{\theta'}[(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau-f]$, proving commutation with $\mathsf R$.
\end{proof}

When $X$ is K\"ahler, the closed positive perturbations are cofinal among all Hermitian perturbations: a small positive multiple of one fixed K\"ahler form is below any given Hermitian form. Hence the compatibility formula determines an envelope system from its closed-positive components. Conversely, a compatible family on that cofinal collection extends uniquely: for $\eta>0$ choose a closed positive $\kappa\leq\eta$ and set $\Gamma^\eta=Q_{\theta+\eta}[\Gamma^\kappa]$. Two choices give the same result by passing to a smaller closed positive form and using the transition identity.

This identifies the change of indexing collection. To compare with the actual spaces in \cite{SGPT}, one must still impose their positive-volume and initial-potential hypotheses. We have worked with a fixed left-constant range $[a,c]$ and have made no claim about the unrestricted source space, its volume, its Duistermaat--Heckman measure, or its Berkovich interpretation.

\section{Why a volume does not follow from the system}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:envelope-system-raw-volume-failure}
The expression suggested by the source volume formula,
\[
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
   \int_X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\omega+\epsilon h}
                 (\Gamma^{\epsilon h}_a),
\]
when this limit exists, need not be invariant under the smooth-gauge bijection of envelope systems. Here $\omega,h$ are Hermitian forms.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the homogeneous curve with every finite slice equal to zero and cutoff $c=0$, choosing $a<0$. Every component is still zero because $\omega+\eta$ is positive and zero is its $Q$-envelope for the unit-ideal class. The displayed limit is therefore
\[
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\int_X(\omega+\epsilon h)^n
                          =\int_X\omega^n.
\]
Choose the smooth perturbation $f$ from \cref{prop:raw-i-volume-gauge-failure}, so that $\omega'=\omega+\ddc f$ is Hermitian and $\int_X(\omega')^n\neq\int_X\omega^n$. The gauge bijection sends every slice to $Q_{\omega'+\eta}[-f]=0$, again because the weight is smooth and the background is positive. Its corresponding limit is $\int_X(\omega')^n$, which is different. Both limits exist, so their failure of invariance is not a convergence issue.
\end{proof}

The formal envelope system survives a non-closed background, but the source volume formula requires a replacement. Recovery under suitable nondegeneracy conditions, continuity for curves not constant on a left half-line, and a compatible finite volume are distinct research problems.
