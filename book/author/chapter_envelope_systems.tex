\chapter{Envelope systems over Hermitian perturbations}
\label{chap:hermitian-envelope-systems}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and fix real numbers $a<c$.

For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the multiplier-ideal envelope $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ of \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope} is the regularized supremum of the nonpositive $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions with the multiplier ideals of $u$. It depends on the background $\theta$. When $\theta$ is perturbed by Hermitian forms, the envelopes of one multiplier-ideal class in the perturbed backgrounds form a compatible family, and as the perturbation tends to zero these normalized representatives decrease to a potential in the original background. Projecting the limit back to the perturbed backgrounds recovers the family exactly when the limit keeps the multiplier ideals of the family; otherwise it gives the greatest family below it that comes from the original background.

We apply this to test curves that are constant for parameters $\tau\leq a$ and have cutoff $c$. Recovery can fail even in a K\"ahler background: on a ruled surface, at zero volume, the limit acquires an additional divisorial pole. On the other hand, for big backgrounds on manifolds with the bounded mass property, recovery holds by \cref{thm:positive-volume-system-recovery} when the lower volumes $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}$ of the initial slices (\cref{def:singularity-volumes}) have a positive limit. On the Hopf threefold, whose upper mass is unbounded, recovery holds instead for systems whose components over scalar multiples of the standard Gauduchon form are invariant along the elliptic fibers, provided that the limiting initial slice on the projective base has positive lower volume (\cref{thm:hopf-transverse-recovery}). Smooth changes of the background commute with recovery, whereas the limiting Monge--Amp\`ere mass can change when the background is not closed.

We also use the ordinary singularity envelope $P_\theta[u]$ of \cref{def:singularity-envelope}, the ordinary and multiplier-ideal singularity orders of \cref{sec:psh-singularity-types}, and the extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}$ of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}. A potential $u$ with $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=u$ is called $P_\theta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed.

\section{Curves constant on a left half-line}
\label{sec:left-constant-curves}

We restrict the envelope test curves of \cref{def:hermitian-envelope-testcurve} by requiring that each slice be fixed by the multiplier-ideal envelope and that the curve be constant for $\tau\leq a$. Constancy identifies the initial potential explicitly as $\Gamma_a$, and it confines the associated ray between the translates of this potential by $at$ and by $ct$.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{definition}\label{def:bounded-left-i-curve}\index{Test curve!constant left part}
Let $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$ be the set of families $\Gamma=(\Gamma_\tau)_{\tau<c}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Gamma_\tau\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $P_\theta[\Gamma_\tau]_{\mathcal I}=\Gamma_\tau$ for every $\tau<c$;
\item the family is pointwise concave and decreasing in $\tau$;
\item $\Gamma_\tau=\Gamma_a$ for $\tau\leq a$.
\end{enumerate}
The cutoff of $\Gamma$ is $c$, and we extend $\Gamma$ by $\Gamma_c=\lim_{\tau\uparrow c}\Gamma_\tau$ and $\Gamma_\tau=-\infty$ for $\tau>c$. Such curves are ordered by the pointwise order of their slices $\Gamma_\tau$, $\tau<c$.
\end{definition}

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Every slice has supremum zero by \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection}. Hence, by \cref{thm:normalized-compactness,cor:decreasing-limit}, the decreasing limit $\Gamma_c$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$; in particular, it is not identically $-\infty$. It is also $P_\theta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed. Indeed, $\Gamma_c\leq0$ is a competitor for its own envelope, and $\Gamma_c\leq\Gamma_\tau$, so the order preservation of \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations} gives
\[
 \Gamma_c\leq P_\theta[\Gamma_c]_{\mathcal I}\leq\Gamma_\tau
 \quad(\tau<c).
\]
Taking the infimum over $\tau$ proves equality.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-left-i-ray}
A curve $\Gamma\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$ is an envelope test curve with initial potential $\varphi=\Gamma_a$. Its Legendre transform
\[
 U_t=\Bigl(\sup_{\tau<c}(\Gamma_\tau+t\tau)\Bigr)^*\qquad(t>0)
\]
is an envelope ray with initial potential $\varphi$ and slope $c$, in the sense of \cref{def:hermitian-envelope-ray}, and satisfies
\[
 \varphi+at\leq U_t\leq\varphi+ct\qquad(t>0).
\]
In particular $U_t\sim\varphi$ for every $t>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}, a $P_\theta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed potential is also fixed by the ordinary envelope $P_\theta$. The regularized supremum of the curve is $\Gamma_a$, since the curve is decreasing and constant for $\tau\leq a$. Hence all the conditions of \cref{def:hermitian-envelope-testcurve} hold, and \cref{thm:testcurve-to-envelope-ray} applies. The term $\tau=a$ in the transform gives the lower bound, and the inequalities $\Gamma_\tau\leq\varphi$ for $\tau<c$ give the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Transition maps and envelope systems}
\label{sec:envelope-system-transitions}

Curves over different backgrounds are related by projecting each slice to the ideal envelope in the larger background. For these projections to form a consistent system, projecting in two steps must agree with projecting directly. This follows because each projection preserves multiplier ideals, while in each background a multiplier-ideal class has a unique representative fixed by the ideal envelope. The same fact gives injectivity: equal projected slices have equal multiplier ideals, hence come from equal fixed representatives.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-i-curve-transition}
Let $\beta\geq\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Slicewise projection, $\Gamma\mapsto(P_\beta[\Gamma_\tau]_{\mathcal I})_{\tau<c}$, defines an injective and order-preserving map
\[
 P_\beta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}\colon\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)\longrightarrow
                       \mathscr T_{a,c}(\beta).
\]
For every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta'$ with $\beta\geq\beta'\geq\theta$, these maps satisfy
\[
 P_\beta\bigl[P_{\beta'}[\Gamma]_{\mathcal I}\bigr]_{\mathcal I}=P_\beta[\Gamma]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\beta\geq\theta$, every slice is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic, and by \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection} its projection is a $\beta$-plurisubharmonic function fixed by $P_\beta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$. Decreasingness, constancy for $\tau\leq a$, and order preservation follow from \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations}.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
For concavity, let $r,t<c$ and $0<s<1$, and put $\tau=(1-s)r+s t$. By the same proposition and the concavity of the original curve,
\[
 \begin{split}
 (1-s)P_\beta[\Gamma_r]_{\mathcal I}+sP_\beta[\Gamma_t]_{\mathcal I}
 &\leq P_\beta[(1-s)\Gamma_r+s\Gamma_t]_{\mathcal I}\\
 &\leq P_\beta[\Gamma_\tau]_{\mathcal I}.
 \end{split}
\]
Here the first inequality follows by combining the scaling and addition formulas of that proposition, with backgrounds $(1-s)\beta$ and $s\beta$.

Each projection preserves the multiplier ideals at every weight. Hence the two sides of the transition identity are $P_\beta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed representatives of the same multiplier-ideal class, and they are equal. If two projected curves agree, their original slices have the same multiplier ideals; since those slices are $P_\theta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed, they agree as well. This proves injectivity.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
Let $\mathscr H(X)$ denote the set of Hermitian forms on $X$; for $\eta\in\mathscr H(X)$ we also write $\eta>0$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:hermitian-envelope-system}\index{Envelope system}
An \emph{envelope system over $\theta$}, with parameters $a<c$, is a family
\[
 \boldsymbol\Gamma=(\Gamma^\eta)_{\eta\in\mathscr H(X)},
 \qquad \Gamma^\eta\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta+\eta),
\]
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-system-compatibility}
 \Gamma^\eta=P_{\theta+\eta}[\Gamma^{\eta'}]_{\mathcal I}
 \quad\hbox{whenever }\eta\geq\eta'.
\end{equation}
Write $\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)$ for the set of these systems, ordered componentwise. For $\Gamma\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$, set
\[
 \mathsf E(\Gamma)^\eta=P_{\theta+\eta}[\Gamma]_{\mathcal I}
 \qquad(\eta\in\mathscr H(X)).
\]
A system is \emph{realized in the background $\theta$} if it equals $\mathsf E(\Gamma)$ for some $\Gamma\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$.
\end{definition}

By \cref{prop:hermitian-i-curve-transition}, $\mathsf E(\Gamma)$ is an envelope system, and the map $\mathsf E$ is injective. All components of an envelope system have the same multiplier ideals at each $\tau<c$: compare two components $\Gamma^\eta$ and $\Gamma^{\eta'}$ with the component indexed by $\eta+\eta'$.

If $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the constant family $\Gamma_\tau=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, $\tau<c$, with cutoff $c$, lies in $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$, and its projections form the envelope system $\mathsf E(\Gamma)$.

\section{The limiting curve and recovery}\label{sec:system-limit-recovery}

Every Hermitian form dominates all sufficiently small multiples of a fixed Hermitian form. A single decreasing sequence of components therefore determines the limit of the whole system. Projecting the resulting curve back gives the greatest realized system below the original one, and strict inequality means that recovery fails.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-system-retraction}\index{Envelope system!retraction}
Fix a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, and let $j$ range over the positive integers. For every $\boldsymbol\Gamma\in\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)$ and every $\tau<c$, the sequence $\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau$ decreases, and its limit
\[
 (\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau
 =\lim_{j\to\infty}\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau
\]
is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, in particular not identically $-\infty$. These limits define an element $\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma$ of $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$, which does not depend on $h$. The maps $\mathsf E$ and $\mathsf R$ satisfy
\[
 \mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id},
 \qquad \mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol\Gamma)
                       \leq\boldsymbol\Gamma.
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Both maps preserve order. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-system-order-adjunction}
 \mathsf E(\Gamma)\leq\boldsymbol\Gamma
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma\leq\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma.
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol\Gamma)$ is the greatest system below $\boldsymbol\Gamma$ that is realized in the original background.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the limit.} Fix $\tau<c$ and put $q_j=\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau$. By compatibility, $q_j=P_{\theta+h/j}[q_{j+1}]_{\mathcal I}$, and $q_{j+1}\leq0$ is a competitor for this envelope; hence $q_j\geq q_{j+1}$. The functions $q_j$ have supremum zero and a common smooth lower curvature bound. By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness,cor:decreasing-limit}, their decreasing limit $q$ is not identically $-\infty$ and has supremum zero. Passing to the limit in the distributional inequalities
\[
 \theta+h/j+\ddc q_j\geq0
\]
gives $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.

The defining family of an ideal envelope grows when the background increases. Together with $q\leq q_j$ and order preservation, this gives
\[
 q\leq P_\theta[q]_{\mathcal I}
 \leq P_{\theta+h/j}[q]_{\mathcal I}
 \leq P_{\theta+h/j}[q_j]_{\mathcal I}=q_j.
\]
Taking the infimum over $j$ proves $P_\theta[q]_{\mathcal I}=q$. Concavity, decreasingness and constancy on the left half-line pass to the pointwise limits, so these limits form a curve in $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$.

\emph{Step 2: independence of $h$.} For every $\eta>0$ there is $j$ with $h/j\leq\eta$, and compatibility then gives $\Gamma^\eta_\tau\geq\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau$. Consequently
\[
 (\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau
 =\inf_{\eta\in\mathscr H(X)}\Gamma^\eta_\tau,
\]
which proves that $\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma$ does not depend on $h$. This formula also shows that $\mathsf R$ preserves order.

\emph{Step 3: the two compositions.} Write $q=(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau$. Since $q\leq\Gamma^\eta_\tau$ and $\Gamma^\eta_\tau$ is fixed by its envelope, order preservation gives $P_{\theta+\eta}[q]_{\mathcal I}\leq\Gamma^\eta_\tau$. This proves $\mathsf E\mathsf R\leq\mathrm{id}$.

Now start with a curve $\Gamma\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$. Its slice $\Gamma_\tau$ is a competitor in every larger background, so
\[
 \Gamma_\tau\leq(\mathsf R\mathsf E\Gamma)_\tau
                  \leq P_{\theta+\eta}[\Gamma_\tau]_{\mathcal I}
 \quad(\eta>0).
\]
The two outer terms have the same multiplier ideals, so the two ideal inclusions given by these inequalities show that the middle term has those ideals as well. Since both the middle term and $\Gamma_\tau$ are $P_\theta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed, they are equal. This proves $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$.

\emph{Step 4: the adjunction.} By \cref{prop:hermitian-i-curve-transition}, $\mathsf E$ preserves order. If $\mathsf E(\Gamma)\leq\boldsymbol\Gamma$, applying $\mathsf R$ gives $\Gamma=\mathsf R\mathsf E(\Gamma)\leq\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma$. Conversely, if $\Gamma\leq\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma$, applying $\mathsf E$ and using $\mathsf E\mathsf R\leq\mathrm{id}$ gives $\mathsf E(\Gamma)\leq\boldsymbol\Gamma$. This proves \eqref{eq:hermitian-system-order-adjunction}, and the final maximality assertion follows from it.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-system-realization-criterion}\index{Envelope system!realization}
A system $\boldsymbol\Gamma\in\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)$ is realized in $\theta$ if and only if, for every $\tau<c$, its limiting slice $(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau$ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to one, and hence every, slice $\Gamma^\eta_\tau$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a realized system, this follows from $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$ and the preservation of multiplier ideals by each projection. Conversely, the assumed equivalence and the fact that $\Gamma^\eta_\tau$ is fixed by its envelope in the larger background give
\[
 P_{\theta+\eta}[(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau]_{\mathcal I}
                       =\Gamma^\eta_\tau.
\]
Thus the system equals $\mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol\Gamma)$.
\end{proof}

By \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}, multiplier-ideal equivalence means equality of all Lelong numbers on all local modifications. Recovery therefore holds exactly when the limiting slices keep these Lelong numbers. They are the same for all components of a system, whereas the limiting slice can have larger values: in the ruled-surface example of \cref{sec:envelope-boundary-example}, one of them increases from zero to one. For big backgrounds on manifolds with the bounded mass property, \cref{thm:positive-volume-system-recovery} excludes such an increase when the initial slices have positive limiting lower volume.

When $X$ is K\"ahler, the K\"ahler forms are cofinal among the Hermitian forms: a sufficiently small positive multiple of a fixed K\"ahler form lies below any given Hermitian form. The compatibility condition therefore determines a system by its components indexed by K\"ahler forms. Conversely, every compatible family indexed by K\"ahler forms extends uniquely to a system: for $\eta>0$, choose a K\"ahler form $\kappa\leq\eta$ and set $\Gamma^\eta=P_{\theta+\eta}[\Gamma^\kappa]_{\mathcal I}$. Two choices of $\kappa$ give the same result, as one sees by passing to a common smaller K\"ahler form and using the transition identity.

\section{Changes of representative and limiting mass}\label{sec:system-gauge-mass}

Subtracting a smooth function transports a curve to a new background, and projection restores the envelope normalization. The gauge estimate is uniform in the perturbation, so this transport also respects the limiting curve. The limiting Monge--Amp\`ere mass behaves differently: in a nonclosed background, it can change even for the system all of whose slices are zero.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-system-gauge}\index{Envelope system!smooth change of background}
Let $f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$ and $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$. For $\Gamma\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$ and $\boldsymbol\Gamma\in\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)$, the formulas
\[
 (\mathsf G_f\Gamma)_\tau=P_{\theta'}[\Gamma_\tau-f]_{\mathcal I},
 \qquad
 (\mathsf G_f\boldsymbol\Gamma)^\eta_\tau
                    =P_{\theta'+\eta}[\Gamma^\eta_\tau-f]_{\mathcal I}
\]
define bijections $\mathsf G_f\colon\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)\to\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta')$ and $\mathsf G_f\colon\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)\to\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta')$. They satisfy the cocycle law $\mathsf G_g\mathsf G_f=\mathsf G_{f+g}$, where $\mathsf G_g$ is formed in the background $\theta'$, and they commute with $\mathsf E$ and $\mathsf R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtracting $f$ changes the background from $\theta$ to $\theta'$ and preserves concavity, decreasingness and constancy on the left half-line. Slicewise projection preserves these properties by the argument of \cref{prop:hermitian-i-curve-transition}. Since $f$ is smooth, every new slice has the multiplier ideals of the original one. Applying the construction with $-f$ therefore gives a $P_\theta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed slice with the same ideals as the original slice, hence the original slice itself. This proves that $\mathsf G_{-f}$ is the inverse of $\mathsf G_f$; the same uniqueness of fixed representatives proves the cocycle law.

Compatibility of the transformed system and commutation with $\mathsf E$ follow by comparing multiplier ideals in the common final background. For commutation with $\mathsf R$, the gauge estimate of \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge}, applied in each background $\theta+\eta$, gives
\[
 \Gamma^\eta_\tau+\min_Xf
 \leq (\mathsf G_f\boldsymbol\Gamma)^\eta_\tau+f
 \leq\Gamma^\eta_\tau+\max_Xf,
\]
with constants independent of $\eta$. Pass to the decreasing limits along a cofinal sequence. The new limit has the same ordinary singularity type as the old limit minus $f$, and hence the same multiplier ideals. It is $P_{\theta'}[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed by \cref{thm:hermitian-system-retraction}, so it equals $P_{\theta'}[(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau-f]_{\mathcal I}$. This proves commutation with $\mathsf R$.
\end{proof}

For closed backgrounds on a compact K\"ahler manifold, \cite[Section~13.1]{SGPT} indexes such systems by K\"ahler perturbations, as above, and defines their volume as a limit of the masses of the initial potentials. The components of those systems are general test curves fixed by the ideal envelope, with initial potentials of positive mass, whereas our curves are constant on a prescribed left half-line and our system spaces impose no mass condition. For nonclosed positive backgrounds, the corresponding limit of masses is not invariant under the gauge bijections.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:envelope-system-raw-volume-failure}\index{Envelope system!failure of raw-mass invariance}
There are a compact complex surface $X$, Hermitian forms $\omega,h$ on $X$, and a smooth real function $f$ with $\omega'=\omega+\ddc f>0$, with the following property. For every $a<c$ there is a system $\boldsymbol\Gamma\in\mathscr S_{a,c}(\omega)$ such that the limits
\[
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\omega+\epsilon h}
          (\Gamma^{\epsilon h}_a),
 \qquad
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\omega'+\epsilon h}
          ((\mathsf G_f\boldsymbol\Gamma)^{\epsilon h}_a)
\]
both exist and are different.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Take the torus $X$ and the smooth perturbation of \cref{prop:raw-i-volume-gauge-failure}, so that $\omega'=\omega+\ddc f$ is Hermitian and $\int_X(\omega')^2\neq\int_X\omega^2$, and fix any Hermitian form $h$. In every background $\omega+\eta$, let every slice with $\tau<c$ be zero. This is an envelope system for every $a<c$, since zero is the multiplier-ideal envelope of the unit-ideal class in a positive background. Its first limit is
\[
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\int_X(\omega+\epsilon h)^2
                          =\int_X\omega^2.
\]
The gauge bijection sends every slice to $P_{\omega'+\eta}[-f]_{\mathcal I}=0$, since $f$ is smooth and the new background is positive. The second limit is therefore $\int_X(\omega')^2$, which is different.
\end{proof}
