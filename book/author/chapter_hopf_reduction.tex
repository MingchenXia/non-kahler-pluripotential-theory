\chapter{Hopf fibrations and toric potentials}
\label{chap:hopf-reduction}

The scalar Hopf manifold $X_n$ fibers over $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ with elliptic fibers, and its Gauduchon form splits into a closed horizontal part pulled back from the base and a vertical part. This chapter shows how much of the pluripotential theory of $X_n$ reduces to the base. Because the fibers are compact, potentials in pulled-back closed classes are constant on the fibers and descend to the base, and fiber integration identifies the weighted section norms on the two spaces. For a descended potential of the Gauduchon form, fiber integration also computes the Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere measure and reduces the Monge--Amp\`ere equation to an equation on the base, where closedness of the background fixes the normalizing constant and gives uniqueness. This happens although unrestricted masses on the Hopf threefold are unbounded: large masses require potentials that vary along the fibers. Finally, for smooth base potentials invariant under rotations of the individual homogeneous coordinates, logarithmic coordinates turn positivity of the curvature into convexity, and strict positivity on the base makes the gradient a parametrization of the relative interior of a simplex.

Throughout the chapter, $n\geq2$, and $X_n$, the projection $\pi\colon X_n\to\mathbb P^{n-1}$, the forms $\alpha,\zeta,\omega$ and the probability volume form $\mathrm{d}V=\omega^n/(2n)$ are those of \cref{sec:hopf-homogeneous-sections}. Write $\sigma$ for the Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ normalized by $\int_{\mathbb P^{n-1}}\sigma^{n-1}=1$, and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}$ for the extended non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}. The fiber calculations use the following identities, where $y\in\mathbb P^{n-1}$:
\[
 \alpha=\pi^*\sigma,\qquad \omega=\alpha+\zeta,\qquad
 \zeta^2=0,\qquad
 \mathrm{d}V=\tfrac12\alpha^{n-1}\wedge\zeta,\qquad
 \int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}\zeta=2.
\]

\section{Weighted sections on the projective base}\label{sec:hopf-projective-sections}

For an integer $k\geq0$, the line bundle $L_k$ of \cref{sec:hopf-homogeneous-sections}, with its metric $h_k$, is the pullback of $\mathcal O(k)$ with its Fubini--Study metric. By the proof of \cref{prop:hopf-bergman-exact}, sections of $L_k^m$ are represented by homogeneous polynomials $f$ of degree $km$ in the covering coordinates $z\in\mathbb C^n\setminus\{0\}$, exactly as sections of $\mathcal O(km)$ on $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, and the pointwise squared norm $|f(z)|^2|z|^{-2km}$ is invariant under every nonzero complex scalar. If the weight also descends, the weighted integrand is a function on the base, and fiber integration identifies the norms and Bergman kernels of \cref{sec:weighted-section-spaces} on the two spaces.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hopf-weighted-section-descent}\index{Hopf manifold!section descent}
Let $k\geq0$ be an integer and $u\in\PSH(X_n,k\alpha)$. Then $u=\pi^*v$ for a unique $v\in\PSH(\mathbb P^{n-1},k\sigma)$. For every positive integer $m$, pullback identifies the weighted section spaces isometrically: for a section $s$ in the space on the base,
\[
 \begin{split}
 H^0\bigl(\mathbb P^{n-1},\mathcal O(km)\otimes\mathcal I(mv)\bigr)
 &\simeq H^0\bigl(X_n,L_k^m\otimes\mathcal I(mu)\bigr),\\
 \|\pi^*s\|_{X_n,m,u}^2&=\|s\|_{\mathbb P^{n-1},m,v}^2.
 \end{split}
\]
Here the norm on the base is defined with the Fubini--Study metric and the volume form $\sigma^{n-1}$. The kernels $K$ and, off their pole sets, $B$ on $X_n$ are the pullbacks of the corresponding kernels on the base. Pullback also identifies the bounded weighted section algebras $\mathcal W_\bullet(v)$ and $\mathcal W_\bullet(u)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The descent of the potential follows from \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion}. By the proof of \cref{prop:hopf-bergman-exact}, the unweighted section spaces on both manifolds consist of the same homogeneous polynomials of degree $km$, and the pointwise norms of corresponding sections agree because the metrics correspond. For every nonnegative Borel function $F$ on the base, fiber integration gives
\[
 \int_{X_n}(F\circ\pi)\,\mathrm{d}V
 =\frac12\int_{\mathbb P^{n-1}}F\sigma^{n-1}
                         \int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}\zeta
 =\int_{\mathbb P^{n-1}}F\sigma^{n-1}.
\]
Applied to $F=|s|^2\mathrm{e}^{-mv}$, with arbitrary values on the pole set, this formula gives equality of the two possibly infinite integrals. Since finiteness of the weighted norm characterizes the multiplier-ideal section spaces, this proves the asserted isometry. An orthonormal basis therefore pulls back to an orthonormal basis, which proves the kernel identities. Finally, a nonnegative function on the base is bounded if and only if its pullback is bounded, so pullback also identifies the bounded weighted sections, compatibly with multiplication.
\end{proof}

\section{Descended measures and equations}

The pulled-back curvature of a descended potential has no component in the fiber direction; the form $\zeta$ supplies it. Since $\zeta^2=0$, the Hermitian top power of a descended potential consists of a single mixed term, and fiber integration of this term determines the mass and reduces the Monge--Amp\`ere equation for descended potentials to the base.

For a positive Borel measure $\nu$ on $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, we write $\pi^*\nu\wedge\zeta$ for the measure on $X_n$ defined, on nonnegative Borel functions $F$, by
\[
 \int_{X_n}F\,\pi^*\nu\wedge\zeta
 =\int_{\mathbb P^{n-1}}
        \left(\int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}F\zeta\right)\mathrm{d}\nu(y).
\]
When $\nu$ is given by a smooth top-degree form, this agrees with the pullback of the form wedged with $\zeta$.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hopf-descended-hermitian-ma}\index{Hopf manifold!descended equation}
For a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, the pullback $u=\pi^*v$ belongs to $\PSH(X_n,\omega)$ if and only if $v\in\PSH(\mathbb P^{n-1},\sigma)$. In this case
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)
 =n\,\pi^*\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\sigma(v)\wedge\zeta.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \int_{X_n}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)
 =2n\int_{\mathbb P^{n-1}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\sigma(v)
 \leq2n,
\]
with equality for bounded $v$. For an integer $k\geq0$ and $v\in\PSH(\mathbb P^{n-1},k\sigma)$,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{k\alpha}(\pi^*v)=0
\]
as a measure on the $n$-dimensional manifold $X_n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $v$ is $\sigma$-plurisubharmonic, then
\[
 \omega+\ddc u=\pi^*(\sigma+\ddc v)+\zeta\geq0.
\]
Conversely, suppose that the left-hand side is positive. Wedge it with the smooth semipositive form $\zeta$ and push the result forward to the base. Since $\zeta^2=0$ and the fiber integral of $\zeta$ is two,
\[
 \pi_*\bigl((\omega+\ddc u)\wedge\zeta\bigr)
 =2(\sigma+\ddc v)\geq0;
\]
this identity of currents follows by testing against smooth forms on the base and integrating over the fibers. It proves the converse.

For the measure formula, first let $v\in\PSH(\mathbb P^{n-1},\sigma)$ be bounded. By \cref{lem:bounded-products-submersion}, its bounded products commute with $\pi^*$. Since $\zeta^2=0$ and the base has dimension $n-1$, expanding the power gives
\[
 (\omega+\ddc u)^n
 =n\,\pi^*(\sigma+\ddc v)^{n-1}\wedge\zeta,
\]
and fiber integration identifies the right-hand side with the measure defined above.

For general $v$, normalize $v\leq0$ and set $v_j=\max(v,-j)$. Since $\sigma$ is positive, these are bounded $\sigma$-plurisubharmonic functions, and their pullbacks are exactly $u_j=\max(u,-j)$. Multiply the bounded identity by the common indicator function $\mathbf1_{\{u>-j\}}=\pi^*\mathbf1_{\{v>-j\}}$ and pass to the increasing limits defining the extended measures. Fiber integration of nonnegative functions justifies the limit on the right-hand side, including over the poles.

The mass formula follows because each fiber has $\zeta$-mass two. The extended mass on the base is at most one: on $\{v>-j\}$, its truncated measure is bounded above by the full bounded product, whose mass is $\int\sigma^{n-1}=1$ by Stokes' theorem for the closed form $\sigma$. For bounded $v$ nothing is removed, and equality holds.

For the last assertion, take bounded truncations of $v\in\PSH(\mathbb P^{n-1},k\sigma)$. By \cref{lem:bounded-products-submersion}, the $n$th powers of their pulled-back currents vanish. Restricting to the truncation levels and taking the increasing limit shows that the extended measure is zero; this includes the case $k=0$.
\end{proof}

If an $\omega$-plurisubharmonic potential is constant on the fibers, the local-section argument in \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion} shows that it descends to a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on the base. The theorem therefore bounds its Hermitian mass by $2n$. On the Hopf threefold this bound is six, so the unbounded masses of \cref{chap:hopf} require potentials that vary along the fibers.

For the equation, let $p>1$ and let $f\geq0$ belong to $L^p(\mathbb P^{n-1},\sigma^{n-1})$, with $\int f\sigma^{n-1}>0$. For potentials $u=\pi^*v$ with bounded $v\in\PSH(\mathbb P^{n-1},\sigma)$, the equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-reduced-equation}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!Hopf reduction}
 (\omega+\ddc u)^n=c(f\circ\pi)\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad c>0,
\end{equation}
is equivalent to the base equation
\[
 (\sigma+\ddc v)^{n-1}=\frac{c}{2n}f\sigma^{n-1}.
\]
Indeed, the base equation implies \eqref{eq:hopf-reduced-equation} by \cref{thm:hopf-descended-hermitian-ma} and the formula for $\mathrm{d}V$. Conversely, pushing \eqref{eq:hopf-reduced-equation} forward to the base gives $2n(\sigma+\ddc v)^{n-1}=cf\sigma^{n-1}$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Integrating the base equation determines the constant:
\[
 c=\frac{2n}{\int_{\mathbb P^{n-1}}f\sigma^{n-1}}.
\]
Since $\sigma$ is closed, it satisfies the Guan--Li condition \eqref{uni-eq:GL}. Applied to $(\mathbb P^{n-1},\sigma)$, \cref{uni-thm:GL-intro} gives a continuous solution of the base equation with this constant, unique up to additive constants among bounded $\sigma$-plurisubharmonic functions. Consequently \eqref{eq:hopf-reduced-equation} has a continuous solution, unique up to additive constants among descended potentials. For smooth positive $f$, \cref{thm:tw-smooth} on the base gives a smooth solution $v$, and the constant is again determined by Stokes' theorem.

For a real parameter $\lambda$, the same calculation shows that the equation with right-hand side $c\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}(f\circ\pi)\mathrm{d}V$ is equivalent to the base equation with right-hand side $(c/(2n))\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}f\sigma^{n-1}$.

\section{Logarithmic coordinates and the gradient simplex}

For potentials invariant under rotations of the individual coordinates, the complex Hessian becomes the real Hessian of a function of the logarithmic coordinates. Invariance under complex scaling gives this Hessian a null direction; transverse to it, positivity becomes convexity, and boundedness of the potential confines the gradient to a simplex. Logarithmic growth makes every interior point of the simplex a gradient value, and strict positivity on the base makes the gradient injective modulo the null direction.

In this section, $k>0$ is a real number. Let $v$ be a smooth real function on $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ that is invariant under the torus action
\[
 [z_1:\cdots:z_n]\longmapsto[\mathrm e^{\mathrm it_1}z_1:\cdots:\mathrm e^{\mathrm it_n}z_n],
 \qquad t\in\mathbb R^n,
\]
and put $u=\pi^*v$. On the locus where all covering coordinates $z_i$ are nonzero, set $x_i=\log|z_i|^2$. By this invariance, $u$ is a function of $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, which we denote by $u(x)$, and we set
\[
 F(x)=k\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^n \mathrm{e}^{x_i}\right)+u(x).
\]
Write $\nabla F=(F_i)$ and $D^2F=(F_{ij})$ for its Euclidean gradient and Hessian, and let $\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)$. Since $u$ is invariant under complex scaling, for every $t\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-convex-translation}
 F(x+t\mathbf1)=F(x)+kt.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hopf-convex-hyperplane}\index{Toric potential}\index{Gradient simplex}
On this locus, $k\alpha+\ddc u\geq0$ if and only if $F$ is convex. If $F$ is convex, its gradient lies in
\[
 \Sigma_k=\{p\in\mathbb R^n:p_i\geq0,\ \textstyle\sum_i p_i=k\},
\]
and $D^2F\,\mathbf1=0$. If $k\sigma+\ddc v$ is positive definite on $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, the gradient induces a bijection from $\mathbb R^n/\mathbb R\mathbf1$ onto the relative interior of $\Sigma_k$.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
On the covering space, $k\alpha+\ddc u=\ddc F$. In the coordinates $z_i$, the Hermitian coefficient matrix of this form is a positive constant times
\[
 \left(\frac{F_{ij}}{z_i\overline z_j}\right)_{i,j}.
\]
This matrix is congruent to the real Hessian $(F_{ij})$ through the invertible diagonal matrix with entries $1/z_i$, which proves the first equivalence. Differentiating \eqref{eq:hopf-convex-translation} gives $\sum_i F_i=k$ and $D^2F\,\mathbf1=0$.

Since $v$ is bounded on the compact base, there is a constant $C$ such that
\[
 \left|F(x)-k\log\sum_i \mathrm{e}^{x_i}\right|\leq C.
\]
Fix $x$ and a coordinate vector $e_i$. Since $n\geq2$, the convex function $t\mapsto F(x+te_i)$ is bounded above as $t\to-\infty$. Its derivative cannot be negative anywhere: otherwise the supporting line at such a point would tend to $+\infty$ as $t\to-\infty$. Thus $F_i\geq0$, which proves $\nabla F\in\Sigma_k$.

Now assume that $k\sigma+\ddc v$ is positive definite. The kernel of the differential of the projection $\mathbb C^n\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb P^{n-1}$ is exactly the complex scalar direction, so the Hessian of $F$ is positive definite transverse to $\mathbf1$. In particular $F$ is strictly convex on $H=\{x:\sum_i x_i=0\}$. Along each line $x+te_i$, the derivative of $F$ is strictly increasing and nonnegative everywhere, hence positive. Thus every gradient lies in the relative interior of $\Sigma_k$.

Conversely, take $p\in\mathbb R^n$ with $p_i>0$ and $\sum_i p_i=k$. The function $F(x)-p\cdot x$ is invariant under translation along $\mathbf1$, and we claim that it tends to $+\infty$ on $H$ as $|x|\to\infty$. Indeed,
\[
 k\max_i x_i-p\cdot x
 =\sum_i p_i(\max_j x_j-x_i)
\]
is strictly positive at every nonzero vector of $H$, and hence has a positive minimum on the unit sphere of $H$. Since $F$ differs from $k\log\sum_i \mathrm{e}^{x_i}$ by a bounded function and $\log\sum_i \mathrm{e}^{x_i}\geq\max_i x_i$, the claim follows. Hence $F(x)-p\cdot x$ has a unique minimizer on $H$. At this point $\nabla F-p$ is a multiple of $\mathbf1$; both vectors have coordinate sum $k$, so the multiple is zero. This proves surjectivity, and strict convexity on $H$ proves injectivity.
\end{proof}
