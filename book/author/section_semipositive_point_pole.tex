\subsection{A full-mass point pole without a smooth maximizing test}
\label{subsec:point-pole-dual-nonattainment}

The envelope criteria above do not require $L^p$ integrability of the density. In the following example on projective space, with a closed background, an explicit threshold solution is unbounded, while its envelopes $P_{\delta\omega}(u)$ in smaller backgrounds are not identically $-\infty$. The dual supremum is approached by smooth tests but attained by no smooth semipositive test.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:point-pole-full-mass-nonattainment}\index{Gauduchon duality!point-pole nonattainment}\index{Monge--Amp\`ere density!point-pole integrability}
Let $X=\mathbb P^n$, $n\geq2$, and let $\omega$ be the normalized Fubini--Study form, with $\int_X\omega^n=1$ and $\mathrm{d}V=\omega^n$. For $0<\alpha<1$ put
\[
 \psi=\log\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n|z_i|^2}{\sum_{i=0}^n|z_i|^2},
 \qquad u=1-(1-\psi)^\alpha,
 \qquad o=[1:0:\cdots:0].
\]
The function $u$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$, satisfies $\sup_Xu=0$, has $o$ as its only pole, and has extended mass one. For every $0<\delta<1$, the envelope $P_{\delta\omega}(u)$ is not identically $-\infty$. The measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)$ is $f\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ and $f\notin L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ for every $p>1$.

Moreover, $b_\omega(f)=1$ and $\rho_\omega(f^{1/n})=1$, so $u$ is a threshold solution for $f$. No smooth semipositive $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$ attains $D_{f^{1/n}}(T)=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The numerator in the definition of $\psi$ vanishes only at $o$, and locally $\omega+\ddc\psi=\ddc\log\sum_{i=1}^n|z_i|^2\geq0$. The function $\chi(t)=1-(1-t)^\alpha$ is increasing and convex on $t\leq0$, with $0\leq\chi'\leq\alpha$. Applying the chain inequality to bounded truncations of $\psi$ and taking the decreasing limit, we obtain $u=\chi\circ\psi\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, with
\[
 A=\omega+\ddc u\geq(1-\alpha)\omega.
\]
The function $u$ is smooth off $o$, and it attains its supremum zero on $\{z_0=0\}$. Since $u=-(1-\psi)^\alpha+1$, \cref{prop:sublinear-threshold-envelope} shows that all the envelopes $P_{\delta\omega}(u)$ are not identically $-\infty$.

To compute the mass, use the chart $z_0=1$ and write $s=\sum_{i=1}^n|z_i|^2$. A local potential $F$ for $A$, as a function of $s$, and the function $q=sF'$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-pole-radial-flux}
 \begin{aligned}
 F(s)&=\log(1+s)+1-\left(1-\log\frac{s}{1+s}\right)^\alpha,\\
 q(s)&=sF'(s)=\frac{s+\alpha(1-\log(s/(1+s)))^{\alpha-1}}{1+s}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The eigenvalues of $A$ are $q/s$ in the tangential directions and $q'$ in the radial direction. Polar integration gives the radial top-degree measure $nq^{n-1}q'\,\mathrm{d}s$, where the angular normalization follows from the Fubini--Study case $q=s/(1+s)$, whose radial density is $ns^{n-1}(1+s)^{-n-1}\,\mathrm{d}s$ and whose total mass is one. Consequently, for $0<s_1<s_2$,
\[
 \int_{\{s_1<|z|^2<s_2\}}A^n=q(s_2)^n-q(s_1)^n.
\]
Here $q(s)\to0$ as $s\downarrow0$ and $q(s)\to1$ as $s\to\infty$. On each compact annulus the extended measure equals the smooth top power, and it puts no mass on $o$ or on the hyperplane at infinity, since both are pluripolar. Exhausting the affine chart by annuli therefore gives extended mass exactly one. (This computation does not use the vanishing of the Lelong numbers.)

Near $o$ write $L=1-\log s$. Differentiating \eqref{eq:point-pole-radial-flux} gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \frac qs\sim\alpha s^{-1}L^{\alpha-1},\qquad
 q'\sim\alpha(1-\alpha)s^{-1}L^{\alpha-2},\\
 f(s)\asymp s^{-n}L^{-1-n(1-\alpha)}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The smooth volume form, written in terms of $s$, is comparable to $s^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}s$, so the contribution of a neighborhood of $o$ to $\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}V$ is comparable to $\int^\infty L^{-1-n(1-\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}L$, which is finite. For every $p>1$, the corresponding integral of $f^p$ contains an exponentially growing factor $\exp(n(p-1)L)$ and diverges. The measure is smooth off $o$ and puts no mass on $o$, so it is $f\,\mathrm{d}V$ on all of $X$. Since its total mass is one and $\omega$ is closed, \cref{thm:invariant-mass-threshold-envelopes} gives $b_\omega(f)=1$, and hence $\rho_\omega(f^{1/n})=b_\omega(f)^{-1/n}=1$.

Suppose that a smooth semipositive $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$ attained this value. The positive current $A$ satisfies $\int_XA\wedge T=1$ by Stokes' theorem for currents. On $X\setminus\{o\}$, the matrix arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 A\wedge T\geq f^{1/n}k_T\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
The right side has total mass one by attainment, so the nonnegative difference of the two sides vanishes off $o$. Since $A$ is smooth and strictly positive there, the equality case gives $T=tA^{n-1}$ on $X\setminus\{o\}$. Indeed, the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality for the positive semidefinite coefficient matrix of $T$ conjugated by $A^{1/2}$ becomes an equality only when all its eigenvalues agree; at points where $T$ is degenerate they therefore all vanish. The function $t=(T\wedge A)/A^n$ is smooth and nonnegative on $X\setminus\{o\}$.

Since $A$ is closed, the equation $\ddc T=0$ becomes $\ddc t\wedge A^{n-1}=0$, a locally strictly elliptic scalar equation on $X\setminus\{o\}$. All eigenvalues of $A$ tend to infinity at $o$, and since $n\geq2$, so do all eigenvalues of $A^{n-1}$, which are products of $n-1$ eigenvalues of $A$. Since the coefficient matrix of the smooth form $T$ is bounded, it follows that $t\to0$ at $o$.

Set $t(o)=0$. The resulting continuous nonnegative function on compact $X$ is not identically zero, by the normalization of $T$, so it attains a positive maximum away from $o$. The strong maximum principle on the connected manifold $X\setminus\{o\}$ forces it to be constant there, contrary to its zero limit at $o$. This excludes every smooth semipositive maximizing test; no assumption on the degeneracy set of $T$ was needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:point-pole-entropy-energy-threshold}\index{Entropy!point-pole threshold}
For the density and potential of \cref{prop:point-pole-full-mass-nonattainment},
\[
 \int_X f\log f\,\mathrm{d}V<+\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha<1-\frac1n.
\]
For every real $r>0$,
\[
 \int_X(-u)^r f\,\mathrm{d}V<+\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha r<n(1-\alpha).
\]
In particular, finite entropy of the density can coexist with an unbounded threshold solution of full mass and with the absence of a smooth semipositive maximizing test.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Only a neighborhood of $o$ can contribute a divergence. There the estimates in the proof of \cref{prop:point-pole-full-mass-nonattainment} give $f\,\mathrm{d}V\asymp L^{-1-n(1-\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}L$, $\log f\sim nL$ and $-u\asymp L^\alpha$. Hence the entropy integral converges exactly when $n(1-\alpha)>1$, and the weighted integral exactly when $\alpha r<n(1-\alpha)$. In the equality cases both integrals are comparable to $\int^\infty L^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}L$ and diverge. The negative part of $f\log f$ is bounded, hence integrable for the probability volume, so the entropy criterion is unambiguous.
\end{proof}

These are integrals of the single density $f$. They should be distinguished from uniform moment or entropy bounds for the normalized determinant measures of a maximizing sequence, as in \cref{thm:gauduchon-moment-threshold}; since the present example has no smooth semipositive maximizer, it lies outside the setting of \cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation}. The proposition concerns the explicit threshold solution $u$; identifying it with the greatest normalized threshold subsolution $\Phi_f$ would require a separate uniqueness argument.
