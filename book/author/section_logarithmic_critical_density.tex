\subsection{A logarithmic density with threshold one}
\label{subsec:logarithmic-critical-density}

The power capacity condition can hold for a density outside every $L^p$ class with $p>1$. A bounded continuous potential with logarithmic modulus at one point gives such a datum on each fixed Hermitian background.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:logarithmic-critical-density}\index{Monge--Ampere measure@Monge--Amp\`ere measure!logarithmic density}\index{Uniform tails!logarithmic root estimate}
In the setting of \cref{thm:power-capacity-critical-equation}, fix $\alpha>0$. There is $u\in\PSH(X,h)\cap C(X)$ whose measure $\mathrm{MA}_h(u)=f\,\mathrm dV$ satisfies
\[
 f\in L^1(\mathrm dV),\qquad
 f\notin L^p(\mathrm dV)\quad\text{for every }p>1.
\]
For some $A>0$ it satisfies \eqref{eq:datum-power-capacity}, and $b_h(f)=1$; thus $u$ is a threshold solution for $f$. For a constant $C$ and all sufficiently large $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-root-tail}
 \rho_h\bigl(f^{1/n}\mathbf1_{\{f>K\}}\bigr)
       \leq C(\log K)^{-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a point $x_0\in X$ and holomorphic coordinates $z$ centered at $x_0$ on a neighborhood of the closed unit ball $\{|z|\leq1\}$, and write $R=|z|$ and $B_r=\{R<r\}$. Choose a smooth cutoff $0\leq\chi\leq1$ supported in $B_1$ and equal to one on a smaller ball. Put
\[
 q(s)=(1-s)^{-\alpha},\qquad s=\log R^2,
 \qquad \psi=\chi q(\log R^2)+1-\chi.
\]
Set $\psi(x_0)=0$ and extend $\psi=1$ outside the ball. In the smaller ball, $q'=\alpha(1-s)^{-\alpha-1}>0$ and $q''=\alpha(\alpha+1)(1-s)^{-\alpha-2}>0$, so $\psi$ is plurisubharmonic there, including its continuous extension at $x_0$. The transition is smooth and $\ddc\psi\geq-K_0h$ globally for a fixed $K_0$. For sufficiently small $\delta>0$, the normalized potential $u=\delta\psi-\sup_X\delta\psi$ satisfies $h+\ddc u\geq h/2$.

By \cref{prop:bounded-products}, the bounded product $\mathrm{MA}_h(u)$ does not charge the pluripolar set $\{x_0\}$, and off $x_0$ the potential is smooth. Thus the measure, which has finite mass, is absolutely continuous; write it as $f\,\mathrm dV$ with $f\in L^1(\mathrm dV)$.

In the smaller ball, where $u=\delta q(\log R^2)-\delta$, the tangential and radial eigenvalues of the complex Hessian of $q(\log R^2)$ are $q'/R^2$ and $q''/R^2$. With the normalization $\ddc=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial$, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-density-radial}
 (\ddc u)^n
 =\frac{\delta^nn!\alpha^n(\alpha+1)}{\pi^n}
    R^{-2n}(1-\log R^2)^{-n(\alpha+1)-1}\,\mathrm d\lambda,
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm d\lambda$ is Euclidean volume.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For $r$ smaller than the radius of the smaller ball, integration over $B_r$ gives
\[
 \int_{B_r}(\ddc u)^n
       =\delta^n\alpha^n(1-\log r^2)^{-n(\alpha+1)}.
\]
In the expansion of $\mathrm{MA}_h(u)$ into the terms $\binom nk(\ddc u)^k\wedge h^{n-k}$, all terms are positive in this ball. For $k<n$, their densities are bounded by
$CR^{-2k}(1-\log R^2)^{-k(\alpha+1)}$, and their masses on $B_r$ are $O(r^2)$. For $p>1$, the pure top term gives a positive multiple of the divergent integral
\[
 \int_0^r R^{2n(1-p)-1}
       (1-\log R^2)^{-p(n(\alpha+1)+1)}\,\mathrm dR
\]
as a lower bound for $\int_{B_r}f^p\,\mathrm dV$, since smooth volume densities are comparable. Thus $f\notin L^p$. For all sufficiently small $r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-density-ball}
 \int_{B_r}f\,\mathrm dV
 \leq C\bigl[(1-\log r^2)^{-n(\alpha+1)}+r^2\bigr],
 \qquad \mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_{X\setminus B_r}f\leq Cr^{-2n}.
\end{equation}

By \eqref{eq:global-volume-capacity}, applied with $h$ in place of $\omega$, every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies
\[
 \mathrm dV(E)\leq C\exp\bigl(-a\operatorname{Cap}_h(E)^{-1/n}\bigr).
\]
Let $s=\operatorname{Cap}_h(E)>0$ be small and choose $r=\exp[-as^{-1/n}/(4n)]$. Splitting $E$ inside and outside $B_r$ and applying \eqref{eq:logarithmic-density-ball}, we obtain
\[
 \int_Ef\,\mathrm dV
 \leq Cs^{1+\alpha}
       +C\exp[-as^{-1/n}/(2n)]
       +C\exp[-as^{-1/n}/2]
 \leq C's^{1+\alpha}.
\]
For larger $s$ use the finite total mass; for $s=0$ the volume estimate gives $\mathrm dV(E)=0$. This proves \eqref{eq:datum-power-capacity} for every Borel set.

Suppose that $v\in\PSH(X,h)$ and $a>1$ satisfy $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v)\geq af\,\mathrm dV$. For every real $A$, the bounded potential $u+A$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}_h(u+A)=f\,\mathrm dV\leq a^{-1}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v)$, $P_{h/2}(u+A)\geq\inf_Xu+A$ and $v\preceq u+A$. \Cref{thm:singular-strict-domination}, with $\delta=1/2$ and $c=a^{-1}$, gives $v\leq u+A$, which is impossible for $A$ small at a point where $v$ is finite. Hence every subsolution coefficient is at most $1$, and $u$ attains $1$, so $b_h(f)=1$ by \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}.

Finally take $r_K=K^{-1/(4n)}$. The outside bound in \eqref{eq:logarithmic-density-ball} gives $f\leq CK^{1/2}<K$ almost everywhere on $X\setminus B_{r_K}$ for large $K$. Therefore
\[
 \int_{\{f>K\}}f\,\mathrm dV
 \leq C(\log K)^{-n(\alpha+1)}+CK^{-1/(2n)}
 \leq C'(\log K)^{-n(\alpha+1)}.
\]
Apply \eqref{eq:power-capacity-critical-bound} to $g=f\mathbf1_{\{f>K\}}$, for which $g^{1/n}=f^{1/n}\mathbf1_{\{f>K\}}$. Since the exponent there is $\alpha/(n(1+\alpha))$, the last bound gives \eqref{eq:logarithmic-root-tail}.
\end{proof}

The potential has logarithmic modulus at $x_0$ and is not H\"older there. Its root-tail estimate follows from the capacity bound despite the failure of higher $L^p$ integrability. The construction applies to each fixed background, including one with $v_-(h)=0$.
