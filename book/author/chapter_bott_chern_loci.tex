\chapter{Bott--Chern classes and singular loci}
\label{chap:bott-chern-loci}

\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

This part of the book returns to closed backgrounds. A closed real $(1,1)$-form has a Bott--Chern class, and positivity can then be asked of the class rather than of a chosen representative. On a compact K\"ahler manifold, the positivity of a class is described by its non-nef and non-K\"ahler loci, by intersection numbers and by volumes. The present chapter asks which parts of this description survive on an arbitrary compact complex manifold, where no K\"ahler metric controls masses, and how closed classes interact with the Hermitian equations of the preceding parts. In \cref{chap:lck}, closed classes arise from the Lee form of a locally conformally K\"ahler metric.

Throughout the chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$ as in the Conventions, and $\omega$ is a fixed Hermitian form on $X$; sections that require a K\"ahler manifold or a special geometry say so at their beginning. Without a K\"ahler class, Gauduchon metrics provide the substitute mass control. Pairing a closed current with the $(n-1)$st power of a Gauduchon metric eliminates its potential, so the currents of a fixed class whose negative part is bounded by a multiple of $\omega$ have uniformly bounded mass. With this compactness, regularization defines the non-nef locus through Lelong numbers of minimal currents and the non-K\"ahler locus through polar sets of K\"ahler currents. These loci separate the singularities forced by the class from those of a particular current. Restricted volumes, which describe the same loci on K\"ahler manifolds, behave differently: computed with a Hermitian error, they can be infinite even for the zero class. When $X$ belongs to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$, pulling a class back to a K\"ahler resolution of each subvariety gives instead a finite restricted mass; on a smooth subvariety, its positivity detects bigness of the restricted class. For a divisorial trace with bounded remainder, the non-pluripolar mass is the top intersection of a nef residual class; positive mass produces a K\"ahler current, but possibly with a larger singularity than the trace.

Closed classes also constrain the Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere equations of earlier chapters. A divisorial part of $\omega+\ddc u$ consumes Gauduchon trace but contributes no density to the non-pluripolar measure, so it lowers the largest coefficient of a subsolution. At the maximal coefficient, weak limits of maximizing Gauduchon tests annihilate the class of the divisor. On the elliptic threefold of \cref{sec:vanishing-lower-mass}, closedness forces every positive current to descend to the base surface. No closed class is big there, although small Hermitian perturbations of the boundary classes have positive variational volume, whose degeneration can be computed explicitly.

Smooth solutions raise a metric question. For a big class on a compact K\"ahler manifold, the solution of the Monge--Amp\`ere equation with a smooth positive density defines a K\"ahler metric on the ample locus, but its smoothness there does not determine the distance to the complement. Zeros of the density can come from ramification, from collapsed exceptional fibers or from a singular Hessian, and they give different normal lengths even when the determinant vanishes to the same order. For radial equations, boundedness of the potential and finiteness of the distance are governed by two different integrals: a continuous minimal remainder can have a complete end, whereas a H\"older remainder forces finite distance. For exponential equations with a divisorial pole, dividing the determinant by its tangential part gives a lower bound for normal length and a sharp logarithmic threshold for completeness. Explicit surfaces show that minimal singularities and uniqueness of the solution do not prevent incompleteness, even in a nef and big class.

At the boundary of the nef cone, smooth semipositive representatives have a different metric behavior. On a complex torus, a constant semipositive form whose kernel leaves are dense is the only closed positive current in its class, and adding a small fixed K\"ahler form produces metrics whose diameters tend to zero. An explicit family of flat metrics collapses in the same way at a class with many semipositive representatives, so the collapse depends on how the class is approached. On the resolution of a Kummer quotient, an irrational nef boundary class has many smooth semipositive representatives. In a family of blown-up projective planes, degree-zero curves on the nearby fibers force a larger singularity along one component of the central curve, so a prescribed semipositive metric on the special fiber does not extend to the family. Finally, on Oeljeklaus--Toma manifolds the natural closed semipositive class contains positive currents supported on real hypersurfaces but no singular potentials, and in dimension three a natural Gauduchon metric has masses on both sides of its background mass.

\section{Closed classes with Hermitian lower bounds}\label{sec:bc-hermitian-bounds}\index{Bott--Chern class!positivity}\label{def:bc-class-positivity}\index{Big class}

Testing a closed current against a Gauduchon form eliminates its potential. The resulting trace depends only on the class, and it bounds the mass of every almost positive representative.

Recall from \cref{sec:bc-closed-currents} that $H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\R)$ is the space of smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms modulo $\ddc$ of smooth real functions, and that a class is \emph{pseudoeffective} if it contains a closed positive current. It is \emph{nef} if, for every $\varepsilon>0$, it has a smooth representative bounded below by $-\varepsilon\omega$ (\cref{sec:nef-cartier-realizations}). Fix a class $\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\R)$ and a smooth closed representative $\theta$. By \cref{lem:bc-one-one-current-description}, the closed real $(1,1)$-currents in $\alpha$ are exactly the currents $T=\theta+\ddc u$ with $u$ a real distribution, and the potential of a positive one is quasi-plurisubharmonic. A closed current $T$ with $T\geq\delta\omega$ for some $\delta>0$ is called a \emph{K\"ahler current}; we also say that such a current is \emph{strict}. The class $\alpha$ is \emph{big} if it contains a K\"ahler current, that is, if $\theta$ is big in the sense of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}. None of these properties depends on the choice of $\theta$, because replacing $\theta$ by $\theta+\ddc f$ with $f$ smooth only changes the potentials by $-f$.

For a pseudoeffective class $\alpha$ and $\varepsilon>0$, let
\[
 \alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]
 =\{T\in\alpha:T\geq-\varepsilon\omega\}
\]
be the set of closed currents in $\alpha$ whose negative part is bounded by $\varepsilon\omega$.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bc-gauduchon-compactness}
For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the currents in $\alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]$ have uniformly bounded distributional mass.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$. For $T=\theta+\ddc u$ in $\alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]$, the current $T+\varepsilon\omega$ is positive, and since $\ddc\gamma^{n-1}=0$, Stokes' theorem for currents gives
\[
 \int_X(T+\varepsilon\omega)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 =\int_X(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\]
The right-hand side does not depend on $T$. Since $\gamma$ is comparable to any fixed reference metric, this trace bounds all coefficient measures of the positive current $T+\varepsilon\omega$. Subtracting the fixed smooth form $\varepsilon\omega$ gives the asserted bound for $T$. In dimension one the same argument applies with $\gamma^{n-1}=1$ and the ordinary Stokes theorem.
\end{proof}

The mass bound of \cref{prop:bc-gauduchon-compactness} also shows that a nef class $\alpha$ is pseudoeffective. Indeed, choose smooth representatives $\theta_j\in\alpha$ with $\theta_j\geq-\varepsilon_j\omega$, where $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$. They have the same Gauduchon trace and hence uniformly bounded mass. A weak cluster value is a closed current, positive because the negative parts tend to zero, and it belongs to $\alpha$ because the smooth representative map of \cref{lem:constant-trace-bc-representative} is continuous.

\section{Minimal multiplicities and the non-nef locus}
\label{sec:bc-minimal-multiplicities}

In this section $\alpha$ is pseudoeffective. A current in $\alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]$ may have singularities that the class does not force. To isolate the forced ones, take in each family a current with the mildest singularities and let the allowed negative error tend to zero. The Lelong numbers that persist define the non-nef locus.

For $\varepsilon>0$ put
\[
 V_\varepsilon=V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega},
 \qquad T_{\min,\varepsilon}=\theta+\ddc V_\varepsilon,
\]
where $V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}=P_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}(0)$ is the normalized envelope of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}. Since $\alpha$ is pseudoeffective, $\PSH(X,\theta+\varepsilon\omega)$ is nonempty, so $V_\varepsilon$ is $(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)$-plurisubharmonic and $T_{\min,\varepsilon}\in\alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]$. Moreover, $V_\varepsilon$ has minimal singularities in this family: $u-\sup_Xu\leq V_\varepsilon$ for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta+\varepsilon\omega)$.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{definition}\label{def:bc-minimal-multiplicity}\index{Minimal multiplicity}\index{Non-nef locus}
For $x\in X$, the \emph{minimal multiplicity} of $\alpha$ at $x$ is
\[
 \nu(\alpha,x)=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\nu(T_{\min,\varepsilon},x).
\]
The \emph{non-nef locus} is
\[
 E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)=\{x\in X:\nu(\alpha,x)>0\}.
\]
\end{definition}

As $\varepsilon$ decreases, the family $\alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]$ shrinks, so the Lelong numbers of its minimal current can only increase; the supremum is therefore a limit as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Two Hermitian forms are comparable up to a constant factor, so the families defined by them are nested after rescaling $\varepsilon$; hence $\nu(\alpha,x)$ does not depend on $\omega$. Replacing $\theta$ by $\theta+\ddc f$ changes the minimal potentials by $-f$ up to a bounded normalization error, which does not affect Lelong numbers.

The smooth regularization of \cref{subsec:psh-closed-regularization} turns vanishing Lelong numbers into an arbitrarily small loss of positivity. This gives the converse direction in the following characterization.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bc-non-nef}
For a pseudoeffective class $\alpha$, the set $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)$ is a countable union of analytic subsets. Moreover,
\[
 \alpha\text{ is nef}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)=\varnothing.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the monotonicity just noted,
\[
 E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)
 =\bigcup_{k,\ell\geq1}
       \{x:\nu(T_{\min,1/\ell},x)\geq1/k\},
\]
and each set in this union is analytic by Siu's analyticity theorem (\cref{subsec:psh-lelong-levels}).

If $\alpha$ is nef, each family $\alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]$ contains a smooth current $\theta+\ddc f$. By minimality, $V_\varepsilon\geq f-\sup_Xf$, so $V_\varepsilon$ is bounded and all Lelong numbers of $T_{\min,\varepsilon}$ vanish. Conversely, if the non-nef locus is empty, every $T_{\min,\varepsilon}$ has zero Lelong numbers. The regularization just recalled then gives a smooth representative of $\alpha$ bounded below by $-2\varepsilon\omega$. Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, $\alpha$ is nef.
\end{proof}

The approximating families cannot be replaced by the single family of positive currents in $\alpha$: even for a nef class, a positive current with minimal singularities in $\alpha$ itself may have nonzero Lelong numbers; see the example discussed in \cref{sec:history-closed-loci}.

\section{The non-K\"ahler locus}
\label{sec:bc-non-kahler-locus}

A big class contains K\"ahler currents, but in general none of them is smooth. The points at which every K\"ahler current in the class is singular form its non-K\"ahler locus. Analytic regularization, maxima of potentials and the descending chain condition for analytic sets produce a single K\"ahler current whose singular set is exactly this locus.

For an almost positive closed $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$, write
\[
 E_+(T)=\{x\in X:\nu(T,x)>0\}
\]
for its \emph{positive Lelong set}, where $\nu(T,x)$ is the Lelong number of a local potential of $T$ at $x$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:bc-non-kahler-locus}\index{Non-Kahler locus@Non-K\"ahler locus}
The \emph{non-K\"ahler locus} of a big class $\alpha$ is
\[
 E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=\bigcap_{T}E_+(T),
\]
where $T$ ranges over the K\"ahler currents in $\alpha$. For a pseudoeffective class that is not big, we set $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=X$.
\end{definition}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bc-non-kahler-locus}
Let $\alpha$ be big. There is a K\"ahler current in $\alpha$ with analytic singularities, smooth off its polar set, whose positive Lelong set is exactly $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$. In particular this locus is a proper analytic subset, and
\[
 E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha),
 \qquad
 E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\alpha\text{ is K\"ahler}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: reduction to analytic singularities.} By the analytic regularization of \cref{subsec:psh-closed-regularization}, a K\"ahler current $T\in\alpha$ can be approximated by currents in $\alpha$ with analytic singularities and smooth remainder away from their poles. After a sufficiently small loss of positivity these are still K\"ahler currents, and their positive Lelong sets are contained in $E_+(T)$. Hence the intersection defining $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$ may be taken over K\"ahler currents with analytic singularities and smooth remainder.

\emph{Step 2: intersections.} Let $\theta+\ddc u_1$ and $\theta+\ddc u_2$ be two such currents. By \cref{lem:max-psh}, $\max(u_1,u_2)$ is the potential of a current dominating $\delta\omega$ for a common $\delta>0$, and its Lelong number at each point is the minimum of those of $u_1$ and $u_2$. Regularizing this current as in Step~1 gives a K\"ahler current with analytic singularities whose positive Lelong set is contained in the intersection of the two previous sets.

\emph{Step 3: realization.} The positive Lelong sets of Step~1 are analytic subsets of $X$. By the descending chain condition for analytic subsets of a compact complex manifold, some finite intersection of them is minimal, and it then equals the full intersection $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$. Repeated use of Step~2 gives a single K\"ahler current with analytic singularities whose polar set, which is also its positive Lelong set, is contained in this finite intersection. Since $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$ is contained in the positive Lelong set of every K\"ahler current in $\alpha$, equality holds.

\emph{Step 4: the two consequences.} For a positive current $T\in\alpha$ and $\varepsilon>0$, we have $T\in\alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]$, so minimality gives $\nu(T_{\min,\varepsilon},x)\leq\nu(T,x)$. Taking the supremum over $\varepsilon$ and then the intersection over K\"ahler currents $T$ gives $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$. If the non-K\"ahler locus is empty, the current of Step~3 has no poles and is therefore a K\"ahler form in $\alpha$. Conversely, if $\alpha$ contains a K\"ahler form, using it in the defining intersection gives $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bc-no-isolated-locus}
The non-K\"ahler locus of a big class has no isolated points.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\theta+\ddc\Phi\geq\delta\omega$ be a current as in \cref{thm:bc-non-kahler-locus}, and suppose that $x$ is an isolated point of its polar set. Choose holomorphic coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$ on a ball centered at $x$ that contains no other pole. For a sufficiently large constant $A>0$ and any real constant $C$, the local function $q(z)=A|z|^2-C$ satisfies $\theta+\ddc q\geq\delta'\omega$ on a smaller ball, for some $\delta'>0$.

Since $\Phi$ is smooth on a closed annular collar near the boundary of this smaller ball, we can choose $C$ so that $q<\Phi$ on the collar. Since $\Phi\to-\infty$ at $x$, the function $\max(\Phi,q)$ agrees with $q$ near $x$ and with $\Phi$ on the collar. By \cref{lem:qpsh-gluing}, it glues with $\Phi$ outside the ball to the potential of a K\"ahler current in $\alpha$ that is smooth near $x$. Its Lelong number at $x$ is zero, contradicting $x\in E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$.
\end{proof}

Bigness of a closed class has a strong global consequence. A compact complex manifold carries a K\"ahler current if and only if it belongs to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$; this is the classical modification criterion discussed in \cref{sec:history-closed-loci}. Hence a compact complex manifold outside class $\mathcal C$ has no big Bott--Chern class at all, whereas the strictly positive nonclosed backgrounds studied in this book exist on every compact complex manifold.

\section{Null loci and numerical restricted volumes}

In this section $X$ is K\"ahler and $\omega$ is a K\"ahler form. For a closed class $\alpha$ and an irreducible analytic subvariety $V\subset X$ of positive dimension, the intersection number $\int_V\alpha^{\dim V}$ is computed by pulling back a smooth closed representative to a resolution of $V$; by Stokes' theorem it does not depend on the representative. For a nef class $\alpha$, the subvarieties on which this number vanishes form the classical \emph{null locus}
\[
 \operatorname{Null}(\alpha)
 =\bigcup_{\substack{V\subset X\ \mathrm{irreducible},\ \dim V>0\\
                       \int_V\alpha^{\dim V}=0}}V.
\]
For a nef and big class, the classical theorem discussed in \cref{sec:history-closed-loci} gives
\[
 E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)=\operatorname{Null}(\alpha).
\]
The same equality holds on compact manifolds in class $\mathcal C$.

For a pseudoeffective class that is not nef, intersection numbers are replaced by numerical restricted volumes. Let $V\subset X$ be an irreducible compact analytic subvariety of dimension $k>0$, with regular locus $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. If $V\subset E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha)$, the numerical restricted volume of $\alpha$ along $V$ is defined to be zero. Otherwise, for real $\varepsilon>0$, let $T$ range over the closed currents in $\alpha$ with $T\geq-\varepsilon\omega$ that have analytic singularities with smooth remainder and whose polar set does not contain $V$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bc-numerical-restricted-volume}
 \langle\alpha^k\rangle_{X\mid V}
 =\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\ \sup_T
 \int_{V_{\mathrm{reg}}}
      \bigl((T+\varepsilon\omega)|_{V_{\mathrm{reg}}}\bigr)_{\mathrm{ac}}^k,
\end{equation}
where the subscript $\mathrm{ac}$ denotes the absolutely continuous part of a measure on $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Because $T$ has smooth remainder, the integrand is the extension by zero of a smooth measure defined away from an analytic subset of $V$.

The \emph{numerical null locus} of $\alpha$ is the union of the positive-dimensional irreducible subvarieties on which \eqref{eq:bc-numerical-restricted-volume} vanishes. For big classes, the trace theory of \cite[Section~8.3]{SGPT} expresses these restricted volumes as masses of trace currents on $V$; this is why singularity types and restrictions of multiplier ideals enter into the identification of this locus. Such a trace need not equal the pointwise restriction of a singular potential; see \cref{chap:trace-data}.

\section{Restricted volumes and Hermitian perturbations}

We return to an arbitrary compact complex manifold $X$ with a Hermitian form $\omega$. In \eqref{eq:bc-numerical-restricted-volume}, the negative part of $T$ is measured by a closed K\"ahler form. If the closed perturbation is replaced by the Hermitian form $\omega$, the supremum can be infinite even for the zero Bott--Chern class $\{0\}$ and $V=X$. Unbounded Monge--Amp\`ere masses of smooth potentials, such as those on the Hopf threefold, already produce this failure.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bc-zero-class-infinite-error-volume}
Suppose there are smooth real functions $v_j$ on $X$, for positive integers $j$, such that
\[
 \omega+\ddc v_j>0,
 \qquad \int_X(\omega+\ddc v_j)^n\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
Then for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bc-zero-class-infinite-error-volume}
 \sup_{\substack{T\in\{0\}\ \mathrm{smooth,\ closed}\\
                          T\geq-\varepsilon\omega}}
       \int_X(T+\varepsilon\omega)^n=+\infty.
\end{equation}
This occurs for the Gauduchon form on the Hopf threefold in \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and take $T_j=\varepsilon\ddc v_j$. Each $T_j$ is smooth and closed and represents the zero Bott--Chern class. Moreover,
\[
 T_j+\varepsilon\omega
 =\varepsilon(\omega+\ddc v_j)>0,
\]
and its top mass $\varepsilon^n\int_X(\omega+\ddc v_j)^n$ tends to $+\infty$. The functions $v_j$ on the Hopf threefold are provided by \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}.
\end{proof}

The currents $T_j$ are smooth, so requiring analytic singularities with smooth remainder does not exclude them. Moreover, the supremum is infinite for each fixed $\varepsilon$, so letting $\varepsilon$ tend to zero afterwards cannot produce a finite limit. By contrast, if $\omega$ is K\"ahler and $T=\ddc v$ is smooth, Stokes' theorem gives
\[
 \int_X(T+\varepsilon\omega)^n
 =\varepsilon^n\int_X\omega^n\longrightarrow0.
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
On the other hand, the zero class is nef, its non-nef locus is empty, and its only positive representative is the zero current. Indeed, a positive representative $\ddc u\geq0$ would make $u$ a global plurisubharmonic function on a compact manifold, hence constant. Thus \eqref{eq:bc-zero-class-infinite-error-volume} measures the nonlinear mass of the Hermitian error family rather than positivity of the closed class.

A numerical theory of restricted volumes in a Hermitian background must therefore either control the approximating family or use a different invariant. Two qualitative loci avoid this supremum. The non-Hermitian locus of a big background is defined through its strict currents, whose exterior derivative is prescribed by the background; Dang's null locus tests failure of bigness after restriction to subvarieties. The singularities allowed in the first definition, and the equality of the two loci for nef and big backgrounds, are recalled in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems}.
