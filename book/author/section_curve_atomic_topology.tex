\subsection{Total variation of the atomic data}
\label{sec:curve-atomic-topology}

The distance calculation extends to all multiplier-ideal envelopes $P_\beta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$ of \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope} on the curve $Y$. Such an envelope is determined by its atomic curvature, and the maximum of two of them has as atomic curvature the common part of the two atomic measures. The formula for the distance of ordered potentials can therefore compare envelopes whose poles are unrelated. Throughout this subsection, fix a smooth positive form $\beta$ on $Y$, put $A=\int_Y\beta$, and choose a nonpositive Green kernel $G$ for $\eta=\beta/A$. All atomic measures below have countable support and finite total mass.

For a positive atomic measure $D$ with $D(Y)\leq A$, set
\[
 w_D(x)=\int_YG(x,p)\,\mathrm{d}D(p),\qquad
 q_D=P_\beta[w_D]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
As in the proof of \cref{thm:curve-trace-realization}, $w_D$ is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function, not identically $-\infty$,
with $\ddc w_D=D-D(Y)\eta$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}
The function $w_D$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic, and
\[
 q_D\sim w_D,\qquad
 \int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\beta(q_D)
 =\int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\beta(w_D)=A-D(Y).
\]
The map $D\mapsto q_D$ is a bijection from the positive atomic measures of mass at most $A$ onto the set of all envelopes $P_\beta[a]_{\mathcal I}$, $a\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$, and $q_D$ has positive mass exactly when $D(Y)<A$. The measure $D$ is the atomic part of
$\beta+\ddc q_D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
One has
\[
 \beta+\ddc w_D=D+(A-D(Y))\eta\geq0.
\]
Every atom of $D$ is a pole of $w_D$, and the smooth measure $\eta$ charges
no polar set, including any additional poles caused by accumulation of
the atoms. Hence $\mathrm{MA}_\beta(w_D)=(A-D(Y))\eta$.

Put $q=q_D$. By \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection}, $q$ has the same multiplier ideals, and hence the same Lelong numbers at points, as $w_D$. Applied to $\ddc w_D=D-D(Y)\eta$, \cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination} gives $q\preceq w_D$, and the envelope inequality $q\geq w_D-\sup_Yw_D$ proves $q\sim w_D$. By \cref{cor:polar-order-maximum}, the polar part of $\beta+\ddc q$ is therefore also $D$, and Stokes' theorem gives the non-pluripolar mass $A-D(Y)$.

For any $a\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$, its Lelong numbers at points give an atomic
measure $D_a$ of mass at most $A$. On a smooth curve these numbers
determine all multiplier ideals, by \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}.
Thus $a\sim_{\mathcal I}w_{D_a}$, so
$P_\beta[a]_{\mathcal I}=q_{D_a}$.
If $D_a(Y)=A$, the atoms exhaust the whole curvature measure, and the
non-pluripolar mass is zero. This proves surjectivity and the description of the potentials of positive mass. Distinct atomic measures give
distinct Lelong numbers, which proves injectivity.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-atomic-source-metric}\index{Trace!atomic metric}
For positive atomic measures $D,E$ with masses less than $A$,
\[
 \frac1{96}\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq d_{S,\beta}(q_D,q_E)
 \leq\frac12\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Thus the singularity distance is a metric on these ideal envelopes, and
convergence for it is exactly convergence of the atomic measures in total variation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $d_S=d_{S,\beta}$ and $a\lor b=\max(a,b)$. For potentials $u,v\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ of positive mass, besides the formula for ordered potentials we use the inequality
\cite[Lemma~6.2.1]{SGPT}
\[
 d_S(u,v)\leq d_S(u,u\lor v)+d_S(v,u\lor v)
             \leq48d_S(u,v)
\]
on a curve; here $48=3(1+1)2^{1+2}$ is the constant of that lemma in dimension one.

Put $z=\max(w_D,w_E)$. By \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}, the polar parts of the curvature measures of $w_D$ and $w_E$ are $D$ and $E$. By \eqref{eq:polar-maximum-measure}, the polar part of the curvature measure of $z$ is $D\wedge E$, the atomic measure with the smaller coefficient at each point. Stokes' theorem therefore gives
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\beta(z)(Y)=A-(D\wedge E)(Y).
\]
The formula for ordered potentials yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_S(w_D,z)+d_S(w_E,z)
 &=\frac12\bigl(D(Y)+E(Y)-2(D\wedge E)(Y)\bigr)\\
 &=\frac12\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Combining this equality with the two inequalities above proves both bounds, since replacing $w_D,w_E$ by the ordinarily equivalent $q_D,q_E$ does not change the singularity distance. The lower bound and the injectivity in \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model} make the pseudometric a metric on these envelopes.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:curve-ideal-source-completeness}
For $0<\varepsilon<A$, the normalized ideal envelopes satisfying
$\mathrm{MA}_\beta(q)(Y)\geq\varepsilon$ form a complete space for
$d_{S,\beta}$. Analytic ideal envelopes are dense in this space.
The space is not totally bounded.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}, the space corresponds to the positive atomic measures of
mass at most $A-\varepsilon$. This set is complete for total variation.
Indeed, a Cauchy sequence has as limit a positive finite measure with the
same mass bound. The union of the countable supports of the sequence is
countable, and total-variation convergence shows that the limit gives zero mass to
the complement of that union, so the limit is again atomic. The two metric
estimates of \cref{thm:curve-atomic-source-metric} transfer completeness to the ideal envelopes.

Finite truncation, followed by rational approximation of each coefficient from below, preserves the mass bound and converges in total variation. The resulting Green potentials have finitely many logarithmic poles with rational coefficients, and their ideal envelopes have the same ordinary types by \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}. These envelopes therefore have analytic singularities, and the upper metric estimate proves density.

Finally, fix $0<a<A-\varepsilon$. The measures $a\delta_p$, for distinct
$p\in Y$, have pairwise total-variation distance $2a$, so their ideal
envelopes have pairwise $d_S$ distance at least $a/48$. This infinite
separated family rules out total boundedness.
\end{proof}

This topology differs from the one given by the $L^1$ metric on ideal envelopes of \cref{prop:ideal-lone-metric}, since that metric is totally bounded. The sequence in \cref{prop:positive-mass-ideal-discontinuity} makes the difference quantitative: it converges for that $L^1$ metric while remaining at a fixed positive $d_S$ distance from its limit.
