\subsection{Total variation of the atomic data}
\label{sec:curve-atomic-topology}

The ordered calculation extends to a comparison for arbitrary ideal
envelopes of positive mass on the curve. Fix a smooth positive form
$\theta$ on $Y$, write $A=\int_Y\theta$, and let $G\leq0$ be a Green
kernel for $\eta=\theta/A$. A positive atomic measure below means a
countable sum of Dirac masses with finite total mass. We use the norm
$\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=|\mu|(Y)$.

For a positive atomic measure $D$ with $D(Y)<A$, set
\[
 w_D(x)=\int_YG(x,p)\,\mathrm{d}D(p),\qquad
 q_D=P_\theta[w_D]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
The Green construction gives a genuine quasi-plurisubharmonic function
with $\ddc w_D=D-D(Y)\eta$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}
The function $w_D$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, and
\[
 q_D\simeq w_D,\qquad
 \int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\theta(q_D)
 =\int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_D)=A-D(Y).
\]
The map $D\mapsto q_D$ is a bijection onto the normalized ideal envelopes
of positive non-pluripolar mass. The measure $D$ is the atomic part of
$\theta+\ddc q_D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
One has
\[
 \theta+\ddc w_D=D+(A-D(Y))\eta\geq0.
\]
Every atom of $D$ is a pole of $w_D$. The smooth measure $\eta$ charges
no polar set, including any additional poles caused by accumulation of
the atoms. Hence $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_D)=(A-D(Y))\eta$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Put $q=q_D$. Ideal projection retains each point Lelong number, so
$\theta+\ddc q-D$ is a positive measure. This remains true for a
countable set of atoms. As distributions,
\[
 \ddc(q-w_D)
 =(\theta+\ddc q-D)-(A-D(Y))\eta
 \geq-(A-D(Y))\eta.
\]
Thus $q-w_D$ has a global quasi-plurisubharmonic representative $b$,
which is bounded above on compact $Y$. The envelope inequality
$q\geq w_D-\sup_Yw_D$ bounds it below. Consequently $b$ is bounded and
$q\simeq w_D$. The positive measure
$(A-D(Y))\eta+\ddc b$ charges no polar set, by local boundedness after
smooth correction. Since
\[
 \theta+\ddc q=D+\bigl((A-D(Y))\eta+\ddc b\bigr),
\]
the polar part of this curvature is exactly $D$. Stokes proves its
non-pluripolar mass is $A-D(Y)$.

For any $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$, its point Lelong numbers give an atomic
measure $D_u$ of mass at most $A$. On a smooth curve these numbers
determine all multiplier ideals, by \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}.
If $D_u(Y)<A$, then $u\sim_{\mathcal I}w_{D_u}$, so
$P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=q_{D_u}$.
If $D_u(Y)=A$, its atoms exhaust the full curvature measure and its
non-pluripolar mass is zero. Applied to an ideal envelope, this proves
surjectivity onto the positive-mass locus. Distinct atomic measures have
distinct Lelong data, proving injectivity.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-atomic-source-metric}
For positive atomic measures $D,E$ with masses less than $A$,
\[
 \frac1{96}\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq d_{S,\theta}(q_D,q_E)
 \leq\frac12\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Thus the source distance is a metric on these ideal envelopes and
induces exactly total-variation convergence of their atomic data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Besides the ordered-distance formula, we use the source inequality
\cite[chapter on comparison of singularities]{SGPT}
\[
 d_S(u,v)\leq d_S(u,u\lor v)+d_S(v,u\lor v)
             \leq48d_S(u,v)
\]
on a curve. Here $u\lor v=\max(u,v)$; the source constant in dimension
$n$ is $3(n+1)2^{n+2}$.

First suppose $D,E$ have finite support and put $z=w_D\lor w_E$.
At each possible pole the logarithmic coefficient of $z$ is the smaller
of the two coefficients, with a bounded local remainder. Away from this
finite set it is locally bounded. Write $D\wedge E$ for the atomic
measure with these minimum coefficients. It follows that
$\mathrm{MA}_\theta(z)(Y)=A-(D\wedge E)(Y)$.
The two ordered comparisons therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_S(w_D,z)+d_S(w_E,z)
 &=\frac12\bigl(D(Y)+E(Y)-2(D\wedge E)(Y)\bigr)\\
 &=\frac12\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The source inequality proves both bounds for $w_D,w_E$. Their ordinary
equivalence with $q_D,q_E$ leaves the source distance unchanged.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For countable supports, choose increasing finite restrictions
$D_m\leq D$ and $E_m\leq E$ whose masses converge to the full masses.
Because $G\leq0$, one has $w_D\leq w_{D_m}$. The ordered formula gives
\[
 d_S(w_D,w_{D_m})=\tfrac12(D-D_m)(Y)\longrightarrow0,
\]
and likewise for $E$. The triangle inequality now passes
$d_S(w_{D_m},w_{E_m})$ to $d_S(w_D,w_E)$, while the total-variation
norms converge as well. This proves the estimates in general.
In particular no description of the poles of $w_D\lor w_E$ is needed
for infinite supports. The lower estimate and the lemma's injectivity
make the source pseudometric a metric on these canonical representatives.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:curve-ideal-source-completeness}
For $0<\varepsilon<A$, the normalized ideal envelopes satisfying
$\mathrm{MA}_\theta(q)(Y)\geq\varepsilon$ form a complete space for
$d_{S,\theta}$. Analytic ideal envelopes are dense in this space.
The space is not totally bounded.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By the lemma, the space corresponds to the positive atomic measures of
mass at most $A-\varepsilon$. This set is complete for total variation.
Indeed a Cauchy sequence has a positive finite-measure limit with the
same mass bound. The union of the sequence's countable supports is
countable, and total-variation convergence gives zero limit mass off
that union. The limit is therefore still atomic. The two metric
estimates transfer completeness to the ideal envelopes.

Finite truncation followed by rational approximation of each coefficient
from below preserves the mass bound and converges in total variation.
The resulting Green potentials have finitely many rational logarithmic
poles; their ideal envelopes have the same ordinary types by the lemma.
They are analytic, and the upper metric estimate proves density.

Finally fix $0<a<A-\varepsilon$. The measures $a\delta_p$, for distinct
$p\in Y$, have pairwise total-variation distance $2a$. Their ideal
envelopes have pairwise source distance at least $a/48$. This infinite
separated family rules out total boundedness.
\end{proof}

This identifies the source topology on positive-mass ideal envelopes
on a curve. Arbitrary ordinary singularities need not be represented by
their ideal envelopes. In particular this does not identify the elementary
$L^1$ ideal metric of \cref{prop:ideal-lone-metric} with the source metric:
the former is totally bounded, and \cref{prop:positive-mass-ideal-discontinuity}
exhibits a fixed positive source-distance gap along an elementary-metric
convergent sequence.
