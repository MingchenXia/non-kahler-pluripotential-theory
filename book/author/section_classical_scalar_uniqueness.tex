\begin{proposition}\label{prop:classical-scalar-uniqueness}\index{Normalization scalar!classical uniqueness}
Let $u,v\in C^2(X,\mathbb R)$, and set $A=\omega+\ddc u$ and $B=\omega+\ddc v$.
If $A,B\geq0$ and $A^n=cB^n$ for a constant $c>0$, then $c=1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$. At a maximum point of $u-(1+\varepsilon)v$, the complex Hessian of this function is nonpositive, so that
\[
 A+\varepsilon\omega\leq(1+\varepsilon)B.
\]
At this point $B>0$, because $A+\varepsilon\omega>0$. Monotonicity of the determinant and $A^n=cB^n$ give $c\leq(1+\varepsilon)^n$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives $c\leq1$, and interchanging the two potentials gives $c^{-1}\leq1$.
\end{proof}
