\section{Symmetry and the vanishing-exponent approximation}
\label{sec:symmetric-equation-selection}

Uniqueness of the positive-exponent equation forces its solutions to preserve the symmetries of the data. Uniform compactness carries those symmetries to every zero-exponent cluster limit. Applied to the projective example of \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness}, this produces a solution distinct from the two exchanged by its involution.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:symmetric-equation-clusters}
Let $\omega$ be Hermitian, let $\mathrm{d}V$ be a smooth positive volume form, and let $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, be nonzero. Let $c>0$ be the unique scalar for the bounded equation $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u)=cf\,\mathrm{d}V$. For $\lambda>0$ denote by $u_\lambda$ the unique bounded solution of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\omega(u_\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f\,\mathrm{d}V,
 \qquad a_\lambda=u_\lambda-\sup_Xu_\lambda.
\]
The family $\{a_\lambda:0<\lambda\leq1\}$ is relatively compact in $C(X)$. Every cluster limit as $\lambda\downarrow0$ is a normalized bounded solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(a)=cf\,\mathrm{d}V$.

Suppose a group $G$ of biholomorphisms of $X$ preserves $\omega$ and the measure $f\,\mathrm{d}V$. Then every $a_\lambda$ and every such cluster limit is $G$-invariant. In particular, the zero-exponent equation has a $G$-invariant normalized solution.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose one continuous solution $u$ of the zero-exponent equation using \cref{thm:kn-continuous}, and put $m=\inf_Xu$, $M=\sup_Xu$. The barriers in \cref{prop:vanishing-exponent} give, with
\[
 w_\lambda=u_\lambda-\frac{\log c}{\lambda},
\]
the uniform bounds
\[
 u-M\leq w_\lambda\leq u-m.
\]
Consequently $|w_\lambda|\leq M-m$ and $a_\lambda=w_\lambda-\sup_Xw_\lambda$ is uniformly bounded between $-2(M-m)$ and zero. Its measure is
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\omega(a_\lambda)
       =c \mathrm{e}^{\lambda w_\lambda}f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
The positive-exponent existence theorem gives continuous $u_\lambda$. The displayed densities have a uniform $L^p$ bound for $0<\lambda\leq1$, so \cref{thm:lp-uniform-compactness} gives relative compactness of the normalized family in $C(X)$.

If $\lambda_j\downarrow0$ and $a_{\lambda_j}\to a$ uniformly, then $a$ is $\omega$-psh and $\sup_Xa=0$. The bounds for $w_\lambda$ give $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda w_\lambda}\to1$ uniformly, hence the densities tend to $cf$ in $L^p$. Bounded local product continuity under uniform convergence identifies the limiting measure with $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(a)$, proving its equation.

For each $g\in G$, pullback of the positive-exponent equation shows that $u_\lambda\circ g$ solves the same equation. Its uniqueness gives $u_\lambda\circ g=u_\lambda$. Surjectivity of $g$ preserves the supremum, so $a_\lambda\circ g=a_\lambda$. Uniform convergence preserves these equalities for every $g$. Compactness supplies at least one cluster limit as $\lambda\downarrow0$, yielding the invariant solution.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:numa-third-solution}
On $\mathbb P(\mathbb C^2\oplus\mathbb C^2)$, with block homogeneous coordinates $[A:B]$, the data in \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness} can be chosen so that the same zero-exponent equation has at least three distinct normalized bounded solutions. Two are smooth, and a third is H\"older continuous and fixed by the involution $\iota[A:B]=[B:A]$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the radial coordinate $r$, rank-one forms $P,Q,R$, Fubini--Study form $\omega_X$, and scalar functions $F,x,y,z$ from \cref{subsec:numa-rank-directions,subsec:numa-scalar-coefficients}. Set $w=F(r)$, $\omega_0=xP+yQ+zR$, $H=\ddc w/2$, $A_0=\omega_0-H$ and $B_0=\omega_0+H$ as in \cref{subsec:numa-equal-cubes}. The Hermitian form is $\omega=\omega_0+\ddc\rho$, with $\rho=\varepsilon(r^2-1)\kappa(r)$ from \cref{subsec:numa-positive-background}. Choose the cutoff $\kappa$ even; all its required support and plateau properties are preserved by this choice. Then $\rho$ is even, while $w=F(r)$ is odd. The involution sends $r$ to $-r$, interchanges $P,Q$, and fixes $R$. The coefficient identities $x(-r)=y(r)$ and $z(-r)=z(r)$ show that $\iota^*\omega_0=\omega_0$. Thus $\iota^*\omega=\omega$, and $\iota^*\omega_X=\omega_X$.

Since $\iota^*H=-H$, the involution interchanges $A_0$ and $B_0$. Their cubes are equal, so $f\omega_X^3$ is invariant. It also interchanges $\widetilde u=-w/2-\rho$ and $\widetilde v=w/2-\rho$. These functions therefore have the same supremum, and the normalized solutions $u,v$ are interchanged. They are distinct, so neither is invariant.

Apply \cref{prop:symmetric-equation-clusters} to the group generated by $\iota$. Here $c=1$, as the two constructed solutions already have that scalar. An invariant cluster solution $a$ cannot equal either $u$ or $v$. Its bounded equation with smooth density gives H\"older regularity by \cref{thm:hermitian-holder-elementary}. This gives a third solution; smoothness of that third solution is not asserted.
\end{proof}

The corollary follows from the supplied counterexample and the approximation theory proved here. The compactness argument alone does not identify a unique cluster limit. In the next section we use the specific radial coefficients to prove that, for these symmetric projective data, the whole normalized family converges to the explicit $C^{1,1}$ solution of \cref{prop:numa-radial-continuum}. The general selection question remains separate from uniqueness of all solutions of the limiting equation.
