\section{Transverse concentration of Gauduchon tests}
\label{sec:gauduchon-transverse-concentration}\index{Gauduchon current!transverse concentration}

Let $\mathcal T_\omega$ consist of the smooth strictly positive members of the set $\mathscr G_\omega$ of normalized Gauduchon currents of \cref{sec:gauduchon-linear-duality}. Thus every $\Theta\in\mathcal T_\omega$ satisfies $\ddc\Theta=0$ and $\int_X\omega\wedge\Theta=1$.
These tests form a weakly relatively compact family, but their pairings with a form concentrating transversely to a hypersurface need not stay bounded. A uniform bound would, by the Lamari criterion, produce an $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function whose current charges the local hypersurface. Analyticity of Lelong level sets then forces a global hypersurface.

Choose a product coordinate chart $(z,\zeta)\in U\times\Delta_r$, with $z\in\mathbb C^{n-1}$, and a smooth compactly supported function $0\leq\chi\leq1$ on $U$, equal to one on a nonempty connected smaller polydisk $U'$. Put $\sigma_\zeta=(\mathrm i/(2\pi))\,\mathrm d\zeta\wedge\mathrm d\bar\zeta$. Fix $p\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb C)$, nonnegative and supported in the closed unit disk, with $\int p\,\sigma_\zeta=1$. For small $\delta>0$ extend
\[
 B_\delta=\chi(z)\delta^{-2}p(\zeta/\delta)\sigma_\zeta
\]
by zero to $X$, and set
\[
 C_\delta=\sup_{\Theta\in\mathcal T_\omega}
                      \int_XB_\delta\wedge\Theta.
\]
The form $B_\delta$ is smooth and semipositive. For fixed $\delta$ it is bounded above by a finite multiple of $\omega$, so $C_\delta<\infty$.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:divisor-free-transverse-concentration}\index{Divisor!transverse concentration obstruction}
If $C_{\delta_j}$ stays bounded along a sequence $\delta_j\downarrow0$, then some global analytic hypersurface contains $U'\times\{0\}$. In particular, on a manifold without analytic hypersurfaces, $C_\delta\to\infty$ as $\delta\downarrow0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $C_{\delta_j}\leq M$ and put $\kappa=(M+1)^{-1}$. For every Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, the normalized form $\Theta=\gamma^{n-1}/\int_X\omega\wedge\gamma^{n-1}$ belongs to $\mathcal T_\omega$, so
\[
 \int_X(\omega-\kappa B_{\delta_j})\wedge\Theta\geq1-\kappa M>0.
\]
The generalized Lamari criterion, \cref{thm:lamari}, applied to the smooth form $\omega-\kappa B_{\delta_j}$, gives $v_j$ with
\[
 \omega+\ddc v_j\geq\kappa B_{\delta_j},\qquad\sup_Xv_j=0.
\]
Since $B_{\delta_j}\geq0$, the potentials belong to the family $\mathcal K_\omega$ of normalized $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions. By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, a subsequence converges in $L^1$ to $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$.
As currents, the forms $B_{\delta_j}$ converge weakly to $\chi[\zeta=0]$, where $[\zeta=0]$ is the current of integration on the local hypersurface $\{\zeta=0\}$. Passing to the limit in distributions therefore gives
\[
 \omega+\ddc v\geq\kappa\chi[\zeta=0].
\]
Near each point of $U'\times\{0\}$, choose a smooth local function $\rho$ with $\ddc\rho\geq\omega$. Since $\chi=1$ on $U'$, the closed positive current $\ddc(v+\rho)$ dominates $\kappa[\zeta=0]$ there. Hence the Lelong number of $v$, which does not change under the smooth correction $\rho$, is at least $\kappa$ at every point of $U'\times\{0\}$.
By Siu's theorem in \cref{subsec:psh-lelong-levels}, the set $Z=\{\nu(v,\cdot)\geq\kappa\}$ is analytic. It lies in the pole set of $v$, which has measure zero, so $Z$ has dimension at most $n-1$. The compact analytic set $Z$ has finitely many global irreducible components. Their intersections with the connected hypersurface $U'\times\{0\}$ are analytic subsets covering it. At least one intersection is the whole hypersurface, since finitely many proper analytic subsets cannot cover an open polydisk. That component has dimension $n-1$ and is the required global hypersurface.
If no such hypersurface exists, there is no bounded sequence $C_{\delta_j}$ with $\delta_j\downarrow0$, which proves the asserted divergence.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cartier-transverse-concentration-bound}\index{Cartier divisor!bounded transverse tests}
Suppose an effective Cartier divisor $D$ agrees, with multiplicity one, with $\{\zeta=0\}$ throughout the chosen coordinate chart. Then $C_\delta$ is uniformly bounded for all sufficiently small $\delta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $s$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal O_X(D)$. In the chart write $s=\zeta e$ with $e$ a nonvanishing holomorphic frame. Choose a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ with $|e|_h=1$ on a neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp}\chi\times\{|\zeta|\leq\delta_0\}$ for a fixed small $\delta_0>0$.
The smooth global functions $\psi_\delta=\log(|s|_h^2+\delta^2)$ satisfy $\ddc\psi_\delta\geq-C\omega$ with a constant $C\geq1$ independent of $\delta$. Off the divisor their complex Hessians are a bounded scalar multiple of the negative curvature of $h$, plus a semipositive gradient term; continuity gives the same lower bound at the divisor. Fix $0<\epsilon\leq(2C)^{-1}$, so $\omega+\epsilon\ddc\psi_\delta\geq\omega/2$.
Where $|e|_h=1$, we have $|s|_h^2=|\zeta|^2$, and therefore
\[
 \ddc\psi_\delta
 =\frac{\delta^2}{(|\zeta|^2+\delta^2)^2}\sigma_\zeta
 \geq\frac1{4\delta^2}\sigma_\zeta
 \quad(|\zeta|\leq\delta).
\]
If $P=\|p\|_\infty$, then $B_\delta\leq P\delta^{-2}\sigma_\zeta$ on its support. For $\delta\leq\delta_0$, this support lies in the region of the preceding display, where $\omega+\epsilon\ddc\psi_\delta\geq\epsilon\ddc\psi_\delta$; off the support, $\omega+\epsilon\ddc\psi_\delta\geq\omega/2\geq0$. Therefore
\[
 \omega+\epsilon\ddc\psi_\delta\geq\frac{\epsilon}{4P}B_\delta
\]
globally for $\delta\leq\delta_0$. Since $\ddc\Theta=0$, Stokes' theorem gives, for every $\Theta\in\mathcal T_\omega$,
\[
 1=\int_X(\omega+\epsilon\ddc\psi_\delta)\wedge\Theta
      \geq\frac{\epsilon}{4P}\int_XB_\delta\wedge\Theta.
\]
Taking the supremum proves $C_\delta\leq4P/\epsilon$.
\end{proof}

The proposition requires the divisor to agree with $\{\zeta=0\}$ on the entire chart. The global hypersurface given by \cref{thm:divisor-free-transverse-concentration} contains $U'\times\{0\}$ but can have further branches in the chart, so the two results are not converse to each other. Divergence of the transverse pairings also does not, by itself, bound from below the volume $\int_X\gamma^n$ of the root $\gamma$ of a test $\Theta=\gamma^{n-1}$.
