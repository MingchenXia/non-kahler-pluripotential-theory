\chapter{Relative equations under positive lower mass}
\label{chap:relative-finite-measures}

\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, let $\theta$ be a smooth semipositive big real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be a model reference satisfying \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}: $\varphi$ is an envelope fixed point, $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$ in the sense of \cref{def:singularity-envelope}, and $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty$ for some $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$.

Throughout this chapter, unless explicitly stated otherwise, we assume that $\theta$ has positive lower mass:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-positive-lower-volume}
 v_-(\theta)>0.
\end{equation}
Here $v_-(\theta)=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(0)$ is the infimum of $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)$ over all bounded $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$; see \cref{def:bmp} and the discussion following \cref{def:singularity-volumes}. The bounded mass property is not assumed.

Given a nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu$, we look for solutions of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$, $\lambda>0$, and of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu$, $c>0$, with the singularities of $\varphi$. Approximating $\mu$ by measures with $L^p$ densities gives solutions of the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi$ by \cref{thm:als-prescribed-lp}, but the constants in their bounds $|u-\varphi|\leq C$ need not remain uniform, so a limit can leave this type. What survives the limit is membership in the relative envelope class of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}: stretching the potential away from $\varphi$ by any finite factor still leaves an envelope that is not identically $-\infty$.

Positive lower mass of $\theta$ prevents these stretched envelopes from collapsing. On disjoint contact sets, one finite measure controls the rooftop envelopes of arbitrarily many potentials. Consequently, a sequence of potentials whose measures are dominated by a fixed finite measure has, after extraction, a common lower envelope in the relative class. This envelope keeps limits in the class and controls the measures of deep sublevel sets, so convergence in capacity passes to the Monge--Amp\`ere measures themselves. When the dominating measures are bounded Monge--Amp\`ere measures that vary with the potentials, exponential comparison provides the common envelope instead.

For a positive exponent the solution is unique. At exponent zero the scalar is unique, but the normalized potential need not be: on projective manifolds with nonclosed Hermitian backgrounds, the same smooth measure can have several smooth normalized solutions (\cref{cor:analytic-nonuniqueness-envelope-class}).

Since bounded mass is not assumed, members of the relative class may have infinite extended mass; the envelope constructions of \cref{sec:relative-envelope-room} remain available for them. The solutions constructed below have finite mass because the right-hand sides of their equations are finite.

Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ with $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty$, and write
\[
 \mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi),
 \qquad \mathcal E_0=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,0),
 \qquad \mu_u=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u).
\]
Here $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\psi)$ denotes the relative envelope class of a reference $\psi$ (\cref{def:relative-envelope-class}), and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta$ is the extended non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, a positive measure whose total mass may be infinite. As in \cref{def:singularity-order}, $u\preceq v$ means $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C$, and $u\sim v$ means $u\preceq v\preceq u$. For $u,w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $b\geq1$, we use the stretching envelopes $Q_b(u,w)=P_\theta(bu-(b-1)w)$ introduced before \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}, and the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_N)=P_\theta(\min_iu_i)$. Fix also a Hermitian form $\omega\geq\theta$ for capacity and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ for $L^1$ convergence.

For $a\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality} gives $P_\theta[a]=P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$. Since $a-\sup_Xa$ is one of the competitors defining $P_\theta[a]$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-normalized-model-bound}
 a-\sup_Xa\leq P_\theta[a]=\varphi.
\end{equation}

\section{Equations for finite measures}
\label{sec:relative-general-setting}

For a positive exponent, the right-hand side $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$ changes when $u$ is translated, and this fixes the additive constant. At exponent zero, translation leaves the equation unchanged; instead, a positive scalar in front of the prescribed measure is needed to match its mass.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:als-general-measure}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!relative finite measures}
Let $\mu$ be a nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $\lambda>0$ there is a unique
$u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ with $\mu_u=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$.
\item There are $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and a unique $c>0$ with $\mu_u=c\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem is proved in \cref{sec:general-measure-relative}. The case of the reference zero, which has minimal singularities because $\theta\geq0$, is treated first in \cref{thm:general-measure-minimal}; its solutions then serve as comparison potentials for the reference $\varphi$. For general measures the theorem asserts membership in $\mathcal E_\varphi$, not a bounded difference from $\varphi$.

\section{Domination}
\label{sec:relative-finite-domination}

The domination principle compares a potential $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ with a potential $v$ whose measure is larger on $\{u<v\}$. We first treat the case in which $\mu_u$ is finite. Stretching $u$ away from $\max(u,v)$ produces envelopes whose measures are bounded, by contact support, by the fixed measure $\mu_u$; the small tails of this finite measure then exclude collapse. By \cref{thm:relative-uniform-mass-lower} and \eqref{eq:relative-positive-lower-volume}, the uniform lower mass bound is
\[
 \mu_a(X)\geq\kappa:=(1-\varepsilon)^n v_-(\theta)>0
 \qquad(a\in\mathcal E_\varphi).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-finite-domination}
Let $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ have finite $\mu_u$, and let $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $v\preceq\varphi$. If, for some $0\leq c<1$,
\[
 \mu_u\leq c\mu_v\quad\hbox{on }\{u<v\},
\]
then $u\geq v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to prove $u\geq w$ for $w=\max(u,v)$. Upward stability (\cref{prop:relative-elementary}) gives $w\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, and locality gives $\mu_u\leq c\mu_w$ on $\{u<w\}$. For $b>1$ put $q_b=Q_b(u,w)$, which belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$ by \cref{lem:relative-stretch-closure}. It satisfies $q_b\leq u$, and by contact support its measure is carried by
\[
 D_b=\{b^{-1}q_b+(1-b^{-1})w=u\}.
\]
On $D_b$ the potential $b^{-1}q_b+(1-b^{-1})w$ touches $u$ from below, so positivity of mixed products and ordered contact give
\[
 b^{-n}\mu_{q_b}+(1-b^{-1})^n\mu_w\leq\mu_u.
\]
Choose $b$ so large that $(1-b^{-1})^n>c$. Comparing this inequality with $\mu_u\leq c\mu_w$ on $D_b\cap\{u<w\}$ forces both $\mu_w$ and $\mu_{q_b}$ to vanish there. Since $\mu_w(X)$ may be infinite, this subtraction is first carried out on common finite truncation levels of the potentials, where all measures are finite; exhausting the complements of the pole sets then gives the assertion for the extended measures.

It follows that $\mu_{q_b}$ is carried by $D_b\cap\{u=w\}\subseteq\{q_b=u\}$. Since $q_b\leq u$, ordered contact applied once more gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-contact-fixed-measure}
 \mu_{q_b}\leq\mathbf1_{\{q_b=u\}}\mu_u,
\end{equation}
with coefficient one, independent of $b$.

As $b\to\infty$ through the integers, the potentials $q_b$ decrease, because the obstacles $bu-(b-1)w=u-(b-1)(w-u)$ do. Suppose that they decreased to $-\infty$ identically. Then \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} would give $M_b:=\sup_Xq_b\to-\infty$, and \eqref{eq:relative-contact-fixed-measure} would imply
\[
 \kappa\leq\int_X\mu_{q_b}
 \leq\mu_u(\{u\leq M_b\})\longrightarrow0,
\]
because $\mu_u$ is finite and does not charge the pole set of $u$. This is impossible, so the decreasing limit $q$ is not identically $-\infty$. Now fix $a>0$. On $\{u<w-a\}$, the defining inequality of $q_b$ gives $q_b\leq u-(b-1)a$, so $q=-\infty$ at every point of this set where $u$ is finite. Since neither $q$ nor $u$ is identically $-\infty$, their pole sets have zero smooth volume, and hence so does $\{u<w-a\}$. Thus $u\geq w$ almost everywhere, and then everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. In particular $u\geq v$.
\end{proof}
