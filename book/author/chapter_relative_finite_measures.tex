\chapter{Relative equations under positive lower mass}
\label{chap:relative-finite-measures}

\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, with a smooth semipositive big real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ and a model reference $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Given a nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu$, we seek solutions of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$, $\lambda>0$, and of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu$, $c>0$, relative to $\varphi$. The main difficulty is to keep the singularity condition when approximating $\mu$: a bounded difference from the reference may be lost in the limit.

Recall from \cref{sec:relative-envelope-room} that, as a model reference, $\varphi$ satisfies \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}: $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$ in the sense of \cref{def:singularity-envelope}, and $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty$ for some $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, which we fix.

Throughout this chapter, unless explicitly stated otherwise, we assume that $\theta$ has positive lower mass:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-positive-lower-volume}
 v_-(\theta)>0.
\end{equation}
Here $v_-(\theta)=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(0)$ is the infimum of $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)$ over all bounded $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$; see \cref{def:bmp} and the discussion following \cref{def:singularity-volumes}. The bounded mass property is not assumed.

Approximating $\mu$ by measures with $L^p$ densities gives solutions of the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi$ by \cref{thm:als-prescribed-lp}, but the constants in their bounds $|u-\varphi|\leq C$ need not remain uniform, so a limit can leave this type. What survives the limit is membership in the relative envelope class of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}: stretching the potential away from $\varphi$ by any finite factor still leaves an envelope that is not identically $-\infty$.

Positive lower mass of $\theta$ prevents these stretched envelopes from collapsing. On disjoint contact sets, one finite measure controls the rooftop envelopes of arbitrarily many potentials. Consequently, a sequence of potentials whose measures are dominated by a fixed finite measure has, after extraction, a common lower envelope in the relative class. This envelope keeps limits in the class and controls the measures of deep sublevel sets, so convergence in capacity passes to the Monge--Amp\`ere measures themselves. When the dominating measures are Monge--Amp\`ere measures of bounded potentials that vary along the sequence, exponential comparison provides the common envelope instead.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For a positive exponent the solution is unique. At exponent zero the scalar is unique, but the normalized potential need not be: on projective manifolds with nonclosed Hermitian backgrounds, the same smooth measure can have several smooth normalized solutions (\cref{cor:analytic-nonuniqueness-envelope-class}).

Since bounded mass is not assumed, members of the relative class may have infinite extended mass; the envelope constructions of \cref{sec:relative-envelope-room} remain available for them. The solutions constructed below have finite mass because the right-hand sides of their equations are finite.

Write
\[
 \mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi),
 \qquad \mathcal E_0=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,0),
 \qquad \mu_u=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u).
\]
Here $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\psi)$ denotes the relative envelope class of a reference $\psi$ (\cref{def:relative-envelope-class}), and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta$ is the extended non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, a positive measure whose total mass may be infinite. For $u,w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $b\geq1$, we use the stretching envelopes $Q_b(u,w)=P_\theta(bu-(b-1)w)$ introduced before \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}, and the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_N)=P_\theta(\min_iu_i)$. Fix also a Hermitian form $\omega\geq\theta$ for capacity and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ for $L^1$ convergence.

For $a\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality} gives $P_\theta[a]=P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$. Since $a-\sup_Xa$ is one of the competitors defining $P_\theta[a]$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-normalized-model-bound}
 a-\sup_Xa\leq P_\theta[a]=\varphi.
\end{equation}

\section{Equations for finite measures}
\label{sec:relative-general-setting}

For a positive exponent, the right-hand side $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$ changes when $u$ is translated, and this fixes the additive constant. At exponent zero, translation leaves the equation unchanged; instead, a positive scalar in front of the prescribed measure is needed to match its mass.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:als-general-measure}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!relative finite measures}
Let $\mu$ be a nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $\lambda>0$ there is a unique
$u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ with $\mu_u=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$.
\item There are $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and a unique $c>0$ with $\mu_u=c\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem is proved in \cref{sec:general-measure-relative}. The case of the reference zero is treated first in \cref{thm:general-measure-minimal}; its solutions then serve as comparison potentials for the reference $\varphi$. For general measures the theorem asserts membership in $\mathcal E_\varphi$, not a bounded difference from $\varphi$.

\section{Domination}
\label{sec:relative-finite-domination}

The domination principle compares a potential $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ with a potential $v$ whose measure is larger on $\{u<v\}$. We first treat the case in which $\mu_u$ is finite. The argument is that of \cref{thm:big-relative-domination}: stretching $u$ away from $\max(u,v)$ produces envelopes whose measures are bounded by the fixed measure $\mu_u$, and the small tails of this finite measure exclude collapse. Here bounded mass is not assumed, and the lower mass bound for the stretched envelopes comes from \cref{thm:relative-uniform-mass-lower} and \eqref{eq:relative-positive-lower-volume}:
\[
 \mu_a(X)\geq\kappa:=(1-\varepsilon)^n v_-(\theta)>0
 \qquad(a\in\mathcal E_\varphi).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-finite-domination}
Let $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ have finite $\mu_u$, and let $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $v\preceq\varphi$. If, for some $0\leq c<1$,
\[
 \mu_u\leq c\mu_v\quad\hbox{on }\{u<v\},
\]
then $u\geq v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We follow the proof of \cref{thm:big-relative-domination}, with the extended measures $\mu_a$ in place of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a)$. Put $w=\max(u,v)$, which belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$ by upward stability (\cref{prop:relative-elementary}), and fix $b>1$ with $(1-b^{-1})^n>c$. As in the first part of that proof, \cref{lem:relative-stretch-closure}, contact support, positivity of mixed products and ordered contact give $q_b=Q_b(u,w)\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-contact-fixed-measure}
 q_b\leq u,\qquad
 \mu_{q_b}\leq\mathbf1_{\{q_b=u\}}\mu_u.
\end{equation}
The measures subtracted there are finite, because on the contact set of $q_b$ the measure $\mu_w$ is at most $(1-b^{-1})^{-n}\mu_u$. The collapse argument that ends that proof uses \eqref{eq:relative-contact-fixed-measure}, the lower bound for $\mu_{q_b}(X)$, here $\kappa$, and the finiteness of $\mu_u$; it gives $u\geq w\geq v$.
\end{proof}
