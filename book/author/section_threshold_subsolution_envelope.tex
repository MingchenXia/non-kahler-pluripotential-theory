\subsection{The greatest normalized threshold subsolution}\label{subsec:threshold-envelope}

Suppose $b_h(f)>0$. The threshold subsolutions with supremum zero form a family that is stable under finite maxima and under $L^1$ limits. Its upper envelope is therefore again a normalized threshold subsolution, and it dominates all the others.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:greatest-normalized-threshold}\index{Subsolution!greatest normalized threshold}
Suppose $b=b_h(f)>0$. The family
\[
 \mathcal S_f=\{s\in\PSH(X,h):\sup_Xs=0,\
             \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(s)\geq bf\,\mathrm{d}V\}
\]
is nonempty and compact in $L^1(X)$. Its upper envelope
\[
 \Phi_f=\left(\sup_{s\in\mathcal S_f}s\right)^*
\]
belongs to $\mathcal S_f$ and is therefore its greatest member. Moreover, let $u_\lambda$ be the greatest exponential solutions constructed in \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential} below, and $v_\lambda=u_\lambda-\sup_Xu_\lambda$. Every $L^1$ limit of a sequence $v_{\lambda_j}$ with $\lambda_j\downarrow0$ belongs to $\mathcal S_f$, and hence lies below $\Phi_f$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The family is nonempty by \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}. By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, an $L^1$ limit of members of $\mathcal S_f$ is $h$-plurisubharmonic with supremum zero, and \cref{lem:density-subsolution-lone-closure}, with $g_j=g=bf$, shows that it satisfies the measure inequality. Thus $\mathcal S_f$ is a closed subset of the compact set of normalized $h$-plurisubharmonic functions, hence compact. It is also stable under finite maxima: if $s_1,s_2\in\mathcal S_f$, then $\max(s_1,s_2)$ has supremum zero, and \cref{prop:ordered-contact-general}, applied on the sets where the maximum equals $s_1$ or $s_2$, gives $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(\max(s_1,s_2))\geq bf\,\mathrm{d}V$.

By Choquet's lemma there is a sequence $(s_i)_{i\geq1}$ in $\mathcal S_f$ with $(\sup_is_i)^*=\Phi_f$. Fix $s_0\in\mathcal S_f$. The finite maxima $t_N=\max(s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_N)$ belong to $\mathcal S_f$, increase with $N$, and satisfy $s_0\leq t_N\leq0$; their regularized limit is $\Phi_f$. Hence $t_N\to\Phi_f$ almost everywhere and, by dominated convergence, in $L^1$. \Cref{lem:density-subsolution-lone-closure} gives $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(\Phi_f)\geq bf\,\mathrm{d}V$, and $s_0\leq\Phi_f\leq0$ forces $\sup_X\Phi_f=0$. Thus $\Phi_f\in\mathcal S_f$, and by construction it dominates every member of $\mathcal S_f$.

Finally, suppose $v_{\lambda_j}\to v$ in $L^1$ with $\lambda_j\downarrow0$. The measures $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v_{\lambda_j})=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_ju_{\lambda_j}}f\,\mathrm{d}V$ have densities converging in $L^1$ to $bf$, by \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential}. Applying \cref{lem:density-subsolution-lone-closure} to these densities and potentials shows that $v\in\mathcal S_f$.
\end{proof}

The proposition gives a greatest normalized threshold subsolution, but not the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(\Phi_f)=bf\,\mathrm{d}V$; in particular, it does not show that $\Phi_f$ has finite mass.
