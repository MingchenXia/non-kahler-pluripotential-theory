\subsection{A ruled residual and its subtraction cost}
\label{subsec:ruled-residual-gauduchon-cost}

On the ruled surface of \cref{sec:nonclosed-ruled-trace-masses}, the Gauduchon variational volume depends on the data differently from the lower and upper volumes of the ordinary singularity type computed in \cref{thm:nonclosed-ruled-ordinary-volumes}; in particular, it depends on the measure $\mu$. We keep the notation of that section: $Y=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ with the forms $w,w_1,w_2$, the divisor $D=\{p\}\times\mathbb P^1$, the background $\theta=cw_1+aw_2$ of \eqref{eq:ruled-trace-background}, and $0\leq b<c$. Put $\beta=(c-b)w_1+aw_2$. Let $m>0$ be a smooth function on the first factor, with $\int_{\mathbb P^1}mw=1$, and put
\[
 \mu=mw_1\wedge w_2,\qquad k=c-b,\qquad H=\int_{\mathbb P^1}\frac{m}{a}w.
\]
In dimension two the Gauduchon metrics, which serve as tests in \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume}, are exactly the Hermitian forms $T$ with $\ddc T=0$, and $D_\mu(T)=\int_Y\sqrt{T^2/\mu}\,\mu$.

\enlargethispage{4\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ruled-residual-gauduchon-volume}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!ruled residual}
The infimum in \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} for $\beta$ is attained, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruled-residual-gauduchon-volume}
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta;bD)=\mathcal V_\mu(\beta)=\frac{2(c-b)}{H},
 \qquad \mathcal V_\mu(\theta)=\frac{2c}{H}.
\end{equation}
Writing $V=2k/H$ and $g=Vm/(2a)$, a minimizing test is the closed positive form
\[
 T=\frac ga w_1+w_2.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\int_{\mathbb P^1}gw=VH/2=k$, there is a smooth function $f$ on the first factor with $\ddc f=(g-k)w$. Then $\Omega=\beta+\ddc f=gw_1+aw_2>0$ and $\Omega^2=2ga\,w_1\wedge w_2=V\mu$. For every Gauduchon metric $T$, Stokes' theorem and the pointwise arithmetic--geometric mean inequality give
\[
 \int_Y\beta\wedge T=\int_Y\Omega\wedge T
       \geq\sqrt V\,D_\mu(T).
\]
The displayed $T$ is closed, since $g/a$ depends only on the first factor, and $T=a^{-1}\Omega$, so equality holds pointwise. Explicitly,
\[
 \int_Y\beta\wedge T=2k,
 \qquad D_\mu(T)=\sqrt V\int_{\mathbb P^1}\frac ma w=\sqrt V H.
\]
Hence the quotient of $T$ is $\sqrt V$, the smallest possible value, which proves the formula for $\mathcal V_\mu(\beta)$ and its attainment, in accordance with \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}. With the Fubini--Study metric on $\mathcal O_Y(D)$, the curvature form of $D$ is $w_1$, so the residual background $\theta-b w_1$ of $bD$ is $\beta$. The case $b=0$ gives the formula for $\theta$.
\end{proof}

Along a subtraction path, the rate at which the root volume decreases is the integrand of the subtraction cost of \cref{sec:nef-boundary-subtraction}. In the present family this rate is explicit. Fix a real constant $d$ and a smooth real function $e$ on the first factor. On an interval of values of $s$ where $c_s=c-sd>b$ and $a_s=a-se>0$, keep $b,m$ fixed and put
\[
 k_s=c_s-b,\qquad H_s=\int_{\mathbb P^1}\frac{m}{a_s}w,
 \qquad V_s=\frac{2k_s}{H_s},\qquad
 T_s=\frac{V_sm}{2a_s^2}w_1+w_2.
\]
By \cref{prop:ruled-residual-gauduchon-volume}, applied with $c_s$ and $a_s$ in place of $c$ and $a$, $V_s$ is the variational volume of $\beta_s=k_sw_1+a_sw_2=\beta-s(dw_1+ew_2)$, and $T_s$ is a minimizing Gauduchon metric for it.
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ruled-residual-subtraction-cost}
For the residual background $\beta_s=k_sw_1+a_sw_2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruled-residual-subtraction-cost}
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}\sqrt{V_s}
     =-\frac{\int_Y(d w_1+e w_2)\wedge T_s}{D_\mu(T_s)}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every compact segment $[s_0,s_1]$ in that interval,
\[
 \sqrt{V_{s_0}}-\sqrt{V_{s_1}}
   =\int_{s_0}^{s_1}
       \frac{\int_Y(d w_1+e w_2)\wedge T_s}{D_\mu(T_s)}\,\mathrm ds.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
We have $H_s'=\int_{\mathbb P^1}(em/a_s^2)\,w$, $D_\mu(T_s)=\sqrt{V_s}H_s$ and
\[
 \int_Y(d w_1+e w_2)\wedge T_s=d+\frac{V_s}{2}H_s'.
\]
Differentiating $V_s=2k_s/H_s$, with $k_s'=-d$, gives \eqref{eq:ruled-residual-subtraction-cost}; integration proves the second identity. Since $\beta_s=\beta-s(dw_1+ew_2)$ and $T_s$ is a minimizing metric for $\beta_s$, this is the first variation formula of \cref{prop:gaud-volume-first-variation}, made explicit along the residual family.
\end{proof}

Let $\tau=(|z|^2-1)/(|z|^2+1)$ be the height function on the first sphere. Its distribution under $w$ is $\mathrm d\tau/2$ on $[-1,1]$, as radial integration shows. For $c=1$, $b=1/4$, $a=2+\tau$ and $m=1$, as in the example at the end of \cref{sec:nonclosed-ruled-trace-masses}, we obtain $H=(\log3)/2$, and the residual and original variational volumes are $3/\log3$ and $4/\log3$. The ordinary-type volumes from \eqref{eq:nonclosed-ruled-ordinary-volumes} are instead $3/2$ and $9/2$, independent of $m$. The subtraction identity of \cref{cor:ruled-residual-subtraction-cost} differentiates the variational volume, which depends on the density. The invariance of the ordinary-type volumes under modifications in \cref{prop:ruled-pullback-ordinary-volumes} has no direct counterpart here: on a modification, the pullback of $\mu$ degenerates along the exceptional divisor, so it is not one of the smooth positive probability densities used to define the variational volume upstairs.

The distinction persists along a segment that ends at a nef background. In the following example the subtraction cost is computed along such a segment, together with the ordinary-type volumes of a type that has no analytic representative, in the range of exponents in which its closed first moment is infinite.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:ruled-logarithmic-nef-endpoint}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!logarithmic ruled endpoint}
Let $\mu=w_1\wedge w_2$, let
$\tau$ be the height function on the first sphere, as above, and set
\[
 \theta_s=w_1+(2+\tau-s)w_2,\qquad 0\leq s<1.
\]
Fix the ordinary type of $U=1-(1-G_p)^\alpha$ with $1/2\leq\alpha<1$, and write $\sigma_-(s),\sigma_+(s)$ for the lower and upper volumes of \cref{def:singularity-volumes} of this type in the background $\theta_s$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruled-logarithmic-moving-volumes}
 V_s:=\mathcal V_\mu(\theta_s)=\frac4{\log((3-s)/(1-s))},\qquad
 \sigma_-(s)=2(1-s),\qquad \sigma_+(s)=2(3-s).
\end{equation}
Let $T_s$ be minimizing Gauduchon metrics for $\theta_s$. The improper subtraction cost
\[
 \int_0^1\frac{\int_Yw_2\wedge T_s}{D_\mu(T_s)}\,\mathrm ds
\]
is finite and equals $2/\sqrt{\log3}$, while $\sigma_-(s)\to0$ and $\sigma_+(s)\to4$ as $s\to1$. The endpoint $\theta_1=w_1+(1+\tau)w_2$ is nef, has zero variational volume, and is not big.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Take $c=1$, $b=0$, $a_s=2+\tau-s$ and $m=1$ in \cref{prop:ruled-residual-gauduchon-volume}. Since $\tau$ has the law $\mathrm d\tau/2$ on $[-1,1]$,
\[
 H_s=\frac12\int_{-1}^1\frac{\mathrm dt}{2+t-s}
     =\frac12\log\frac{3-s}{1-s}.
\]
Thus $V_s=2/H_s$. Since $b=0<c$, \cref{thm:nonclosed-ruled-ordinary-volumes}, applied with $a_s$ in place of $a$, shows that every $\theta_s$-plurisubharmonic function of this ordinary type has finite positive mass, and gives the lower and upper volumes $2\min a_s=2(1-s)$ and $2\max a_s=2(3-s)$. The type has zero Lelong numbers by \cref{lem:ruled-sublog-small-curvature}, and trivial multiplier ideals but no analytic representative by \cref{prop:ruled-intrinsic-ideal-orders} with $b=0$. Its closed first moment is infinite for $\alpha\geq1/2$, as shown after the proof of \cref{thm:nonclosed-ruled-ordinary-volumes}; neither extremum uses such a moment.

For the minimizing metrics $T_s=V_s(2+\tau-s)^{-2}w_1/2+w_2$, \cref{cor:ruled-residual-subtraction-cost} with $d=0$ and $e=1$ gives
\[
 \frac{\int_Yw_2\wedge T_s}{D_\mu(T_s)}
 =-\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}\sqrt{V_s}
 =\frac{2}{(1-s)(3-s)[\log((3-s)/(1-s))]^{3/2}}.
\]
Integrating over $[0,s_1]$ with $s_1<1$ gives $\sqrt{V_0}-\sqrt{V_{s_1}}$. Since $V_{s_1}\to0$ as $s_1\to1$, the improper integral is $\sqrt{V_0}=2/\sqrt{\log3}$. By \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}, every minimizing metric is a constant multiple of $T_s$, and the quotient does not change under such multiples, so the cost does not depend on this choice.

The endpoint is smooth and semipositive, hence nef. Monotonicity in \cref{prop:gaud-volume-elementary} gives
\[
 0\leq\lambda_\mu(\theta_1)\leq\lambda_\mu(\theta_s)
       \longrightarrow0,
\]
so its variational volume vanishes. To exclude bigness, note that every Hermitian form dominates a positive multiple of $w_1+w_2$. If $\theta_1$ were big, there would be a quasi-plurisubharmonic $q$ and $\epsilon>0$ with $\theta_1+\ddc q\geq\epsilon(w_1+w_2)$. Choose a nonzero smooth nonnegative $(1,1)$-form $\zeta$ on the first sphere supported where $1+\tau<\epsilon$. Its pullback is closed, so distributional Stokes gives
\[
 0\leq\int_Y(\theta_1+\ddc q-\epsilon(w_1+w_2))\wedge\zeta
       =\int_{\mathbb P^1}(1+\tau-\epsilon)\zeta<0,
\]
a contradiction. Thus the positive segment ends outside the big cone, although its upper ordinary-type volume has a positive limit.
\end{proof}
