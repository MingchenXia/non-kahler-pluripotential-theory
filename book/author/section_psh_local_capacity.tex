\section{Local products and capacity}
\label{sec:psh-local-capacity}\index{Capacity!local}

Bedford--Taylor products of bounded plurisubharmonic functions, and the capacity defined through them, measure the exceptional sets of plurisubharmonic functions and control bounded approximation. We recall these classical facts of local pluripotential theory from \cite{SGPT,GZ17}. The \emph{plurifine topology} is the coarsest topology that makes all local plurisubharmonic functions continuous. It is finer than the ordinary topology, and quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are plurifine continuous; in particular, their strict superlevel sets are plurifine open.\index{Plurifine topology}

\begin{theorem}[Bedford--Taylor]
\label{thm:bt-input}\index{Bedford--Taylor product}\index{Plurisubharmonic function!quasicontinuity}
Let $1\leq k\leq n$. If $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a domain in $\C^n$, their mixed product
$\ddc p_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc p_k$ is a positive closed current of order zero. It is symmetric, multilinear in its current factors, and continuous under simultaneous locally bounded decreasing approximation. It does not charge pluripolar sets and is local in the plurifine topology.
\end{theorem}

Here plurifine locality means that if two families of such functions agree on a plurifine open Borel set, then their products have the same restriction to that set.

For concentric coordinate balls $B'\Subset B$ and a Borel set $E\subset B'$, the \emph{relative Bedford--Taylor capacity} is
\[
 \operatorname{cap}(E,B)=\sup\Bigl\{\int_E(\ddc v)^n:v\in\PSH(B),\ 0\leq v\leq1\Bigr\}.
\]
If $v$ is bounded, nonconstant and plurisubharmonic on $B$, applying the definition to $(v-\inf_Bv)/(\sup_Bv-\inf_Bv)$ gives
\[
 (\ddc v)^n(E)\leq(\sup_Bv-\inf_Bv)^n\operatorname{cap}(E,B);
\]
for constant $v$ both sides vanish. Write $\operatorname{Vol}(E)=\int_E\mathrm{d}V$, where $\mathrm{d}V$ is a fixed smooth positive volume form. We use two standard local estimates on these balls, with constants $C,a>0$ depending only on the nested balls and on $\mathrm{d}V$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $w\leq0$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$ and $v$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic on $B$, then
\[
 \int_{B'}|w|(\ddc v)^n
 \leq C\|w\|_{L^1(B)}\|v\|_{L^\infty(B)}^n.
\]
Since adding a constant to $v$ does not change $(\ddc v)^n$, one may first normalize $v$ by such a constant. This is the weighted Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality.
\item For every Borel set $E\subset B'$,
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}(E)\leq C\exp\bigl(-a\operatorname{cap}(E,B)^{-1/n}\bigr),
\]
where the right-hand side is zero when the capacity is zero. This is the classical local volume--capacity estimate.
\end{enumerate}
Both are classical results of local pluripotential theory \cite[Chapters 3 and 4]{GZ17}; the Bedford--Taylor products of \cite[Section~2.1]{SGPT} use the same normalization, and the capacity defined there through global potentials has the same null sets, namely the pluripolar sets \cite[Theorem~2.3.2]{SGPT}.

The dependence on the range of the potential can be read off from the usual cutoff argument. Let $M>0$ and let $p$ be plurisubharmonic on $B$ with $0\leq p\leq M$. Let $\kappa=\ddc|z|^2$ be the Euclidean form, and choose a cutoff function $\chi$ supported in $B$, with $0\leq\chi\leq1$, equal to one on a smaller ball and satisfying $-C\kappa\leq\ddc\chi\leq C\kappa$. Integration by parts gives, for $1\leq j\leq n$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int\chi(\ddc p)^j\wedge\kappa^{n-j}
 &=\int p\,\ddc\chi\wedge(\ddc p)^{j-1}\wedge\kappa^{n-j}\\
 &\leq CM\int_{\operatorname{supp}\chi}(\ddc p)^{j-1}\wedge\kappa^{n-j+1}.
 \end{split}
\]
Iterating this estimate with nested cutoffs between $B'$ and $B$, and stopping at degree one or at degree zero, we obtain respectively
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-cln-range}
 \begin{split}
 \int_{B'}(\ddc p)^j\wedge\kappa^{n-j}
 &\leq C_jM^{j-1}\int_B\ddc p\wedge\kappa^{n-1},\\
 \int_{B'}(\ddc p)^j\wedge\kappa^{n-j}
 &\leq C_jM^j.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The constants depend only on the nested balls and on $j$. For bounded $p$, the integrations by parts are justified by local decreasing regularization.

\begin{definition}\label{def:relative-extremal-function}\index{Extremal function!relative}
For a compact set $E$ in an open Euclidean ball $B\subset\mathbb C^n$, the \emph{relative extremal function} is
\[
 h_{E,B}=\sup\{u\in\PSH(B):-1\leq u\leq0,\ u|_E\leq-1\}.
\]
\end{definition}

The constant $-1$ is a competitor, and the family is bounded above by zero. The local upper-envelope theorem therefore gives $h_{E,B}^*\in\PSH(B)$ with $-1\leq h_{E,B}^*\leq0$. Allowing all competitors $u\leq0$ with $u|_E\leq-1$ gives the same supremum, because the truncation $\max(u,-1)$ of such a competitor is a competitor in the original family.

A sequence of functions $f_j$ converges to $f$ \emph{in local capacity} if $\operatorname{cap}(K\cap\{|f_j-f|>\epsilon\},B)\to0$ for every $\epsilon>0$, every coordinate ball $B$ and every compact set $K\subset B$. An assertion holds \emph{quasi-everywhere}, abbreviated q.e., if it holds outside a pluripolar set; in particular, differences of functions that may both be $-\infty$ are considered only off such a set. A positive measure is \emph{non-pluripolar} if it gives zero mass to every pluripolar set.

A function is \emph{quasi-continuous} if, for every $\epsilon>0$, it is continuous outside an open set of capacity less than $\epsilon$. Plurisubharmonic functions have this property for the local Bedford--Taylor capacity, and a Borel set has zero capacity if and only if it is pluripolar. A locally uniformly bounded monotone sequence of plurisubharmonic functions converges in capacity: to its limit if it decreases, and to the upper semicontinuous regularization of its limit if it increases. If uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic functions converge in capacity, their local products converge weakly, and this remains true after the products are multiplied by uniformly bounded quasi-continuous coefficients converging in capacity. These local results, recalled in \cite{SGPT,GZ17}, also apply to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions after a common smooth correction.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Mixed Monge--Amp\`ere inequality]\label{thm:local-mixed-input}
Let $U\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a domain, and let $\beta=\ddc|z|^2$ be the Euclidean form. Let $a,b$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $U$, and let $f,g$ be nonnegative measurable functions on $U$ with
\[
  (\ddc a)^n\geq f\beta^n,
  \qquad
  (\ddc b)^n\geq g\beta^n.
\]
Then, for $0\leq k\leq n$,
\[
  (\ddc a)^k\wedge(\ddc b)^{n-k}
  \geq f^{k/n}g^{(n-k)/n}\beta^n.
\]
\end{theorem}

The comparison principle of Bedford and Taylor also has mixed forms \cite{GZ17}. Let $a,b$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $U$ such that $E=\{a<b\}\Subset U$, and let $S$ be a positive closed $(n-1,n-1)$-current that is a product of Hessians of bounded plurisubharmonic functions and smooth closed positive forms. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-mixed-BT-comparison}
  \int_E\ddc b\wedge S\leq\int_E\ddc a\wedge S.
\end{equation}
Indeed, apply the Bedford--Taylor integration by parts formula to $b_\epsilon=\max\{a,b-\epsilon\}$, and let $\epsilon\downarrow0$, using monotone continuity to pass to the limit. Replacing one factor at a time, we obtain, with $P=\ddc a$ and $Q=\ddc b$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-power-comparison}
  \int_E P^{n-j}\wedge Q^j\leq\int_E P^n,
  \qquad 0\leq j\leq n.
\end{equation}

The local envelope theorem for bounded obstacles \cite{GZ17} shows where an envelope can carry Monge--Amp\`ere mass. Let $B$ be a ball with a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic defining function $h<0$, and let $f$ be a bounded quasi-continuous obstacle with $Ah\leq f\leq0$ for some $A>0$. Then the envelope
\[
 q=\bigl(\sup\{v\in\PSH(B):v\leq f\ \text{q.e.}\}\bigr)^*
\]
satisfies $Ah\leq q\leq0$, tends to zero at the boundary, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-envelope-contact}
 \mathbf1_{\{q<f\}}(\ddc q)^n=0.
\end{equation}

For a locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic function $v$ and a smooth convex increasing function $F$ on a neighborhood of the range of $v$, the bounded Bedford--Taylor chain rule reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-convex-chain-rule}
 \ddc F(v)=F'(v)\ddc v+F''(v)\,\mathrm{d}v\wedge\dc v.
\end{equation}
The gradient term, and its products with positive Bedford--Taylor factors of bounded potentials, are nonnegative. These identities belong to the bounded local calculus of \cite{GZ17}; they follow by decreasing regularization, and bounded potentials have the local Sobolev regularity needed to define the gradient term. In particular, inequalities derived from this formula survive local smoothing with a lower curvature error tending to zero.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-bounded-measure-carrier}
Let $B$ be a coordinate ball and let $\tau$ be a finite non-pluripolar measure with compact support in $B$. Then there is a bounded plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $B$ such that $\tau\ll(\ddc v)^n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $n\geq2$, we use Cegrell's decomposition theorem. On a bounded hyperconvex domain in $\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq2$, let $\mathcal E_0$ consist of the bounded negative plurisubharmonic functions with zero boundary values and finite total Monge--Amp\`ere mass. The theorem states that every positive compactly supported measure $\tau$ has a decomposition
\[
 \tau=g(\ddc v)^n+\tau_s,
 \qquad v\in\mathcal E_0,\qquad
 0\leq g\in L^1((\ddc v)^n),
\]
where $\tau_s$ is a positive measure carried by a pluripolar set. If $\tau$ is non-pluripolar, then $\tau_s=0$, so $\tau\ll(\ddc v)^n$.

In dimension one, the assertion follows directly from the Green potential. Let $a\leq0$ be the Green potential of $\tau$ on the disk, normalized by $\ddc a=\tau$. Then $v=\mathrm{e}^a$ is bounded and subharmonic, and
\[
 \ddc v\geq \mathrm{e}^a\tau.
\]
To justify this inequality, set $a_M=\max(a,-M)$. The chain rule \eqref{eq:bounded-convex-chain-rule} in dimension one gives
$\ddc \mathrm{e}^{a_M}\geq \mathrm{e}^{a_M}\ddc a_M$, and the right-hand side is at least $\mathrm{e}^a\tau$ on $\{a>-M\}$. Let $M\to\infty$, using the decreasing convergence of the bounded subharmonic functions $\mathrm{e}^{a_M}$ on the left and monotone convergence on the right. Since $\{a=-\infty\}$ is polar and $\tau$ charges no polar set, $\mathrm{e}^a>0$ almost everywhere for $\tau$. Thus $\tau\ll\ddc v$.
\end{proof}

For a signed measure $\nu$, write $|\nu|$ for its total variation measure and $\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}(B)}=|\nu|(B)$. On a curve, the Green representation controls capacity by the total variation of the Laplacian, together with an $L^1$ term for the harmonic remainder.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:planar-variation-capacity}
Let $B'\Subset B$ be concentric disks in $\mathbb C$. There is a constant $C$, depending only on these disks, such that for all quasi-subharmonic functions $u,v$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ and all $t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-variation-capacity}
 \operatorname{cap}(B'\cap\{|u-v|>t\},B)
 \leq\frac Ct\left(\|u-v\|_{L^1(B)}
                   +\|\ddc(u-v)\|_{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right).
\end{equation}
Here the difference $u-v$ is formed off the pole sets of $u$ and $v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $f=u-v$ and write the Jordan decomposition on $B$ as $\ddc f=\alpha^+-\alpha^-$. Let $g^\pm\leq0$ be the Green potentials of $\alpha^\pm$ on $B$ with zero boundary values. The $L^1(B)$ norm of the Green kernel of the disk is bounded uniformly in its pole, so
\[
 \|g^+\|_{L^1(B)}+\|g^-\|_{L^1(B)}
 \leq C\|\ddc f\|_{\mathrm{TV}(B)}.
\]
Indeed, on the unit disk the kernel is $\log|(z-\zeta)/(1-\overline\zeta z)|^2$; its logarithmic singularity is uniformly integrable, and $|1-\overline\zeta z|\leq2$. Scaling gives the bound on $B$.

The distribution $h=f-g^++g^-$ is harmonic, and the interior mean-value estimate gives
\[
 \|h\|_{L^\infty(B')}
 \leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^1(B)}+\|\ddc f\|_{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right).
\]
The identity $u+g^-=v+g^++h$ holds almost everywhere, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. Thus $|u-v|\leq-g^+-g^-+|h|$ off the polar sets of $u$ and $v$. Let $0\leq a\leq1$ be a subharmonic test function on $B$. Since the measure $\ddc a$ charges no polar set, this pointwise bound, the weighted Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimate and \eqref{eq:local-cln-range} yield
\[
 \int_{B'}|u-v|\,\ddc a
 \leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^1(B)}+\|\ddc f\|_{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right).
\]
Restricting the integral to $B'\cap\{|u-v|>t\}$, dividing by $t$ and taking the supremum over $a$ proves \eqref{eq:planar-variation-capacity}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Polar parts on curves}
\label{sec:local-polar-parts}\index{Polar part of a measure}

Every locally finite signed measure $\nu$ on a Riemann surface splits uniquely as the sum of its \emph{polar part} $\nu^{\mathrm{pol}}$, which is carried by a polar Borel set, and a measure that charges no polar set. To construct the splitting locally, choose countably many polar Borel sets whose $|\nu|$-masses approach the supremum over all polar Borel sets, and restrict $\nu$ to their union, which is again polar. Uniqueness allows these local decompositions to be patched together and shows that $\nu\mapsto\nu^{\mathrm{pol}}$ is linear.

For a locally quasi-subharmonic function $u$, the \emph{polar curvature} of $u$ is the positive measure $\mu_u=(\ddc u)^{\mathrm{pol}}=\mathbf1_{\{u=-\infty\}}\ddc u$. The second equality holds because smooth corrections do not change polar parts, while bounded truncations and plurifine locality show that $\mathbf1_{\{u>-j\}}\ddc u$ charges no polar set. The following inverse maximum principle shows that an upper bound for the difference of two potentials orders their polar curvatures.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:planar-inverse-maximum}
Let $B\subset\mathbb C$ be a disk and let $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(B)$ have a distributional Laplacian that is a signed Radon measure. If $f\leq0$ almost everywhere, then $(\Delta f)^{\mathrm{pol}}\geq0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to prove $\Delta f(K)\geq0$ for every compact polar set $K\subset B$; restriction to smaller disks and inner regularity then give the assertion. Choose open neighborhoods $O_j$ of $K$ with smooth boundaries such that $\overline O_{j+1}\subset O_j\Subset B$ and $\bigcap_j\overline O_j=K$. Let $h_j$ be the function that equals one on $\overline O_j$, is harmonic on $B\setminus\overline O_j$, and vanishes on $\partial B$. The Dirichlet problem on this smooth domain shows that $h_j$ is continuous with $0\leq h_j\leq1$. Its outward normal derivative on $\partial O_j$ is nonpositive, so the jump of the derivative across $\partial O_j$ gives $\Delta h_j\leq0$ on $B$ in the sense of distributions. The maximum principle gives $h_{j+1}\leq h_j$.

Since the functions $h_j$ decrease and satisfy $0\leq h_j\leq1$, they converge in $L^1_{\loc}(B)$. Let $h$ be the superharmonic representative of this limit; it may differ from the pointwise limit on $K$. It is harmonic off $K$, so its Laplacian is supported on the polar set $K$. On the other hand, $-h$ is bounded and subharmonic, so its Laplacian charges no polar set, by \cref{thm:bt-input}. Hence $h$ is harmonic on $B$. Since $0\leq h\leq h_1$ and $h_1$ tends to zero at $\partial B$, the maximum principle gives $h=0$. It follows that $h_j\to0$ smoothly on compact subsets of $B\setminus K$, while $h_j=1$ on $K$.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Choose $0\leq\chi\in C_c^\infty(B)$ equal to one near $K$. For large $j$, regularize $h_j$ by convolution on a neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp}\chi$, obtaining smooth functions $h_{j,\epsilon}$ with $\Delta h_{j,\epsilon}\leq0$. Since $f\leq0$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_B\chi h_{j,\epsilon}\,\mathrm{d}(\Delta f)
 &=\int_B f\,\Delta(\chi h_{j,\epsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
 &\geq\int_B f\bigl(2\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla h_{j,\epsilon}
                  +h_{j,\epsilon}\Delta\chi\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.
 \end{split}
\]
For fixed $j$, the convolutions converge uniformly to $h_j$ on $\operatorname{supp}\chi$, because $h_j$ is continuous. On the support of the derivatives of $\chi$, the function $h_j$ is harmonic for all large $j$. We may therefore first let $\epsilon\downarrow0$ and then let $j\to\infty$. The smooth convergence away from $K$ makes the right-hand side tend to zero, and dominated convergence for the signed measure $\Delta f$ makes the left-hand side tend to $\Delta f(K)$. This proves $\Delta f(K)\geq0$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:polar-order-maximum}
Let $u,v$ be locally quasi-subharmonic functions on a Riemann surface. If $u\leq v+C$ on an open set for some constant $C$, then $\mu_u\geq\mu_v$ there. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-maximum-measure}
 \mu_{\max(u,v)}=\mu_u\wedge\mu_v,
\end{equation}
where the right-hand side is the greatest measure dominated by both $\mu_u$ and $\mu_v$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:planar-inverse-maximum} to $u-v-C$ in local coordinates. Since smooth functions have zero polar part, linearity of the polar part gives the first assertion. Applying it to $u-\max(u,v)$ and to $v-\max(u,v)$ gives the inequality $\leq$ in \eqref{eq:polar-maximum-measure}.

For the reverse inequality, add a common smooth correction to make $u$ and $v$ subharmonic on a coordinate disk. On a smaller disk, let $g$ be the Green potential of the restriction of $\lambda=\mu_u\wedge\mu_v$ to that disk, so that $\ddc g=\lambda$. The distributions $u-g$ and $v-g$ have positive Hessians and hence subharmonic representatives $a$ and $b$. Since $\max(u,v)=g+\max(a,b)$ almost everywhere, the Hessian of $\max(u,v)$ dominates $\lambda$. Restricting this inequality to a polar Borel set carrying $\lambda$ proves the reverse inequality on the smaller disk, and these local statements give it on the whole surface.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Products at small pole sets}
\label{subsec:small-pole-local-products}

A top-degree product can carry mass on a pole set, and the non-pluripolar product discards this mass. When the unbounded locus is sufficiently small, the full local current product is well defined. Singularity order then compares the restrictions of these products to the pole set, which we call \emph{residual measures}\index{Residual measure}, and bounded perturbations leave these measures unchanged.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:small-pole-local-products}\index{Monge--Ampere product@Monge--Amp\`ere product!small unbounded locus}
Let $\Omega\subset\C^n$ be a domain and $S\subset\Omega$ a closed set of Hausdorff dimension zero. Suppose that $u_1,\ldots,u_q$, $1\leq q\leq n$, are plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ and locally bounded on $\Omega\setminus S$. Then their inductively defined full current product
\[
 \bigl(\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\bigr)_{\mathrm{full}}
\]
is a closed positive current of order zero. It is symmetric, multilinear, local in the ordinary topology, and continuous under simultaneous decreasing approximation by plurisubharmonic functions. The weighted current
$u_1\bigl(\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_q\bigr)_{\mathrm{full}}$
has coefficients of locally finite variation. These full products may charge $S$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the classical theorem on products with unbounded potentials, together with its monotone continuity statement, in \cite[Chapter III, Theorem~4.5 and Proposition~4.9, pp.~152--156]{DemCADG}, with the underlying current $T=1$. For every choice of $m$ of the functions, that theorem requires the intersection of their unbounded loci to have zero Hausdorff measure of dimension $2n-2m+1$. This intersection lies in $S$, and $2n-2m+1\geq1$, so the measure is zero. The same hypothesis gives local integrability of the weighted current. Symmetry, multilinearity and locality on open sets follow from the local construction of the currents and decreasing regularization.
\end{proof}

The subscript distinguishes this product from the non-pluripolar product used later; the two coincide for locally bounded functions.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:small-pole-residual-invariance}\index{Residual mass!bounded perturbations}\index{Residual mass!singularity order}
Let $\Omega\subset\C^n$ be a domain and $S\subset\Omega$ a closed set of Hausdorff dimension zero. Let $a,b$ be plurisubharmonic on $\Omega$ and locally bounded off $S$, with $\{a=-\infty\}=S$. If $a\leq b+C$ near $S$ for some finite constant $C$, then, as positive Radon measures,
\[
 \mathbf1_S(\ddc b)^n_{\mathrm{full}}
       \leq\mathbf1_S(\ddc a)^n_{\mathrm{full}}.
\]
If $a-b$ is bounded near $S$, equality holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compact set $E\subset S$ and $\varepsilon>0$, and put
$w_c=\max\{b-c,(1+\varepsilon)a\}$ for $c>0$.
Since $a=-\infty$ on the compact set $E$, upper semicontinuity gives a neighborhood of $E$ on which $a<-(C+c)/\varepsilon$ and $a\leq b+C$. Off $S$, on this neighborhood,
$b-c-(1+\varepsilon)a\geq-C-c-\varepsilon a>0$,
while on $S$ the second branch of the maximum equals $-\infty$. Thus $w_c=b-c$ near $E$, and locality on open sets gives
$(\ddc w_c)^n_{\mathrm{full}}(E)=(\ddc b)^n_{\mathrm{full}}(E)$.

As $c\to\infty$, the functions $w_c$ decrease to $(1+\varepsilon)a$. \Cref{thm:small-pole-local-products} applies to every $w_c$ with the same set $S$, so their full measures converge weakly to $(1+\varepsilon)^n(\ddc a)^n_{\mathrm{full}}$. Since the mass of a compact set is upper semicontinuous under weak convergence of positive measures, we obtain
\[
 (\ddc b)^n_{\mathrm{full}}(E)
       \leq(1+\varepsilon)^n(\ddc a)^n_{\mathrm{full}}(E).
\]
Here we apply the semicontinuity after multiplying the measures by a fixed nonnegative cutoff function with compact support, equal to one near $E$; both masses on $E$ are finite by the theorem. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and applying inner regularity to all compact sets $E\subset S$ gives the asserted inequality of measures. If $a-b$ is bounded near $S$, then also $\{b=-\infty\}=S$, so the roles of $a$ and $b$ can be interchanged, and equality follows.
\end{proof}
