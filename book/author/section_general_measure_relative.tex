\section{Equations with a model reference}
\label{sec:general-measure-relative}

For a model reference, the first approximation varies the bounded carrier potential. Exponential comparison gives a common lower envelope, and moving-measure continuity preserves the equation at the limit. With the carrier then fixed, minimal-class solutions bound the prescribed-reference solutions from above while the density is approximated. Positive lower mass supplies the complementary bound that prevents collapse.

\begin{proof}[of \cref{thm:als-general-measure}]
Let $\mu$ be the given nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure. Apply \cref{lem:bounded-measure-carrier} to the big background $\omega$ to write
\[
 \mu=f\nu,\qquad \nu=\omega_\psi^n,\qquad
 \psi\in\PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^\infty(X),\qquad
 0\leq f\in L^1(\nu).
\]
\emph{Positive continuous density.} Fix $\lambda>0$ and first suppose $f$ is continuous and strictly positive. Choose smooth strictly $\omega$-plurisubharmonic $\psi_j\downarrow\psi$ by \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization}. These potentials are uniformly bounded; write $\|\psi_j\|_\infty\leq L$ and $\nu_j=\omega_{\psi_j}^n$. Bounded Bedford--Taylor continuity gives $\nu_j\rightharpoonup\nu$. The prescribed ordinary-type theorem \cref{thm:als-prescribed-lp} supplies $u_j\sim\varphi$ with
\[
 \mu_{u_j}=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}f\nu_j.
\]
Indeed $f\nu_j$ has a continuous positive density relative to smooth volume, hence belongs to every finite $L^p$ space.

We first give a common upper bound. Choose $K$ with
$\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(\psi_j+K)}\leq\min_Xf$ for all $j$. Since $\omega\geq\theta$, positivity of extended mixed products gives
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(\psi_j+K)}\omega_{\psi_j+K}^n
 \leq f\nu_j
 =\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u_j}\mu_{u_j}
 \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u_j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u_j).
\]
Apply \cref{cor:singular-exponential-order} in background $\omega$, with first potential $\psi_j+K$ and second potential $u_j$. The first is bounded and admits a smaller-background envelope, and $u_j\preceq\psi_j+K$. Thus $u_j\leq\psi_j+K\leq L+K=:C$.

By \eqref{eq:relative-normalized-model-bound}, $u_j\leq\varphi+C$. The exponential common-envelope proposition gives a single $h\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ below all $u_j$, since
\[
 \mu_{u_j}\leq\|f\|_\infty \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}\omega_{\psi_j}^n.
\]
The bounds $h\leq u_j\leq\varphi+C$ and \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} give a genuine $L^1$ limit $u$ after extraction. The moving-measure theorem now applies: $\psi_j\to\psi$ in capacity and $\mu_{u_j}\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda C}\|f\|_\infty\nu_j$. It gives $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, capacity convergence and $\mu_{u_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_u$.

The right-hand sides converge weakly to $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\nu$. To see this, $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\to0$ in capacity with a common bound, so its integral against $\nu_j$ tends to zero by their uniform capacity estimate. The fixed function $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ is bounded and quasi-continuous. Approximate it by a continuous function outside an open set of arbitrarily small capacity; bounded product continuity and the same capacity bound allow this fixed weight in $\nu_j\rightharpoonup\nu$. Multiplication by the continuous $f$ and a continuous test function preserves both estimates. Hence $\mu_u=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\nu$.

\emph{Bounded measurable density.} Suppose next $0\leq f\in L^\infty(\nu)$ and $\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}\nu>0$. Choose positive continuous $f_j$, uniformly bounded by a constant $F$, with $f_j\to f$ almost everywhere for $\nu$. Such a sequence follows from density of continuous functions in $L^1(\nu)$, clipping to a common interval, adding positive constants tending to zero and extracting a summably convergent subsequence.

There is a bounded nonnegative $g$, nonzero as a measure, such that $g\leq f_j$ for all $j$. Indeed, choose $a>0$ with $\nu(\{f\geq2a\})>0$. Egorov's theorem gives a measurable subset $E$ of positive measure on which the convergence is uniform. For some $N$, $f_j\geq a$ on $E$ for $j\geq N$. The finitely many remaining positive continuous functions have positive minima on $X$. Their minima and $a$ have a positive lower bound $b$, so $g=b\mathbf1_E$ works.

By the first step, let $u_j\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ solve $\mu_{u_j}=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}f_j\nu$. The already proved minimal-reference theorem \cref{thm:general-measure-minimal}, applied to the finite nonzero measure $g\nu$, gives $s\in\mathcal E_0$ satisfying
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(s)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}g\nu.
\]
Apply \cref{cor:relative-extended-exponential-order} with reference zero, first potential $s\in\mathcal E_0$ and second potential $u_j\preceq0$. The measure inequality is $g\nu\leq f_j\nu$, so $u_j\leq s$. With $C=\sup_Xs$ we have
\[
 \mu_{u_j}\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda C}F\nu,
 \qquad
 \kappa\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_j}F\nu(X).
\]
The two bounds on the suprema give a genuine $L^1$ convergent subsequence. The fixed-measure continuity theorem puts its limit $u$ in $\mathcal E_\varphi$ and identifies the weak limit as $\mu_u$.

\Cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals} gives $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ in $L^1(\nu)$. Also $f_j\to f$ in $L^1(\nu)$ by dominated convergence. Since both factors have uniform bounds,
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}f_j\longrightarrow \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f
 \quad\hbox{in }L^1(\nu).
\]
Thus the right-hand sides converge in total variation, proving the equation for $f$.

\emph{Integrable density.} For general $f\in L^1(\nu)$, apply the second step to $f_j=\min(f,j)$ and obtain $u_j\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ with $\mu_{u_j}=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}f_j\nu$. Exponential order gives $u_{j+1}\leq u_j$, because $f_j\leq f_{j+1}$. Furthermore
\[
 \kappa\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_j}\mu(X),
 \qquad \mu_{u_j}\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_1}\mu.
\]
The decreasing limit is genuine, and the fixed-measure continuity theorem gives $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $\mu_{u_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_u$. Dominated convergence gives $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}f_j\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f$ in $L^1(\nu)$, so $\mu_u=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$. \Cref{cor:relative-extended-exponential-order}, applied in both directions, proves uniqueness.

\emph{Zero exponent.} For each positive integer $j$, solve the equation just proved with exponent $1/j$, obtaining $u_j\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ with $\mu_{u_j}=\mathrm{e}^{u_j/j}\mu$. By the minimal-reference theorem, choose $s\in\mathcal E_0$ solving
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(s)=\mathrm{e}^s\mu.
\]
Put $H=\max(0,\sup_Xs)$ and $w_j=s+(j-1)H$. Then $w_j/j\geq s$, whence
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-w_j/j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(w_j)
 \leq\mu
 =\mathrm{e}^{-u_j/j}\mu_{u_j}.
\]
As in the bounded-density step, exponential order applies with reference zero, first potential $w_j\in\mathcal E_0$ and second potential $u_j\preceq0$. It gives $u_j\leq w_j\leq jH$. Only an upper bound for $s$ is needed; its mass is finite by its equation.

Set $M_j=\sup_Xu_j$, $v_j=u_j-M_j$ and $c_j=\mathrm{e}^{M_j/j}$. Then
\[
 \sup_Xv_j=0,\qquad
 \frac{\kappa}{\mu(X)}\leq c_j\leq \mathrm{e}^H,\qquad
 \mu_{v_j}=c_j\mathrm{e}^{v_j/j}\mu\leq \mathrm{e}^H\mu.
\]
The lower bound follows from $\mu_{v_j}(X)\geq\kappa$ and $v_j\leq0$. Extract so that $v_j\to v$ in $L^1(X)$ and $c_j\to c>0$. The limit $v$ is genuine and normalized.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
By \cref{lem:normalized-exponential-npp}, $a_j=\mathrm{e}^{v_j/j}\to1$ in $L^1(\mu)$. Consequently $\mu_{v_j}=c_ja_j\mu\to c\mu$ in total variation. The fixed-measure continuity theorem gives $v\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and identifies $\mu_v=c\mu$. Finally, applying the scalar assertion of \cref{cor:relative-extended-exponential-order} in both directions proves that $c$ is unique.
\end{proof}
