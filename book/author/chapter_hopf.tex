\chapter{Unbounded mass on Hopf manifolds}
\label{chap:hopf}

By \cref{prop:gauduchon-range-growth}, every compact complex curve or surface has the bounded mass property. The property can fail in dimension three even when the metric is Gauduchon. We prove this on a Hopf threefold, then extend the construction to the scalar Hopf manifolds $(\C^n\setminus\{0\})/\langle z\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-1}z\rangle$ of all dimensions $n\geq3$.

The mass polynomial of \cref{thm:mass-expansion} provides the starting point. On a threefold, apart from the constant $\int_X\omega^3$, it has only a linear and a quadratic term. The linear term vanishes for a Gauduchon metric, and for the standard Hopf metric the quadratic term is a positive multiple of the Dirichlet energy along the elliptic fibers over $\mathbb P^2$. We therefore seek smooth potentials with large vertical energy whose perturbed forms stay positive. A fiber heat kernel supplies the concentration. Varying its time across the base controls the mixed derivatives; a function of the base then restores positivity without changing the vertical energy. The higher-dimensional extension follows by comparing the angular densities.

We next use the fiber structure to study minimum mass, damping and weak limits. Fiber averaging identifies the bounded potentials of minimum mass. For normalized potentials invariant under unitary rotations of the last two coordinates, a positive fiber flux yields the sharp square-root damping threshold. At the endpoint of this threshold, concentration on a divisor separates individual finiteness from uniform tails and capacity continuity. Finally, we classify the excess measures produced by shrinking smooth potentials. They are exactly the lifts of base measures by the normalized area measures on the elliptic fibers, for the standard metric and for every other Hermitian metric on this threefold.

\begin{theorem}[Xia--Zhang]\label{thm:hopf-unbounded}\index{Hopf manifold!unbounded mass}
The compact threefold
\[
 X=(\C^3\setminus\{0\})/\langle z\mapsto \mathrm{e}^{-1}z\rangle
\]
admits a Gauduchon form $\omega$ and smooth real functions $V_j$ such that
\[
 \omega+\ddc V_j>0,\qquad
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc V_j)^3\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
In particular, $X$ does not have the bounded mass property.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Hopf metric and its mass}

The projection to $\mathbb P^2$ separates the horizontal and vertical parts of the metric. We normalize the fiber measure to have mass one; this fixes the constants in the mass formula.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
On the covering space $\C^3\setminus\{0\}$, with coordinates $z=(z_0,z_1,z_2)$, put
\[
 s=\log|z|^2,\qquad
 \alpha=\ddc s,\qquad
 \beta=\mathrm{d}s\wedge\dc s
       =\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial s\wedge\bar\partial s,
 \qquad \omega=\alpha+\beta.
\]
The contraction $z\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-1}z$ changes $s$ by the constant $-2$, so these forms descend to $X$. A closed spherical shell between two successive radii is a compact fundamental domain; this also proves that the quotient is compact. Let $\omega_{\FS}$ be the Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^2$, given by $\ddc\log(1+|\xi|^2)$ in affine coordinates $\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2)$. Projectivization gives a holomorphic map
\[
 \pi\colon X\longrightarrow\mathbb P^2,
 \qquad \alpha=\pi^*\omega_{\FS},\qquad
 \int_{\mathbb P^2}\omega_{\FS}^2=1.
\]
The form $\alpha$ has rank two and is positive transversally to the fibers, while the rank-one form $\beta$ is positive on the vertical line. Their kernels intersect only in zero, and therefore $\omega>0$.

We shall use the identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-torsion}
 \alpha^3=0,\quad \beta^2=0,\quad \mathrm{d}\alpha=0,
 \quad \ddc\beta=-\alpha^2,
 \quad \ddc(\omega^2)=0.
\end{equation}
The first three follow from the definitions and the dimension of the base. For the fourth, differentiating $\beta=\mathrm i\partial s\wedge\bar\partial s/(2\pi)$ gives $\ddc\beta=(\partial\bar\partial s)^2/(4\pi^2)=-\alpha^2$. Finally,
$\omega^2=\alpha^2+2\alpha\wedge\beta$, so $\ddc(\omega^2)=-2\alpha^3=0$. Thus $\omega$ is Gauduchon, although $\ddc\omega=-\alpha^2\ne0$.

On the affine base chart $U_0=\{[z_0:z_1:z_2]\in\mathbb P^2:z_0\ne0\}\simeq\C^2$ write
\[
 \xi_j=z_j/z_0\quad(j=1,2),\qquad w=\log z_0.
\]
The choice of logarithm and the contraction change $w$ by elements of
$\Lambda=\mathbb Z+2\pi\mathrm i\mathbb Z$. Hence
\[
 \pi^{-1}(U_0)\simeq U_0\times E,\qquad
 E=\C/\Lambda,\qquad
 \mathrm{d}A_E=\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}\mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar w,
 \quad\int_E\mathrm{d}A_E=1.
\]
On another base chart the fiber coordinate changes by a holomorphic function of the base. Consequently, the vertical derivative $\partial_w$ and the fiber integral below are globally defined. For a smooth function $V$ on $X$, write $V_w=\partial V/\partial w$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hopf-mass}\index{Hopf manifold!mass formula}
For every $V\in C^\infty(X,\R)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-mass}
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc V)^3
 =6+6\int_{\mathbb P^2}
       \left(\int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}|V_w|^2\,\mathrm{d}A_E\right)
       \omega_{\FS}^2(y).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\beta$ restricts to $2\mathrm{d}A_E$ on each fiber,
\[
 \int_X\omega^3=3\int_X\alpha^2\wedge\beta=6.
\]
By \eqref{eq:mass-expansion} and \eqref{eq:hopf-torsion}, followed by an integration by parts against the closed form $\alpha^2$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc V)^3-\int_X\omega^3
 &=3\int_X V\ddc\omega\wedge\ddc V\\
 &=-3\int_X V\alpha^2\wedge\ddc V
 =3\int_X\mathrm{d}V\wedge\dc V\wedge\alpha^2.
 \end{split}
\]
Wedging with $\alpha^2$ removes every component of $\mathrm{d}V\wedge\dc V$ except the vertical one, which is
$\mathrm i|V_w|^2\mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar w/(2\pi)=2|V_w|^2\mathrm{d}A_E$. Integration over the fibers proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\section{The radial--vertical block}

We look for potentials that depend on the base point only through its distance from the origin of the affine chart. In a suitable frame, the matrix of $\omega+\ddc V$ then consists of one tangential horizontal coefficient and a two-by-two radial--vertical block, whose Schur complement isolates the mixed derivative that must be controlled. Subscripts denote partial derivatives in the indicated variables.

For $\xi\ne0$ introduce
\[
 x=\log|\xi|^2,\qquad
 \mu=\frac{\mathrm{e}^x}{1+\mathrm{e}^x},\qquad \mu_x=\mu(1-\mu).
\]
We use smooth real functions $V\colon \mathbb R\times E\to\mathbb R$, written $V=V(x,w)$, that are independent of $x$ for $x$ near $-\infty$ and constant in both variables for $x$ near $+\infty$. These conditions allow $V$ to be extended smoothly across $\xi=0$, and over the complement of the affine chart by the same constant. The region of the base where $V$ is nonconstant may depend on $V$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hopf-positivity}\index{Hopf manifold!positivity criterion}
For such a function, at $\xi\ne0$ the inequality $\omega+\ddc V>0$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-positivity}
 1+V_{w\bar w}>0,\qquad \mu+V_x>0,\qquad
 Q[V]:=V_{xx}+\mu-
       \frac{|\mu+V_{x\bar w}|^2}{1+V_{w\bar w}}>0.
\end{equation}
At $\xi=0$, the inequality $\omega+\ddc V>0$ is equivalent to $1+V_{w\bar w}>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In these coordinates,
\[
 s=w+\bar w+\log(1+\mathrm{e}^x),\qquad
 \partial s=\mathrm{d}w+\mu\partial x,
\]
and
\[
 2\pi\alpha
 =\mathrm i\mu\partial\bar\partial x
  +\mathrm i\mu(1-\mu)\partial x\wedge\bar\partial x.
\]
Choose horizontal vectors of unit length for $2\pi\alpha$, one orthogonal to the radial direction and the other
\[
 e_r=\frac{\xi_1\partial_{\xi_1}+\xi_2\partial_{\xi_2}}
                {\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}.
\]
Adjoin the vertical vector $\partial_w$. In this frame the matrix of $2\pi(\omega+\ddc V)$ is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 (\mu+V_x)/\mu&0&0\\
 0&(\mu+V_{xx})/[\mu(1-\mu)]&
       (\mu+V_{x\bar w})/\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}\\
 0&(\mu+V_{wx})/\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}&1+V_{w\bar w}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Taking the Schur complement of the last diagonal entry gives \eqref{eq:hopf-positivity}. At $\xi=0$, the derivatives of $V$ in the base directions vanish and $\partial s=\mathrm{d}w$, which gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

A rapid cutoff of a concentrated fiber potential can make the mixed derivative $V_{x\bar w}$ too large for this criterion. We therefore spread the concentration by the heat equation before cutting off, so that the fiber derivatives are already exponentially small in the cutoff region.

\section{Heat potentials and the mixed derivative}

The fiber potential will be built from the heat kernel of $E$. It has large vertical energy at small times and decays at large times, where it can be cut off, and a lower bound for its logarithmic Hessian controls vertical positivity. To control the radial--vertical block when the time varies, we also need an estimate relating the kernel to its logarithmic gradient.

For $t>0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-heat}
 k_t(w)=\frac2t\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
                 \exp\left(-\frac{|w-\lambda|^2}{t}\right).
\end{equation}
This is a smooth positive $\Lambda$-periodic function of integral one: unfolding the integral reduces it to the Gaussian integral on $\C$, since $\mathrm{d}A_E=\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b/(2\pi)$ for $w=a+\mathrm ib$.
For $(p,q)\in\mathbb Z^2$ set
\[
 \nu_{p,q}=p+\frac{\mathrm iq}{2\pi},\qquad
 \chi_{p,q}(w)=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm ip\operatorname{Re}w+
                         \mathrm iq\operatorname{Im}w}.
\]
These characters form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(E,\mathrm{d}A_E)$. Computing the Fourier coefficients by the same unfolding gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-heat-fourier}
 k_t=\sum_{p,q}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi^2|\nu_{p,q}|^2t}\chi_{p,q},
 \qquad \partial_tk_t=\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}k_t.
\end{equation}
All differentiated series converge locally uniformly for $t>0$.

\Needspace{19\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hopf-fiber-potential}
Let $\eta>0$ and define
\[
 F_\eta(t,w)=-\eta\int_t^\infty(k_r(w)-1)\,\mathrm{d}r.
\]
Then $F_\eta$ is smooth and real, and
\begin{align}
 (F_\eta)_t=(F_\eta)_{w\bar w}
   &=\eta(k_t-1),\label{eq:hopf-F-heat}\\
 (\log k_t)_{w\bar w}&\geq-1/t,\label{eq:hopf-log-hessian}\\
 \int_E|(F_\eta)_w(t,w)|^2\mathrm{d}A_E
   &=2\eta^2\log(1/t)+O(\eta^2)\quad(0<t\leq1).
   \label{eq:hopf-energy}
\end{align}
The error term in \eqref{eq:hopf-energy} is uniform in $t$ and $\eta$. For fixed nonnegative integers $a,m$ there is also a uniform estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-large-time}
 \|\partial_t^aF_\eta(t,\cdot)\|_{C^m(E)}
 \leq C_{a,m}\eta \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}\quad(t\geq1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Integrating the nonconstant Fourier modes gives
\[
 F_\eta(t,w)=-\frac{\eta}{\pi^2}
 \sum_{(p,q)\ne(0,0)}
 \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\pi^2|\nu_{p,q}|^2t}}{|\nu_{p,q}|^2}\chi_{p,q}(w).
\]
This proves smoothness, reality and \eqref{eq:hopf-F-heat}. The least nonzero value of $\pi^2|\nu_{p,q}|^2$ is $1/4$, and any fixed number of differentiations introduces coefficients of polynomial growth in $|\nu_{p,q}|$. Factoring out $\mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$ for $t\geq1$ therefore leaves a convergent Gaussian series, which proves \eqref{eq:hopf-large-time}.

To prove \eqref{eq:hopf-log-hessian}, fix a lift of $w$ and give $\Lambda$ the probability weights
\[
 P_\lambda=
 \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-|w-\lambda|^2/t}}
      {\sum_{\lambda'}\mathrm{e}^{-|w-\lambda'|^2/t}}.
\]
Logarithmic differentiation of the Gaussian sum yields
\[
 t\partial_{\bar w}\log k_t=-\sum_\lambda(w-\lambda)P_\lambda,
\]
and one more derivative gives
\[
 (\log k_t)_{w\bar w}
 =-\frac1t+\frac1{t^2}
 \left(\sum_\lambda|w-\lambda|^2P_\lambda
       -\left|\sum_\lambda(w-\lambda)P_\lambda\right|^2\right).
\]
The expression in parentheses is a variance and is nonnegative.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Finally, Parseval's identity gives, with $\mathcal E_\eta(t)$ denoting the energy in \eqref{eq:hopf-energy},
\[
 \mathcal E_\eta(t)=\frac{\eta^2}{\pi^2}
   \sum_{(p,q)\ne(0,0)}
   \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi^2|\nu_{p,q}|^2t}}{|\nu_{p,q}|^2},
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_\eta'(t)=-2\eta^2(k_{2t}(0)-1).
\]
The Gaussian formula at the origin implies
\[
 k_{2t}(0)=\frac1t+
    O\left(t^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-1/(4t)}\right)\quad(0<t\leq1).
\]
To see this, isolate the zero lattice vector and use
$\mathrm{e}^{-|\lambda|^2/(2t)}\leq \mathrm{e}^{-1/(4t)}\mathrm{e}^{-|\lambda|^2/4}$ for $\lambda\ne0$. The error term is integrable near $t=0$, and $\mathcal E_\eta(1)=O(\eta^2)$. Integrating the derivative between $t$ and $1$ proves \eqref{eq:hopf-energy}.
\end{proof}

The Schur complement subtracts a square involving the mixed derivative. When the time parameter is allowed to vary, the heat equation produces a compensating positive square, and the following bound relating the heat kernel to its logarithmic gradient controls the region where that square is small.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hopf-gradient}
For a constant $C_0\geq1$ depending only on $E$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-gradient}
 tk_t(w)\leq C_0\exp\left(
   -\frac{|t\partial_{\bar w}\log k_t(w)|^2}{C_0t}\right)
 \quad(0<t\leq1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nearest lattice point $\lambda_0$, and put
$d=|w-\lambda_0|$ and $s_\lambda=|w-\lambda|^2-d^2\geq0$.
The distance $d$ is bounded by the diameter of a fundamental rectangle. By the triangle inequality, $s_\lambda\geq|\lambda-\lambda_0|^2/4-C$, and outside a fixed finite set of $\lambda$ we have $s_\lambda\geq|\lambda-\lambda_0|^2/8$. Consequently,
\[
 \sum_\lambda (1+|w-\lambda|)\mathrm{e}^{-s_\lambda/t}\leq C,
 \qquad tk_t(w)\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-d^2/t}.
\]
The formula for $t\partial_{\bar w}\log k_t$ in the proof of \cref{lem:hopf-fiber-potential} gives, with
$G=|t\partial_{\bar w}\log k_t|$,
\[
 G\leq d+\sum_{\lambda\ne\lambda_0}|w-\lambda|\mathrm{e}^{-s_\lambda/t}.
\]
If $d\geq1/4$, this is at most $Cd$. If $d<1/4$, the nonzero lattice distances are at least one and
\[
 s_\lambda\geq|\lambda-\lambda_0|
                    (|\lambda-\lambda_0|-2d)
              \geq\tfrac12|\lambda-\lambda_0|^2.
\]
Summing the resulting Gaussian bound gives
$G\leq C(d+\mathrm{e}^{-c/t})$ in all cases. When $G>2C\mathrm{e}^{-c/t}$, we have $d\geq G/(2C)$, and the bound $tk_t\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-d^2/t}$ gives \eqref{eq:hopf-gradient}. In the remaining case $G^2/t$ is bounded, and \eqref{eq:hopf-gradient} follows from $tk_t\leq C$ after increasing $C_0$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hopf-square}
There is a constant $C$ depending only on $E$ with the following property. For $0<\kappa<1/(200C_0)$, $0<\eta<1/10$, $0<t\leq1$ and $1/2\leq\mu\leq1$, define $\sigma>0$ and the functions $h,\zeta$ of $w\in E$ by
\[
 \sigma^2=\frac{2\kappa}{\eta}\log\frac1{\eta t},\qquad
 h=\eta k_t,\qquad
 \zeta=\frac{\sigma}{\mu}\,t\partial_{\bar w}\log k_t.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-square}
 \kappa tk_t-
 \frac{\mu^2(1-\eta)h}{1-\eta+h}
       \left|\zeta-\frac1{1-\eta}\right|^2
 \leq C\kappa\exp\left(-\frac1{C\sigma^2t}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $\sigma^2t\geq1/100$, the bound $tk_t\leq C_0$ gives \eqref{eq:hopf-square} after enlarging $C$.
Assume therefore that $\sigma^2t<1/100$. If
$|\zeta-(1-\eta)^{-1}|\geq1/4$, the subtracted expression is at least $h/[100(1+h)]$. Since $\log(1/(\eta t))>2$, the definition of $\sigma$ gives
\[
 \kappa t(1+h)\leq\frac{\eta}{400}+C_0\kappa\eta
 <\frac{\eta}{100}.
\]
Multiplication by $k_t/(1+h)$ shows that the left side of \eqref{eq:hopf-square} is nonpositive.

Otherwise $|\zeta|\geq3/4$, so
\[
 |t\partial_{\bar w}\log k_t|=\frac{\mu|\zeta|}{\sigma}
 \geq\frac3{8\sigma}.
\]
Discarding the nonnegative square and applying \cref{lem:hopf-gradient} gives \eqref{eq:hopf-square} in this case as well.
\end{proof}

\section{Variation of the heat time}

The heat time will remain small on a fixed base ball and then increase with the radial variable until the fiber potential can be cut off. We choose its rate of increase so that the negative part of the Schur complement has small integral in the radial variable. Fix $\kappa$ as in \cref{lem:hopf-square}.

All estimates below concern the limit $\eta\downarrow0$, with $\kappa$ fixed. For each $\eta$ choose the heat time $\varepsilon$ on the central ball with $0<\varepsilon\leq\eta^{1/\eta^2}$; the proof of \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded} takes $\varepsilon=\eta^{1/\eta^2}$, and \cref{sec:hopf-rotational-damping} uses smaller values. Write
\[
 L=\log(1/\eta),\quad
 q_0=\sqrt{\log\frac1{\eta\varepsilon}},\quad
 a=\sqrt{\frac\kappa{2\eta}}.
\]
Fix a smooth nondecreasing $\chi\colon \R\to[0,1]$, independent of $\eta$, equal to zero on $(-\infty,0]$ and one on $[1,+\infty)$, with $\int_0^1\chi=1/2$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-scale}
 q(x)=q_0-\int_0^{ax}\chi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,
 \qquad t(x)=\eta^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-q(x)^2}.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Thus $t=\varepsilon$ on $x\leq0$, and $q=q_0+1/2-ax$ on $x\geq a^{-1}$. Put
\[
 b=\frac{q_0-1/2}{a},\qquad
 x_* =\frac{q_0+1/2}{a}.
\]
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For small $\eta$, we have $0<a^{-1}<b<x_*$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-scale-size}
 q(b)=1,\qquad q(x_*)=0.
\end{equation}
Fix also a smooth cutoff $\vartheta\colon \R\to[0,1]$, independent of $\eta$, zero on $(-\infty,0]$ and one on $[1,+\infty)$, and define
\[
 W(x,w)=\vartheta(q(x))F_\eta(t(x),w).
\]
This function equals $F_\eta(\varepsilon,w)$ for $x\leq0$ and vanishes for $x\geq x_*$. Moreover, by \eqref{eq:hopf-F-heat},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-vertical-positive}
 1+W_{w\bar w}=1+\vartheta(q)\eta(k_t-1)>1-\eta.
\end{equation}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hopf-small-defect}
For the functions just constructed, let
\[
 d_\eta(x)=\max_{w\in E}\max\{-Q[W](x,w),0\}.
\]
There are $C,\eta_0>0$, depending only on $\kappa$, $\chi$ and $\vartheta$, such that the following holds whenever $0<\eta<\eta_0$ and $\eta x_*\leq1/(10\mathrm{e})$. The function $d_\eta$ is continuous and supported in $[0,b]$, $Q[W]>0$ outside $[0,b]\times E$, $Q[W]\geq2/27$ on $[-2,-1]\times E$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-small-defect}
 \int_\R d_\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\bigl(\eta b+\sqrt{\eta L}\bigr),\qquad
 \sup_{\R\times E}\max\{-W_x,0\}\leq C\sqrt{\eta L}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first derive an exact formula for $Q[W]$ on $[0,b]$, then bound its negative part separately on $[0,a^{-1}]$ and on $[a^{-1},b]$, integrate these bounds, and finally examine the ends of the radial line.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: the Schur complement on $[0,b]$.} Write $F=F_\eta$ for the heat potential. On $[0,b]$ we have $q\geq1$, hence $\vartheta(q)=1$ and $W=F(t(x),w)$. Set
\[
 h=\eta k_t,\qquad \sigma=(\log t)_x,\qquad
 \zeta=\frac{\sigma t}{\mu}\partial_{\bar w}\log k_t.
\]
The heat equation gives $F_t=h-\eta$, and the chain rule gives
\[
 W_x=\sigma tF_t,\quad
 W_{xx}=(\sigma_x+\sigma^2)tF_t+\sigma^2t^2F_{tt},
 \quad W_{x\bar w}=\sigma tF_{t\bar w}.
\]
Substituting these in $Q[W]$, using
$h_t=h((\log k_t)_{w\bar w}+|\partial_{\bar w}\log k_t|^2)$,
and completing the square, we obtain the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-Q-identity}
 \begin{split}
 Q[W]={}&\sigma^2t\{h[1+t(\log k_t)_{w\bar w}]-\eta\}
       +\sigma_xtF_t+\mu(1-\mu)-\frac{\mu^2\eta}{1-\eta}\\
 &+\frac{\mu^2(1-\eta)h}{1-\eta+h}
       \left|\zeta-\frac1{1-\eta}\right|^2.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:hopf-log-hessian}, the part $\sigma^2th[1+t(\log k_t)_{w\bar w}]$ is nonnegative.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: the initial interval $[0,a^{-1}]$.} Here
\[
 \sigma=2aq\chi(ax),\qquad
 \sigma_x=-\frac\kappa\eta\chi(ax)^2
               +\frac\kappa\eta q\chi'(ax).
\]
Moreover $q_0-1/2\leq q\leq q_0$ and $t\leq\varepsilon \mathrm{e}^{q_0}$. Since $\log(1/\varepsilon)\geq L/\eta^2$ and $q_0^2\leq2\log(1/\varepsilon)$, for small $\eta$ both $t$ and $q_0^2t$ are at most $\varepsilon^{1/4}\leq\eta^{1/(4\eta^2)}$, which tends to zero faster than any power of $\eta$. In particular,
\[
 \kappa q\chi'(ax)t\leq\eta,\qquad \sigma^2t\leq\eta^2.
\]
At each point where $\chi(ax)>0$, apply \cref{lem:hopf-square} with $\kappa\chi(ax)^2$ in place of $\kappa$; its constant is uniform in $x$. The positive square in \eqref{eq:hopf-Q-identity} then absorbs the negative term $-\kappa\chi^2tk_t$ coming from $\sigma_xtF_t$. The remaining part of $\sigma_xtF_t$, which involves $\chi'$, is at least $-\eta$, because $F_t\geq-\eta$. The other negative terms in \eqref{eq:hopf-Q-identity} are $O(\eta)$, and so is the exponential error of \cref{lem:hopf-square}, since $\sigma^2t\leq\eta^2$. Thus $d_\eta\leq C\eta$ on this interval. At points where $\chi(ax)=0$, the smooth nonnegative function $\chi$ has a minimum, so $\chi'(ax)=0$ and directly
\[
 Q[W]=\mu-\frac{\mu^2}{1-\eta+h}>0,
\]
since $\mu\leq\mu(a^{-1})<1-\eta$ for small $\eta$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 3: the interval $[a^{-1},b]$.} Here the formulas simplify to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-time-change}
 \sigma=2aq,\qquad \sigma_x=-\kappa/\eta,\qquad
 \mathrm{d}x=-\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{a}=\sqrt{\frac\eta{2\kappa}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{tq}.
\end{equation}
For $t\leq1$, \cref{lem:hopf-square} and \eqref{eq:hopf-Q-identity} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-defect-small-time}
 d_\eta(x)\leq2\eta+2\kappa q^2t
          +C\kappa\exp\left(-\frac\eta{C\kappa q^2t}\right).
\end{equation}
Indeed, $-\eta\sigma^2t=-2\kappa q^2t$ and
$\sigma_xtF_t=-\kappa tk_t+\kappa t$; these are the two terms for which the scale was chosen. When $t\geq1$, we insert the exponential derivative estimates \eqref{eq:hopf-large-time} directly in the definition of $Q$. The denominator stays above $1-\eta$, and the chain rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-defect-large-time}
 d_\eta(x)\leq C\kappa q^2t^2\mathrm{e}^{-t/4}.
\end{equation}
The perturbing derivatives are bounded by constants times
$\eta \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$, $\eta\sigma t \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$ and
$\eta[(|\sigma_x|+\sigma^2)t+\sigma^2t^2]\mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$. Since $q,t\geq1$ here, each is bounded by a constant times the right side of \eqref{eq:hopf-defect-large-time}. Since $1+W_{w\bar w}\geq1-\eta$, subtracting the two quotients gives
\[
 |Q[W]-Q[0]|\leq |W_{xx}|+
 C\bigl(|W_{x\bar w}|+|W_{x\bar w}|^2+|W_{w\bar w}|\bigr).
\]
The square of the mixed derivative is at most $C\kappa\eta q^2t^2\mathrm{e}^{-t/2}$, so it satisfies the same bound. Since $Q[0]>0$, this proves \eqref{eq:hopf-defect-large-time}.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 4: integration of the negative part.} The constant $2\eta$ and the initial interval contribute $O(\eta b)$. In the small-time region, $q\geq\sqrt L$ and $t=\eta^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-q^2}$. Therefore
\[
 \int 2\kappa q^2t\,\mathrm{d}x
 \leq\frac{2\kappa}{a\eta}
       \int_{\sqrt L}^{\infty}q^2\mathrm{e}^{-q^2}\,\mathrm{d}q
 \leq C\sqrt{\eta L}.
\]
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For the exponential term the integral is at most
\[
 \frac{C\kappa}{a}
 \int_{\sqrt L}^{q_0}
 \exp\left(-\frac{\eta^2\mathrm{e}^{q^2}}{C\kappa q^2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}q.
\]
The interval $[\sqrt L,2\sqrt L]$ has length $O(\sqrt L)$. For $q\geq2\sqrt L$ and sufficiently small $\eta$,
$\eta^2\mathrm{e}^{q^2}/q^2\geq \mathrm{e}^{q^2/4}$. The integrand on this remaining interval is bounded by $\exp(-\mathrm{e}^{q^2/4}/(C\kappa))$, which is integrable on $[1,+\infty)$. The prefactor is $O(\sqrt\eta)$, and the initial interval contributes $O(\sqrt{\eta L})$, so the full expression is $O(\sqrt{\eta L})$. In the large-time region, \eqref{eq:hopf-time-change} gives
\[
 \int d_\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
 \leq C\sqrt\eta\int_1^{1/(\mathrm{e}\eta)}qt \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}\,\mathrm{d}t
 \leq C\sqrt{\eta L}\int_1^\infty t \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}\,\mathrm{d}t.
\]
This proves the integral bound on $[0,b]$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 5: the radial slope.} The negative part of $W_x$ is uniformly small as well. For $t\leq1$, we have $F_t\geq-\eta$ and $0\leq\sigma\leq2aq$, so
\[
 \max\{-W_x,0\}\leq\sqrt{2\kappa\eta}\,
        t\sqrt{\log\frac1{\eta t}}\leq C\sqrt{\eta L}.
\]
The last inequality follows because $t\sqrt{L+\log(1/t)}$ is increasing on $(0,1]$ when $L>1$. For $t\geq1$ and $q\geq1$, use
$|W_x|\leq C\sqrt\eta\,qt \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$ and $q\leq\sqrt L$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 6: the ends.} It remains to consider the two ends, including the cutoff region. If $b\leq x\leq x_*$, then $0\leq q\leq1$ and
$1/(\mathrm{e}\eta)\leq t\leq1/\eta$. The derivatives of the cutoffs and scale have at most polynomial growth in $\eta^{-1}$. Thus \eqref{eq:hopf-large-time} implies
\[
 \sup_w\bigl(|W_x|+|Q[W]-Q[0]|\bigr)
 \leq C\mathrm{e}^{-1/(5\mathrm{e}\eta)}.
\]
But $Q[0]=\mu(1-\mu)\geq \mathrm{e}^{-x_*}/4\geq\mathrm{e}^{-1/(10\mathrm{e}\eta)}/4$ on this interval, because $\eta x_*\leq1/(10\mathrm{e})$. Hence $Q[W]>0$ there for small $\eta$. For $x\geq x_*$, $W=0$ and positivity is immediate. For $x\leq0$, $W_x=0$ and
\[
 Q[W]\geq\mu\left(1-\frac\mu{1-\eta}\right)>0,
\]
since $\mu\leq1/2$. On $[-2,-1]$, the bounds $1/9\leq\mu\leq3/10$ and $1-\eta\geq9/10$ give $Q[W]\geq2/27$. Finally, $Q[W]$ is continuous, with positive denominator, so the maximum of its negative part over the compact fiber $E$ is continuous in $x$. This proves the assertions about support, positivity and continuity, and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{A base correction and unbounded mass}

Adding a function of $x$ alone changes $Q[W]$ by its second derivative and leaves the vertical energy unchanged. The integral bound for $d_\eta$ lets us choose this second derivative larger than $d_\eta$ and compensate its integral on an interval where $Q[W]$ is uniformly positive. The correction has small first derivative, so the remaining horizontal coefficient stays positive as well.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hopf-base-correction}
Fix a smooth $\psi\geq0$ supported in $(-2,-1)$ with $\int_\R\psi=1$, and put $m_0=\min\{\mu(-2)/2,\,1/(27\|\psi\|_\infty)\}$. Let $\eta$ and $W$ be as in \cref{lem:hopf-small-defect}, with $\max\{-W_x,0\}\leq1/4$. Let $r\geq0$ be a smooth function supported in $[-1/2,b+1/2]$ with $r>d_\eta$ on $[0,b]$ and $m:=\int_\R r\,\mathrm{d}x<m_0$, and put
\[
 g(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x(x-s)
                   (r(s)-m\psi(s))\,\mathrm{d}s,
 \qquad V=W+g.
\]
Then $-m\leq g'\leq0$, $g=0$ for $x\leq-2$, $g$ is constant for $x\geq b+1/2$, and $-m(x+2)\leq g(x)\leq0$ for $x\geq-2$. The function $V$ extends to a smooth function on $X$ with $\omega+\ddc V>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $g''=r-m\psi$, where $\psi$ is supported to the left of the support of $r$ and both terms have integral $m$, we have $g'=-m\int_{-\infty}^x\psi$ for $x\leq-1/2$ and $g'=-m+\int_{-\infty}^xr$ for $x\geq-1/2$. This gives $-m\leq g'\leq0$, with $g'=0$ for $x\leq-2$ and for $x\geq b+1/2$, and hence the assertions about $g$. In particular, $V$ is independent of $x$ near $-\infty$ and constant near $+\infty$, so it extends smoothly to $X$.

Since $g$ is independent of $w$, the Schur complement changes by exactly its second derivative:
\[
 Q[V]=Q[W]+r-m\psi.
\]
This is positive on $[0,b]$ because $r>d_\eta$ there. On $(-2,-1)$ it is at least $2/27-m\|\psi\|_\infty>0$. Elsewhere $Q[W]>0$, $\psi=0$ and $r\geq0$.
The vertical coefficient remains above $1-\eta$ by \eqref{eq:hopf-vertical-positive}. For the remaining coefficient, if $x\leq-2$, both $W_x$ and $g'$ vanish; if $-2\leq x\leq0$, then $W_x=0$ and $\mu\geq\mu(-2)>m\geq|g'|$; and if $x\geq0$, then $\mu\geq1/2$, $W_x\geq-1/4$ and $g'\geq-m>-1/8$. In all cases $\mu+V_x>0$. Therefore \cref{lem:hopf-positivity} gives $\omega+\ddc V>0$ over $U_0$. Near the fibers over $\mathbb P^2\setminus U_0$, the function $V$ is constant, so $\omega+\ddc V=\omega>0$ there as well.
\end{proof}

Such functions $r$ exist with $\int_\R r\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_\R d_\eta\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta$ for every $\delta>0$: add a small positive constant to a uniform smooth approximation of the compactly supported continuous function $d_\eta$, and multiply by a cutoff equal to one on $[0,b]$ and supported in $[-1/2,b+1/2]$.

\begin{proof}[of \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}]
Take $\varepsilon=\eta^{1/\eta^2}$. Then $q_0^2=(1+\eta^{-2})L$, so $b,x_*=O(\sqrt{L/\eta})$ and $\eta x_*\to0$. For small $\eta$, \cref{lem:hopf-small-defect} therefore gives
\[
 \int_\R d_\eta\,\mathrm{d}x=o(1),\qquad
 \sup_{\R\times E}\max\{-W_x,0\}=o(1).
\]
Choose $r_\eta$ as in \cref{lem:hopf-base-correction} with $m_\eta=\int_\R r_\eta\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_\R d_\eta\,\mathrm{d}x+\eta=o(1)$, and let $g_\eta$ and $V_\eta=W+g_\eta$ be the corresponding functions. For small $\eta$, $m_\eta<m_0$ and $\max\{-W_x,0\}\leq1/4$, and that lemma shows that $V_\eta$ is smooth on $X$ with $\omega+\ddc V_\eta>0$.

On the fixed ball $B=\{|\xi|^2<\mathrm{e}^{-3}\}\Subset U_0$, one has
$V_\eta=F_\eta(\varepsilon,w)$, including at $\xi=0$. Thus
\[
 \int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}|(V_\eta)_w|^2\mathrm{d}A_E
 =2\eta^2\log(1/\varepsilon)+O(\eta^2)
 =2\log(1/\eta)+O(\eta^2)\longrightarrow+\infty
\]
uniformly for $y\in B$. Since $\int_B\omega_{\FS}^2>0$, the mass identity \eqref{eq:hopf-mass} shows that $\int_X(\omega+\ddc V_\eta)^3\to+\infty$, and the theorem follows by taking $V_j=V_{\eta_j}$ along any sequence $\eta_j\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Minimum mass and fiber averaging}

We return to the threefold $X=H_3$ and its form $\omega$.

Averaging along the elliptic fibers removes the variation measured by \eqref{eq:hopf-mass}. The Poincar\'e inequality on the fibers bounds the distance from a potential to its average by the excess mass. Bounded regularization preserves this inequality, which therefore identifies all bounded potentials of minimal mass and shows that potentials of almost minimal mass are close to them.

Let $E$ act on $X$ by scalar multiplication: $\tau_a[z]=[\mathrm{e}^a z]$ for $a\in E$. The action preserves $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\omega$, and $\mathrm{d}A_E$ is the Haar probability measure of $E$. For a bounded Borel function $u\colon X\to\mathbb R$, set
\[
 \mathrm{d}\nu=\frac{\omega^3}{6}=\frac12\alpha^2\wedge\beta,
 \qquad
 Au(p)=\int_Eu(\tau_a(p))\,\mathrm{d}A_E(a)
 \quad(p\in X).
\]
Thus $Au$ is constant on each fiber, and $\nu$ is the probability measure whose disintegration along $\pi$ has fiber measures $\mathrm{d}A_E$ and base measure $\omega_{\FS}^2$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hopf-fiber-average}\index{Hopf manifold!fiber averaging}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^\infty(X)$, the average $Au$ is the pullback of a bounded $\omega_{\FS}$-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^2$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-average-mass}
 \|u-Au\|_{L^2(X,\nu)}^2
 \leq\frac23\left(\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^3-6\right).
\end{equation}
The coefficient $2/3$ is optimal, even for smooth $u$ with $\omega+\ddc u>0$. Moreover,
\[
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^3=6
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 u=\pi^*v\quad\text{for some }
 v\in\PSH(\mathbb P^2,\omega_{\FS})\cap L^\infty(\mathbb P^2).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First suppose that $u$ is smooth and $\omega+\ddc u>0$. Averaging the positive forms $\tau_a^*(\omega+\ddc u)$ gives $\omega+\ddc Au>0$. Since $Au$ is smooth and constant on the fibers, $Au=\pi^*v$ for a smooth function $v$ on $\mathbb P^2$. At each point, the kernel of $\partial s$ maps isomorphically onto the tangent space of the base, and $\beta$ vanishes on it. Restricting $\omega+\ddc Au=\pi^*(\omega_{\FS}+\ddc v)+\beta$ to this kernel therefore shows that $\omega_{\FS}+\ddc v>0$.

For a smooth function $f\colon E\to\mathbb R$, the nonconstant Fourier characters in \eqref{eq:hopf-heat-fourier}, indexed by $(p,q)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$, have eigenvalues
$\pi^2p^2+q^2/4\geq1/4$ for $-\partial_w\partial_{\bar w}$. Parseval's identity therefore gives
\[
 \int_E\left|f-\int_Ef\,\mathrm{d}A_E\right|^2\mathrm{d}A_E
 \leq4\int_E|f_w|^2\,\mathrm{d}A_E.
\]
Applying this inequality on every fiber, integrating against $\omega_{\FS}^2$ and using the mass identity \eqref{eq:hopf-mass}, we obtain \eqref{eq:hopf-average-mass}.

For bounded $u$, choose uniformly bounded smooth functions $u_j\downarrow u$ with $\omega+\ddc u_j>0$ by \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization}. Their averages decrease pointwise to $Au$ by dominated convergence. The corresponding smooth base potentials $v_j$ decrease to a bounded $\omega_{\FS}$-plurisubharmonic function $v$, so $Au=\pi^*v$. Dominated convergence gives $u_j\to u$ and $Au_j\to Au$ in $L^2(X,\nu)$, while bounded Bedford--Taylor continuity gives convergence of the total masses. Thus \eqref{eq:hopf-average-mass} passes to $u$.

If the mass is $6$, the inequality gives $u=Au$ almost everywhere. Since both functions are bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic, \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} gives equality everywhere. Conversely, if $u=\pi^*v$ with bounded $v\in\PSH(\mathbb P^2,\omega_{\FS})$, then \cref{lem:hopf-basic-potentials}(3), applied on $X=H_3$ with $\omega=\omega_3$ and $a=b=1$, gives mass $6$.

It remains to prove that the constant $2/3$ is optimal. Choose a nonzero real $\rho\in C_c^\infty(U_0)$. For $\delta\in\mathbb R$, the function
\[
 u(\xi,w)=\delta\rho(\xi)\cos(\operatorname{Im}w)
\]
extends by zero to a smooth function on $X$. For sufficiently small nonzero real $\delta$, it satisfies $\omega+\ddc u>0$. Its fiber average is zero, and its only fiber frequencies are $(p,q)=(0,\pm1)$, with eigenvalue $1/4$. Hence equality holds in the fiber inequality, and therefore in \eqref{eq:hopf-average-mass}, with nonzero left-hand side. No smaller coefficient can hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hopf-lower-mass}\index{Hopf manifold!minimum mass}
The Hopf metric satisfies
\[
 v_-(\omega)=6,\qquad v_+(\omega)=+\infty.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound $v_-(\omega)\geq6$ follows from \eqref{eq:hopf-average-mass}, and it is attained by constants. \Cref{thm:hopf-unbounded} gives $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$.
\end{proof}
