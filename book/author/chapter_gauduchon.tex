\chapter{Gauduchon metrics and linear mass}
\label{chap:gauduchon}

Throughout this chapter, $\omega$ is a fixed Hermitian form on $X$, and $n\geq2$ unless a section specifies another setting.

By \cref{chap:mass}, the Monge--Amp\`ere mass of a potential need not be determined by a nonclosed background. A linear quantity survives. If $\gamma$ is a Gauduchon metric, that is, a Hermitian form with $\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0$, and if $u$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic for a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$, then $\int_X(\theta+\ddc u)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}$ depends only on $\theta$. Gauduchon's theorem produces such metrics in every conformal class, and these Gauduchon degrees take over the role of cohomological pairings. They bound the mass of positive currents, they decide whether $\theta$ admits a positive current $\theta+\ddc u$, and their normalized infimum is the largest lower curvature bound attainable in the background $\theta$; by weak compactness, this infimum is a minimum over Gauduchon currents. Applied to a perturbed form $\omega_u$, the same conformal construction gives the probability measure that characterizes the range of the linearized Monge--Amp\`ere operator.

Gauduchon degrees of holomorphic line bundles are defined in the same way, using the curvature of a Hermitian metric, and a positive degree excludes a metric with nonpositive curvature. Averaging the same argument over the fibers obstructs plurisubharmonic norms on vector bundles near a direct sum of line bundles of positive degree. On Hirzebruch surfaces this yields stable ample bundles that carry no Finsler metric of semipositive Kobayashi curvature, and it bounds the convexity of the complement of the zero section in their projective completions. The scalar Gauduchon equation also improves bundle metrics: a conformal change spreads directional curvature positivity from an open set to the whole manifold, which applies to the tangent bundles of Ricci-flat K\"ahler surfaces. Finally, on projective manifolds, Griffiths positivity admits a duality criterion analogous to the Lamari criterion, with a positive measure over the dual bundle in place of a Gauduchon current.

\section{Conformal Gauduchon metrics}

By \cref{prop:mass-variation}, the first variation of the mass at $u=0$ vanishes exactly when $\ddc(\omega^{n-1})=0$. This condition can be achieved in every conformal class: finding a conformal factor amounts to finding a positive generator of the kernel of the adjoint of a scalar elliptic operator.

\begin{definition}\label{def:gauduchon-metric}\label{def:balanced-pluriclosed}\index{Gauduchon metric!definition}\index{Hermitian metric!balanced}\index{Hermitian metric!pluriclosed}
A Hermitian form $\gamma$ is \emph{Gauduchon}, or a \emph{Gauduchon metric}, if
\[
 \ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0.
\]
It is \emph{balanced} if $\mathrm d(\gamma^{n-1})=0$, and \emph{pluriclosed} if $\ddc\gamma=0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Gauduchon's theorem]\label{thm:gauduchon}\index{Gauduchon metric!conformal representative}
Every Hermitian form $\eta$ has a conformal Gauduchon representative. More precisely, there is a smooth strictly positive function $\rho$, unique up to multiplication by a positive constant, such that
\[
 \ddc(\rho\eta^{n-1})=0.
\]
The associated Gauduchon form is $\gamma=\rho^{1/(n-1)}\eta$.
\end{theorem}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
For a smooth real function $v$ on $X$, define
\[
 Lv=n\frac{\ddc v\wedge\eta^{n-1}}{\eta^n}.
\]
This real second-order operator has positive definite principal symbol and no zeroth-order term. By the maximum principle, its kernel consists of the constants. Its index is zero, since the linear path joining it to a Laplace operator consists of elliptic operators. Fredholm theory and elliptic regularity therefore show that the formal adjoint $L^*$ of $L$ with respect to $\eta^n$ has a one-dimensional kernel, generated by a nonzero smooth real function $\rho$.

We first prove that $\int_X\rho\eta^n\ne0$. Otherwise the constant function $1$ would be orthogonal to the kernel of $L^*$, and the Fredholm alternative would solve $Lv=1$, which is impossible at a maximum point of $v$. We may therefore rescale so that $\int_X\rho\eta^n=1$.

Next we show that $\rho\geq0$. Suppose instead that $\rho$ is negative somewhere. Choose a nonzero smooth function $h\geq0$ supported where $\rho<0$, and put $c=\int_Xh\rho\eta^n<0$. The function $h-c$ is strictly positive and orthogonal to the kernel of $L^*$, so the Fredholm alternative would solve $Lv=h-c>0$, which is again impossible at a maximum point of $v$.

In real local coordinates $(x_1,\ldots,x_{2n})$, write $L^*=A^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j+B^i\partial_i+C$, where $\partial_i=\partial/\partial x_i$, the coefficients are smooth, and repeated indices are summed from $1$ to $2n$. The leading symbol $(A^{ij})$ is positive definite. Since $\rho\geq0$,
\[
 (L^*-\max(C,0))\rho=-\max(C,0)\rho\leq0,
\]
and the operator $L^*-\max(C,0)$ has nonpositive zeroth-order term. By the strong minimum principle, a zero of $\rho$ would force $\rho$ to vanish identically on the connected manifold $X$. Hence $\rho>0$.

Finally, integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_X\rho Lv\,\eta^n
 =n\int_Xv\,\ddc(\rho\eta^{n-1}).
\]
Thus the equation $L^*\rho=0$ is precisely $\ddc(\rho\eta^{n-1})=0$, and the one-dimensional adjoint kernel gives uniqueness up to positive scaling. Since $\gamma^{n-1}=\rho\eta^{n-1}$, the form $\gamma$ is Gauduchon.
\end{proof}

A K\"ahler form, that is, a closed Hermitian form, is both balanced and pluriclosed, and every balanced form is Gauduchon. On surfaces, balanced forms are K\"ahler, and the Gauduchon and pluriclosed conditions coincide.

\section{Conservation of the trace mass}

The power $\gamma^{n-1}$ of a Gauduchon metric annihilates every complex Hessian under integration. It therefore assigns to a positive current $\theta+\ddc u$ a mass depending only on its smooth background $\theta$, also when the potential $u$ is unbounded.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gauduchon-trace}\index{Gauduchon trace!conservation}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, let $u$ be a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function, and let $\gamma$ be a Gauduchon metric. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauduchon-trace}
 \int_X(\theta+\ddc u)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 =\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0$, distributional integration by parts gives
\[
 \langle\ddc u,\gamma^{n-1}\rangle
 =\langle u,\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})\rangle=0.
\]
The product on the left of \eqref{eq:gauduchon-trace} is a positive measure, whose total mass is therefore the finite number on the right.
\end{proof}

We call $\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}$ the \emph{Gauduchon degree} of $\theta$ with respect to $\gamma$. Here $\theta+\ddc u$ is the full distributional current, including any mass carried by the pole set of $u$. The non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of \cref{chap:products} discards this mass; \cref{ex:p1-raw-tail} illustrates the difference on a curve.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:trace-bounds}
There are constants $0<a\leq b<+\infty$, depending only on $\omega$ and a chosen Gauduchon metric, such that every $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ satisfies
\[
 a\leq\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq b.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix a Gauduchon form $\gamma$ on $X$ and choose positive constants $c,C$ with
$c\gamma^{n-1}\leq\omega^{n-1}\leq C\gamma^{n-1}$.
Wedging these inequalities with the positive current $\omega+\ddc u$ and integrating, we obtain, by \cref{prop:gauduchon-trace}, the bounds
$cA$ and $CA$, where $A=\int_X\omega\wedge\gamma^{n-1}>0$.
\end{proof}

The trace bound controls the mass of positive currents and hence their weak compactness. By itself it gives no bound for the Monge--Amp\`ere mass, since a bound on the integral of a trace does not bound the integral of its $n$th power.

\Needspace{19\baselineskip}
The pointwise arithmetic--geometric mean inequality relates the conserved trace to the density in a smooth Monge--Amp\`ere equation. Fix a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ on $X$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gauduchon-amgm}
For any two Hermitian forms $\eta,\gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauduchon-amgm}
 \eta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 \geq\left(\frac{\eta^n}{\gamma^n}\right)^{1/n}\gamma^n
\end{equation}
pointwise. Consequently, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $f$ a smooth strictly positive function. If $u\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$ and $c>0$ satisfy $(\theta+\ddc u)^n=c f\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $\theta+\ddc u>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauduchon-c-bound}
 c^{1/n}
 \int_X\left(\frac{f\,\mathrm{d}V}{\gamma^n}\right)^{1/n}\gamma^n
 \leq\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
\end{equation}
for every Gauduchon metric $\gamma$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In a frame where $\gamma$ is the identity, let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n>0$ be the eigenvalues of $\eta$. The ratios of the two sides of \eqref{eq:gauduchon-amgm} to $\gamma^n$ are
$n^{-1}\sum_j\lambda_j$ and $(\prod_j\lambda_j)^{1/n}$, respectively, and their comparison is the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality.

For the second assertion, apply \eqref{eq:gauduchon-amgm} with $\eta=\theta+\ddc u$, integrate, and use \eqref{eq:gauduchon-trace}.
\end{proof}

The corresponding density inequality for unbounded potentials in a Hermitian background is proved in \cref{lem:unbounded-gauduchon-trace-density}.

\section{The Gauduchon criterion for positivity}

The trace identity gives a necessary condition for $\PSH(X,\theta)$ to be nonempty: every Gauduchon degree of $\theta$ must be nonnegative. The condition is also sufficient. A Hahn--Banach separation produces a positive current with the prescribed pairings, and a scalar elliptic equation shows that its difference from $\theta$ is $\ddc$-exact.

\begin{theorem}[The generalized Lamari criterion]
\label{thm:lamari}\index{Gauduchon criterion for positivity}
For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item There exists $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\item $\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq0$ for every Gauduchon metric $\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
To apply separation, we express the test in terms of smooth strictly positive $\ddc$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-forms $\Gamma$. Every strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\Gamma$ has a unique strictly positive $(1,1)$-root $\gamma$, that is, $\Gamma=\gamma^{n-1}$. Indeed, in a frame in which $\Gamma$ is diagonal, and with the same wedge normalization on both sides, the coefficients $g_i$ of $\Gamma$ are the products $\prod_{j\neq i}\lambda_j$ of the eigenvalues $\lambda_j$ of $\gamma$. Hence
\[
 \lambda_i=\frac{(\prod_jg_j)^{1/(n-1)}}{g_i}.
\]
This spectral formula uses only a positive determinant and a matrix inverse, so the root depends smoothly on the coefficients of $\Gamma$, also at repeated eigenvalues, and pointwise uniqueness makes the local roots agree on overlaps. Consequently, $\ddc\Gamma=0$ says exactly that the root $\gamma$ is Gauduchon.

\begin{proof}[of \cref{thm:lamari}]
Necessity is \cref{prop:gauduchon-trace}. For sufficiency, let $F$ be the real vector space of smooth real $(n-1,n-1)$-forms, and let
\[
 E=\{\Gamma\in F:\ddc\Gamma=0\},\qquad
 \ell(\Gamma)=\int_X\theta\wedge\Gamma\quad(\Gamma\in E).
\]
Fix a strictly positive $\Gamma_0\in E$, which exists by \cref{thm:gauduchon}. By the root calculation above, the hypothesis says that $\ell$ is nonnegative on the strictly positive elements of $E$. Adding $\epsilon\Gamma_0$ and letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$, we see that $\ell$ is also nonnegative on the semipositive elements of $E$.

\emph{Step 1: a positive extension of $\ell$.} For $\Phi\in F$ put
\[
 p(\Phi)=\inf\{\ell(\Gamma):\Gamma\in E,\ \Gamma\geq\Phi\}.
\]
This is a finite sublinear function. To see finiteness, choose $C$ with $-C\Gamma_0\leq\Phi\leq C\Gamma_0$. The admissible element $C\Gamma_0$ gives an upper bound, while every admissible $\Gamma$ satisfies $\Gamma+C\Gamma_0\geq0$ and hence $\ell(\Gamma)\geq-C\ell(\Gamma_0)$. Sublinearity follows by adding and rescaling competitors. For $\Phi\in E$, the nonnegativity of $\ell(\Gamma-\Phi)$ for admissible $\Gamma$ shows that $p(\Phi)=\ell(\Phi)$.

The Hahn--Banach theorem gives a real linear extension $T$ of $\ell$ to $F$ with $T(\Phi)\leq p(\Phi)$ for all $\Phi\in F$. If $\Phi\geq0$, the zero form is admissible for $p(-\Phi)$, so $T(-\Phi)\leq0$, that is, $T(\Phi)\geq0$. Moreover, the bounds by $C\Gamma_0$ show that $T$ is continuous for the uniform norm. It is therefore a positive $(1,1)$-current, and the current $S=T-\theta$ annihilates $E$.

\emph{Step 2: $S$ is $\ddc$-exact.} To recover a potential from $S$, consider the scalar operator
\[
 Dv=\frac{\ddc(v\Gamma_0)}{\mathrm{d}V},
\]
where $\mathrm dV$ is the smooth positive volume form fixed above. Since $\ddc\Gamma_0=0$, this is an elliptic scalar operator with no zeroth-order term. Its kernel consists of the constants and its index is zero, while Stokes' theorem gives $\int_XDv\,\mathrm{d}V=0$. Consequently its range is exactly the space of smooth functions of integral zero, and by elliptic estimates its inverse on functions normalized by $\int_Xv\,\mathrm{d}V=0$ is continuous in the smooth topology.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
For a smooth top-degree form $\zeta$ with $\int_X\zeta=0$, solve $Dv=\zeta/\mathrm{d}V$ with this normalization and set $R\zeta=v\Gamma_0$. Then $\ddc R\zeta=\zeta$, and $R$ is continuous. Define a distribution $u$ on such top-degree forms by
\[
 \langle u,\zeta\rangle=S(R\zeta),
\]
and extend it to all top-degree forms by assigning an arbitrary value on one fixed volume form of integral one. For every $\Phi\in F$, the form $\Phi-R(\ddc\Phi)$ belongs to $E$, and $S$ annihilates it. It follows that
\[
 \langle\ddc u,\Phi\rangle
 =S(R(\ddc\Phi))=S(\Phi).
\]
Thus $\theta+\ddc u=T\geq0$, and \cref{prop:local-correction} gives a representative of $u$ in $\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\end{proof}

Applying the criterion to $\theta-a\omega$, for $a\in\mathbb R$, determines the largest lower curvature bound attainable in the background $\theta$.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gauduchon-threshold}
\index{Gauduchon degree!curvature threshold}
For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$, set
\[
 \kappa_\omega(\theta)=
 \inf_{\gamma\ \mathrm{Gauduchon}}
 \frac{\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}
      {\int_X\omega\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}.
\]
This is a finite real number, and it is the largest real $a$ for which there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ satisfying
\[
 \theta+\ddc u\geq a\omega.
\]
In particular, this largest value is attained, and $\theta$ is big, in the sense of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}, precisely when $\kappa_\omega(\theta)>0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $\theta$ is smooth, $-C\omega\leq\theta\leq C\omega$ for some $C$, so the infimum lies in $[-C,C]$. If $\theta+\ddc u\geq a\omega$, the trace identity applied to the background $\theta-a\omega$ shows that $a$ is at most each of the ratios in the definition, hence $a\leq\kappa_\omega(\theta)$. Conversely, for $a=\kappa_\omega(\theta)$, all Gauduchon degrees of $\theta-a\omega$ are nonnegative, and \cref{thm:lamari} applied to this smooth form gives the required $u$. The last assertion follows, since bigness means the existence of a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic $u$ with $\theta+\ddc u\geq\delta\omega$ for some $\delta>0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The adjoint measure of the linearization}

The derivative of the logarithmic Monge--Amp\`ere operator $u\mapsto\log(\omega_u^n/\mathrm dV)$ annihilates the constants, so its linearized equation can be solved only under a compatibility condition. The Gauduchon multiplier of \cref{thm:gauduchon} identifies this condition. Fix $u\in\HH_\omega$ and write $\eta=\omega_u$. For a smooth real direction $v$, logarithmic differentiation gives the linearized operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearized-ma}
 L_u v=n\,\frac{\ddc v\wedge\eta^{n-1}}{\eta^n}.
\end{equation}
Choose the Gauduchon multiplier $\rho_u$ of $\eta$ given by \cref{thm:gauduchon}, normalized by
\[
 \int_X\rho_u\eta^n=1,
 \qquad\text{and put}\qquad \mathrm{d}\mu_u:=\rho_u\eta^n.
\]
The \emph{adjoint measure} $\mu_u$ is a smooth positive probability measure.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:adjoint-gauduchon}\index{Adjoint measure!smooth}
For every smooth real $v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint-identity}
 \int_X L_uv\,\mathrm{d}\mu_u=0.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for smooth real $h$, the equation $L_uv=h$ has a smooth solution if and only if $\int_Xh\,\mathrm{d}\mu_u=0$. When it exists, the solution is unique modulo constants.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
The integral in \eqref{eq:adjoint-identity} is
\[
 n\int_X\ddc v\wedge\rho_u\eta^{n-1}
 =n\int_Xv\,\ddc(\rho_u\eta^{n-1})=0.
\]
This proves that the compatibility condition is necessary.

By the Fredholm argument in the proof of \cref{thm:gauduchon}, the range of $L_u:C^{k+2,\alpha}(X)\to C^{k,\alpha}(X)$ has codimension one for every integer $k\geq0$ and $0<\alpha<1$. The nonzero functional $h\mapsto\int_Xh\,\mathrm{d}\mu_u$ annihilates that range, so its kernel is the range. Elliptic regularity gives a smooth solution, and since the kernel of $L_u$ consists of the constants, the solution is unique modulo constants.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
By its defining equation and the uniqueness in \cref{thm:gauduchon}, the multiplier $\rho_u$ is constant precisely when $\eta$ is Gauduchon. Thus
\[
 \mu_u=\frac{\eta^n}{\int_X\eta^n}
 \quad\text{if }\ddc(\eta^{n-1})=0.
\]
On a surface, $\ddc\omega_u=\ddc\omega$, so every positive potential perturbation of a Gauduchon form is still Gauduchon. In higher dimension this persistence requires more than the Gauduchon condition at $u=0$. Write $M=\int_X\omega^n$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gauduchon-potential-persistence}
Every form $\omega_u$ with $u\in\HH_\omega$ is Gauduchon if and only if
\[
 \int_X\omega_u^n=M
 \quad\text{for every }u\in\HH_\omega.
\]
In that case the normalized multiplier is $\rho_u=M^{-1}$ and $\mu_u=M^{-1}\omega_u^n$ for every $u\in\HH_\omega$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If all the forms $\omega_u$ are Gauduchon, the mass derivative \eqref{eq:mass-first-variation} vanishes at every point of $\HH_\omega$ and in every direction. Since $\HH_\omega$ is convex, integration along the segment from $0$ to $u$ gives the mass identity. Conversely, \cref{prop:mass-variation} shows that constant mass forces $\ddc(\omega_u^{n-1})=0$ for every $u\in\HH_\omega$. In that case the normalized multiplier is the constant $M^{-1}$, by \cref{thm:gauduchon}.
\end{proof}

By \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}, this persistence is equivalent to $\ddc(\omega^j)=0$ for all integers $1\leq j\leq n-1$. The following example distinguishes it from the single Gauduchon identity in dimension three.

\begin{example}\label{ex:gauduchon-persistence-torus}
Let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$ and $X=E^3$. For $1\leq j\leq3$, write $z_j=x_j+\mathrm i y_j$ for the coordinates on the covering space, and let $\eta_j$ be the form on $X$ induced by $(\mathrm i/2)\mathrm dz_j\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_j$. The periodic functions
\[
 b=\exp(\cos(2\pi x_1)),\qquad c=b^{-1}
\]
define a Hermitian form
\[
 \omega=\eta_1+b\eta_2+c\eta_3.
\]
Since $bc=1$,
\[
 \omega^2=2b\eta_1\wedge\eta_2
          +2c\eta_1\wedge\eta_3+2\eta_2\wedge\eta_3.
\]
The functions $b$ and $c$ depend only on $x_1$, so $\ddc b$ and $\ddc c$ are multiples of $\eta_1$, and $\ddc(\omega^2)=0$. Thus $\omega$ is Gauduchon.

Put $v=\cos(2\pi x_2)$ and $h=-\pi\cos(2\pi x_2)$, so that $\ddc v=h\eta_2$. For a real $s$ with $0<|s|<1/(\pi\mathrm e)$, the form
\[
 \omega_{sv}=\eta_1+(b+sh)\eta_2+c\eta_3
\]
is positive, since $b\geq\mathrm e^{-1}$ and $|h|\leq\pi$. However,
\[
 \ddc(\omega_{sv}^2)
 =2s\ddc\omega\wedge\ddc v
 =2sh\ddc c\wedge\eta_2\wedge\eta_3\ne0.
\]
Indeed, $\ddc c=(c''/(4\pi))\eta_1$, where the prime denotes differentiation in $x_1$, and $c''(0)=4\pi^2\mathrm e^{-1}$, while $h=-\pi$ at $x_2=0$. Thus a Gauduchon form can have arbitrarily small positive potential perturbations that are not Gauduchon.
\end{example}

When persistence holds, the one-form $v\mapsto\int_Xv\,\mathrm d\mu_u$ on $\HH_\omega$ is the Monge--Amp\`ere one-form of \cref{chap:energy} divided by $M$. By \cref{thm:raw-primitive}, it therefore has a primitive exactly when $\omega$ is closed.

\section{Limits of adjoint measures}
\label{sec:adjoint-measure-limits}

When every positive potential perturbation of $\omega$ is Gauduchon, the adjoint probability is the normalized Monge--Amp\`ere measure. Mass invariance and the continuity of bounded products then extend this description to bounded potentials.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-adjoint-persistence}
Assume that $\omega_v$ is Gauduchon for every $v\in\HH_\omega$. For every $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^\infty(X)$, the measure
\[
 \mu_u:=M^{-1}(\omega+\ddc u)^n
\]
is a probability measure, which agrees with the preceding definition when $u\in\HH_\omega$. If $u_j,u\in\PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^\infty(X)$ are uniformly bounded and $u_j\to u$ in capacity, then $\mu_{u_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_u$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:gauduchon-potential-persistence,thm:mass-invariance-characterization}, the positive bounded product $(\omega+\ddc u)^n$ has mass $M$, and for $u\in\HH_\omega$ its normalization is the smooth adjoint measure by \cref{prop:gauduchon-potential-persistence}. The continuity of bounded products under convergence in capacity, in the sense of \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity}, gives weak convergence of the products, and division by $M$ preserves it.
\end{proof}

The proposition applies in particular to Gauduchon surfaces. For a general Hermitian background, passing from smooth $u_j\in\HH_\omega$ to a limit $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ requires control of the multipliers $\rho_{u_j}$ as well as of the potentials. The supporting probabilities of \cref{chap:normalization} recover $\mu_u$ in the smooth case and give a conditional approximation theorem. We do not know whether, in general, the limit is independent of the approximating sequence, or whether a weak linearization satisfies an adjoint identity.
