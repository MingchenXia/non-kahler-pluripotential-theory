The regularized envelope does not change if the obstacle is imposed only outside a pluripolar subset of $K$. Within the growth class this can be seen directly: a function in $\mathcal L_\delta$ with poles on the exceptional set repairs each competitor.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:affine-logarithmic-pole-barrier}
For every pluripolar set $E\subseteq\mathbb C^n$ and every $\delta>0$, there is $b\in\mathcal L_\delta$ with $b=-\infty$ on $E$. Given a non-pluripolar compact set $K$, it can be chosen with $b\leq0$ on $K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $E_j=E\cap\overline B(0,j)$. Each $E_j$ is pluripolar as a subset of $\mathbb P^n$: it is locally pluripolar near every affine point, and a neighborhood of the hyperplane at infinity does not meet this bounded set. By Josefson's theorem on compact manifolds, \cite[Theorem~1.1.5]{SGPT}, there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^n$, not identically $-\infty$, that equals $-\infty$ on $E_j$. Rescaling it so that it becomes $\theta_1$-plurisubharmonic and subtracting its supremum gives
\[
 p_j\in\PSH(\mathbb P^n,\theta_1),\qquad
 p_j\leq0,\qquad p_j|_{E_j}=-\infty.
\]
The $L^1$ norms of these normalized potentials are uniformly bounded by \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}. The partial sums
\[
 s_N=\sum_{j=1}^N2^{-j}p_j
\]
decrease, have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms, and remain $\theta_1$-plurisubharmonic because $\theta_1\geq0$ and the coefficients sum to at most one. By \cref{cor:decreasing-limit}, their limit $p$ belongs to $\PSH(\mathbb P^n,\theta_1)$. Every point of $E$ is a pole of one of the summands, hence $p=-\infty$ on $E$. By \cref{lem:affine-projective-growth},
\[
 b=\delta(p+\chi/2)\in\mathcal L_\delta
\]
has the required properties. Its supremum on $K$ is finite, since $b$ is bounded above on the compact set $K$ and is not identically $-\infty$ on $K$, which is non-pluripolar. Subtracting this supremum gives $b\leq0$ on $K$.
\end{proof}
\index{Pluripolar set!logarithmic pole barrier}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:affine-polar-obstacle-invariance}
The envelope $F_\delta$ is unchanged if $u|_K\leq\psi$ is imposed only quasi-everywhere, or only on $K\setminus E$ for any pluripolar $E\subseteq K$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $u\in\mathcal L_\delta$ satisfy $u\leq\psi$ on $K\setminus E$, where $E$ is pluripolar. Choose $b$ as in the lemma, with $b=-\infty$ on $E$ and $b\leq0$ on $K$, and put $m=\inf_K\psi$. For $\epsilon>0$ define
\[
 u_\epsilon=\frac{u+\epsilon b+\epsilon m}{1+\epsilon}.
\]
This function belongs to $\mathcal L_\delta$. On $K\setminus E$, the inequalities $u\leq\psi$, $b\leq0$ and $m\leq\psi$ give $u_\epsilon\leq\psi$, and on $E$ the pole of $b$ gives $u_\epsilon=-\infty$. Thus $u_\epsilon$ is a competitor for $V_\delta$, so $u_\epsilon\leq F_\delta$; moreover, $u_\epsilon\to u$ almost everywhere as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. By \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, $u\leq F_\delta$ everywhere. Taking the supremum over $u$ and regularizing proves the asserted equalities.

Alternatively, apply \cref{prop:compact-obstacle-polar-invariance} on $\mathbb P^n$ with the big background $\theta_\delta$ and the bounded obstacle $\psi-\delta\chi/2$, and then use \eqref{eq:affine-projective-envelope}.
\end{proof}

These equalities concern regularized envelopes. The raw envelope $V_\delta$ is still defined with the obstacle imposed at every point of $K$.
