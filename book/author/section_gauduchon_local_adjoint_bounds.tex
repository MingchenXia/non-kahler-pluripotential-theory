\subsection{Uniform bounds near a smooth density}
\label{sec:gaud-local-adjoint-bounds}

The endpoint estimate can fail for the family of all tests while the maximizing tests for a family of densities remain uniformly controlled. For smooth densities close in $C^{2,\alpha}$ to the normalized volume form $\omega^n/\int_X\omega^n$ of the background, a fixed-point argument keeps the solution forms close to $\omega$ and their Gauduchon multipliers close to that of $\omega$. This gives uniform bounds for both the determinant density of the maximizing current and the density of the supporting probability.

In this subsection we assume $n\geq3$ and fix $0<\alpha<1$. All H\"older norms are defined using a fixed finite atlas and fixed smooth connections. Put
\[
 H=\frac{\omega^n}{\mathrm{d}V},\qquad
 V=\int_X\omega^n,\qquad F_*=\log(H/V).
\]
Thus $\mathrm{e}^{F_*}\mathrm{d}V=\omega^n/V$ is a probability measure. By \cref{thm:gauduchon}, there is a unique smooth positive function $\chi_0$, the normalized Gauduchon multiplier of $\omega$, such that
\[
 \ddc(\chi_0\omega^{n-1})=0,\qquad
 \int_X\chi_0\omega^n=1.
\]
Write $m=\min_X\chi_0$, $a=\max_X\chi_0$ and $C_0=\|\chi_0\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}$.

We first specify the constants that determine the size of the neighborhood. For a smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\Omega$, set
\[
 L_\omega v=\frac{n\ddc v\wedge\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^n},
 \qquad A_\Omega\eta=\frac{\ddc(\eta\Omega^{n-1})}{\mathrm{d}V}.
\]
The compatibility argument of \cref{prop:adjoint-gauduchon,prop:smooth-dependence} shows that
\[
 \mathcal L(v,c)=L_\omega v-c,
 \qquad \int_Xv\,\mathrm{d}V=0,
\]
is an isomorphism from the space of pairs $(v,c)\in C^{4,\alpha}\times\R$ with this normalization onto $C^{2,\alpha}$. Let $K\geq1$ bound its inverse for the norm $\|v\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}+|c|$. The formal adjoint of $A_\omega$ with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$ is $(H/n)L_\omega$, whose kernel consists of the constants and whose index is zero. Consequently $A_\omega$ has kernel $\R\chi_0$ and range
\[
 Z_0=\left\{g\in C^{0,\alpha}:\int_Xg\,\mathrm{d}V=0\right\}.
\]
Its restriction to
\[
 E_\chi=\left\{\eta\in C^{2,\alpha}:\int_X\eta\omega^n=0\right\}
\]
has a bounded inverse $\mathcal R\colon Z_0\to E_\chi$. Fix $G\geq1$ bounding this inverse.

We normalize the H\"older norms of forms and of endomorphism fields so that they dominate pointwise operator norms and are submultiplicative under composition of endomorphisms; the subscript $\mathrm{alg}$ below refers to this norm on endomorphism fields. Choose $D\geq1$ so that
\[
 \|\ddc v\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq D\|v\|_{C^{4,\alpha}},\qquad
 \|\omega^{-1}\ddc v\|_{C^{2,\alpha},\mathrm{alg}}
                      \leq D\|v\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}.
\]
Let $C_{\mathrm{tr}}\geq1$ be a bound for the trace map on endomorphism fields, and put $Q=2C_{\mathrm{tr}}D^2$. Expanding $\Omega^{n-1}-\omega^{n-1}$ gives a constant $C_A\geq1$ such that
\[
 \|A_\Omega-A_\omega\|_{C^{2,\alpha}\to C^{0,\alpha}}
       \leq C_A\|\Omega-\omega\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}
 \quad\text{if }\|\Omega-\omega\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq1.
\]
Only two derivatives of $\Omega$ enter this polynomial estimate. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-local-adjoint-radius}
 r=\min\left\{\frac1{2D},\frac1{4KQ},
                   \frac{m}{4GC_ADC_0}\right\},
 \qquad \varepsilon=\frac r{2K}.
\end{equation}
These constants depend only on $\omega$, $\mathrm{d}V$, $\alpha$ and the chosen norms.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gaud-local-controlled-solution}
Let $F$ be smooth with $\|F-F_*\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq\varepsilon$. The solution form $\Omega_F=\omega+\ddc u_F$ of $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u_F)=\mathrm{e}^{B_0(F)+F}\mathrm{d}V$ and its Gauduchon multiplier $\chi_F$, normalized as below, satisfy
\begin{gather*}
 \tfrac12\omega\leq\Omega_F\leq\tfrac32\omega,
 \qquad \frac m2\leq\chi_F\leq\frac{3a}2,\\
 \ddc(\chi_F\Omega_F^{n-1})=0,
 \qquad \int_X\chi_F\omega^n=1.
\end{gather*}
The potential can be normalized by $\int_Xu_F\,\mathrm{d}V=0$ with
$\|u_F\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}+|B_0(F)-\log V|\leq r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\|v\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}\leq1/(2D)$ put
\[
 N(v)=\log\det(I+\omega^{-1}\ddc v)-L_\omega v.
\]
The logarithm series starts at degree two. If the endomorphism norms of $\omega^{-1}\ddc v$ and $\omega^{-1}\ddc w$ are at most $\rho\leq1/2$, telescoping their powers bounds the difference series by $C_{\mathrm{tr}}\rho/(1-\rho)$ times the endomorphism difference. Hence
\begin{align*}
 \|N(v)\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}&\leq Q\|v\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}^2,\\
 \|N(v)-N(w)\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}
 &\leq Q(\|v\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}+\|w\|_{C^{4,\alpha}})
                                      \|v-w\|_{C^{4,\alpha}}.
\end{align*}
On the closed radius-$r$ ball of normalized pairs $(v,c)$, the map
\[
 (v,c)\longmapsto\mathcal L^{-1}(F-F_*-N(v))
\]
has image norm at most $K\varepsilon+KQr^2\leq3r/4$ and Lipschitz constant at most $2KQr\leq1/2$. Its fixed point $(u,c)$ satisfies
\[
 \log\frac{(\omega+\ddc u)^n}{\omega^n}-c=F-F_*.
\]
The estimate $Dr\leq1/2$ gives the form comparison. The scalar is $b=\log V+c$. Differentiating the local equation gives a uniformly elliptic equation for each first derivative of $u$, with coefficients initially in $C^{2,\alpha}$. Interior Schauder estimates improve the regularity one order at a time, so $u$ is smooth. Uniqueness in \cref{thm:tw-smooth} identifies it with $u_F$ and gives $b=B_0(F)$. No uniform higher-derivative bounds on $F$ enter the estimates above.

Put $\Delta A=A_{\Omega_F}-A_\omega$. Stokes' theorem makes its image lie in $Z_0$. On the complete space $E_\chi$, the affine map
\[
 \eta\longmapsto-\mathcal R\Delta A(\chi_0+\eta)
\]
has Lipschitz constant
$\kappa=GC_ADr\leq m/(4C_0)\leq1/4$. Its fixed point obeys
\[
 \|\eta\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}
       \leq\frac{\kappa C_0}{1-\kappa}
       \leq2\kappa C_0\leq m/2.
\]
Since $A_\omega\chi_0=0$ and $A_\omega\mathcal R$ is the identity on $Z_0$, the function $\chi_F=\chi_0+\eta$ satisfies $A_{\Omega_F}\chi_F=0$, and it has the stated normalization and bounds. Elliptic regularity makes it smooth. Every Gauduchon multiplier of $\Omega_F$ with the same normalization satisfies the same affine fixed-point equation, so this multiplier is the one given by \cref{thm:gauduchon}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gaud-local-adjoint-endpoint}\index{Adjoint measure!local uniform density bounds}\index{Gauduchon metric!local determinant bounds}
For every smooth $F$ in the ball of \cref{lem:gaud-local-controlled-solution}, let $T_F$ be the maximizing current of \cref{thm:weak-gaud-compact-duality} and $\nu_F$ the supporting probability of $B_0$ at $F$; both are unique. With
\[
 H_{\max}=\max_XH,\qquad
 C_\nu=\frac{3^{n+1}}4aH_{\max},\qquad
 C_k=\frac{3^n}2aH_{\max}^{(n-1)/n},
\]
their densities satisfy
\[
 \nu_F=q_F\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad
 \|q_F\|_{L^\infty}\leq C_\nu,\qquad
 \|k_{T_F}\|_{L^\infty}\leq C_k.
\]
In particular, for $p=n/(n-1)$,
\[
 \|q_F\|_{L^p}\leq C_\nu^{1/n},\qquad
 \|k_{T_F}\|_{L^p}\leq C_k.
\]
The constants do not involve $v_-(\omega)$; in particular, these bounds hold also when $v_-(\omega)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $\Omega=\Omega_F$, $\chi=\chi_F$ and
\[
 Z=\int_X\chi\omega\wedge\Omega^{n-1}.
\]
The form comparison and $\int_X\chi\omega^n=1$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-local-factor-normalization}
 2^{-(n-1)}\leq Z\leq(3/2)^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
Since $\ddc(\chi\Omega^{n-1})=0$, Stokes' theorem also gives $Z=\int_X\chi\Omega^n$. The form $T=(\chi/Z)\Omega^{n-1}$ is normalized and is a constant multiple of the $(n-1)$st power of the Gauduchon metric conformal to $\Omega$. By \cref{thm:weak-gaud-compact-duality}, it is the unique maximizing current, so $T=T_F$. The equality case in the proof of that theorem gives $\nu_F=\Omega\wedge T_F$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-local-adjoint-densities}
 \begin{aligned}
 T_F&=\frac\chi Z\Omega^{n-1},\\
 \nu_F&=\Omega\wedge T_F=\frac\chi Z\Omega^n,\\
 q_F&=\frac\chi Z\frac{\Omega^n}{\mathrm{d}V},\\
 k_{T_F}&=\frac\chi Z
             \left(\frac{\Omega^n}{\mathrm{d}V}\right)^{(n-1)/n}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last identity uses the positive root $\gamma=(\chi/Z)^{1/(n-1)}\Omega$, for which $T_F=\gamma^{n-1}$. Uniqueness of the supporting probability follows from \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints}.

Now $\chi/Z\leq(3a/2)2^{n-1}$ and $\Omega^n/\mathrm{d}V\leq(3/2)^nH_{\max}$, and multiplying gives exactly $q_F\leq C_\nu$ and $k_{T_F}\leq C_k$. Since $\int_Xq_F\,\mathrm{d}V=1$, we have $\int_Xq_F^p\,\mathrm{d}V\leq C_\nu^{p-1}$, which gives the sharper $L^p$ estimate for $q_F$. The $L^p$ estimate for $k_{T_F}$ follows from $\int_X\mathrm{d}V=1$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gaud-local-density-family}
The ball of \cref{lem:gaud-local-controlled-solution} contains smooth injectively parametrized families, of arbitrarily large finite dimension, of data $F$ normalized by $\int_X\mathrm{e}^F\mathrm{d}V=1$. It also contains a smooth path of such data whose maximizing currents are pairwise distinct.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a small smooth function $f$, put
\[
 F(f)=F_*+f-\log\int_X\mathrm{e}^{F_*+f}\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
If $F(f)=F(g)$, then $f-g$ is constant, so the map $f\mapsto F(f)$ is injective on functions of zero $\mathrm{d}V$-mean. By its continuity in $C^{2,\alpha}$, it maps a sufficiently small neighborhood of zero in any fixed finite-dimensional space of such smooth functions into the ball, and these spaces can have arbitrary dimension.

For the path, choose a smooth real $\psi$ of zero $\mathrm{d}V$-mean whose complex Hessian at some point $x_0$ has, in an $\omega$-unitary frame, a matrix $Q_0$ that is not a multiple of the identity. A cutoff of $|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2$ in local coordinates, minus its mean, is such a function. For small real $t$ put
\[
 \Omega_t=\omega+t\ddc\psi,\qquad
 V_t=\int_X\Omega_t^n,\qquad
 F_t=\log\frac{\Omega_t^n}{V_t\,\mathrm{d}V}.
\]
These normalized data tend to $F_*$ in $C^{2,\alpha}$ as $t\to0$, and by uniqueness in \cref{thm:tw-smooth} their solution forms are the forms $\Omega_t$. If $F_s=F_t$, then $\log(\Omega_s^n/\Omega_t^n)$ is the constant $\log(V_s/V_t)$. The signs of the complex Hessian of $(s-t)\psi$ at a maximum and at a minimum force this constant to be zero. Integrating the derivative of $\log\det$ along the segment between $\Omega_t$ and $\Omega_s$ then gives a uniformly elliptic equation for $(s-t)\psi$ without zeroth-order term, and the maximum principle forces $(s-t)\psi$ to be constant. Since $\psi$ is not constant, $s=t$.

If the maximizing currents for $F_s$ and $F_t$ agree, \eqref{eq:gaud-local-adjoint-densities} makes $\Omega_s^{n-1}$ and $\Omega_t^{n-1}$ proportional at $x_0$. Positive cofactor matrices determine positive forms up to scale, so $I+sQ_0$ and $I+tQ_0$ are proportional. Since $I$ and $Q_0$ are linearly independent, comparing coefficients gives $s=t$.
\end{proof}

The small $C^{2,\alpha}$ neighborhood gives a condition directly on the prescribed density. The next estimate instead assumes uniform bounds for the solution forms and covers a larger smooth family without derivative bounds on its densities. Whether such uniform bounds hold for the maximizers of arbitrary bounded families of continuous densities remains open when $v_-(\omega)=0$. The concentrating sequences of \cref{prop:gaud-determinant-concentration} consist of near-maximizers, so these estimates do not apply to them.
