\section{Pullback and descent}
\label{sec:psh-descent}

Pullback by a holomorphic map preserves the inequality $\theta+\ddc u\geq0$. Conversely, on a submersion with compact connected fibers, a potential that is positive with respect to a pulled-back background, which has no vertical component, must be constant on the fibers and therefore descends. Potentials also descend under modifications, and there a Jacobian factor accounts for the change in multiplier-ideal integrability.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\subsection{Holomorphic disks}

A smooth background form need not have local $\ddc$-potentials, but its pullback to a disk does, since a real $(1,1)$-form on a disk is closed. Testing on holomorphic disks therefore characterizes the exact lower curvature bound in a way that is preserved by holomorphic maps.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-disk-criterion}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on a complex manifold $M$, and let $u$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on $M$. Then $\theta+\ddc u\geq0$ if and only if the following condition holds: for every holomorphic map $f\colon\Delta\to M$ from the unit disk such that $u\circ f$ is not identically $-\infty$, and for every smooth function $a$ on an open subset of $\Delta$ with $\ddc a=f^*\theta$, the function $u\circ f+a$ is subharmonic there.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\theta+\ddc u\geq0$. On a coordinate ball, convolve $u$ and $\theta$ with a smooth nonnegative radial kernel. The resulting smooth functions $u_\epsilon$ satisfy
\[
 \ddc u_\epsilon\geq-\theta_\epsilon,
 \qquad\theta_\epsilon\longrightarrow\theta\quad\text{smoothly}.
\]
Write $u=p-r$ locally, with $p$ plurisubharmonic and $r$ smooth. The convolutions $p_\epsilon$ decrease to $p$. On a smaller disk on which $u\circ f$ is not identically $-\infty$, $p_\epsilon\circ f$ therefore converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ to $p\circ f$, while $r_\epsilon\circ f$ converges smoothly. Pulling back the smooth inequality and passing to the limit in the sense of distributions gives $\ddc(u\circ f)+f^*\theta\geq0$. Since $u\circ f=p\circ f-r\circ f$ is the sum of a subharmonic and a smooth function, this distributional inequality makes $u\circ f+a$ subharmonic.

Conversely, test on affine complex lines in a coordinate ball. For a fixed nonzero constant direction $\xi$, local integrability of $u$ implies that the restriction of $u$ to almost every line parallel to $\xi$ is not identically $-\infty$. Fubini's theorem, applied to nonnegative smooth test functions, then gives positivity of the scalar distribution obtained by evaluating the Hermitian coefficient matrix of $\theta+\ddc u$ on $\xi$. Positivity for a countable dense set of constant directions proves positivity of the Hermitian matrix of coefficient measures, which is the current inequality.
\end{proof}

Consequently, holomorphic pullback preserves the inequality $\theta+\ddc u\geq0$ wherever the pulled-back function is not identically $-\infty$: apply the disk criterion to the compositions with the given map. We also use descent of subharmonicity under the finite map $\rho(t)=t^k$, $k\geq1$, of the disk. If $h\circ\rho$ is subharmonic, local inverse branches of $\rho$ show that $h$ is subharmonic off zero. Moreover, $h$ is bounded above near zero, and its value at zero is its upper limit there, since
\[
 h(0)=(h\circ\rho)(0)
      =\limsup_{t\to0,\ t\ne0}h(t^k)
      =\limsup_{z\to0,\ z\ne0}h(z).
\]
By the removable-singularity theorem, $h$ is subharmonic at zero as well.

\subsection{Compact fibers}

The connected components of the fibers of a submersion determine a finite covering of the base, and potentials first descend to this covering.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:submersion-component-cover}\index{Descent!cover of fiber components}\index{Submersion!cover of fiber components}
Let $p\colon M\to Y$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion between connected compact complex manifolds. There is a factorization
\[
 M\xrightarrow{\pi}Y'\xrightarrow{r}Y,\qquad p=r\circ\pi,
\]
where $Y'$ is a connected compact complex manifold, $r$ is a finite unramified holomorphic covering and $\pi$ is a surjective holomorphic submersion with connected fibers. The points of $r^{-1}(y)$ correspond to the connected components of $p^{-1}(y)$, which are the fibers of $\pi$ over those points.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a Hermitian metric on $M$. The orthogonal complement of $\ker\mathrm dp$ gives a smooth horizontal lift of tangent vectors on $Y$. Around $y_0\in Y$ choose a coordinate ball $B$ centered at $y_0$, with compact closure inside a larger coordinate ball. For $y\in B$, lift the straight segment from $y_0$ to $y$. The ordinary differential equation for horizontal transport has a solution along the whole segment, since its projection remains in the compact closure of $B$, whose inverse image is compact. Smooth dependence on the starting point and on $y$, together with transport along the reversed segment, gives a smooth trivialization
\[
 p^{-1}(B)\simeq B\times p^{-1}(y_0)
\]
over $B$.

The compact manifold $p^{-1}(y_0)$ has finitely many connected components. The trivialization partitions $p^{-1}(B)$ into finitely many open and closed pieces, each of which meets every fiber in one connected component. On overlaps, two such local partitions identify the same fiber components, and their labels differ by locally constant permutations. Glue one copy of each base ball for every component, using these identifications. Equivalently, take the quotient of $M$ that identifies points in the same connected component of a fiber. The local partitions make this quotient a Hausdorff covering space: distinct points over one base point lie on different sheets, and points over different base points are separated by their images in $Y$.

Call the quotient $Y'$ and its projection $r$. Coordinates of $Y$ on each sheet give holomorphic charts with holomorphic transition maps, so $r$ is a local biholomorphism. It is a finite covering, and $Y'$ is compact because a finite covering of a compact space is compact. Locally, the quotient map $\pi$ is $p$ restricted to one of the open pieces, so it is a holomorphic submersion with the asserted connected fibers. Surjectivity of $\pi$ and connectedness of $M$ make $Y'$ connected.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:psh-descent-submersion}\index{Descent!submersion}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion between connected compact complex manifolds, with connected fibers. Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Pullback gives a bijection
\[
 \PSH(X,\theta)\longrightarrow\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta).
\]
Every member on the right is constant on each fiber, with the value $-\infty$ allowed on an entire fiber.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On a coordinate ball $V\subset X$, choose a smooth function $\rho$ with $\ddc\rho\geq\theta$. If $u\in\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$, then $u+\rho\circ\pi$ is plurisubharmonic on $\pi^{-1}(V)$. Its restriction to a fiber is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$, and since the fiber is compact and connected, the maximum principle shows that it is constant in either case. As $\rho\circ\pi$ is constant on fibers, $u=v\circ\pi$ for a uniquely defined function $v$ on $X$.

A holomorphic submersion admits local holomorphic sections. Composing $u+\rho\circ\pi$ with such a section shows that $v+\rho$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$ on a smaller ball. The latter alternative would make $u=-\infty$ on a nonempty open set, which is impossible for a quasi-plurisubharmonic function. Hence $v$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic. Distributional differentiation in local product coordinates gives
\[
 \pi^*(\theta+\ddc v)=\pi^*\theta+\ddc u\geq0.
\]
Positivity of a pulled-back current of this form implies positivity on the base: test in product coordinates against products with a nonnegative compactly supported volume form of integral one in the fiber variables. It follows that $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.

Conversely, by \cref{lem:smooth-disk-criterion}, the pullback of a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function by $\pi$ is $\pi^*\theta$-plurisubharmonic; in local product coordinates it is clearly not identically $-\infty$. The pullback map is injective because $\pi$ is surjective.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Modifications}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:psh-descent-modification}\index{Descent!modification}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Pullback gives a bijection
\[
 \PSH(X,\theta)\longrightarrow\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $S\subset X$ be a proper analytic subset over whose complement $\pi$ is an isomorphism, and put $D=\pi^{-1}(S)$. By \cref{lem:smooth-disk-criterion}, pullback preserves the curvature inequality, and the pullback of a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function is not identically $-\infty$, since $\pi$ is an isomorphism off $D$. The pullback map is injective because $\pi$ is surjective.

To prove surjectivity, let $b\in\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$, and define $u=b\circ\pi^{-1}$ off $S$. On a coordinate neighborhood in $X$, choose a smooth function $\rho$ with $\ddc\rho\geq\theta$. Then $b+\rho\circ\pi$ is plurisubharmonic on the inverse image of this neighborhood. By properness, its local upper bounds give a local upper bound for $u+\rho$ near $S$, so the removable-singularity theorem extends $u+\rho$ plurisubharmonically across $S$. The resulting quasi-plurisubharmonic extensions of $u$ agree almost everywhere on overlaps, hence everywhere, and glue to a function on $X$.

The current $\theta+\ddc u$ is positive off $S$. Locally it is the sum of the positive current $\ddc(u+\rho)$ and the smooth form $\theta-\ddc\rho$. The smooth form gives $S$ zero mass, so the restriction of $\theta+\ddc u$ to $S$ is positive as well. Thus $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. The plurisubharmonic functions $b+\rho\circ\pi$ and $(u+\rho)\circ\pi$ agree off $D$, hence almost everywhere, hence everywhere. Therefore $b=u\circ\pi$.
\end{proof}

Pullback by a modification preserves $L^1$ convergence within a family with a common lower curvature bound. Compactness controls the pullbacks near the exceptional locus, where a direct change of variables would involve a vanishing Jacobian.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:modification-lone-pullback}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a modification between connected complex manifolds, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. If $v_j\to v$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)$ and all $v_j,v$ belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$, then
\[
 v_j\circ\pi\longrightarrow v\circ\pi
       \quad\hbox{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(Y).
\]
In particular, on compact manifolds this is convergence in $L^1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:qpsh-hartogs}, the functions $v_j$ are uniformly bounded above on relatively compact subsets of $X$. The pullbacks are therefore locally uniformly bounded above and have the common smooth lower curvature bound $-\pi^*\theta$. After a smooth local correction, the compactness theorem for plurisubharmonic functions shows that every subsequence has a further subsequence that either converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ or tends locally uniformly to $-\infty$ on each connected coordinate region in $Y$.

Every such region meets the complement of the exceptional locus. There $\pi$ is a biholomorphism, and change of variables gives $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ convergence to $v\circ\pi$, which excludes convergence to $-\infty$. Each subsequential limit agrees with $v\circ\pi$ off the exceptional locus, hence almost everywhere, since this analytic set has smooth volume zero. Thus the limit is unique, and applying this argument on a countable cover by coordinate regions proves convergence of the whole sequence in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(Y)$.
\end{proof}

Convergence in capacity is also preserved by modifications. The comparison takes place away from small neighborhoods of the exceptional sets, so no uniform comparison of capacities on the whole manifolds is needed.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:modification-capacity-pullback}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a modification between connected compact complex manifolds, equipped with Hermitian forms $\omega_Y,\omega_X$. Write $\operatorname{Cap}_Y=\operatorname{Cap}_{\omega_Y}$ and $\operatorname{Cap}_X=\operatorname{Cap}_{\omega_X}$ for the Hermitian capacities of \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity}. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $v_j,v$ on $X$,
\[
 v_j\longrightarrow v\text{ in capacity on }X
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_j\circ\pi\longrightarrow v\circ\pi\text{ in capacity on }Y.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a proper analytic subset $S\subset X$ such that $\pi$ is an isomorphism outside $D=\pi^{-1}(S)$. Both $S$ and $D$ have capacity zero. For $\epsilon>0$, choose an open neighborhood $V$ of $D$ of capacity less than $\epsilon$. By properness, there is a neighborhood $U_0$ of $S$ with $\pi^{-1}(U_0)\subset V$; shrink it to an open set $U$ with $\operatorname{Cap}_X(U)<\epsilon$. The inverse of $\pi$ is biholomorphic near the compact set $X\setminus U$. A finite cover by nested coordinate charts and the comparison of local and global capacities in \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity} give a constant $C_\epsilon$ such that, for every Borel set $E\subset X$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Cap}_Y(\pi^{-1}E)&\leq\epsilon+C_\epsilon\operatorname{Cap}_X(E),\\
 \operatorname{Cap}_X(E)&\leq\epsilon+C_\epsilon\operatorname{Cap}_Y(\pi^{-1}E).
 \end{split}
\]
Apply these inequalities to $E=\{|v_j-v|>\delta\}$ for each fixed $\delta>0$, omitting the pluripolar pole sets, and let first $j\to\infty$ and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Multiplier ideals under modifications}
\label{sec:psh-birational-ideals}

Throughout this subsection, $\pi\colon Y\to X$ is a modification of connected compact complex manifolds of dimension $n$. There is an analytic subset $S\subset X$ of codimension at least two such that $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $X\setminus S$. Let $K_X$ and $K_Y$ be the canonical line bundles. Set $D=\pi^{-1}(S)$ and
\[
 R_\pi=K_Y\otimes\pi^*K_X^{-1}.
\]
The determinant of the differential of $\pi$ is a canonical holomorphic section $J_\pi$ of $R_\pi$; in local coordinates its coefficient is the holomorphic Jacobian determinant $\operatorname{Jac}\pi$, and its zeros are contained in $D$. These standard facts about modifications of complex manifolds include the case $n=1$, in which every modification is an isomorphism.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:adjoint-ideal-transform}\index{Multiplier ideal!birational transform}
Let $a$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on $X$. Pullback followed by multiplication with $J_\pi$ induces an isomorphism
\[
 \pi_*\bigl(R_\pi\otimes\mathcal I(a\circ\pi)\bigr)
       =\mathcal I(a).
\]
More precisely, for every holomorphic line bundle $M$ on $X$, the map
\[
 s\longmapsto J_\pi\,\pi^*s
\]
is an isomorphism
\[
 H^0\bigl(X,M\otimes\mathcal I(a)\bigr)
 \simeq H^0\bigl(Y,\pi^*M\otimes R_\pi
                         \otimes\mathcal I(a\circ\pi)\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The assertion is local on $X$. In coordinates and local frames, the map sends a holomorphic coefficient $f$ to $(f\circ\pi)\operatorname{Jac}\pi$. By the change-of-variables formula, local integrability of
\[
 |f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-a}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 |f\circ\pi|^2|\operatorname{Jac}\pi|^2\mathrm{e}^{-a\circ\pi}
\]
are equivalent. For the implication from integrability on $Y$ to integrability near a point of $X$, take a smaller relatively compact neighborhood of the point; by properness, its inverse image has compact closure, so finitely many charts on which the integrability holds suffice. Smooth positive coordinate densities are comparable on these charts, and analytic exceptional sets have smooth volume zero, so they do not change the integrals.

It remains to show that every section on $Y$ is of the form $J_\pi\pi^*s$. Divide it by $J_\pi$ off $D$ and use the inverse of $\pi$ there; this gives a holomorphic section of $M$ off $S$. Since $S$ has codimension at least two, Hartogs' extension theorem gives a holomorphic section $s$ on $X$. Its image $J_\pi\pi^*s$ agrees with the given section off $D$, hence everywhere by the identity theorem. The integrability equivalence then places $s$ in the required multiplier ideal. The same argument on each open set proves the sheaf identity as well as the global identification.
\end{proof}

Note that the lemma does not identify $\pi^*\mathcal I(a)$ with $\mathcal I(a\circ\pi)$: the Jacobian is part of the holomorphic coefficient on $Y$ and cannot be removed from the integrability test.

We also need the invariance of the ideal order $\preceq_{\mathcal I}$ of \cref{sec:psh-singularity-types}, which involves all positive weights. The proof is local, so that we never have to extend a modification of a small open subset of $Y$ to a global one.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:birational-ideal-order}
For quasi-plurisubharmonic $a,b$ on $X$,
\[
 a\preceq_{\mathcal I}b
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a\circ\pi\preceq_{\mathcal I}b\circ\pi.
\]
The assertion is local on $X$ and also holds for a proper bimeromorphic map between noncompact smooth complex manifolds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The reverse implication follows at every positive weight from the sheaf identity in \cref{lem:adjoint-ideal-transform}: tensor the inclusion of ideals on $Y$ with $R_\pi$ and apply the left-exact direct image functor. The proof of that sheaf identity is local on the target, since properness makes each fiber compact and gives the required finite cover by integrability charts; compactness of the manifolds themselves is not needed.

For the forward implication, fix a coordinate neighborhood $W\subset Y$, a local modification $\sigma\colon Z\to W$ with smooth source, and a prime divisor $E$ on $Z$. After shrinking $W$ around the point under consideration, its image is contained in a coordinate neighborhood $U\subset X$. Put $F=\pi\circ\sigma$ and
\[
 A_F(E)=1+\operatorname{ord}_E\operatorname{Jac}F.
\]
The map $F$ is holomorphic, and it is injective and biholomorphic off a proper analytic subset; it need not be a proper modification of $U$.

Let $m$ be a positive integer, and let $\nu_E$ and $\operatorname{ord}_E$ be the generic Lelong number and the generic vanishing order along $E$, as in \cref{sec:psh-divisorial-estimates}. The proof of \cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error} applies to the map $F$ on relatively compact coordinate patches and gives
\[
 \max\left\{\nu_E(a\circ F)-\frac{A_F(E)}m,0\right\}
 \leq \frac1m\operatorname{ord}_E\bigl(\mathcal I(ma)\mathcal O_Z\bigr)
 \leq\nu_E(a\circ F).
\]
In more detail, the upper inequality follows by pulling back the local Bergman lower bound on $U$. For the lower one, a germ $f\in\mathcal I(ma)$ pulls back to a coefficient $(f\circ F)\operatorname{Jac}F$ that is square integrable against $\mathrm e^{-ma\circ F}$, because on a relatively compact patch of $Z$ the change-of-variables integral is bounded by the integral over the image of the patch. Generic divisorial removal and the one-variable integral then give
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_E(f\circ F)+A_F(E)>m\nu_E(a\circ F).
\]
This estimate only uses the injectivity of $F$ off the exceptional set and the compactness of the patch, not properness of $F$ over $U$. The same estimates hold for $b$.

The hypothesis gives $\mathcal I(ma)\subseteq\mathcal I(mb)$ for every $m>0$, so the orders of the pulled-back ideals satisfy the reverse inequality. Letting $m\to\infty$ in the two estimates proves
\[
 \nu_E(a\circ F)\geq\nu_E(b\circ F).
\]
This holds for every local divisor over $Y$. Blowing up a point on the source of any local modification recovers the Lelong number at that point as a generic Lelong number along the exceptional divisor; in dimension one the point already is a divisor. The local criterion in \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} now gives $a\circ\pi\preceq_{\mathcal I}b\circ\pi$. No extension of $\sigma$ beyond its local domain was used.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ideal-equivalence-modification}
For a proper bimeromorphic map $\rho\colon Z\to Y$ between complex manifolds and quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $a,b$ on $Y$,
\[
 a\sim_{\mathcal I}b
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a\circ\rho\sim_{\mathcal I}b\circ\rho.
\]
The statement is local on $Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the local order equivalence of \cref{lem:birational-ideal-order} to both $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$. Together, the two order relations are equivalent to equality of the multiplier ideals at every positive weight.
\end{proof}
