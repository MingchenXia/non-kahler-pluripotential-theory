\section{Ramified section tests and a positive root metric}
\label{sec:ramified-root-section-tests}

The untwisted test of \cref{cor:untwisted-evaluation-immersion} can fail even for a line bundle with a positively curved metric, because the evaluation map of a globally generated bundle may fail to be an immersion where a holomorphic map ramifies. We give an explicit example on the product of a branched double cover of the projective line with a projective space, where the complete spaces of sections can be computed. The same example gives a family of weighted tests, built from a root of the Fubini--Study weight of a very ample power instead of the twists of the preceding section, whose first successful level is explicit and can be made arbitrarily large.

Fix integers $n\geq2$ and $q\geq2$. Choose a section $\beta\in H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(2q))$ with $2q$ distinct zeros. Let $\pi\colon C\to\mathbb P^1$ be the double cover defined in the total space of $\mathcal O(q)$ by the equation $w^2=\beta$, where $w$ is the tautological coordinate, and put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 X=C\times\mathbb P^{n-1},\qquad L_C=\pi^*\mathcal O(1),\\
 L=L_C\boxtimes\mathcal O(1),\qquad A=L^q,\qquad F=L^{-1}.
 \end{gathered}
\]

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ramified-root-complete-sections}
The manifold $X$ is smooth compact projective. The line bundle $L$ is globally generated, $A$ is very ample, and for every integer $k\geq0$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^0(X,L^k)&=
 \bigl(H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(k))
       \oplus H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(k-q))\bigr)
       \\&\qquad\otimes H^0(\mathbb P^{n-1},\mathcal O(k)).
 \end{aligned}
\]
In the second summand, the sections are multiplied by the coordinate $w$ of the cover. In particular,
\[
 h^0(X,L^k)=\bigl(k+1+\max(k-q+1,0)\bigr)
             \binom{n-1+k}{k}.
\]
For a basis $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_N$ of $H^0(X,A)$, the Fubini--Study weight and the corresponding root weight, in a local frame of $L$ and the induced frame of $A=L^q$, are
\[
 \phi_A=\log\sum_{i=1}^N|\sigma_i|^2,
 \qquad \phi=\phi_A/q.
\]
The metric $\mathrm e^{-\phi}$ on $L$ has strictly positive curvature. Thus it is strictly Griffiths-positive, and $G(v)=\mathrm e^{\phi}|v|^2$ is strictly plurisubharmonic on $F$ off its zero section.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The direct image of the structure sheaf of $C$ is $\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(-q)\,w$, with multiplication of the second summand given by $w^2=\beta$. Tensoring by $\mathcal O(k)$, the local basis $1,w$ gives the stated decomposition of sections on $C$. Simple zeros of $\beta$ make the cover smooth: near a branch point the equation gives a coordinate $w$ with the base coordinate a nonzero multiple of $w^2$ to leading order. The cover is connected because the rational function representing $\beta$ has a simple zero and therefore is not a square. Finiteness over compact $\mathbb P^1$ makes $C$ compact.

On $C$, the complete sections of $L_C^q$ are the degree-$q$ base monomials together with $w$. The map to projective space defined by these sections separates the base points by the Veronese coordinates, separates the two sheets by $w$, and has injective differential. At the ramification points the coordinate $w$ gives the missing derivative, and elsewhere the base coordinates do. Compactness makes this map an embedding. This shows that $C$ is projective and that $L_C^q$ is very ample.

For the decomposition of sections on the product, expand a section on each $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ fiber in its complete degree-$k$ monomial basis. The coefficients depend holomorphically on $C$ and transform as sections of $L_C^k$. This proves the displayed tensor identity without a cohomological vanishing assumption. The dimensions follow from the monomial counts, since line bundles of negative degree on $\mathbb P^1$ have no nonzero sections. The degree-one base sections generate $L_C$, hence $L$. The product of the curve embedding and the degree-$q$ Veronese embedding, followed by the Segre embedding, shows that $A$ is very ample.

Its complete linear system therefore defines an immersion, so the Fubini--Study metric on $A$ has strictly positive curvature. Dividing its weight by $q$ defines a metric on $L$, since the transition functions of $A=L^q$ are the $q$th powers of those of $L$, and its curvature is $1/q$ times that positive form. The final assertion follows from \cref{lem:dual-bundle-strict-levi}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The weighted family and its first successful level}

Keep the root metric $\mathrm e^{-\phi}$ on $L$, and choose the sphere bundle $S$ and the compact set $K$ for $F$ as in \cref{sec:projective-first-jet-positivity}. For each integer $j\geq0$, use the complete space
\[
 \widetilde V_j=H^0(X,L^{j+1})
\]
and pair its basis with $v\in F_x$ to get a local column $p_j(v)$, written in a local frame of $L^j$. For every Hermitian coefficient matrix $B$, define
\[
 \widetilde G_{j,B}(v)=\mathrm e^{-j\phi(x)}p_j(v)^*B p_j(v).
\]
These are globally defined functions, quadratic on each fiber. Their Levi matrices are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \widetilde\Psi_j(v,\xi)=\mathrm e^{-j\phi}
       (r_jr_j^*-j\kappa p_jp_j^*),\\
 r_j=\mathrm dp_j(\xi)-j\phi_u p_j,\qquad
 \kappa=\phi_{u\bar u},
 \end{gathered}
\]
where $u$ is the projection of $\xi$ to the base. As in \eqref{eq:first-jet-levi-matrix}, direct differentiation gives $\mathcal L\widetilde G_{j,B}(\xi)=\operatorname{tr}(B\widetilde\Psi_j)$, and $\widetilde\Psi_j$ transforms in the same way under changes of frames.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ramified-root-first-success}\index{Griffiths positivity!ramified root metric}\index{Griffiths positivity!finite atomic obstruction}
The first integer $j\geq0$ for which some $\widetilde G_{j,B}$, with $B$ an arbitrary Hermitian matrix, is strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section is $j=q-1$. At every lower level, zero belongs to $\operatorname{conv}(\widetilde\Psi_j(K))$; one atom witnesses this at $j=0$, and two atoms suffice at each $1\leq j<q-1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $j=q-1$, choose the basis $\sigma_i$ that defines $\phi_A$ and take $B=\operatorname{Id}$. Since $p_j(v)=v(\sigma_i(x))_i$ in the chosen frames,
\[
 \widetilde G_{q-1,\operatorname{Id}}(v)
       =\mathrm e^{-(q-1)\phi}|v|^2\mathrm e^{q\phi}
       =\mathrm e^\phi|v|^2.
\]
This function is strictly plurisubharmonic by \cref{lem:ramified-root-complete-sections}.

Now suppose that $0\leq j<q-1$. Then $j+1<q$, so by \cref{lem:ramified-root-complete-sections} every section in $\widetilde V_j$ comes from the first summand of the decomposition, which does not involve $w$. At a ramification point of $C$, choose a local coordinate $t$ on $C$, and use frames of the bundles pulled back from $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{n-1}$. Writing $\zeta$ for the coordinates of the factor $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, the column has the form $p_j(v)=vU_j(\pi(t),\zeta)$. It is nonzero for $v\ne0$, since $L^{j+1}$ is globally generated, and since $\pi$ ramifies at the point, its derivative in the $t$ direction vanishes there when $v$ is held fixed.

Choose such a fiber vector $v$ on the sphere bundle $S$. Write tangent vectors to the total space of $F$ by their components in the $t$ direction, in the $\zeta$ directions and in the fiber direction. Let $\xi_v=(0,0,v)$ be the vertical radial vector, let $u=\partial/\partial t$, and let $\xi_h=(u,0,j\phi_u v)$. At this point $\mathrm dp_j(\xi_v)=p_j$ and $r_j(\xi_h)=0$. Thus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widetilde\Psi_j(v,\xi_v)&=\mathrm e^{-j\phi}p_jp_j^*,\\
 \widetilde\Psi_j(v,\xi_h)&=-j\kappa\mathrm e^{-j\phi}p_jp_j^*,
 \qquad\kappa>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Let $a_v$ and $a_h$ be the squared norms of $\xi_v$ and $\xi_h$ for the form $b$, and divide each tangent vector by its norm to obtain points of $K$. At $j=0$ the second matrix is zero, which gives the witness with one atom. For $j>0$, the matrix at the second normalized point is $-c$ times the matrix at the first, with $c=j\kappa a_v/a_h>0$. The probability measure giving these two points the weights $c/(1+c)$ and $1/(1+c)$ has zero matrix integral, which excludes strict positivity for every Hermitian $B$. This proves failure at all lower levels, so the first successful level is $j=q-1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation to the fixed very ample criterion}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ramified-positive-untwisted-failure}\index{Griffiths positivity!positive bundle with untwisted failure}
For the data $E=L$ and $A=L^q$, with the Fubini--Study metric of a basis of $H^0(X,A)$, the criterion of \cref{thm:projective-first-jet-positive-metric} applies with $m_0=0$. Its untwisted level fails, although $E$ carries a strictly Griffiths-positive metric, and a higher level succeeds. The atom witnessing the failure at level zero annihilates only the Levi forms of the level-zero test space, and does not give a measure with $D^*T=0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:ramified-root-complete-sections}, $L$ is globally generated and $A$ is very ample, as the criterion requires. At a ramification point, the complete untwisted evaluation map has a nonzero tangent vector in its kernel, in the direction of the cover, as in the preceding proof; hence \cref{cor:untwisted-evaluation-immersion} gives failure at level zero. The positive root metric and \cref{thm:projective-first-jet-positive-metric} give success at some higher level. Moreover, the Levi form of the dual of this positive metric pairs strictly positively with every nonzero positive matrix-valued measure on $W\to S$, so the atom at level zero cannot annihilate the Levi forms of all smooth Hermitian forms.
\end{proof}

The level $m$ of the criterion in \cref{thm:projective-first-jet-positive-metric} corresponds to $j=qm$ in the root-weight family, since $E\otimes A^m=L^{qm+1}$ and $m\phi_A=qm\phi$. The first success at $j=q-1$ therefore gives no bound on the first successful level $m$ of that criterion. The root-weight family is nested in steps of $q$, because multiplication by the sections of $A$ uses $\sum_i|\sigma_i|^2=\mathrm e^{q\phi}$; we do not claim that it is nested in steps of one. As $q$ grows, the root-weight family has arbitrarily many failed initial levels, although the bundle carries a strictly positive metric.
