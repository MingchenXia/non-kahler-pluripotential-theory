\section{Ramified section tests and a positive root metric}
\label{sec:ramified-root-section-tests}

A positive globally generated line bundle can fail the untwisted immersion test. A double cover gives an explicit example with a computable complete section space. It also gives a related family of weighted tests whose first successful level is explicit. We specify its weight separately from the very ample twist in the preceding section.

Fix integers $n\geq2$ and $q\geq2$. Choose a section $\beta\in H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(2q))$ with $2q$ distinct zeros. Let $\pi:C\to\mathbb P^1$ be the double cover defined in the total space of $\mathcal O(q)$ by $w^2=\beta$, and put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 X=C\times\mathbb P^{n-1},\qquad L_C=\pi^*\mathcal O(1),\\
 L=L_C\boxtimes\mathcal O(1),\qquad A=L^q,\qquad F=L^{-1}.
 \end{gathered}
\]

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ramified-root-complete-sections}
The manifold $X$ is smooth compact projective. The line bundle $L$ is globally generated, $A$ is very ample, and for every integer $k\geq0$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^0(X,L^k)&=
 \bigl(H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(k))
       \oplus H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(k-q))\bigr)
       \\&\qquad\otimes H^0(\mathbb P^{n-1},\mathcal O(k)).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second summand has the odd cover coefficient $w$. In particular
\[
 h^0(X,L^k)=\bigl(k+1+\max(k-q+1,0)\bigr)
             \binom{n-1+k}{k}.
\]
For a basis $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_N$ of the complete space $H^0(X,A)$, its Fubini--Study weight and the corresponding root weight in a local frame of $L$ are
\[
 \phi_A=\log\sum_{i=1}^N|\sigma_i|^2,
 \qquad \phi=\phi_A/q.
\]
The metric $\mathrm e^{-\phi}$ on $L$ has strictly positive curvature. Thus $L$ is strictly Griffiths-positive and $G(v)=\mathrm e^{\phi}|v|^2$ is strictly plurisubharmonic on $F$ off its zero section.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The finite cover algebra is $\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(-q)$, with multiplication of its odd part given by $\beta$. Its local basis $1,w$ gives the stated section decomposition on $C$ after tensoring by $\mathcal O(k)$. Simple zeros of $\beta$ make the cover smooth: near a branch point the equation gives a coordinate $w$ with the base coordinate a nonzero multiple of $w^2$ to leading order. The cover is connected because the rational function representing $\beta$ has a simple zero and therefore is not a square. Finiteness over compact $\mathbb P^1$ makes $C$ compact.

On $C$, the complete sections of $L_C^q$ are the degree-$q$ base monomials together with $w$. Their projective map separates the base points by the Veronese coordinates, separates the two sheets by $w$, and has injective differential. At ramification the $w$ coordinate supplies the missing derivative; elsewhere the base coordinates do. Compactness makes this map an embedding. It proves projectivity and very ampleness directly.

For the product section decomposition, expand a section on each $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ fiber in its complete degree-$k$ monomial basis. The coefficients depend holomorphically on $C$ and transform as sections of $L_C^k$. This proves the displayed tensor identity without a cohomological vanishing assumption. The dimensions follow from the monomial counts, with no sections of negative degree on $\mathbb P^1$. The degree-one base sections generate $L_C$, hence $L$. The product of the curve embedding and the degree-$q$ Veronese embedding, followed by Segre, proves that $A$ is very ample.

Its complete section map is consequently an immersion, so the Fubini--Study metric on $A$ has strictly positive curvature. Dividing its weight by $q$ defines a genuine metric on $L$, since the transition of $A=L^q$ is the $q$th power of the transition of $L$. Its curvature is $1/q$ of that positive form. The final assertion follows from \cref{lem:dual-bundle-strict-levi}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The weighted family and its exact first successful level}

Retain this root metric on $L$. Choose the fiber sphere and compact tangent set $K$ for $F$ as in the preceding section. For each integer $j\geq0$, use the complete space
\[
 \widetilde V_j=H^0(X,L^{j+1})
\]
and pair its basis with $v\in F_x$ to get the local column $p_j(v)$ with values in $L^j$. For every Hermitian coefficient matrix $B$, define
\[
 \widetilde G_{j,B}(v)=\mathrm e^{-j\phi(x)}p_j(v)^*B p_j(v).
\]
These are globally defined fiberwise quadratic functions. Their Levi matrices are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \widetilde\Psi_j(v,\xi)=\mathrm e^{-j\phi}
       (r_jr_j^*-j\kappa p_jp_j^*),\\
 r_j=\mathrm dp_j(\xi)-j\phi_u p_j,\qquad
 \kappa=\phi_{u\bar u}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
where $u$ is the base projection of $\xi$. Direct differentiation gives $\mathcal L\widetilde G_{j,B}(\xi)=\operatorname{tr}(B\widetilde\Psi_j)$, with the same frame covariance as \eqref{eq:first-jet-levi-matrix}.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ramified-root-first-success}\index{Griffiths positivity!ramified root metric}\index{Griffiths positivity!finite atomic obstruction}
The first integer $j\geq0$ for which some $\widetilde G_{j,B}$ is strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section is $j=q-1$. At every lower level, zero belongs to $\operatorname{conv}(\widetilde\Psi_j(K))$. One atom witnesses this at $j=0$, and two atoms suffice at each $1\leq j<q-1$. The conclusion includes all Hermitian coefficient matrices.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $j=q-1$, choose the basis $\sigma_i$ that defines $\phi_A$ and take $B=\operatorname{Id}$. Since $p_j(v)=v(\sigma_i(x))_i$ in the chosen frames,
\[
 \widetilde G_{q-1,\operatorname{Id}}(v)
       =\mathrm e^{-(q-1)\phi}|v|^2\mathrm e^{q\phi}
       =\mathrm e^\phi|v|^2.
\]
The preceding lemma makes this strictly plurisubharmonic.

Suppose $0\leq j<q-1$. Then $j+1<q$, so every section in $\widetilde V_j$ comes from the even summand of the complete decomposition. At a branch point of $C$, choose a local coordinate $t$ and product bundle frames pulled back from the base. With $\zeta$ denoting the other factor, the column has the form $p_j(v)=vU_j(\pi(t),\zeta)$. It is nonzero for $v\ne0$, since $L^{j+1}$ is globally generated, and its derivative in the $t$ direction is zero at ramification when $v$ is held fixed.

Choose such a fiber vector on the sphere. Let $\xi_v=(0,0,v)$ be its vertical tangent vector. Let $u=\partial/\partial t$ and let $\xi_h=(u,j\phi_u v)$ be a tangent vector to the total space, with zero component in the other factor. At this point $\mathrm dp_j(\xi_v)=p_j$ and $r_j(\xi_h)=0$. Thus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widetilde\Psi_j(v,\xi_v)&=\mathrm e^{-j\phi}p_jp_j^*,\\
 \widetilde\Psi_j(v,\xi_h)&=-j\kappa\mathrm e^{-j\phi}p_jp_j^*,
 \qquad\kappa>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Let $a_v$ and $a_h$ be their squared tangent norms in the fixed positive metric. Divide each tangent vector by its norm to obtain points of $K$. At $j=0$ the second matrix is zero, giving the one-atom witness. At $j>0$ the normalized second matrix is $-c$ times the first, with $c=j\kappa a_v/a_h>0$. The probability giving these two points respective weights $c/(1+c)$ and $1/(1+c)$ has zero matrix integral. It prevents strict positivity for every Hermitian $B$. This proves failure at all lower levels and the exact first success.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation to the fixed very ample criterion}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ramified-positive-untwisted-failure}\index{Griffiths positivity!positive bundle with untwisted failure}
For the fixed data $E=L$, $A=L^q$ and its complete Fubini--Study metric, the projective criterion of \cref{thm:projective-first-jet-positive-metric} applies with $m_0=0$. Its untwisted level fails, despite strict Griffiths positivity of $E$. A higher level succeeds. The single zero-level atom annihilates only the zero-level test space, and does not give a measure with $D^*T=0$ on all smooth metrics.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The lemma verifies every hypothesis of the fixed projective data. At a branch point, the complete untwisted evaluation map has a nonzero kernel tangent in the cover direction, as in the preceding proof. Hence \cref{cor:untwisted-evaluation-immersion} gives failure. The positive root metric and \cref{thm:projective-first-jet-positive-metric} give success at some higher level. This same positive metric pairs strictly positively with every nonzero positive matrix-valued measure on the compact tangent set, so the zero-level atom cannot annihilate all smooth metric Levi forms.
\end{proof}

The integer $m$ of the very ample criterion corresponds to $j=qm$ in the root-weight family: $E\otimes A^m=L^{qm+1}$ and $m\phi_A=qm\phi$. The explicit first success $j=q-1$ therefore gives no first-success bound for that subsequence. The root-weight family has $q$-step nesting, since multiplication by the complete sections of $A$ uses $\sum_i|\sigma_i|^2=\mathrm e^{q\phi}$; one-step nesting is not asserted. The parameter $q$ permits arbitrarily many failed initial levels in the specified root-weight family while its bundle already has a strict positive metric.
