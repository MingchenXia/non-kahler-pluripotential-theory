\subsection{Generic uniqueness}
\label{sec:generic-adjoints}

The gap between the two directional derivatives vanishes densely along every affine line. Upper semicontinuity of that gap and separability of $C(X)$ turn this one-dimensional fact into uniqueness of the supporting probability on a dense $G_\delta$ in the uniform norm.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:generic-adjoint-uniqueness}\label{cor:generic-adjoint-approximation}\index{Adjoint measure!generic uniqueness}
The set
\[
 \mathscr U_\varphi
   =\{F\in C(X,\mathbb R):\mathcal A_\varphi(F)
                                  \text{ is a singleton}\}
\]
is a dense $G_\delta$. At every $F\in\mathscr U_\varphi$, the functional $B_\varphi$ is G\^ateaux differentiable and its derivative is integration against minus the unique supporting probability. If $F_j\to F$ uniformly and $\nu_j\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F_j)$, then $\nu_j$ converge weakly to that probability.
\end{theorem}
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
For $h\in C(X,\mathbb R)$ define the width
\[
 w_h(F)=\max_{\nu\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F)}\int_Xh\,\mathrm{d}\nu
         -\min_{\nu\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F)}\int_Xh\,\mathrm{d}\nu\geq0.
\]
The extrema exist by weak compactness. This width is upper semicontinuous in $F$. Indeed, if $F_j\to F$, choose maximizing and minimizing probabilities at $F_j$. Along a subsequence realizing the upper limit of the widths, compactness of the space of probabilities gives weak limits of both choices. By \cref{prop:weak-adjoint-stability}, both limits belong to $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$. Their difference of integrals is at most $w_h(F)$.

For every $\epsilon>0$ the set $\{F:w_h(F)<\epsilon\}$ is therefore open. It is dense as well. If $h=0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise fix $F$ and any open ball about it. The function
\[
 b(t)=B_\varphi(F+th)
\]
is finite and concave on the real line. Its nondifferentiability set is at most countable: its finite one-sided derivatives are monotone, and the nonempty open intervals between unequal derivatives at distinct points are disjoint, so each can be assigned a distinct rational number. Choose a differentiability point $t$ sufficiently close to zero that $F+th$ remains in the given ball. The one-sided derivative formula of \cref{prop:normalization-directional} gives $w_h(F+th)=0$. This proves density.

The compact manifold $X$ is metrizable, so $C(X,\mathbb R)$ is separable. Choose a countable uniformly dense family $(h_j)$. The Baire theorem shows that
\[
 \bigcap_{j,k\geq1}\{F:w_{h_j}(F)<1/k\}
\]
is a dense $G_\delta$. At every point of this intersection, all supporting probabilities have the same integral against every $h_j$. Uniform approximation and their unit masses then give equality of their integrals against every continuous function. The Riesz representation theorem makes the probabilities identical. Conversely, a singleton has zero width in every direction, so the displayed intersection is exactly $\mathscr U_\varphi$. The derivative follows from \cref{prop:normalization-directional}, and the approximation assertion follows from \cref{prop:weak-adjoint-stability}.
\end{proof}
