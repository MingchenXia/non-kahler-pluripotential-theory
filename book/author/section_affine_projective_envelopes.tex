\subsection{Affine growth and projective envelopes}
\label{sec:affine-projective-envelopes}
\index{Envelope!affine growth}
\index{Contact set!growth parameter}

The contact property concerns the regularized envelope. For the classical weighted extremal functions on $\mathbb C^n$, the obstacle is imposed at every point of a compact set, and one may ask when the regularized envelope still touches the obstacle there. Compactifying $\mathbb C^n$ to $\mathbb P^n$ turns the logarithmic growth allowance into a semipositive background. This correspondence distinguishes contact of the raw supremum with the obstacle from contact of its upper semicontinuous regularization, and it shows how both depend on the growth allowance.

In the rest of this section we work on $\mathbb C^n$ and on $\mathbb P^n$ rather than on $X$. Write $\chi(z)=\log(1+\lVert z\rVert^2)$ on $\mathbb C^n$, and let $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^n$ with this local potential, so that $\int_{\mathbb P^n}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}^n=1$ with our normalization of $\ddc$. For $\delta>0$ put
\[
 \theta_\delta=\tfrac\delta2\omega_{\mathrm{FS}},\qquad
 \mathcal L_\delta=
 \{u\in\PSH(\mathbb C^n):
    u(z)\leq\delta\log^+\lVert z\rVert+C_u\}.
\]
Here $\log^+=\max(\log,0)$, and $C_u$ is a constant depending on $u$. Thus $\mathcal L_\delta$ is the class of plurisubharmonic functions of logarithmic growth at most $\delta$. Let $\mathcal L_\delta^+$ consist of the members of $\mathcal L_\delta$ that are also bounded below by $\delta\log^+\lVert z\rVert-C$ for some constant $C$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:affine-projective-growth}
The map $u\mapsto u-\delta\chi/2$, followed by the canonical extension across the hyperplane at infinity, is a bijection from $\mathcal L_\delta$ onto $\PSH(\mathbb P^n,\theta_\delta)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots:Z_n]$ and the affine chart $Z_0\ne0$. On a chart $Z_i\ne0$ meeting $H=\{Z_0=0\}$, the local plurisubharmonic representative of $p=u-\delta\chi/2$ is
\[
 p+\frac\delta2\log\frac{\sum_{j=0}^n|Z_j|^2}{|Z_i|^2}
 =u+\delta\log|Z_0/Z_i|.
\]
This function is locally bounded above near $H$, by the growth bound on $u$, because near each point of $H$ the norm of the affine coordinates is at most a constant divided by $|Z_0/Z_i|$. By \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-pluripolar}, it therefore extends canonically across $H$. Since the local potentials of $\theta_\delta$ differ by smooth functions on overlaps, the extensions agree there.

Conversely, every $p\in\PSH(\mathbb P^n,\theta_\delta)$ is bounded above, since $\mathbb P^n$ is compact. The function $u=p+\delta\chi/2$ is plurisubharmonic on the affine chart, and
\[
 0\leq\tfrac12\chi-\log^+\lVert z\rVert\leq\tfrac12\log2
\]
gives the required growth bound. Neither function is identically $-\infty$ on the affine chart. Uniqueness of the canonical extension shows that the two maps are inverse to each other.
\end{proof}

Fix a non-pluripolar compact set $K\subset\mathbb C^n$ and a bounded real function $\psi$ on $K$. Define the raw and the regularized envelope
\[
 V_\delta=\sup\{u\in\mathcal L_\delta:u|_K\leq\psi\},
 \qquad F_\delta=V_\delta^*,
\]
where the obstacle condition is imposed at every point of $K$. On $\mathbb P^n$ put
\[
 G_\delta=\sup\{p\in\PSH(\mathbb P^n,\theta_\delta):
                 p|_K\leq\psi-\delta\chi/2\}.
\]

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:affine-projective-envelope}
The regularized envelope $G_\delta^*$ on $\mathbb P^n$ is bounded and $\theta_\delta$-plurisubharmonic. On the affine chart,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-projective-envelope}
 V_\delta=\tfrac\delta2\chi+G_\delta,\qquad
 F_\delta=\tfrac\delta2\chi+G_\delta^*\in\mathcal L_\delta^+.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $V_\delta=F_\delta$ outside a pluripolar set, in particular almost everywhere.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:affine-projective-growth}, the map $u\mapsto u-\delta\chi/2$ is a bijection between the two families of competitors, which proves the identity for $V_\delta$. Constants below $\inf_K(\psi-\delta\chi/2)$ are competitors for $G_\delta$.

Since all competitors for $G_\delta$ lie below the constant $\sup_K(\psi-\delta\chi/2)$ on $K$, \cref{lem:compact-obstacle-upper-bound} gives a common upper bound for them. Together with a constant competitor, the upper-envelope theorem shows that $G_\delta^*$ is bounded and $\theta_\delta$-plurisubharmonic. Addition of the smooth function $\delta\chi/2$ in \eqref{eq:affine-projective-envelope} commutes with regularization, and the two-sided bounds for $G_\delta^*$ give $F_\delta\in\mathcal L_\delta^+$.

Locally, adding a potential of $\theta_\delta$ makes the competitors plurisubharmonic. The upper-envelope theorem recalled in \cref{chap:framework} shows that regularization changes their supremum only on a pluripolar set. Thus $G_\delta=G_\delta^*$ outside a pluripolar set, and adding $\delta\chi/2$ gives the same assertion for $V_\delta$.
\end{proof}

Changing $\delta$ changes both the background $\theta_\delta$ and the obstacle $\psi-\delta\chi/2$. Nevertheless, the set of parameters for which contact holds has a simple structure. Put
\[
 \Lambda=\{\delta>0:V_\delta|_K=\psi\},\qquad
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}(a)=
 \{\delta\in\Lambda:F_\delta(a)=\psi(a)\}\quad(a\in K).
\]
The parameters $\delta\in\Lambda$ are those for which the raw envelope touches the obstacle at every point of $K$; we say that \emph{raw contact} holds for them. For $a\in K$, the parameters in $\Lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$ are those for which, in addition, the regularized envelope touches the obstacle at $a$; we say that \emph{regular contact} holds at $a$.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:affine-growth-contact-ray}
The set $\Lambda$ is empty, $(0,\infty)$, or $[\lambda,\infty)$ for some $\lambda>0$. If $\Lambda=[\lambda,\infty)$, each $\Lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$ is empty, $\{\lambda\}$, or $\Lambda$. If $\Lambda=(0,\infty)$, each $\Lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$ is empty or $\Lambda$. The same alternatives hold for $\bigcap_{a\in K}\Lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $m=\inf_K\psi$. Inclusion of the growth classes and scaling of competitors give, for $0<\alpha<\beta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-growth-raw-slope}
 0\leq V_\beta-V_\alpha
 \leq\frac{\beta-\alpha}{\alpha}(V_\alpha-m).
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $u$ is a competitor for $V_\beta$, then $(\alpha/\beta)(u-m)+m$ is a competitor for $V_\alpha$, since $\psi\geq m$. The constant $m$ is also a competitor, so all terms are finite and the right-hand side is nonnegative. The estimate shows that $V_\delta(z)$ depends continuously on $\delta>0$ at every point $z$. Since $V_\delta\leq\psi$ on $K$ and $V_\delta$ increases with $\delta$, the set $\Lambda$ is closed upwards, and by continuity it is relatively closed in $(0,\infty)$. This proves the three possible forms of $\Lambda$.

For $0\leq t\leq1$, convex combinations of competitors give
\[
 V_{(1-t)\alpha+t\beta}\geq(1-t)V_\alpha+tV_\beta.
\]
By \cref{prop:affine-projective-envelope}, the same inequality holds for the regularized envelopes almost everywhere, and hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. Thus $F_\delta(z)$ is a concave nondecreasing function of $\delta$ at every point $z$.

For $a\in K$ and $\delta\in\Lambda$, the defect $d_\delta=F_\delta(a)-\psi(a)$ is nonnegative, concave and nondecreasing in $\delta$. Suppose $d_\alpha=0$, and that there is $\eta\in\Lambda$ with $\eta<\alpha$. For every $\beta>\alpha$, write $\alpha$ as a convex combination of $\eta$ and $\beta$ with positive weights. Concavity then forces both nonnegative defects $d_\eta$ and $d_\beta$ to vanish, and monotonicity gives $d_\delta=0$ for all $\delta\in\Lambda$ with $\delta<\alpha$. Hence a zero of the defect at a parameter other than the smallest element of $\Lambda$ forces every defect to vanish. This proves the alternatives for each $\Lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$, and intersecting them gives the alternatives for $\bigcap_{a\in K}\Lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$.
\end{proof}

Thus raw contact persists as the growth allowance increases, whereas regular contact at a point may hold only at the smallest contact parameter. The correspondence with $\mathbb P^n$ keeps the background and the obstacle matched throughout this variation.
