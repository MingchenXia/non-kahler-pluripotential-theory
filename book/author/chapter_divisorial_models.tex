\chapter{Divisorial currents on compact modifications}
\label{chap:divisorial-models}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold and $Y\subset X$ a connected compact complex submanifold of dimension $r\geq1$. Fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ and a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $\nu(u,Y)=0$. We study the divisorial trace data $\tau_{Y,u}$ of \cref{chap:trace-data} on smooth compact modifications of $Y$.

A model carries only part of this data: blowing up can reveal vanishing that had higher codimension on the original manifold. Summing the visible coefficients gives a closed positive current on each model, controlled by its Gauduchon mass. These currents agree under pushforward, while pullback acquires an effective exceptional correction. The full trace envelope, which imposes the inequalities on every model at once, pulls back exactly. The difference explains why the ordinary type of a model's divisorial part need not realize the full trace data.

The divisorial currents are closed even when $\theta$ is not. With a closed restricted background, subtracting them gives residual Bott--Chern classes compatible between models. To identify the exceptional correction for an infinite divisorial sum, we need continuity of pullback for positive closed currents. A scalar Gauduchon equation supplies this continuity. Residual classes with a nonzero prescribed exterior derivative belong to the affine spaces of \cref{chap:nonclosed-bclasses}.

Use the analytic approximants $u_m$ and errors $\epsilon_m$ of \cref{cor:nonclosed-analytic-approximation}. For a smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$, put
\[
 \theta_Z=\pi^*(\theta|_Y),\qquad
 \omega_Z^0=\pi^*(\omega|_Y),\qquad
 b_m(E)=\operatorname{ord}_E\bigl(\mathcal I(mu)\mathcal O_Y\bigr)
 \quad(m\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}).
\]
Here $E$ is a prime divisor on $Z$, and the order is that of the image ideal pulled back to $Z$. Write $[E]$ for its integration current. By \cref{thm:intrinsic-trace-profile},
\[
 \frac{b_m(E)}m\longrightarrow\tau_{Y,u}(E),\qquad
 \tau_{Y,u}(E)=\sup_{m\geq1}\frac{b_m(E)}m.
\]
The analytic approximants satisfy $\ddc((u_m|_Y)\circ\pi)\geq-\theta_Z-\epsilon_m\omega_Z^0$ by \cref{cor:restricted-analytic-divisorial-bound}.

\section{Divisorial trace currents}\label{sec:divisorial-model-bound}

The restricted analytic approximants dominate the finite divisors of their image ideals. Pairing with a Gauduchon metric removes the potential term from this inequality. The resulting bound survives the limit of each coefficient and controls their entire sum.

For a positive integer $m$, with $E$ ranging over prime divisors on $Z$, define the finite divisorial current
\[
 D_{m,Z}=\frac1m\sum_E b_m(E)[E].
\]
The sum is finite because the image ideal on the smooth compact manifold $Z$ is coherent and nonzero. Its codimension-one zero components form a finite family. Define initially through finite subsums
\[
 S_Z(u;Y)=\sum_E\tau_{Y,u}(E)[E].
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:trace-divisorial-current-budget}\index{Divisorial current!trace budget}
The last sum defines a closed positive $(1,1)$-current with finite mass on $Z$. For every Gauduchon metric $\gamma$ on $Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-divisorial-linear-budget}
 \int_Z S_Z(u;Y)\wedge\gamma^{r-1}
 \leq\int_Z\theta_Z\wedge\gamma^{r-1}.
\end{equation}
For $r=1$ take $\gamma^{r-1}=1$. Moreover
\[
 0\leq D_{m,Z}\leq S_Z(u;Y),\qquad
 \int_Z\bigl(S_Z(u;Y)-D_{m,Z}\bigr)\wedge\gamma^{r-1}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
In particular $D_{m,Z}$ converges weakly as a current to $S_Z(u;Y)$. The dyadic sequence $D_{2^j,Z}$ increases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $w_m=(u_m|_Y)\circ\pi$. By \cref{cor:restricted-analytic-divisorial-bound}, these pullbacks are genuine, and
\[
 T_m=\theta_Z+\epsilon_m\omega_Z^0+\ddc w_m\geq0,
 \qquad \nu(w_m,E)=b_m(E)/m.
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\Cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} gives $D_{m,Z}\leq T_m$. Current Stokes and the Gauduchon identity therefore give
\[
 \int_ZD_{m,Z}\wedge\gamma^{r-1}
 \leq\int_Z\theta_Z\wedge\gamma^{r-1}
       +\epsilon_m\int_Z\omega_Z^0\wedge\gamma^{r-1}.
\]
Pass to the limit in the coefficients of any fixed finite set of prime divisors, using \cref{thm:intrinsic-trace-profile}. Then take the supremum over finite sets. This proves \eqref{eq:trace-divisorial-linear-budget} as a finite-subsum bound.

Every prime divisor has strictly positive $\gamma$-area. Hence there are at most countably many positive coefficients: for each positive integer $k$, at most finitely many terms can have weighted mass at least $1/k$, and their union includes every positive term. Enumerate these divisors. Their finite partial sums are increasing positive closed currents with a uniform trace-mass bound. They therefore converge, in coefficient measures and weakly as currents, to a positive current of finite mass. Distributional differentiation is continuous on currents, so the limit is closed. This constructs $S_Z(u;Y)$ and proves the integral inequality.

The supremum formula for $\tau_{Y,u}(E)$ gives $b_m(E)/m\leq\tau_{Y,u}(E)$ for every $m$ and $E$. The full integer limit gives coefficientwise convergence. Apply dominated convergence to the countable series with summable weights $\tau_{Y,u}(E)\int_E\gamma^{r-1}$. It yields the asserted vanishing trace mass of $S_Z-D_{m,Z}$. A fixed smooth test form is bounded in absolute value on positive $(1,1)$-currents by a constant times this trace mass, proving weak convergence. Finally $b_{2m}(E)\geq2b_m(E)$ gives $D_{2m,Z}\geq D_{m,Z}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Compatibility between models}

Under a modification, a divisor and its strict transform define the same order on the image ideals. Exceptional divisors disappear under pushforward but can carry additional vanishing. Divisibility of the pulled-back ideals measures that additional vanishing.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:trace-divisorial-model-compatibility}\index{Divisorial current!compatibility between models}
Let $g:Z'\to Z$ be a smooth compact modification, and view both manifolds as models over $Y$. Then
\[
 g_*S_{Z'}(u;Y)=S_Z(u;Y).
\]
The assignment is additive and positively homogeneous in $u$. For every finite set $\mathcal F$ of prime divisors on $Z$, the finite effective real divisor
\[
 A_{\mathcal F}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}\tau_{Y,u}(E)E
\]
satisfies
\[
 [g^*A_{\mathcal F}]\leq S_{Z'}(u;Y).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $E'$ is the strict transform of a prime divisor $E\subset Z$, the map is biholomorphic near their generic points. They define the same vanishing order on the image ideals from $Y$, and hence $\tau_{Y,u}(E')=\tau_{Y,u}(E)$. Also $g_*[E']=[E]$.

For an exceptional prime divisor $F$, its image has codimension at least two. The pullback of any smooth $(r-1,r-1)$ test form restricts to zero on its regular locus, because the map there has complex rank at most $r-2$. Thus $g_*[F]=0$. In dimension one a modification between smooth compact curves is an isomorphism, so there are no such exceptional divisors. Pushforward is continuous on currents for this proper map. Applying it to increasing finite sums, or equivalently testing their summable positive measures, proves the displayed compatibility. Additivity and homogeneity follow coefficientwise from \cref{prop:trace-profile-calculus}.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For the final inequality, let $\mathfrak a_{m,Z}=\mathcal I(mu)\mathcal O_Z$ be the image ideal on $Z$, for a positive integer $m$. Its divisorial vanishing gives
\[
 \mathfrak a_{m,Z}\subseteq
 \mathcal O_Z\left(-\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}b_m(E)E\right).
\]
\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Indeed on a smooth manifold a holomorphic function with the indicated generic orders is divisible by the corresponding local Cartier equations, with multiplicity. Pull back this inclusion and take the order at any prime divisor $F\subset Z'$. It gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-ideal-model-inequality}
 b_m(F)\geq\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}
                 b_m(E)\operatorname{ord}_F(g^*E).
\end{equation}
Divide by $m$ and pass to the limit; the sum is finite. Thus every coefficient of $g^*A_{\mathcal F}$ is at most $\tau_{Y,u}(F)$. Both sides are divisorial positive currents, which proves the asserted current inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{The trace envelope as a common majorant}

The trace envelope imposes the inequalities for all divisors over $Y$, including those absent from any one model. It therefore supplies a single potential whose pullbacks dominate every divisorial trace current. Its background is the original restriction $\theta|_Y$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:original-background-trace-majorant}\index{Trace!common envelope majorant}
There exists $v\in\PSH(Y,\theta|_Y)$ such that for every smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$,
\[
 \nu(v\circ\pi,E)\geq\tau_{Y,u}(E)
 \quad\hbox{for every prime }E\subset Z,
\]
and
\[
 \pi^*(\theta|_Y)+\ddc(v\circ\pi)\geq S_Z(u;Y).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $v=\mathcal T_{\theta|_Y}(u;Y)$ from \eqref{eq:trace-profile-envelope}. By \cref{thm:restricted-analytic-envelope-limit}, it belongs to $\PSH(Y,\theta|_Y)$ and satisfies every defining trace inequality. On a fixed model $Z$, \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} therefore allows removal of every finite partial sum of $S_Z(u;Y)$ from $\theta_Z+\ddc(v\circ\pi)$. Passing to the current limit of these sums, constructed in \cref{thm:trace-divisorial-current-budget}, preserves positivity and proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}

The choice in the proof is canonical: \cref{thm:trace-envelope-modification} identifies $v\circ\pi$ with the trace envelope in background $\theta_Z$ for the inherited data. Its Lelong numbers may still be larger than those prescribed. Exact realization requires the reverse inequality, as established in \cref{chap:trace-data} for analytic singularities, K\"ahler targets in sufficiently large backgrounds, and curve fibrations. The ordinary type attached to $S_Z$ records only the divisorial part on that particular model.
