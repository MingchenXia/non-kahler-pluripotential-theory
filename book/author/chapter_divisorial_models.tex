\chapter{Divisorial currents on compact modifications}
\label{chap:divisorial-models}

The divisorial trace data of a potential along a submanifold $Y$ (\cref{chap:trace-data}) assign a number to every prime divisor on every smooth compact modification of $Y$. A single model sees only part of these data, since blowing up can reveal vanishing that had higher codimension on $Y$. Summing the coefficients visible on one model gives a closed positive current, whose mass is controlled by a Gauduchon degree. These currents agree under pushforward, while their pullbacks acquire an effective exceptional correction. The trace envelope, which imposes the trace inequalities on all models at once, pulls back exactly. This discrepancy explains why the ordinary singularity type of the divisorial part on one model need not realize the full trace data.

The divisorial currents are closed even when the background is not. In a closed restricted background, subtracting them gives residual Bott--Chern classes that are compatible between models. To identify the exceptional correction for an infinite divisorial sum, we need pullback to be continuous on closed positive currents; this continuity comes from choosing, in each Bott--Chern class, the representative of constant Gauduchon trace. Residual classes with a nonzero prescribed exterior derivative belong to the affine spaces of \cref{chap:nonclosed-bclasses}.

Throughout the chapter, $Y\subset X$ is a connected compact complex submanifold of dimension $r\geq1$, $\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $\omega$ a Hermitian form on $X$, and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has generic Lelong number $\nu(u,Y)=\inf_{y\in Y}\nu(u,y)=0$. Let $u_m$ and $\epsilon_m$ be the analytic approximants and curvature errors of \cref{cor:nonclosed-analytic-approximation}: $u_m$ has analytic singularities of type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$, and $\theta+\ddc u_m\geq-\epsilon_m\omega$ with $\epsilon_m\to0$. For a smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$, put
\[
 \theta_Z=\pi^*(\theta|_Y),\qquad
 \omega_Z^0=\pi^*(\omega|_Y),\qquad
 b_m(E)=\operatorname{ord}_E\bigl(\mathcal I(mu)\mathcal O_Y\bigr)
 \quad(m\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}).
\]
Here $E$ is a prime divisor on $Z$, $[E]$ denotes its integration current, and $b_m(E)$ is the vanishing order along $E$ of the image ideal $\mathfrak a_m(u;Y)=\mathcal I(mu)\mathcal O_Y$ pulled back to $Z$. By \cref{thm:intrinsic-trace-profile}, the trace numbers satisfy
\[
 \frac{b_m(E)}m\longrightarrow\tau_{Y,u}(E),\qquad
 \tau_{Y,u}(E)=\sup_{m\geq1}\frac{b_m(E)}m.
\]
By \cref{cor:restricted-analytic-divisorial-bound}, the restrictions $u_m|_Y$ are not identically $-\infty$, and the curvature bound pulls back to $\ddc((u_m|_Y)\circ\pi)\geq-\theta_Z-\epsilon_m\omega_Z^0$.

\section{Divisorial trace currents}\label{sec:divisorial-model-bound}

On a model $Z$, the curvature current of the pulled-back analytic approximant dominates the divisor of the image ideal of $\mathcal I(mu)$. Pairing with a Gauduchon metric removes the potential from this inequality, and the resulting bound passes to the limit in each coefficient and controls the sum of all trace coefficients. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$ on $Z$, we call $\int_ZT\wedge\gamma^{r-1}$ the \emph{trace mass} of a positive $(1,1)$-current $T$; when $r=1$, $\gamma^{r-1}=1$ and $\gamma$ may be any Hermitian metric.

For a positive integer $m$, with $E$ ranging over the prime divisors on $Z$, define the finite divisorial current
\[
 D_{m,Z}=\frac1m\sum_E b_m(E)[E].
\]
The sum is finite: the pulled-back image ideal is a nonzero coherent ideal on the compact manifold $Z$, so its zero set has only finitely many components of codimension one. We also consider the possibly infinite sum
\[
 S_Z(u;Y)=\sum_E\tau_{Y,u}(E)[E],
\]
which a priori is defined only through its finite subsums.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:trace-divisorial-current-budget}\index{Divisorial current!trace budget}
The sum $S_Z(u;Y)$ defines a closed positive $(1,1)$-current of finite mass on $Z$. For every Gauduchon metric $\gamma$ on $Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-divisorial-linear-budget}
 \int_Z S_Z(u;Y)\wedge\gamma^{r-1}
 \leq\int_Z\theta_Z\wedge\gamma^{r-1},
\end{equation}
where $\gamma^{r-1}=1$ if $r=1$. Moreover,
\[
 0\leq D_{m,Z}\leq S_Z(u;Y),\qquad
 \int_Z\bigl(S_Z(u;Y)-D_{m,Z}\bigr)\wedge\gamma^{r-1}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
In particular, $D_{m,Z}$ converges weakly to $S_Z(u;Y)$ as currents, and the dyadic sequence $D_{2^j,Z}$ is increasing.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $w_m=(u_m|_Y)\circ\pi$. By \cref{cor:restricted-analytic-divisorial-bound}, these functions are not identically $-\infty$, and
\[
 T_m=\theta_Z+\epsilon_m\omega_Z^0+\ddc w_m\geq0,
 \qquad \nu(w_m,E)=b_m(E)/m.
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\Cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} gives $D_{m,Z}\leq T_m$. Integration by parts for currents and the Gauduchon condition therefore give
\[
 \int_ZD_{m,Z}\wedge\gamma^{r-1}
 \leq\int_Z\theta_Z\wedge\gamma^{r-1}
       +\epsilon_m\int_Z\omega_Z^0\wedge\gamma^{r-1}.
\]
For any fixed finite set of prime divisors, let $m\to\infty$ in the coefficients of these divisors, using \cref{thm:intrinsic-trace-profile}, and then take the supremum over all finite sets. This proves \eqref{eq:trace-divisorial-linear-budget} for every finite subsum.

Every prime divisor has strictly positive $\gamma$-area. Hence at most countably many coefficients are positive: for each positive integer $k$, only finitely many terms can have trace mass at least $1/k$, and every positive term is among these for some $k$. Enumerate the corresponding divisors. The finite partial sums are increasing positive closed currents with uniformly bounded trace mass. They therefore converge, in the sense of coefficient measures and weakly as currents, to a positive current of finite mass, which is closed because differentiation is continuous on currents. This constructs $S_Z(u;Y)$ and proves the integral inequality.

The supremum formula for $\tau_{Y,u}(E)$ gives $b_m(E)/m\leq\tau_{Y,u}(E)$ for every $m$ and $E$, and the limit along the integers gives coefficientwise convergence. Dominated convergence for the countable series with summable weights $\tau_{Y,u}(E)\int_E\gamma^{r-1}$ shows that the trace mass of $S_Z-D_{m,Z}$ tends to zero. Since a fixed smooth test form is bounded in absolute value, on positive $(1,1)$-currents, by a constant times the trace mass, this gives weak convergence. Finally, $b_{2m}(E)\geq2b_m(E)$ gives $D_{2m,Z}\geq D_{m,Z}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Compatibility between models}

Under a further modification, a prime divisor and its strict transform define the same vanishing order on the image ideals, while exceptional divisors disappear under pushforward. Hence the trace currents are compatible with pushforward. Under pullback, exceptional divisors can carry additional vanishing, and divisibility of the pulled-back ideals shows that this additional vanishing is nonnegative.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:trace-divisorial-model-compatibility}\index{Divisorial current!compatibility between models}
Let $g:Z'\to Z$ be a smooth compact modification, and regard $Z'$ as a model over $Y$ through $\pi\circ g$. Then
\[
 g_*S_{Z'}(u;Y)=S_Z(u;Y).
\]
The current $S_Z(u;Y)$ is additive and positively homogeneous in $u$. For every finite set $\mathcal F$ of prime divisors on $Z$, the finite effective real divisor
\[
 A_{\mathcal F}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}\tau_{Y,u}(E)E
\]
satisfies
\[
 [g^*A_{\mathcal F}]\leq S_{Z'}(u;Y).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every prime divisor on $Z'$ is either the strict transform of a prime divisor on $Z$ or exceptional for $g$. If $E'$ is the strict transform of a prime divisor $E\subset Z$, then $g$ is biholomorphic near generic points of $E'$. Hence $E'$ and $E$ define the same vanishing order on the image ideals from $Y$, so $\tau_{Y,u}(E')=\tau_{Y,u}(E)$, and $g_*[E']=[E]$.

The image of an exceptional prime divisor $F$ has codimension at least two. The pullback of a smooth $(r-1,r-1)$ test form therefore restricts to zero on the regular locus of $F$, where the restriction of $g$ has complex rank at most $r-2$; thus $g_*[F]=0$. In dimension one, a modification between smooth compact curves is an isomorphism, and there are no exceptional divisors. Pushforward under the proper map $g$ is continuous on currents. Applying it to the increasing finite partial sums, or equivalently testing their summable positive coefficient measures, proves the displayed compatibility. Additivity and homogeneity follow coefficientwise from \cref{prop:trace-profile-calculus}.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For the inequality, fix a positive integer $m$ and let $\mathfrak a_{m,Z}=\mathcal I(mu)\mathcal O_Z$ denote the image ideal on $Z$. Its divisorial vanishing gives
\[
 \mathfrak a_{m,Z}\subseteq
 \mathcal O_Z\left(-\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}b_m(E)E\right).
\]
\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Indeed, on a manifold, a holomorphic function vanishing to the indicated generic orders is divisible by the corresponding powers of the local equations of the divisors. Pulling back this inclusion and taking the order along a prime divisor $F\subset Z'$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-ideal-model-inequality}
 b_m(F)\geq\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}
                 b_m(E)\operatorname{ord}_F(g^*E).
\end{equation}
Divide by $m$ and let $m\to\infty$; the sum is finite. Thus every coefficient of $g^*A_{\mathcal F}$ is at most the corresponding coefficient $\tau_{Y,u}(F)$ of $S_{Z'}(u;Y)$. Since both sides are positive divisorial currents, this proves the asserted inequality of currents.
\end{proof}

\section{The trace envelope as a common majorant}

The trace envelope of \cref{sec:trace-envelope} imposes the trace inequalities along all divisors over $Y$, including those that do not appear on a given model. It therefore gives a single potential on $Y$, in the original background $\theta|_Y$, whose pulled-back curvature current dominates the trace current on every model.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:original-background-trace-majorant}\index{Trace!common envelope majorant}
There exists $v\in\PSH(Y,\theta|_Y)$ such that, for every smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$,
\[
 \nu(v\circ\pi,E)\geq\tau_{Y,u}(E)
 \quad\hbox{for every prime divisor }E\subset Z,
\]
and
\[
 \pi^*(\theta|_Y)+\ddc(v\circ\pi)\geq S_Z(u;Y).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take for $v$ the trace envelope $\mathcal T_{\theta|_Y}(u;Y)$ of \eqref{eq:trace-profile-envelope}. By \cref{thm:restricted-analytic-envelope-limit}, it belongs to $\PSH(Y,\theta|_Y)$ and satisfies every defining trace inequality. On a fixed model $Z$, \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} therefore allows us to subtract every finite partial sum of $S_Z(u;Y)$ from $\theta_Z+\ddc(v\circ\pi)$ while preserving positivity. Passing to the limit of these partial sums, constructed in \cref{thm:trace-divisorial-current-budget}, preserves positivity and proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}

The potential in the proof is canonical: by \cref{thm:trace-envelope-modification}, $v\circ\pi$ is the trace envelope on $Z$ in the background $\theta_Z$ for the same trace data. Its generic Lelong numbers may nevertheless exceed the prescribed trace numbers. Exact realization of the trace data requires the reverse inequality, which \cref{chap:trace-data} establishes for analytic singularities, for K\"ahler $Y$ in sufficiently large closed backgrounds, and for pullbacks from curves. By contrast, the ordinary singularity type attached to $S_Z$ by \cref{cor:divisorial-current-ordinary-type} records only the divisorial part on the particular model $Z$.
