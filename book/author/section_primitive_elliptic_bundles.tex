\subsection{Primitive elliptic bundles}
\label{sec:primitive-elliptic-bundles}

On a primitive elliptic bundle, the variation of top-degree mass is a vertical Dirichlet integral. This gives an exact upper mass and a positive lower bound for separated potentials. The lower mass over all potentials remains a geometric question: the collapse construction over a product of curves does not transfer directly to a base with no curves.

Let $(B,\eta)$ be a connected compact K\"ahler surface and let $L\to B$
be a holomorphic line bundle such that
\[
 \int_Bc_1(L)\wedge\eta=0.
\]
Choose a smooth Hermitian metric on $L$ with primitive curvature. To do
so, start with any metric: the trace of its curvature has mean zero, and
the scalar Poisson equation supplies a smooth change of metric cancelling
that trace. Write $\pi:L\to B$ for the bundle projection and $w$ for a nonzero fiber vector, whose norm is taken with this metric. Let $c$ be the smooth closed curvature representative of $-c_1(L)$, so $\eta\wedge c=0$. On the complement $L^\times$ of the zero section, put
\[
 t=\log|w|^2,\qquad \ddc t=\pi^*c.
\]
We use the same symbol $\pi$ for the induced quotient projection below.
For $q>1$ form the quotient
\[
 X=L^\times/\langle w\mapsto qw\rangle,
 \qquad \pi:X\longrightarrow B,
 \qquad T=2\log q,
 \qquad A=\int_B\eta^2.
\]
The action increases $t$ by $T$. It is free and properly discontinuous,
and the compact annulus $1\leq|w|\leq q$ meets every orbit.
Hence $X$ is a connected compact complex threefold. For a trivializing open set $U\subset B$, a local holomorphic frame of $L$ identifies the fibration with $U\times(\C^*/q^{\mathbb Z})$.
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Both $\mathrm{d}t$ and $\dc t$ descend, and we set
\[
 D=\mathrm{d}t\wedge\dc t,\qquad \omega=\pi^*\eta+D.
\]
The rank-one form $D$ is semipositive and positive on the vertical line.
Its kernel projects isomorphically onto the tangent space of $B$, so
$\omega$ is Hermitian.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:primitive-elliptic-metric}
The metric $\omega$ is balanced and satisfies
\[
 \ddc\omega=-\pi^*(c^2)\geq0,\qquad
 \int_X\omega^3=3TA.
\]
For every smooth real $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-elliptic-mass}
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^3
 =3TA-3\int_X\mathrm{d}u\wedge\dc u\wedge\bigl(-\pi^*(c^2)\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular $v_+(\omega)=3TA$, and the canonical bundles satisfy $K_X\simeq\pi^*K_B$.
If $c_1(L)\ne0$ in real cohomology, $X$ admits neither a pluriclosed
metric nor a closed K\"ahler current. If $c_1(L)=0$ in real cohomology,
the chosen $\omega$ is K\"ahler.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $a=\mathrm{d}t$ and $b=\dc t$. Then
$\mathrm{d}a=0$, $\mathrm{d}b=\pi^*c$, $\dc a=-\pi^*c$ and $\dc b=0$.
Consequently
\[
 D^2=0,\qquad \mathrm{d}D=-a\wedge\pi^*c,
 \qquad \ddc D=-\pi^*(c^2),
\]
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\omega^2)=-2\pi^*(\eta\wedge c)\wedge a=0.
\]
The eigenvalues of $c$ relative to $\eta$ are $\lambda$ and $-\lambda$,
so $c^2=-\lambda^2\eta^2$. This proves the asserted sign.

Fix an elliptic fiber $E=\pi^{-1}(b_0)$, where $b_0\in B$. In a logarithmic fiber coordinate $x+\mathrm{i}y$, the real periods are $\log q$ and $2\pi$, while $t=2x+$ a constant and $D|_E=\pi^{-1}\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y$. Thus $\int_ED=T$. Since $\omega^3=3\pi^*(\eta^2)\wedge D$, the volume formula follows.

To compute the mass variation, expand the cube in \eqref{eq:primitive-elliptic-mass}.
The terms with one and three factors $\ddc u$ integrate to zero:
$\omega^2$ is closed, and $\ddc u$ is exact and closed. For the remaining
term, put $S=\ddc\omega$. Integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_X(\ddc u)^2\wedge\omega
 =\int_Xu\,\ddc u\wedge S
 =-\int_X\mathrm{d}u\wedge\dc u\wedge S,
\]
where the last equality uses $\mathrm{d}S=0$. This proves the identity.
Its last integral is nonnegative, and $u=0$ attains the upper bound.
The equality of smooth and bounded mass extrema in
\cref{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema} proves the assertion about $v_+$.

If $c$ is exact, Stokes gives $\int_Bc^2=0$. The pointwise identity
$c^2=-\lambda^2\eta^2$ then makes $c=0$, and $\omega$ is closed.
Otherwise $S=-\pi^*(c^2)$ is nonzero, smooth and semipositive.
For a pluriclosed metric $\gamma$, Stokes would give
\[
 0<\int_X\gamma\wedge S
 =\int_X\omega\wedge\ddc\gamma=0.
\]
For a closed positive current $R\geq\varepsilon\omega$, it would likewise
give $0<\int_XR\wedge S=\int_X\omega\wedge\ddc R=0$.
All pairings are with smooth forms.

Finally, let $w$ be a local nonzero holomorphic fiber coordinate and
$\sigma$ a local holomorphic two-form on $B$.
The form $\pi^*\sigma\wedge\mathrm{d}w/w$ is invariant under the quotient
action. Changing the line-bundle frame adds to $\mathrm{d}w/w$ a horizontal
one-form, whose wedge with $\sigma$ vanishes. These identifications have
the transition functions of $K_B$ and prove $K_X\simeq\pi^*K_B$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
For separated potentials, positivity bounds both the amplitude and the downward slope of the vertical derivative. The sharp variance bound under these constraints determines how much mass such a potential can lose. For $M,T>0$, set
\[
 F(M,T)=
 \begin{cases}
  T^2/12,&T\leq2M,\\
  M^2-4M^3/(3T),&T\geq2M.
 \end{cases}
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:periodic-variance-bound}
If a smooth real $T$-periodic function $p$ has mean zero, $|p|<M$ and
$p'>-1$, then
\[
 \frac1T\int_0^T p^2\,\mathrm{d}s<F(M,T).
\]
The supremum of the left side over this class is $F(M,T)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $p=0$ the inequality follows from $F(M,T)>0$. Otherwise set $a=\max p>0$,
$-b=\min p<0$ and $r=a+b$. On a forward interval $I$ from a maximum
to a subsequent minimum, integration of $-p'<1$ gives
$r<|I|\leq T$; also $r<2M$. The function $G(s)=(a-s)(s+b)$ is nonnegative on the range of $p$. Since $-p'<1$, integration on $I$ gives
\[
 abT-\int_0^T p^2\,\mathrm{d}s
 =\int_0^T G(p)\,\mathrm{d}s
 \geq-\int_I G(p)p'\,\mathrm{d}s
 =\frac{r^3}{6}.
\]
Since $ab\leq r^2/4$, the average square is at most
$r^2/4-r^3/(6T)$. This expression increases strictly for $0<r<T$.
Its value at $\min\{2M,T\}$ is $F(M,T)$.

For sharpness when $T\geq2M$, use equal constant intervals at $M$ and
$-M$, each of length $(T-2M)/2$, joined by a descent of slope $-1$
and length $2M$. Close the period by an upward jump.
When $T\leq2M$, use $p_0(s)=T/2-s$ for $0\leq s<T$, with its upward
jump at the join. Both profiles have mean zero, satisfy $|p_0|\leq M$
and $p_0'\geq-1$ distributionally, and have average square $F(M,T)$.
Convolution with a nonnegative periodic approximate identity preserves the mean and the weak derivative bound. Multiplication by $1-\varepsilon$ makes both inequalities strict. As the convolution scale and $\varepsilon$ tend to zero, the resulting functions converge to the profile in $L^2$. This also treats $T=2M$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:primitive-elliptic-separated}
Suppose $c_1(L)\ne0$ in real cohomology, and put
\[
 \kappa=-\frac{\int_Bc^2}{A}>0.
\]
Every smooth potential $u=f(t)+\pi^*v$ with $f$ of period $T$,
$v\in C^\infty(B,\R)$ and $\omega+\ddc u>0$ satisfies
\[
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^3
 >3TA\bigl(1-\kappa F(\kappa^{-1/2},T)\bigr)>0.
\]
Let $\lambda\geq0$ be the nonnegative eigenvalue function of $c$ relative to $\eta$, so $c^2=-\lambda^2\eta^2$, and put $M_0=1/\max_B\lambda$. For the subclass $v=0$, the exact mass infimum is
\[
 3TA\bigl(1-\kappa F(M_0,T)\bigr)>0.
\]
It is approached by smooth strictly admissible radial potentials and is
not attained in that class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $p=f'$. The chain rule gives
\[
 \omega+\ddc u
 =\pi^*(\eta+pc+\ddc v)+(1+p')D.
\]
Positivity on the vertical line implies $p'>-1$. Every base point sees
every value of $t$ modulo $T$, so restriction to $\ker\partial t$ makes
$\eta+pc+\ddc v$ a K\"ahler form on $B$ for each value of $p$.
Its square has integral
\[
 \int_B(\eta+pc+\ddc v)^2=A(1-\kappa p^2)>0.
\]
Here primitivity removes the mixed intersection and Stokes removes the
exact terms. Hence $|p|<\kappa^{-1/2}$.

Only the vertical derivative enters \eqref{eq:primitive-elliptic-mass}.
For a periodic function $F_0$, fiber integration gives
$\int_E F_0(t)D=\int_0^T F_0(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$, and therefore
\[
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^3
 =3TA\left(1-\frac\kappa T\int_0^T p^2\,\mathrm{d}s\right).
\]
Apply \cref{lem:periodic-variance-bound} with $M=\kappa^{-1/2}$.
The formula for $F$ gives $F(M,T)<M^2$, proving the positive bound.

For $v=0$, diagonalizing $c$ against $\eta$ shows that the horizontal
positivity condition is exactly $|p|<M_0$. Together with $p'>-1$ it
also suffices for positivity of the full form. Moreover $\kappa$ is the
$\eta^2$-average of $\lambda^2$, so $\kappa\leq M_0^{-2}$.
The lemma proves the claimed positive infimum and nonattainment.
Its smooth approximating functions have mean zero and hence periodic
primitives $f_\varepsilon$. The potentials $f_\varepsilon(t)$ prove sharpness.
\end{proof}

The first bound is not asserted to be sharp for the separated class on an arbitrary base. Positivity of the class $[\eta]+p[c]$ alone does not supply one function $v$ that makes all of its indicated representatives positive. In the earlier $\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1$ example, $A=2$, $\kappa=1$ and $T=4\pi$; the bound is $8$ and is sharp.

A base with no curves removes the horizontal curve averages used in the known collapse example. The following construction uses the K3 period theorem and K\"ahler-cone description \cite[Theorems 7.4.1 and 8.5.2 in the author draft]{HuyK3}.

\begin{example}\label{ex:primitive-elliptic-k3}\index{Elliptic fibration!primitive K3 example}
There is a K3 surface $B$ with $\operatorname{Pic}(B)=\mathbb Z[L]$,
$c_1(L)^2=-4$, and a K\"ahler form $\eta$ orthogonal to $c_1(L)$.
It has no compact curves. The associated $X$ has trivial canonical bundle,
a balanced metric of finite upper mass, and no pluriclosed metric or
closed K\"ahler current. It has no divisors, and every compact irreducible
curve is an elliptic fiber.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
In a hyperbolic plane of the K3 lattice choose a basis $e,f$ with
$e^2=f^2=0$ and $e\cdot f=1$, and put $\ell=e-2f$. This is primitive and has square $-4$.
Choose a period in $\ell^\perp$ outside the hyperplanes perpendicular
to integral vectors not in $\mathbb Z\ell$. Such a period exists by
Baire's theorem: the period domain in $\ell^\perp$, of signature $(3,18)$,
is nonempty and each excluded hyperplane is proper. The period theorem
and the integral $(1,1)$-class description give the asserted Picard group.
An irreducible curve has nonzero class by its positive K\"ahler area.
Its possible square would be $-4m^2<-2$, contradicting adjunction.
Thus there are no curves.

The orthogonal complement of $\ell$ in $H^{1,1}(B,\R)$ has signature $(1,18)$. Choose a positive-square class there, with sign in the positive component containing K\"ahler classes. As there are no rational curves, the K\"ahler-cone theorem makes it K\"ahler. Choose $\eta$ in that class. \Cref{prop:primitive-elliptic-metric} gives the metric and canonical-bundle assertions.

If a divisor $Z\subset X$ dominated $B$, the projection of its integration
current would be $\pi_*[Z]=m$ for a positive generic degree $m$.
Stokes and the projection formula would then give
\[
 0=\int_X[Z]\wedge\ddc\omega
   =m\int_B(-c^2)>0.
\]
Thus no divisor dominates $B$. The proper image of any other irreducible
divisor would be a curve or a point. The base has no curves, and a fiber
has dimension one, so both alternatives are impossible.
The proper image of an irreducible compact curve is likewise a point;
the curve is therefore an entire elliptic fiber.
\end{proof}

\begin{problem}\label{prob:primitive-elliptic-lower-mass}
Determine the lower Monge--Amp\`ere mass of the primitive elliptic bundles
above, in particular those of \cref{ex:primitive-elliptic-k3}.
Does it vanish whenever the real Chern class is nonzero?
\end{problem}

The estimates for separated potentials do not answer this question. The known collapse on $\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1$ leaves that class of potentials. On the K3 example every compact curve is vertical, so averaging such curves cannot supply the horizontal directions used in \cref{lem:lower-mass-curve-average}. A proof of collapse would need a different construction, while positive lower mass would require a bound for all admissible potentials or all normalized Gauduchon tests.
