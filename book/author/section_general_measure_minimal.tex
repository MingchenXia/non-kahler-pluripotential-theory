\section{General measures in the minimal envelope class}
\label{sec:general-measure-minimal}

In the minimal envelope class, a bounded carrier measure reduces the equation to an integrable density. Truncating that density gives bounded solutions dominated by one fixed finite measure. Dominated continuity then passes to the full density; a vanishing exponent produces the normalized equation.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:general-measure-minimal}
Let $\mu$ be a finite non-pluripolar measure with $\mu(X)>0$.
For every $\lambda>0$ there is a unique $u\in\mathcal E_0$ satisfying
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta^{\mathrm{ext}}(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu.
\]
There also exist $v\in\mathcal E_0$, normalized by $\sup_Xv=0$, and a unique positive scalar $c$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta^{\mathrm{ext}}(v)=c\mu.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The reference $0$ is model because $\theta\geq0$, and $P_{\epsilon\theta}(0)=0$ for every $\epsilon>0$. Every bounded $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function belongs to $\mathcal E_0$, since a sufficiently negative constant is a competitor for each of its stretched envelopes. The lower estimate of \cref{thm:relative-uniform-mass-lower}, with $\epsilon\downarrow0$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimal-lower-mass}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(w)(X)\geq v_-(\theta)
 \qquad(w\in\mathcal E_0).
\end{equation}

\emph{Positive exponent.} Write $\mu=f\nu$ by \cref{lem:bounded-measure-carrier}, with $\nu=\mathrm{MA}_\theta(\psi)$ and $\psi$ bounded. Let $f_j=\min(f,j)$ for positive integers $j$. These densities are bounded and nonzero as measures. \Cref{thm:bounded-subsolution} gives bounded $u_j$ with
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_j)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}f_j\nu.
\]
Exponential order shows that $u_j$ decreases, because $f_j$ increases. In particular $u_j\leq u_1$. On the other hand
\[
 v_-(\theta)\leq\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_j)(X)
       \leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_j}\mu(X).
\]
Thus their suprema have a common lower bound. The decreasing limit $u$ is a genuine $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function, and convergence holds in $L^1(X)$. Moreover
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_j)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_1}\mu.
\]
Apply \cref{thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity} with reference zero. It gives $u\in\mathcal E_0$ and weak convergence of the Monge--Amp\`ere measures to $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$. The right sides converge in total variation to $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\nu$: the functions $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}f_j$ converge almost everywhere for $\nu$ and are bounded by $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_1}f\in L^1(\nu)$. This proves the equation. \Cref{cor:relative-extended-exponential-order} gives uniqueness.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\emph{Zero exponent.} For each positive integer $j$, let $u_j\in\mathcal E_0$ be the just-constructed solution with exponent $1/j$. We first bound the normalization. Let $h$ be the bounded solution of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(h)=\mathrm{e}^h\min(f,1)\nu,
\]
supplied by \cref{thm:bounded-subsolution}. Set $H=\|h\|_\infty$ and $w_j=h+(j-1)H$. Then $w_j/j\geq h$, and
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-w_j/j}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_j)
 \leq \mathrm{e}^{-h}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(h)
 \leq\mu
 =\mathrm{e}^{-u_j/j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j).
\]
Exponential order, now with exponent $1/j$, gives $u_j\leq w_j$. Put
\[
 M_j=\sup_Xu_j,\qquad v_j=u_j-M_j,
 \qquad c_j=\mathrm{e}^{M_j/j}.
\]
Then $\sup_Xv_j=0$ and $0<c_j\leq \mathrm{e}^H$. Equation \eqref{eq:minimal-lower-mass} also gives
\[
 v_-(\theta)\leq c_j\int_X \mathrm{e}^{v_j/j}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
                 \leq c_j\mu(X),
\]
so $c_j\geq v_-(\theta)/\mu(X)>0$. After extraction, \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives $v_j\to v$ in $L^1(X)$, with $v$ genuine and normalized, and $c_j\to c>0$.

By \cref{lem:normalized-exponential-npp}, $a_j=\mathrm{e}^{v_j/j}\to1$ in $L^1(\mu)$. Consequently
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(v_j)=c_ja_j\mu
       \longrightarrow c\mu
 \quad\hbox{in total variation},\qquad
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(v_j)\leq \mathrm{e}^H\mu.
\]
Apply \cref{thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity} again. It gives $v\in\mathcal E_0$ and identifies the limit as $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(v)$. The scalar assertion in \cref{cor:relative-extended-exponential-order}, applied in both directions to two solutions, proves uniqueness of $c$.
\end{proof}

These minimal-class solutions also give upper bounds for the prescribed-reference approximants: exponential order with reference zero compares them with every $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential. This comparison will control the normalization in \cref{sec:general-measure-relative}.
