For the Monge--Amp\`ere equation, the trace estimate of \cref{thm:hermitian-second-estimate} does not involve the gradient. For other equations the maximum principle may bound the trace only in terms of the gradient. Since traces such as $\operatorname{tr}_gh=n+\Delta_gu$ are positive, an upper bound for the trace bounds $|\Delta_gu|$; if it depends at most linearly on the gradient, the following lemma closes the estimate: elliptic regularity for the fixed operator $\Delta_g$ bounds a H\"older norm of the gradient linearly in its supremum, and interpolation with the oscillation bounds the supremum. A quadratic dependence is invariant under the rescalings $u\mapsto u(x+z/R)$ in local coordinates, and the argument does not apply to it. The second-order estimates for the Gauduchon ansatz and for complex Hessian equations, \cref{thm:stw-second-order-reconstructed,hessian:prescribed-potential-trace}, are quadratic in the gradient; there the gradient is bounded instead by rescaling at points of maximal gradient and a Liouville theorem for the limit (\cref{prop:stw-gradient-from-second-order,hessian:hessian-trace-to-gradient}).

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:fixed-trace-gradient-interpolation}\index{Gradient estimate!linear trace criterion}\index{Laplacian!interpolation bound}
Let $A,M\geq0$. There is $C=C(g,A,M)$ such that every smooth real function $v$ on $X$ satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{osc}_Xv\leq M,\qquad
 \|\Delta_gv\|_{L^\infty}
       \leq A\bigl(1+\|\mathrm{d}v\|_{L^\infty(g)}\bigr)
\]
also satisfies
\[
 \|\mathrm{d}v\|_{L^\infty(g)}+\|\Delta_gv\|_{L^\infty}\leq C.
\]
Here all volume and function-space norms use the fixed smooth metric $g$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Subtract $\inf_Xv$, so that $0\leq v\leq M$, and put $K=\|\mathrm{d}v\|_{L^\infty(g)}$. Fix $p>2n$ and $0<\alpha<1-2n/p$. The fixed operator $\Delta_g$ is uniformly elliptic on the underlying real manifold. The local $W^{2,p}$ estimates, patched by a finite atlas, and Morrey's inequality give
\[
 \|v\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}
 \leq C_g\|v\|_{W^{2,p}}
 \leq C_g\bigl(\|v\|_{L^p}+\|\Delta_gv\|_{L^p}\bigr)
 \leq C_g\bigl(M+A(1+K)\bigr).
\]
These are the standard linear elliptic $L^p$ estimates of \cite[Chapter~9]{GT01} and the Sobolev embedding of \cite[Chapter~7]{GT01}; the constants depend on the fixed metric and on the chosen $p,\alpha$.

For $0<t\leq t_0$, with $t_0$ fixed by the atlas, integration along a coordinate segment of length $t$ gives
\[
 K\leq C_g t^{-1}M+C_g t^\alpha\|v\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}.
\]
Indeed, the difference quotient is bounded by $M/t$, since $0\leq v\leq M$, and its difference from the derivative at the initial point is bounded by $C_gt^\alpha\|v\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}$. A finite atlas with smaller charts controls every point and coordinate direction; equivalence with the $g$-norm gives the displayed estimate. Consequently,
\[
 K\leq C_gt^{-1}M+C_g^2t^\alpha(M+A)
                      +C_g^2At^\alpha K.
\]
Choose $t\leq t_0$ so small that $C_g^2At^\alpha\leq1/2$. This choice depends only on $g,A$, including when $A=0$. Absorbing the last term bounds $K$ in terms of $g,A,M$, and the assumed trace estimate then bounds $\|\Delta_gv\|_{L^\infty}$.
\end{proof}
