\chapter{Background forms and local singularities}
\label{chap:framework}

The singular part of a potential is a local object, whereas positivity of the current it defines is a global condition. A smooth background form connects these two descriptions. A smooth change of potential can be absorbed into the background, leaving the current and its singularity type unchanged. Allowing the background to be nonclosed changes its relation to local potentials and line-bundle curvature. To compare singularities, we use domination up to a constant and inclusion of multiplier ideals.

\section{Plurisubharmonic potentials}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, and let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on $X$. There are two distinct extensions of the K\"ahler setting: one can study a closed background on a non-K\"ahler manifold, or allow the background itself to be nonclosed. In both cases positivity is expressed through quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, which are locally sums of plurisubharmonic functions and smooth real functions.

\begin{definition}\label{def:theta-psh}
A quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $X$ is \emph{$\theta$-plurisubharmonic} if
\[
 \theta+\ddc u\geq0
\]
as a current. We denote the set of these functions by $\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\end{definition}

If $\theta=\omega$, every constant belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:local-correction}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. On each relatively compact coordinate ball with coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$, there is a constant $A>0$ such that $u+A|z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic.
Conversely, if $u$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic on the compact manifold $X$, then there is a constant $C>0$ such that $u\in\PSH(X,C\omega)$.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
On a coordinate ball with compact closure in a larger chart, smoothness of $\theta$ gives
\[
 \theta\leq A\ddc|z|^2
\]
for a sufficiently large $A$. Consequently,
\[
 \ddc(u+A|z|^2)\geq A\ddc|z|^2-\theta\geq0.
\]
Thus $u+A|z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic.

For the converse, choose finitely many relatively compact charts covering $X$ on which $u=p_j+f_j$, with $p_j$ plurisubharmonic and $f_j$ smooth. On the closure of each smaller chart, the smooth form $\ddc f_j$ is bounded below by $-C_j\omega$. Thus $\ddc u\geq-C_j\omega$ there. Taking the maximum of finitely many $C_j$ proves the global inequality.
\end{proof}

For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, choose coordinates $z$ and a constant $A>0$ as in \cref{prop:local-correction}, and put $p=u+A|z|^2$. Then
\[
 \theta+\ddc u=\ddc p+(\theta-A\ddc|z|^2).
\]
Thus local plurisubharmonic theory applies to $p$, while the smooth remainder records the original background. It must be retained when forming products or integrating by parts.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:no-local-potential}
If $\theta=\ddc\rho$ on an open set for a smooth function $\rho$, then $\mathrm{d}\theta=0$ on that set. In particular, at a point where $\mathrm{d}\theta\neq0$, no neighborhood admits such a representation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The identity follows from $\mathrm{d}\ddc=0$.
\end{proof}

For a closed real $(1,1)$-form, smooth local $\ddc$-potentials do exist on sufficiently small coordinate balls by the local $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma. This local fact does not require the compact manifold to admit a K\"ahler metric. Thus a closed background on a non-K\"ahler manifold and a non-closed background are already different at the local level.

\section{Changes of background}

Let $\eta$ be another smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. A positive current can be written as a smooth background plus a complex Hessian in more than one way. A smooth change of decomposition changes the potential by a bounded function; positive linear combinations also allow us to compare potentials belonging to different backgrounds.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gauge}
Let $f$ be smooth and real, and set $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$. Then
\[
 \PSH(X,\theta)\longrightarrow\PSH(X,\theta'),
 \qquad u\longmapsto u-f
\]
is a bijection. It preserves the positive current and changes the potential by a bounded function. Moreover, $\mathrm{d}\theta'=\mathrm{d}\theta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every $u$ in the source,
\[
 \theta'+\ddc(u-f)=\theta+\ddc u.
\]
The inverse is $v\mapsto v+f$, and $f$ is bounded by compactness. The last assertion follows from $\mathrm{d}\ddc f=0$.
\end{proof}

The quotient of smooth real $(1,1)$-forms by smooth $\ddc$-terms can therefore be useful even for non-closed forms. It must be distinguished from the usual Bott--Chern cohomology group, whose representatives are closed. The terminology in the literature is not uniform: some authors use an enlarged Bott--Chern space for the former quotient. We shall always specify whether its representatives are required to be closed.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:convexity}
If $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $v\in\PSH(X,\eta)$, then, for $a,b\geq0$ with $a+b>0$,
\[
 au+bv\in\PSH(X,a\theta+b\eta).
\]
In particular, $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is convex. If $\eta\geq\theta$, then $\PSH(X,\theta)\subseteq\PSH(X,\eta)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Sums with nonnegative coefficients preserve quasi-plurisubharmonicity, with zero-coefficient terms omitted. The curvature identity is
\[
 a\theta+b\eta+\ddc(au+bv)
 =a(\theta+\ddc u)+b(\eta+\ddc v)\geq0.
\]
For the inclusion, add the positive smooth form $\eta-\theta$ to $\theta+\ddc u$.
\end{proof}

By \cref{prop:local-correction}, any two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions belong to $\PSH(X,C\omega)$ for a common sufficiently large $C$. This lets us compare their singularities within one positive background. Its differential is still $C\,\mathrm{d}\omega$, so enlarging $C$ does not remove any failure of closedness.

\section{Singularity type and multiplier ideals}

We compare quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ first by domination up to a constant, then through weighted local integrability of holomorphic functions. The first comparison gives ordinary singularity types; the second gives multiplier ideals. A logarithmic pole on a disc will show why the ideals at all positive weights enter the comparison.

\begin{definition}\label{def:singularity-order}
For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $u,v$, write $u\preceq v$ if $u\leq v+C$ for some $C\in\R$. Write $u\sim v$ if $u\preceq v$ and $v\preceq u$.
\end{definition}

The convention $u\preceq v$ means that $u$ is at least as singular as $v$. In particular, a logarithmic pole lies below a bounded potential in this order.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:singularity-elementary}
The relation $\preceq$ is a preorder, and $\sim$ is an equivalence relation. Adding a bounded smooth function preserves the equivalence class. In particular, all smooth functions on $X$ have the same singularity type.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Reflexivity follows with $C=0$. If $u\leq v+C$ and $v\leq w+D$, then $u\leq w+C+D$, proving transitivity. The usual equivalence relation associated to a preorder gives $\sim$. If $|f|\leq A$, then $u-A\leq u+f\leq u+A$. Finally, every smooth function on compact $X$ is bounded.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:multiplier-ideal}
For a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $X$, the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(u)$ is the sheaf whose local sections are holomorphic functions $g$ for which $|g|^2\mathrm{e}^{-u}$ is locally integrable against a smooth positive volume form.
\end{definition}

Changing the volume form does not change local integrability: on relatively compact coordinate neighborhoods, the ratio of two smooth positive volume forms is bounded above and below by positive constants. This definition thus requires no chosen Hermitian metric.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ideal-order}
If $u\preceq v$, then $\mathcal I(ku)\subseteq\mathcal I(kv)$ for every real $k>0$. If $u\sim v$, then these ideals agree for every $k>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $g$ be a local holomorphic function on $X$. An inequality $u\leq v+C$ implies $\mathrm{e}^{-kv}\leq \mathrm{e}^{kC}\mathrm{e}^{-ku}$. Multiplying by $|g|^2$ shows that local integrability for $ku$ implies local integrability for $kv$. Apply the same argument in both directions when $u\sim v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:ideal-disc}
On a disc in $\mathbb C$ centered at the origin, with coordinate $z$, let $\lambda\geq0$ and put $u(z)=\lambda\log|z|^2$ for $\lambda>0$, while $u=0$ for $\lambda=0$. Then
\[
 \mathcal I(u)_0=(z^{\lfloor\lambda\rfloor}).
\]
Indeed, if a nonzero holomorphic germ has order $m$, its squared modulus is comparable to $|z|^{2m}$ near the origin. For a sufficiently small radius $\epsilon>0$, its weighted integral is finite precisely when
\[
 \int_0^\epsilon r^{2m-2\lambda+1}\,\mathrm{d}r<\infty,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad m>\lambda-1.
\]
The smallest nonnegative integer satisfying this strict inequality is $\lfloor\lambda\rfloor$. At an integer value of $\lambda$, the excluded borderline exponent is $-1$, so the strictness of the integrability condition matters.
\end{example}

The example also explains why one multiplier ideal is insufficient to compare singularities: different coefficients $\lambda$ can have the same integer part. For a real weight $k>0$, applying the calculation to $ku$ gives $\mathcal I(ku)_0=(z^{\lfloor k\lambda\rfloor})$. The ideals at all $k>0$ recover $\lambda$, since for real coefficients $0\leq\lambda<\mu$ the two integer parts differ as soon as $k(\mu-\lambda)>1$.

We use the $\mathcal I$-preorder defined by inclusion of $\mathcal I(ku)$ in $\mathcal I(kv)$ for every real weight $k>0$. Requiring equality at all these weights gives the ideal equivalence relation used in \cref{chap:hermitian-igood}. Both definitions make sense on any complex manifold; their relation to envelopes and global masses requires further arguments.

\section{Curvature of line bundles}

Let $X$ be a complex manifold and let $\omega$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. To connect potentials with holomorphic sections, we ask when a current $\omega+\ddc u$, with $u$ locally integrable, can be the curvature of a line-bundle metric. The transition law for its local weights forces a closedness condition on the background.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chern-closed}
The curvature current of a singular Hermitian metric with locally integrable weights on a holomorphic line bundle is closed. In particular, a non-closed form $\omega$ cannot itself be the curvature of such a metric, nor can $\omega+\ddc u$ be closed at a point where $\mathrm{d}\omega\neq0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $h$ be the singular Hermitian metric on the holomorphic line bundle $L\to X$ in the statement. Choose a local holomorphic frame $e$ of $L$ and write its squared norm as $|e|_h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi}$. The curvature current is locally $\ddc\varphi$. If $e'=ge$ is another frame, with $g$ holomorphic and nowhere zero, the new weight is $\varphi-\log|g|^2$. Since $\log|g|^2$ is pluriharmonic, these local currents glue. Each is closed because $\mathrm{d}\ddc=0$. The final assertion follows from
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\omega+\ddc u)=\mathrm{d}\omega
\]
in the sense of currents.
\end{proof}

When studying sections with multiplier-ideal conditions, we shall therefore specify both the line bundle with its closed curvature and any auxiliary Hermitian form on $X$. An algebraic interpretation then requires an argument relating the line bundle's invariants to that analytic background.
