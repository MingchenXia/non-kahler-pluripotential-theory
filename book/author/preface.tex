\preface

The aim of this manuscript is to study singularities of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on compact Hermitian manifolds. Its point of departure is Mingchen Xia's \emph{Singularities in global pluripotential theory} \cite{SGPT}. We follow the notation and the progression from local singularities to global comparison, while asking which arguments remain valid when the background form is not closed.

There are two different changes of setting. We may keep a closed real $(1,1)$-form and allow the underlying compact complex manifold to be non-K\"ahler. Alternatively, we may replace the closed background by a Hermitian form. The second change is present even on a complex manifold that happens to admit a K\"ahler metric. It changes the differential identities satisfied by the Monge--Amp\`ere operator. We shall keep these two situations separate.

For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $u,v$ on the same complex manifold, ordinary singularities are compared by $u\leq v+C$ for a real constant $C$. The local language of singularities remains available. Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, Lelong numbers, multiplier ideals and this comparison are defined without choosing a K\"ahler metric. The global mass, however, can change under a smooth perturbation of the potential. A quantity that changes under a smooth perturbation cannot, by itself, classify singularities. This elementary observation is the starting point of the book.

The question is therefore not only how to solve a Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere equation. We also want to understand the hierarchy of ordinary singularity types, envelope equivalence and multiplier-ideal equivalence. Writing $\mathcal I$ for multiplier ideals, the source book brings these notions together in the theory of $\mathcal I$-good singularities. A Hermitian counterpart must explain which of its characterizations remain equivalent, which need a different invariant, and which have no presently justified analogue.

The main theory is developed without either uniform mass property. The examples in \cref{chap:hopf,sec:vanishing-lower-mass} show that bounded mass and positive lower mass can each fail. Extended measures, finite damping and envelopes provide the general framework. \Cref{part:mass-bounds} collects finite-volume comparison, relative equations under positive lower mass, and arbitrary-big equations under bounded mass and positive lower reference volume. Each chapter states its additional assumptions. General definitions, characterizations of the mass properties and counterexamples remain in the main sequence.

For a Hermitian background, positive lower mass is equivalent to solvability, up to a positive scalar, for every nonzero finite positive non-pluripolar measure. When it vanishes, a positive $L^1$ density can admit no scaled subsolution of any singularity type. The smooth and $L^p$ equations, $p>1$, remain solvable. Thus the general theory must specify the admissible right-hand sides as well as the singularities of the potential.

Gauduchon metrics enter in several ways. Their linear trace controls normalized potentials even when nonlinear mass is infinite. Their adjoint equation differentiates the normalizing constant of a smooth Monge--Amp\`ere equation. Their variational formula then defines a finite volume for smooth background classes and extends to a compact optimization over positive currents. These constructions retain useful global information without restoring the missing conservation of Monge--Amp\`ere mass.

Degenerate equations exhibit a further difference: a smooth density can have distinct normalized smooth solutions even under bounded mass. The examples include quadratic zeros on two real hypersurfaces and a real analytic density. Positive uniqueness results depend on regularity and the geometry of the positive-density set. For certain symmetric data, positive-exponent approximation selects one explicit $C^{1,1}$ solution. Existence, uniqueness of the scalar, uniqueness of the potential and selection by approximation are therefore separate questions.

The geometric chapters treat closed Bott--Chern classes and locally conformally K\"ahler metrics. Boucksom's singular loci remain meaningful with Hermitian lower bounds, but a direct Hermitian-error definition of volume can be infinite even in the zero class. In LCK geometry, Gauduchon's condition makes the Lee form harmonic and yields Chern--Ricci-flat Vaisman rigidity. Twisted potentials preserve the LCK equation and lead to different volume questions. The final chapter connects the theory to nefness, bigness, the Fujiki class, null loci, Chern--Ricci flow, and balanced and pluriclosed metrics.

The recent literature supplies substantial parts of the subject. Its definitions of volumes, full mass classes and singularity distances depend on precise background assumptions. We aim for a self-contained exposition apart from standard references and the source book; results still labeled as external inputs remain proof obligations.
