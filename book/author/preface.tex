\preface

The aim of this manuscript is to study singularities of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on compact Hermitian manifolds. Its point of departure is Mingchen Xia's \emph{Singularities in global pluripotential theory} \cite{SGPT}. We follow the notation and the progression from local singularities to global comparison, while asking which arguments remain valid when the background form is not closed.

There are two different changes of setting. We may keep a closed real $(1,1)$-form and allow the underlying compact complex manifold to be non-K\"ahler. Alternatively, we may replace the closed background by a Hermitian form. The second change is present even on a complex manifold that happens to admit a K\"ahler metric. It changes the differential identities satisfied by the Monge--Amp\`ere operator. We shall keep these two situations separate.

The local language of singularities remains available. Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, Lelong numbers, multiplier ideals and the comparison $u\leq v+C$ are defined without choosing a K\"ahler metric. The global mass, however, can change under a smooth perturbation of the potential. A quantity that changes under a smooth perturbation cannot, by itself, classify singularities. This elementary observation is the starting point of the book.

The question is therefore not only how to solve a Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere equation. We also want to understand the hierarchy of ordinary singularity types, envelope equivalence and multiplier-ideal equivalence. In the source book, these notions meet in the theory of $\mathcal I$-good singularities. A Hermitian counterpart must explain which of its characterizations remain equivalent, which need a different invariant, and which have no presently justified analogue.

The main theory in this manuscript is developed without the bounded mass property. The August 2026 counterexample of Xia--Zhang \cite{XZ26} makes this essential. Extended measures, finite damped measures and their convergence provide the general framework. Results under bounded mass are retained as special regimes, with their additional conclusions identified explicitly.

Gauduchon metrics enter in several ways. Their linear trace controls normalized potentials even when nonlinear mass is infinite. Their adjoint equation differentiates the normalizing constant of a smooth Monge--Amp\`ere equation. Their variational formula then defines a finite volume for smooth background classes and extends to a compact optimization over positive currents. These constructions retain useful global information without restoring the missing conservation of Monge--Amp\`ere mass.

Degenerate equations exhibit a further difference: a smooth density can have distinct normalized smooth solutions even when bounded mass holds. Our projective examples include densities with exactly quadratic zeros on two real hypersurfaces and a prescribed real analytic density. Positive results depend on the regularity and geometry: on every compact Hermitian surface, $C^2$ solutions are unique when the positive-density set is dense; in every dimension, a connected positive-density region gives uniqueness for $C^{1,1}$ potentials with continuous common density. For certain symmetric data, positive-exponent approximation selects one explicit $C^{1,1}$ solution despite loss of second derivatives. Existence, uniqueness of the scaling constant, uniqueness of a potential and selection by approximation are therefore separate questions.

The chapters on closed Bott--Chern classes and locally conformally K\"ahler geometry make the geometric distinctions explicit. The supplied transcendental Nakamaye argument is developed in full, using the trace theory of \cite{SGPT}; its two local regularization lemmas also hold with a Hermitian reference metric. Boucksom's singular loci remain meaningful with Hermitian lower bounds, but a direct Hermitian-error supremum for volume can be infinite even in the zero class. In LCK geometry, Gauduchon's condition makes the Lee form harmonic and leads to a proof of Chern--Ricci-flat Vaisman rigidity. Twisted potentials preserve the LCK equation and lead to volume questions different from those for ordinary potentials. The final research chapter connects these methods to nefness and bigness, the Fujiki class, Hermitian null loci, Chern--Ricci flow, and balanced and pluriclosed metrics.

The recent literature already supplies substantial parts of the subject. We compare its definitions carefully rather than identify them by name. In particular, upper and lower volumes, relative full mass classes and expressions measuring the separation of singularities must always be read together with their background assumptions. Our aim is a self-contained exposition apart from standard references and the source book; recent results still labeled as external inputs mark proof obligations in this working draft.

\section*{Status of this draft}
This is an AI-assisted research manuscript prepared for mathematical review, not an author-approved revision of \cite{SGPT}. Proofs and external inputs are recorded in the text; unresolved steps remain visible as questions or explicit dependencies. The accompanying comparison and verification records track the parts of the source theory still to be developed. No novelty claim is made.

Imported results are cited with their hypotheses. Questions and conjectures are distinguished from proved statements, and conditional arguments state the missing input.
