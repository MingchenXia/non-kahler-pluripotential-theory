\preface

This book studies singularities of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on compact Hermitian manifolds. Its central questions concern the information retained by an ordinary singularity type, an envelope or a family of multiplier ideals, and how that information controls measures, equations and holomorphic sections. Mingchen Xia's \emph{Singularities in global pluripotential theory} \cite{SGPT} provides the starting point: the comparison of these notions and their relation through $\mathcal I$-good singularities.

There are two changes of setting to distinguish. A closed background on a non-K\"ahler manifold still has local potentials. A nonclosed background requires a smooth local correction whose remainder enters products and integration by parts. This second change occurs even on a manifold admitting a K\"ahler metric. Local singularity theory remains available, but the global Monge--Amp\`ere mass can vary under smooth perturbations of the potential. The mass therefore need not be an invariant of the singularity type.

The Hopf examples give potentials with infinite mass, while other Hermitian examples have vanishing lower mass. Extended non-pluripolar measures allow infinite values; damping gives finite weighted measures whose limits are controlled by convergence in capacity. Gauduchon metrics supply a complementary linear tool: in a fixed background, their finite traces determine normalized potentials even when the nonlinear mass is infinite. These constructions make it possible to develop a general theory before imposing additional mass bounds.

Envelopes connect local singularity data with global positivity. Ordinary and multiplier-ideal envelopes retain different information, although they agree for analytic singularities. Analytic approximation gives a criterion for their equality in a Hermitian background. This supplies part of the $\mathcal I$-goodness theory; its metric and volume characterizations require separate arguments and hypotheses. Interpolation, test curves and restricted multiplier ideals then connect envelopes with rays and trace data. The distinction between a compatible system of traces and a potential realizing it remains essential.

The equation theory begins with smooth positive densities and nonzero nonnegative $L^p$ densities, $p>1$. In a Hermitian background these admit solutions up to a positive scalar, whereas an arbitrary finite non-pluripolar measure need not. Prescribing a singularity class imposes further conditions on the reference and the data. Degenerate densities can also admit distinct normalized smooth solutions. Existence, uniqueness of the scalar, uniqueness of the potential and selection by approximation are consequently separate questions. Gauduchon's adjoint equation describes the variation of the scalar; the resulting dual problem defines a finite variational volume through positive currents.

The geometric constructions ask which features of the singularities survive passage to sections, subspaces and modifications. Weighted section spaces and partial kernels relate multiplier ideals to growth and equilibrium. Divisorial classes and envelope systems retain data on different models, while examples separate their linear compatibility from control of nonlinear mass. Closed Bott--Chern classes and locally conformally K\"ahler geometry give further settings in which positivity must be compared with a differential constraint on the background.

The general tools for plurisubharmonic functions are collected in \cref{chap:framework}. Readers familiar with local pluripotential theory can return there as particular compactness, regularization and envelope arguments are used. The subsequent development keeps the singularity questions alongside the measure and equation theory. \Cref{part:mass-bounds} gathers the additional finite-volume comparison and relative-equation results under their bounded-mass or positive-lower-mass assumptions. A condition on one density or reference potential is kept distinct from a uniform property of the background.

The exposition assumes familiarity with complex manifolds, differential forms and currents. The remaining mathematical problems are formulated in \cref{chap:research-program}. Paper sources, their precise hypotheses and their relation to the proofs are discussed in the final historical chapter, \cref{chap:history}; book references also appear beside the arguments that use them. The appendix treats descent and resolution-independent constructions on unibranch compact complex spaces.

This AI-assisted working manuscript is prepared for mathematical review and makes no novelty claim. It is not an author-approved revision of the source book. The aim is a self-contained treatment apart from standard references and the source book; any remaining external input is identified as such. Conditional statements and unresolved questions are kept distinct from proved results.
