\preface

This book studies singularities of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on compact Hermitian manifolds and their role in measures, equations and holomorphic sections. Ordinary singularity types, envelopes and multiplier ideals retain different information about a potential. Comparing them is the central problem, following Mingchen Xia's \emph{Singularities in global pluripotential theory} \cite{SGPT} and its theory of $\mathcal I$-good singularities.

A closed background on a non-K\"ahler manifold still has local potentials. Allowing the background itself to be nonclosed changes the calculus: a smooth local correction reduces a quasi-plurisubharmonic function to a plurisubharmonic one, but its remainder enters products and integration by parts. This change occurs even on a manifold admitting a K\"ahler metric. In particular, the global Monge--Amp\`ere mass can vary under smooth perturbations of the potential and therefore need not be an invariant of its ordinary singularity type.

Envelopes give a way to compare singularities while retaining positivity in the chosen background. The ordinary and multiplier-ideal envelopes agree for analytic singularities. Envelopes of analytic approximants in slightly enlarged backgrounds decrease to the ideal envelope; their convergence to the ordinary envelope characterizes equality of the two. This develops one aspect of $\mathcal I$-goodness. In a Hermitian background, analytic classes are dense throughout the $L^1$ metric space of ideal-envelope representatives, so this density alone cannot characterize goodness. The relation with nonlinear mass requires further hypotheses.

The measure theory must accommodate the infinite mass of the Hopf examples and the vanishing lower mass of other Hermitian examples. Extended non-pluripolar products give measures with possibly infinite total mass. For nonpositive potentials, exponential damping gives finite weighted measures continuous under convergence in capacity. Gauduchon traces provide a complementary linear control: in a fixed background, they determine normalized potentials even when the nonlinear mass is infinite. These constructions precede the finite-volume comparison theory under additional mass bounds.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
In a Hermitian background, a nonzero nonnegative $L^p$ density, $p>1$, admits a continuous solution up to a positive scalar; smooth positive data give smooth solutions. An arbitrary finite non-pluripolar measure need not be solvable. Prescribing singularities imposes further conditions on the reference and the data, while degenerate densities can admit distinct normalized smooth solutions. Existence, scalar uniqueness, potential uniqueness and selection by approximation therefore require separate arguments.

Varying the density turns the normalization scalar into a functional. In the smooth positive regime, Gauduchon adjoint probabilities describe its variation. The dual problem leads to a finite variational volume through positive currents. These equation invariants also reveal a limit to singularity recovery: distinct divisorial types can have the same entire scalar functional and supporting probabilities, even though their normalized potentials differ. Stability of the functional and recovery of the singularities are thus different problems.

The geometric constructions ask what survives passage from potentials to rays, sections, subspaces and modifications. Interpolation and test curves organize families of envelopes. Restricted multiplier ideals encode traces; weighted section spaces and partial kernels relate these ideals to growth and equilibrium. Divisorial classes and envelope systems compare data on different models. Compatibility among these data and their realization by a single potential are distinct requirements. Closed Bott--Chern classes and locally conformally K\"ahler geometry bring additional differential constraints into this comparison.

The exposition assumes familiarity with complex manifolds, differential forms and currents. General plurisubharmonic tools are collected in \cref{chap:framework}; readers familiar with local theory can return there for the compactness, regularization and envelope arguments as needed. \Cref{part:mass-bounds} gathers the additional volume and relative-equation theory under bounded-mass or positive-lower-mass assumptions. Conditions on an individual density or reference remain attached to the results that use them. The appendix carries descent and resolution-independent measures to unibranch compact spaces and applies them to equations on projective cones.

The remaining mathematical problems are formulated in \cref{chap:research-program}. Paper sources and the precise dependencies of the proofs are discussed in the final historical chapter, \cref{chap:history}; book references also appear beside the arguments that use them. This AI-assisted working manuscript is prepared for mathematical review, makes no novelty claim and is not an author-approved revision of the source book. The aim is a self-contained treatment apart from standard references and the source book; remaining external inputs and conditional statements are identified in the text.
