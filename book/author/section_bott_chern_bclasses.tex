\section{Bott--Chern classes and closed currents}\label{sec:bc-closed-currents}

Bott--Chern cohomology records a closed $(1,1)$-current modulo $\ddc$ of global functions, and so separates the smooth background of the current from its potential. When $\theta$ is closed, subtracting the trace current from the pulled-back background gives a residual class on each model, and compatibility with pushforward organizes these classes into a b-divisor class in the sense of \cite[section \emph{The notion of b-divisors}]{SGPT}.

\index{Bott--Chern class!definition}For a complex manifold $Z$, let $\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb R}$ denote the bundle of real $(1,1)$-forms and define
\[
 H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R)
 =\frac{\{\beta\in C^\infty(Z,\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb R}):\mathrm{d}\beta=0\}}
        {\{\ddc f:f\in C^\infty(Z,\mathbb R)\}}.
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bc-one-one-current-description}
Replacing smooth forms and smooth functions in this quotient by real currents and real distributions gives the same space. In particular, every closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $Z$ can be written
\[
 T=\beta+\ddc w
\]
with $\beta$ smooth and closed and $w$ a global quasi-plurisubharmonic function.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The decomposition of \cref{lem:closed-current-global-potential} shows that the natural map from the smooth quotient to the current quotient is surjective, and it gives a quasi-plurisubharmonic potential for positive currents. The same lemma shows that two distributional potentials in such decompositions differ by a smooth function; equivalently, a distribution whose $\ddc$ is smooth is itself smooth. Hence two smooth forms that are identified in the current quotient are already identified in the smooth quotient, and the map is injective.
\end{proof}

\index{Pseudoeffective class}From now on, $Z$ is connected and compact. By the lemma, every closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ has a Bott--Chern class, written $\{T\}_{\mathrm{BC}}$; the class is \emph{pseudoeffective} if it contains a closed positive current. Pullback of classes is defined on smooth representatives. Pushforward of classes under a modification is defined on current representatives; it is well defined because pushforward commutes with $\ddc$, and by the lemma the resulting class is again a smooth Bott--Chern class. For a modification $g$ and every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta$ on its target, $g_*g^*\beta=\beta$ as currents, by the change of variables formula for the degree-one map $g$.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
A Gauduchon metric $\gamma$ defines a linear functional on this cohomology:
\[
 \ell_\gamma(\{T\}_{\mathrm{BC}})=\int_ZT\wedge\gamma^{\dim Z-1}.
\]
Integration by parts for currents shows that it does not depend on the representative. On a positive representative it is nonnegative, and it vanishes only if that representative is zero, by the strict positivity of the metric. In particular, the pseudoeffective cone contains no line: if $T_1,T_2\geq0$ represent opposite classes, their sum has zero Gauduchon pairing, hence $T_1=T_2=0$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
Gauduchon pairings detect closed curvature information that is invisible in de Rham cohomology. Consider the Hopf manifold $X_n$, $n\geq2$, with its Gauduchon form $\omega$ and covering coordinates $z\in\mathbb C^n\setminus\{0\}$, as in \cref{sec:hopf-homogeneous-sections}. The form $\alpha=\ddc\log|z|^2$ is exact as a de Rham form, because $\dc\log|z|^2$ descends to the quotient: the logarithm changes only by a constant under the contraction. Nevertheless, by the calculation in \cref{prop:hopf-bergman-exact},
\[
 \ell_\omega(\{\alpha\}_{\mathrm{BC}})
 =\int_{X_n}\alpha\wedge\omega^{n-1}=2(n-1)>0,
\]
so the Bott--Chern class of $\alpha$ is nonzero.

We return to the smooth compact modifications of the manifold $Y$ fixed at the beginning of the chapter. Two such modifications $\pi:Z\to Y$ and $\pi':Z'\to Y$ are compared through modifications $g:Z'\to Z$ with $\pi'=\pi\circ g$; we then say that $Z'$ dominates $Z$.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{definition}\label{def:bott-chern-bdivisor-class}\index{Bott--Chern class!b-divisor}
A \emph{Bott--Chern b-divisor class} over $Y$ is a family
\[
 \mathbb A=(\mathbb A_Z)_{\pi:Z\to Y},\qquad
 \mathbb A_Z\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R),
\]
indexed by the smooth compact modifications of $Y$ and compatible with pushforward: $g_*\mathbb A_{Z'}=\mathbb A_Z$ whenever $g:Z'\to Z$ is a modification over $Y$. It is \emph{pseudoeffective} if every $\mathbb A_Z$ is pseudoeffective. It is \emph{Cartier} if there are a model $Z$ and a class $a_Z\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R)$ such that $\mathbb A_{Z'}=g^*a_Z$ for every model $g:Z'\to Z$ dominating $Z$; the pair $(Z,a_Z)$ is then called a \emph{Cartier realization} of $\mathbb A$.
\end{definition}

When the models are K\"ahler, the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma identifies this cohomology with the real $(1,1)$-cohomology used in \cite[section \emph{The notion of b-divisors}]{SGPT}, and the definition agrees with the one given there.

We now return to the trace data fixed at the beginning of the chapter. When $\theta$ is closed, the common majorant of \cref{prop:original-background-trace-majorant} provides positive representatives of the residual classes.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:trace-bott-chern-bclass}\index{Bott--Chern class!residual trace}
If the ambient background $\theta$ is closed, the classes
\[
 \mathbb A_Z(u;Y,\theta)
 =\{\pi^*(\theta|_Y)-S_Z(u;Y)\}_{\mathrm{BC}}
\]
form a pseudoeffective Bott--Chern b-divisor class. They depend only on the ambient multiplier-ideal class of $u$ and the Bott--Chern class of $\theta|_Y$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:original-background-trace-majorant}, there is one $v$ on $Y$ such that
\[
 R_Z=\pi^*(\theta|_Y)+\ddc(v\circ\pi)-S_Z(u;Y)\geq0
\]
on every model. Since $\theta$ and $S_Z$ are closed, $R_Z$ is closed, and its class is the class in the statement. This proves pseudoeffectivity. Compatibility with pushforward follows from \cref{thm:trace-divisorial-model-compatibility} together with $g_*g^*(\theta_Z)=\theta_Z$. Finally, the trace data do not change under ambient multiplier-ideal equivalence, and replacing $\theta|_Y$ by $\theta|_Y+\ddc f$ with $f$ smooth changes no class; this proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
If $\theta$ is not closed, the same expression $R_Z$ is still a positive current, but
\[
 \mathrm{d}R_Z=\pi^*(\mathrm{d}\theta|_Y).
\]
It is therefore not closed in general and defines no Bott--Chern class. Classes whose representatives have a prescribed nonzero exterior derivative are treated in \cref{chap:nonclosed-bclasses}.

\section{The b-divisor class of a closed positive current}

For a closed positive current, the construction can be carried out with its own potential in place of the restricted ambient ideals. The trace coefficients then become the generic Lelong numbers of the pulled-back potential along divisors, and divisorial removal gives the positive residual currents directly.

In this section, $T$ is a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $Y$, written $T=\beta+\ddc w$ as in \cref{lem:bc-one-one-current-description}. On a smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$, the pullback $\pi^*T$ is the positive current defined by composing local plurisubharmonic potentials with $\pi$; since $\pi$ is surjective, these compositions are not identically $-\infty$. With $E$ ranging over the prime divisors on $Z$, set
\[
 N_Z(T)=\sum_E\nu(w\circ\pi,E)[E],\qquad
 \operatorname{Reg}_Z(T)=\pi^*T-N_Z(T).
\]
By divisorial removal (\cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal}), the second current is positive and closed. The following lemma identifies the coefficients of $N_Z(T)$ with trace numbers when the ambient manifold is $Y$ itself.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:self-trace-is-siu}
Let $w$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$, and take the ambient manifold in \eqref{eq:trace-profile} to be $Y$ itself. For every prime divisor $E$ on a smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$, the resulting self-trace satisfies
\[
 \tau_{Y,w}(E)=\nu(w\circ\pi,E).
\]
In particular, for the potential $w$ of the closed positive current $T$, one has $N_Z(T)=S_Z(w;Y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $w$ is not identically $-\infty$, its Lelong number vanishes at almost every point; hence $\nu(w,Y)=0$, and the self-trace is defined. Fix a divisor $E$ on a compact modification and apply \cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error} on a coordinate neighborhood of a point of its image in $Y$. With $\nu_E=\nu(w\circ\pi,E)$ and $A_E$ the log discrepancy of $E$, it gives
\[
 \max\{\nu_E-A_E/m,0\}
 \leq\frac1m\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(mw)
 \leq\nu_E.
\]
The limit along the integers defining $\tau_{Y,w}(E)$ is therefore $\nu_E$. Since all coefficients agree, the two divisorial currents are equal.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:closed-current-bclass}
The family
\[
 \mathbb D(T)_Z=\{\operatorname{Reg}_Z(T)\}_{\mathrm{BC}}
\]
is a pseudoeffective Bott--Chern b-divisor class. For closed positive currents $T_1,T_2$ and $a\geq0$,
\[
 \mathbb D(T_1+T_2)=\mathbb D(T_1)+\mathbb D(T_2),
 \qquad \mathbb D(aT_1)=a\mathbb D(T_1).
\]
On a K\"ahler manifold, with K\"ahler models, it agrees with the b-divisor class of the same current in \cite[subsection \emph{The b-divisors of currents}]{SGPT}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the lemma, $N_Z(T)=S_Z(w;Y)$, so \cref{thm:trace-divisorial-model-compatibility} gives $g_*N_{Z'}(T)=N_Z(T)$ for every modification $g:Z'\to Z$ over $Y$. Moreover, $g_*g^*(\pi^*T)=\pi^*T$. To see this, write the current as a smooth form plus $\ddc$ of a potential; the change of variables formula for the degree-one map $g$ applies both to the smooth form and to the locally integrable pulled-back potential, and pushforward commutes with $\ddc$. Therefore
\[
 g_*\operatorname{Reg}_{Z'}(T)=\operatorname{Reg}_Z(T).
\]
Taking classes proves compatibility, and positivity of the regular parts proves pseudoeffectivity. Generic Lelong numbers are additive under addition of positive currents and scale under multiplication by nonnegative constants. Hence so are the divisorial parts and the regular parts, which proves the two identities. In the K\"ahler case, the regular parts and, by the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma, their cohomology classes are exactly those of the construction cited in the statement.
\end{proof}

\section{Nef Cartier realizations}\label{sec:nef-cartier-realizations}

If $T$ has analytic singularities, a log resolution splits its pullback into a finite divisor and a current with bounded potentials. The bounded part acquires no divisorial component on higher models, so its class determines the whole b-divisor class there, and smooth regularization of its bounded potential shows that this class is nef.

\index{Nef class!definition}A class $a\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R)$ is \emph{nef} if, for one Hermitian metric $\gamma$, it has for every $\epsilon>0$ a smooth representative $\beta_\epsilon\geq-\epsilon\gamma$. Since any two Hermitian metrics are comparable, this does not depend on the metric. A Cartier b-divisor class is called \emph{nef} if it has a Cartier realization $(Z,a_Z)$ with $a_Z$ nef.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-closed-potential-nef}
On a connected compact complex manifold $Z$, let $\beta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $v$ a bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic function. If $R=\beta+\ddc v\geq0$ is closed, then $\{R\}_{\mathrm{BC}}$ is nef.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $R$ is closed, so is $\beta$. Fix a Hermitian metric $\gamma$. By \cref{prop:bounded-almost-regularization}, there are smooth functions $v_j$ and numbers $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$ such that
\[
 \beta+\ddc v_j\geq-\epsilon_j\gamma.
\]
These forms are closed representatives of $\{R\}_{\mathrm{BC}}$. Given $\epsilon>0$, choosing $j$ with $\epsilon_j\leq\epsilon$ gives the estimate required in the definition of nefness.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:analytic-current-nef-cartier}\index{Nef class!Cartier realization}
If the closed positive current $T$ has analytic singularities, then $\mathbb D(T)$ is Cartier and has a nef Cartier realization on a smooth compact modification.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Resolve the analytic singularity ideal, using the log-resolution theorem in \cite[section on analytic singularities, theorem on existence of log resolutions]{SGPT}. On a suitable smooth compact model $\pi:Z\to Y$,
\[
 \pi^*T=[D]+R,
\]
where $D$ is a finite effective rational divisor and $R$ is a closed positive current with locally bounded potentials. To obtain a global bounded potential, choose smooth metrics on the line bundles of the components of $D$ and subtract the corresponding logarithms of squared norms of the canonical sections from $w\circ\pi$. The remainder $v$ is globally bounded, and
\[
 R=\beta_Z+\ddc v
\]
for a smooth closed form $\beta_Z$. By the lemma, the class of $R$ is nef.

On every further compact modification $g:Z'\to Z$, the current $g^*R$ still has bounded local potentials and therefore zero Lelong numbers. Hence the divisorial part of $g^*\pi^*T$ is exactly $[g^*D]$, and its regular part is $g^*R$. It follows that $\mathbb D(T)_{Z'}=g^*\{R\}_{\mathrm{BC}}$. Thus $(Z,\{R\}_{\mathrm{BC}})$ is a nef Cartier realization.
\end{proof}

For a general current, analytic regularization gives closed approximants bounded below by $-\epsilon_m\gamma$. Adding $\epsilon_m\gamma$ makes them positive but, when $\gamma$ is not closed, changes their exterior derivative. The approximation then takes place in the larger spaces of \cref{chap:nonclosed-bclasses}, and does not by itself approximate the original Bott--Chern b-divisor class by nef Cartier classes.
