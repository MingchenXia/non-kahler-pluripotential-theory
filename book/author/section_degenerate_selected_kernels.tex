\section{Degenerate curvature and growing vanishing orders}
\label{sec:degenerate-selected-kernels}

In the compact models above, the vanishing orders are proportional to the tensor degree. To see the transition when the order grows more slowly, we pass to a model space of entire functions. There the local metric fixes the scale of the full kernel, while the selecting ideal creates a second, larger radius. Grouping the monomials according to the residues of their degrees expresses the transition at that radius through Poisson tails.

In this section $r$ and $q$ denote new integers. Fix an integer $r\geq1$. On $\mathbb C$, with coordinate $z=x+\mathrm{i}y$ and area measure $\mathrm{d}A=\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y$, put $\phi(z)=|z|^{2r}$. For a real $m>0$, let $H_{m,r}$ be the Hilbert space of entire functions with norm
\[
 \|f\|_{m,r}^2=\int_{\mathbb C}|f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\phi}\,\mathrm{d}A.
\]
\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
For an integer $q\geq0$, let $H_{m,r}^{(q)}$ consist of the functions vanishing to order at least $q$ at zero. Since the weight is bounded below on each fixed disk, the local submean and Cauchy inequalities bound point and jet evaluations, so this subspace is closed. Write $K_{m,r}^{(q)}(z,w)$ for its reproducing kernel, holomorphic in $z$ and antiholomorphic in $w$, and put
\[
 B_{m,r}^{(q)}(z)=\mathrm{e}^{-m\phi(z)}K_{m,r}^{(q)}(z,z).
\]
Thus the ideal $(z^q)$ selects the functions, while the smooth weight determines the norm. We use $\Gamma(a)=\int_0^\infty t^{a-1}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t$ for $a>0$.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:degenerate-fock-kernel}
For every $m>0$ and integer $q\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degenerate-fock-series}
 K_{m,r}^{(q)}(z,w)=\frac r\pi\sum_{k=q}^\infty
 \frac{m^{(k+1)/r}(z\bar w)^k}{\Gamma((k+1)/r)}.
\end{equation}
The series and all its derivatives converge uniformly on compact sets. For $\zeta\in\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degenerate-fock-scaling}
 m^{-1/r}B_{m,r}^{(q)}(m^{-1/(2r)}\zeta)
 =\frac r\pi\mathrm{e}^{-|\zeta|^{2r}}
       \sum_{k=q}^\infty\frac{|\zeta|^{2k}}{\Gamma((k+1)/r)}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, if $m$ tends to infinity through positive integers and $q=q_m\to\infty$, the left side tends to zero uniformly on compact $\zeta$-sets.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The monomials are orthogonal by rotational invariance. For every integer $k\geq0$, polar integration with radial variable $\rho$ and the substitution $t=m\rho^{2r}$ give
\[
 \|z^k\|_{m,r}^2=2\pi\int_0^\infty\rho^{2k+1}\mathrm{e}^{-m\rho^{2r}}\,\mathrm{d}\rho
 =\frac\pi r m^{-(k+1)/r}\Gamma((k+1)/r).
\]
For an entire function $f(z)=\sum_{k\geq0}a_kz^k$, Parseval's identity on circles and Tonelli's theorem yield
$\|f\|_{m,r}^2=\sum_{k\geq0}|a_k|^2\|z^k\|_{m,r}^2$.
Thus the normalized monomials form an orthonormal basis, and those with $k\geq q$ form an orthonormal basis of the selected subspace. This proves \eqref{eq:degenerate-fock-series}. By Stirling's formula, the reciprocal Gamma coefficients decay faster than every fixed geometric progression, even after multiplication by any fixed power of $k$; this gives uniform convergence on compact sets together with all derivatives. Substituting $z=w=m^{-1/(2r)}\zeta$ gives \eqref{eq:degenerate-fock-scaling}. On a disk $|\zeta|\leq R$ of fixed radius, the series in \eqref{eq:degenerate-fock-scaling} is bounded by the tail, from the index $q$ on, of the convergent series with $|\zeta|$ replaced by $R$. This tail tends to zero as $q\to\infty$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For $r=1$ and $q=0$ the density is the constant $m/\pi$. For general $r$ and $q=0$ its value at zero is $rm^{1/r}/(\pi\Gamma(1/r))$, whereas it vanishes at zero when $q>0$. The scale $m^{-1/(2r)}$ therefore describes fixed vanishing orders, but a growing order requires a further rescaling.

\subsection{A uniform tail estimate}
\label{sec:degenerate-kernel-tail}

Grouping the monomial degrees by their residues modulo $r$ expresses the kernel through weighted Poisson tails. The estimate of Gamma ratios below removes those weights, with an error independent of the vanishing threshold.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Write $\mathbb P$ and $\mathbb E$ for probability and expectation. For $T>0$, let $N_T$ be a Poisson random variable with parameter $T$, so that
$\mathbb P(N_T=\ell)=\mathrm{e}^{-T}T^\ell/\ell!$ for integers $\ell\geq0$. For a real $0<\alpha\leq1$, define
\[
 w_{\alpha,T}(\ell)=T^{\alpha-1}\frac{\Gamma(\ell+1)}{\Gamma(\ell+\alpha)}.
\]
All constants in the next lemma may depend on $\alpha$, but not on $T$.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:poisson-gamma-weight}
There is a constant $C_\alpha>0$ such that, for $T\geq1$,
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl|w_{\alpha,T}(N_T)-1\bigr|\leq C_\alpha T^{-1/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Recall that $\mathbb E N_T=T$ and $\operatorname{Var}(N_T)=T$. Stirling's formula, applied to the ratio of Gamma functions, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{\Gamma(\ell+1)}{\Gamma(\ell+\alpha)}
 &=\ell^{1-\alpha}(1+O_\alpha(\ell^{-1}))&&\quad(\ell\to\infty),\\
 \frac{\Gamma(\ell+1)}{\Gamma(\ell+\alpha)}
 &\leq C_\alpha(\ell+1)^{1-\alpha}&&\quad(\ell\geq0).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second bound follows from the first after the constant is increased to account for finitely many small integers. Since $0\leq1-\alpha<1$, it implies
\[
 w_{\alpha,T}(\ell)\leq C_\alpha\left(1+\frac{\ell+1}{T}\right),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(w_{\alpha,T}(N_T)+1)^2\leq C_\alpha
 \quad(T\geq1).
\]
For $T\geq2$, consider the event $E_T=\{|N_T-T|\leq T/2\}$. On this event, the expansion of the Gamma ratio and the bounded derivative of $s\mapsto s^{1-\alpha}$ on $[1/2,3/2]$ give
\[
 |w_{\alpha,T}(N_T)-1|
 \leq C_\alpha\left(\frac{|N_T-T|}{T}+\frac1T\right).
\]
By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, the expectation of the right side is at most $C_\alpha T^{-1/2}$. On the complement, Chebyshev's inequality gives $\mathbb P(E_T^c)\leq4/T$, and the preceding second-moment bound together with the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives the same estimate for the contribution of $E_T^c$. Enlarging the constant handles $1\leq T\leq2$.
\end{proof}

For integers $q\geq0$ and $0\leq s<r$, put
\[
 L_s(q)=\left\lceil\frac{q-s}{r}\right\rceil\geq0.
\]
If a monomial degree is written as $k=r\ell+s$, the condition $k\geq q$ becomes $\ell\geq L_s(q)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:degenerate-kernel-poisson}
There is a constant $C_r>0$, depending only on $r$, such that for $m>0$, $z\ne0$ and $T=m|z|^{2r}\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degenerate-poisson-comparison}
 \left|\frac{\pi B_{m,r}^{(q)}(z)}{mr^2|z|^{2r-2}}
 -\frac1r\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\mathbb P\{N_T\geq L_s(q)\}\right|
 \leq C_r T^{-1/2}
 \quad(q\geq0).
\end{equation}
In particular, the error is uniform in the vanishing order $q$.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Let $\rho=|z|>0$ and put $\alpha_s=(s+1)/r$ for integers $0\leq s<r$. Grouping the positive series \eqref{eq:degenerate-fock-series} by the residues $s$ and using $T=m\rho^{2r}$, we obtain the exact identity
\[
 \begin{split}
 B_{m,r}^{(q)}(z)
 &=\frac r{\pi\rho^2}\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}
     \mathrm{e}^{-T}\sum_{\ell\geq L_s(q)}
           \frac{T^{\ell+\alpha_s}}{\Gamma(\ell+\alpha_s)}\\
 &=\frac{rT}{\pi\rho^2}\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}
     \mathbb E\bigl[w_{\alpha_s,T}(N_T)
                         \mathbf1_{\{N_T\geq L_s(q)\}}\bigr].
 \end{split}
\]
Divide by $mr^2\rho^{2r-2}/\pi=r^2T/(\pi\rho^2)$ and apply \cref{lem:poisson-gamma-weight} to each of the finitely many residues. Since the indicators are bounded by one independently of their thresholds, the resulting error is uniform in $q$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The limiting envelope and the transition scale}
\label{sec:degenerate-kernel-limit}

A vanishing order proportional to $m$ removes the kernel density inside a fixed circle. Inside that circle the limit of the potentials is logarithmic, and outside it agrees with the smooth weight. Throughout this subsection, $m$ tends to infinity through positive integers. For $\lambda>0$, set $q_m=\lfloor m\lambda\rfloor$ and $R_\lambda=(\lambda/r)^{1/(2r)}$. Define
\[
 V_\lambda(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 |z|^{2r},&|z|\geq R_\lambda,\\
 \lambda\log(|z|^2/R_\lambda^2)+\lambda/r,&0<|z|<R_\lambda,\\
 -\infty,&z=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
In the following theorem, $\delta_0$ denotes the unit point mass at zero. Recall that in dimension one the non-pluripolar measure of a subharmonic function discards the mass carried by its polar set.

\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:degenerate-growing-kernel-limit}
One has
\[
 \frac1m\log K_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z,z)\longrightarrow V_\lambda(z)
\]
pointwise with extended values and in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb C)$. The kernel measures converge locally in total variation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degenerate-kernel-measure-limit}
 \frac1m B_{m,r}^{(q_m)}\,\mathrm{d}A
 \longrightarrow\mathbf1_{\{|z|>R_\lambda\}}\ddc\phi.
\end{equation}
The function $V_\lambda$ is the largest subharmonic function $v$ on $\mathbb C$ such that $v\leq\phi$ and $v(z)\leq\lambda\log|z|^2+C$ near zero for some constant $C$. Its curvature current is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degenerate-envelope-current}
 \ddc V_\lambda=\lambda\delta_0+
                  \mathbf1_{\{|z|>R_\lambda\}}\ddc\phi.
\end{equation}
Thus the limit in \eqref{eq:degenerate-kernel-measure-limit} is the non-pluripolar measure of this envelope, rather than its full distributional Laplacian.
\end{theorem}
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $z\ne0$, put $\rho=|z|$ and $T=m\rho^{2r}$. Then $L_s(q_m)/T\to\lambda/(r\rho^{2r})$, and this limit differs from one when $\rho\neq R_\lambda$. Chebyshev's inequality and \eqref{eq:degenerate-poisson-comparison} therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degenerate-density-pointwise}
 \frac1m B_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z)\longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
 0,&0<\rho<R_\lambda,\\
 r^2\rho^{2r-2}/\pi,&\rho>R_\lambda.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The selected density is at most the full density, that is, the density with $q=0$. When $T\geq1$, the same comparison with $q=0$ bounds it by $C_r m\rho^{2r-2}$; when $T\leq1$, \eqref{eq:degenerate-fock-scaling} with $q=0$ bounds it by $C_r m^{1/r}\leq C_r m$. Thus $m^{-1}B_{m,r}^{(q_m)}$ is uniformly bounded on each compact set. Since the circle $\rho=R_\lambda$ and the origin have zero area, dominated convergence and
$\ddc\phi=(r^2/\pi)|z|^{2r-2}\mathrm{d}A$
prove \eqref{eq:degenerate-kernel-measure-limit}.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
To compute the limit of the potentials inside the circle, fix $0<\rho<R_\lambda$ and write
\[
 d_{m,k}=\frac{m^{(k+1)/r}\rho^{2k}}{\Gamma((k+1)/r)}
 \quad(k\geq q_m).
\]
The estimate of Gamma ratios from Stirling's formula gives
\[
 \frac{d_{m,k+1}}{d_{m,k}}
 \leq\left(\frac{rm\rho^{2r}}{q_m+1}\right)^{1/r}
           \left(1+\frac{C_r}{q_m+1}\right)
 \quad(k\geq q_m)
\]
for all sufficiently large $m$. The right side tends to $(r\rho^{2r}/\lambda)^{1/r}<1$. Hence the sum of these terms lies between $d_{m,q_m}$ and a fixed multiple of that term, and Stirling's formula yields
\[
 \frac1m\log K_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z,z)
 \longrightarrow\lambda\log\rho^2+
                 \frac\lambda r\bigl(1-\log(\lambda/r)\bigr)
 =V_\lambda(z).
\]
For $\rho>R_\lambda$, the limit of the densities in \eqref{eq:degenerate-density-pointwise} is positive. Hence $m^{-1}\log B_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z)\to0$, and therefore $m^{-1}\log K_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z,z)$ tends to $\phi(z)$.

At $\rho=R_\lambda$, put $k_m=\max\{q_m,\lfloor rm\rho^{2r}\rfloor\}$. Then $k_m/m\to r\rho^{2r}=\lambda$. The single monomial of degree $k_m$ and Stirling's formula give
$\liminf m^{-1}\log K_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z,z)\geq\phi(z)$,
and the bound for the full kernel already proved gives the reverse inequality for the upper limit. At zero the selected kernel vanishes for all sufficiently large $m$. This proves pointwise convergence everywhere.

For local $L^1$ convergence, the bounds for the full kernel give a uniform upper bound for $m^{-1}\log K_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z,z)$ on each compact set. The single selected monomial of degree $q_m$ gives the lower bound
\[
 \frac1m\log K_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z,z)
 \geq c_m+\frac{q_m}{m}\log|z|^2,
\]
where $c_m$ is bounded as $m\to\infty$, again by Stirling's formula. Since $0\leq q_m/m\leq\lambda$, the negative part on a fixed compact set is bounded by a constant plus $2\lambda|\log|z||$ near zero, which is locally integrable. Dominated convergence proves the $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ assertion.

We turn to the envelope. The two radial expressions for $V_\lambda$ and their first radial derivatives agree at $R_\lambda$, because $rR_\lambda^{2r}=\lambda$. Away from zero and that circle, direct differentiation proves \eqref{eq:degenerate-envelope-current}. Since the first derivatives match, the circle carries no measure, and $\ddc\log|z|^2=\delta_0$ gives the atom at zero. The right side is positive, so $V_\lambda$ is subharmonic. For $t=|z|^{2r}/(\lambda/r)<1$, the inequality $\log t+1\leq t$ proves $V_\lambda\leq\phi$ inside the circle; outside it there is equality.

Finally, let $v$ be subharmonic with $v\leq\phi$ and $v\leq\lambda\log|z|^2+C$ near zero. On the punctured disk $0<|z|<R_\lambda$, the function $v-\lambda\log|z|^2$ is subharmonic and bounded above near zero, so it extends subharmonically across zero. On the boundary circle it is at most $\lambda/r-\lambda\log R_\lambda^2$, since $v\leq\phi=\lambda/r$ there. The maximum principle gives $v\leq V_\lambda$ inside the disk, and outside the disk this follows from $v\leq\phi$. Since $V_\lambda$ itself satisfies the conditions, it is the largest such function. Removing the atom at the pole gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The measures in this theorem have infinite total mass on $\mathbb C$, so their convergence is asserted only on compact sets. The weighted potentials $m^{-1}\log B_{m,r}^{(q_m)}$ converge to $V_\lambda-\phi$. The radius $R_\lambda$ is created by the selecting ideal even when $r=1$, whereas $r>1$ also reflects the curvature zero at the origin.

For any integer sequence $q_m\to\infty$, the transition at the boundary circle is governed by the central limit theorem for the same Poisson tails. Let $\Phi$ be the standard Gaussian distribution function,
\[
 \Phi(\tau)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}
                        \mathrm{e}^{-s^2/2}\,\mathrm{d}s
 \quad(\tau\in\mathbb R).
\]

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:degenerate-kernel-transition}\index{Bergman kernel!Gaussian transition}
Let $(q_m)$ be a sequence of nonnegative integers with $q_m\to\infty$, and fix a real $\tau$. Choose $z_m\ne0$ with
\[
 m|z_m|^{2r}=\frac{q_m}{r}+\tau\sqrt{\frac{q_m}{r}},
\]
which is positive for all sufficiently large $m$. Then
\[
 \frac{\pi B_{m,r}^{(q_m)}(z_m)}{mr^2|z_m|^{2r-2}}
 \longrightarrow\Phi(\tau).
\]
In particular, this applies both when $q_m/m\to\lambda>0$ and when $q_m/m\to0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first recall the central limit theorem for Poisson variables in the form used here. For a fixed real $M>0$, Stirling's formula for $\ell!$ gives, uniformly over integers $\ell$ with $|\ell-T|\leq M\sqrt T$,
\[
 \mathbb P(N_T=\ell)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi T}}
   \exp\left(-\frac{(\ell-T)^2}{2T}\right)
           (1+O_M(T^{-1/2}))\quad(T\to\infty).
\]
Indeed, insert the expansion of the factorial in $\mathrm{e}^{-T}T^\ell/\ell!$ and expand the logarithm in powers of $(\ell-T)/T$; the cubic remainder is $O_M(T^{-1/2})$. Riemann sums over bounded intervals therefore converge to Gaussian integrals, and Chebyshev's bound
$\mathbb P(|N_T-T|>M\sqrt T)\leq M^{-2}$
then allows $M\to\infty$. Consequently, if real thresholds $a_T$ satisfy $(a_T-T)/\sqrt T\to a\in\mathbb R$, then
$\mathbb P(N_T\geq a_T)\to1-\Phi(a)$;
rounding changes the normalized threshold by at most $T^{-1/2}$, and $\Phi$ is continuous.

Set $T_m=m|z_m|^{2r}$. Then $T_m\to\infty$ and, for every integer $0\leq s<r$,
\[
 \frac{L_s(q_m)-T_m}{\sqrt{T_m}}\longrightarrow-\tau.
\]
Apply the preceding Poisson limit in \eqref{eq:degenerate-poisson-comparison}. The uniform error there tends to zero, and each of the finitely many probabilities tends to $1-\Phi(-\tau)=\Phi(\tau)$.
\end{proof}

For a sequence $q_m\to\infty$, put $R_m=(q_m/(rm))^{1/(2r)}$. At fixed $\tau$, the radii in \cref{prop:degenerate-kernel-transition} satisfy
\[
 |z_m|=R_m\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2\sqrt{rq_m}}+O_{r,\tau}(q_m^{-1})\right).
\]
If $q_m=o(m)$, these radii tend to zero, while their ratio to the scale $m^{-1/(2r)}$ of the full kernel tends to infinity; when $r>1$, they shrink to a curvature zero. To transfer this transition to a compact manifold, both the local comparison of norms and the error of the global extension must be uniform in the growing vanishing order. The next section establishes both estimates on a curve.
