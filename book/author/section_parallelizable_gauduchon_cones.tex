\subsection{Invariant backgrounds on compact Lie-group quotients}
\label{sec:parallelizable-gauduchon-cones}

On a compact quotient of a complex Lie group, the backgrounds with constant coefficients in an invariant coframe form a cone of nonclosed backgrounds on which everything can be computed explicitly. With respect to the normalized invariant volume, the root volume is the $n$th root of a determinant, the minimizing metric is the background itself, and the Hessian metric is the trace metric on positive Hermitian matrices. Its unit-volume hypersurface is complete, so the boundary of the cone of coefficient matrices lies at infinite distance after normalization.

Let $G$ be a connected complex Lie group of dimension $n\geq2$, and let $X=\Gamma\backslash G$ be compact, where the discrete subgroup $\Gamma$ acts on the left. Choose a left-invariant holomorphic coframe $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$, which descends to $X$. For a Hermitian matrix $A$ put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \theta_A=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k}A_{jk}\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_k,
 \qquad h_0=\theta_I,\\
 H=\int_Xh_0^n,\qquad \mu=\frac{h_0^n}{H}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here $\operatorname{Herm}(n)$ is the real vector space of Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices, and $A>0$ (respectively $A\geq0$) means that $A$ is positive definite (respectively semidefinite). Fix a real linear subspace $W\subset\operatorname{Herm}(n)$ containing a positive matrix, and write $V_W=\{\theta_A:A\in W\}$. Throughout this subsection, the probability form is the normalized invariant volume $\mu$ just defined.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:parallelizable-gaud-volume}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!invariant backgrounds}
The backgrounds in $V_W\cap\mathscr P$ are exactly the forms $\theta_A$ with $A\in W$ and $A>0$. Moreover,
\[
 V_W\cap\ddc C^\infty(X,\R)=\{0\}.
\]
Every positive $\theta_A$ is balanced. Its root volume and its unique minimizing Gauduchon metric normalized by $D_\mu=1$ are given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallelizable-gaud-volume}
 \lambda_\mu(\theta_A)=(H\det A)^{1/n},\qquad
 \gamma_A=\frac{\theta_A}{\lambda_\mu(\theta_A)},\qquad
 \gamma_A^n=\mu.
\end{equation}
On this coefficient cone the Hessian metric is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallelizable-gaud-hessian}
 g_A(U,Z)=\frac1n\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}UA^{-1}Z),
 \qquad U,Z\in W.
\end{equation}

For $A\in W$, there is a real distribution $u$ with $\theta_A+\ddc u\geq0$ exactly when $A\geq0$. The closure of $\{A\in W:A>0\}$ is $W\cap\{A\geq0\}$, and each of its points $A$ is represented by the smooth semipositive form $\theta_A$. For $A=0$, the only positive current $\ddc u$ is zero. If $G$ is nonabelian, every positive background in this cone is nonclosed, and adding a smooth $\ddc$ term never makes it closed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Invariant averages and balancedness.}
Let $Z_1,\ldots,Z_n$ be the invariant holomorphic vector fields dual to $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$, and set $\Upsilon=\alpha_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_n$. This holomorphic top-degree form and its conjugate are closed. For an invariant holomorphic vector field $Z$, evaluation on the invariant frame gives
\[
 \mathcal L_Z\Upsilon=-\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}Z)\Upsilon,
 \qquad \mathcal L_Z\bar\Upsilon=0.
\]
The second identity also follows from Cartan's formula. Since $h_0^n$ is a positive constant multiple of the appropriately oriented form $\Upsilon\wedge\bar\Upsilon$, Stokes' theorem on compact $X$ gives
\[
 0=\int_X\mathcal L_Zh_0^n=-H\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}Z).
\]
Thus the fields $Z_j$ and their conjugates have zero divergence with respect to $\mu$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallelizable-zero-mean}
 \int_XZ_jf\,\mu=\int_X\bar Z_jf\,\mu=0
 \qquad(f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb C)).
\end{equation}
Cartan's formula also gives $\mathrm d(\iota_{Z_j}\Upsilon)=0$. These contractions span the invariant $(n-1,0)$-forms. The form $\theta_A^{n-1}$ is a constant linear combination of their wedges with conjugates, so $\mathrm d\theta_A^{n-1}=0$.

The holomorphic fields commute with the antiholomorphic fields, and $\mathrm d\alpha_j$ has type $(2,0)$. The coefficient matrix of $\ddc u$ is therefore $(Z_j\bar Z_k u)$. For smooth $u$ its entries have $\mu$-mean zero by \eqref{eq:parallelizable-zero-mean}, so the matrix of $\mu$-means of the coefficients of $\theta_A+\ddc u$ is $A$. If $\theta_A+\ddc u>0$, averaging the positive coefficient matrix gives $A>0$. The converse follows by taking $u=0$. The same average shows that $V_W$ contains no nonzero smooth $\ddc$ direction.

For a distribution $u$, the adjoints of $Z_j$ and $\bar Z_k$ with respect to $\mu$ annihilate constants, so the mean of each $Z_j\bar Z_k u$ is still zero. The coefficients of a positive current in this global frame form a positive matrix of measures, whose matrix of total masses is semipositive; for $\theta_A+\ddc u\geq0$ this gives $A\geq0$. Conversely, $\theta_A$ itself is a positive current when $A\geq0$. If $A=0$, the positive trace measure of $\ddc u$ has total mass zero, and polarization then makes the entire current zero. Finally, for a fixed positive $A_+\in W$, the matrices $A+\varepsilon A_+$ with $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ approximate every semipositive $A\in W$ from the positive cone. This proves the closure assertion.

\emph{The quotient and its equality case.}
Put $Q=H\det A$, so that $\theta_A^n=Q\mu$. For every smooth positive Hermitian form $\gamma$, the matrix arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 \frac{\theta_A\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}{\gamma^n}
 =\frac1n\operatorname{tr}_\gamma\theta_A
 \geq\left(\frac{\theta_A^n}{\gamma^n}\right)^{1/n}.
\]
Integration yields $\int_X\theta_A\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq Q^{1/n}D_\mu(\gamma)$, which bounds every Gauduchon quotient in \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} from below. The balanced, hence Gauduchon, metric $\gamma=\theta_A$ has $D_\mu(\theta_A)=Q^{(n-1)/n}$ and attains equality. This proves the formula for $\lambda_\mu(\theta_A)$.

For a minimizing Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, the continuous nonnegative difference of the two sides of the pointwise inequality has integral zero. Pointwise equality then forces $\gamma=f\theta_A$ for a smooth positive function $f$. Set $q=f^{n-1}$ and $T=\theta_A^{n-1}$. Since $\mathrm dT=0$, the Gauduchon equation becomes $\ddc q\wedge T=0$, and
\[
 0=\int_Xq\ddc q\wedge T=-\int_X\mathrm dq\wedge\dc q\wedge T.
\]
The last integrand is nonnegative and vanishes exactly where $\mathrm dq=0$. Thus $f$ is constant. As $n\geq2$ and
\[
 D_\mu(c\theta_A)=c^{n-1}\lambda_\mu(\theta_A)^{n-1},
\]
normalization determines $c=\lambda_\mu(\theta_A)^{-1}$. This proves the unique minimizer and its volume identity.

Differentiating $-\log\lambda_\mu(\theta_A)=-(\log H+\log\det A)/n$ twice gives \eqref{eq:parallelizable-gaud-hessian}. The corresponding squared norm of $U$ is $\|A^{-1/2}UA^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm F}^2/n$, which is positive for $U\ne0$; here $\|B\|_{\mathrm F}^2=\operatorname{tr}(B^*B)$ denotes the squared Frobenius norm.

Finally,
\[
 \partial\theta_A=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k}A_{jk}\,\mathrm d\alpha_j\wedge\bar\alpha_k.
\]
For invertible $A$, the vanishing of this form forces every $\mathrm d\alpha_j=0$, by independence of the conjugate coframe, and the Lie algebra would then be abelian. Since adding $\ddc u$ does not change $\mathrm d\theta_A$, this proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive-matrix-hessian-distance}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!matrix distance}
On the cone of all positive definite Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices, the Riemannian distance of the metric \eqref{eq:parallelizable-gaud-hessian} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-matrix-hessian-distance}
 d_{\mathrm{full}}(A,B)=\frac1{\sqrt n}
       \|\log(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\|_{\mathrm F}.
\end{equation}
For endpoints with equal determinant, this is also the intrinsic distance in the hypersurface of all positive definite matrices with that determinant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the trace formula, the congruence $M\mapsto P^*MP$ by a fixed invertible matrix $P$ is an isometry. Taking $P=A^{-1/2}$, we may assume that the endpoints are $I$ and $C=A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}>0$. The curve $\exp(t\log C)$, $0\leq t\leq1$, has constant speed $\|\log C\|_{\mathrm F}/\sqrt n$, which gives the upper bound for the distance.

For the reverse inequality, diagonalization gives the convergent resolvent formula for the matrix logarithm and its derivative
\begin{align*}
 \log M&=\int_0^\infty\bigl((1+s)^{-1}I-(M+sI)^{-1}\bigr)\,\mathrm ds,\\
 D\log_M[U]&=\int_0^\infty(M+sI)^{-1}U(M+sI)^{-1}\,\mathrm ds.
\end{align*}
On a neighborhood of $M$ where the spectrum is bounded below by a positive constant, differentiation under the integral sign is justified by an integrable bound. In a unitary basis diagonalizing $M$, with eigenvalues $d_i>0$, the derivative has entries $c_{ij}U_{ij}$, where
\[
 c_{ij}=\frac{\log d_i-\log d_j}{d_i-d_j}\quad(d_i\ne d_j),
 \qquad c_{ij}=\frac1{d_i}\quad(d_i=d_j).
\]
Writing $d_i/d_j=\mathrm e^{2s}$ gives $\sqrt{d_id_j}|c_{ij}|=|s/\sinh s|\leq1$, including the limiting value at $s=0$. Hence
\[
 \|D\log_M[U]\|_{\mathrm F}
 \leq\|M^{-1/2}UM^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm F}.
\]
Applying this along a piecewise $C^1$ path of positive matrices from $I$ to $C$ and integrating, we bound its length below by $\|\log C\|_{\mathrm F}/\sqrt n$. This proves the distance formula. In terms of the original endpoints, the length-minimizing curve is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-matrix-exponential-path}
 M(t)=A^{1/2}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})^tA^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Its determinant is $(\det A)^{1-t}(\det B)^t$, so for endpoints with equal determinant it stays in the fixed-determinant hypersurface.
\end{proof}

Write $\lambda(A)=\lambda_\mu(\theta_A)$ and
\[
 S_W=\{A\in W:A>0,\ H\det A=1\}.
\]
The radial derivative of $\lambda$ is positive, so $S_W$ is a smooth hypersurface of the cone $\{A\in W:A>0\}$. We write $d_W$ for the intrinsic path distance of $S_W$ with the metric induced by \eqref{eq:parallelizable-gaud-hessian}.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:parallelizable-unit-volume-completion}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!complete invariant cone}
The metric space $(S_W,d_W)$ is complete and proper, and a sequence in $S_W$ is Cauchy exactly when its coefficient matrices converge to a positive matrix in $S_W$. For $A,B\in S_W$,
\begin{align}
 d_W(A,B)&\geq\frac1{\sqrt n}
       \|\log(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\|_{\mathrm F},\label{eq:parallelizable-intrinsic-lower}\\
 d_W(A,B)&\leq\frac{\|A-B\|_{\mathrm F}}
       {\sqrt n\min\{\lambda_{\min}(A),\lambda_{\min}(B)\}}.
       \label{eq:parallelizable-intrinsic-upper}
\end{align}
Here $\lambda_{\min}$ denotes the smallest eigenvalue. The normalized minimizing metrics of \eqref{eq:parallelizable-gaud-volume} satisfy, pointwise on $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallelizable-minimizer-order}
 \mathrm e^{-\sqrt n d_W(A,B)}\gamma_A
 \leq\gamma_B\leq\mathrm e^{\sqrt n d_W(A,B)}\gamma_A.
\end{equation}
In particular, the completion of $S_W$ adds no point, and no point of the boundary of the coefficient cone is a limit of a Cauchy sequence.

If $A_j>0$ in $W$ converges to a nonzero singular semipositive $A_0$, then $\widehat A_j=A_j/\lambda(A_j)$ lies in $S_W$ and, for fixed $A_\star\in S_W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallelizable-singular-boundary-rate}
 d_W(A_\star,\widehat A_j)
 \geq\frac{|\log\det A_j|}{n\sqrt n}+O(1)
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\end{equation}
By contrast, a sequence of coefficient matrices tending to zero can normalize to a point of $S_W$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Paths in $W$.}
Every path in $S_W$ is a path in the hypersurface of all positive matrices with $H\det A=1$, so \cref{lem:positive-matrix-hessian-distance} gives \eqref{eq:parallelizable-intrinsic-lower}. To obtain an upper bound by paths inside $W$, put
\[
 U=B-A,\qquad C(t)=A+tU,\qquad N(t)=\frac{C(t)}{\lambda(C(t))}.
\]
Since the positive cone is convex and $W$ is linear, $N(t)$ stays in $S_W$ and joins $A$ to $B$. With $K=C^{-1/2}UC^{-1/2}$, differentiation gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 g_N(N',N')=\frac1n\operatorname{tr}(K^2)
                 -\frac1{n^2}(\operatorname{tr}K)^2
 \leq\frac{\|U\|_{\mathrm F}^2}{nm^2},\\
 m=\min\{\lambda_{\min}(A),\lambda_{\min}(B)\}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Indeed $C(t)\geq mI$ and $(\log\lambda(C))'=\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}U)/n$. Integrating proves \eqref{eq:parallelizable-intrinsic-upper}, and shows in particular that $S_W$ is connected.

Put $d=d_W(A,B)$. Every eigenvalue of $A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}$ has a logarithm of absolute value at most $\|\log(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\|_{\mathrm F}\leq\sqrt n\,d$, by \eqref{eq:parallelizable-intrinsic-lower}. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallelizable-coefficient-order}
 \mathrm e^{-\sqrt n d}A\leq B\leq\mathrm e^{\sqrt n d}A.
\end{equation}
Since $\gamma_A=\theta_A$ on $S_W$, this proves \eqref{eq:parallelizable-minimizer-order}.

\emph{Cauchy sequences and bounded balls.}
For a Cauchy sequence $(A_j)$ in $(S_W,d_W)$, comparison with one fixed element of the sequence, using \eqref{eq:parallelizable-coefficient-order}, gives $mI\leq A_j\leq MI$ with fixed positive $m,M$. Comparing two elements then gives
\[
 \|A_j-A_k\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq M\bigl(\mathrm e^{\sqrt n d_W(A_j,A_k)}-1\bigr),
\]
where $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is the operator norm. The coefficient matrices therefore form a Cauchy sequence. Their limit lies in the closed linear space $W$, is positive, and satisfies $H\det A=1$, and the upper bound \eqref{eq:parallelizable-intrinsic-upper} proves convergence to that point in $d_W$. Conversely, coefficient convergence to a positive point gives convergence in $d_W$ by the same bound. This proves completeness and the Cauchy criterion. The minimizing forms then converge in $C^\infty$, because their coefficients multiply a fixed smooth coframe.

By these estimates, $d_W$ induces the topology of coefficient convergence. A closed ball of radius $R$ around $A_\star$ is contained in the compact matrix interval
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathrm e^{-\sqrt nR}A_\star\leq A\leq\mathrm e^{\sqrt nR}A_\star,\\
 A\in W,\qquad H\det A=1.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The ball is closed in that interval, hence compact. This proves properness. In particular, distances from a fixed reference point are bounded exactly when the eigenvalues relative to that point are bounded above and away from zero.

\emph{The coefficient boundary.}
Write $\lambda_{\max}$ for the largest eigenvalue. If $A_j\to A_0$ with $A_0\geq0$ singular and nonzero, then $\lambda(A_j)\to0$ while $\lambda_{\max}(A_j)$ has a positive limit, so the largest eigenvalue of $\widehat A_j$ tends to infinity. Applying \eqref{eq:parallelizable-coefficient-order} to $A_\star,\widehat A_j$ gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_W(A_\star,\widehat A_j)
 &\geq\frac1{\sqrt n}\log
       \frac{\lambda_{\max}(A_j)}{\lambda(A_j)\lambda_{\max}(A_\star)}\\
 &=\frac{|\log\det A_j|}{n\sqrt n}+O(1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The error term is bounded because $A_0\ne0$. This proves \eqref{eq:parallelizable-singular-boundary-rate}. For comparison, $A_j=t_jA_\star$ with $t_j\downarrow0$ normalizes to $A_\star$ for every $j$; its coefficient limit, the zero matrix, gives no new point of the completion.
\end{proof}

By \cref{prop:gaud-volume-metric-product}, the whole cone $\{\theta_A:A\in W,\ A>0\}$ is then complete as well. The argument uses the invariant probability and the linearity of $W$; in other geometries the unit-volume hypersurface can have a boundary at finite distance, as in \cref{ex:gaud-metric-blowup}.

The length-minimizing path \eqref{eq:positive-matrix-exponential-path} need not stay in a proper subspace $W$. For $n=3$, take
\[
 W=\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{I,\operatorname{diag}(0,1,2)\},
 \qquad a=H^{-1/3}.
\]
The points $aI$ and $a6^{-1/3}\operatorname{diag}(1,2,3)$ of $S_W$ have, on the path \eqref{eq:positive-matrix-exponential-path}, the midpoint $a6^{-1/6}\operatorname{diag}(1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. The diagonal entries fail the relation $x_1-2x_2+x_3=0$, since $1-2\sqrt2+\sqrt3\ne0$. Thus this path leaves $W$, whereas the normalized segment $N(t)$ used in the proof remains in it.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:iwasawa-complete-gaud-cone}\index{Iwasawa manifold!Gauduchon background cone}
On $G=\mathbb C^3$ take the multiplication
\[
 z\cdot w=(z_1+w_1,z_2+w_2,z_3+w_3+z_1w_2),
\]
the Gaussian lattice $\Gamma=\mathbb Z[\mathrm i]^3$, and $X=\Gamma\backslash G$. This is a compact non-K\"ahler manifold of dimension three, with invariant coframe
\[
 \alpha_1=\mathrm dz_1,\qquad \alpha_2=\mathrm dz_2,\qquad
 \alpha_3=\mathrm dz_3-z_1\mathrm dz_2.
\]
For its full nine-dimensional cone $W=\operatorname{Herm}(3)$ and the normalized invariant probability,
\[
 H=\frac6{\pi^3},\qquad
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta_A)=\frac6{\pi^3}\det A,\qquad
 S=\{A>0:\det A=\pi^3/6\}.
\]
Every positive background is nonclosed. The eight-dimensional unit-volume hypersurface is complete and proper, and its intrinsic distance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iwasawa-unit-volume-distance}
 d_S(A,B)=\frac1{\sqrt3}\|\log(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\|_{\mathrm F}.
\end{equation}
\end{example}

\begin{proof}
The bilinear cross term makes the multiplication associative, with identity zero and inverse $(-z_1,-z_2,-z_3+z_1z_2)$. These operations preserve the Gaussian lattice. Its action by left multiplication is free and properly discontinuous: for a compact set $K\subset G$, elements $\gamma$ with $\gamma K\cap K\ne\varnothing$ belong to the compact set $KK^{-1}$, containing only finitely many lattice elements.

Choose Gaussian integers $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ that move $z_1,z_2$ into the half-open unit square, and then $\gamma_3$ that moves $z_3+\gamma_1z_2$ into it. This gives a representative in the real six-dimensional unit cube. If two points of the half-open cube are equivalent, then $\gamma_1=\gamma_2=0$ and then $\gamma_3=0$. The cube is therefore a measurable fundamental domain, and $X$ is compact, being the image of its closure.

This coframe is left-invariant, descends to $X$, and has
\[
 \mathrm d\alpha_3=-\alpha_1\wedge\alpha_2,\qquad
 \alpha_1\wedge\alpha_2\wedge\alpha_3
       =\mathrm dz_1\wedge\mathrm dz_2\wedge\mathrm dz_3.
\]
The density of $h_0^3$ is $6/\pi^3$ times real Lebesgue volume on the fundamental cube, giving the stated $H$. The coefficient of $\alpha_1\wedge\alpha_2\wedge\bar\alpha_3$ in $\partial\theta_A$ is $-\mathrm iA_{33}/(2\pi)$, so positivity makes every such background nonclosed.

To see that $X$ admits no K\"ahler metric at all, suppose that a positive closed $(1,1)$-form $\kappa$ existed, and average its coefficient functions in this coframe against $\mu$. Their mean is a positive matrix $A$. In the coefficients of $\partial\kappa$, derivative terms have mean zero by \eqref{eq:parallelizable-zero-mean}; the remaining means are exactly the coefficients of $\partial\theta_A$. Closedness would force them to vanish, contradicting $A_{33}>0$. Thus $X$ is non-K\"ahler.

The assertions about the volume, the minimizers and completeness follow from \cref{prop:parallelizable-gaud-volume,thm:parallelizable-unit-volume-completion}. As $W$ is the full matrix space, the equal-determinant exponential path stays in $S$, and \cref{lem:positive-matrix-hessian-distance} gives \eqref{eq:iwasawa-unit-volume-distance}. For $A_\star=aI$, $a=(\pi^3/6)^{1/3}$, and with $\lambda_1(A),\lambda_2(A),\lambda_3(A)$ the eigenvalues of $A$, this becomes
\[
 d_S(A_\star,A)^2=\frac13\sum_{j=1}^3
       \left(\log\frac{\lambda_j(A)}a\right)^2.
\]
The three logarithms sum to zero.

For a concrete degeneration, take $A_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\varepsilon)$, $0<\varepsilon\leq1$. As $\varepsilon\to0$, the coefficients converge to the nonzero smooth semipositive form $\theta_{\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)}$. The normalized matrices and their distances, on the other hand, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iwasawa-diagonal-degeneration}
 \begin{gathered}
 \widehat A_\varepsilon
   =a\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon^{-1/3},\varepsilon^{-1/3},\varepsilon^{2/3}),\\
 d_S(A_\star,\widehat A_\varepsilon)=\frac{\sqrt2}{3}|\log\varepsilon|.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus, after normalization, the boundary form $\theta_{\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)}$ lies at infinite distance.
\end{proof}
