\subsubsection{Current cones and endpoint equations}
\label{sec:warped-current-endpoint-equations}

We keep the product $X=B\times C$, the backgrounds $\theta=b\kappa+(ca+e)w$ with real parameters $b,c,e$, and the probability $\mu=\sigma\wedge w$ of \cref{sec:warped-gauduchon-completion}, and write $h=\kappa+w$ for the product Hermitian form. Pullbacks of nonnegative top-degree forms on $B$ are closed, and testing against them determines which backgrounds have positive current representatives, not only which have smooth positive ones. At a finite end of the unit-volume hypersurface, the reciprocal of the fiber coefficient is integrable with an exponent greater than one, so the equation on the base still has a continuous solution.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:warped-current-cones}\index{Cone duality!warped product currents}
In this real span, the pseudoeffective and nef cones both equal
\[
 \{b\geq0,\ e\geq0,\ e+cM\geq0\}.
\]
A background in this span is big, that is, admits a representative $\theta+\ddc u\geq\delta h$ with $\delta>0$, if and only if $b>0$, $e>0$ and $e+cM>0$. Thus the big backgrounds in this span form exactly the cone $\mathscr C$ of \cref{thm:warped-gauduchon-volume}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $L=ca+e$, and suppose that a quasi-plurisubharmonic $u$ satisfies $\theta+\ddc u\geq\delta h$ for some $\delta\geq0$. Pairing with the closed semipositive form $\kappa^{m-1}\wedge w$ and using Stokes' theorem for currents gives $b\geq\delta$. For every smooth nonnegative top-degree form $\eta$ on $B$, its pullback to $X$ is closed for degree reasons, and
\[
 \int_X(\theta+\ddc u-\delta h)\wedge\eta
   =\int_B(L-\delta)\eta\geq0.
\]
If $L<\delta$ at some point, a nonzero form $\eta$ supported in the open set where $L<\delta$ contradicts this inequality. Hence $L\geq\delta$ everywhere. For $\delta=0$ these conditions are exactly $b\geq0$, $e\geq0$ and $e+cM\geq0$; conversely, they make $\theta$ semipositive, and such forms are nef because $\theta+\varepsilon h>0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. Conversely, if $\theta$ is nef, applying the same tests to smooth positive representatives of $\theta+\varepsilon h$ and letting $\varepsilon$ tend to zero gives these inequalities. For $\delta>0$ the conditions force the three strict inequalities, and conversely these make $\theta$ itself strictly positive.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:warped-continuous-endpoint-equation}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!finite warped boundary}\index{Regularity!continuous solution without C11@continuous solution without $C^{1,1}$}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:warped-endpoint-length} on the extreme sets, let $\theta=b\kappa+L_0w$, with $b>0$, be one of the boundary forms $\theta_+(b)$ or $\theta_-(b)$ of \cref{thm:warped-unit-volume-completion} at a finite end. There is a continuous $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function $u$, pulled back from $B$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:warped-continuous-endpoint-equation}
 (\theta+\ddc u)^n=\mu.
\end{equation}
Here the product is the usual product of bounded potentials. No solution of this equation is of class $C^{1,1}$ on $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $f=a$ for $\theta_+(b)$ and $f=1-a/M$ for $\theta_-(b)$, and let $r>1$ be the corresponding ratio $r_0$ or $r_M$. The coefficient $L_0$ is a positive constant multiple of $f$. In each tube around an extreme component, the integral of $f^{-p}$ converges exactly when
\[
 \int_0^{s_0}s^{\operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb R}Z_i-1-k_ip}\,\mathrm{d}s
\]
converges, where $s$ is the normal radius and $s_0>0$ is small. Thus $1/L_0\in L^p(B,\sigma)$ for every $1<p<r$. The zero set of $L_0$ has volume zero, so the value assigned to the density there is irrelevant. The formulas for $\theta_\pm(b)$ give
\[
 H_0:=\int_B\frac{\sigma}{L_0}=nb^m.
\]
The closed form $b\kappa$ satisfies the Guan--Li condition, so \cref{uni-thm:GL-intro} on $B$, applied to the density of $\sigma/L_0$ relative to $(b\kappa)^m$, gives a continuous $\phi$ with $A=b\kappa+\ddc\phi\geq0$ and $A^m=c_0\sigma/L_0$, where $c_0=\int_B(b\kappa)^m/H_0=b^m/H_0$. Set $u=\phi\circ p_B$, where $p_B\colon X\to B$ is the projection. Then $\theta+\ddc u=A+L_0w\geq0$.

The pullback identity of \cref{lem:bounded-products-submersion} gives $A^{m+1}=0$ on $X$, and $(L_0w)^2=0$. The multilinear expansion of \cref{prop:bounded-products} therefore gives
\[
 (A+L_0w)^n=nL_0 A^m\wedge w
       =\frac{nb^m}{H_0}\sigma\wedge w=\mu.
\]
The cancellation $L_0(\sigma/L_0)=\sigma$ holds as measures, since the zero set of $L_0$ has volume zero and $A^m$ is absolutely continuous with density $c_0/L_0$ relative to $\sigma$.

Suppose that some solution $q$, not necessarily pulled back from $B$, were of class $C^{1,1}$ on $X$. Since $\mu/h^n$ is smooth and strictly positive on compact $X$, \cref{prop:c11-ellipticity} would give $\theta+\ddc q\geq\delta h$ for some $\delta>0$. This contradicts \cref{prop:warped-current-cones}, because $L_0$ vanishes somewhere.
\end{proof}
