\subsection{Envelope criteria and extension across the degeneracy set}
\label{subsec:semipositive-envelope-extension}

The capacity criterion above requires the obstacle envelope of $u_0$ in a smaller background $\delta\omega$ not to be identically $-\infty$. We give two sufficient conditions of different origin: invariance of the Monge--Amp\`ere mass controls the contact measures of envelopes of stretched truncations, while a sublinear bound on the singularity provides explicit competitors. Both conditions also give zero Lelong numbers. In the setting of \cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation}, if the maximizing form has a globally smooth root of rank at least two, zero Lelong numbers then allow the threshold solution to be extended smoothly across the analytic degeneracy set.

Inequalities between quasi-plurisubharmonic functions refer to their canonical representatives. We will use the following consequence of \cref{lem:gauduchon-uniform-lelong}, where $C_\omega$ is the bound for the Lelong numbers of $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions given there: if $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ and, for every $0<\delta<1$, some $v_\delta\in\PSH(X,\delta\omega)$ satisfies $v_\delta\leq u$, then
\[
 0\leq\nu(u,x)\leq\nu(v_\delta,x)
       =\delta\nu(v_\delta/\delta,x)\leq\delta C_\omega.
\]
Thus $u$ has zero Lelong numbers; the bound is uniform in $\delta$ because $v_\delta/\delta\in\PSH(X,\omega)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:invariant-mass-threshold-envelopes}\index{Envelope!invariant mass criterion}
Suppose $\ddc(\omega^j)=0$ for $1\leq j<n$, and put $V=\int_X\omega^n$. For every nonzero $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, with $M=\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}V$, one has
\[
 b_\omega(f)=\frac VM,
\]
and every threshold subsolution $u$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)=(V/M)f\,\mathrm{d}V$, so it has mass $V$. Every $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $\int_X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)=V$ has zero Lelong numbers, and $P_{\delta\omega}(u)$ is not identically $-\infty$ for every $0<\delta<1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}, every bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function has mass $V$, and truncation therefore bounds every extended mass by $V$. For $f_k=\min(f,k)$, integration of the bounded equation gives $c_\omega(f_k)=V/\int_Xf_k\,\mathrm{d}V$, so the definition \eqref{eq:individual-truncated-normalizer} gives $b_\omega(f)=V/M$. A threshold subsolution has mass at least $b_\omega(f)M=V$. Its mass is consequently $V$, and the nonnegative difference between its measure and $b_\omega(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V$ has mass zero.

Now let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ have extended mass $V$. Normalize $u\leq0$, put $\mu_u=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)$, and fix $a>1$. For $u_j=\max(u,-j)$, let $w_j=P_\omega(au_j)$. Comparison with constants gives $-aj\leq w_j\leq0$. The bounded contact theorem \cref{thm:bounded-contact-support} shows that $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(w_j)$ is concentrated on the contact set $C_j=\{w_j=au_j\}$. Since the two functions are canonical representatives and $w_j\leq au_j$ almost everywhere, this inequality holds everywhere. Both functions are $a\omega$-plurisubharmonic, so positivity and \cref{prop:ordered-contact-general} give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\omega(w_j)
 \leq\mathbf1_{C_j}\mathrm{MA}_{a\omega}(w_j)
 \leq a^n\mathbf1_{C_j}\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u_j).
\]
For integers $j\geq N$, locality of the products on $\{u>-N\}$ and the mass identity for bounded functions yield
\[
 \int_{\{u_j\leq-N\}}\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u_j)
       =V-\mu_u(\{u>-N\})=\mu_u(\{u\leq-N\}).
\]
Choose $N$ with $a^n\mu_u(\{u\leq-N\})<V$; this is possible because $\mu_u$ is finite and puts no mass on the pole set of $u$. If $\sup_Xw_j<-aN$, the contact set $C_j$ lies in $\{u_j<-N\}$, and the preceding inequalities contradict $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(w_j)=V$. Hence $\sup_Xw_j\geq-aN$ for $j\geq N$, and for smaller $j$ the bound $\sup_Xw_j\geq-aj$ suffices.

The envelopes $w_j$ decrease with $j$, and by compactness of normalized quasi-plurisubharmonic functions this uniform lower bound for their suprema prevents their limit from being identically $-\infty$. Their limit $w\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ satisfies $w\leq au$, also at the poles of $u$. With $\delta=1/a$, the function $\delta w\in\PSH(X,\delta\omega)$ is therefore a competitor for $P_{\delta\omega}(u)$, so this envelope is not identically $-\infty$. Since $a>1$ was arbitrary, this holds for every $0<\delta<1$, and the Lelong estimate preceding the theorem completes the proof. The normalization $u\leq0$ does not affect any conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sublinear-threshold-envelope}\index{Envelope!sublinear singularity criterion}
Let $\psi\leq0$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with $\ddc\psi\geq-C\omega$ for some $C>0$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ satisfy, everywhere on $X$,
\[
 u\geq-A(1-\psi)^\alpha-B,
 \qquad A>0,\quad B\in\mathbb R,\quad0<\alpha<1.
\]
Then $P_{\delta\omega}(u)$ is not identically $-\infty$ for every $0<\delta<1$, and $u$ has zero Lelong numbers.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\alpha<\beta<1$ and $\varepsilon>0$ with $\varepsilon\beta C\leq\delta$. The number
\[
 K=B+\sup_{t\geq1}(At^\alpha-\varepsilon t^\beta)
\]
is finite, and $v_\delta=-\varepsilon(1-\psi)^\beta-K\leq u$. The function $t\mapsto-\varepsilon(1-t)^\beta$ is increasing and convex on $t\leq0$, with derivative at most $\varepsilon\beta$. Applying the chain inequality for quasi-plurisubharmonic functions to $\max(\psi,-j)$ and passing to the decreasing limit as $j\to\infty$, we obtain $\ddc v_\delta\geq-\varepsilon\beta C\omega\geq-\delta\omega$. The limit is not identically $-\infty$ because $\psi$ is finite almost everywhere; at the poles of $\psi$ it takes the value $-\infty$, where the comparison with $u$ still holds. Thus $v_\delta\in\PSH(X,\delta\omega)$ is a competitor for $P_{\delta\omega}(u)$ for each $\delta$, and the Lelong estimate preceding \cref{thm:invariant-mass-threshold-envelopes} proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{A globally smooth root of rank at least two}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rank-two-dual-smooth-extension}\index{Gauduchon duality!smooth extension across degeneration}
Assume the hypotheses of \cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation}, with analytic degeneracy set $Z$, and suppose that $T=\gamma^{n-1}$ for a smooth semipositive $(1,1)$-form $\gamma$ on $X$ whose rank is at least two everywhere. If the threshold solution $u_0$ with $\sup_Xu_0=0$ has zero Lelong numbers, then $u_0$ is smooth on $X$, and hence so is every threshold subsolution.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $U=X\setminus Z$, $b=b_\omega(f)$ and $A=\omega+\ddc u_0$. On $U$ the form $\gamma$ is positive, since $\gamma^{n-1}=T$ is, so it is the positive root of $T$ used in the proof of \cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation}. That proof shows that $A=r\gamma$ on $U$, where $r=(bf\,\mathrm{d}V/\gamma^n)^{1/n}$ is smooth and nonnegative on $U$.

Since $\ddc u_0$ is closed,
\[
 \mathrm dr\wedge\gamma+r\,\mathrm d\gamma=\mathrm d\omega
       \quad\hbox{on }U.
\]
For a semipositive form $\gamma$ of rank at least two, the real-linear map $W_\gamma\colon\xi\mapsto\xi\wedge\gamma$ from real one-forms to three-forms is injective at every point. Indeed, write $\gamma=\sum_k\lambda_k\,\mathrm dx_k\wedge\mathrm dy_k$ at the point in suitable complex linear coordinates $z_k=x_k+\mathrm iy_k$, with $\lambda_k\geq0$ and at least two $\lambda_k$ positive. For each index $j$ choose $k\neq j$ with $\lambda_k>0$. The coefficient of $\mathrm dx_j\wedge\mathrm dx_k\wedge\mathrm dy_k$ in $\xi\wedge\gamma$ is $\lambda_k$ times the coefficient of $\mathrm dx_j$ in $\xi$, and similarly for $\mathrm dy_j$. Hence $\xi\wedge\gamma=0$ forces $\xi=0$.

Choose an auxiliary Riemannian metric. The left inverse $L_\gamma=(W_\gamma^*W_\gamma)^{-1}W_\gamma^*$ is smooth and uniformly bounded on compact $X$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-two-extension-first-order}
 \mathrm dr=\alpha-r\beta,\qquad
 \alpha=L_\gamma(\mathrm d\omega),\quad
 \beta=L_\gamma(\mathrm d\gamma),
\end{equation}
with smooth one-forms on $X$. In particular $|\mathrm d\log(1+r)|\leq C$ on $U$.

Points of $U$ can be joined by short paths avoiding $Z$. In a coordinate ball, stratify $Z$ into countably many smooth strata of real dimension at most $2n-2$. For a fixed point outside $Z$, the endpoints of segments from it that meet one stratum lie in a cone of dimension at most $2n-1$, which is Lebesgue-null. Given two nearby points outside $Z$, choose a generic intermediate point in a ball of radius comparable to their distance; both connecting segments then avoid $Z$, and their combined length is bounded by a fixed multiple of that distance. Using a finite connected cover by coordinate balls, with intermediate points chosen in the overlaps, we also obtain a uniform bound for the lengths of connecting paths in $U$.

The bound on $\mathrm d\log(1+r)$ now bounds $r$ on $U$, and \eqref{eq:rank-two-extension-first-order} bounds its derivative. The short paths therefore give a unique locally Lipschitz extension of $r$ across $Z$. Since $Z$ is Lebesgue-null, the weak derivative of the extension satisfies \eqref{eq:rank-two-extension-first-order} on $X$. The right side is continuous, so $r$ is $C^1$, and iterating makes it smooth.

Set $S=r\gamma$. It is smooth and semipositive, and $\mathrm dS=\mathrm d\omega$ on $X$ by density of $U$. The current $R=A-S$ is therefore closed, and it is supported on $Z$. It is also positive: since $A=S$ off $Z$ and $S$ puts no mass on $Z$, the current $R$ is the restriction of the positive current $A$ to $Z$. The support theorem recalled in \cref{sec:psh-divisorial-estimates} expresses $R$ as a nonnegative combination of currents of integration along the components of $Z$ of codimension one. Locally $S-\omega$ is smooth and closed, so it has a smooth local $\ddc$-potential, and the coefficients of $R$ are the generic Lelong numbers of $u_0$ along these components. All of them vanish, so $\ddc u_0=S-\omega$ is smooth. Taking a Euclidean trace and applying elliptic regularity gives a smooth function equal to $u_0$ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere, since both functions are quasi-plurisubharmonic. Threshold subsolutions differ from $u_0$ by constants.
\end{proof}

By \cref{thm:invariant-mass-threshold-envelopes,prop:sublinear-threshold-envelope}, the condition on the Lelong numbers holds under either of the conditions above. The smooth root and its rank bound are needed: a smooth semipositive $(n-1,n-1)$-form need not have a smooth $(1,1)$-root across its degeneracy set. On a surface the rank condition already forces $\gamma>0$ everywhere.

\subsubsection{Two attained instances}

\begin{example}\label{ex:hopf-surface-invariant-attainment}\index{Gauduchon duality!Hopf surface attainment}
On the Hopf surface $X=(\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\})/\langle z\mapsto2z\rangle$, the background $\omega=|z|^{-2}\ddc|z|^2$ satisfies $\ddc\omega=0$, so its Monge--Amp\`ere mass is invariant. There are smooth densities whose threshold solutions are smooth and nonconstant and whose maximizing tests are smooth strictly positive forms.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The form is invariant under $z\mapsto2z$, so it descends to $X$, and it is positive. For $s=|z|^2$ and $F(s)=s^{-1}$, the identity $sF''+2F'=0$ gives $\ddc\omega=\ddc(s^{-1})\wedge\ddc s=0$, while $\omega$ is not closed. Choose a nonconstant smooth real $q$ and small nonzero $\varepsilon$ such that $A=\omega+\ddc(\varepsilon q)>0$. Put $V=\int_X\omega^2$, $\mathrm{d}V=\omega^2/V$, $f=A^2/\mathrm{d}V$ and $u=\varepsilon q-\sup_X\varepsilon q$. Invariance of the mass gives $\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}V=\int_XA^2=V$, hence $b_\omega(f)=1$ by \cref{thm:invariant-mass-threshold-envelopes}.

The form $T=A/V$ satisfies $\ddc T=0$, $\int_X\omega\wedge T=1$ and $D_{\sqrt f}(T)=1$. For every smooth strictly positive $Q\in\mathscr G_\omega$, the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality and Stokes' theorem give $D_{\sqrt f}(Q)\leq\int_XA\wedge Q=1$. Thus $T$ is a smooth strictly positive maximizer. Since $u$ is a threshold solution, \cref{cor:smooth-dual-exact-equation} shows that every threshold subsolution is $u$ plus a constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:quadric-rank-two-dual-attainment}\index{Gauduchon duality!quadric ramification}
Let $n\geq3$, and view the smooth quadric of dimension $n$ as a double cover of $\mathbb P^n$. For the restricted Fubini--Study form and a suitable smooth density, the maximizing test is a smooth semipositive form whose smooth root has rank at least two everywhere and degenerates along the ramification divisor.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 X=\{[w:z_0:\cdots:z_n]:w^2=\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^nz_i^2\}
       \subset\mathbb P^{n+1},\qquad F([w:z])=[z].
\]
The quadratic form is nonsingular, and $F$ is finite of degree two, with smooth ramification divisor $Z=\{w=0\}$. Local branch coordinates show that $\mathrm dF$ has rank $n-1$ on $Z$ and $n$ elsewhere. Let $\omega$ be the restriction to $X$ of the normalized Fubini--Study form of $\mathbb P^{n+1}$, and let $A=F^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, where $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is the normalized Fubini--Study form of $\mathbb P^n$. Then
\[
 u=\log\frac{\sum_i|z_i|^2}{|w|^2+\sum_i|z_i|^2}
\]
is a smooth function on $X$ with $-\log2\leq u\leq0$, $\sup_Xu=0$ and $A=\omega+\ddc u$. The change of variables for the branched cover gives $\int_XA^n=2$, and Stokes' theorem gives $V=\int_X\omega^n=2$.

Set $\mathrm{d}V=\omega^n/V$, $f=A^n/\mathrm{d}V$, $T=A^{n-1}/V$ and $\gamma=A/V^{1/(n-1)}$. Since $\omega$ is closed, \cref{thm:invariant-mass-threshold-envelopes} gives $b_\omega(f)=V/\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}V=1$. Stokes' theorem gives $\ddc T=0$ and $\int_X\omega\wedge T=1$, and computing the determinant density gives $D_{f^{1/n}}(T)=1$. The arithmetic--geometric mean inequality, which remains valid for semipositive $A$, bounds $D_{f^{1/n}}(Q)$ by $\int_XA\wedge Q=1$ for every smooth strictly positive $Q\in\mathscr G_\omega$. Thus $T$ is a maximizer, strictly positive off the proper analytic set $Z$, and by \cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation} the threshold solution with supremum zero is $u$. Its root $\gamma$ has rank $n-1\geq2$ on $Z$ and is positive elsewhere, as required.
\end{proof}

The invariant-mass and sublinear conditions settle the envelope condition only in special situations. We do not know whether general moment or entropy bounds imply either of them, or the hypothesis on the smooth root.
