\chapter{Perron interpolation and the geodesic equation}
\label{chap:hermitian-rays}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Fix a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ for the $L^1(X)$ topology.

In K\"ahler geometry, the smooth geodesic equation for the $L^2$ metric on potentials is a homogeneous Monge--Amp\`ere equation on a product. Product positivity also defines Perron interpolation for a nonclosed background, but the two constructions can differ: integration by parts in the action produces torsion terms. Even a smooth Perron segment given by constant translation can fail to be stationary for that action.

The Perron construction itself rests on convexity and barriers. Convexity bounds every admissible path by the endpoint chord, while a lower barrier ensures that the envelope attains the endpoints. For potentials of the same ordinary singularity type, two affine barriers suffice and give uniform Lipschitz control in time. Maximality on each finite subinterval will supply the link with Legendre duality in the next chapter.

\section{Subgeodesics and endpoint bounds}\label{sec:strip-positivity}

For a nonempty open interval $I\subset\R$, write
\[
 S_I=\{z\in\C:\Real z\in I\},\qquad
 \pi:X\times S_I\longrightarrow X.
\]
A family $u_t\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, $t\in I$, is a \emph{subgeodesic} if its complexification $U(x,z)=u_{\Real z}(x)$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic and satisfies $\pi^*\theta+\ddc U\geq0$ on the product.\index{Subgeodesic} For finite $a<b$ and $u_a,u_b\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, a subgeodesic on $(a,b)$ is said to run from $u_a$ to $u_b$ if it converges to them in $L^1(X)$ at the respective endpoints.

The elementary operations below construct lower barriers. Vertical convexity and the minimum principle from \cref{sec:psh-minimum} then give the continuity and upper bounds needed for the Perron envelope.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-subgeodesic-calculus}
Subgeodesics are preserved by adding an affine function of $t$, by a positive affine reparametrization of time, by finite maxima, and by convex combinations. For $h\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$, replacing $\theta$ by $\theta+\ddc h$ and $u_t$ by $u_t-h$ also preserves them.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Affine functions of $\Real z$ have zero complex Hessian, and a positive affine change of time is induced by a holomorphic change of strip coordinates. Convex combinations preserve the current inequality, and smooth gauge changes preserve the current itself. Maxima are covered by \cref{lem:max-psh}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}
A subgeodesic is continuous in $L^1(X)$ on the interior of its parameter interval. If $a<b$ are finite endpoints and its $L^1$ endpoint limits are $u_a,u_b\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-chord-bound}
 u_t\leq\frac{b-t}{b-a}u_a+\frac{t-a}{b-a}u_b,
 \qquad a<t<b.
\end{equation}
Moreover the boundary upper limits hold jointly in space and time:
\[
 \limsup_{\substack{y\to x\\t\downarrow a}}u_t(y)\leq u_a(x),
 \qquad
 \limsup_{\substack{y\to x\\t\uparrow b}}u_t(y)\leq u_b(x).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a compact interval $J\Subset I$, choose $a'<\inf J\leq\sup J<b'$ in $I$. By \cref{lem:invariant-strip-convexity}, the path lies below the chord through $u_{a'},u_{b'}$, hence below a constant on $J$. The function $\inf_{a'<t<b'}u_t$ is an integrable lower bound by \cref{prop:hermitian-kiselman}. For almost every $x$, the function $t\mapsto u_t(x)$ is finite and convex, hence continuous. Dominated convergence proves continuity in $L^1$ on $J$.

For the endpoint inequality, choose sequences approaching $a,b$ along which the stipulated $L^1$ convergence holds almost everywhere. Apply convexity between these times and pass to the limit. For each $t$, this proves \eqref{eq:hermitian-chord-bound} almost everywhere in $x$, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.

For the joint limit at $a$, the coefficient of $u_a$ tends to one and the term involving $u_b$ has a vanishing coefficient and a uniform upper bound. Upper semicontinuity of $u_a$ gives the assertion when $u_a(x)$ is finite. At a pole, bound $u_a$ above near $x$ by an arbitrary real number, take the joint limit, and let that number tend to $-\infty$. The argument at $b$ is the same.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\section{Perron segments}\label{sec:perron-segments}

The chord bound survives taking a regularized supremum. A single competitor attaining the endpoints will bound that supremum from below and force the same endpoint limits. Fix $u_0,u_1\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and call a subgeodesic on $(0,1)$ \emph{admissible} if its joint boundary upper limits are at most $u_0,u_1$, respectively. Define
\[
 U=\left(\sup\{V:V\text{ is an admissible complexification}\}\right)^*.
\]
The regularization is taken on $X\times S_{(0,1)}$. Denote its slices by $u_t^{\mathrm P}$. When these form a subgeodesic attaining the prescribed endpoints in $L^1$, they define the \emph{Perron segment}, with $u_0^{\mathrm P}=u_0$ and $u_1^{\mathrm P}=u_1$. Affine reparametrization gives the same construction on any finite interval.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-perron}\index{Perron interpolation}
If an admissible competitor exists, the Perron envelope is the largest admissible subgeodesic. If one competitor attains $u_0,u_1$ in $L^1$, so does the envelope. In that case, its restriction to each $[a,b]\subset[0,1]$, $a<b$, is the Perron segment between its own endpoints.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Vertical convexity and the boundary inequalities bound every competitor by $(1-t)u_0+t u_1$. This bound is jointly upper semicontinuous on the interior and locally bounded above, so it holds after regularization. The local envelope theorem of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} gives a product potential, still invariant in $\Im z$. It dominates an admissible competitor, so each slice belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$. The chord bound gives the joint boundary inequalities by the endpoint argument in \cref{prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}. The envelope therefore belongs to its defining family and is its largest member.

If a competitor $w_t$ has the required $L^1$ endpoints, then
\[
 w_t\leq u_t^{\mathrm P}\leq(1-t)u_0+t u_1.
\]
In particular, almost everywhere,
\[
 |u_t^{\mathrm P}-u_0|\leq |w_t-u_0|+t|u_1-u_0|,
\]
which proves $u_t^{\mathrm P}\to u_0$ in $L^1$ as $t\downarrow0$. The argument at $1$ is identical.

For $0\leq a<b\leq1$, let $v_t$ be the Perron envelope between $u_a^{\mathrm P}$ and $u_b^{\mathrm P}$. The original path restricted to $(a,b)$ attains these endpoints and is admissible by \cref{prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}. Thus $v_t$ attains them and dominates $u_t^{\mathrm P}$. Its joint boundary bounds permit gluing its complexification to $U$ outside the smaller strip by \cref{lem:qpsh-gluing}. At an original endpoint, the same bounds preserve admissibility for $u_0,u_1$. The resulting competitor cannot exceed $U$, proving equality on $(a,b)$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
A bounded difference between the endpoints supplies the required lower barrier explicitly.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:ordinary-type-perron}\index{Perron interpolation!fixed ordinary type}
Suppose $u_0\sim u_1$ and choose $C\geq0$ with
$u_0-C\leq u_1\leq u_0+C$. The Perron segment exists and attains both endpoints. Every slice has their ordinary singularity type, and
\[
 u_0-Ct\leq u_t^{\mathrm P}\leq u_0+Ct,
 \qquad
 u_1-C(1-t)\leq u_t^{\mathrm P}\leq u_1+C(1-t).
\]
Away from the common pole set,
\[
 |u_t^{\mathrm P}-u_s^{\mathrm P}|\leq C|t-s|,
 \qquad0\leq s,t\leq1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider the barrier
\[
 w_t=\max\{u_0-Ct,\ u_1-C(1-t)\}.
\]
It is a subgeodesic by \cref{prop:hermitian-subgeodesic-calculus}, and the bounded difference gives $w_0=u_0,w_1=u_1$. Upper semicontinuity gives the joint boundary upper limits. Off the common poles, $|w_t-u_0|\leq Ct$ and $|w_t-u_1|\leq C(1-t)$, so $w_t$ attains both endpoints in $L^1$. Apply \cref{prop:hermitian-perron}.

The lower bounds follow from $w_t\leq u_t^{\mathrm P}$ and the upper bounds from the chord estimate. They also give the asserted singularity type. Off the common poles, put $f(t)=u_t^{\mathrm P}(x)$. For $0<s<t<1$, monotonicity of convex secant slopes gives
\[
 -C\leq\frac{f(s)-f(0)}s
 \leq\frac{f(t)-f(s)}{t-s}
 \leq\frac{f(1)-f(t)}{1-t}\leq C.
\]
The endpoint estimates cover $s=0$ and $t=1$, proving the Lipschitz bound on $[0,1]$.
\end{proof}

For endpoints of different types, a common lower potential still gives an admissible constant path. Endpoint attainment requires a barrier approaching the prescribed values; \cref{prop:hermitian-perron} separates this requirement from existence of the envelope.

\Needspace{25\baselineskip}
\section{The smooth product equation}

For a smooth path, product positivity compares acceleration with the spatial gradient of the velocity. Let $u_t$ be smooth in $(x,t)\in X\times I$, suppose $\eta_t=\theta+\ddc u_t>0$, and write, in local holomorphic coordinates $(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$,
\[
 \theta=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{i,j=1}^n
        g_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar x_j,
 \qquad H=(g_{i\bar j}+\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}u),
 \qquad \xi=(\partial_i\dot u)_{i=1}^n.
\]
Dots denote time derivatives, $\xi$ is a column vector, and $\xi^*$ is its conjugate transpose. Since $t=\Real z$, the product form $\pi^*\theta+\ddc U$ has coefficient matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 H&\tfrac12\xi\\
 \tfrac12\xi^*&\tfrac14\ddot u
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-schur-geodesic}\index{Geodesic equation!homogeneous product equation}
The product form is semipositive exactly when
\[
 \ddot u\geq\xi^*H^{-1}\xi.
\]
Its $(n+1)$st power vanishes exactly when equality holds. Equivalently, the homogeneous product equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-hcma-smooth}
 \ddot u\,\eta_t^n
 =n\,\mathrm{d}\dot u\wedge\dc\dot u\wedge\eta_t^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Schur complement of $H$ is $(\ddot u-\xi^*H^{-1}\xi)/4$. Since $H>0$, the block matrix is semipositive exactly when this complement is nonnegative, and its determinant vanishes exactly when the complement vanishes.

The scalar identity is
\[
 \mathrm{d}\dot u\wedge\dc\dot u=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\dot u\wedge\bar\partial\dot u,
 \qquad
 n\frac{\mathrm{d}\dot u\wedge\dc\dot u\wedge\eta_t^{n-1}}{\eta_t^n}
 =\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}\xi\xi^*)=\xi^*H^{-1}\xi.
\]
Since $\eta_t^n>0$, multiplying this identity by $\eta_t^n$ gives \eqref{eq:hermitian-hcma-smooth}.
\end{proof}

This calculation identifies the smooth homogeneous product equation. A weak formulation and proof for nonsmooth Perron segments require a separate argument.

\Needspace{25\baselineskip}
\section{The \texorpdfstring{$L^2$}{L2} action and torsion}

The product equation uses the spatial form pointwise. In the $L^2$ action, integration by parts also differentiates its powers, producing terms absent from that equation. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$. On the space $\HH_\omega$ of \cref{chap:mass}, consider the metric
\[
 G_u(v,w)=\int_Xvw\,\omega_u^n,
 \qquad v,w\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R).
\]
For a smooth path $u_t\in\HH_\omega$, $0\leq t\leq1$, its action is
\[
 \mathscr S(u)=\frac12\int_0^1\int_X\dot u_t^{\,2}\,\omega_{u_t}^n\,\mathrm{d}t.
\]
Stationarity under smooth variations fixing both endpoints gives the formal geodesic equation for this metric.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-ltwo-stationarity}\index{Geodesic equation!Hermitian metric}\index{Torsion!geodesic equation}
Put $\eta_t=\omega_{u_t}$ and $T_t=\eta_t^{n-1}$. The path is stationary for $\mathscr S$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-ltwo-geodesic}
 \begin{split}
 \ddot u_t\,\eta_t^n={}&n\,\mathrm{d}\dot u_t\wedge\dc\dot u_t\wedge T_t\\
 &+n\dot u_t\bigl(\mathrm{d}\dot u_t\wedge\dc T_t
                  -\dc\dot u_t\wedge \mathrm{d}T_t\bigr)
   +\frac n2\dot u_t^{\,2}\ddc T_t.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
All differentials on the right act only in the $X$ variable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Vary the path as $u_t+s v_t$, where $v_t$ is smooth in $(x,t)$ and $v_0=v_1=0$, and put $f=\dot u_t$. Differentiating the action at $s=0$ gives
\[
 \delta\mathscr S
 =\int_0^1\left(\int_X f\dot v_t\,\eta_t^n
       +\frac n2\int_X f^2\ddc v_t\wedge T_t\right)\mathrm{d}t.
\]
Integration in time uses
$\partial_t(\eta_t^n)=n\ddc f\wedge T_t$. Integration on $X$ uses \cref{lem:ibp} with the entire form $f^2T_t$. Thus
\[
 \delta\mathscr S
 =\int_0^1\int_Xv_t\left(-\ddot u_t\eta_t^n
       -nf\ddc f\wedge T_t+\frac n2\ddc(f^2T_t)\right)\mathrm{d}t.
\]
The full product rule is
\[
 \begin{split}
 \ddc(f^2T_t)={}&2f\ddc f\wedge T_t
       +2\mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc f\wedge T_t\\
 &+2f\bigl(\mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc T_t-\dc f\wedge \mathrm{d}T_t\bigr)
       +f^2\ddc T_t.
 \end{split}
\]
After cancellation with the volume derivative, the integrand is $v_t$ times the difference between the right and left sides of \eqref{eq:hermitian-ltwo-geodesic}. Positivity of $\eta_t$ on $X\times[0,1]$ permits every sufficiently small variation. The fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations gives both implications.
\end{proof}

For a K\"ahler background, $\mathrm{d}T_t=\dc T_t=0$ and the equation reduces to \eqref{eq:hermitian-hcma-smooth}. The Gauduchon condition on $\eta_t$ removes only the last term. Constant translations isolate that term and provide an exact test for stationarity.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:translations-gauduchon}\index{Geodesic equation!constant translations}
Fix $u\in\HH_\omega$ and a nonzero constant $a$. The path $u_t=u+at$ is a Perron segment and solves the smooth homogeneous product equation. It is stationary for the $L^2$ action if and only if
\[
 \ddc(\omega_u^{n-1})=0.
\]
For $n\geq2$, this says precisely that $\omega_u$ is Gauduchon.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The path is admissible and equals the chord between its endpoints, so no admissible competitor can be larger. It is therefore the Perron segment. Its time acceleration and the spatial derivatives of its velocity vanish, which proves the homogeneous product equation. In \eqref{eq:hermitian-ltwo-geodesic}, the sole remaining term is
$\frac n2a^2\ddc(\omega_u^{n-1})$. This vanishes exactly under the displayed condition.
\end{proof}

The three-dimensional torus in \cref{ex:gauduchon-persistence-torus} carries a Gauduchon form $\omega$ and a smooth potential $u\in\HH_\omega$ for which $\omega_u$ is not Gauduchon. Thus $u_t=u+t$ is a smooth Perron segment that is not stationary for the $L^2$ action. On a surface, the Gauduchon condition persists under potential perturbations by \cref{prop:gauduchon-potential-persistence}, but it does not cancel the first-derivative terms in \eqref{eq:hermitian-ltwo-geodesic}. Relating Perron interpolation to a complete metric on Hermitian singularity classes remains a separate problem.
