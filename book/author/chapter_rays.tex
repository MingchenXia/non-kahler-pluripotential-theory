\chapter{Perron interpolation and the geodesic equation}
\label{chap:hermitian-rays}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Fix a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ for the $L^1(X)$ topology.

This part studies two operations on singular potentials: interpolation between two potentials along an auxiliary real parameter, and restriction to a complex submanifold. A one-parameter family of $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions can be regarded as a single function on the product of $X$ with a strip in $\mathbb C$, and positivity of its curvature current on the product is a convexity condition in the parameter. The largest such family with prescribed endpoints, the Perron envelope, interpolates between two potentials; in K\"ahler geometry it gives the weak geodesics of the space of potentials. A Legendre transform in the parameter relates rays made of Perron segments to concave decreasing families of envelope fixed points, the test curves of \cref{chap:hermitian-testcurves}. Restriction behaves differently: the restriction of a potential to a submanifold can acquire poles that are invisible to its multiplier ideals, and \cref{chap:trace-data} works instead with the information carried by the restricted multiplier ideals. All these constructions use only positivity of currents, so they apply to nonclosed backgrounds.

In K\"ahler geometry, the smooth geodesic equation for the Mabuchi $L^2$ metric on potentials is a homogeneous Monge--Amp\`ere equation on such a product. Positivity on the product still defines Perron interpolation for a nonclosed background, but the two constructions can now differ: integration by parts in the $L^2$ action produces torsion terms. Even a smooth Perron segment given by translation by constants can fail to be stationary for that action.

The Perron construction itself rests on convexity and barriers. Convexity in the parameter bounds every admissible path by the chord between its endpoints, while a lower barrier ensures that the envelope attains the endpoints. For two potentials of the same ordinary singularity type, two affine barriers suffice and give a Lipschitz bound in the parameter. Maximality on each finite subinterval is the property that links Perron segments with Legendre duality in \cref{chap:hermitian-testcurves}.

\section{Subgeodesics and endpoint bounds}\label{sec:strip-positivity}

As in \cref{lem:invariant-strip-convexity}, for a nonempty open interval $I\subset\R$ write
\[
 S_I=\{z\in\C:\Real z\in I\},\qquad
 \pi\colon X\times S_I\longrightarrow X,
\]
where $\pi$ is the projection. A family $u_t\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, $t\in I$, is a \emph{subgeodesic} if its complexification $U(x,z)=u_{\Real z}(x)$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic and satisfies $\pi^*\theta+\ddc U\geq0$ on $X\times S_I$.\index{Subgeodesic} For finite $a<b$ and $u_a,u_b\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, a subgeodesic on $(a,b)$ \emph{runs from $u_a$ to $u_b$} if $u_t\to u_a$ in $L^1(X)$ as $t\downarrow a$ and $u_t\to u_b$ as $t\uparrow b$.

The elementary operations below produce lower barriers. Convexity in $t$, given by \cref{lem:invariant-strip-convexity}, and the minimum principle of \cref{sec:psh-minimum} then give the continuity and upper bounds needed for the Perron envelope.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-subgeodesic-calculus}
Subgeodesics are preserved by adding an affine function of $t$, by a positive affine reparametrization of time, by finite maxima, and by convex combinations. For $h\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$, replacing $\theta$ by $\theta+\ddc h$ and $u_t$ by $u_t-h$ also preserves them.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Affine functions of $\Real z$ have zero complex Hessian, and a positive affine change of time is induced by a holomorphic affine change of the strip coordinate. Convex combinations preserve the inequality between currents, and the change $(\theta,u_t)\mapsto(\theta+\ddc h,u_t-h)$ preserves the current itself. Maxima are covered by \cref{lem:max-psh}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}
A subgeodesic is continuous in $L^1(X)$ on the interior of its parameter interval. If the interval is $(a,b)$ with $a<b$ finite, and the subgeodesic runs from $u_a$ to $u_b$, where $u_a,u_b\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-chord-bound}
 u_t\leq\frac{b-t}{b-a}u_a+\frac{t-a}{b-a}u_b,
 \qquad a<t<b.
\end{equation}
Moreover, the following upper limits, taken jointly in space and time, hold at the endpoints:
\[
 \limsup_{\substack{y\to x\\t\downarrow a}}u_t(y)\leq u_a(x),
 \qquad
 \limsup_{\substack{y\to x\\t\uparrow b}}u_t(y)\leq u_b(x).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a compact interval $J\Subset I$, choose $a'<\inf J\leq\sup J<b'$ in $I$. By \cref{lem:invariant-strip-convexity}, the path lies below the chord through $u_{a'}$ and $u_{b'}$, hence below a constant on $J$, and by \cref{prop:hermitian-kiselman} the function $\inf_{a'<t<b'}u_t$ is an integrable lower bound. For almost every $x$, the function $t\mapsto u_t(x)$ is finite and convex, hence continuous, and dominated convergence proves continuity in $L^1$ on $J$.

For the chord bound, choose sequences of times tending to $a$ and $b$ along which the assumed $L^1$ convergence holds almost everywhere. Applying convexity between these times and passing to the limit proves \eqref{eq:hermitian-chord-bound} almost everywhere in $x$, for each $t$, and hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.

For the joint upper limit at $a$, note that in \eqref{eq:hermitian-chord-bound} the coefficient of $u_a$ tends to one, while the term involving $u_b$ has a vanishing coefficient and a uniform upper bound. Upper semicontinuity of $u_a$ gives the assertion when $u_a(x)$ is finite. At a pole of $u_a$, bound $u_a$ near $x$ above by an arbitrary real number, take the joint upper limit, and let that number tend to $-\infty$. The argument at $b$ is the same.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\section{Perron segments}\label{sec:perron-segments}

The chord bound survives taking a regularized supremum, and a single competitor attaining the endpoints bounds that supremum from below and forces the same endpoint limits. Fix $u_0,u_1\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and call a subgeodesic on $(0,1)$ \emph{admissible} if its joint upper limits at $t=0$ and $t=1$, in the sense of \cref{prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}, are at most $u_0$ and $u_1$, respectively. Define the \emph{Perron envelope}
\[
 U=\left(\sup\{V:V\text{ is an admissible complexification}\}\right)^*,
\]
where the upper semicontinuous regularization is taken on $X\times S_{(0,1)}$, and denote the slices of $U$ by $u_t^{\mathrm P}$. When these slices form a subgeodesic running from $u_0$ to $u_1$, they define the \emph{Perron segment} from $u_0$ to $u_1$, and we set $u_0^{\mathrm P}=u_0$ and $u_1^{\mathrm P}=u_1$. An affine change of parameter gives the same construction on any finite interval.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-perron}\index{Perron interpolation}
If an admissible subgeodesic exists, the Perron envelope is the largest admissible subgeodesic. If some admissible subgeodesic runs from $u_0$ to $u_1$, so does the envelope, which is then the Perron segment. In that case, for every $[a,b]\subset[0,1]$ with $a<b$, its restriction to $[a,b]$ is the Perron segment between $u_a^{\mathrm P}$ and $u_b^{\mathrm P}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Convexity in $t$ and the upper limits at the endpoints bound every competitor by $(1-t)u_0+t u_1$. This bound is jointly upper semicontinuous on the interior and locally bounded above, so it persists after regularization. The local envelope theorem of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} shows that $U$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic with $\pi^*\theta+\ddc U\geq0$, and $U$ is still independent of $\Im z$. Since it dominates an admissible competitor, each slice belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$. The chord bound gives the joint upper limits at the endpoints, by the argument at the end of the proof of \cref{prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}. The envelope therefore belongs to its defining family and is its largest member.

If a competitor $w_t$ runs from $u_0$ to $u_1$, then
\[
 w_t\leq u_t^{\mathrm P}\leq(1-t)u_0+t u_1.
\]
In particular, almost everywhere,
\[
 |u_t^{\mathrm P}-u_0|\leq |w_t-u_0|+t|u_1-u_0|,
\]
which proves $u_t^{\mathrm P}\to u_0$ in $L^1$ as $t\downarrow0$. The argument at $t=1$ is the same.

For $0\leq a<b\leq1$, let $v_t$ be the Perron envelope on $(a,b)$ between $u_a^{\mathrm P}$ and $u_b^{\mathrm P}$. The original path restricted to $(a,b)$ runs between these endpoints and is admissible by \cref{prop:subgeodesic-endpoint-control}. Thus $v_t$ also runs between them and dominates $u_t^{\mathrm P}$. The joint upper limits of $v_t$ at $a$ and $b$ allow us to glue its complexification to $U$ outside the smaller strip, by \cref{lem:qpsh-gluing}; if $a=0$ or $b=1$, the same upper limits show that the glued family is still admissible for $u_0,u_1$. The resulting competitor cannot exceed $U$, which proves $v_t=u_t^{\mathrm P}$ on $(a,b)$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
When the endpoints differ by a bounded function, an explicit lower barrier is available.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:ordinary-type-perron}\index{Perron interpolation!fixed ordinary type}
Suppose that $u_0\sim u_1$, and choose $C\geq0$ with
$u_0-C\leq u_1\leq u_0+C$. Then the Perron segment from $u_0$ to $u_1$ exists, every slice has the ordinary singularity type of $u_0$ and $u_1$, and
\[
 u_0-Ct\leq u_t^{\mathrm P}\leq u_0+Ct,
 \qquad
 u_1-C(1-t)\leq u_t^{\mathrm P}\leq u_1+C(1-t).
\]
Moreover, away from the common pole set,
\[
 |u_t^{\mathrm P}-u_s^{\mathrm P}|\leq C|t-s|,
 \qquad0\leq s,t\leq1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider the barrier
\[
 w_t=\max\{u_0-Ct,\ u_1-C(1-t)\}.
\]
It is a subgeodesic by \cref{prop:hermitian-subgeodesic-calculus}, and the bounded difference gives $w_0=u_0$ and $w_1=u_1$. Upper semicontinuity gives the joint upper limits at the endpoints. Off the common poles, $|w_t-u_0|\leq Ct$ and $|w_t-u_1|\leq C(1-t)$, so $w_t$ runs from $u_0$ to $u_1$, and \cref{prop:hermitian-perron} applies.

The lower bounds follow from $w_t\leq u_t^{\mathrm P}$, and the upper bounds from the chord estimate; together they give the asserted singularity type. Off the common poles, put $f(t)=u_t^{\mathrm P}(x)$. For $0<s<t<1$, monotonicity of the slopes of secants of the convex function $f$ gives
\[
 -C\leq\frac{f(s)-f(0)}s
 \leq\frac{f(t)-f(s)}{t-s}
 \leq\frac{f(1)-f(t)}{1-t}\leq C.
\]
The two-sided estimates at the endpoints cover the cases $s=0$ and $t=1$, which proves the Lipschitz bound on $[0,1]$.
\end{proof}

For endpoints of different types, the constant path at a common lower potential $v\leq\min(u_0,u_1)$, $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, is still admissible, so the envelope exists whenever such a potential exists. Attaining the endpoints requires a barrier approaching the prescribed values; \cref{prop:hermitian-perron} separates this requirement from the existence of the envelope.

\section{The smooth product equation}

For a smooth path, positivity on the product compares the acceleration with the spatial gradient of the velocity. Let $u_t$ be smooth in $(x,t)\in X\times I$, suppose that $\eta_t=\theta+\ddc u_t>0$, and write, in local holomorphic coordinates $(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$,
\[
 \theta=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\sum_{i,j=1}^n
        g_{i\bar j}\,\mathrm dx_i\wedge\mathrm d\bar x_j,
 \qquad H=(g_{i\bar j}+\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}u),
 \qquad \xi=(\partial_i\dot u)_{i=1}^n.
\]
Here dots denote derivatives in $t$, $\xi$ is a column vector, and $\xi^*$ is its conjugate transpose. Since $t=\Real z$, the form $\pi^*\theta+\ddc U$ on the product has, in the coordinates $(x_1,\ldots,x_n,z)$, the coefficient matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 H&\tfrac12\xi\\
 \tfrac12\xi^*&\tfrac14\ddot u
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-schur-geodesic}\index{Geodesic equation!homogeneous product equation}
The form $\pi^*\theta+\ddc U$ is semipositive exactly when
\[
 \ddot u\geq\xi^*H^{-1}\xi.
\]
Its $(n+1)$st power vanishes exactly when equality holds. Equivalently, the homogeneous Monge--Amp\`ere equation on the product is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-hcma-smooth}
 \ddot u\,\eta_t^n
 =n\,\mathrm{d}\dot u\wedge\dc\dot u\wedge\eta_t^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Schur complement of $H$ in the block matrix is $(\ddot u-\xi^*H^{-1}\xi)/4$. Since $H>0$, the block matrix is semipositive exactly when this complement is nonnegative, and its determinant, which is $\det H$ times the complement, vanishes exactly when the complement vanishes.

To rewrite the equation in terms of forms, note that
\[
 \mathrm{d}\dot u\wedge\dc\dot u=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\dot u\wedge\bar\partial\dot u,
 \qquad
 n\frac{\mathrm{d}\dot u\wedge\dc\dot u\wedge\eta_t^{n-1}}{\eta_t^n}
 =\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}\xi\xi^*)=\xi^*H^{-1}\xi.
\]
Multiplying the second identity by $\eta_t^n>0$ gives \eqref{eq:hermitian-hcma-smooth}.
\end{proof}

This calculation identifies the homogeneous equation for smooth paths. Whether nonsmooth Perron segments solve a weak form of this equation is a separate question, which we do not address.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$L^2$}{L2} action and torsion}

The homogeneous equation on the product involves the spatial form $\eta_t$ only pointwise. In the $L^2$ action, integration by parts also differentiates the powers of $\eta_t$, which produces terms absent from that equation. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$, write $\omega_u=\omega+\ddc u$, and recall from \cref{chap:mass} the space $\HH_\omega$ of smooth real functions $u$ with $\omega_u>0$. On $\HH_\omega$ consider the Riemannian metric
\[
 G_u(v,w)=\int_Xvw\,\omega_u^n,
 \qquad v,w\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R).
\]
For a smooth path $u_t\in\HH_\omega$, $0\leq t\leq1$, its action is
\[
 \mathscr S(u)=\frac12\int_0^1\int_X\dot u_t^{\,2}\,\omega_{u_t}^n\,\mathrm{d}t.
\]
Stationarity under smooth variations fixing both endpoints is the formal geodesic equation of this metric.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-ltwo-stationarity}\index{Geodesic equation!Hermitian metric}\index{Torsion!geodesic equation}
Let $u_t\in\HH_\omega$, $0\leq t\leq1$, be a smooth path, and put $\eta_t=\omega_{u_t}$ and $T_t=\eta_t^{n-1}$. The path is stationary for $\mathscr S$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-ltwo-geodesic}
 \begin{split}
 \ddot u_t\,\eta_t^n={}&n\,\mathrm{d}\dot u_t\wedge\dc\dot u_t\wedge T_t\\
 &+n\dot u_t\bigl(\mathrm{d}\dot u_t\wedge\dc T_t
                  -\dc\dot u_t\wedge \mathrm{d}T_t\bigr)
   +\frac n2\dot u_t^{\,2}\ddc T_t.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
All differentials on the right act only in the $X$ variable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Vary the path as $u_t+s v_t$, where $v_t$ is smooth in $(x,t)$ and $v_0=v_1=0$, and put $f=\dot u_t$. Differentiating the action at $s=0$, using $\partial_s(\omega_{u_t+sv_t}^n)|_{s=0}=n\ddc v_t\wedge T_t$, gives
\[
 \delta\mathscr S
 =\int_0^1\left(\int_X f\dot v_t\,\eta_t^n
       +\frac n2\int_X f^2\ddc v_t\wedge T_t\right)\mathrm{d}t.
\]
We integrate by parts in $t$ in the first term, using
$\partial_t(\eta_t^n)=n\ddc f\wedge T_t$, and on $X$ in the second, using \cref{lem:ibp} with the whole form $f^2T_t$. Thus
\[
 \delta\mathscr S
 =\int_0^1\int_Xv_t\left(-\ddot u_t\eta_t^n
       -nf\ddc f\wedge T_t+\frac n2\ddc(f^2T_t)\right)\mathrm{d}t.
\]
The product rule gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \ddc(f^2T_t)={}&2f\ddc f\wedge T_t
       +2\mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc f\wedge T_t\\
 &+2f\bigl(\mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc T_t-\dc f\wedge \mathrm{d}T_t\bigr)
       +f^2\ddc T_t.
 \end{split}
\]
After the term $nf\ddc f\wedge T_t$ cancels, the integrand is $v_t$ times the difference between the right-hand and left-hand sides of \eqref{eq:hermitian-ltwo-geodesic}. Since $\eta_t>0$ on the compact set $X\times[0,1]$, every smooth $v_t$ gives an admissible variation for small $|s|$, and the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations gives both implications.
\end{proof}

For a K\"ahler background, $\mathrm{d}T_t=\dc T_t=0$, and the equation reduces to \eqref{eq:hermitian-hcma-smooth}. The Gauduchon condition $\ddc T_t=0$ on $\eta_t$ removes only the last term. Translations by constants isolate that term and give an exact test for stationarity.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:translations-gauduchon}\index{Geodesic equation!constant translations}
Fix $u\in\HH_\omega$ and a nonzero constant $a$. The path $u_t=u+at$, $0\leq t\leq1$, is a Perron segment and solves the homogeneous equation \eqref{eq:hermitian-hcma-smooth}. It is stationary for the $L^2$ action if and only if
\[
 \ddc(\omega_u^{n-1})=0.
\]
For $n\geq2$, this means that $\omega_u$ is a Gauduchon metric.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The path is admissible and equals the chord between its endpoints, so by the chord bound no admissible competitor can be larger; it is therefore the Perron segment. Its acceleration and the spatial derivatives of its velocity vanish, which proves \eqref{eq:hermitian-hcma-smooth}. In \eqref{eq:hermitian-ltwo-geodesic}, the only remaining term is
$\frac n2a^2\ddc(\omega_u^{n-1})$, which vanishes exactly under the stated condition.
\end{proof}

The three-dimensional torus in \cref{ex:gauduchon-persistence-torus} carries a Gauduchon form $\omega$ and a smooth potential $u\in\HH_\omega$ for which $\omega_u$ is not Gauduchon. Thus $u_t=u+t$ is a smooth Perron segment that is not stationary for the $L^2$ action. On a surface, the Gauduchon condition persists under perturbation of the potential, since $\ddc\omega_u=\ddc\omega$ (compare \cref{prop:gauduchon-potential-persistence}), but it does not cancel the terms in \eqref{eq:hermitian-ltwo-geodesic} involving first derivatives of $T_t$. On the Hopf background, basic potentials give a complete metric whose geodesics are the full Perron segments. Its relation to the raw action reveals a further distinction between stationarity and minimization.
