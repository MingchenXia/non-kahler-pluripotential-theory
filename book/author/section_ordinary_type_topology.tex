\section{A topology on ordinary singularity types}
\label{sec:ordinary-type-topology}

The ordinary and the ideal envelope of a potential record different parts of its singularity. If a topology on ordinary singularity types requires both envelopes to converge, their equality becomes a closed condition, which every limit of analytic singularity types satisfies. For the converse, the dyadic approximants of a potential with equal envelopes must converge to it. We therefore add coordinates that these approximants control in a fixed Hermitian background: controlled inclusions of multiplier ideals, and the weighted total variation of divisorial Lelong numbers on each fixed modification. The closure of the analytic types is then exactly the set of types with equal envelopes, on the whole space of ordinary types.

This topology depends on the zero obstacle hidden in the envelopes, and a smooth change of background moves that obstacle. Testing all smooth obstacles removes this dependence, and raises the question whether equality of the envelopes below zero implies equality below every obstacle. Examples of zero volume built from divisors in fibrations and products over curves answer this question on explicit classes of potentials. On these classes, polar curvature on the curve factors also gives complete metrics, which we compare with the complete topologies known in positive volume at the end of the section.

Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$ and write
\[
 \mathfrak S(X,\omega)=\PSH(X,\omega)/{\sim}.
\]
In this section $[u]$ denotes the ordinary class of $u$, that is, its class modulo bounded differences. For each smooth compact modification $\pi:Y\to X$, fix a Hermitian form $\gamma_\pi$ on $Y$. For a prime divisor $E\subset Y$, set $w_E=\int_E\gamma_\pi^{n-1}$, and let $\nu_E(u)=\nu(u\circ\pi,E)$ be the generic Lelong number of \cref{sec:psh-divisorial-estimates}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:fixed-model-divisorial-variation}\index{Divisor!total variation on a fixed model}
For $u,v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, the sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-model-divisorial-variation}
 d_\pi([u],[v])=\sum_{E\subset Y\text{ prime}}w_E|\nu_E(u)-\nu_E(v)|
\end{equation}
is finite and defines a pseudometric on $\mathfrak S(X,\omega)$. Changing $\gamma_\pi$ gives an equivalent pseudometric on this fixed model.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The current $T=\pi^*\omega+\ddc(u\circ\pi)$ is positive. Divisorial removal in \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} gives, for every finite family of prime divisors,
\[
 \sum_Ew_E\nu_E(u)\leq\int_YT\wedge\gamma_\pi^{n-1}<\infty.
\]
Taking the supremum over finite families shows that the sum converges and that only countably many coefficients are positive. The same bound holds for $v$. Bounded changes of $u$ or $v$ do not affect the coefficients, and the triangle inequality holds term by term. Finally, two Hermitian forms on the compact manifold $Y$ bound one another by fixed positive multiples, and so do the corresponding divisor weights.
\end{proof}

For $t>0$ and $a>1$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:controlled-ideal-entourage}
 \mathcal U(t,a)=\{([u],[v]):\mathcal I(atu)\subseteq\mathcal I(tv),\quad
                         \mathcal I(atv)\subseteq\mathcal I(tu)\}.
\end{equation}
These are relations between ordinary classes, since a bounded change leaves every multiplier ideal unchanged. Let $d_I$ be the pullback of the metric \eqref{eq:ideal-lone-metric}, in background $\omega$, under $[u]\mapsto[u]_{\mathcal I}$, and put
\[
 d_O([u],[v])=\|P_\omega[u]-P_\omega[v]\|_{L^1(\mathrm dV)}.
\]
Both are pseudometrics on ordinary classes: $d_I$ compares the ideal envelopes, whereas $d_O$ also detects differences of ordinary envelopes within a single multiplier-ideal class.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ordinary-type-uniformity}\index{Singularity type!uniform topology}
Finite intersections of the relations $d_O<r$, $d_I<r$, $d_\pi<r$ and $\mathcal U(t,a)$, with $r,t>0$ and $a>1$, form a base of entourages for a uniformity on $\mathfrak S(X,\omega)$. Denote its topology by $\tau_{\omega,0}$, where the subscript $0$ records the zero obstacle in the envelopes. A net $[u_j]$ converges to $[u]$ if and only if both envelopes converge in $L^1$, $d_\pi([u_j],[u])\to0$ for every fixed $\pi$, and $([u_j],[u])\in\mathcal U(t,a)$ eventually for every fixed $t,a$. Smooth positive choices of $\mathrm dV$ and of the $\gamma_\pi$ do not change the uniformity.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All generators are symmetric and contain the diagonal. Multiplier ideals decrease as the weight increases, as one sees from local integrability after subtracting a local upper bound from the potential. For $b=\sqrt a$, put $\mathcal V=\mathcal U(t,b)\cap\mathcal U(bt,b)$. If $([x],[y]),([y],[z])\in\mathcal V$, then
\[
 \mathcal I(atx)\subseteq\mathcal I(bty)\subseteq\mathcal I(tz),
\]
and the same argument with $x$ and $z$ exchanged shows that $\mathcal V\circ\mathcal V\subseteq\mathcal U(t,a)$. For the pseudometric relations, halving the radius gives the corresponding refinement under composition. Taking finite intersections of these refinements proves the axioms of a uniformity, and convergence in it means eventual membership in each generating entourage. Bounded ratios of smooth positive volume forms and \cref{lem:fixed-model-divisorial-variation} show that the auxiliary choices do not matter.
\end{proof}

Each entourage involves only finitely many fixed modifications. The definition therefore requires neither a countable cofinal family of modifications nor convergence that is uniform over all modifications.

\subsection{Analytic closure}

We now show that the dyadic approximants of \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density} converge in $\tau_{\omega,0}$ exactly when the two envelopes agree. Let $\mathcal A_\omega$ be the set of ordinary classes with analytic singularities, with rational coefficients as in \cref{sec:analytic-types}; the rational curvature correction in \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density} keeps the approximants in this set. In the controlled ideal relations, the scale factor of that correction has to be taken into account.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ordinary-topology-analytic-closure}\index{Analytic singularity!closure in the ordinary-type topology}
The closure and the sequential closure of $\mathcal A_\omega$ in $\tau_{\omega,0}$ are both equal to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary-topology-analytic-closure}
 \mathcal G_\omega=\{[u]:P_\omega[u]=P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}\}.
\end{equation}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, the dyadic sequence $b_j$ of \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density} satisfies
\[
 [b_j]\longrightarrow[u]\text{ in }\tau_{\omega,0}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad [u]\in\mathcal G_\omega.
\]
The approximants may be chosen with one positive rational analytic coefficient on all charts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We use the notation of \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density}: $m_j=2^j$, $a_j$ has type $(1/m_j,\mathcal I(m_ju))$, $c_j=(1+\epsilon_j)^{-1}\uparrow1$ is rational, and $b_j=c_ja_j$. The ordinary and ideal envelopes of $b_j$ agree and decrease to $P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Thus the two envelope coordinates of $[b_j]$ converge to those of $[u]$ exactly when $[u]\in\mathcal G_\omega$. It remains to show that the other coordinates always converge.

Fix $t>0$ and $a>1$, and choose $1<s<a$. For large $j$, one has $ac_j>s$ and $m_j\geq st/(s-1)$, so \cref{lem:bergman-controlled-ideals} gives
\[
 \mathcal I(atb_j)=\mathcal I(atc_ja_j)
 \subseteq\mathcal I(sta_j)\subseteq\mathcal I(tu).
\]
Since $u\preceq a_j\preceq b_j$, the reverse relation $\mathcal I(atu)\subseteq\mathcal I(tb_j)$ holds for every $j$. The factor $c_j$ enters the weight in the first inclusion, which is why we chose $s<a$.

For a divisor $E$ on a fixed modification $\pi$, the Bergman divisorial estimate gives
\[
 c_j\max\left\{\nu_E(u)-\frac{A(E)}{m_j},0\right\}
 \leq\nu_E(b_j)\leq c_j\nu_E(u)\leq\nu_E(u),
\]
where $A(E)$ is the log discrepancy, computed over a coordinate neighborhood of the center of $E$. Each coefficient therefore converges to $\nu_E(u)$, and the summability in \cref{lem:fixed-model-divisorial-variation} gives $d_\pi([b_j],[u])\to0$ by dominated convergence. This proves convergence of all the remaining coordinates.

Finally, analytic classes have equal envelopes by \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}. The map $[u]\mapsto(P_\omega[u],P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I})$ is continuous into $L^1\times L^1$, whose diagonal is closed. Thus every limit of a net of analytic classes lies in $\mathcal G_\omega$. The sequence $b_j$ gives the converse and the sequential assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Separation and mass}

The topology $\tau_{\omega,0}$ sees an ordinary class only through its envelopes, its multiplier ideals and its divisorial Lelong numbers. Distinct ordinary types can have the same ordinary envelope, and such types cannot be separated.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ordinary-topology-nonseparation}\index{Singularity type!nonseparation}
Two classes have the same $\tau_{\omega,0}$ neighborhoods if and only if their ordinary envelopes coincide. This topology is not $T_0$ and cannot be induced by a metric on $\mathfrak S(X,\omega)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The coordinate $d_O$ distinguishes different ordinary envelopes. Conversely, potentials with equal ordinary envelopes have equal multiplier ideals at every weight by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}. Their ideal envelopes and all their divisorial coefficients therefore agree, so every generating entourage has identical sections at the two classes.

To produce distinct classes of this kind, choose a cutoff logarithmic pole $w\leq0$ supported in a coordinate ball, plurisubharmonic near its pole and smooth on the annulus where the cutoff varies. After multiplication by a small positive constant, $w\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. Put $\chi(s)=1-\sqrt{1-s}$ for $s\leq0$ and $u=\chi(w)$. The inequalities $0<\chi'\leq1/2$ and $\chi''\geq0$, applied first to bounded truncations, give $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. In local coordinates $z$ centered at the pole, $u$ has size comparable to $\sqrt{-\log|z|^2}$, so it is unbounded and $\mathrm e^{-tu}$ is integrable for every $t>0$. Hence all its multiplier ideals are trivial and $P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}=0$.

For every $0<\delta\leq1$, the function $\delta s-\chi(s)$ is bounded above on $(-\infty,0]$. Hence $\delta w\preceq u$, and $\delta w$ is a competitor for the ordinary envelope of $u$. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ off the pole gives $P_\omega[u]=0$. Thus $[u]$ and $[0]$ are distinct classes with the same neighborhoods.
\end{proof}

By \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, the two envelopes have the extended masses
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(P_\omega[u]),\qquad
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}).
\]
In the present positive background, both are at most $\int_X\omega^n$. For the ideal envelope this follows from \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i,prop:fixed-point-finite}. For the ordinary envelope, normalize $u\leq0$ and put $p_j=P_\omega(u+j,0)$ and $p=P_\omega[u]$, with the obstacle envelopes of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}. Then $p_j\to p$ in capacity by \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}. For $k>0$, set $\eta_k=\min\{1,(u+k)_+\}$. If $j>k$, the contact set of $p_j$ with $u+j$ lies outside $\{\eta_k>0\}$, so \cref{prop:rooftop-measure} gives $\eta_k\mathrm{MA}_\omega(p_j)\leq\eta_k\omega^n$. Testing against a nonnegative continuous function and applying \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc}, we obtain the same inequality for $p$. Letting $k\to\infty$ proves $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(p)\leq\omega^n$, since the remaining set $\{u=-\infty\}$ is pluripolar.

The potential itself can nevertheless have infinite mass: \cref{prop:goodness-infinite-mass} gives such a $u$ whose two envelopes are both zero. Its ordinary class belongs to $\mathcal G_\omega$. Thus the topology and its analytic-closure criterion need no mass condition on the representative, whereas the two canonical envelopes never have infinite mass in a Hermitian background.

\subsection{Transport with an obstacle}

A smooth change of background $\omega'=\omega+\ddc\chi$ identifies $\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $\PSH(X,\omega')$ by $u\mapsto u-\chi$, but it moves the zero obstacle to $-\chi$. To compare the topologies in different backgrounds, we therefore need envelopes below an arbitrary smooth obstacle.

For a smooth real obstacle $F$, we use the ordinary envelope $P_{\omega,F}[u]$ of \cref{def:singularity-envelope}, and we write $P_{\omega,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$ for the ideal envelope defined with $v\leq F$ in place of $v\leq0$. The competitor family of the latter contains a translate of $u$. Replacing a competitor by its maximum with this translate shows that the ideal envelope may equivalently be defined with the condition $v\preceq_{\mathcal I}u$. The same notation applies to any smooth real background that admits $u$. Define $\tau_{\omega,F}$ by replacing the two envelope coordinates in \cref{prop:ordinary-type-uniformity} by these obstacle envelopes.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ordinary-topology-background-transport}
Let $\chi\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$ and $\omega'=\omega+\ddc\chi>0$. The map $[u]\mapsto[u-\chi]$ is a uniform isomorphism
\[
 (\mathfrak S(X,\omega),\tau_{\omega,F})
 \longrightarrow(\mathfrak S(X,\omega'),\tau_{\omega',F-\chi}).
\]
For $s>0$, $[u]\mapsto[su]$ is a uniform isomorphism from $\tau_{\omega,F}$ to $\tau_{s\omega,sF}$. Both maps transport the ordinary and the ideal envelopes exactly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For either envelope operator $P$, the correspondence of competitors gives
\[
 P_{\omega',F-\chi}[u-\chi]=P_{\omega,F}[u]-\chi,
 \qquad P_{s\omega,sF}[su]=sP_{\omega,F}[u].
\]
Smooth additions leave all multiplier ideals and divisorial Lelong numbers unchanged. Scaling multiplies the divisorial and envelope distances by $s$ and changes the ideal weights by $t\mapsto st$. Each map and its inverse therefore transport the generating entourages to generating entourages.
\end{proof}

For two smooth obstacles, translation of competitors gives
\[
 P_{\omega,F}[u]+\min_X(G-F)
 \leq P_{\omega,G}[u]
 \leq P_{\omega,F}[u]+\max_X(G-F),
\]
and the same inequalities hold for the ideal envelopes. By the local construction in \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, the extended measures satisfy $\mathrm{MA}_{\omega'}(u-\chi)=\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u)$ and $\mathrm{MA}_{s\omega}(su)=s^n\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u)$, also when the total mass is infinite. If instead the zero obstacle is kept in both backgrounds, the transport gives $P_{\omega',0}[u-\chi]=P_{\omega,\chi}[u]-\chi$, so the change of background compares the obstacles $0$ and $\chi$. The bounded comparison of obstacles does not make this change continuous, even on a curve.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ordinary-topology-gauge-failure}\index{Singularity type!failure of smooth-gauge continuity}
Let $Y$ be a compact connected Riemann surface and $\theta>0$ a smooth form of mass one. If $F$ is a nonconstant smooth function with $\theta'=\theta+\ddc F>0$, then the bijection
\[
 \Gamma_F:(\mathfrak S(Y,\theta),\tau_{\theta,0})
 \longrightarrow(\mathfrak S(Y,\theta'),\tau_{\theta',0}),
 \qquad [u]\longmapsto[u-F],
\]
and its inverse are both discontinuous. Equivalently, $\tau_{\theta,0}$ and $\tau_{\theta,F}$ are incomparable topologies on the same space of ordinary types.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The smooth function $\theta'/\theta$ has integral one against the probability measure $\theta$, and it is not constant: otherwise $\ddc F=0$, and the harmonic function $F$ would be constant on $Y$. Choose $c$ with $\min_Y(\theta'/\theta)<c<1$, so that $\theta'\not\geq c\theta$.

Next choose probability measures $\mu_j$ without atoms, each carried by a polar set, that converge weakly to $\theta$. To construct them, start with finite atomic probability measures converging weakly to $\theta$, as in \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion}, and replace each atom by a nonatomic probability measure carried by a polar Cantor set in a small coordinate disc about that atom, keeping its weight; the construction in \cref{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-model} provides such measures in every disc. If the diameters of the discs tend to zero uniformly, uniform continuity of continuous test functions preserves weak convergence, and the finite union of the polar carriers is polar.

Let $q_j$ be the normalized Green potentials with $\theta+\ddc q_j=\mu_j$ and $\sup_Yq_j=0$. By normalized compactness, the sequence has $L^1$ cluster values, and each of them has curvature $\theta$ and supremum zero, hence vanishes. Thus $q_j\to0$ in $L^1$. Put $u_j=cq_j$. Then
\[
 \theta+\ddc u_j=(1-c)\theta+c\mu_j,\qquad
 u_j\leq P_\theta[u_j]\leq0.
\]
Since $\mu_j$ has no atoms, all point Lelong numbers of $u_j$ vanish. Because modifications of a smooth curve are isomorphisms, \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} gives trivial multiplier ideals at every weight. Thus $P_\theta[u_j]_{\mathcal I}=0$, all controlled ideal relations with $[0]$ hold, and every divisorial distance to $[0]$ vanishes. The bounds above give convergence of the ordinary envelopes to zero in $L^1$. Consequently $[u_j]\to[0]$ in $\tau_{\theta,0}$.

Set $v_j=P_{\theta,F}[u_j]$. Every competitor $h\preceq u_j$ satisfies $\theta+\ddc h\geq c\mu_j$, by \cref{cor:polar-order-maximum}. The family of competitors below $F$ contains a translate of $u_j$ and is stable under finite maxima, so Choquet selection and distributional convergence of increasing maxima show that $v_j$ satisfies the same inequality. If $[u_j]\to[0]$ in $\tau_{\theta,F}$, the ordinary-envelope coordinate would give $v_j\to P_{\theta,F}[0]=F$ in $L^1$, because $F$ is a bounded $\theta$-plurisubharmonic competitor. Passing to the limit in the current inequality would then give $\theta'\geq c\theta$, a contradiction.

By \cref{prop:ordinary-topology-background-transport}, $[u]\mapsto[u-F]$ is a uniform isomorphism from $\tau_{\theta,F}$ onto $\tau_{\theta',0}$, so the sequence above shows that $\Gamma_F$ is discontinuous. The same argument applied to $\theta'$ and $-F$ shows that the inverse is discontinuous. Under this transport, continuity of $\Gamma_F$ and of its inverse are precisely the two comparisons between $\tau_{\theta,0}$ and $\tau_{\theta,F}$, which proves incomparability.
\end{proof}

The sequence $u_j$ has trivial multiplier ideals, and its polar curvature in the sense of \cref{sec:local-polar-parts}, that is, the restriction $\mathbf1_{\{u_j=-\infty\}}(\theta+\ddc u_j)$ of its curvature to its pole set, is $c\mu_j$. By the classification of curve models through their polar curvature in \cref{lem:curve-polar-model} and the bounded obstacle comparison, its ordinary envelopes in both backgrounds have non-pluripolar mass $1-c$. The discontinuity therefore persists among models whose masses are bounded below by a fixed positive constant. On the same curve, the total variation of polar curvature is a complete metric on ordinary models, by \cref{thm:curve-model-volume-distance}, and after ordinary projection it is invariant under smooth changes of background; it keeps the diffuse curvature that the sequence above loses.
