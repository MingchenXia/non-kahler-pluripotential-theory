\section{An incomplete metric in a big class}
\label{sec:incomplete-divisorial-metric}

The ample locus of a big closed class is the complement of its non-K\"ahler locus. A positive solution of a Monge--Amp\`ere equation on this locus need not define a complete metric. We give an explicit example in which the minimal divisorial singularity cancels the pole of an exponential equation. The question of completeness for this equation is raised in \cite[Question~3.5]{DNGL26}. The example concerns arbitrary big classes and divisors; the additional compatibility conditions arising from a K\"ahler--Ricci flow require separate analysis.

For a compact K\"ahler manifold $Y$ and a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ representing a big class, $\mathcal E^1(Y,\theta)$ denotes the classical finite-energy class for a big closed class on a compact K\"ahler manifold, relative to a potential with minimal singularities; see \cite[Section~2]{BEGZ10}. The brackets $\langle\cdot\rangle$ denote non-pluripolar products, and $\operatorname{Amp}(\alpha)$ is the complement of the non-K\"ahler locus of a big closed class $\alpha$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:incomplete-divisorial-metric}
There exist a smooth projective surface $Y$, a smooth irreducible divisor $E$, a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ with big class $\alpha$, a Hermitian metric on $\mathcal O_Y(E)$, a defining section $\sigma$, and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V_Y$ with the following properties. The equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incomplete-divisorial-equation}
 \left\langle(\theta+\ddc\rho)^2\right\rangle
 =\mathrm{e}^{\rho}|\sigma|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}V_Y
\end{equation}
has a unique solution $\rho\in\mathcal E^1(Y,\theta)$, and this solution has minimal singularities. Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Amp}(\alpha)=Y\setminus E
\]
is precisely the open locus on which $\theta+\ddc\rho$ is smooth and strictly positive. Its restriction to every nonempty connected open subset of $Y\setminus E$ is incomplete. In particular, no dense Zariski open subset carries a complete induced metric.
\end{theorem}

We first prove the elementary extension estimate used to identify the minimal singularities. Let $p=[1:0:0]\in\mathbb P^2$, and let $\omega_2$ be the Fubini--Study form given in the affine coordinates $z_1=Z_1/Z_0$, $z_2=Z_2/Z_0$ by
\[
 \omega_2=\ddc\log(1+|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2).
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projective-puncture-upper-bound}
Every $\omega_2$-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^2\setminus\{p\}$ is bounded above there.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For such a function $v$, the function
$h=v+\log(1+|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2)$ is plurisubharmonic on a punctured bidisc $\Delta_R^2\setminus\{0\}$. Choose $0<r<R$. The compact set
\[
 K=\{|z_1|\leq r,\ |z_2|=r\}
\]
avoids the puncture, so $h\leq M$ on $K$ for a finite $M$. For each $0<|z_1|\leq r$, the subharmonic maximum principle in the $z_2$-disc gives $h(z_1,z_2)\leq M$ for $|z_2|\leq r$. For $z_1=0$ and $0<|z_2|<r$, apply the maximum principle in the $z_1$-disc, whose boundary is already covered by this bound. Thus $v$ is bounded above near $p$. Upper semicontinuity and compactness bound it on the complement of a smaller neighborhood.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:incomplete-divisorial-metric}]
\emph{The surface and the divisor.}
Use the incidence model
\[
 Y=\{([Z_0:Z_1:Z_2],[W_1:W_2])\in\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^1:
           Z_1W_2=Z_2W_1\}.
\]
The projections $\pi:Y\to\mathbb P^2$ and $q:Y\to\mathbb P^1$ identify $Y$ with the blow-up at $p$, with exceptional divisor $E=\pi^{-1}(p)$. Let $\omega_1$ be the Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^1$ with the same normalization as $\omega_2$. Write
\[
 \beta=\pi^*\omega_2,\qquad \gamma=q^*\omega_1,
 \qquad \Omega=\beta+\gamma,
\]
The form $\Omega$ is K\"ahler, being the restriction of the product metric.

The projection $(Z_0,Z_1,Z_2)\mapsto(Z_1,Z_2)$ gives a section $\sigma$ of
\[
 \pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(1)\otimes q^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1).
\]
Indeed, its image belongs to the tautological line represented by $[W_1:W_2]$. The section vanishes simply along $E$, identifying this line bundle with $\mathcal O_Y(E)$. For the product of the Fubini--Study and induced tautological metrics, its squared norm is
\[
 s=|\sigma|^2=
 \frac{|Z_1|^2+|Z_2|^2}{|Z_0|^2+|Z_1|^2+|Z_2|^2}.
\]
In a blow-up chart $\pi(t,w)=(t,tw)$ and $q(t,w)=[1:w]$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incomplete-divisor-norm}
 s=\frac{|t|^2(1+|w|^2)}{1+|t|^2(1+|w|^2)},
 \qquad \ddc\log s=[E]+\gamma-\beta.
\end{equation}
The current identity follows either from this formula and the second blow-up chart or from Poincar\'e--Lelong.

\emph{The big class and its equation.}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incomplete-current}
 \theta=3\beta-2\gamma,\qquad \rho=2\log s,
 \qquad T=\theta+\ddc\rho=\beta+2[E].
\end{equation}
Thus $\rho\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$. The class $\alpha=\{\theta\}$ is big: the potential $\psi=(5/2)\log s$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incomplete-strict-current}
 \theta+\ddc\psi
 =\tfrac12(\beta+\gamma)+\tfrac52[E]\geq\tfrac12\Omega.
\end{equation}
It is not nef, since $\int_E\theta=-2$: the restriction of $\beta$ to $E$ vanishes and $q|_E$ is an isomorphism to $\mathbb P^1$. A nef class has nonnegative degree on every curve by its defining smooth approximations.

On $Y\setminus E$ define $\mathrm{d}V_Y=\beta^2/s$. To check extension across $E$, put
\[
 \mathrm{d}\lambda_{t,w}
 =\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}2\mathrm{d}t\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar t
       \wedge\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}2\mathrm{d}w\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar w.
\]
Use the analogous Euclidean volume $\mathrm{d}\lambda_{z_1,z_2}$ in the affine coordinates of $\mathbb P^2$. The normalized Fubini--Study volume there is
\[
 \omega_2^2=\frac{2}{\pi^2}
 \frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{z_1,z_2}}{(1+|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2)^3}.
\]
Since the complex Jacobian of $(t,w)\mapsto(t,tw)$ is $t$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \beta^2&=\frac{2}{\pi^2}
 \frac{|t|^2\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t,w}}
      {[1+|t|^2(1+|w|^2)]^3},\\
 \mathrm{d}V_Y&=\frac{2}{\pi^2}
 \frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t,w}}
      {(1+|w|^2)[1+|t|^2(1+|w|^2)]^2}.
\end{align*}
The second expression is smooth and strictly positive at $t=0$. Interchanging the two homogeneous coordinates gives the extension on the other chart. The extensions agree off $E$, hence everywhere on their overlap.

The non-pluripolar product of $T$ agrees with $\beta^2$ off $E$ and gives no mass to $E$. The smooth measure $\beta^2$ also gives no mass to $E$, so
\[
 \langle T^2\rangle=\beta^2,
 \qquad
 \mathrm{e}^{\rho}|\sigma|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}V_Y
   =s^2s^{-1}\frac{\beta^2}{s}=\beta^2.
\]
The right side of \eqref{eq:incomplete-divisorial-equation} is defined on $Y\setminus E$ and extended by zero on $E$; no product of zero and infinity is taken there. This proves the equation and gives $\int_Y\langle T^2\rangle=\int_{\mathbb P^2}\omega_2^2=1$.

\emph{Minimal singularities and energy.}
For $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$, the function $u-\rho$ on $Y\setminus E$ descends to an $\omega_2$-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^2\setminus\{p\}$, because
\[
 \beta+\ddc(u-\rho)=\theta+\ddc u\geq0
 \quad\text{on }Y\setminus E.
\]
By \cref{lem:projective-puncture-upper-bound}, $u\leq\rho+C_u$ there. Near $E$, \eqref{eq:incomplete-divisor-norm} gives $u(t,w)\leq2\log|t|^2+O(1)$. Add a smooth local potential of the closed form $\theta$, restrict to a transverse disc, and apply the submean inequality at its center. Letting the radius tend to zero shows that $u=-\infty$ on $E$. The comparison therefore holds on all of $Y$, proving minimality.

Let $V_\theta$ be the normalized minimal envelope. Since $s\leq1$, minimality gives
\[
 \rho\leq V_\theta\leq\rho+C.
\]
Thus $\rho-V_\theta$ is bounded off $E$. The defining relative energy is
\[
 E_\theta(\rho)=\frac13\sum_{j=0}^2
 \int_Y(\rho-V_\theta)
 \left\langle(\theta+\ddc\rho)^j\wedge
                (\theta+\ddc V_\theta)^{2-j}\right\rangle.
\]
The mixed measures have finite mass in the closed K\"ahler theory and do not charge $E$. Hence $E_\theta(\rho)>-\infty$, so $\rho\in\mathcal E^1(Y,\theta)$. Here we use the standard finiteness of non-pluripolar products and the finite-energy definition in \cite[Proposition~1.6 and Section~2.2]{BEGZ10}. A potential with minimal singularities has full mass; in this example that mass is $1$.

\emph{Uniqueness.}
Suppose $u\in\mathcal E^1(Y,\theta)$ is another solution, and let $\mathrm{d}\mu=s^{-1}\mathrm{d}V_Y$ on $Y\setminus E$, extended by zero on $E$. This measure is locally smooth and positive off $E$, but has infinite total mass. The full-mass comparison principle \cite[Corollary~2.3]{BEGZ10} gives
\[
 \int_{\{u<\rho\}}\mathrm{e}^{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
 \leq\int_{\{u<\rho\}}\mathrm{e}^{u}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
\]
Both integrals are finite, since the two Monge--Amp\`ere measures have mass $1$. The strictly larger integrand on the left forces $\mu(\{u<\rho\})=0$. Interchanging $u$ and $\rho$ gives $\mu(\{\rho<u\})=0$ as well. They therefore agree almost everywhere for smooth volume on $Y\setminus E$, and hence on $Y$, since $E$ has zero smooth volume. Equality of the canonical upper-semicontinuous representatives of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions gives $u=\rho$ everywhere.

\emph{The ample locus and incompleteness.}
The current in \eqref{eq:incomplete-strict-current} is smooth off $E$, while every global $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential is $-\infty$ on $E$. Thus no K\"ahler current in $\alpha$ can be smooth near a point of $E$. The characterization in \cref{thm:bc-non-kahler-locus} gives
\[
 \operatorname{Amp}(\alpha)=Y\setminus E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)
 =Y\setminus E.
\]
The current $T$ equals $\beta$ there and is strictly positive because $\pi$ is a biholomorphism away from $E$. Its nonzero divisorial part $2[E]$ prevents smoothness at every point of $E$.

Let $U\subset Y\setminus E$ be a nonempty connected open set, and set $W=\pi(U)$. The map $\pi:(U,T|_U)\to(W,\omega_2|_W)$ is an isometry. Choose $x\in W$ and a smooth path $c:[0,1]\to\mathbb P^2$ from $x$ to $p$. Its first exit time
\[
 t_0=\min\{t\in[0,1]:c(t)\notin W\}
\]
is positive, and $c([0,t_0))\subset W$. This path has finite Fubini--Study length. For $t_k\nearrow t_0$, its tail lengths show that $c(t_k)$ is Cauchy for the intrinsic metric of $W$. It has no limit there: intrinsic convergence implies ambient convergence, whose limit is $c(t_0)\notin W$. Thus $U$ is incomplete. Applying this argument to each connected component proves the assertion for a possibly disconnected dense Zariski open set.
\end{proof}

The cancellation $\mathrm{e}^{\rho}|\sigma|^{-2}=s$ leaves the deleted point at finite distance. Consequently, the big-class exponential equation with an arbitrary smooth divisor does not force completeness, even for its unique finite-energy solution with minimal singularities. The example has a non-nef class and does not settle the same question with a nef hypothesis. It also does not settle a flow conjecture in which the class, divisor and singularity coefficients must arise from specified initial data; compare \cite[Section~3.1]{DNGL26}.
