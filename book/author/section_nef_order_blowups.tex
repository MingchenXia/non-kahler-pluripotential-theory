\subsection{Strict changes under one blowup}\label{sec:nef-strict-blowups}

The inequalities of \cref{prop:nef-modification-volume} can be strict. A blowup changes how much smooth volume lies near the zero set of a coefficient. Near a point of $\mathbb P^m$, the Fubini--Study volume of the ball $\{|z|^2<s\}$ is of order $s^m$; after the point is blown up, the corresponding neighborhood of the exceptional divisor has volume of order $s$ for a K\"ahler form upstairs. For product backgrounds whose fiber coefficient vanishes at the blown-up point, the smooth equation can be solved explicitly, and this change in the distribution of volume produces a strict increase of the order.

Throughout this subsection let $B=\mathbb P^m$ with $m\geq2$, and let $H$ be the Fubini--Study form normalized by $\int_BH^m=1$. Let $\pi\colon\widetilde B\to B$ be the blowup of the origin of the affine chart $\{Z_0\neq0\}$. The projection $\rho\colon\widetilde B\to\mathbb P^{m-1}$ from the incidence description gives the form $L=\rho^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, with $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ normalized in the same way; we also write $H$ for $\pi^*H$. The incidence embedding of $\widetilde B$ in $\mathbb P^m\times\mathbb P^{m-1}$ makes $\kappa=H+L$ a K\"ahler form.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nef-strict-blowup-orders}\index{Nef degeneration!strict blowup jump}
For all integers $m\geq2$ and $p,k\geq1$, there are a single blowup, along a smooth center, of a projective manifold of dimension $m+p$ and a smooth semipositive nonclosed background on it such that the background and its pullback both have common orders, namely
\[
 a_{\mathrm{down}}=\max\{p-m/k,0\},\qquad a_{\mathrm{up}}=p-1/k.
\]
Every positive integer occurs as the increase $a_{\mathrm{up}}-a_{\mathrm{down}}$, for a suitable dimension. In the same family, a background of positive volume can pull back to a background of volume zero, with both orders equal to zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the backgrounds.} Put $W_0=\mathbb P^p$, with Fubini--Study form $\omega$ normalized by $\int_{W_0}\omega^p=1$. Write $s=|z|^2$ on the affine chart of $B$. Choose a smooth function $Q(s)\geq0$ that equals $s^k$ near zero, is positive for $s>0$, and is constant for large $s$; it defines a smooth function $q$ on $B$. Put $n=m+p$, choose $b>0$, and define
\[
 \theta=bH+q\omega\quad\text{on }B\times W_0,\qquad
 \widetilde\theta=bH+(q\circ\pi)\omega
 \quad\text{on }\widetilde B\times W_0.
\]
The map $\Pi=\pi\times\mathrm{id}$ is the blowup of the smooth center $\{0\}\times W_0$, and $\widetilde\theta=\Pi^*\theta$. Fix the K\"ahler perturbation directions $h=H+\omega$ and $\widetilde h=\kappa+\omega$, and the smooth positive probability densities $\mu=H^m\wedge\omega^p$ and $\widetilde\mu=\kappa^m\wedge\omega^p/(2^m-1)$. Both backgrounds are smooth and semipositive, and they are not closed, since $\mathrm d\theta=\mathrm dq\wedge\omega\neq0$.

\emph{Step 2: explicit solutions.} Write $\nu_{\mathrm{down}}=H^m$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{up}}=\kappa^m/(2^m-1)$ for the base forms; we show below that both are probability forms. For either product, write the perturbed background as $\xi=A+g\omega$, where downstairs $A=(b+\epsilon)H$, upstairs $A=(b+\epsilon)H+\epsilon L$, and in both cases $g=q+\epsilon$, with pullback understood upstairs. The form $A$ is K\"ahler on the relevant base. Let $\nu$ be the corresponding base probability form, and put $M=\int A^m$ and $J=\int g^{-p}\nu$. The smooth equation of \cref{thm:tw-smooth}, together with Stokes' theorem, gives a smooth $u$ on the base with
\[
 \alpha=A+\ddc u>0,\qquad \alpha^m=(M/J)g^{-p}\nu.
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Let $\Xi(s)$ denote the mass of $\nu$ on the set $\{|z|^2<s\}$. These cumulative masses are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-radial-reference-masses}
 \begin{split}
 \Xi_{\mathrm{down}}(s)&=\left(\frac{s}{1+s}\right)^m,\\
 \Xi_{\mathrm{up}}(s)&=\frac{\left(1+\frac{s}{1+s}\right)^m-1}{2^m-1}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
To check these formulas and to compute $M$, write $t=\log|z|^2$ on the punctured affine chart. For a radial potential $F(t)$, polar integration of its complex Hessian gives
\[
 \int_{s_1<|z|^2<s_2}(\ddc F(\log|z|^2))^m
 =F'(\log s_2)^m-F'(\log s_1)^m.
\]
The potentials of $H$ and $L$ are $\log(1+\mathrm{e}^t)$ and $t$. Thus the potential of $xH+yL$ has slopes $y$ and $x+y$ at the two ends, and the total mass of $xH+yL$ is $(x+y)^m-y^m$. The cases $(x,y)=(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$ give \eqref{eq:nef-radial-reference-masses}; the exceptional divisor and the hyperplane at infinity have zero smooth volume. In particular, $M=(b+\epsilon)^m$ downstairs and $M=(b+2\epsilon)^m-\epsilon^m$ upstairs. The form
\[
 \Omega=\alpha+g\omega=\xi+\ddc u>0,\qquad
\Omega^n=\binom{n}{m}\frac{M}{J}\nu\wedge\omega^p
\]
solves the normalized equation, since $\nu\wedge\omega^p$ is the chosen probability density. The Gauduchon metric conformal to it is
\[
 \gamma_*=g^{-p/(n-1)}\Omega.
\]
Indeed, its $(n-1)$st power is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-balanced-product-power}
 \gamma_*^{n-1}
 =\binom{n-1}{m}g^{-1}\alpha^m\wedge\omega^{p-1}
 +\binom{n-1}{m-1}\alpha^{m-1}\wedge\omega^p.
\end{equation}
In the first term a function on the base multiplies a top-degree form on the base, and every factor in the second term is closed; hence both terms are closed, and $\gamma_*$ is balanced, in particular Gauduchon. \Cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula} gives the exact identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-strict-blowup-volumes}
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h)
 =\binom{n}{m}\frac{(b+\epsilon)^m}{J_{\mathrm{down}}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{\widetilde\mu}(\widetilde\theta+\epsilon\widetilde h)
 =\binom{n}{m}
 \frac{(b+2\epsilon)^m-\epsilon^m}{J_{\mathrm{up}}},
\end{equation}
where $J_{\mathrm{down}}=\int_B(q+\epsilon)^{-p}\nu_{\mathrm{down}}$ and $J_{\mathrm{up}}=\int_{\widetilde B}(q\circ\pi+\epsilon)^{-p}\nu_{\mathrm{up}}$, and shows that $\gamma_*$ attains the infimum.

\emph{Step 3: asymptotics of the denominators.} Near zero, differentiation of \eqref{eq:nef-radial-reference-masses} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-radial-measure-asymptotics}
 \mathrm{d}\Xi_{\mathrm{down}}=(m s^{m-1}+O(s^m))\,\mathrm{d} s,\qquad
 \mathrm{d}\Xi_{\mathrm{up}}=\left(\frac{m}{2^m-1}+O(s)\right)\mathrm{d} s.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
For a density $(a s^{d-1}+O(s^d))\,\mathrm{d} s$ near zero and $q=s^k$, the integral of $(q+\epsilon)^{-p}$ over a fixed small interval $[0,s_0]$ has the following leading behavior as $\epsilon\downarrow0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-radial-singular-integral}
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{a}{k}\mathrm B(d/k,p-d/k)\epsilon^{d/k-p}
     &kp>d,\\[4pt]
 \displaystyle\frac{a}{k}\log(1/\epsilon)&kp=d,\\[4pt]
 \text{a finite positive limit}&kp<d.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathrm B(u,v)=\int_0^\infty t^{u-1}(1+t)^{-u-v}\,\mathrm{d} t$ is the Beta function. In the first case substitute $s=\epsilon^{1/k}v$ and then $t=v^k$. In the second case the integrand is asymptotic to $a/s$ on the range $\epsilon^{1/k}\ll s\ll1$. The error term $O(s^d)$ is negligible: on a sufficiently small fixed interval its relative size is arbitrarily small, while the integral over the complementary interval stays bounded. For $kp<d$, monotone convergence applies, since the limiting density is integrable. The numerators in \eqref{eq:nef-strict-blowup-volumes} tend to $\binom{n}{m}b^m>0$, and the integrals over the complement of a neighborhood of the origin stay bounded. Hence \eqref{eq:nef-radial-measure-asymptotics} and \eqref{eq:nef-radial-singular-integral}, with $d=m$ downstairs and $d=1$ upstairs, give the stated orders. Downstairs the order is $p-m/k$ when $kp>m$ and zero when $kp\leq m$. Upstairs $kp\geq1=d$, and the order $p-1/k$ vanishes exactly in the logarithmic case $k=p=1$.

\emph{Step 4: examples.} For $m=k=2$ and $p=1$, the denominators satisfy $J_{\mathrm{down}}\sim\log(1/\epsilon)$ and $J_{\mathrm{up}}\sim(\pi/3)\epsilon^{-1/2}$, so the volumes are asymptotic to $3b^2/\log(1/\epsilon)$ and $(9b^2/\pi)\epsilon^{1/2}$, respectively. The order changes from $0$ to $1/2$. For $m=2$ and $k=p=1$, the downstairs denominator has a finite positive limit, while $J_{\mathrm{up}}\sim(2/3)\log(1/\epsilon)$. Thus a background of positive volume downstairs pulls back to one whose perturbed volumes are asymptotic to $9b^2/[2\log(1/\epsilon)]$, and both orders are zero. Finally, for an integer $j\geq1$, choose $m=j+2$, $p=j+1$ and $k=1$. Then $kp<m$ gives a positive limit of the volumes downstairs, while the orders are $0$ and $j$.
\end{proof}
