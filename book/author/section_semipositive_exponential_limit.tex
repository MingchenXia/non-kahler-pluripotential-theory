\subsection{The limit of the exponential equations}
\label{subsec:semipositive-exponential-setting}

The uniqueness just proved identifies the limit, as $\lambda\downarrow0$, of the normalized solutions of the exponential equations $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mathrm{d}V$. To control their normalization, we use the probability measure obtained from the maximizing form and the density. Normalized potentials have bounded moments with respect to this measure, and Jensen's inequality converts this bound into an estimate for the constants and a common lower barrier for the potentials.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:semipositive-dual-exponential-limit}
Assume the hypotheses of \cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation}, and let $u_0$ be its unique threshold solution with $\sup_Xu_0=0$. For real $\lambda>0$, let $u_\lambda$ be the greatest solution of the exponential equation \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation}, with $\omega$ in place of $h$, given by \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential}, and put
\[
 v_\lambda=u_\lambda-\sup_Xu_\lambda,\qquad
 c_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_\lambda}.
\]
There is a constant $C$ independent of $\lambda>0$ such that
\[
 b\leq c_\lambda\leq b\,\mathrm{e}^{C\lambda},\qquad
 u_0-C\leq v_\lambda\leq0.
\]
Moreover $v_\lambda\to u_0$ in $L^1(X)$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $p=f^{1/n}$ and introduce the probability measure
\[
 \mathrm{d}\nu=b^{1/n}p k_T\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
It has mass one because $T$ maximizes. Its density belongs to $L^n$, since $p\in L^n$ and the smooth semipositive form $T$ has bounded determinant density $k_T$. By \cref{lem:uniform-exponential}, $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions with supremum zero are uniformly exponentially integrable, so their moments of every finite order are uniformly bounded. H\"older's inequality with the exponents $n$ and $n/(n-1)$ therefore gives
\[
 \int_X(-v_\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq C.
\]

Since $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(v_\lambda)=c_\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda}f\,\mathrm{d}V$, the trace inequality of \cref{lem:unbounded-gauduchon-trace-density}, applied with the positive regularizations of $T$ and passed to the limit as in the proof of \cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation}, gives $(\omega+\ddc v_\lambda)\wedge T\geq(c_\lambda/b)^{1/n}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda/n}\,\nu$. Integrating, we obtain
\[
 1\geq(c_\lambda/b)^{1/n}
       \int_X\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda/n}\,\mathrm{d}\nu
 \geq(c_\lambda/b)^{1/n}\mathrm{e}^{-C\lambda/n},
\]
where the last step is Jensen's inequality. This proves the upper bound for $c_\lambda$. The lower bound and the comparison
\[
 v_\lambda\geq u_0-\lambda^{-1}\log(c_\lambda/b)
\]
follow from \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential} applied with $v=u_0$, and together with the upper bound they give the common lower barrier $u_0-C$.

Take any sequence $\lambda_j\downarrow0$. By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, a subsequence of $(v_{\lambda_j})$ converges in $L^1$ to some $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with supremum zero, and \cref{prop:greatest-normalized-threshold} makes $v$ a threshold subsolution. By the uniqueness in \cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation}, $v-u_0$ is constant, and since both functions have supremum zero, $v=u_0$. Since every sequence tending to zero has a subsequence with this same $L^1$ limit, the whole family converges.
\end{proof}

Under the additional envelope condition of the next corollary, the lower barrier upgrades this to convergence in capacity. If the maximizing form is strictly positive everywhere, \cref{cor:smooth-dual-exponential-limit} already gives uniform convergence.
