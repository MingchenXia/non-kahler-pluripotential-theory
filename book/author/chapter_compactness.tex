\chapter{Gauduchon traces and the topology of potentials}
\label{chap:compactness}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form with $\PSH(X,\theta)\ne\varnothing$. Fix a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$ and a smooth positive probability volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ for $L^1$ norms. In dimension one, $\gamma$ may be any Hermitian metric.

Taking the Gauduchon trace reduces recovery of a potential, modulo constants, to a scalar elliptic equation. On normalized potentials, weak trace convergence is equivalent to $L^1$ convergence. The elliptic inverse and smoothing of measure tests give sharp quantitative estimates for this equivalence. Replacing the smooth trace form by a positive pluriharmonic current requires control of potential gradients. For continuous potentials, a trace-energy estimate supplies a canonical positive product with joint continuity.
