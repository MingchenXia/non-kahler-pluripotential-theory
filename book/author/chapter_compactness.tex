\chapter{Gauduchon traces and the topology of potentials}
\label{chap:compactness}

Throughout this chapter, $\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive probability volume form used for $L^1$ norms, and $\gamma$ is a Gauduchon metric, that is, a Hermitian form with $\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0$ (\cref{def:gauduchon-metric}). In dimension one every Hermitian form is Gauduchon, since $\gamma^{n-1}=1$.

The current $\theta+\ddc u$ has a conserved linear trace even when its non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure has infinite mass, as we shall see in \cref{chap:products}. By \cref{prop:gauduchon-trace}, the positive measure $(\theta+\ddc u)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}$ has the same total mass for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. The ellipticity of $f\mapsto\ddc f\wedge\gamma^{n-1}$ makes the trace more informative than this mass alone: its kernel consists of the constants, so the trace measure determines the potential up to an additive constant.

On mean-normalized potentials, this correspondence also identifies the topologies: weak convergence of trace measures is equivalent to $L^1$ convergence of potentials. Lipschitz tests give a complete metric for this topology, and the inverse elliptic operator compares it with the $L^1$ norm by sharp power bounds. Extending the conserved pairing to a positive pluriharmonic current requires more than this elliptic inversion. For continuous potentials, a gradient-energy estimate defines the derivative terms in the product and gives continuity under uniform approximation.
