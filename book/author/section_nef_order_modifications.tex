\subsection{Comparison under an arbitrary modification}\label{sec:nef-modification-comparison}

A pulled-back Gauduchon test becomes degenerate along the exceptional locus. Adding a small positive test repairs this degeneracy. To compare the perturbed volumes uniformly, we first bound the upstairs metric pairing by the downstairs one over the entire test cone.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Here $\pi:Y\to X$ is a modification of connected compact complex $n$-folds, with $n\geq2$. We use Hironaka's analytic elimination of indeterminacies in the following form: the map $\pi$ is dominated by a composition of smooth-center blowups $p:Z\to X$, with a modification $F:Z\to Y$ satisfying $p=\pi\circ F$. It follows by resolving the inverse meromorphic map; the maps agree on the isomorphism locus and hence everywhere. This classical input is discussed in \cref{sec:history-gaud-volume}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nef-blowup-metric}
Let $p:Z\to X$ be a finite composition of blowups along smooth centers of codimension at least two, and let $h_X$ be a Hermitian form. There are $C_0>0$, an effective $p$-exceptional real divisor $D$, and a smooth curvature representative $\eta_D$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-blowup-positive-metric}
 h_Z=C_0p^*h_X-\eta_D>0.
\end{equation}
Every component of $D$ maps into a set of complex codimension at least two in $X$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First let $q:M'\to M$ be one blowup, with exceptional divisor $E$. On $E$, the restriction of $\mathcal O(-E)$ is relatively positive on the projective fibers. A metric on the normal bundle of the center induces a Hermitian metric on this restriction whose curvature is positive on their tangent spaces. Extend the metric to $\mathcal O(-E)$ over $M'$ by extending the smooth logarithm of its ratio to a fixed reference metric from the closed submanifold $E$. Let $\beta$ be its curvature. The semipositive form $q^*h_M$ has, on $E$, kernel precisely the tangent space to the fiber, and $\beta$ is positive on that kernel; off $E$, $q^*h_M$ is positive. Compactness now gives $Cq^*h_M+\beta>0$ for a sufficiently large $C$. Indeed a contrary sequence of unit tangent vectors, with $C\to\infty$, would converge to a vector in the kernel of $q^*h_M$ on which $\beta$ is nonpositive. With $\eta_E=-\beta$, this is $Cq^*h_M-\eta_E>0$. Iterating the construction, pulling back preceding exceptional divisors with positive coefficients and adding the new exceptional divisor, proves \eqref{eq:nef-blowup-positive-metric} while retaining effectivity and exceptionality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nef-uniform-pairing}
Fix Hermitian forms $h_X,h_Y$ for a modification $\pi:Y\to X$. There is $K>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-uniform-pairing}
 \int_Y h_Y\wedge\pi^*\Psi\leq K\int_X h_X\wedge\Psi
\end{equation}
for every smooth strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\Psi$ on $X$ with $\ddc\Psi=0$. The constant is independent of $\Psi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the analytic domination $p=\pi\circ F$ above and \cref{lem:nef-blowup-metric}. Compactness gives $a>0$ with $h_Z\geq aF^*h_Y$. Pulling back the semipositive form $\Psi$ and integrating yields
\[
 a\int_Yh_Y\wedge\pi^*\Psi
 \leq\int_Zh_Z\wedge p^*\Psi
 =C_0\int_Xh_X\wedge\Psi-\int_Dp^*\Psi.
\]
The change-of-variables equalities use the degree-one maps $F,p$, and Poincar\'e--Lelong converts the curvature pairing to the divisor pairing under $\ddc p^*\Psi=0$. The last integral vanishes: on the regular locus of each component of $D$, the restriction of $p$ has rank at most $n-2$, so the restriction of the $(n-1,n-1)$-form $p^*\Psi$ is zero. Taking $K=C_0/a$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nef-modification-volume}
Let $\pi:Y\to X$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds of dimension $n\geq2$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth nef background on $X$. Its pullback is nef. For fixed Hermitian forms $h_X,h_Y$ and smooth positive probability forms $\mu_X,\mu_Y$, there are $C,K>0$, independent of $\epsilon$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-main-modification-comparison}
 \mathcal V_{\mu_Y}(\pi^*\theta+\epsilon h_Y)
 \leq C\mathcal V_{\mu_X}(\theta+K\epsilon h_X)
 \qquad(\epsilon>0).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
 \underline a(\pi^*\theta)\geq\underline a(\theta),\qquad
 \overline a(\pi^*\theta)\geq\overline a(\theta).
\]
In dimension one a modification is an isomorphism and preserves both orders.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pullback preserves nefness: pull back a smooth approximation $\theta+\ddc u_t\geq-t h_X$, compare $\pi^*h_X\leq C'h_Y$, and choose $t<\epsilon/C'$ to obtain a positive representative of $\pi^*\theta+\epsilon h_Y$. Thus both volumes in \eqref{eq:nef-main-modification-comparison} are positive.

Choose $c>0$ with $\mu_Y\geq c\pi^*\mu_X$: the ratio $\pi^*\mu_X/\mu_Y$ is smooth, nonnegative, and bounded on compact $Y$. Fix a smooth positive Gauduchon metric $\gamma_0$ upstairs by \cref{thm:gauduchon}. For an arbitrary downstairs Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, write $\Psi=\gamma^{n-1}$ and define $\Gamma_\delta$, $\delta>0$, by
\[
 \Gamma_\delta^{n-1}=\pi^*\Psi+\delta\gamma_0^{n-1}.
\]
A strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form has a unique smooth positive $(1,1)$-root of this form: solve for its Hermitian matrix from the positive cofactor matrix. Hence $\Gamma_\delta$ is a smooth positive Gauduchon metric.

For $\theta$ nef and $\epsilon>0$, \cref{lem:nef-uniform-pairing} gives the numerator limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-modification-numerator}
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\int_Y(\pi^*\theta+\epsilon h_Y)
 \wedge\Gamma_\delta^{n-1}
 =\int_X\theta\wedge\Psi+\epsilon\int_Yh_Y\wedge\pi^*\Psi
 \leq\int_X(\theta+K\epsilon h_X)\wedge\Psi.
\end{equation}
The first term is nonnegative by nefness and the second is strictly positive on the isomorphism locus, so the limit is positive.

The denominator satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-modification-denominator}
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}D_{\mu_Y}(\Gamma_\delta)
 =\int_Y(\pi^*\gamma^n)^{(n-1)/n}\mu_Y^{1/n}
 \geq c^{1/n}D_{\mu_X}(\gamma)>0.
\end{equation}
To justify the limit on the exceptional locus, represent $\Gamma_\delta^{n-1}$ by its positive cofactor matrix. The density $(\Gamma_\delta^n)^{(n-1)/n}$ is a fixed dimensional constant times the $1/n$ power of that matrix's determinant. It extends continuously to positive semidefinite matrices. Compactness gives dominated convergence. The inequality follows from $\mu_Y\geq c\pi^*\mu_X$, and the final equality is change of variables for the degree-one map $\pi$.

Test upstairs with $\Gamma_\delta$, use \eqref{eq:nef-modification-numerator}--\eqref{eq:nef-modification-denominator}, and let $\delta\downarrow0$. For each downstairs $\gamma$ this gives
\[
 \lambda_{\mu_Y}(\pi^*\theta+\epsilon h_Y)
 \leq c^{-1/n}
 \frac{\int_X(\theta+K\epsilon h_X)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}{D_{\mu_X}(\gamma)}.
\]
Taking the infimum over the full downstairs test family and then the $n$th power proves \eqref{eq:nef-main-modification-comparison} with $C=c^{-1}$.

Divide its logarithm by $\log\epsilon<0$ and use the constant-factor and direction comparisons from \cref{prop:nef-degeneration-orders}; the inequality reverses and gives $\underline a(\pi^*\theta)\geq\underline a(\theta)$ and $\overline a(\pi^*\theta)\geq\overline a(\theta)$. A proper degree-one map of smooth compact curves is an isomorphism, settling $n=1$.
\end{proof}
