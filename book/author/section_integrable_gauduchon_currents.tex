\section{Integrable densities in the compact Gauduchon problem}
\label{sec:integrable-gauduchon-currents}

Retain $X$, $n\geq2$, the Hermitian form $\omega$, probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$ and current set $\mathscr G_\omega$ from \cref{sec:weak-gauduchon-duality}. All $L^r$ spaces in this section use $\mathrm{d}V$. The root norm $\rho_\omega$, its finite domain $\mathcal D_\omega$, its vanishing-tail subspace $\mathcal D_\omega^0$ and the threshold $b_\omega$ are those of \cref{prop:root-uniform-tails}; for nonzero $p\geq0$, $b_\omega(p^n)=\rho_\omega(p)^{-n}$. For $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$, write $\Gamma_0(T)=k_T\,\mathrm{d}V$ as in \cref{thm:gaud-determinant-subcritical}. If $p\geq0$ is measurable, define
\[
 D_p(T)=\int_Xp k_T\,\mathrm{d}V,
\]
allowing $+\infty$. For a smooth positive normalized Gauduchon form this is the test $L_{\omega,\gamma}(p)$. The space $\mathcal D_\omega^0$ from \cref{prop:root-uniform-tails} supplies a class of unbounded weights for which compactness still gives attainment.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:integrable-current-attainment}
For every nonnegative $p\in L^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integrable-current-supremum}
 \sup_{T\in\mathscr G_\omega}D_p(T)=\rho_\omega(p),
\end{equation}
with $+\infty$ allowed. If $p\in\mathcal D_\omega^0$, the functional $D_p$ is finite, concave and weakly upper semicontinuous on $\mathscr G_\omega$. Its maximum is attained, and the measures $p k_T\,\mathrm{d}V$, as $T$ ranges over this set, have uniformly integrable densities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a smooth strictly positive $p$, \cref{thm:weak-gaud-compact-duality} applied to $F=n\log p$ proves \eqref{eq:integrable-current-supremum}. By \cref{thm:gaud-determinant-subcritical}, for every real $r>n$ and every $q\in L^r$, there is a constant $C_r$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-root-test-bound}
 \sup_{T\in\mathscr G_\omega}\int_X|q| k_T\,\mathrm{d}V
       \leq C_r\|q\|_{L^r}.
\end{equation}
Approximate a bounded nonnegative $p$ in $L^{2n}$ by smooth strictly positive functions. This estimate and \eqref{eq:root-subcritical-bound} give uniform convergence of the current functionals and convergence of the root norms. Thus the supremum formula holds for bounded $p$. Their current functionals are also upper semicontinuous and concave, by \cref{lem:weak-gaud-determinant-dual} and uniform convergence. For general $p\in L^n$, use positive integer truncation levels $K$. Monotone truncation gives
\[
 \sup_T D_p(T)=\sup_T\sup_K D_{\min(p,K)}(T)
       =\sup_K\rho_\omega(\min(p,K))=\rho_\omega(p).
\]
Only two suprema have been interchanged; this step alone gives no attainment.

Now suppose $p\in\mathcal D_\omega^0$. By the supremum formula applied to the tail,
\[
 0\leq D_p(T)-D_{\min(p,K)}(T)
 \leq \rho_\omega(p\mathbf1_{\{p>K\}})\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly in $T$. Hence $D_p$ is finite and a uniform limit of upper semicontinuous concave functionals. Compactness of $\mathscr G_\omega$ gives a maximizer. Finally, fix $1<q<n/(n-1)$. For every measurable $E$,
\[
 \sup_T\int_Ep k_T\,\mathrm{d}V
 \leq\rho_\omega(p\mathbf1_{\{p>K\}})
       +K C_q\,\mathrm{d}V(E)^{1-1/q}.
\]
Choosing first $K$ and then the volume of $E$ proves uniform absolute continuity. The total masses are bounded by $\rho_\omega(p)$, so the densities are uniformly integrable.
\end{proof}

A maximizing current is a finite positive $\ddc$-closed current. The theorem asserts neither a smooth minimizing Gauduchon metric nor an exact Monge--Amp\`ere solution at the subsolution threshold. These are different attainment questions.

\subsection{Supporting probabilities for an integrable density}

Retain $X$, $n$, $\omega$, $\mathrm{d}V$, $\mathscr G_\omega$ and the functionals $D_p$, $\rho_\omega$, $b_\omega$ of \cref{sec:integrable-gauduchon-currents}. Here $k_T$ is the density of $\Gamma_0(T)$, and $\mathcal D_\omega^0$ is the root space with uniformly vanishing tails. All integrability and convergence in measure refer to $\mathrm{d}V$.

Let $f\in L^1$ be nonzero and nonnegative, and assume $p=f^{1/n}\in\mathcal D_\omega^0$. For a continuous real $F$, set
\[
 B_f(F)=\log b_\omega(\mathrm{e}^Ff)
       =-n\log\rho_\omega(p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}),\qquad
 M_f(F)=\rho_\omega(p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}).
\]
Both quantities are finite and $M_f(F)>0$, by the order-ideal property. Let $\mathscr M_f(F)$ be the currents maximizing $D_{p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}}$, and put
\[
 \nu_{T,F}=\frac{p \mathrm{e}^{F/n} k_T}{M_f(F)}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \quad(T\in\mathscr M_f(F)).
\]
These are probability measures. Define $\mathcal A_f(F)$ to be all Borel probability measures satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integrable-threshold-support}
 B_f(G)\leq B_f(F)-\int_X(G-F)\,\mathrm{d}\nu
 \qquad(G\in C(X,\mathbb R)).
\end{equation}

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:integrable-threshold-adjoints}
The functional $B_f$ is concave, decreasing and $1$-Lipschitz for the uniform norm, and $B_f(F+a)=B_f(F)-a$ for constants $a$. Moreover,
\[
 \mathcal A_f(F)=\{\nu_{T,F}:T\in\mathscr M_f(F)\}.
\]
This set is nonempty, convex and weakly compact. Its measures are absolutely continuous. For each $R>0$, their densities, with $\|F\|_\infty\leq R$, are uniformly integrable. For $H\in C(X,\mathbb R)$, its one-sided derivatives satisfy
\[
 (B_f)'_+(F;H)=-\max_{\nu\in\mathcal A_f(F)}\int_XH\,\mathrm{d}\nu,
 \qquad
 (B_f)'_-(F;H)=-\min_{\nu\in\mathcal A_f(F)}\int_XH\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each $T$, H\"older's inequality makes $F\mapsto\int_Xp \mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_T\,\mathrm{d}V$ logarithmically convex. Taking suprema preserves the inequality defining logarithmic convexity. Thus $B_f=-n\log M_f$ is concave. Order and homogeneity give the translation rule and, by comparison with constant translates, the Lipschitz estimate.

For fixed $F$, the weight $p\mathrm{e}^{F/n}$ belongs to $\mathcal D_\omega^0$, so its determinant-weighted densities are uniformly integrable. If $T_j\to T$ and $D_{p\mathrm{e}^{F/n}}(T_j)\to M_f(F)$, \cref{prop:selected-gauduchon-attainment} makes $T$ a maximizer and gives convergence of the weighted measures. For $T_1,T_2\in\mathscr G_\omega$ and real $0\leq t\leq1$, matrix determinant concavity gives
\[
 p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_{tT_1+(1-t)T_2}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq t p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_{T_1}\,\mathrm{d}V
       +(1-t)p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_{T_2}\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
For two maximizing currents, equal total masses turn this into equality. The set $\mathscr N_f(F)$ of displayed probabilities is therefore nonempty, compact and convex.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
A maximizing $T$ gives
\[
 M_f(G)\geq M_f(F)\int_X\mathrm{e}^{(G-F)/n}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{T,F}.
\]
Jensen's inequality proves \eqref{eq:integrable-threshold-support}, so $\mathscr N_f(F)\subset\mathcal A_f(F)$. For the reverse inclusion, the derivative argument of \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints} applies with the finite measure $p k_T\,\mathrm{d}V$ in place of $k_T\,\mathrm{d}V$. Explicitly, uniform Taylor expansion gives
\[
 D_{p \mathrm{e}^{(F+tH)/n}}(T)
 =D_{p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}}(T)+\frac tn\int_X H p \mathrm{e}^{F/n} k_T\,\mathrm{d}V+O(t^2),
\]
where the error is uniform in $T$ since $D_{p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}}(T)\leq M_f(F)$. Testing maximizers at $F$ bounds the right difference quotients of $M_f$ from below. Maximizers at $F+tH$ are near-maximizers at $F$, by the uniform multiplicative bounds, so the preceding convergence of weighted measures gives the reverse bound. Consequently
\[
 (B_f)'_+(F;H)=-\max_{\nu\in\mathscr N_f(F)}\int_XH\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\]
Every $\nu\in\mathcal A_f(F)$ satisfies
$\int_XH\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq\max_{\sigma\in\mathscr N_f(F)}\int_XH\,\mathrm{d}\sigma$ for all continuous $H$, by its supporting inequality. Separation from a compact convex set yields $\nu\in\mathscr N_f(F)$. Replacing $H$ by $-H$ proves the left-derivative formula.

Finally $M_f(F)\geq \mathrm{e}^{-R/n}\rho_\omega(p)$ when $\|F\|_\infty\leq R$. Thus the displayed probability densities are at most $\mathrm{e}^{2R/n}\rho_\omega(p)^{-1}p k_T$. The uniform integrability in the preceding theorem proves all the stated absolute-continuity assertions.
\end{proof}

When $f=1$, this is the zero-reference construction of \cref{chap:normalization}. For general $f$, the functional is defined by the maximal subsolution coefficient; it does not presume exact equation existence. No prescribed-singularity analogue is inferred from this Hermitian-background argument. Under positive lower mass, every nonnegative $L^1$ density satisfies the root-space hypothesis; for nonzero densities, \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-exact-threshold} also identifies $b_\omega(f)$ as the unique envelope-class equation scalar. Without that property, the uniform tail condition gives an explicit sufficient hypothesis for this compact dual description.
