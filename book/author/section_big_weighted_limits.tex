\section{Weighted measure addition}
\label{sec:big-weighted-limits}

The sum of two exponentially weighted Monge--Amp\`ere measures can have infinite mass. To realize it by a potential in $\mathcal E_\varphi$, we approximate the inputs while keeping a common lower bound in that class. This bound gives capacity convergence even across different ordinary types and localizes the limiting equation away from deep sublevels. Weighted measures are defined off the pole sets and assigned zero mass there.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:big-weighted-common-bound}
Let $a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have a common upper bound and converge in $L^1(X)$ to $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $a\preceq\varphi$. Suppose there is $h\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ such that $h\leq a_j$ for every $j$. If, for some $\lambda>0$,
\[
 \int_X|a_j-a|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda a}\,\mu_{a_j}\longrightarrow0,
\]
then $a\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $a_j\to a$ in capacity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, the bounds pass to the limit, so $h\leq a\preceq\varphi$. Upward stability gives $a\in\mathcal E_\varphi$.
Start with any subsequence and extract again so that the displayed integrals are at most $2^{-j}$ and smooth-volume almost-everywhere convergence holds. The finite roofs
\[
 p_{j,N}=P_\theta(a_j,\ldots,a_N)
\]
are genuine because $h$ is a competitor. Disjoint first-contact pieces give
\[
 \int_X\min(1,|p_{j,N}-a|)\mathrm{e}^{\lambda a}\,\mu_{p_{j,N}}
 \leq\sum_{k=j}^N\int_X|a_k-a|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda a}\,\mu_{a_k}
 \leq2^{1-j}.
\]
No relative-class assertion about these finite roofs is needed. Their decreasing limits $p_j=P_\theta(\inf_{k\geq j}a_k)$ are genuine and at least $h$. Almost-everywhere convergence gives $p_j\leq a$. Hence $p_j$ and their increasing canonical limit $p$ lie in $\mathcal E_\varphi$.

The limits in $N$ and then in $j$ converge in capacity by \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}, using the genuine lower bound $h$. Apply weighted lower semicontinuity \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} twice to the bounded weights
$\min(1,|p_{j,N}-a|)\mathrm{e}^{\lambda a}$ and their limits. They converge in capacity: discard deep sublevels of $h$ and $a$, then use continuity on the remaining finite ranges. We obtain
\[
 \int_X\min(1,|p-a|)\mathrm{e}^{\lambda a}\,\mu_p=0.
\]
The exponential is positive off a pluripolar set, which $\mu_p$ ignores. Zero domination therefore gives $p=a$. The upper tail envelopes $(\sup_{k\geq j}a_k)^*$ decrease to $a$. Squeezing proves capacity convergence along this extraction. The subsequence criterion gives it for the original sequence.
\end{proof}

The following limiting equation is written for exponent one. Rescaling the background and potentials gives the general exponent in \cref{thm:big-weighted-addition}.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:big-truncated-addition-limit}
Let $u,v,\sigma\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be nonpositive, with $u,v\preceq\varphi$, and let $t_j\uparrow\infty$. Each of $u_j,v_j$ is either the fixed corresponding potential or its truncation
\[
 u_j=\max(u,\sigma-t_j),\qquad
 v_j=\max(v,\sigma-t_j).
\]
Either or both inputs may be fixed. Suppose their Monge--Amp\`ere masses are uniformly bounded. Fix $h\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and let $a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy
\[
 \mu_{a_j}=\mathrm{e}^{a_j-u_j}\mu_{u_j}+\mathrm{e}^{a_j-v_j}\mu_{v_j},
 \qquad h\leq a_j\leq\min(u_j,v_j).
\]
If $a_j\to a$ in $L^1(X)$, then $a\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, convergence holds in capacity, and
\[
 a\leq\min(u,v),\qquad
 \mu_a=\mathrm{e}^{a-u}\mu_u+\mathrm{e}^{a-v}\mu_v.
\]
The measure on the right is finite, even when $\mathrm{e}^{-u}\mu_u+\mathrm{e}^{-v}\mu_v$ has infinite total mass.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The bounds give $h\leq a\leq\min(u,v)\preceq\varphi$, so $a\in\mathcal E_\varphi$. After discarding a finite prefix, we may assume $t_j\geq0$, so all potentials $a_j,a$ are nonpositive. Let $E_j$ be the intersection of the strict sets $\{u>\sigma-t_j\}$ and $\{v>\sigma-t_j\}$ for those potentials that are actually truncated. A fixed potential imposes no condition. On $E_j$ locality gives $\mu_{u_j}=\mu_u$ and $\mu_{v_j}=\mu_v$. Since $a,a_j\leq u_j,v_j$, we have
\[
 \mathrm{e}^a\mu_{a_j}
 \leq \mathrm{e}^{(a+a_j)/2}(\mu_{u_j}+\mu_{v_j}).
\]
If $x\notin E_j$, one truncated input and its limit are at most $-t_j$, so both $a_j(x),a(x)\leq-t_j$. Also $|a_j-a|\mathrm{e}^{(a+a_j)/4}$ is bounded by an absolute constant, since $a_j,a\leq0$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-addition-weighted-error}
 \int_X|a_j-a|\mathrm{e}^a\,\mu_{a_j}
 \leq\int_X|a_j-a|\mathrm{e}^{(a+a_j)/2}(\mu_u+\mu_v)
       +C \mathrm{e}^{-t_j/2}.
\end{equation}
Here the assumed uniform mass bound is used only in the last term. The fixed measure $\mu_u+\mu_v$ is finite by bounded mass. \Cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals}, applied to $\mathrm{e}^{a_j},\mathrm{e}^a$, gives convergence in measure of $a_j$ to $a$ for that measure, since $a$ is finite almost everywhere. The integrand in the first term of \eqref{eq:big-addition-weighted-error} is uniformly bounded and tends to zero in measure. Its integral tends to zero. The preceding lemma now proves capacity convergence. Truncation levels $t_j$ are retained when a subsequence is taken.

To identify the equation, for $T>0$ choose a continuous nondecreasing function $g_T:[-\infty,0]\to[0,1]$, zero on $[-\infty,-2T]$ and one on $[-T,0]$, with $g_T\uparrow1$ at finite points. Put $\chi_T=g_T(h)$. We have $h\leq u,v$ by passing to the limit in the upper bounds. On $\{\chi_T>0\}$, both $u$ and $v$ exceed $-2T$. Therefore, once $t_j>2T$, every truncated input agrees there with its limit, and
\[
 \chi_T\mu_{a_j}
 =\chi_T\mathrm{e}^{a_j}(\mathrm{e}^{-u}\mu_u+\mathrm{e}^{-v}\mu_v).
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta$. On the left replace $a_j$ by $\max(a_j,-2T)$ and expand the bounded product as
\[
 (\theta+\ddc\max(a_j,-2T))^n
 =\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk
   (\Omega+\ddc\max(a_j,-2T))^k\wedge(\theta-\Omega)^{n-k}.
\]
The cutoff vanishes at the truncation level. Bounded capacity continuity with the fixed quasi-continuous weight $\chi_T$ therefore gives convergence to $\chi_T\mu_a$, after testing against a continuous function. The uncut product may be signed; its terms have uniformly capacity-controlled total variations.

On the right, $\chi_T \mathrm{e}^{-u}$ and $\chi_T \mathrm{e}^{-v}$ are bounded by $\mathrm{e}^{2T}$. Fixed-measure exponential convergence gives convergence in total variation to
$\chi_T\mathrm{e}^a(\mathrm{e}^{-u}\mu_u+\mathrm{e}^{-v}\mu_v)$. Let $T\to\infty$. The limiting right side is at most $\mu_u+\mu_v$, because $a\leq u,v$. Both sides ignore the polar set of $h$, so the cutoff identities prove the global equation and its finiteness.
\end{proof}
