\section{Products with smooth backgrounds}
\label{sec:psh-bounded-products}

The Bedford--Taylor construction also defines products after adding smooth forms to complex Hessians. Independence of the smooth correction makes these products global; positivity requires retaining the original lower curvature bound. Write $\theta_v=\theta+\ddc v$ and fix $1\leq k\leq n$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Let $u$ be locally bounded and $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. On a relatively compact coordinate ball with coordinates $z$, choose $A>0$ and put $q=A|z|^2$ so that $p=u+q$ is plurisubharmonic. Set $\beta=\theta-\ddc q$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-expansion}
 (\theta+\ddc u)^k
 :=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}(\ddc p)^j\wedge\beta^{k-j}.
\end{equation}
Each summand is defined by \cref{thm:bt-input} and multiplication by a smooth form. The signed terms in this expansion should not individually be assumed positive.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-products}\index{Monge--Ampere product@Monge--Amp\`ere product!bounded potentials}
The construction \eqref{eq:bounded-expansion}, and its mixed version, is independent of the local correction $q$ and of the decomposition of each current into a smooth form and a $\ddc$-potential. It defines global products of currents with locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials. The products are symmetric, multilinear, plurifinely local, and give no mass to pluripolar sets. If every factor is positive, their mixed product is positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the overlap of two coordinate balls, the two corrected potentials differ by a smooth function. The multilinear rule
$\ddc(p+f)=\ddc p+\ddc f$, valid for bounded local products by smooth approximation, and the binomial formula identify the two expansions. The same argument handles a smooth change of the decomposition of a current. Any two potential decompositions of the same $(1,1)$-current do differ smoothly: their difference has smooth $\ddc$, and taking its local Euclidean trace gives smoothness by elliptic regularity. Symmetry, multilinearity and locality then follow term by term. Wedges of bounded Bedford--Taylor products with smooth forms have locally finite variation and do not charge pluripolar sets, so these properties also pass to the finite expansion.

It remains to prove positivity; it does not follow from the signs of the summands. Write the factors as $T_i=\theta_i+\ddc u_i$, $1\leq i\leq k$, where each $\theta_i$ is smooth and real and each $u_i$ is locally bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic. Assume $T_i\geq0$. Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and a local positive constant form $\kappa$. After shrinking a neighborhood of a chosen point, each smooth background $\theta_i$ admits a constant, hence closed, form $\theta_i'$ such that
\[
 -\varepsilon\kappa\leq\theta_i-\theta_i'
 \leq\varepsilon\kappa.
\]
Then
$S_i=\theta_i'+\varepsilon\kappa+\ddc u_i$ is positive and closed there, with a bounded local plurisubharmonic potential. Moreover
\[
 T_i+2\varepsilon\kappa
 =S_i+(\theta_i-\theta_i'+\varepsilon\kappa).
\]
All terms on the right are positive, and the second term is smooth. Expanding the mixed product therefore gives a sum of positive currents. Although the neighborhoods depend on $\varepsilon$, they cover the original coordinate ball. The product of the $T_i+2\varepsilon\kappa$ is thus positive throughout that ball. By multilinearity it converges weakly to the product of the $T_i$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, proving positivity.
\end{proof}

The local expansion also gives continuity along uniformly locally bounded decreasing sequences, by applying \cref{thm:bt-input} to the corrected potentials and then summing the finitely many terms. Each bounded top-degree product has finite mass on compact $X$. For bounded $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, we write $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=(\theta+\ddc u)^n$.

Decreasing maxima compare the products of ordered potentials on their contact set. The comparison also holds with fixed positive factors, which will be useful in integration by parts.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-ordered-contact}
Let $a\leq b$ be locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on an $n$-dimensional complex manifold, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form such that $\theta_a,\theta_b\geq0$. For $1\leq k\leq n$, let $W$ be a product of $n-k$ positive currents with smooth backgrounds and locally bounded potentials, with $W=1$ when $k=n$. Then
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{a=b\}}\theta_a^k\wedge W
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{a=b\}}\theta_b^k\wedge W.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The functions $m_\delta=\max(a+\delta,b)$ satisfy
$b\leq m_\delta\leq b+\delta$ and decrease to $b$ as $\delta\downarrow0$. Their products $\theta_{m_\delta}^k\wedge W$ are positive and converge weakly to $\theta_b^k\wedge W$ by bounded decreasing continuity. Locality on $\{a+\delta>b\}$, which contains $\{a=b\}$, identifies their restrictions there with $\theta_a^k\wedge W$. Thus, for every compact $K\subset\{a=b\}$ in a coordinate chart, the compact-set inequality for weak convergence gives
\[
 \int_K\theta_a^k\wedge W
 =\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\int_K\theta_{m_\delta}^k\wedge W
 \leq\int_K\theta_b^k\wedge W.
\]
The measures are locally finite. Inner regularity extends the inequality to Borel subsets of the contact set, and a countable chart covering proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

The products commute with pullback by a holomorphic submersion. Here the pullback of a current is defined in local product coordinates by pulling back its distributional coefficients.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-products-submersion}
Let $\pi:Z\to Y$ be a holomorphic submersion of complex manifolds. For $1\leq r\leq\dim Z$, let $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ be locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $Y$ and let $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms. Then
\[
 \bigwedge_{i=1}^r\bigl(\pi^*\theta_i+\ddc(u_i\circ\pi)\bigr)
 =\pi^*\!\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^r(\theta_i+\ddc u_i)\right).
\]
For $r>\dim Y$, the product on the left vanishes and the right side is interpreted as zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work in product coordinates in which $\pi$ is a coordinate projection. On a smaller base chart choose smooth quadratics $q_i$ such that $p_i=u_i+q_i$ are plurisubharmonic, and approximate each $p_i$ by a decreasing sequence of smooth plurisubharmonic functions. Their pullbacks have the same properties on the product chart. For smooth forms pullback commutes with wedge products. Apply bounded decreasing continuity to the local expansions \eqref{eq:bounded-expansion} on both charts to obtain the asserted identity. Pullback of currents by the projection is weakly continuous: pairing with a compactly supported test form amounts to pairing the original current with its integral in the fiber variables. If $r>\dim Y$, every smooth approximating product is zero by degree, so its limit is zero as well.
\end{proof}

For $C^{1,1}$ potentials, these measure-valued products agree with the products of their measurable Hessian coefficients.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:c11-products}
Let $u_1,\ldots,u_k$ be locally $C^{1,1}$ quasi-plurisubharmonic functions and let $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_k$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms. The bounded product
\[
 (\theta_1+\ddc u_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge(\theta_k+\ddc u_k)
\]
coincides with the almost-everywhere wedge of the locally bounded measurable coefficients of its factors.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
On a smaller coordinate ball, add a common smooth quadratic $q$ so that each $u_i+q$ is plurisubharmonic, and convolve with a smooth radial probability kernel. The Hessians of the convolutions are uniformly locally bounded and converge to the weak Hessians in every finite $L^s$ space. Their finite products therefore converge in $L^1$ on smaller balls. The potentials converge uniformly, so bounded Bedford--Taylor continuity identifies this limit with their pluripotential product. Expanding off the smooth correction, as in \eqref{eq:bounded-expansion}, proves the assertion for the original factors.
\end{proof}

The mixed density inequality survives a smooth change of background. Work on an open set $U\subset\mathbb C^n$ and put $\beta=\ddc|z|^2$.

\Needspace{21\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:interior-mixed-background}\label{lem:nonclosed-mixed-density}
Let $T_i=\vartheta_i+\ddc u_i\geq0$, $i=1,2$, where $\vartheta_i$ are smooth real $(1,1)$-forms and $u_i$ are locally bounded. If
\[
  T_i^n\geq F_i\beta^n,
  \qquad F_i\geq0\text{ measurable},
\]
then, for every integer $0\leq k\leq n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-mixed-background}
  T_1^k\wedge T_2^{n-k}
  \geq F_1^{k/n}F_2^{(n-k)/n}\beta^n.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $a,b\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-minkowski-background}
  (aT_1+bT_2)^n
  \geq\bigl(aF_1^{1/n}+bF_2^{1/n}\bigr)^n\beta^n.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $W\Subset U$. On a ball $B_r(x)$ choose a constant form
\[
  A_i=\vartheta_i(x)+Cr\beta
\]
so that $S_i=A_i-\vartheta_i$ satisfies $0\leq S_i\leq2Cr\beta$. If $q_i$ is a quadratic potential for $A_i$, then $u_i+q_i$ is plurisubharmonic and
\[
  \bigl(\ddc(u_i+q_i)\bigr)^n=(T_i+S_i)^n\geq F_i\beta^n.
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Apply \cref{thm:local-mixed-input} to the two closed Hessians. After expansion, every term not equal to $T_1^k\wedge T_2^{n-k}$ contains some $S_i$ and is bounded by $C'r\mu$, where
\[
  \mu=\sum_{a+b\leq n-1}T_1^a\wedge T_2^b\wedge\beta^{n-a-b}
\]
is locally finite.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
A finite cover and a partition of unity give on $W$
\[
  T_1^k\wedge T_2^{n-k}
  \geq F_1^{k/n}F_2^{(n-k)/n}\beta^n-C'r\mu.
\]
Letting $r\downarrow0$ proves \eqref{eq:interior-mixed-background}; the binomial expansion gives \eqref{eq:interior-minkowski-background}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:interior-convolution}
Suppose $u$ is continuous, $T=\vartheta+\ddc u\geq0$, and $T^n\geq g^n\beta^n$ with $g\geq0$ continuous. For a nonnegative convolution kernel $\chi_\delta$ of mass one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-convolution}
  (T*\chi_\delta)^n\geq(g*\chi_\delta)^n\beta^n
\end{equation}
on every smaller domain on which the convolution is defined.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Approximate the convolution by finite convex combinations of translates and apply \eqref{eq:interior-minkowski-background}. The background forms converge smoothly and the potentials converge uniformly on compact sets. To pass to the limit, add a common quadratic and telescope the difference of two powers. After pairing with a test form, integration by parts bounds each term by the uniform difference of the potentials times a mixed-current mass. Successive integration against nested cutoffs gives a uniform bound for those masses. Thus the bounded-potential products converge and \eqref{eq:interior-convolution} follows.
\end{proof}

The convolution estimate also identifies the determinant of the absolutely continuous coefficient matrix when the top product has a continuous density.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:continuous-density-coefficients}
Let $u$ be continuous and $T=\vartheta+\ddc u\geq0$, with $\vartheta$ smooth. If $T^n=F\beta^n$ for continuous $F\geq0$, then
\[
 \det_\beta T_{\mathrm{ac}}=F\qquad\text{almost everywhere}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take a nonnegative smooth radial mollifier $\chi_\delta$ supported in the ball of radius $\delta$. At Lebesgue points of the coefficient densities, $T*\chi_\delta\to T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ almost everywhere. Indeed, the convolution of a singular coefficient measure is bounded by $C\delta^{-2n}|\lambda_s|(B_\delta)$, which tends to zero almost everywhere by differentiation of measures. Apply \cref{lem:interior-convolution} with $g=F^{1/n}$ to obtain $\det_\beta T_{\mathrm{ac}}\geq F$.

For the reverse inequality, write the convolved current as the Hessian of a locally bounded decreasing psh regularization plus a convergent smooth correction. Bounded product continuity gives $(T*\chi_\delta)^n\rightharpoonup T^n$. Fatou's lemma against a nonnegative compactly supported test function then gives $\det_\beta T_{\mathrm{ac}}\,\beta^n\leq T^n=F\beta^n$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-convex-pair}
Let $h\leq a$ be locally bounded $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions, and let $\chi$ be smooth on a neighborhood of $(-\infty,0]$ with $0\leq\chi'\leq1$ and $\chi''\geq0$. Then $w=a+\chi(h-a)$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic and
\[
 \theta_w=(1-\chi'(h-a))\theta_a+\chi'(h-a)\theta_h
       +\chi''(h-a)\,\mathrm{d}(h-a)\wedge\dc(h-a).
\]
In particular, $\theta_w^n\geq\chi'(h-a)^n\theta_h^n$. Positivity of $\theta_w$ and this product inequality also hold for convex $\chi$ of class $C^{1,1}$ on compact intervals, with $0\leq\chi'\leq1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The function $G(x,y)=x+\chi(y-x)$, for $y\leq x$, is convex and nondecreasing in each variable: its first derivatives are $1-\chi'$ and $\chi'$, and its Hessian is
\[
 \chi''\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\geq0.
\]
For smooth potentials the chain rule gives the identity. For bounded potentials, add a common quadratic correction and convolve. Convolution preserves the order, and $G(x+r,y+r)=r+G(x,y)$ retains the common correction. The bounded chain rule \eqref{eq:bounded-convex-chain-rule} and its gradient products give the identity in the limit. Freezing the smooth background as in \cref{prop:bounded-products} preserves its exact lower bound. Every term on the right is positive; positivity of the bounded mixed products gives the last assertion.

For a locally $C^{1,1}$ weight, extend it convexly across zero with derivative in $[0,1]$ and smooth it in one variable. The smooth convex weights and their first derivatives converge uniformly on compact intervals, while their derivatives remain in $[0,1]$. On each smaller coordinate ball the corresponding potentials converge uniformly to $w$. Bounded product continuity gives convergence of their top powers; the right-hand weights converge uniformly against the fixed measure $\theta_h^n$. Passing to the limit proves positivity and the product inequality with the original smooth background.
\end{proof}
