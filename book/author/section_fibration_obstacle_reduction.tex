\subsection{Relative divisors and fiber obstacles}
\label{subsec:fibration-obstacle-reduction}

Removing weighted divisorial poles leaves a current pulled back from the base. Compact fibers force its potential to descend, and fiber minima reduce both obstacle envelopes to base envelopes. The induced obstacle may be infinite over a fiber contained in the divisor, but normalized compactness still bounds its competitors. This gives zero top-degree mass and base criteria for relative-envelope membership and all-obstacle equality. Singular or ramified divisors are allowed; an explicit multiplier-ideal product requires relative normal crossings.

Let $p:M\to Y$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion between connected compact complex manifolds, with connected fibers, and put $d=\dim Y$ and $n=\dim M$, where $1\leq d<n$. Let $H_1,\ldots,H_s$ be a nonempty finite family of distinct prime divisors on $M$, each mapping onto $Y$, and put $H=\bigcup_jH_j$. For positive real weights $\lambda_j$, choose smooth Hermitian metrics $h_j$ on $\mathcal O_M(H_j)$, with canonical sections $s_j$ and Chern curvatures $\kappa_j$. Set
\[
 A=\sum_{j=1}^s\lambda_jH_j,\qquad
 \Psi=\sum_{j=1}^s\lambda_j\log|s_j|_{h_j}^2,\qquad
 \kappa_A=\sum_{j=1}^s\lambda_j\kappa_j.
\]
Thus $\kappa_A+\ddc\Psi=[A]=\sum_j\lambda_j[H_j]$.
For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta$ on $Y$ and $w\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$, put
\[
 \Theta=p^*\beta+\kappa_A,\qquad u=\Psi+w\circ p.
\]
The form $\beta$ need not be closed or positive. For continuous real obstacles, use the same competitor definitions as for smooth obstacles. Put
\[
 Z=\{y\in Y:p^{-1}(y)\subset H\}.
\]

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}\index{Envelope!fibration reduction}\index{Obstacle!fiber minimum}\index{Obstacle!extended fiber minimum}\index{Singularity volume!zero-volume fibration}
For every $F\in C^\infty(M,\mathbb R)$, the function
\[
 \widehat F(y)=\inf_{x\in p^{-1}(y)\setminus H}\{F(x)-\Psi(x)\},
 \qquad\inf\varnothing=+\infty,
\]
is continuous in the extended real sense, finite on $Y\setminus Z$ and equal to $+\infty$ on $Z$. Using the usual competitor definitions for the base envelopes below this extended obstacle, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibration-obstacle-reduction}
 \begin{split}
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]&=\Psi+P_{\beta,\widehat F}[w]\circ p,\\
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
     &=\Psi+P_{\beta,\widehat F}[w]_{\mathcal I}\circ p.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Every representative of the ordinary type of $u$ or of $P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ has zero extended mass. Consequently both singularity volumes of these types are zero.

If the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $w$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $Y$, then those of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $p$ is open, $Y\setminus Z=p(M\setminus H)$ is open and nonempty. The obstacle is bounded below by $\min_MF-\sup_M\Psi$. On a fiber meeting the complement, $\Psi\to-\infty$ near $H$, so the infimum of $F-\Psi$ is attained outside $H$. A local section through a minimizer gives the upper semicontinuity inequality. For $y_j\to y$ with bounded fiber minima, compactness gives a convergent subsequence of minimizers. Its limit lies outside $H$, giving the lower semicontinuity inequality. If $y\in Z$, such a bounded subsequence would have its limit in $H$, a contradiction. Thus $\widehat F\to+\infty$ at $Z$ and has the asserted extended continuity.

Choose a relatively compact nonempty base ball $U$ and a holomorphic section $s$ on its neighborhood with image outside $H$. There is a finite $C_U$ with $\widehat F\leq F\circ s-\Psi\circ s\leq C_U$ on $U$. Fix a smooth probability volume $\mathrm dV$ on $Y$. For any $b\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ below $\widehat F$, normalized compactness in \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives
\[
 -C_\beta\leq\int_U(b-\sup_Yb)\,\mathrm dV
       \leq(C_U-\sup_Yb)\int_U\mathrm dV.
\]
Hence all such $b$ satisfy $\sup_Yb\leq K_F=C_U+C_\beta/\int_U\mathrm dV$. A sufficiently low translate of $w$ belongs to both base competitor families. Their regularized suprema are therefore genuine $\beta$-plurisubharmonic potentials. Extended continuity preserves their obstacle bound after regularization.

Since each $H_j$ maps onto $Y$, the pullback of $w$ has generic Lelong number zero along $H_j$. Let $v$ be an ordinary competitor for $u$, or an ideal competitor with $v\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. Its generic Lelong number along $H_j$ is at least $\lambda_j$, by ordinary order or \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}. Divisorial removal in \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} gives
\[
 \Theta+\ddc v-[A]\geq0.
\]
Hence $v-\Psi$ has a quasi-plurisubharmonic representative $a$ satisfying $p^*\beta+\ddc a\geq0$. Compact-fiber descent in \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion} gives $a=b\circ p$ for a unique $b\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$. The equality $v=\Psi+b\circ p$ holds for the canonical representatives. Conversely every potential of this form is $\Theta$-plurisubharmonic.

Ordinary order is exactly $v\preceq u$ if and only if $b\preceq w$. Canceling $\Psi$ outside $H$ gives the base order almost everywhere by Fubini in product charts, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}; the reverse implication follows by pullback and addition. Ideal equivalence is exactly $b\sim_{\mathcal I}w$. Indeed, \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus} permits both addition and cancellation of $\Psi$. The pullback formula in \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback} then gives one direction, and restriction of the extended ideals along any local section gives the other. No section outside $H$ is needed for this ideal comparison.

If the divisors have relative simple normal crossings, the ideals have an explicit form. Here this means that near every point there are product coordinates $(y,z)$ with $p(y,z)=y$ and the components through the point given by distinct fiber coordinate hyperplanes. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divisorial-product-ideals}
 \mathcal I\bigl(t(\Psi+b\circ p)\bigr)
   =\mathcal O_M\!\left(-\sum_j\lfloor t\lambda_j\rfloor H_j\right)
                    \bigl(\mathcal I(tb)\mathcal O_M\bigr)
       \qquad(t>0).
\end{equation}
To see this, work in such coordinates. Up to a smooth correction, $\Psi$ is a sum of weights $\lambda_j\log|z_j|^2$ over the components present. Since $b$ is bounded above locally, integrability forces vanishing to order at least $\lfloor t\lambda_j\rfloor$ along each hyperplane. After factoring their product, the remaining fiber weight is
\[
 \prod_j|z_j|^{-2(t\lambda_j-\lfloor t\lambda_j\rfloor)}.
\]
It is integrable and bounded below by a positive constant on a small polydisc. Integrability of the quotient against this weight implies its unweighted fiber integrability, so \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback} puts it in the extension of $\mathcal I(tb)$. Conversely, local generators of that ideal and the integrable product weight give weighted integrability by Fubini. The same pullback lemma applies outside $H$. For one smooth transverse divisor of weight one, this is the formula with its ideal sheaf raised to $\lfloor t\rfloor$.

The bound $v\leq F$ is equivalent to $b\leq\widehat F$, with no constraint at a fiber in $Z$. The competitor families therefore give \eqref{eq:fibration-obstacle-reduction} outside $H$. Regularization commutes with pullback in product coordinates there. The common bound $b\leq K_F$ and the divergence $\Psi\to-\infty$ prevent regularization from changing the pole on $H$.

Suppose now that the two base envelopes agree at every smooth obstacle. Uniform obstacle comparison extends this equality to continuous real obstacles. The functions $G_j=\min(\widehat F,j)$ are continuous and real-valued. Every base competitor below $\widehat F$ has finite supremum and hence lies below $G_j$ for some sufficiently large integer $j$. Thus each full competitor family is the union of its families below $G_j$. Equality for all $G_j$ gives equality below $\widehat F$ by taking regularized suprema. The reduction proves equality at every smooth obstacle upstairs.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
For the mass assertion, take any $b\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ and write $a=\Psi+b\circ p$. On a relatively compact chart outside $H$, the functions $b_j=\max(b,-j)$ are locally bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic. There
\[
 \Theta+\ddc(\Psi+b_j\circ p)=p^*(\beta+\ddc b_j).
\]
Its $n$th bounded product is zero by \cref{lem:bounded-products-submersion}, since $n>d$. This bounded-product identity does not require $b_j$ to be $\beta$-plurisubharmonic; \cref{prop:bounded-products} defines the products for locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials. Plurifine locality identifies this zero product with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Theta(a)$ on $\{b\circ p>-j\}$ in the chart. These sets cover the finite locus of $b\circ p$, while the remaining pole sets and $H$ receive no extended mass. Thus $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Theta(a)=0$.

Divisorial removal and descent put every representative ordinarily equivalent to $u$ in this form. The ideal model is also of this form, with base potential $P_{\beta,\widehat 0}[w]_{\mathcal I}$, by the proved identity. The same removal and descent apply to every representative of its ordinary type. All their masses are zero, proving both volume assertions.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fibration-relative-envelope-class}\index{Relative envelope class!fibration equivalence}\index{Relative envelope class!whole-fiber reduction}
For $\varphi\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ put $v=\Psi+\varphi\circ p$. Then
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,v)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta,\varphi).
\]
In particular, for $q_M=P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_Y=P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}$,
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,q_M)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta,q_Y).
\]
Every ordinary representative of $u$ or $q_M$ has zero extended mass, so both singularity volumes of these types vanish.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Ordinary order $u\preceq v$ is equivalent to $w\preceq\varphi$. One implication follows by addition and pullback. Conversely, cancel $\Psi$ outside $H$, which has smooth volume zero. Fubini in product charts gives the base inequality almost everywhere, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} makes it hold everywhere. Relative-envelope membership requires, for every $a\in(0,1)$, a potential $h\in\PSH(M,\Theta)$ with
\[
 av+(1-a)h\leq u.
\]
A base competitor $c\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ with $a\varphi+(1-a)c\leq w$ gives such an $h$ by taking $h=\Psi+c\circ p$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Conversely, the pullbacks of $w$ and $\varphi$ have generic Lelong number zero along each $H_j$, since $H_j$ maps onto $Y$ and there are base points where both are finite. Thus $u$ and $v$ have generic Lelong number $\lambda_j$ there. The displayed inequality forces
\[
 a\lambda_j+(1-a)\nu(h,H_j)\geq\lambda_j
\]
for every $j$, so $\nu(h,H_j)\geq\lambda_j$. Divisorial removal and compact-fiber descent give $h=\Psi+c\circ p$ with $c\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$. Cancellation outside $H$ and the pole sets, followed by Fubini, gives $a\varphi+(1-a)c\leq w$ almost everywhere on the base. The inequality extends to all of $Y$ by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. This proves the first equivalence.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For the ideal reference, \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection} gives $q_M\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. Its generic divisorial coefficients are $\lambda_j$, so removal and descent give
\[
 q_M=\Psi+b\circ p,\qquad b\in\PSH(Y,\beta).
\]
Cancellation in \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus} gives $b\circ p\sim_{\mathcal I}w\circ p$. By \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback}, these ideals are extensions of the base ideals. Restriction along any local section therefore gives $b\sim_{\mathcal I}w$, even over a fiber contained in $H$.

Put $C=\max\{0,\sup_M\Psi\}$ and $K=\max\{0,\sup_Yb\}$. The potential $\Psi+q_Y\circ p-C$ is below zero and ideal-equivalent to $u$, hence is a competitor for $q_M$. Also $b-K$ is a competitor for $q_Y$. Canceling the first inequality almost everywhere and extending as above gives
\[
 q_Y-C\leq b\leq q_Y+K.
\]
Thus $b\sim q_Y$. The first equivalence with reference $b$, and reference invariance in \cref{prop:relative-elementary}, prove the second. This argument uses the existing ideal model itself, not a finite upper bound for the fiber obstacle. If $w$ belongs to this base relative class, \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality of the two upstairs envelopes at every smooth obstacle.

For any ordinary representative of $u$ or $q_M$, removal and descent give the form $\Psi+c\circ p$ with $c\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$. Outside $H$, replace $c$ locally by $\max(c,-k)$ and retain $\Psi$. These bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials have currents pulled back from the $d$-dimensional base, whose $n$th products vanish by \cref{lem:bounded-products-submersion}, since $n>d$. Plurifine locality identifies this product with the original extended measure on $\{c\circ p>-k\}$. These sets cover its finite locus; the pole sets and $H$ receive no mass. This proves the mass and volume assertions.
\end{proof}

For the all-obstacle equivalence below, suppose every fiber meets $M\setminus H$, so that its induced obstacles are finite and continuous.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fibration-obstacle-equality}\label{cor:zero-volume-product-obstacles}
The ordinary and ideal envelopes of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $M$ if and only if those of $w$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $Y$.

Put $q=P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}$. If $w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta,q)$, then
\[
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]=P_{\Theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
       \quad\text{for every smooth real }F.
\]
If $Y$ is a curve and $\beta$ is positive, write
\[
 T_w=\beta+\ddc w,\qquad
 \mu_w=\mathbf1_{\{w=-\infty\}}T_w,\qquad
 D_w=\sum_{y\in Y}\nu(w,y)\delta_y.
\]
Then zero-obstacle equality upstairs, the equality $\mu_w=D_w$, and equality upstairs at every smooth obstacle are equivalent.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The uniform obstacle comparison extends base equality from smooth to continuous obstacles, so \eqref{eq:fibration-obstacle-reduction} gives the forward passage to $M$. Conversely, for a smooth $B$ on $Y$ take $F=B\circ p$. Then $\widehat F=B+\widehat 0$. Equality upstairs gives equality of the base envelopes below this obstacle. For any continuous $G$, choose smooth $B_j\to G-\widehat 0$ uniformly. The obstacle bound gives equality below $G$ by passage to the limit. This proves the all-obstacle equivalence.

Under the relative-class hypothesis, \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality of both base envelopes at every smooth obstacle. The proved equivalence gives the stated equality upstairs.

In the curve case, $G=\widehat 0$ is continuous but need not be constant. Obstacle comparison gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_\beta[w]+\min_YG&\leq P_{\beta,G}[w]
                      \leq P_\beta[w]+\max_YG,\\
 q+\min_YG&\leq P_{\beta,G}[w]_{\mathcal I}
                      \leq q+\max_YG.
 \end{split}
\]
The polar curvatures of these two base envelopes are respectively $\mu_w$ and $D_w$, by \cref{lem:curve-polar-model,lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model,cor:polar-order-maximum}. Thus zero-obstacle equality upstairs forces $\mu_w=D_w$. Conversely, under this measure equality the curve classification in \cref{prop:curve-all-obstacle-topology} gives equality below every smooth base obstacle. Uniform approximation extends it to every $\widehat F$, and the reduction gives all-obstacle equality upstairs. Taking $F=0$ proves the remaining implication.
\end{proof}

The crossing hypothesis in \eqref{eq:divisorial-product-ideals} concerns the explicit ideal formula. The envelope reduction holds even when it fails.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:ramified-divisor-ideal-factorization}\index{Multiplier ideal!ramified divisor}\index{Envelope!ramified divisor reduction}
On $M=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, let $p,r$ be the projections and let $\sigma$ be the probability Fubini--Study form. With homogeneous coordinates $[y_0:y_1]$ and $[z_0:z_1]$, the divisor
\[
 H=\{y_0z_0^2+y_1z_1^2=0\}
\]
is smooth, meets every fiber properly and ramifies over the first factor. Give $\mathcal O_M(H)=\mathcal O_M(1,2)$ its product metric, with logarithmic divisor potential $\Psi$ and curvature $\kappa_H=p^*\sigma+2r^*\sigma$. Put $\Theta=2p^*\sigma+2r^*\sigma$ and choose $w\in\PSH(\mathbb P^1,\sigma)$ with $\sigma+\ddc w=\delta_{[0:1]}$. Both obstacle envelopes of $u=\Psi+w\circ p$ reduce to the base, but the product formula for its multiplier ideal fails at a ramification point.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
For each $[z_0:z_1]$ there is a unique point $[y_0:y_1]=[-z_1^2:z_0^2]$ on $H$, so $H$ is a smooth graph and its map to the base has degree two. No fiber lies in $H$. Since $\Theta=p^*\sigma+\kappa_H$, \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} gives both envelope identities.

Near $([0:1],[1:0])$, use $y=y_0/y_1$ and $z=z_1/z_0$. Up to smooth additions,
\[
 \Psi=\log|y+z^2|^2,\qquad w=\log|y|^2,
 \qquad u=\log|y(y+z^2)|^2.
\]
At $t=3/4$, each separate weight has trivial multiplier ideal, so the right side of \eqref{eq:divisorial-product-ideals} would be $\mathcal O_M$. However $1\notin\mathcal I(3u/4)$ at this point. Indeed, on the region $y=cz^2$ with $|c-1|<1/4$ and $0<|z|<\epsilon$, the volume change in the $y$ variable is $|z|^4$. The weighted integral of $1$ is bounded below by a positive constant times
\[
 \int_{0<|z|<\epsilon}|z|^{-6}|z|^4\,\mathrm d\lambda_z
   =2\pi\int_0^\epsilon\frac{\mathrm dr}{r}=+\infty,
\]
where $\mathrm d\lambda_z$ is planar area. Thus the full ideal is proper. The envelope identity instead follows from ideal equivalence under addition and does not require this product formula.
\end{proof}

A divisor containing an entire fiber has an infinite fiber obstacle there. The next example still admits an explicit zero-obstacle model.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:whole-fiber-incidence-envelope}\index{Obstacle!infinite fiber minimum}\index{Envelope!incidence divisor}
Let $M=\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^1$, with projections $p,r$, and let $\sigma_j$ be the standard Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^j$. In the positive closed background
\[
 \Theta=2p^*\sigma_2+r^*\sigma_1,
\]
there is a logarithmic potential $\Psi$ of a smooth prime divisor containing the fiber over $E=[0:0:1]$. Its zero-obstacle fiber infimum is finite and continuous off $E$ and tends to $+\infty$ there. Nevertheless,
\[
 P_\Theta[\Psi]=P_\Theta[\Psi]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi+q\circ p,
\]
where $q\in\PSH(\mathbb P^2,\sigma_2)$ is bounded, $0\leq q\leq\log4$ and $q(E)=\log4$. Every ordinary representative of this model has zero extended mass, so both singularity volumes vanish. The two envelopes also agree below every smooth obstacle.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Use homogeneous coordinates $[y_0:y_1:y_2]$ and $[z_0:z_1]$, and take
\[
 H=\{y_0z_0+y_1z_1=0\},\qquad
 \Psi=\log\frac{|y_0z_0+y_1z_1|^2}
 {(|y_0|^2+|y_1|^2+|y_2|^2)(|z_0|^2+|z_1|^2)}.
\]
Outside $E$, the divisor is the graph $[z_0:z_1]=[-y_1:y_0]$. Its closure includes the whole fiber over $E$, so $H$ is prime. It is smooth outside that fiber by the graph description, and smooth along it because the derivatives in the $y_0,y_1$ directions are $z_0,z_1$, which cannot both vanish. Its product metric has curvature $p^*\sigma_2+r^*\sigma_1$, hence
\[
 \Theta+\ddc\Psi=p^*\sigma_2+[H].
\]
Writing $S=|y_0|^2+|y_1|^2$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives the fiber obstacle
\[
 G(y)=-\max_{z\in\mathbb P^1}\Psi(y,z)
      =\log\frac{S+|y_2|^2}{S},\qquad G(E)=+\infty.
\]
It has no finite continuous extension at $E$.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
The largest $\sigma_2$-plurisubharmonic potential below $G$ is
\[
 q(y)=\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\log\frac{4|y_2|^2}{S+|y_2|^2},& S\leq|y_2|^2,\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle\log\frac{S+|y_2|^2}{S},& S\geq|y_2|^2.
 \end{cases}
\]
To verify positivity, work on $y_2\ne0$ with $x=(y_0/y_2,y_1/y_2)$ and $t=\log|x|^2$. Adding the Fubini--Study potential $L(x)=\log(1+|x|^2)$ gives
\[
 q+L=\begin{cases}
 \log4,&t\leq0,\\
 2\log(1+\mathrm e^t)-t,&t\geq0.
 \end{cases}
\]
This function and its first derivative match at zero. On $t>0$ its derivatives are $(\mathrm e^t-1)/(\mathrm e^t+1)\geq0$ and $2\mathrm e^t/(1+\mathrm e^t)^2>0$. The radial complex Hessian is therefore positive; the constant inner part and the matching first derivative give a plurisubharmonic function across the unit sphere. The expression is constant near $x=0$, and $q=G$ is smooth near $y_2=0$, so $q$ is globally $\sigma_2$-plurisubharmonic and bounded.

If $b\in\PSH(\mathbb P^2,\sigma_2)$ lies below $G$, then $b+L\leq\log4$ on $|x|=1$. The maximum principle on the unit ball gives $b\leq q$ inside it. Outside, the obstacle already gives $b\leq G=q$. For $0<R=|x|^2\leq1$, the inequality $q\leq G$ is $4R\leq(1+R)^2$; at $E$ it is automatic. The displayed formula also gives the asserted bounds, proving maximality.

For every ordinary or ideal competitor $v$ for $\Psi$, ordinary order or \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} gives $\nu(v,H)\geq1$. Divisorial removal and compact-fiber descent in \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal,prop:psh-descent-submersion} give $v=\Psi+b\circ p$ with $b\in\PSH(\mathbb P^2,\sigma_2)$. The bound $v\leq0$ gives $b\leq G$ off $E$, hence everywhere with its extended value. Thus $v\leq\Psi+q\circ p$. Conversely, this upper bound is an admissible competitor for both envelopes, since it lies below zero and differs boundedly from $\Psi$. This proves the envelope identities directly.

On $M\setminus H$, bounded local truncations of $b$ have products pulled back from a two-dimensional base, so their third products vanish by \cref{lem:bounded-products-submersion}. Plurifine locality gives zero extended mass. Divisorial removal puts every ordinary representative of the model in this form, proving both volume assertions. Finally bounded equivalence gives $\Psi\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,P_\Theta[\Psi]_{\mathcal I})$ by \cref{prop:relative-elementary}; \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality at every smooth obstacle.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:zero-volume-unbounded-mass-fibration}\index{Bounded mass property!failure with zero model volume}
There is a positive Hermitian background $\Theta$ on a compact fourfold which fails bounded mass and admits a potential $u$ with no analytic ordinary type such that
\[
 P_\Theta[u]=P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi,
 \qquad u\not\sim\Psi.
\]
Both envelopes agree below every smooth obstacle, and their common zero-obstacle model has both singularity volumes zero.
\end{example}
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Let $(Y,\omega)$ be the Hopf threefold of \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}. On $\mathbb P^1$, let $\sigma$ be the probability Fubini--Study form, choose a point $D$ and a logarithmic potential $\psi$ with $\sigma+\ddc\psi=[D]$ and $\sup\psi=0$. On $M=Y\times\mathbb P^1$, with projections $p,r$, put
\[
 \Theta=p^*\omega+r^*\sigma,\qquad
 H=Y\times D,\qquad \Psi=\psi\circ r.
\]
Choose the infinite-mass potential $U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\omega,0)$ of \cref{prop:infinite-envelope-class}, which gives $P_\omega[U]=0$. Its trivial multiplier ideals also give $P_\omega[U]_{\mathcal I}=0$. Set $u=\Psi+U\circ p$. Here $\widehat 0=0$, so the obstacle reduction and corollary give the envelope identities, all-obstacle equality and zero singularity volumes. Moreover $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,\Psi)$ by \cref{prop:fibration-relative-envelope-class}. Infinite extended mass makes $U$ unbounded; restricting to a section outside $H$ proves $u\not\sim\Psi$. If an analytic potential $a$ were ordinarily equivalent to $u$, \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} would give $u\sim a\sim P_\Theta[a]=P_\Theta[u]=\Psi$, a contradiction.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Finally, let $V_j$ be the smooth potentials of \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}. Their pullbacks are bounded and $\Theta$-plurisubharmonic. By \cref{prop:curve-product-extended-ma},
\[
 \int_M\mathrm{MA}_\Theta(V_j\circ p)
   =4\int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\omega(V_j)\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
Thus $\Theta$ itself fails bounded mass.
\end{proof}
