\section{Relative divisors and fiber obstacles}
\label{subsec:fibration-obstacle-reduction}

To test equality of the envelopes below all obstacles beyond curves, we need potentials whose envelopes can be computed. Consider a holomorphic submersion with compact connected fibers, and a potential that is the sum of a logarithmic potential $\Psi$ with fixed weights along some divisors and the pullback of a potential $w$ on the base. Every competitor has at least the poles of $\Psi$, and removing them leaves a current pulled back from the base. Since the fibers are compact and connected, the remaining potential descends to the base, and minimizing the obstacle over the fibers reduces both envelopes to envelopes on the base. Over a fiber contained in the divisor the induced obstacle is infinite, but normalized compactness still bounds the competitors. Because the remaining current comes from a base of smaller dimension, all these potentials have zero top-degree mass. The reduction gives criteria on the base for membership in relative envelope classes and for equality of the envelopes below all obstacles. The divisors may be singular or ramified over the base; only the explicit product formula for the multiplier ideals requires relative normal crossings.

Let $p\colon M\to Y$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion between connected compact complex manifolds, with connected fibers, and put $d=\dim Y$ and $n=\dim M$, where $1\leq d<n$. Let $H_1,\ldots,H_s$ be a finite family of distinct prime divisors on $M$ and put $H=\bigcup_jH_j$. For positive real weights $\lambda_j$, choose smooth Hermitian metrics $h_j$ on $\mathcal O_M(H_j)$, with canonical sections $s_j$ and Chern curvatures $\kappa_j$. Set
\[
 A=\sum_{j=1}^s\lambda_jH_j,\qquad
 \Psi=\sum_{j=1}^s\lambda_j\log|s_j|_{h_j}^2,\qquad
 \kappa_A=\sum_{j=1}^s\lambda_j\kappa_j.
\]
Thus $\kappa_A+\ddc\Psi=[A]=\sum_j\lambda_j[H_j]$.
For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta$ on $Y$ and $w\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$, put
\[
 \Theta=p^*\beta+\kappa_A,\qquad u=\Psi+w\circ p.
\]
The form $\beta$ need not be closed or positive. Envelopes below continuous real obstacles are defined by the same competitor families as for smooth obstacles. Let
\[
 Z=\{y\in Y:p^{-1}(y)\subset H\}
\]
be the set of points over which the whole fiber lies in $H$.

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}\index{Envelope!fibration reduction}\index{Obstacle!fiber minimum}\index{Obstacle!extended fiber minimum}\index{Singularity volume!zero-volume fibration}
For every $F\in C(M,\mathbb R)$, the function
\[
 \widehat F(y)=\inf_{x\in p^{-1}(y)\setminus H}\{F(x)-\Psi(x)\},
 \qquad\inf\varnothing=+\infty,
\]
is continuous in the extended real sense, finite on $Y\setminus Z$ and equal to $+\infty$ on $Z$. Defining the base envelopes below this extended-real obstacle by the usual competitor families, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibration-obstacle-reduction}
 \begin{split}
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]&=\Psi+P_{\beta,\widehat F}[w]\circ p,\\
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
     &=\Psi+P_{\beta,\widehat F}[w]_{\mathcal I}\circ p.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
For $F=0$ and $L=-\sup_M\Psi$, there is a constant $K$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibration-zero-obstacle-bounds}
 \begin{split}
 P_\beta[w]+L&\leq P_{\beta,\widehat 0}[w]\leq P_\beta[w]+K,\\
 P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}+L&\leq P_{\beta,\widehat 0}[w]_{\mathcal I}
       \leq P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}+K.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Every representative of the ordinary type of $u$ or of $P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ has zero extended mass. Consequently both singularity volumes of these types are zero.

If the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $w$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $Y$, then those of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first study the induced obstacle and bound the base competitors, then describe all competitors upstairs by divisorial removal and descent, and finally prove the mass assertion.

\emph{Step 1: the induced obstacle.} Since $p$ is open, $Y\setminus Z=p(M\setminus H)$ is open and nonempty. The function $F-\Psi$ is bounded below by $\min_MF-\sup_M\Psi$ and tends to $+\infty$ near $H$, because $\Psi\to-\infty$ there. On every fiber that meets $M\setminus H$, its infimum is therefore attained outside $H$. A local holomorphic section through a minimizer gives upper semicontinuity of $\widehat F$. Conversely, let $y_j\to y$ with bounded fiber minima. By compactness, a subsequence of the minimizers converges, and its limit lies outside $H$ because $F-\Psi$ remains bounded along it; this gives lower semicontinuity. If $y\in Z$, the same limit would lie in $H$, a contradiction. Thus $\widehat F\to+\infty$ at $Z$, and $\widehat F$ is continuous in the extended sense.

\emph{Step 2: a uniform bound for the base competitors.} Choose a relatively compact nonempty ball $U\subset Y\setminus Z$ and a holomorphic section $s$ of $p$ on a neighborhood of $\overline U$ with image outside $H$; such a pair exists because local sections pass through every point of the open set $M\setminus H$. There is a finite $C_U$ with $\widehat F\leq F\circ s-\Psi\circ s\leq C_U$ on $U$. Fix a smooth probability volume form $\mathrm dV$ on $Y$. For every $b\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ below $\widehat F$, normalized compactness in \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives
\[
 -C_\beta\leq\int_U(b-\sup_Yb)\,\mathrm dV
       \leq(C_U-\sup_Yb)\int_U\mathrm dV.
\]
Hence every such $b$ satisfies $\sup_Yb\leq K_F=C_U+C_\beta/\int_U\mathrm dV$. A sufficiently low translate of $w$ belongs to both base competitor families. Their regularized suprema are therefore $\beta$-plurisubharmonic functions, not identically $-\infty$, and by the extended continuity of $\widehat F$ they still lie below $\widehat F$. For $F=0$, one has $\widehat 0\geq L$, so translates by $L$ of the competitors below zero are competitors below $\widehat 0$; conversely, every competitor $b$ below $\widehat 0$ gives the competitor $b-K_0$ below zero. This proves \eqref{eq:fibration-zero-obstacle-bounds} with $K=K_0$.

\emph{Step 3: the competitors upstairs.} The pullback of $w$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, so its generic Lelong numbers are nonnegative, and $\nu(u,H_j)=\lambda_j+\nu(w\circ p,H_j)\geq\lambda_j$. Let $v$ be an ordinary competitor for $u$, or an ideal competitor with $v\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. By the ordinary order or by \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}, its generic Lelong number along $H_j$ is at least $\lambda_j$. Divisorial removal in \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} gives
\[
 \Theta+\ddc v-[A]\geq0.
\]
Hence $v-\Psi$ has a quasi-plurisubharmonic representative $a$ satisfying $p^*\beta+\ddc a\geq0$. Descent along the compact connected fibers, \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion}, gives $a=b\circ p$ for a unique $b\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$, and $v=\Psi+b\circ p$ for the canonical representatives. Conversely, every potential of this form is $\Theta$-plurisubharmonic.

For such $v$, one has $v\preceq u$ if and only if $b\preceq w$. Indeed, canceling $\Psi$ outside $H$ gives the base inequality almost everywhere, by Fubini's theorem in product charts, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}; the reverse implication follows by pullback and addition. Similarly, $v\sim_{\mathcal I}u$ if and only if $b\sim_{\mathcal I}w$: \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus} permits both addition and cancellation of $\Psi$, the pullback formula in \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback} then gives one direction, and restriction of the extended ideals along any local section gives the other. This comparison of ideals does not need a section outside $H$.

\emph{Step 4: the envelope identities.} The bound $v\leq F$ is equivalent to $b\leq\widehat F$, with no constraint over $Z$. The competitor families upstairs and on the base therefore correspond to each other, which gives \eqref{eq:fibration-obstacle-reduction} outside $H$, since regularization commutes with pullback in product coordinates there. On $H$ both sides equal $-\infty$: every competitor is at most $\Psi+K_F$, by the bound $b\leq K_F$, and this upper semicontinuous function equals $-\infty$ on $H$, so regularization does not change the values there.

\emph{Step 5: equality below all obstacles.} Suppose that the two base envelopes agree below every smooth obstacle. Uniform obstacle comparison extends this equality to continuous real obstacles. The functions $G_j=\min(\widehat F,j)$ are continuous and real-valued. Every base competitor below $\widehat F$ has finite supremum and hence lies below $G_j$ for every sufficiently large integer $j$. Each full competitor family is thus the union of its subfamilies below the $G_j$, and equality for all $G_j$ gives equality below $\widehat F$ after taking regularized suprema. By the reduction, the envelopes upstairs agree below every smooth obstacle.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\emph{Step 6: zero mass.} Take any $b\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ and write $a=\Psi+b\circ p$. On a relatively compact chart outside $H$, the functions $b_j=\max(b,-j)$ are locally bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic, and there
\[
 \Theta+\ddc(\Psi+b_j\circ p)=p^*(\beta+\ddc b_j).
\]
Since $n>d$, the $n$th bounded product of this current is zero by \cref{lem:bounded-products-submersion}. This identity does not require $b_j$ to be $\beta$-plurisubharmonic: \cref{prop:bounded-products} defines the products for locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials. By plurifine locality, this zero product agrees with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Theta(a)$ on $\{b\circ p>-j\}$ in the chart. These sets cover the locus where $b\circ p$ is finite, while the remaining pole set and $H$ receive no extended mass. Thus $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Theta(a)=0$.

By divisorial removal and descent, every representative ordinarily equivalent to $u$ has this form. By the identity already proved, the ideal model is also of this form, with base potential $P_{\beta,\widehat 0}[w]_{\mathcal I}$, where $\widehat0$ is the induced obstacle for $F=0$. The same removal and descent apply to every representative of its ordinary type. All these masses are zero, which proves both volume assertions.
\end{proof}

Recall from \cref{def:relative-envelope-class} that $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$ means that $u\preceq\varphi$ and that, for every $a\in(0,1)$, there is a \emph{stretching competitor} $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $a\varphi+(1-a)h\leq u$; here $a=1-b^{-1}$ in the notation of that definition.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fibration-relative-envelope-class}\index{Relative envelope class!fibration equivalence}\index{Relative envelope class!whole-fiber reduction}\index{Divisor!vertical fibration reduction}
For $\varphi\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ put $v=\Psi+\varphi\circ p$. Then
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,v)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta,\varphi).
\]
In particular, for $q_M=P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_Y=P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}$,
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,q_M)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta,q_Y).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Step~3 of the proof of \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}, $u\preceq v$ if and only if $w\preceq\varphi$. Membership in the relative envelope class requires, for every $a\in(0,1)$, a stretching competitor $h\in\PSH(M,\Theta)$ with
\[
 av+(1-a)h\leq u.
\]
A base competitor $c\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$ with $a\varphi+(1-a)c\leq w$ gives such an $h$, namely $h=\Psi+c\circ p$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Conversely, put $\rho_j=\nu(w\circ p,H_j)$ and $\sigma_j=\nu(\varphi\circ p,H_j)$. The ordinary order $w\preceq\varphi$ gives $\rho_j\geq\sigma_j\geq0$. Comparing generic Lelong numbers along $H_j$ in the stretching inequality $av+(1-a)h\leq u$ gives
\[
 a(\lambda_j+\sigma_j)+(1-a)\nu(h,H_j)
       \geq\lambda_j+\rho_j.
\]
Consequently $\nu(h,H_j)\geq\lambda_j+(\rho_j-a\sigma_j)/(1-a)\geq\lambda_j$. Divisorial removal and descent along the fibers give $h=\Psi+c\circ p$ with $c\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$. Canceling $\Psi$ outside $H$ as in Step~3, we obtain $a\varphi+(1-a)c\leq w$ on $Y$. This proves the first equivalence.

By \eqref{eq:fibration-obstacle-reduction} for $F=0$, $q_M=\Psi+b\circ p$ with $b=P_{\beta,\widehat 0}[w]_{\mathcal I}$, and \eqref{eq:fibration-zero-obstacle-bounds} gives $b\sim q_Y$. The first equivalence with reference $b$, together with the invariance of the class under ordinarily equivalent references in \cref{prop:relative-elementary}, proves the second equivalence.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fibration-obstacle-equality}\label{cor:zero-volume-product-obstacles}\index{Envelope!all-obstacle fibration criterion}
The ordinary and ideal envelopes of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $M$ if and only if those of $w$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $Y$.

Put $q=P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}$. If $w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta,q)$, then
\[
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]=P_{\Theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
       \quad\text{for every smooth real }F.
\]
If $Y$ is a curve, write
\[
 T_w=\beta+\ddc w,\qquad
 \mu_w=\mathbf1_{\{w=-\infty\}}T_w,\qquad
 D_w=\sum_{y\in Y}\nu(w,y)\delta_y
\]
for the curvature measure of $w$, its polar part and its atomic part. Then equality of the two envelopes of $u$ below zero, the equality $\mu_w=D_w$, and equality of the two envelopes of $u$ below every smooth obstacle are equivalent.
They are also equivalent to
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
That equality on $Y$ implies equality on $M$ is part of \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}. For the converse, we approximate a smooth obstacle on the base by the induced obstacles of continuous obstacles upstairs. Suppose that the envelopes upstairs agree below every smooth obstacle. Uniform obstacle comparison extends this equality to continuous real obstacles. Fix a smooth real function $B$ on $Y$ and put $G=\widehat 0$. The continuous obstacles
\[
 F_j=B\circ p+\max(\Psi,-j)\qquad(j\geq1)
\]
have induced base obstacles
\[
 G_j=B+\max(G-j,0),
\]
with value $+\infty$ on $Z$. The set $Z$ has smooth volume zero: in each product chart of the submersion, the preimage of $Z$ lies in $H$, which has smooth volume zero, and Fubini's theorem gives the assertion on the base. By the reduction and the assumed equality, the two base envelopes below $G_j$ agree off $Z$, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}:
\[
 q_j=P_{\beta,G_j}[w]_{\mathcal I}=P_{\beta,G_j}[w].
\]
Write $q_B=P_{\beta,B}[w]_{\mathcal I}$. Then $q_B\leq q_j\leq q_1$, and the $q_j$ decrease to a $\beta$-plurisubharmonic potential $r$, not identically $-\infty$, with convergence in $L^1$; indeed, their negative parts are bounded by that of $q_B$, and \cref{cor:decreasing-limit} applies. Off $Z$, the bounds $q_j\leq G_j\downarrow B$ give $r\leq B$, and almost-everywhere order in \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} extends the inequality to all of $Y$.

Each $q_j$ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $w$. To see this without assuming $G_j$ finite, put $Q_0=P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $Q_j=P_{\Theta,F_j}[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Translation of the competitor families gives
\[
 Q_0+\min_MF_j\leq Q_j\leq Q_0+\max_MF_j.
\]
Thus $Q_j\sim Q_0\sim_{\mathcal I}u$ by \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection}. Since $Q_j=\Psi+q_j\circ p$, cancellation and reflection of multiplier ideals under submersions, \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus,lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback}, give $q_j\sim_{\mathcal I}w$. The same bounded comparison with $P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}$ gives $q_B\sim_{\mathcal I}w$.

Let $\pi$ be any smooth local modification of a coordinate neighborhood in the base. The bounds $q_B\circ\pi\leq q_j\circ\pi\leq q_1\circ\pi$ give decreasing local $L^1$ convergence to $r\circ\pi$. At every point $x$ of this modification, \cref{lem:lelong-lone-semicontinuity} and the ordinary order give
\[
 \nu(w\circ\pi,x)
 \leq\nu(r\circ\pi,x)
 \leq\nu(q_B\circ\pi,x)=\nu(w\circ\pi,x).
\]
All these Lelong numbers are therefore equal, and \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} gives $r\sim_{\mathcal I}w$. Together with $r\leq B$, this makes $r$ a competitor for $q_B$. Hence $r=q_B$.

Put $\epsilon_j=\max\{0,\sup_Y(q_j-B)\}$. The upper semicontinuous functions $q_j-B$ decrease to $q_B-B\leq0$ on the compact manifold $Y$, so for every $\epsilon>0$ the decreasing compact sets $\{q_j-B\geq\epsilon\}$ are eventually empty. Thus $\epsilon_j\to0$. Every ordinary competitor $b$ below $G_j$ satisfies $b\leq q_j$, so $b-\epsilon_j\leq B$ and $b-\epsilon_j\preceq w$. Taking regularized suprema yields
\[
 q_j-\epsilon_j\leq P_{\beta,B}[w].
\]
Since $q_B\leq q_j$, letting $j\to\infty$ proves $q_B\leq P_{\beta,B}[w]$. The reverse inequality always holds, so the two envelopes agree below $B$. Since $B$ was arbitrary, this proves the converse.

Under the relative-class hypothesis, \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality of the two base envelopes below every smooth obstacle, and the equivalence just proved gives the stated equality upstairs.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
In the curve case, \eqref{eq:fibration-zero-obstacle-bounds} shows that the two base envelopes below $\widehat 0$ are ordinarily equivalent to $P_\beta[w]$ and $q$. By \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models,cor:polar-order-maximum}, their polar curvatures are therefore $\mu_w$ and $D_w$, respectively. Thus equality of the envelopes upstairs below zero forces $\mu_w=D_w$. Conversely, under this equality of measures, \cref{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion} gives equality below every smooth obstacle on the base, hence upstairs by the first assertion. Taking $F=0$ proves the remaining implication. The same curve criterion identifies the equality of measures with membership of $w$ in the base relative envelope class of $q$, and the equivalence for ideal references in \cref{prop:fibration-relative-envelope-class} gives the last assertion upstairs.
\end{proof}

The reduction does not compute the multiplier ideals of $u$. These ideals have an explicit form when the divisors have relative simple normal crossings, that is, when near every point there are product coordinates $(y,z)$ with $p(y,z)=y$ in which the components through the point are distinct coordinate hyperplanes in the fiber variables. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divisorial-product-ideals}
 \mathcal I\bigl(t(\Psi+b\circ p)\bigr)
   =\mathcal O_M\!\left(-\sum_j\lfloor t\lambda_j\rfloor H_j\right)
                    \bigl(\mathcal I(tb)\mathcal O_M\bigr)
       \qquad(t>0,\ b\in\PSH(Y,\beta)).
\end{equation}
To see this, work in such coordinates. Up to a smooth correction, $\Psi$ is the sum of the weights $\lambda_j\log|z_j|^2$ over the components through the point. Since $b$ is locally bounded above, integrability forces vanishing to order at least $\lfloor t\lambda_j\rfloor$ along each hyperplane. After this product of coordinate powers is factored out, the remaining fiber weight is
\[
 \prod_j|z_j|^{-2(t\lambda_j-\lfloor t\lambda_j\rfloor)}.
\]
It is integrable and bounded below by a positive constant on a small polydisk. Integrability of the quotient against this weight therefore implies integrability against the pullback of $\mathrm e^{-tb}$ alone, and \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback} puts the quotient in the extension of $\mathcal I(tb)$. Conversely, local generators of that ideal are weighted-integrable against the integrable product weight, by Fubini's theorem. Outside $H$ the formula is the same pullback lemma. For one smooth divisor of weight one transverse to the fibers, the first factor is the ideal sheaf of the divisor raised to the power $\lfloor t\rfloor$. Without normal crossings the formula can fail, although the envelopes still reduce to the base.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:ramified-divisor-ideal-factorization}\index{Multiplier ideal!ramified divisor}\index{Envelope!ramified divisor reduction}
On $M=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, let $p,r$ be the projections and let $\sigma$ be the probability Fubini--Study form. With homogeneous coordinates $[y_0:y_1]$ and $[z_0:z_1]$, the divisor
\[
 H=\{y_0z_0^2+y_1z_1^2=0\}
\]
is smooth, meets every fiber properly and ramifies over the first factor. Give $\mathcal O_M(H)=\mathcal O_M(1,2)$ its product metric, with logarithmic divisor potential $\Psi$ and curvature $\kappa_H=p^*\sigma+2r^*\sigma$. Put $\Theta=2p^*\sigma+2r^*\sigma$ and choose $w\in\PSH(\mathbb P^1,\sigma)$ with $\sigma+\ddc w=\delta_{[0:1]}$. Both obstacle envelopes of $u=\Psi+w\circ p$ reduce to the base, but the product formula for its multiplier ideals fails at a ramification point.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
For each $[z_0:z_1]$ there is a unique point $[y_0:y_1]=[-z_1^2:z_0^2]$ on $H$, so $H$ is a smooth graph over the second factor, and its projection to the base has degree two. In particular no fiber of $p$ lies in $H$. Since $\Theta=p^*\sigma+\kappa_H$, \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} gives both envelope identities.

Near $([0:1],[1:0])$, use $y=y_0/y_1$ and $z=z_1/z_0$. Up to smooth additions,
\[
 \Psi=\log|y+z^2|^2,\qquad w=\log|y|^2,
 \qquad u=\log|y(y+z^2)|^2.
\]
At $t=3/4$, each of the two weights separately has trivial multiplier ideal, so the right side of \eqref{eq:divisorial-product-ideals} would be $\mathcal O_M$. However, $1\notin\mathcal I(3u/4)$ at this point. Indeed, consider the region where $y=cz^2$ with $|c-1|<1/4$ and $0<|z|<\epsilon$; for fixed $z$, it has area comparable to $|z|^4$ in the $y$ variable. The weighted integral of $1$ is therefore bounded below by a positive constant times
\[
 \int_{0<|z|<\epsilon}|z|^{-6}|z|^4\,\mathrm d\lambda_z
   =2\pi\int_0^\epsilon\frac{\mathrm dr}{r}=+\infty,
\]
where $\mathrm d\lambda_z$ is planar area. Thus the multiplier ideal is proper.
\end{proof}

If the divisor contains an entire fiber, the induced obstacle is infinite over that fiber. The next example still admits an explicit model for the zero obstacle.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:whole-fiber-incidence-envelope}\index{Obstacle!infinite fiber minimum}\index{Envelope!incidence divisor}
Let $M=\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^1$, with projections $p,r$, and let $\sigma_j$ be the probability Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^j$. In the positive closed background
\[
 \Theta=2p^*\sigma_2+r^*\sigma_1,
\]
there is a logarithmic potential $\Psi$ of a smooth prime divisor containing the fiber over $E=[0:0:1]$. Its fiber infimum for the zero obstacle is finite and continuous off $E$ and tends to $+\infty$ at $E$. Nevertheless,
\[
 P_\Theta[\Psi]=P_\Theta[\Psi]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi+q\circ p,
\]
where $q\in\PSH(\mathbb P^2,\sigma_2)$ is bounded, $0\leq q\leq\log4$ and $q(E)=\log4$. Every ordinary representative of this model has zero extended mass, so both singularity volumes vanish. The two envelopes also agree below every smooth obstacle.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Use homogeneous coordinates $[y_0:y_1:y_2]$ and $[z_0:z_1]$, and take
\[
 H=\{y_0z_0+y_1z_1=0\},\qquad
 \Psi=\log\frac{|y_0z_0+y_1z_1|^2}
 {(|y_0|^2+|y_1|^2+|y_2|^2)(|z_0|^2+|z_1|^2)}.
\]
Outside $E$, the divisor is the graph $[z_0:z_1]=[-y_1:y_0]$. Its closure contains the whole fiber over $E$, so $H$ is prime. It is smooth outside that fiber by the graph description, and smooth along it because the derivatives in the $y_0$ and $y_1$ directions are $z_0$ and $z_1$, which cannot both vanish. Its product metric has curvature $p^*\sigma_2+r^*\sigma_1$, hence
\[
 \Theta+\ddc\Psi=p^*\sigma_2+[H].
\]
Writing $S=|y_0|^2+|y_1|^2$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives the fiber obstacle
\[
 G(y)=-\max_{z\in\mathbb P^1}\Psi(y,z)
      =\log\frac{S+|y_2|^2}{S},\qquad G(E)=+\infty.
\]
It has no finite continuous extension at $E$.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
We claim that the largest $\sigma_2$-plurisubharmonic potential below $G$ is
\[
 q(y)=\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\log\frac{4|y_2|^2}{S+|y_2|^2},& S\leq|y_2|^2,\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle\log\frac{S+|y_2|^2}{S},& S\geq|y_2|^2.
 \end{cases}
\]
To see that $q$ is $\sigma_2$-plurisubharmonic, work on $y_2\ne0$ with $x=(y_0/y_2,y_1/y_2)$ and $t=\log|x|^2$. Adding the Fubini--Study potential $L(x)=\log(1+|x|^2)$ gives
\[
 q+L=\begin{cases}
 \log4,&t\leq0,\\
 2\log(1+\mathrm e^t)-t,&t\geq0.
 \end{cases}
\]
This function and its first derivative are continuous at $t=0$. For $t>0$, its first two derivatives are $(\mathrm e^t-1)/(\mathrm e^t+1)\geq0$ and $2\mathrm e^t/(1+\mathrm e^t)^2>0$. It is therefore a convex nondecreasing function of $t$, hence plurisubharmonic in $x$, across the unit sphere as well. The expression is constant near $x=0$, and $q=G$ is smooth near $y_2=0$, so $q$ is globally $\sigma_2$-plurisubharmonic and bounded.

If $b\in\PSH(\mathbb P^2,\sigma_2)$ lies below $G$, then $b+L\leq\log4$ on $|x|=1$, and the maximum principle on the unit ball gives $b\leq q$ inside it. Outside the ball, the obstacle already gives $b\leq G=q$. For $0<R=|x|^2\leq1$, the inequality $q\leq G$ reads $4R\leq(1+R)^2$, and at $E$ it is automatic. The formula for $q$ also gives the asserted bounds, which proves the claim.

By \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}, applied with $\beta=\sigma_2$, $w=0$ and $F=0$, the two envelopes of $\Psi$ are $\Psi$ plus the pullbacks of the two envelopes of $0$ below $G$. Every $\sigma_2$-plurisubharmonic function is at least as singular as $0$, and $q$ is bounded, hence multiplier-ideal equivalent to $0$. Both base envelopes therefore equal $q$, which proves the envelope identities, and the same proposition gives the volume assertions. Finally, since the model is ordinarily equivalent to $\Psi$, \cref{prop:relative-elementary} gives $\Psi\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,P_\Theta[\Psi]_{\mathcal I})$, and \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality below every smooth obstacle.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:zero-volume-unbounded-mass-fibration}\index{Bounded mass property!failure with zero model volume}
On a compact fourfold without the bounded mass property, there are a Hermitian background $\Theta$ and a potential $u$ without an analytic ordinary type such that
\[
 P_\Theta[u]=P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi,
 \qquad u\not\sim\Psi.
\]
Both envelopes agree below every smooth obstacle, and their common model for the zero obstacle has both singularity volumes zero.
\end{example}
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Let $(Y,\omega)$ be the Hopf threefold of \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}. On $\mathbb P^1$, let $\sigma$ be the probability Fubini--Study form, choose a point $D$ and a logarithmic potential $\psi$ with $\sigma+\ddc\psi=[D]$ and $\sup\psi=0$. On $M=Y\times\mathbb P^1$, with projections $p,r$, put
\[
 \Theta=p^*\omega+r^*\sigma,\qquad
 H=Y\times D,\qquad \Psi=\psi\circ r.
\]
Take the potential $U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\omega,0)$ of infinite mass from \cref{prop:infinite-envelope-class}, which satisfies $P_\omega[U]=0$. Its multiplier ideals are trivial, so also $P_\omega[U]_{\mathcal I}=0$. Set $u=\Psi+U\circ p$. Here $\widehat 0=0$, so \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction,cor:fibration-obstacle-equality} give the envelope identities, the equality below all obstacles and the vanishing of the singularity volumes. Moreover $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,\Psi)$ by \cref{prop:fibration-relative-envelope-class}. Since $U$ has infinite extended mass, it is unbounded; restriction to a section outside $H$ therefore proves $u\not\sim\Psi$. If a potential $a$ with analytic singularities were ordinarily equivalent to $u$, \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} would give $u\sim a\sim P_\Theta[a]=P_\Theta[u]=\Psi$, a contradiction.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Finally, let $V_j$ be the smooth potentials of \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}. Their pullbacks are bounded and $\Theta$-plurisubharmonic, and \cref{prop:curve-product-extended-ma} gives
\[
 \int_M\mathrm{MA}_\Theta(V_j\circ p)
   =4\int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\omega(V_j)\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
Thus $v_+(\Theta)=+\infty$, and $M$ fails the bounded mass property.
\end{proof}

The descent of potentials uses that the fibers are connected. Otherwise the residual potential can take independent values on different components of a fiber, as a finite isogeny shows.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:disconnected-fiber-obstacle-descent}\index{Descent!disconnected fiber obstruction}\index{Envelope!disconnected fiber obstruction}
There is a surjective holomorphic submersion $p\colon M\to Y$ from a connected compact K\"ahler surface to an elliptic curve, with four components in every fiber, for which both identities in \eqref{eq:fibration-obstacle-reduction} fail. The pole divisor is smooth and maps onto $Y$, and the obstacle upstairs is smooth.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$, let $\beta$ be its flat probability form, and let $q\colon E\to E$ be multiplication by two. On $M=E\times\mathbb P^1$, denote the projections by $a,r$ and put $p=q\circ a$. Then $q$ is an unramified covering of degree four and $q^*\beta=4\beta$. The manifold $M$ is connected, whereas every fiber of $p$ is the disjoint union of four projective lines.

On $\mathbb P^1$ take the probability Fubini--Study form $\sigma$, a point $D$ and a logarithmic potential $\psi$ with $\sigma+\ddc\psi=[D]$ and $\sup\psi=0$. Put
\[
 H=E\times D,\qquad \Psi=\psi\circ r,\qquad
 \Theta=p^*\beta+r^*\sigma=4a^*\beta+r^*\sigma.
\]
Thus $\Theta+\ddc\Psi=p^*\beta+[H]$, which corresponds to the base potential $w=0$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ so small that
\[
 f(\zeta)=\epsilon\cos(2\pi\operatorname{Re}\zeta)
       \in\PSH(E,4\beta),
\]
and set $F=f\circ a$. The function $f$ is well defined and smooth on $E$. Since it changes sign under translation by $1/2$, it is not constant on the fibers of $q$.

Apply \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} to the projection $a$, which has connected fibers, with base background $4\beta$ and base potential zero. Its induced obstacle is $f$, since $\sup\psi=0$. The bounded potential $f$ is a competitor for both base envelopes and equals their obstacle, so
\[
 P_{\Theta,F}[\Psi]=P_{\Theta,F}[\Psi]_{\mathcal I}
       =\Psi+f\circ a.
\]
For the map $p$, the fiber obstacle of \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} would instead be
\[
 \widehat F(y)=\min_{q(\zeta)=y}f(\zeta).
\]
Every expression $\Psi+b\circ p$ is invariant under translation of the first factor by points of the kernel of $q$. The computed envelope is not invariant under translation by $1/2$, because $\Psi$ is unchanged and $f$ changes sign. It therefore cannot equal either right side of \eqref{eq:fibration-obstacle-reduction} for $p$.
\end{proof}

The correct formula takes the fiber minimum over each connected component of a fiber separately. These components are the points of the finite covering of the base in \cref{lem:submersion-component-cover}.

Envelopes and relative envelope classes also pass through this finite covering. Pullback of competitors and the maximum over the fibers of a finite unramified covering identify the envelopes upstairs and downstairs, and the same fiber maximum transfers stretching competitors from the covering space to the base.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite-cover-envelope-transport}\index{Envelope!finite unramified cover}\index{Relative envelope class!finite unramified cover}
Let $r\colon Y\to X$ be a finite unramified holomorphic covering between connected compact complex manifolds. Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $u,\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and put $\theta'=r^*\theta$ and $u'=u\circ r$. For every $F\in C(X,\mathbb R)$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_{\theta',F\circ r}[u']&=P_{\theta,F}[u]\circ r,\\
 P_{\theta',F\circ r}[u']_{\mathcal I}
   &=P_{\theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}\circ r.
 \end{split}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 u'\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\theta',\varphi\circ r)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $v\in\PSH(Y,\theta')$, define
\[
 v_{\max}(x)=\max_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}v(y).
\]
Over a sufficiently small ball in $X$, this is the finite maximum of the functions obtained by composing $v$ with the local inverse branches of $r$. Each of them is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, so $v_{\max}\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ by \cref{lem:max-psh}. If $v\preceq u'$, then $v_{\max}\preceq u$. If $v\sim_{\mathcal I}u'$, a local biholomorphic change of variables makes each branch multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u$, and the maximum rule in \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus} gives $v_{\max}\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. The obstacle bound $v\leq F\circ r$ gives $v_{\max}\leq F$.

Thus every competitor upstairs is dominated by the pullback of a corresponding competitor downstairs. Conversely, pullback of a competitor downstairs preserves the background inequality, the ordinary order or multiplier-ideal equivalence, and the obstacle bound. These two correspondences between the competitor families give the envelope identities; upper regularization commutes with $r$, since $r$ is locally biholomorphic.

Since $r$ is surjective, it reflects the ordinary order: $u'\preceq\varphi\circ r$ if and only if $u\preceq\varphi$. Fix $a\in(0,1)$. Pullback takes any stretching competitor $h$ with $a\varphi+(1-a)h\leq u$ to a stretching competitor upstairs. Conversely, if $v\in\PSH(Y,\theta')$ satisfies
\[
 a\varphi\circ r+(1-a)v\leq u',
\]
then taking the maximum over the fibers gives $a\varphi+(1-a)v_{\max}\leq u$; this also holds at poles, because the convex sums are defined there. Applying the definition of the relative class for every $a$ proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{38\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fibration-component-cover}\index{Envelope!cover of fiber components}\index{Envelope!curve criterion on a component cover}\index{Obstacle!minimum on a fiber component}
Keep the data $A,H,\Psi,\kappa_A,\beta,w,\Theta,u$ above, but allow the fibers of the submersion $p$ to be disconnected. For the factorization $p=r\circ\pi$ of \cref{lem:submersion-component-cover}, set $\beta'=r^*\beta$ and $w'=w\circ r$. For $F\in C(M,\mathbb R)$ define
\[
 \widehat F_{\mathrm{comp}}(y')
   =\inf_{x\in\pi^{-1}(y')\setminus H}\{F(x)-\Psi(x)\},
 \qquad\inf\varnothing=+\infty.
\]
This obstacle is continuous in the extended real sense on $Y'$, and
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]
   &=\Psi+P_{\beta',\widehat F_{\mathrm{comp}}}[w']\circ\pi,\\
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
   &=\Psi+P_{\beta',\widehat F_{\mathrm{comp}}}[w']_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi.
 \end{split}
\]
Every representative of the ordinary type of $u$ or of $P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ has zero extended mass, and both singularity volumes of these types are zero. The ordinary and ideal envelopes of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $M$ if and only if those of $w'$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $Y'$.

For $\varphi\in\PSH(Y,\beta)$, put $v=\Psi+\varphi\circ p$. Then
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,v)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta,\varphi).
\]
For $q_M=P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_Y=P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}$, one also has
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,q_M)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 w\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta,q_Y).
\]

If $Y$ is a curve, let $\mu_w,D_w$ be the polar and atomic measures of \cref{cor:fibration-obstacle-equality}. Then equality of the envelopes of $u$ below zero, the equality $\mu_w=D_w$, and equality of the envelopes of $u$ below every smooth obstacle are equivalent.
They are also equivalent to $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta,q_M)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The covering map $r$ preserves the curvature inequality, so $w'\in\PSH(Y',\beta')$. Moreover $\dim Y'=\dim Y<\dim M$, and
\[
 \Theta=\pi^*\beta'+\kappa_A,\qquad u=\Psi+w'\circ\pi.
\]
Applying \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} to the submersion $\pi$, which has connected fibers, gives the obstacle identities and the mass assertions, and \cref{cor:fibration-obstacle-equality} for the same submersion gives the equivalence for all obstacles. In \cref{ex:disconnected-fiber-obstacle-descent}, this factorization has $Y'=E$, $r=q$ and $\pi=a$, so it recovers the computed envelope.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For the assertions on relative classes, apply \cref{prop:fibration-relative-envelope-class} to $\pi$, with reference $\varphi\circ r$ on $Y'$, and then transfer membership through $r$ by \cref{prop:finite-cover-envelope-transport}. That proposition, applied with the zero obstacle, also gives
\[
 P_{\beta'}[w']_{\mathcal I}=q_Y\circ r.
\]
Applying the same two equivalences with these ideal references proves the assertion for $q_M$ and $q_Y$.

In the curve case, $Y'$ is a curve. The covering is locally biholomorphic, so curvature measures and point Lelong numbers pull back without multiplicities. The pole set of $w'$ is $r^{-1}\{w=-\infty\}$, and therefore
\[
 \mu_{w'}=r^*\mu_w,\qquad D_{w'}=r^*D_w,
\]
where the pullback of a measure is defined on each sheet through the local inverse of $r$. Such pullbacks reflect equality: over every sufficiently small ball in $Y$, the restriction of a measure is recovered from any sheet over that ball. Hence $\mu_{w'}=D_{w'}$ if and only if $\mu_w=D_w$. The curve case of \cref{cor:fibration-obstacle-equality}, applied to $\pi$, identifies the former equality with the equality of the envelopes upstairs below zero, with their equality below every smooth obstacle and with membership in the relative envelope class upstairs. This proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}
