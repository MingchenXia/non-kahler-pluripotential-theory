\section{Changing the prescribed singularities}

A more singular reference has a smaller normalization scalar. A competitor built from the smaller-background envelope also gives a quantitative comparison with the zero reference, uniform over all logarithmic densities. In this section the reference varies among the model potentials satisfying \eqref{eq:als-model-condition} for the fixed semipositive big background $\theta$, and $B_0,H_0$ denote the functionals of the zero reference in the same background.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:reference-normalization-comparison}
Let $\varphi,\psi$ satisfy \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}. If $\varphi\preceq\psi$, then $B_\varphi\leq B_\psi$ and $H_\varphi\leq H_\psi$. Moreover, if $0<\varepsilon<1$ and $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty$, then
\[
 B_0(G)+n\log(1-\varepsilon)
       \leq B_\varphi(G)\leq B_0(G)
       \qquad(G\in C(X)),
\]
and
\[
 H_0(\nu)+n\log(1-\varepsilon)
       \leq H_\varphi(\nu)\leq H_0(\nu)
       \qquad(\nu\in\mathcal P(X)).
\]
The dual inequalities include infinite values.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $G$, and let $u_\varphi,u_\psi$ solve the equations in the ordinary types of $\varphi$ and $\psi$, with constants $c_\varphi,c_\psi$. Then $u_\varphi\preceq u_\psi$. If $c_\varphi>c_\psi$, the singular strict domination principle applied to $u_\psi$ and $u_\varphi+t$ would give $u_\psi\geq u_\varphi+t$ for every real $t$, which is impossible at a point where both are finite. This proves the monotonicity of $B$, and taking the defining supremum gives the monotonicity of $H$. Since $\varphi\preceq0$ and the zero potential is itself a model reference, this also proves both upper bounds.

For the lower bound, let $v$ be a bounded solution for $G$ with the zero reference, with constant $c_0$, and put
\[
 h=P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)+(1-\varepsilon)v.
\]
The two summands are $\varepsilon\theta$- and $(1-\varepsilon)\theta$-plurisubharmonic, respectively, so positivity and homogeneity give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(h)
       \geq(1-\varepsilon)^n\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)
       =(1-\varepsilon)^nc_0\mathrm{e}^G\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
As in the proof of \cref{lem:relative-density-lower-bound}, this inequality is justified first on finite truncation levels and then off the pole set of $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)$, which has zero smooth volume. Also $h\preceq u_\varphi$. If $c_\varphi<(1-\varepsilon)^nc_0$, strict domination would force $u_\varphi\geq h+t$ for every $t$. Thus $c_\varphi\geq(1-\varepsilon)^nc_0$. Taking logarithms proves the scalar bound, and taking the defining suprema proves the dual bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:small-reference-adjoint-limits}
Let the model references $\varphi_j$ satisfy $P_{\varepsilon_j\theta}(\varphi_j)\not\equiv-\infty$, where $0<\varepsilon_j<1$ and $\varepsilon_j\to0$. Then $B_{\varphi_j}\to B_0$ uniformly on $C(X)$. Let $G_j,G\in C(X)$ with $G_j\to G$ uniformly, and let $\nu_j\in\mathcal A_{\varphi_j}(G_j)$. Then every weak cluster point of $(\nu_j)$ belongs to $\mathcal A_0(G)$, the densities of the $\nu_j$ are uniformly integrable, and a weakly convergent subsequence also converges weakly in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. If $\mathcal A_0(G)$ is a singleton, the whole sequence converges to it in both senses.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $\delta_j=-n\log(1-\varepsilon_j)\to0$. \Cref{prop:reference-normalization-comparison} gives $\Delta(\varphi_j,0)\leq\delta_j$, which is the uniform convergence of $B_{\varphi_j}$ to $B_0$ on $C(X)$. The assertions about supporting probabilities follow from \cref{prop:normalization-distance-stability} with the zero reference as limit.
\end{proof}

For ordinary divisorial types, a comparison of positive residual forms gives a geometric criterion for this convergence of functionals, as the next two sections show. We do not know general convergence criteria for references with degenerate residuals or with arbitrary singularities; \cref{sec:convex-body-reference-stability} treats special families on closed projective backgrounds.
