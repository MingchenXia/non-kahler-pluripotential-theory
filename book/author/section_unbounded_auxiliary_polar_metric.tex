\subsection{A polar metric in a background with unbounded mass}
\label{subsec:unbounded-auxiliary-polar-metric}

The auxiliary factor can make the ambient masses unbounded while leaving the complete product metric unchanged. The Hopf threefold gives an explicit positive background; varying its coefficient over the curve product also makes the background genuinely nonclosed.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:unbounded-auxiliary-polar-metric}\index{Metric completeness!unbounded ambient mass}\index{Bounded mass property!complete zero-volume model locus}
There is a compact complex sixfold with a smooth positive genuinely nonclosed background $\Theta$ such that $v_+(\Theta)=+\infty$, while its separated unit-divisor model locus has a complete geodesic polar metric of diameter $4$. The ideal subspace is closed and is the metric closure of the analytic models, and ideal projection is the unique nearest-point $1$-Lipschitz retraction. All top-volume comparison expressions on this locus vanish. It contains distinct analytic ideal models at polar distance $1$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Take $Y=C_1\times C_2$ with $C_1=C_2=\mathbb P^1$ and normalized Fubini--Study forms $\omega_i$ of area one, and write $\pi_i:Y\to C_i$ for the projections. Let $(T,\omega_T,V_j)$ be the Hopf threefold, Gauduchon form and smooth potentials of \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}. Take $Z=\mathbb P^1$ with its normalized Fubini--Study form $\kappa$, a point divisor $D$ and its normalized potential $\psi$ satisfying $\kappa+\ddc\psi=[D]$. On $M=Y\times T\times Z$ write $p,s,r$ for the projections. Choose a positive nonconstant smooth function $h$ on $Y$ and put
\[
 \Theta=p^*(\pi_1^*\omega_1+\pi_2^*\omega_2)
          +(h\circ p)s^*\omega_T+r^*\kappa,
 \qquad \Psi=\psi\circ r.
\]
This form is strictly positive. The component $\mathrm dh\wedge\omega_T$ of its derivative, with one $Y$ direction and two $T$ directions, is nonzero and cannot be canceled by the component with three $T$ directions. Thus $\Theta$ is genuinely nonclosed.

Put $c=\min_Yh>0$. The smooth functions $W_j=cV_j\circ s$ are strictly $\Theta$-plurisubharmonic, since their vertical current is
\[
 h\omega_T+c\ddc V_j
    =c(\omega_T+\ddc V_j)+(h-c)\omega_T>0.
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Only the degree-three vertical term contributes to the top wedge. The three curve forms have integral one, so positivity of smooth mixed wedges and Fubini's theorem give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_M(\Theta+\ddc W_j)^6
 &=120\int_Y\pi_1^*\omega_1\wedge\pi_2^*\omega_2
                  \int_T(h(y)\omega_T+c\ddc V_j)^3\\
 &\geq120c^3\int_T(\omega_T+\ddc V_j)^3
       \longrightarrow+\infty.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The factor $120=6!/3!$ comes from choosing one term from each curve direction and three from $T$. Each $W_j$ is bounded, so \cref{def:bmp} proves $v_+(\Theta)=+\infty$ and failure of the bounded mass property.

Apply \cref{prop:general-auxiliary-product-metric} to this product, with both curve areas equal to one. It gives a complete metric $\rho$, diameter $4$, ideal analytic closure, nearest-point retraction and vanishing comparison expression on the locus $\Psi+q_1\circ\pi_1\circ p+q_2\circ\pi_2\circ p$, where each $q_i$ ranges over the supremum-zero ordinary $\omega_i$-models. These conclusions use no ambient mass bound.

For $x\in C_1$, let $g_x$ be the normalized point Green potential with $\omega_1+\ddc g_x=[x]$. The potentials
\[
 \Phi_0=\Psi,\qquad
 \Phi_x=\Psi+g_x\circ\pi_1\circ p
\]
are ordinary and ideal models by \cref{lem:general-auxiliary-product-projections}; their unit divisorial poles give analytic singularities. Their polar tuples are $(0,0)$ and $(\delta_x,0)$, so $\rho(\Phi_0,\Phi_x)=\|\delta_x\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1$. Nevertheless every representative of either ordinary type has zero extended mass, by the same lemma. In particular their upper and lower singularity volumes are all zero.
\end{proof}

The complete polar metric concerns the specified product locus.
