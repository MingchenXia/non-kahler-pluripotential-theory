\section{Varying positive backgrounds}\label{sec:geometric-normalization-stability}

A relative inequality between two Hermitian forms compares their scalars independently of the size of the logarithmic density. This converts geometric convergence of backgrounds into the uniform functional comparison used above.

We now vary a smooth positive real $(1,1)$-form $\beta$ on $X$, with reference zero. Write $B_\beta$, $H_\beta$ and $\mathcal A_\beta$ for its functionals and supporting sets. For two such backgrounds set
\[
 d(B_\beta,B_{\beta'})=\sup_{F\in L^\infty(\mathrm{d}V)}|B_\beta(F)-B_{\beta'}(F)|.
\]
The supremum ranges over all bounded tests, with no common bound on their norms.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:geometric-normalization-comparison}\index{Stability!normalization functional}
Suppose $0<\varepsilon<1$ and $(1-\varepsilon)\beta\leq\beta'\leq(1+\varepsilon)\beta$. For every bounded measurable $F$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometric-normalization-comparison}
 n\log(1-\varepsilon)\leq B_{\beta'}(F)-B_\beta(F)\leq n\log(1+\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
In particular $d(B_\beta,B_{\beta'})\leq-n\log(1-\varepsilon)$. The same conclusion holds if the form comparison is satisfied by positive smooth representatives $\beta+\ddc h$ and $\beta'+\ddc h'$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First let $F$ be smooth, with smooth solutions $v,v'$ in the two backgrounds. At a maximum of $v'-(1+\varepsilon)v$,
\[
 0<\beta'+\ddc v'\leq(1+\varepsilon)(\beta+\ddc v).
\]
Taking determinants and canceling $\mathrm{e}^F\mathrm{d}V$ gives the upper bound. At a minimum of $v'-(1-\varepsilon)v$, the reverse form inequality gives the lower bound. For bounded measurable $F$, choose smooth uniformly bounded approximations converging in $L^1$. Apply \cref{thm:normalization-bounded-lone} separately in the two fixed backgrounds. Finally the correspondence $v\mapsto v-h$ preserves both boundedness and the current, so $B_{\beta+\ddc h}=B_\beta$ whenever both backgrounds are positive. This proves the representative assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:geometric-adjoint-stability}
Let $\beta_j$ satisfy the form comparison with $\beta$ and $\varepsilon_j\to0$. Suppose $F_j\to F$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ with a common $L^\infty$ bound. Then $B_{\beta_j}(F_j)\to B_\beta(F)$. For any $\nu_j\in\mathcal A_{\beta_j}(F_j)$, their densities are uniformly integrable and every subsequence has a further subsequence converging weakly in $L^1$ to an element of $\mathcal A_\beta(F)$. If this set is a singleton, the full sequence converges to it weakly in $L^1$ and as probability measures. This holds in particular when $F$ is smooth.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $\delta_j=-n\log(1-\varepsilon_j)$. The scalar assertion follows from \eqref{eq:geometric-normalization-comparison} and \cref{thm:normalization-bounded-lone} in the fixed background $\beta$. Taking suprema over tests gives the extended-valued inequalities
\[
 H_{\beta_j}\leq H_\beta+\delta_j,\qquad H_\beta\leq H_{\beta_j}+\delta_j.
\]
Their finite domains coincide. The support identity therefore gives
\[
 0\leq H_\beta(\nu_j)-B_\beta(F_j)-\int_XF_j\,\mathrm{d}\nu_j\leq2\delta_j.
\]
If $\|F_j\|_\infty\leq M$, this puts the measures in a fixed sublevel $H_\beta\leq B_\beta(0)+2M+2\sup_j\delta_j$. Apply \cref{thm:normalization-dual-compactness}. For a weak $L^1$ cluster limit, uniform integrability gives $\int|F_j-F|\,\mathrm{d}\nu_j\to0$. Lower semicontinuity in the last display and the measurable dual minimum formula identify the limit as a support at $F$. Compactness gives full convergence when that support is unique; \cref{thm:normalization-gauduchon-recovery} supplies uniqueness at smooth $F$.
\end{proof}

Smooth changes of the background also control the potentials. The inverse of the linearized equation used in \cref{prop:smooth-dependence} remains available when the form itself is included among the parameters.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:smooth-background-potential-stability}
If positive $\beta_j\to\beta$ and $F_j\to F$ in $C^\infty$, the solutions normalized by $\int_Xv_j\,\mathrm{d}V=0$ converge in $C^\infty$ to the similarly normalized solution for $(\beta,F)$. Their scalars converge as well.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $k\geq0$ and $0<\alpha<1$. On the open positivity set, consider the smooth map
\[
 (\eta,G,w,a)\longmapsto\log\frac{(\eta+\ddc w)^n}{\mathrm{d}V}-a-G
\]
from $C^{k,\alpha}$ forms and functions, mean-zero $C^{k+2,\alpha}$ potentials, and $\mathbb R$, into $C^{k,\alpha}$. At the limiting solution its derivative in $(w,a)$ is $(h,q)\mapsto\Delta_\Omega h-q$, where $\Omega=\beta+\ddc v$ and $\Delta_\Omega h=n\ddc h\wedge\Omega^{n-1}/\Omega^n$. For a target $g$, choose $q=-\int g\,\mathrm{d}\mu_v$, with the adjoint probability of \cref{prop:adjoint-gauduchon}. The Fredholm alternative then gives the unique mean-zero $h$ solving $\Delta_\Omega h=g+q$, with a bounded inverse as in \cref{prop:smooth-dependence}. In dimension one this is the Poisson alternative.

The implicit function theorem gives a local branch continuous in these spaces. For smooth parameters, bounded uniqueness from \cref{cor:bounded-positive-density-uniqueness} identifies this branch with the global smooth solution. Thus $v_j\to v$ in $C^{k+2,\alpha}$ for every $k$, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

The smooth adjoint probabilities determine the functional once its additive constant is fixed. If $\nu_{\beta,F}$ denotes the unique support at a smooth test, integration of the derivative in \cref{thm:normalization-gauduchon-recovery} along $sF$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint-functional-reconstruction}
 B_\beta(F)=B_\beta(0)-\int_0^1\!\int_XF\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\beta,sF}\,\mathrm{d}s.
\end{equation}
The implicit function theorem makes this derivative continuous along the path. Consequently two positive backgrounds on the same $X,\mathrm{d}V$ have identical bounded-test functionals if and only if their values at zero and all their smooth-test adjoint probabilities agree. One direction follows by differentiation; the other follows from \eqref{eq:adjoint-functional-reconstruction} for smooth tests and bounded $L^1$ approximation for all remaining tests.
