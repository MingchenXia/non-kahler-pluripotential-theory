\section{Intrinsic dimension, lattice and growth}

The group generated by the semigroup records both the available degrees and the horizontal lattice. These determine the correct growth exponent and volume normalization when the body has smaller dimension than the ambient space.

Assume in this section that some positive-degree piece of $W_\bullet$ is nonzero. Let $G\subset\mathbb Z^{n+1}$ be the subgroup generated by $S$, and let $\operatorname{pr}_{n+1}$ be projection to the last coordinate. Define $e\geq1$ by
\[
 \operatorname{pr}_{n+1}(G)=e\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus $e$ is the greatest common divisor of the degrees with nonzero sections. Set
\[
 \Lambda=G\cap(\mathbb R^n\times\{0\}),
 \qquad d=\operatorname{rank}\Lambda.
\]
We identify $\Lambda$ with a lattice in its real horizontal span. Its lattice measure assigns volume one to a fundamental parallelepiped. On a translate of this span use the translated measure, denoted by $\operatorname{vol}_\Lambda$; in dimension zero a point has volume one.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intrinsic-flag-count}
The affine dimension of $\Delta(W_\bullet)$ is $d$. Its lattice volume is finite and strictly positive, and
\[
 \lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\dim W_{eq}}{(eq)^d}
 =\operatorname{vol}_\Lambda\Delta(W_\bullet).
\]
If $e$ does not divide $m$, then $W_m=0$. In particular, all sufficiently large multiples of $e$ have nonzero sections.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $g_0=(b,e)\in G$ and an integral basis $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d$ of $\Lambda$. Every element of $G$ has a unique expression
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^d a_i\lambda_i+qg_0,
 \qquad (a,q)\in\mathbb Z^d\times\mathbb Z.
\]
These coordinates identify $G$ with $\mathbb Z^{d+1}$. Let $S'$ be the image of $S$. It generates that entire lattice, and its height is $q=m/e\geq0$.

The real span of $S$ is the real span of $G$. Since all nonzero elements of $S$ have positive height, the affine span of their height-one normalizations has dimension $\operatorname{rank}G-1=d$. To check this directly, fix one such normalization $z_0$. The vectors $z-z_0$ span the horizontal kernel of the height map on $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}S$: every linear relation with total height zero becomes a real linear combination of these differences after dividing its generators by their heights. Taking the convex hull and closure does not change the affine span. This proves the dimension statement.

The linear change of coordinates carries the cone bound already proved for $S$ to a closed cone with compact height-one slice and with only the origin at height zero. Indeed a vector $(x,m)$ with $|x|\leq C m$ has horizontal coordinates bounded by a constant times $m$, and hence by a constant times $q$. The height-one body for $S'$ is
\[
 \Delta'=e\bigl(\Delta(W_\bullet)-b/e\bigr),
\]
where the right side is expressed in the basis $\lambda_i$. It is full-dimensional in $\mathbb R^d$. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb R^d}\Delta'
 =e^d\operatorname{vol}_\Lambda\Delta(W_\bullet)>0.
\]
For $d\geq1$, apply the semigroup growth theorem cited in \cref{sec:graded-flag-body} to $S'$ with its full lattice. Its degree-$q$ slice is in bijection with $S_{eq}$, so
\[
 \lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\dim W_{eq}}{q^d}
 =\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb R^d}\Delta'.
\]
Divide by $e^d$ to obtain the assertion. Positive limiting volume gives eventual nonvanishing.

For $d=0$, each degree contains at most one valuation vector and hence at most one section dimension. The positive degrees form an additive subsemigroup of $e\mathbb N$ with greatest common divisor $e$. After division by $e$, choose finitely many of its elements with greatest common divisor one. Fix one of them, $a>0$. The nonnegative combinations of the chosen elements realize every residue modulo $a$: their residues form a submonoid of the finite group $\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z$, hence a subgroup, and their greatest common divisor is one. Choose a realized nonnegative integer $N_r$ in each residue. Every integer at least $\max_r N_r$ is some $N_r+ta$, $t\geq0$, and therefore occurs as a degree. Thus $\dim W_{eq}=1$ for all large $q$, which is exactly the zero-dimensional formula.

Finally every nonzero degree lies in the projection $e\mathbb Z$ of $G$, proving vanishing outside these multiples.
\end{proof}

The scaling factor $e^d$ in the transformed body cancels against the denominator $(eq)^d$. The lattice measure accounts for the horizontal index.

The growth dimension also has a description independent of the flag. Write $\mathcal M(X)$ for the field of meromorphic functions on $X$ and define the section-ratio field
\[
 K(W_\bullet)=\{s/t:s,t\in W_m\text{ for some }m\geq0,
                                  \ t\neq0\}\subset\mathcal M(X).
\]
It is a field: addition and multiplication use the common denominator $t_1t_2$ in degree $m_1+m_2$, and inversion exchanges numerator and denominator. It contains $\mathbb C$ because $W_0=\mathbb C$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:flag-dimension-section-field}
The transcendence degree of $K(W_\bullet)$ over $\mathbb C$ is exactly $d$. In particular $d$, the period $e$ and the intrinsic volume in \cref{thm:intrinsic-flag-count} are independent of the chosen complete compact flag whenever two such flags are available.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $d+1$ affinely independent normalized valuation vectors of actual sections. After raising them to a common degree, their ratios against the first section have $d$ linearly independent valuation differences. As in the proof of \cref{prop:full-flag-body-meromorphic}, distinct monomials in these ratios have distinct valuations and cannot cancel in a polynomial relation. Hence the field has transcendence degree at least $d$; for $d=0$ this assertion is immediate.

Conversely let $f_1,\ldots,f_r$, with $r\geq1$, be algebraically independent members of the field. Taking the product of their denominators, write $f_i=A_i/B$ with $A_i,B\in W_M$ and $B\neq0$ for a common positive degree $M$. For every integer $q\geq1$ and multi-index $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in\mathbb N^r$ with $|a|=a_1+\cdots+a_r\leq q$, the sections
\[
 B^{q-|a|}A_1^{a_1}\cdots A_r^{a_r}\in W_{Mq}
\]
are linearly independent: division by $B^q$ turns any linear relation into a polynomial relation in the $f_i$. There are $\binom{q+r}{r}$ such sections. The integer $M$ is a multiple of $e$, so \cref{thm:intrinsic-flag-count} implies $\dim W_{Mq}=O(q^d)$. The binomial lower bound then forces $r\leq d$. This proves equality of transcendence degrees.

The field and the set of nonzero degrees do not depend on the flag, so neither $d$ nor $e$ does. The lattice volume is the limit of the same dimensions with the same denominator for either flag, by \cref{thm:intrinsic-flag-count}. It is therefore independent as well. The bodies and their ambient lattices may still differ.
\end{proof}
