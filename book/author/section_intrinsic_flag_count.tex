\section{Intrinsic dimension, lattice and growth}

The group generated by the valuation semigroup determines the degree period and the lattice in which its body has full dimension. Expressing the semigroup in this lattice gives the growth exponent and coefficient. Ratios of sections identify the exponent with a transcendence degree, making both quantities independent of the flag.

Assume in this section that some positive-degree piece of $W_\bullet$ is nonzero. Let $G\subset\mathbb Z^{n+1}$ be the subgroup generated by $S$, and let $\operatorname{pr}_{n+1}$ be projection to the last coordinate. Define $e\geq1$ by
\[
 \operatorname{pr}_{n+1}(G)=e\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus $e$ is the greatest common divisor of the degrees with nonzero sections. Set
\[
 \Lambda=G\cap(\mathbb R^n\times\{0\}),
 \qquad d=\operatorname{rank}\Lambda.
\]
We identify $\Lambda$ with a lattice in its real horizontal span. Its lattice measure assigns volume one to a fundamental parallelepiped. On a translate of this span use the translated measure, denoted by $\operatorname{vol}_\Lambda$; in dimension zero a point has volume one.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intrinsic-flag-count}
The affine dimension of $\Delta(W_\bullet)$ is $d$. Its lattice volume is finite and strictly positive, and
\[
 \lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\dim W_{eq}}{(eq)^d}
 =\operatorname{vol}_\Lambda\Delta(W_\bullet).
\]
If $e$ does not divide $m$, then $W_m=0$. In particular, all sufficiently large multiples of $e$ have nonzero sections.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $g_0=(b,e)\in G$ and an integral basis $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d$ of $\Lambda$. Every element of $G$ has a unique expression
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^d a_i\lambda_i+qg_0,
 \qquad (a,q)\in\mathbb Z^d\times\mathbb Z.
\]
These coordinates identify $G$ with $\mathbb Z^{d+1}$. Let $S'$ be the image of $S$. It generates that entire lattice, and its height is $q=m/e\geq0$.

The points $(m^{-1}x,1)$, for $(x,m)\in S$ with $m>0$, linearly span the $(d+1)$-dimensional space $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}G$. Since they all have height one, their affine span has dimension $d$. Taking the convex hull and closure does not change this affine span, proving the dimension assertion.

The linear change of coordinates carries the cone bound already proved for $S$ to a closed cone with compact height-one slice and with only the origin at height zero. Indeed a vector $(x,m)$ with $|x|\leq C m$ has horizontal coordinates bounded by a constant times $m$, and hence by a constant times $q$. The height-one body for $S'$ is
\[
 \Delta'=e\bigl(\Delta(W_\bullet)-b/e\bigr),
\]
where the right side is expressed in the basis $\lambda_i$. It is full-dimensional in $\mathbb R^d$. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb R^d}\Delta'
 =e^d\operatorname{vol}_\Lambda\Delta(W_\bullet)>0.
\]
Apply the semigroup growth theorem cited in \cref{sec:graded-flag-body} to $S'$ with its full lattice. Its degree-$q$ slice is in bijection with $S_{eq}$, so
\[
 \lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\dim W_{eq}}{q^d}
 =\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb R^d}\Delta'.
\]
Dividing by $e^d$ gives the asserted limit. Its positivity gives eventual nonvanishing. For $d=0$ the body is a point of volume one, so the integer dimensions converge to one and are eventually equal to one. Every nonzero degree lies in the projection $e\mathbb Z$ of $G$, proving vanishing outside these multiples.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
The growth dimension also has a description independent of the flag. Write $\mathcal M(X)$ for the field of meromorphic functions on $X$ and define the section-ratio field
\[
 K(W_\bullet)=\{s/t:s,t\in W_m\text{ for some }m\geq0,
                                  \ t\neq0\}\subset\mathcal M(X).
\]
It is a field: addition and multiplication use the common denominator $t_1t_2$ in degree $m_1+m_2$, and inversion exchanges numerator and denominator. It contains $\mathbb C$ because $W_0=\mathbb C$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:flag-dimension-section-field}\label{prop:full-flag-body-meromorphic}
The transcendence degree of $K(W_\bullet)$ over $\mathbb C$ is exactly $d$. If $\Delta(W_\bullet)$ has nonempty interior in $\mathbb R^n$, then $X$ has $n$ algebraically independent meromorphic functions. Moreover $d$, the period $e$ and the intrinsic volume in \cref{thm:intrinsic-flag-count} are independent of the chosen complete compact flag whenever two such flags are available.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $d>0$, choose sections $s_0,\ldots,s_d$ of positive degrees $m_0,\ldots,m_d$ with affinely independent normalized valuation vectors. Let $M$ be a common multiple of their degrees and put $t_i=s_i^{M/m_i}\in W_M$. The ratios $f_i=t_i/t_0$ belong to $K(W_\bullet)$. The flag valuation extends to quotients by subtraction, giving
\[
 \nu(f_i)=M\left(\frac{\nu(s_i)}{m_i}
                  -\frac{\nu(s_0)}{m_0}\right),
 \qquad 1\leq i\leq d.
\]
These vectors are linearly independent. Distinct monomials in the $f_i$ therefore have distinct valuations, so the least term in a nonzero polynomial cannot cancel. The ratios are algebraically independent. Thus the transcendence degree is at least $d$, including the immediate case $d=0$.

Conversely let $f_1,\ldots,f_r$, with $r\geq1$, be algebraically independent members of the field. Taking the product of their denominators, write $f_i=A_i/B$ with $A_i,B\in W_M$ and $B\neq0$ for a common positive degree $M$. For every integer $q\geq1$ and multi-index $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in\mathbb N^r$ with $|a|=a_1+\cdots+a_r\leq q$, the sections
\[
 B^{q-|a|}A_1^{a_1}\cdots A_r^{a_r}\in W_{Mq}
\]
are linearly independent: division by $B^q$ turns any linear relation into a polynomial relation in the $f_i$. There are $\binom{q+r}{r}$ such sections. The integer $M$ is a multiple of $e$, so \cref{thm:intrinsic-flag-count} implies $\dim W_{Mq}=O(q^d)$. The binomial lower bound then forces $r\leq d$. This proves equality of transcendence degrees. A body with nonempty interior has $d=n$, giving the stated consequence for $X$.

The field and the set of nonzero degrees do not depend on the flag, so neither $d$ nor $e$ does. The lattice volume is the limit of the same dimensions with the same denominator for either flag, by \cref{thm:intrinsic-flag-count}. It is therefore independent as well. The bodies and their ambient lattices may still differ.
\end{proof}
