\subsection{A square-root threshold}
\label{uni-sec:threshold}

A nonnegative $C^{1,1}$ density grows at most quadratically away from each of its zeros. If its zero set separates a small ball, a family of parallel segments crossing the zero set therefore contributes at least a constant times $\sqrt t$ to the volume of $\{f\leq t\}$ for small $t>0$. Excluding this lower bound makes $\{f>0\}$ connected, and \cref{uni-thm:anchors} then gives uniqueness.

Write $\operatorname{Vol}(E)=\int_E\mathrm{d}V$ for Borel sets $E$.

\begin{theorem}\label{uni-thm:threshold}\index{Uniqueness!square-root threshold}
Let $u,v\in C^2(X)$ satisfy $A,B\geq0$ and
$A^n=B^n=f\,\mathrm{d}V$, where $f\geq0$ is $C^{1,1}$.  If
\begin{equation}
  \liminf_{t\downarrow0}
  \frac{\operatorname{Vol}\{f\leq t\}}{\sqrt t}=0,
  \label{uni-eq:sqrtcriterion}
\end{equation}
then $\{f>0\}$ is connected and $u-v$ is constant.  In particular this
holds when $f^{-1/2}\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, with $f^{-1/2}=+\infty$ on $\{f=0\}$.
The exponent $1/2$ is sharp in every dimension $n\geq3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $Z=\{f=0\}$.  Condition \eqref{uni-eq:sqrtcriterion} first implies
$\operatorname{Vol}(Z)=0$.  We show that the complement of $Z$ in every sufficiently
small coordinate ball is connected.  Fix such a ball with convex Euclidean
image.  If the complement of $Z$ in it were disconnected, choose points $a,b$ in
different components and small closed disks centered at $a$ and $b$, perpendicular to the
segment $[a,b]$ and contained in those respective components.  By making
the disks small, all parallel segments between corresponding points stay
in the ball.  Each segment meets $Z$, since otherwise it would connect
the two components.

On a fixed coordinate region containing the closed ball, the gradient of $f$ is
Lipschitz with some constant $K>0$.  Since $f\geq0$, we have
$\nabla f(z)=0$ at every zero $z$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{uni-eq:quadratic-upper}
 f(x)\leq\frac K2|x-z|^2
\end{equation}
whenever the segment from $z$ to $x$ stays in that region.  The endpoint
disks are compact and disjoint from $Z$, so the parts of the segments near them
contain no zeros.  Thus every parallel segment contains a zero
at a fixed positive distance from its endpoints.  For small $t>0$,
\eqref{uni-eq:quadratic-upper} gives on that segment an interval of length
$2\sqrt{2t/K}$ contained in $\{f\leq t\}$.  Integrating over the
transverse disk, which has positive measure, and using that $\mathrm{d}V$ is comparable to
coordinate volume, we obtain
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}\{f\leq t\}\geq c\sqrt t
\]
for all sufficiently small $t$, contradicting
\eqref{uni-eq:sqrtcriterion}.  This proves the local connectedness assertion.

For each such ball, the complement of $Z$ in it is nonempty, since $Z$ has zero
volume, and it lies in one component of $U=X\setminus Z$.
Two overlapping balls give the same component, because their open overlap
contains a point outside $Z$.  Since $X$ is connected, all these balls give the same component.
Hence $U$ is connected,
and \cref{uni-thm:anchors} shows that $u-v$ is constant.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
If $f^{-1/2}\in L^1$, then, outside the null zero set,
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Vol}\{f\leq t\}}{\sqrt t}
 \leq\int_{\{f\leq t\}}f^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}V\longrightarrow0
\]
by dominated convergence.  This proves the sufficient integrability
condition.  The sharpness assertion follows from the examples in
\cref{uni-sec:examples}, as quantified at the end of that section.
\end{proof}
