\subsection{A semipositive residual with nonzero defect}
\label{sec:two-point-fiber-integrability}

Blowing up two points instead of one, we obtain a residual that is semipositive, cannot be made strictly positive, and still has positive top integral. Unlike the example with one blown-up point, the gap between the two divisorial thresholds now produces a nonzero defect of the largest possible order $m^{q-1}$. Counting the spaces requires the following elementary interpolation of jets at several points.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finite-point-jet-interpolation}
Let $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ be distinct points of $\mathbb P^q$, $q\geq1$, and let $d,j_1,\ldots,j_N$ be nonnegative integers with $\sum_i j_i\leq d+1$. Then the map from $H^0(\mathbb P^q,\mathcal O(d))$ to the jets of order less than $j_i$ at the points $x_i$ is surjective. In particular,
\[
 h^0\left(\mathbb P^q,\mathcal O(d)\otimes\bigcap_i\mathfrak m_{x_i}^{j_i}\right)
 =\binom{d+q}{q}-\sum_i\binom{j_i+q-1}{q},
\]
where $\binom{q-1}{q}=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We may discard the points with $j_i=0$; if no point remains, the target is zero. Choose an affine chart containing all the remaining points, and use the corresponding trivialization of $\mathcal O(d)$. For each pair $i\ne k$ choose an affine linear function $L_{ik}$ vanishing at $x_k$ and nonzero at $x_i$, and set
\[
 Q_i=\prod_{k\ne i}L_{ik}^{j_k}.
\]
Given a jet $b_i$ at $x_i$, divide it by the invertible jet of $Q_i$ at $x_i$, and let $T_i$ be the Taylor polynomial of degree at most $j_i-1$ of the quotient. Then $Q_iT_i$ has the jet $b_i$ at $x_i$ and vanishes to order at least $j_k$ at every other point $x_k$, and its degree is at most $\sum_kj_k-1\leq d$. Hence $\sum_iQ_iT_i$, homogenized to degree $d$, realizes all the prescribed jets. In $q$ variables there are $\binom{j_i+q-1}{q}$ monomials of total degree less than $j_i$, which gives the dimension formula.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:two-point-semipositive-fiber-series}\index{Holomorphic section!two-point boundary defect}
Let $q\geq2$ and let $\rho:B\to\mathbb P^q$ be the blowup of the two points
$x_1=[1:0:\cdots:0]$ and $x_2=[0:1:0:\cdots:0]$, with exceptional divisors $E_1,E_2$. Let $\tau_i:B\to\mathbb P^{q-1}$ be the extension to $B$ of the projection from $x_i$. With the normalized Fubini--Study forms, put
\[
 \alpha=\rho^*\omega_{\mathbb P^q},\qquad
 \gamma_i=\tau_i^*\omega_{\mathbb P^{q-1}},\qquad
 \kappa=\alpha+\gamma_1+\gamma_2>0.
\]
Equip $\mathcal O_B(E_i)=\rho^*\mathcal O(1)\otimes\tau_i^*\mathcal O(-1)$ with the quotient metric, whose curvature is $\eta_i=\alpha-\gamma_i$, and let $s_i$ be its canonical section. Equip
\[
 A=\rho^*\mathcal O(2)\otimes\mathcal O_B(E_1+E_2),
 \qquad \theta=2\alpha+\eta_1+\eta_2
\]
with the product metric. On $X=B\times\mathbb P^1$, with projection $\pi$ and a point $o\in\mathbb P^1$, set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 R_i=\|\pi^*s_i\|^2,\qquad T=\|s_o\|^2,\\
 \varphi=\log(R_1^2+T^3)+\log(R_2^2+T^3),\\
 \omega=4\pi^*\kappa+7\omega_{\mathbb P^1},\\
 \psi=2\log\|s_1\|^2+2\log\|s_2\|^2,
 \qquad \beta=\gamma_1+\gamma_2.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Then $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, $\psi\in\PSH(B,\theta)$, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(\psi)=\beta^q,\qquad
 \int_B\beta^q=2^q-2>0.
\]
No smooth change of gauge makes $\beta$ strictly positive. Nevertheless, writing $V_m$ and $W_m$ for the selected and reference spaces as before, their images in $H^0(B,A^m)$ are, for every $m\geq1$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_m&=s_1^ms_2^m\rho^*H^0\left(\mathbb P^q,\mathcal O(2m)
                 \otimes\mathfrak m_{x_1}^{m-1}\mathfrak m_{x_2}^{m-1}\right),\\
 W_m&=s_1^ms_2^m\rho^*H^0\left(\mathbb P^q,\mathcal O(2m)
                 \otimes\mathfrak m_{x_1}^{m}\mathfrak m_{x_2}^{m}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The defect and the common leading term of the dimensions are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \dim V_m-\dim W_m=2\binom{m+q-2}{q-1}
       \sim\frac{2}{(q-1)!}m^{q-1},\\
 \dim V_m=\frac{2^q-2}{q!}m^q+O(m^{q-1}),\qquad
 \dim W_m=\frac{2^q-2}{q!}m^q+O(m^{q-1}).
 \end{gathered}
\]
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The map $(\rho,\tau_1,\tau_2)$ is an embedding: away from the exceptional divisors $\rho$ is already an embedding, and along $E_i$ the map $\tau_i$ separates the tangent directions of $E_i$ while $\rho$ detects the normal direction. Hence $\kappa>0$. The identity for the quotient metric is the one-point identity of \cref{ex:exceptional-semipositive-fiber-series}, applied at each center. For each $i$, the calculation over a common denominator writes $R_i^2+T^3$ as a sum of squared moduli of holomorphic coefficients over the metric denominator for $\mathcal O(2,3)$. Together with the extension theorem across the pole set, this gives
\[
 \ddc\varphi\geq-4\pi^*\alpha-6\omega_{\mathbb P^1}.
\]
It follows that $\omega+\ddc\varphi\geq4\pi^*\beta+\omega_{\mathbb P^1}\geq0$.

By the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula,
\[
 \theta+\ddc\psi=\beta+2[E_1]+2[E_2]\geq0.
\]
The non-pluripolar product does not charge the divisors and equals $\beta^q$. The class of $\beta$ is $2H-[E_1]-[E_2]$, where $H=\rho^*c_1(\mathcal O(1))$. As in \cref{ex:modified-point-fiber-series}, $H^q=1$, the mixed intersection numbers involving $H$ and some $[E_i]$ vanish, and $[E_i]^q=(-1)^{q-1}$; moreover, the two exceptional divisors are disjoint. Expanding gives $\int_B\beta^q=2^q-2$.

Let $C$ be the strict transform of the line through $x_1$ and $x_2$. Both maps $\tau_i$ are constant on $C$, so $\int_C\beta=0$. By Stokes' theorem, $\int_C(\beta+\ddc u)=0$ for every smooth real function $u$, whereas a strictly positive form has positive integral over $C$. This proves the obstruction, although the top integral is positive.

The selecting potential satisfies $\varphi\geq\pi^*\psi$. Its pole set is the disjoint union of the sets $E_i\times\{o\}$. Near either of them the other summand is smooth, and
\[
 \varphi=\log(|z|^4+|w|^6)+O(1),\qquad
 E_i=\{z=0\},\quad o=\{w=0\}.
\]
Thus $c_i=p_i=2$, $b_{i1}=3$ and $a_i=2/3$. By \cref{thm:crossing-fiber-integrability-band,cor:semipositive-fiber-band}, $W_m=H^0(B,A^m(-2m(E_1+E_2)))$, and $V_m$ is contained in $H^0(B,A^m(-(2m-1)(E_1+E_2)))$; for an integer $\ell$, the strict local integrability condition $\ell+1>2m-2/3$ means $\ell\geq2m-1$. Conversely, by \cref{lem:anisotropic-fiber-integrability}, divisibility by these orders gives integrability in every chart of the pole set, and off the pole set the selecting potential is locally bounded. This proves equality for $V_m$.

Dividing a section of $A^m$ by $s_1^ms_2^m$ gives a section of $\mathcal O(2m)$ away from the two centers, which extends across them by Hartogs' theorem since $q\geq2$. The pullback of the extension, multiplied by $s_1^ms_2^m$, agrees with the original section everywhere, by the identity theorem. If the extended section vanishes to order $j_i$ at $x_i$, the corresponding section of $A^m$ vanishes to order $m+j_i$ generically along $E_i$. The two divisorial conditions therefore give exactly the vanishing orders at $x_1$ and $x_2$ displayed in the statement. Since the two point ideals have disjoint supports, their product equals their intersection.

Apply \cref{lem:finite-point-jet-interpolation} with $d=2m$ and orders $(m,m)$ or $(m-1,m-1)$; in both cases their sum is at most $d+1$. This gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \dim W_m&=\binom{2m+q}{q}-2\binom{m+q-1}{q},\\
 \dim V_m&=\binom{2m+q}{q}-2\binom{m+q-2}{q}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Pascal's identity gives the exact defect, and the leading terms give both growth laws. For $m=1$ the vanishing orders for $V_1$ are zero, and the defect is already two. For $q=2$ the dimensions are $\dim W_m=(m+1)^2$ and $\dim V_m=m^2+4m+1$.
\end{proof}

In this example the residual $\beta$ has zero integral over the curve $C$, so no smooth change of gauge makes it positive, and yet its top integral is positive. Thus the bound $O(m^{q-1})$ of \cref{cor:semipositive-fiber-band} cannot be improved to $o(m^{q-1})$ for semipositive residuals either. As in the previous examples, the selecting potential is finite on most of each fiber over $E_i$ and equals $-\infty$ at the point $o$ of that fiber, so it does not descend, even up to a bounded function.
