The decreasing continuity of \cref{thm:bt-input} persists after the products are multiplied by a bounded potential.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weighted-decreasing-products}\index{Bedford--Taylor product!weighted decreasing limits}
Let $a_j\downarrow a$ and $b_{i,j}\downarrow b_i$, $1\leq i\leq r\leq n$, be locally uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a domain in $\C^n$. Then
\[
 a_j\,\ddc b_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc b_{r,j}
 \longrightarrow a\,\ddc b_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc b_r
\]
weakly as currents. The same holds for quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that become plurisubharmonic after fixed smooth corrections, and after wedging with smooth forms that converge locally in $C^\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For plurisubharmonic functions this is the decreasing convergence theorem \cite[Theorem~3.18]{GZ17} with $T=1$; there $\ddc$ is twice ours, which multiplies both sides by $2^r$. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, add the fixed smooth corrections to the decreasing sequences, apply the plurisubharmonic case to the corrected functions, and expand; the terms involving the correction to $a_j$ converge by the decreasing continuity in \cref{thm:bt-input}. Finally, the local Chern--Levine--Nirenberg bounds show that these finitely many products have uniformly bounded variation on compact sets, so smooth forms converging locally in $C^\infty$ may replace fixed forms in the tests.
\end{proof}
