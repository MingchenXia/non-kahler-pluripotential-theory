Products multiplied by a bounded potential need more than the weak convergence of the unweighted products in \cref{thm:bt-input}. An auxiliary complex variable recovers the weight from an ordinary mixed product in one more dimension, including at top degree.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weighted-decreasing-products}\index{Bedford--Taylor product!weighted decreasing limits}
Let $a_j\downarrow a$ and $b_{i,j}\downarrow b_i$, $1\leq i\leq r\leq n$, be locally uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a domain in $\C^n$. Then
\[
 a_j\,\ddc b_{1,j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc b_{r,j}
 \longrightarrow a\,\ddc b_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc b_r
\]
weakly as currents. The same holds for quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that become plurisubharmonic after fixed smooth corrections, and after wedging with smooth forms that converge locally in $C^\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First let $r=n$, and write $\mu_j=\bigwedge_i\ddc b_{i,j}$ and $\mu=\bigwedge_i\ddc b_i$. Work over a relatively compact ball on which $-M\leq a_j,a\leq M$. On the product of this ball with a disk of radius $R$ in an auxiliary variable $w$, where $\log R^2>M+1$, set
\[
 v_j(z,w)=\max\{a_j(z),\log|w|^2\},\qquad
 v(z,w)=\max\{a(z),\log|w|^2\}.
\]
These are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions, also at $w=0$, and $v_j\downarrow v$. Pulling back the functions $b_{i,j}$ and $b_i$ to the product and applying \cref{thm:bt-input} in dimension $n+1$, we obtain
\[
 T_j=\mu_j\wedge\ddc v_j\longrightarrow T=\mu\wedge\ddc v.
\]
Since $\mu$ is closed, $T=\ddc(v\mu)$. As $\mu$ has top degree in the base variables, only derivatives in $w$ contribute. Testing against product test functions and applying Fubini's theorem therefore identifies $T$ with $\mu(\mathrm dz)$ times the circle measure
\[
 \ddc_w\max\{a(z),\log|w|^2\}
   =\sigma_{\exp(a(z)/2)}.
\]
Here $\sigma_t$ is the uniform probability measure on the circle $|w|=t$: the radial derivative jumps from zero to $2/t$ there, so the Euclidean Laplacian has total mass $4\pi$, and our normalization of $\ddc$ divides that mass by $4\pi$. This distributional computation is valid for every bounded Borel function $a$; no continuity in $z$ is needed. The same description holds for $T_j$.

Choose a smooth radial compactly supported function $\vartheta(w)$, supported away from zero and from the circle $|w|=R$, that equals $\log|w|^2$ on all circles with $-M\leq\log|w|^2\leq M$. For every smooth compactly supported test function $\xi$ on the base,
\[
 \langle T_j,\xi\vartheta\rangle=\int\xi a_j\,\mathrm d\mu_j,
 \qquad
 \langle T,\xi\vartheta\rangle=\int\xi a\,\mathrm d\mu.
\]
The convergence $T_j\to T$ therefore proves the top-degree assertion.

For $r<n$, wedge with $n-r$ constant positive $(1,1)$-forms, each the Hessian of a positive quadratic function, and apply the top-degree case. By polarization, the products of positive constant $(1,1)$-forms span the real constant forms of bidegree $(n-r,n-r)$, and multiplying them by smooth test functions recovers every coefficient of the currents. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, add the fixed smooth corrections to the decreasing sequences, apply the result to the corrected functions, and expand; the term involving the correction to $a_j$ converges by the ordinary decreasing continuity of the products. Finally, the local Chern--Levine--Nirenberg bounds show that these finitely many products have uniformly bounded variation on compact sets. This allows smooth forms converging locally in $C^\infty$ to replace fixed forms in the tests.
\end{proof}
