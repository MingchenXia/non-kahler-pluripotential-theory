\section{Isolated logarithmic poles on a fibration}
\label{sec:fiber-point-kernels}

In sufficiently large tensor powers, an isolated pole on a fiber forces every selected section to vanish at its image on the base. Integrability uses the ambient dimension, so its threshold differs from the base point-pole threshold by at most the fiber dimension. The intervening jets have dimension $O(m^{q-1})$ on a $q$-dimensional base; after normalization by $m^q$, they leave the equilibrium limit unchanged.

For nested section spaces, the normalized codimension is exactly the total variation of their kernel-measure difference.

\Needspace{19\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kernel-codimension-comparison}\index{Bergman kernel!codimension estimate}
Let $B$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $q\geq1$. Let $A\to B$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth Hermitian metric. For every integer $m\geq1$, let $W_m\subseteq V_m\subseteq H^0(B,A^m)$ have the continuous weighted norms determined by the same smooth metric, weight $v\in C(B)$ and smooth positive probability volume $\nu$. Write $d_m=\dim V_m-\dim W_m$. Use the kernels $B_{V_m,v},B_{W_m,v}$ and measures $\beta_{V_m,v}^{(q)},\beta_{W_m,v}^{(q)}$ of \cref{sec:partial-selected-space}, with the selected space indicated by the subscript. Let $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ denote total variation. Then
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 B_{V_m,v}-B_{W_m,v}\geq0,\\
 \|\beta_{V_m,v}^{(q)}-\beta_{W_m,v}^{(q)}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       =\frac{q!d_m}{m^q}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Their determinant functionals, each normalized to vanish at the zero weight, satisfy
\[
 |\mathcal F_{V_m}^{(q)}(v)-\mathcal F_{W_m}^{(q)}(v)|
       \leq\frac{q!d_m}{m^q}\|v\|_\infty.
\]
These statements include zero-dimensional spaces.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Extend an orthonormal basis of $W_m$ to one of $V_m$ for the norm at $v$. The kernel difference is the sum of the squared weighted evaluations of the added basis vectors. It is nonnegative, and its integral is their number $d_m$. This gives the total-variation formula. Apply the first-variation proof of \cref{prop:partial-kernel-variation} to each of the two fixed subspaces and integrate their difference along $tv$, $0\leq t\leq1$. At each $t$, the just-proved positive kernel difference has total mass $q!d_m/m^q$. Its integral against $v$ is therefore bounded in absolute value by that mass times $\|v\|_\infty$. The normalization at zero proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Return to a submersion $\pi:X\to B$ and a pullback line bundle $L=\pi^*A$ as in \cref{sec:partial-submersion}, and put $f=n-q$. Fix distinct points $p_1,\ldots,p_N$ on $X$ and positive rational numbers $a_1,\ldots,a_N$. Suppose $\varphi\in\QPSH(X)$ is locally bounded away from these points and, in local coordinates $x$ centered at $p_j$, has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isolated-selecting-poles}
 \varphi(x)=a_j\log|x|^2+O(1).
\end{equation}
Write
\[
 Z=\{\pi(p_1),\ldots,\pi(p_N)\},\qquad
 a_b=\max_{\pi(p_j)=b}a_j\quad(b\in Z).
\]
For an integer $m\geq1$, set
\[
 r_{m,b}=\max\{\lfloor ma_b\rfloor-n+1,0\},\qquad
 s_{m,b}=\max\{\lfloor ma_b\rfloor-q+1,0\}.
\]
Here $\mathfrak m_b$ denotes the ideal sheaf of the point $b$, with zeroth power $\mathcal O_B$.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:isolated-pole-section-descent}
Pullback identifies the selected space on $X$ with
\[
 V_m=H^0\left(B,A^m\otimes
                       \bigcap_{b\in Z}\mathfrak m_b^{r_{m,b}}\right).
\]
If
\[
 W_m=H^0\left(B,A^m\otimes
                       \bigcap_{b\in Z}\mathfrak m_b^{s_{m,b}}\right),
\]
then $W_m\subseteq V_m$ and
\[
 0\leq\dim V_m-\dim W_m
 \leq\sum_{b\in Z}\sum_{j=r_{m,b}}^{s_{m,b}-1}
                         \binom{j+q-1}{q-1}
 =O(m^{q-1}).
\]
An empty inner sum is zero. The constants depend on the fixed point data, not on $m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}, with a point as center, gives $\mathcal I(m\varphi)_{p_j}=\mathfrak m_{p_j}^{\max\{\lfloor ma_j\rfloor-n+1,0\}}$. Away from the specified points the ideal is the full structure sheaf.

Every section of $L^m$ descends to $B$, by the compact-fiber argument in \cref{prop:partial-kernel-submersion}. Near $p_j$, choose submersion coordinates $(z,\tau)$ with $\pi(z,\tau)=z$. A pulled-back section has a local coefficient $g(z)$ independent of $\tau$. Its order at $(0,0)$ equals its order at $z=0$. The conditions from points in the same fiber are thus the same kind of point-vanishing condition, and their maximum is $r_{m,b}$. This proves the exact space identification.

For the dimension bound, send a section of $V_m$ to its germs modulo $\mathfrak m_b^{s_{m,b}}$ at the finitely many points $b$. The kernel is $W_m$. Local trivializations of $A^m$ therefore give an injection
\[
 V_m/W_m\hookrightarrow
       \bigoplus_{b\in Z}\mathfrak m_b^{r_{m,b}}/
                                      \mathfrak m_b^{s_{m,b}}.
\]
No surjectivity of a jet-evaluation map is required. In dimension $q$, the degree-$j$ homogeneous monomials number $\binom{j+q-1}{q-1}$. They compute the displayed quotient dimensions. Finally $0\leq s_{m,b}-r_{m,b}\leq f$ and $s_{m,b}=O(m)$, proving the bound, also when $q=1$ or $f=0$.
\end{proof}

Equip $A$ with a smooth metric $h_A$ and put $\theta=c_1(A,h_A)$. Choose a smooth positive probability volume $\nu_B$, smooth fiber probabilities as in the preceding sections, and their smooth positive lift $\nu_X=\mathcal L\nu_B$. Use the pullback metric on $L$. For $v\in C(B)$, use $v\circ\pi$ in the continuous norm on $X$.

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:isolated-pole-kernel-limit}
Assume $B$ is K\"ahler and there is $\psi\in\PSH(B,\theta)$ of positive non-pluripolar mass, locally bounded off $Z$, with
\[
 \psi(z)=a_b\log|z-b|^2+O(1)\quad\hbox{near each }b\in Z.
\]
Let $E(v)=E_{\theta,B}[\psi]_{\mathcal I}(v)$. For the selecting weight $\varphi$ in \eqref{eq:isolated-selecting-poles},
\[
 \beta_{X,m,v\circ\pi,\varphi}^{(q)}
      \rightharpoonup\mathcal L\bigl(\mathrm{MA}_\theta(E(v))\bigr).
\]
More precisely, its total-variation distance from the lift of the base partial kernel measure selected by $\psi$ is $O(m^{-1})$. Its determinant functionals satisfy
\[
 \mathcal F_{X,m}^{(q)}(v\circ\pi)\longrightarrow
 \int_0^1\!\left(\int_B
           v\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(E(tv))\right)\mathrm{d}t.
\]
The normalized dimensions converge to the finite positive equilibrium mass. If $n>q$, normalization by $n!/m^n$ gives zero kernel measures in total variation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal} in dimension $q$ identifies the base multiplier-ideal-selected space with $W_m$ in the proposition. Corresponding sections in $V_m$ and on $X$ have equal continuous norms, since the pointwise norm and continuous weight descend and every fiber probability has mass one. The same orthonormal-basis argument as in \cref{prop:partial-kernel-submersion} gives the exact lift of the kernel associated with $V_m$ on $B$.

By \cref{lem:kernel-codimension-comparison,prop:isolated-pole-section-descent}, the normalized kernel difference between $V_m$ and $W_m$ is positive with total mass $O(m^{-1})$. Its lift remains positive with the same mass. Apply \cref{thm:fujiki-partial-kernel-limit} to $B$, using the identity modification: all its closed K\"ahler and positive-mass hypotheses are among the assumptions here. It gives the base $W_m$ convergence, and the vanishing kernel difference proves the asserted limit on $X$.

The determinant comparison bounds the difference of the two functionals by $O(m^{-1})\|v\|_\infty$. The exact norm identification and \cref{cor:fujiki-partial-determinant} on $B$ give the displayed limit. Testing one proves the dimension assertion. The $q$-normalized masses are bounded, so multiplication by $(n!/q!)m^{q-n}$ proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For a positive curvature form $\theta$, \cref{lem:small-isolated-log-poles} constructs the required $\psi$ when the coefficients are sufficiently small. Its curvature dominates a positive multiple of $\theta$ off the poles, so its non-pluripolar mass is positive; closed K\"ahler theory makes it finite. Applying the same lemma on $X$ with a Hermitian metric constructs $\varphi$. If the fibers have positive dimension and the pole set is nonempty and finite, $\varphi$ cannot descend, since it is finite at other points of a fiber containing a pole.

The exact section formula also explains a failure of singularity recovery. Moving an isolated pole within its fiber does not change any selected section space. Adding finitely many poles in that same fiber does not change the spaces when the maximal coefficient there is unchanged. For the fixed pullback metric $h=\pi^*h_A$, continuous weight $v$ and lifted measure $\nu_X$, all kernels and determinant functionals are then exactly the same in every degree. The multiplier-ideal types on $X$ are nevertheless different: at sufficiently large weight one ideal is proper at a point where the other is the full local ring. Under the theorem's hypotheses this loss of information coexists with a positive $q$-dimensional growth limit. Closed-curvature positivity of the selecting potential, present in the source theory, cannot be replaced merely by positivity against a Hermitian background.
