\section{Existence for the Gauduchon ansatz}
\label{sec:stw-continuity}

The potential, gradient and Hessian bounds put the matrices $Q_u$ in a compact set of positive matrices. This yields uniform ellipticity after the real Hessian is projected to its complex part and transformed by $P_n$, so local elliptic regularity gives the compactness needed for closedness in the continuity method; the adjoint probability measure gives openness. The maximum principle then proves uniqueness within the scalar ansatz.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:stw-higher-regularity}
If the prescribed functions $F$ range over a set of smooth functions bounded in every $C^k$ norm, then the smooth solutions $(u,b)$ of the equation in \cref{thm:stw-volume} with $\sup_Xu=0$ are bounded in every $C^k$ norm.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work with $v=u/(2\pi)$ and write $Q=Q_u$ for the matrix in \eqref{eq:stw-cofactor-matrix-scaling}. By \cref{prop:stw-czero-reconstructed,thm:stw-second-order-reconstructed,prop:stw-gradient-from-second-order}, the function $v$, its gradient and its complex Hessian are uniformly bounded, and so are the constants $b$. Hence the eigenvalues of $Q$ are uniformly bounded above, and by the equation its determinant is uniformly bounded below. Every eigenvalue is therefore also bounded below by a positive constant. Choose fixed numbers $0<a<b_0$ such that $aI\leq Q\leq b_0I$ in unitary frames for every solution.

The function $\Delta_\gamma v=\operatorname{tr}_\gamma(\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial v)$ is uniformly bounded, and $\Delta_\gamma$ is a fixed uniformly elliptic operator. Its interior $W^{2,p}$ estimates, applied on a finite cover, bound $v$ in $W^{2,p}$ for every finite $p$. Choose $p>2n$ and $0<\alpha<1-2n/p$; Sobolev embedding gives a uniform $C^{1,\alpha}$ bound. Thus the matrix $R(x)=Q_0(x)+Z_x(\partial v(x))$ is uniformly bounded in $C^\alpha$.

On a coordinate ball, let $\mathcal H_x(A)$ denote the Hermitian matrix of $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial v$ at $x$ in a fixed smooth unitary frame, expressed as a function of the real Hessian $A=D^2_{\mathbb R}v(x)$. It is a real linear function of $A$ depending smoothly on $x$. If a real symmetric matrix $N$ is nonnegative, then $\mathcal H_x(N)\geq0$, and its trace is comparable to $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb R}N$ with uniform positive constants on the ball. This follows by evaluating on a vector and its rotation by $\mathrm i$, or from the coordinate formula for $v_{i\bar j}$.

Freeze the function $\partial v(x)$ already obtained in $R(x)$, and define for all real symmetric matrices $A$
\[
 \widetilde\Phi(A,x)=
  \inf_{aI\leq B\leq b_0I}
    \left\{\log\det B+
       \operatorname{tr}\left[B^{-1}
          \bigl(R(x)+P_n(\mathcal H_x(A))-B\bigr)\right]\right\}.
\]
The infimum is over a fixed compact set of positive Hermitian matrices, and each expression in braces is an affine function of $A$, so $\widetilde\Phi$ is concave in $A$. If $R(x)+P_n(\mathcal H_x(A))$ lies in that compact set, then $\widetilde\Phi(A,x)$ equals its logarithmic determinant: each expression in braces is a tangent affine function of the concave logarithmic determinant, and the tangent at that matrix itself attains equality.

For $N\geq0$, each of these affine functions increases by
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\bigl(B^{-1}P_n(\mathcal H_x(N))\bigr)
\]
when $A$ is replaced by $A+N$. Since $P_n$ preserves positivity and trace, this increment lies between fixed positive multiples of $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb R}N$, uniformly in $B$ and $x$. Taking infima preserves both inequalities, so $\widetilde\Phi$ is uniformly elliptic on all real symmetric matrices.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Smoothness of $\mathcal H_x$ and the uniform $C^\alpha$ bound for $R$ also give
\[
 |\widetilde\Phi(A,x)-\widetilde\Phi(A,y)|
          \leq C|x-y|^\alpha(1+\|A\|).
\]
These are the hypotheses of the local Evans--Krylov estimate for concave uniformly elliptic equations with H\"older dependence on $x$, in the formulation cited in \cref{sec:history-smooth-equations}. The value $\widetilde\Phi(0,x)$ is uniformly bounded, by the compactness of the set of matrices $B$ and the bound for $R$. By the choice of $a$ and $b_0$, the given smooth function solves
\[
 \widetilde\Phi(D^2_{\mathbb R}v,x)=(n-1)(F+b)
\]
on the ball. The Evans--Krylov estimate, applied on smaller balls of a finite cover, yields a uniform $C^{2,\beta}$ bound for some $\beta>0$.

Differentiating the original matrix equation gives linear uniformly elliptic equations for the first derivatives of $v$, whose coefficients, including the first-order coefficients coming from $Z$, are now H\"older continuous. Local Schauder estimates give $C^{3,\beta}$ bounds, and differentiation and induction give all higher bounds. The finite coordinate cover makes the bounds global. The only regularity results used here are the standard $W^{2,p}$ estimates for the Laplacian, the Evans--Krylov estimate for concave equations, and the Schauder estimates.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}[of \cref{thm:stw-volume}]
Let
\[
 F_0=\log\frac{\gamma_0^n}{\gamma^n},\qquad
 F_t=(1-t)F_0+tF,\quad0\leq t\leq1.
\]
We use the continuity method along the path $F_t$. At $t=0$, the pair $u=0$, $b=0$ solves the equation, since $\eta_0=\gamma_0$. During the continuity argument we normalize potentials by $\int_Xu\,\gamma^n=0$; adding a constant does not affect the equation, and at the end we impose $\sup_Xu=0$ instead.

For openness, the derivative of the equation with respect to the mean-zero potential and the scalar is $(w,a)\mapsto\mathcal L_uw-a$. As in the proof of \cref{prop:smooth-dependence}, with the adjoint probability measure of \cref{prop:gaud-volume-linearization} in place of $\mu_u$, it is invertible between H\"older spaces with bounded inverse. Since positivity of $\Gamma_u$ is an open condition, the implicit function theorem gives solutions for all nearby parameters.

For closedness, note that the functions $F_t$ are uniformly bounded in every $C^k$ norm. After supremum normalization, \cref{prop:stw-czero-reconstructed} bounds $u$ and $b$, the second-order and rescaling estimates bound the gradient and the complex Hessian, and \cref{lem:stw-higher-regularity} gives all higher bounds. Subtracting the mean preserves all derivative bounds and changes the potentials by uniformly bounded constants. A subsequence of mean-zero solutions along a convergent sequence of parameters therefore converges smoothly to a solution at the limiting parameter, and the uniform lower bound for the eigenvalues of $Q_u$ obtained in the proof of \cref{lem:stw-higher-regularity} preserves strict positivity in the limit. Thus the set of solvable parameters is nonempty, open and closed, hence equal to $[0,1]$.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
Finally, consider two solutions $(u,b)$ and $(w,c)$ for the same $F$. At a maximum of $u-w$ their gradients agree and their complex Hessians are ordered, so the first-order terms are equal there and, since $P_n$ preserves the order, $Q_u\leq Q_w$. The equation then gives $b\leq c$, and at a minimum the reverse inequality gives $b\geq c$. Hence $b=c$. Along the segment between $u$ and $w$, the matrices $Q$ remain positive because the ansatz is affine in the potential. Integrating the derivative of the logarithmic determinant along that segment produces a uniformly elliptic scalar operator with first-order terms and no zeroth-order term that annihilates $u-w$. The maximum principle shows that $u-w$ is constant, and the supremum normalization makes the constant zero.
\end{proof}
