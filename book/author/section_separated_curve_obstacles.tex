The same coordinatewise computation applies to arbitrary curve potentials, to curve backgrounds that need not be positive, and to smooth obstacles that are sums of functions of the separate coordinates. Keep the curves $C_i$, the product $Y$ and its projections $\pi_i$. Let $\sigma_i$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $C_i$ with $\PSH(C_i,\sigma_i)\ne\varnothing$, that is, with $\int_{C_i}\sigma_i\geq0$ by \cref{lem:curve-background-area}, and choose $u_i\in\PSH(C_i,\sigma_i)$ and $F_i\in C^\infty(C_i,\mathbb R)$. Put
\[
 \gamma=\sum_i\pi_i^*\sigma_i,\qquad
 u=\sum_i u_i\circ\pi_i,\qquad
 F=\sum_i F_i\circ\pi_i.
\]

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections}\index{Envelope!separated smooth obstacles}
For the preceding data,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-curve-obstacle-projections}
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{\gamma,F}[u]
     &=\sum_i P_{\sigma_i,F_i}[u_i]\circ\pi_i,\\
 P_{\gamma,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
     &=\sum_i P_{\sigma_i,F_i}[u_i]_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These identities also hold when some of the curve backgrounds have zero area.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $e_i=P_{\sigma_i,F_i}[u_i]$. Sums of ordinary competitors in the separate coordinates are ordinary competitors on the product, and their regularized supremum is $\sum_i e_i\circ\pi_i$. This gives the lower bound in the first identity.

For the upper bound we use induction on the number of factors, the case of one factor being trivial. Let $v\leq F$ be an ordinary competitor, so that $v\leq u+C$ for some constant $C$. Choose a constant $K$ with $u-K\leq F$ and replace $v$ by $\max(v,u-K)$. This preserves both bounds, after enlarging $C$ if necessary, and ensures that the restrictions of $v$ to the slices used below are not identically $-\infty$.

Write $Y=Y'\times C_m$, and on $Y'$ put $u'=\sum_{i<m}u_i\circ\pi_i$ and $F'=\sum_{i<m}F_i\circ\pi_i$. The regularized partial supremum
\[
 a(t)=\left(\sup_{y'\in Y'}\bigl(v(y',t)-F'(y')\bigr)\right)^*
\]
is $\sigma_m$-plurisubharmonic by the local upper-envelope theorem of \cref{chap:framework}: the functions $t\mapsto v(y',t)-F'(y')$ have the common upper bound $F_m$, and at least one of them is not identically $-\infty$, by the lower bound $v\geq u-K$. The bound $v\leq u+C$ gives
\[
 a\leq u_m+C+\sup_{Y'}(u'-F'),\qquad a\leq F_m.
\]
Thus $a$ is a competitor for $e_m$, and $a\leq e_m$. If $u_m(t)$ is finite, so is $e_m(t)$, since a sufficiently low translate of $u_m$ is a competitor for $e_m$. The function $v(\cdot,t)-e_m(t)$ is then a potential in the product background on $Y'$, not identically $-\infty$, lying below $F'$ and at least as singular as $u'$. By induction, $v(\cdot,t)\leq\sum_{i<m}e_i\circ\pi_i+e_m(t)$. At a pole of $u_m$, the bound $v\leq u+C$ already gives $v=-\infty$. Every competitor is therefore bounded by $\sum_i e_i\circ\pi_i$, and taking the regularized supremum proves the ordinary identity.

For the ideal identity, put $B_i=\int_{C_i}\sigma_i\geq0$ and $\widehat\eta_i=\eta_i/A_i$. By \cref{lem:curve-background-area}, choose smooth functions $\chi_i$ with
\[
 \sigma_i=B_i\widehat\eta_i+\ddc\chi_i.
\]
Let $E_i$ be the atomic part of $\sigma_i+\ddc u_i$. If $B_i>0$, apply \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model} to $u_i+\chi_i$ in the positive background $B_i\widehat\eta_i$. Subtracting $\chi_i$ from the resulting atomic Green potential gives a $\sigma_i$-plurisubharmonic function $g_i$ such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 u_i\sim_{\mathcal I}g_i,\qquad
 P_{\sigma_i}[u_i]_{\mathcal I}\sim g_i,\\
 \ddc g_i=E_i-E_i(C_i)\widehat\eta_i-\ddc\chi_i;
 \end{gathered}
\]
the second relation uses the smooth gauge bounds of \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge}. If $B_i=0$, \cref{lem:curve-background-area} gives $u_i=-\chi_i+c_i$ for a constant $c_i$, and $E_i=0$; taking $g_i=-\chi_i$ gives the same conclusions. In either case,
\[
 \sigma_i+\ddc g_i
       =E_i+(B_i-E_i(C_i))\widehat\eta_i\geq0.
\]

Put $g=\sum_i g_i\circ\pi_i$. Pullback and addition, \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback,lem:hermitian-i-calculus}, give $g\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. The complex Hessian of $g$ is a countable sum of divisor currents supported on coordinate fibers, minus a smooth form. This divisor current has finite mass, because each coordinate fiber has constant mass for a fixed product metric and each $E_i$ has finite mass. Thus \cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination} applies, even when some $B_i$ vanish. Every ideal competitor $v\leq F$ for $u$ therefore satisfies $v\preceq g$, and is bounded by $P_{\gamma,F}[g]$.

Set $r_i=P_{\sigma_i,F_i}[u_i]_{\mathcal I}$. Invariance of the ordinary envelope under ordinary equivalence and \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} give
\[
 P_{\sigma_i,F_i}[g_i]
    =P_{\sigma_i,F_i}[P_{\sigma_i}[u_i]_{\mathcal I}]
    =r_i.
\]
By the ordinary identity already proved, applied to $g$, the ideal envelope of $u$ is therefore at most $\sum_i r_i\circ\pi_i$. Conversely, each $r_i$ lies below $F_i$ and is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u_i$, again by \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection}. Their sum is a $\gamma$-plurisubharmonic ideal competitor for $u$ below $F$, which proves the reverse inequality. No strict positivity of the backgrounds is needed in this comparison.
\end{proof}

The proof of \eqref{eq:separated-curve-obstacle-projections} uses that the obstacle is a sum of functions of the separate coordinates; for an arbitrary smooth obstacle on $Y$, the coordinatewise bounds are not available. Equality of the two envelopes below such mixed obstacles is treated in \cref{subsec:separated-product-all-obstacles}.
