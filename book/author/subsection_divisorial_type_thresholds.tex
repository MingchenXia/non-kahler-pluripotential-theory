\subsection{One-sided divisorial classes and sharp thresholds}
\label{subsec:divisorial-type-thresholds}

The ordinary-type correspondence of \cref{thm:ordinary-divisor-normalization} extends to potentials with additional poles. It then identifies the largest subsolution coefficient in the whole one-sided class, even for merely integrable data. For smooth positive data, the prescribed divisor causes a strict loss relative to the global coefficient.

Keep the divisorial setting above, assume also that $\theta$ is a Hermitian form, and subtract a constant from $\varphi$ so that $\sup_X\varphi=0$. Write $S=\operatorname{Supp}\Gamma$ and $\psi=P_\theta[\varphi]$. For a Hermitian form $h$ and a nonzero density $0\le f\in L^1(\mathrm dV)$, we use the subsolution threshold $b_h(f)$ of \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution} and, for $f\in L^p(\mathrm dV)$ with $p>1$, the normalization scalar $c_h(f)$ of \cref{sec:lower-mass-solvability}; thus $c_h(\mathrm e^F)=\exp B_h(F)$ for bounded $F$. For a nonempty class $\mathcal C\subset\PSH(X,\theta)$, define
\[
 b_{\theta,\mathcal C}(f)=\sup\{c\ge0:\text{some }u\in\mathcal C
          \text{ satisfies }\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
                                      \ge cf\,\mathrm dV\}.
\]
The three classes considered here are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal C_{\preceq}&=\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\preceq\varphi\},\\
 \mathcal C_{\sim}&=\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\sim\varphi\},\\
 \mathcal C_{\mathrm{env}}&=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\psi).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The following lemma describes the reference $\psi$ used in the last definition.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:one-sided-divisor-correspondence}
The map $w\mapsto\varphi+w$ is a bijection from $\PSH(X,\beta)$ onto $\mathcal C_{\preceq}$, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\varphi+w)
                         =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta(w).
\]
The bounded members of $\PSH(X,\beta)$ correspond to $\mathcal C_{\sim}$. The envelope $\psi$ satisfies $\varphi\le\psi\le\varphi+K$ for some finite $K$, meets the model condition \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}, and
\[
 \mathcal C_{\sim}\subset\mathcal C_{\mathrm{env}}
                         \subset\mathcal C_{\preceq}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $u\preceq\varphi$, its generic Lelong numbers dominate the coefficients of $\Gamma$. By \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal},
$\theta+\ddc u-[\Gamma]\ge0$. The locally integrable distribution $w=u-\varphi$ therefore has a canonical $\beta$-psh representative. Off $S$ it equals the difference and is bounded above. The identity $u=\varphi+w$ holds almost everywhere, hence everywhere for the canonical quasi-psh functions. Conversely, $w\in\PSH(X,\beta)$ has finite supremum, and
\[
 \theta+\ddc(\varphi+w)=\beta+\ddc w+[\Gamma]\ge0,
 \qquad \varphi+w\preceq\varphi.
\]
These maps are inverse to each other. The bounded correspondence is \cref{thm:ordinary-divisor-normalization}. Off $S$ the currents agree; locality of the extended products identifies their measures there. Both measures give the analytic set $S$ zero mass by \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, even when $w$ has additional poles or infinite mass. This proves the measure identity on $X$ without subtracting infinite pointwise values.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Let $v$ be a competitor in the definition of $\psi=P_\theta[\varphi]$, that is, $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $v\le0$ and $v\preceq\varphi$. The corresponding $w=v-\varphi\in\PSH(X,\beta)$ satisfies $w\le-\varphi$ off $S$. Hence
\[
 \int_Xw^+\,\mathrm dV\le\int_X(-\varphi)\,\mathrm dV<\infty,
\]
uniformly in $v$. On a fixed finite cover by inner and outer coordinate balls, add smooth quadratic corrections making $w$ locally psh. The submean inequality at a point where $w$ attains its supremum, with uniformly bounded corrections and volume ratios of the balls, gives
$\sup_Xw\le C_1\int_Xw^+\,\mathrm dV+C_2\le K$.
Thus $v=\varphi+w\le\varphi+K$ everywhere. The function $\varphi$ itself is a competitor, so the ordinary envelope construction gives a $\theta$-psh function between $\varphi$ and $\varphi+K$. The upper bound survives upper regularization because $\varphi+K$ is upper semicontinuous. Since $\psi\sim\varphi$ and the envelope depends only on the ordinary type, $P_\theta[\psi]=P_\theta[\varphi]=\psi$. For $\varepsilon<1$ sufficiently close to one, $\beta-(1-\varepsilon)\theta>0$, so $\varphi$ is an $\varepsilon\theta$-psh competitor below $\psi$. Hence $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\psi)$ is not identically $-\infty$, as required in \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}.

If $u\sim\varphi\sim\psi$, choose $K_u\ge0$ with $\psi-K_u\le u$. For each $t\ge1$, the $\theta$-psh function $a_t=\psi-tK_u$ obeys
\[
 t^{-1}a_t+(1-t^{-1})\psi=\psi-K_u\le u.
\]
This proves membership in the relative envelope class of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class} (compare \cref{prop:relative-elementary}), including the convention at $t=1$ and common poles. The other containment follows from its defining order $u\preceq\psi\sim\varphi$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:divisorial-type-thresholds}\index{Subsolution!prescribed divisorial threshold}
For every nonzero nonnegative $f\in L^1(\mathrm dV)$,
\[
 b_{\theta,\mathcal C_{\preceq}}(f)=b_\beta(f),
\]
and this coefficient is attained. If $f$ is smooth and strictly positive, then
\[
 b_{\theta,\mathcal C_{\sim}}(f)
 =b_{\theta,\mathcal C_{\mathrm{env}}}(f)
 =b_{\theta,\mathcal C_{\preceq}}(f)=c_\beta(f),
\]
and the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)=c_\beta(f)f\,\mathrm dV$ has a solution $u\sim\varphi$. If also $\Gamma\ne0$, then
\[
 0<c_\beta(f)<c_\theta(f)=b_\theta(f).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The correspondence lemma and \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution} for the Hermitian form $\beta$ prove the first assertion, including attainment. This assertion allows additional poles and excess or infinite mass.
For smooth positive $f$, \cref{thm:tw-smooth} gives a smooth $w$ with $\beta+\ddc w>0$ and
$(\beta+\ddc w)^n=c_\beta(f)f\,\mathrm dV$.
The potential $\varphi+w$ belongs to the ordinary type and hence to all three classes, and by \cref{lem:one-sided-divisor-correspondence} it solves the asserted equation. The class containments and the first assertion give equality of their coefficients, and \cref{prop:individual-normalizer-lp} gives $b_\beta(f)=c_\beta(f)$.

It remains to prove strictness. Let $v$ be a smooth solution of $(\theta+\ddc v)^n=c_\theta(f)f\,\mathrm dV$, given by \cref{thm:tw-smooth}, and put
$\sigma=\theta+\ddc v$, $\eta=\beta+\ddc w$.
The subsolution $\varphi+w$ and \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution,prop:individual-normalizer-lp} give $c_\beta(f)\le b_\theta(f)=c_\theta(f)$. If equality held, then on $X\setminus S$ the smooth function $a=\varphi+w-v$ would satisfy
\[
 \ddc a=\eta-\sigma,\qquad
 \ddc a\wedge Q=0,\qquad
 Q=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\eta^j\wedge\sigma^{n-1-j}.
\]
The form $Q$ is smooth and strictly positive; for $n=1$, $Q=1$. It therefore defines a real second-order operator $L$ with positive definite principal matrix and no zeroth-order term; for $n=1$, $L$ is a positive function times the Laplacian in each holomorphic chart. Also $a\to-\infty$ near $S$, so its maximum is attained inside a component $U$ of $X\setminus S$. The strong maximum principle makes $a$ constant on $U$. To recall its local argument, put $z=\max_Ua-a\ge0$. If a positive region of $z$ had a boundary zero inside $U$, choose a ball $B(y,R)$ in that region touching the zero set, with closure in a coordinate chart. On its outer half-annulus $R/2<|x-y|<R$, the barrier
$\mathrm e^{-A|x-y|^2}-\mathrm e^{-AR^2}$ has strictly positive $L$ for large $A$. A sufficiently small multiple lies below $z$ on the two boundary spheres. A negative interior minimum of the difference would have nonnegative $L$, whereas its equation gives strictly negative $L$. Thus the comparison holds throughout the annulus and gives a positive inward derivative at the touching zero, contradicting $\nabla z=0$. Connectedness propagates the zero set to all of $U$.
The boundary of $U$ is nonempty and lies in $S$: otherwise $U$ would be both open and closed in connected $X$, although $S\ne\varnothing$. Constancy then contradicts $a\to-\infty$ at that boundary. This proves strictness; no semipositivity of the divisor's smooth curvature representative is required.
\end{proof}

In dimension one, every Hermitian form is closed, and the theorem becomes explicit. By \cref{prop:individual-normalizer-lp}, $b_\beta(f)\int_Xf\,\mathrm dV=\int_X\beta$ for every $f$ as in the first assertion, and integrating $\theta+\ddc\varphi=[\Gamma]+\beta$ gives $\int_X\theta=\deg\Gamma+\int_X\beta$, where $\deg\Gamma$ is the sum of the coefficients of $\Gamma$. Thus the loss caused by the divisor is $c_\theta(f)-c_\beta(f)=\deg\Gamma/\int_Xf\,\mathrm dV$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:critical-no-divisor}\index{Divisor!exclusion at the smooth threshold}
For smooth strictly positive $f$, no threshold subsolution $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ for $f$ has a nonzero divisorial component in $\theta+\ddc u$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that $\theta+\ddc u\ge\lambda_0[E_0]$ for a prime divisor $E_0$ and some $\lambda_0>0$, and put $E=\lambda E_0$ with $0<\lambda\le\lambda_0$. Let $s$ be the canonical section of the line bundle $\mathcal O(E_0)$, choose a smooth Hermitian metric $h_E$ on it, and put $\ell_E=\lambda\log|s|_{h_E}^2$ and $\gamma_E=\lambda c_1(\mathcal O(E_0),h_E)$; then $[E]=\gamma_E+\ddc\ell_E$ by the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula. For $\lambda$ sufficiently small, $\theta-\gamma_E>0$. Divisorial removal, \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal}, gives a $(\theta-\gamma_E)$-psh representative of $u-\ell_E$, whose finite supremum implies $u\preceq\ell_E$. The preceding theorem, applied to the single section $s$ with weight $\lambda$, would then give
\[
 b_\theta(f)\le c_{\theta-\gamma_E}(f)
                         <c_\theta(f)=b_\theta(f),
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}
For $n\geq2$, the later rigidity result \cref{cor:smooth-dual-exact-equation} strengthens this obstruction by determining every smooth-data threshold subsolution through the maximizing Gauduchon form.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:projective-divisor-threshold-loss}
Let $(X_0,h_0)$ be a connected compact Hermitian $m$-fold, let $k\ge2$, and let $\kappa$ be the mass-one Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^k$. On $X_0\times\mathbb P^k$, choose a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm dV$. With pullbacks implicit, put
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 n&=m+k,& C&=\binom nm,& V_0&=\int_{X_0}h_0^m,\\
 H&=h_0+\kappa,& \mu&=(h_0^m/V_0)\wedge\kappa^k,& R&=\mu/\mathrm dV.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For $D=\{Z_0=0\}$ pulled back to the product, take $E=\lambda D$, $0<\lambda<1$, with
\[
 \ell_E=\lambda\log\frac{|Z_0|^2}{\sum_{i=0}^k|Z_i|^2},
 \qquad H_E=h_0+(1-\lambda)\kappa.
\]
The global threshold of $R$ is $CV_0$, while its ordinary, relative envelope and one-sided thresholds prescribed by $E$ are all $CV_0(1-\lambda)^k$, attained by $\ell_E$. If $v_-(h_0)=0$, both $H$ and $H_E$ are nonclosed and have zero lower mass.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The Poincar\'e--Lelong formula gives $[D]=\kappa+\ddc(\ell_E/\lambda)$. Since $h_0^{m+1}=0$ and $\kappa^{k+1}=0$, the binomial expansion gives
\[
 H^n=CV_0R\,\mathrm dV,\qquad
 H_E^n=CV_0(1-\lambda)^kR\,\mathrm dV.
\]
Thus the zero residual solves each smooth positive equation and fixes its unique scalar. The theorem identifies all three restricted coefficients, and the correspondence gives
$\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(\ell_E)=H_E^n$.
If $v_-(h_0)=0$, pullbacks of bounded $h_0$-psh potentials with mass tending to zero have masses multiplied respectively by $C$ and $C(1-\lambda)^k$. Bounded submersion pullback and the product expansion therefore give zero lower mass for both backgrounds. If $h_0$ were closed, Stokes' theorem would give every bounded potential the mass $V_0>0$; hence $h_0$ is nonclosed, and so are both backgrounds.
\end{proof}
