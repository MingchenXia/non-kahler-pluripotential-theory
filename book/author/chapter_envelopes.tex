\chapter{Envelopes and volumes of singularities}
\label{chap:envelopes}

Throughout this chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, with an auxiliary Hermitian metric $\omega$ and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$, and $\theta$ denotes a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ whose positivity is specified in each section.

On a compact K\"ahler manifold, and more generally on a compact complex manifold with the bounded mass property of \cref{def:bmp}, the non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere mass of a potential in a closed background depends only on its singularity type; this is what makes mass a tool for comparing singularities. In a nonclosed background, a bounded change of the potential can change the mass, and a singularity type may contain potentials of different, even infinite, masses. This chapter opens the part of the book on relative singularities and the equations they govern, and introduces two kinds of invariants that take this into account.

The first records the variation of mass: the lower and upper volumes $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ and $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ of a potential $u$ are the infimum and the supremum of the masses of all potentials of the same singularity type. The second replaces a mass condition by a condition on envelopes. Fix a reference potential $\varphi$. Stretching a potential $u$ away from $\varphi$ by a factor $b\geq1$ gives the obstacle $bu-(b-1)\varphi$, which is more singular than $u$ where $u$ lies below $\varphi$. The \emph{relative envelope class} $\mathcal E_\varphi$ consists of the potentials $u$ at least as singular as $\varphi$ for which every such stretched obstacle lies above some $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function. This class is convex and stable under finite rooftops, its members have the same singularity envelope as $\varphi$, and its definition involves no mass.

The main tool is the contact property of envelopes: the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of an envelope is carried by the set where it touches its obstacle. Together with ordered contact, it gives the domination principle for bounded potentials in a semipositive background in which every bounded potential has positive mass, and it bounds the measures of rooftop envelopes. For envelopes of logarithmic growth on $\mathbb C^n$, which correspond to envelopes on $\mathbb P^n$, we also examine how the contact of the regularized envelope with its obstacle depends on the growth allowance.

In a semipositive big background, the contact property also produces positive mass. Let $\varphi$ be a model reference, that is, a fixed point of the singularity envelope that admits a competitor in a smaller background. Every member $u$ of $\mathcal E_\varphi$ then has a competitor in a background $a\theta$ with $0<a<1$, and an envelope in the remaining big background $(1-a)\theta$ has positive mass, which it transfers to $u$ on the contact set. The argument uses only that each bounded potential has positive mass, so it applies even when the infimum of these masses is zero. On the other hand, if $X$ does not have the bounded mass property of \cref{def:bmp}, the relative envelope class of the zero potential in the background $\omega$ contains potentials of infinite mass. Envelope conditions and mass bounds are therefore different requirements, and this distinction is essential for the equations with prescribed singularities in the next chapter.

We use the following notation from \cref{chap:framework}. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $u,v$, we write $u\preceq v$ if $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C$, and $u\sim v$ if $u\preceq v$ and $v\preceq u$; the equivalence class of $u$ is its \emph{singularity type} (\cref{def:singularity-order}). For an obstacle $f$, the envelope $P_\theta(f)$ is the upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of all $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $v\leq f$ quasi-everywhere; it may be identically $-\infty$, and then it does not belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$. For $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the rooftop envelope is $P_\theta(u,v)=P_\theta(\min(u,v))$, and $V_\theta=P_\theta(0)$ is the potential with minimal singularities; see \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}. The singularity envelope $P_\theta[u]$, its variant $P_{\theta,F}[u]$ with a smooth obstacle $F$, and envelope fixed points, that is, potentials with $P_\theta[u]=u$, are defined in \cref{def:singularity-envelope}. We write $\theta_u=\theta+\ddc u$ and $\omega_u=\omega+\ddc u$. For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)$ denotes the extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$ of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}. It is the Bedford--Taylor measure $(\theta+\ddc u)^n$ when $u$ is bounded, it puts no mass on pluripolar sets, and its total mass, the \emph{extended mass} of $u$, may be infinite.

\section{Contact sets}

The Monge--Amp\`ere measure of an envelope is concentrated on the contact set, where the envelope meets its obstacle. For a smooth obstacle and a Hermitian background, the envelope is the uniform limit of solutions of exponential equations whose densities tend to zero wherever the envelope stays strictly below the obstacle. Continuity of bounded products then carries this property to quasi-continuous obstacles, first bounded ones, through monotone approximation, and then general ones, through truncation.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:envelope-contact}\index{Contact set!envelope}
Let $\theta$ be smooth and semipositive, and let $f$ be a quasi-continuous obstacle. If $p=P_\theta(f)$ is not identically $-\infty$, then $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere and
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{p<f\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)=0.
\]
Here the total extended mass of $p$ may be infinite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The local envelope theorem, applied to a countable family realizing the supremum (Choquet's lemma), gives $p\leq f$ outside a pluripolar set. Choose $M>\sup_Xp$ and replace $f$ by $\min(f,M)$. This does not change the envelope, since $p$ itself is a competitor for the smaller obstacle. Since $p<M$, the contact set is also unchanged, so it suffices to prove the theorem for the smaller obstacle; that is, we may assume that $f$ is bounded above.

Set $f_j=\max(f,-j)$ and $p_j=P_\theta(f_j)$. The constants $-j$ are competitors, so $p_j$ is bounded. \Cref{thm:bounded-contact-support} gives $p_j\leq f_j$ quasi-everywhere and
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{p_j<f_j\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p_j)=0.
\]
The sequence $p_j$ decreases and stays above $p$. Its limit is therefore $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, it lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere, and so it is a competitor for $p$. Hence $p_j\downarrow p$.

Fix an integer $k\geq1$ and $C>0$, and define the bounded nonnegative quasi-continuous function
\[
 H_{k,C}=\min\{1,(f-p_k)_+\}\,
                  \min\{1,(p+C)_+\}.
\]
Its values on pluripolar sets are immaterial. Where this coefficient is positive, $p_k<f$ and $p>-C$. For $j\geq k$, we then have
$p_j\leq p_k<f\leq f_j$ and $p_j\geq p>-C$ there. Plurifine locality and \cref{thm:bounded-contact-support} therefore imply
\[
 \int_X H_{k,C}\,
       \mathrm{MA}_\theta(\max(p_j,-C))=0.
\]
The truncated potentials are uniformly bounded and decrease to $\max(p,-C)$, so their measures converge weakly by bounded Bedford--Taylor continuity. The uniform capacity bounds and the weighted convergence in \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity} also allow testing against $H_{k,C}$. Hence the last identity holds with $p$ in place of $p_j$.
By plurifine locality, this says that the extended measure of $p$ vanishes on
$\{p_k<f\}\cap\{p>-C\}$. Off the pole set of $p$, these sets increase to $\{p<f\}$ as first $k$ and then $C$ tend to infinity. Continuity of positive measures along increasing sequences proves the assertion, even if the total extended mass is infinite.
\end{proof}

For a smooth big background, constants need not be competitors, and a strictly $\theta$-plurisubharmonic barrier with poles replaces the constant lower truncations. The same contact property for quasi-continuous obstacles finite almost everywhere is proved in this setting in \cref{thm:big-general-envelope-contact}. Combined with ordered contact, either contact theorem controls the measures of rooftop envelopes. In the following bound, the strict inequality in the first term assigns the common contact set $\{p=u=v\}$ to the second term.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rooftop-measure}\index{Envelope!rooftop}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is semipositive or big, and let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. If $p=P_\theta(u,v)$ is not identically $-\infty$, then
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{p=u<v\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
      +\mathbf1_{\{p=v\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The envelope lies below both obstacles quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere, since the obstacles are upper semicontinuous. By the contact theorem (\cref{thm:envelope-contact} or \cref{thm:big-general-envelope-contact}), its measure is carried by $\{p=\min(u,v)\}$. Partition this set into $\{p=u<v\}$ and $\{p=v\}$, and apply \cref{prop:ordered-contact-general} on each part. All measures vanish on the pluripolar pole sets, which can therefore be discarded throughout.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fixed-point-finite}
Let $\theta$ be smooth and semipositive. Every envelope fixed point $\varphi$ satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{\varphi=0\}}\theta^n.
\]
In particular, its extended mass is finite.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\varphi=P_\theta[\varphi]\leq0$, the function $\varphi$ is a competitor for $P_\theta(\varphi+1,0)$. This rooftop is also a competitor for $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$, being at most $\varphi+1$ and at most zero, so it equals $\varphi$. Now apply \cref{prop:rooftop-measure} with $u=\varphi+1$ and $v=0$. The contact set $\{\varphi=\varphi+1<0\}$ is contained in the pole set of $\varphi$, which the extended measure does not charge. Only contact with zero contributes, and this gives the asserted bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Bounded domination in a semipositive background}

We return to the compact manifold $X$.

The domination principle for Hermitian backgrounds, \cref{thm:bounded-strict-domination}, extends to a smooth semipositive background $\theta$ provided every bounded $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential has positive mass. For bounded potentials $u,v$ satisfying the measure inequality below, contact support bounds $\sup_XP_\theta(bu-(b-1)v)$ from below as the stretching parameter $b>1$ increases. A fixed gap between $u$ and $v$ would then force the $L^1$ norms of these envelopes, normalized by their suprema, to diverge, contradicting compactness.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
We call a smooth semipositive form $\theta$ \emph{non-collapsing on bounded potentials} if
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)>0
 \quad\hbox{for every }w\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^\infty(X).
\]
Every Hermitian form has this property by \cref{cor:bounded-mass-positive}. Bigness also implies it, by the following envelope argument.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:big-noncollapsing}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!individual positivity}
If a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ is big, every bounded $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has strictly positive Monge--Amp\`ere mass.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $a>0$ with $\theta_\rho\geq a\omega$, normalize $\rho\leq0$, and put
\[
 p=P_{a\omega}(u-\rho).
\]
The obstacle is finite almost everywhere and bounded below, so \cref{lem:bounded-below-envelope} shows that $p$ is bounded. The obstacle is quasi-continuous off the pluripolar pole set of $\rho$. The contact theorem, \cref{thm:envelope-contact}, applied in the Hermitian background $a\omega$, shows that $\mathrm{MA}_{a\omega}(p)$ is carried by
$D=\{p+\rho=u\}$, outside the pole set of $\rho$.

By \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, $p+\rho\leq u$ everywhere, and
\[
 \theta+\ddc(p+\rho)
 =(a\omega+\ddc p)+(\theta_\rho-a\omega)
 \geq a\omega+\ddc p\geq0.
\]
On common finite truncation levels, positivity of the mixed products and ordered contact give
\[
 \mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_{a\omega}(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p+\rho)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u).
\]
Exhausting by these levels proves the same inequalities for the extended measures. Since $p$ is bounded, its Monge--Amp\`ere mass with respect to the Hermitian form $a\omega$ is positive by \cref{cor:bounded-mass-positive}. Integrating the inequalities proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Guedj--Lu]\label{thm:semipositive-bounded-domination}\index{Domination principle!bounded potentials}
Let $\theta\geq0$ be non-collapsing on bounded potentials. Suppose that $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^\infty(X)$ and $0\leq c<1$. If
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
 \leq c\,\mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v),
\]
then $u\geq v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the conclusion failed, we could choose $a>0$ such that $E=\{u<v-a\}$ is nonempty. This set has positive volume by \cref{lem:strict-set-positive-volume}. Fix $C$ with $|u|,|v|\leq C$.

For $b>1$ define the bounded envelope
\[
 w_b=P_\theta\bigl(bu-(b-1)v\bigr),\qquad
 D_b=\{w_b=bu-(b-1)v\}.
\]
Since $\theta\geq0$, constants below the obstacle are competitors. By the bounded contact theorem, \cref{thm:bounded-contact-support}, the measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_b)$ is concentrated on $D_b$. The potential
$b^{-1}w_b+(1-b^{-1})v$ lies below $u$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, and it equals $u$ on $D_b$ outside a pluripolar set. Ordered contact and positivity of the mixed terms give
\[
 \mathbf1_{D_b}\left[b^{-n}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_b)
              +(1-b^{-1})^n\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)\right]
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_b}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u).
\]
Choose $b$ so large that $(1-b^{-1})^n>c$. Then the hypothesis forces $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_b)=0$ on $D_b\cap\{u<v\}$. Since $\theta$ is non-collapsing, $w_b$ has positive total mass, so some of this mass is carried by $D_b\cap\{u\geq v\}$. At a point of this set outside the exceptional pluripolar set,
\[
 w_b=bu-(b-1)v=u+(b-1)(u-v)\geq-C.
\]
Consequently $\sup_Xw_b\geq-C$ for all large $b$.

On the other hand, on the fixed set $E$ the obstacle bound gives
$w_b\leq C-(b-1)a$ quasi-everywhere. Hence
\[
 \int_X|w_b-\sup_Xw_b|\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq\bigl((b-1)a-2C\bigr)\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{d}V}(E)
\]
for all sufficiently large $b$. This contradicts the uniform $L^1$ bound for normalized potentials in \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}.
\end{proof}

The exponential comparison principle follows as in \cref{cor:bounded-exponential-comparison}. For unbounded potentials, the stretched envelopes must also be kept in the required singularity class; this is the role of the relative classes introduced below.

\section{Upper and lower volumes}

Let $\theta$ be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Taking the infimum and the supremum of the mass over a singularity type gives two invariants with values in $[0,+\infty]$. They can differ, because a bounded change of potential need not preserve the mass.

\begin{definition}\label{def:singularity-volumes}\index{Singularity volume!upper and lower}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, define
\[
 \begin{split}
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
  &=\inf_{v\in\PSH(X,\theta),\ v\sim u}
       \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v),\\
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
  &=\sup_{v\in\PSH(X,\theta),\ v\sim u}
       \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v).
 \end{split}
\]
These are the \emph{lower} and \emph{upper volumes} of the singularity type of $u$ in the background $\theta$. When $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is nonempty, we write $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}(\theta)$ and $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(\theta)$ for their values at the potential $V_\theta$ with minimal singularities.
\end{definition}

\index{Positive lower mass!semipositive backgrounds}For semipositive $\theta$, the singularity type of the zero potential consists of the bounded potentials. Extending the lower mass of \cref{def:bmp} to this background, we put
\[
 v_-(\theta)=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(0)
 =\inf_{v\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^\infty(X)}
                      \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v).
\]
The condition $v_-(\theta)>0$ is stronger than the non-collapsing property used in bounded domination: it requires a single positive lower bound for the masses of all bounded potentials. Singularity volumes under a bounded mass hypothesis are compared in \cref{chap:mass-bound-volumes}.

Both volumes are invariant under bounded changes of $u$, by definition. They are also invariant under the smooth change of decomposition $(\theta,u)\mapsto(\theta+\ddc f,u-f)$, for $f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$: the map $v\mapsto v-f$ identifies the competitors, their currents and their measures. Scaling gives
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{t\theta}(tu)
  =t^n\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{t\theta}(tu)
  =t^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\quad(t>0).
\]

\section{Relative envelope classes}

Fix a reference potential $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. For $b\geq1$, the stretched obstacle $bu-(b-1)\varphi=\varphi+b(u-\varphi)$ magnifies the difference between $u$ and $\varphi$ by the factor $b$, and so becomes more singular where $u$ lies below $\varphi$. Requiring a competitor below every finite stretch defines a relative class that is stable under convex combinations and finite rooftops.

For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $b\geq1$, define the family of competitors
\[
 \mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)=
 \left\{h\in\PSH(X,\theta):
 b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})\varphi\leq u\right\}.
\]
For $b=1$ the inequality means $h\leq u$. Away from the poles of $u$ and $\varphi$, the inequality means $h\leq bu-(b-1)\varphi$. The convex combination on the left is defined everywhere, so the definition does not require a value of $bu-(b-1)\varphi$ at common poles. When $\mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)$ is nonempty, we write
\[
 Q_b(u,\varphi)=P_\theta(bu-(b-1)\varphi)
\]
for its upper envelope. Since the obstacle is finite almost everywhere, the competitors have a common upper bound by \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}, so $Q_b(u,\varphi)$ is then a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function; in particular, it is not identically $-\infty$. The upper-envelope theorem gives its defining inequality almost everywhere, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} makes it pointwise; thus $Q_b(u,\varphi)$ is the largest member of $\mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:relative-envelope-class}\index{Relative envelope class}
The \emph{relative envelope class} of $\varphi$ is
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\preceq\varphi,
       \ \mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)\ne\varnothing
       \text{ for every }b\geq1\}.
\]
\end{definition}

When the background $\theta$ is fixed, we abbreviate $\mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-elementary}\index{Relative envelope class!smooth gauge transport}
The class $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$ is convex, contains all potentials of the singularity type of $\varphi$, and is stable under bounded changes of its members. If $u$ belongs to it, $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, $u\leq v$ and $v\preceq\varphi$, then $v$ belongs to it. In particular, it is stable under maxima of two members. Replacing $\varphi$ by another member of $\PSH(X,\theta)$ of the same singularity type does not change the class.
For every smooth real function $f$,
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 u-f\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta+\ddc f,\varphi-f).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $u\geq\varphi-C$, then $h=\varphi-bC$ belongs to $\mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)$ for each $b$; hence every $u\sim\varphi$ belongs to the class. More generally, if $u'\geq u-C$ and $h\in\mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)$, then $h-bC\in\mathcal A_b(u',\varphi)$. Since the condition $u\preceq\varphi$ is also unchanged by bounded changes of $u$, the class is stable under such changes. A convex combination of two competitors is a competitor for the same convex combination of the two potentials, which proves convexity. Finally, a competitor for $u$ is a competitor for any larger $v$, and the assumption $v\preceq\varphi$ gives the remaining condition.

If $\varphi'\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $\varphi'\sim\varphi$, choose $C\geq0$ with $|\varphi'-\varphi|\leq C$ off their common pole set. For $h\in\mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)$, the function $h-(b-1)C$ belongs to $\mathcal A_b(u,\varphi')$: the required inequality holds off the poles, and it extends everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. The reverse implication follows by interchanging the references, and $u\preceq\varphi$ is equivalent to $u\preceq\varphi'$.

For the last assertion, note that $h\mapsto h-f$ is a bijection from $\PSH(X,\theta)$ onto $\PSH(X,\theta+\ddc f)$. It preserves the order $\preceq$ and transforms each stretching inequality into
\[
 b^{-1}(h-f)+(1-b^{-1})(\varphi-f)\leq u-f.
\]
This inequality is equivalent to the original one also at poles, because $f$ is smooth and finite. Thus the corresponding families of competitors are in bijection for every $b\geq1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-stretch-closure}
If $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $u\leq\varphi$, then $Q_b(u,\varphi)\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ for every $b\geq1$. More generally, if $u,w\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $u\leq w$, then $Q_b(u,w)\in\mathcal E_\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $b,s\geq1$ and put $h=Q_{bs}(u,\varphi)$. Its defining inequality gives, off the pole sets,
\[
 s^{-1}h+(1-s^{-1})\varphi\leq bu-(b-1)\varphi.
\]
The left-hand side is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, so it is at most $Q_b(u,\varphi)$. Hence $h\in\mathcal A_s(Q_b(u,\varphi),\varphi)$ for every $s$. Also $Q_b(u,\varphi)\leq u\leq\varphi$, so $Q_b(u,\varphi)\preceq\varphi$. The inequalities between convex combinations extend across the poles by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, which proves the first assertion.

For the second assertion, choose a constant $C$ such that $u-C\leq\varphi$ and $w-C\leq\varphi$. Since $Q_b(u-C,w-C)=Q_b(u,w)-C$ and class membership is unchanged by translation, we may assume $u,w\leq\varphi$. Because $u\leq w\leq\varphi$, the obstacle for $Q_b(u,w)$ lies above that for $Q_b(u,\varphi)$ and below $u$. Thus
\[
 Q_b(u,\varphi)\leq Q_b(u,w)\leq u\leq\varphi.
\]
Since $Q_b(u,\varphi)\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ by the first assertion, the stability under increase proved in \cref{prop:relative-elementary} gives the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-finite-rooftops}\index{Relative envelope class!finite rooftops}
If $u_1,\ldots,u_N\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, $N\geq1$, then the rooftop envelope $P_\theta(\min_iu_i)$ is not identically $-\infty$ and belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Adding a common constant to all $u_i$, we may assume $u_i\leq\varphi$. By \cref{lem:relative-stretch-closure,prop:relative-elementary},
\[
 h=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Q_N(u_i,\varphi)\in\mathcal E_\varphi.
\]
Each summand is at most $\varphi$. For every $i$, the defining inequality of $Q_N(u_i,\varphi)$ therefore gives
\[
 h\leq N^{-1}Q_N(u_i,\varphi)+(1-N^{-1})\varphi\leq u_i.
\]
These inequalities between convex combinations also hold at common poles. Thus $h$ is a competitor for $p=P_\theta(\min_i u_i)$, so $p$ is not identically $-\infty$, and $h\leq p\leq u_1\preceq\varphi$. Stability under increase gives $p\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, and undoing the translation preserves this conclusion.
\end{proof}

The finiteness of the family is essential: every $\varphi-j$ belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$, whereas $P_\theta(\inf_{j\geq1}(\varphi-j))\equiv-\infty$. The constructions of common envelopes for sequences in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures} require additional estimates.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-envelope-equality}
If $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, then for every smooth real function $F$,
\[
 P_{\theta,F}[u]=P_{\theta,F}[\varphi].
\]
In particular, $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[\varphi]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The relation $u\preceq\varphi$ gives $P_{\theta,F}[u]\leq P_{\theta,F}[\varphi]$. For the reverse inequality, let $a\in(0,1)$ and choose $h_a\in\mathcal A_{1/(1-a)}(u,\varphi)$, so that $a\varphi+(1-a)h_a\leq u$. Combining competitors below $F$ gives
\[
 aP_{\theta,F}[\varphi]+(1-a)P_{\theta,F}[h_a]
 \leq P_{\theta,F}[u].
\]
Indeed, if $g_1$ and $g_2$ are competitors for $P_{\theta,F}[\varphi]$ and $P_{\theta,F}[h_a]$, then $ag_1+(1-a)g_2$ lies below $F$ and is at least as singular as $a\varphi+(1-a)h_a\leq u$. Taking suprema over $g_1$ and $g_2$ gives the inequality for the unregularized envelopes. Each of these agrees with its regularization almost everywhere, so the inequality holds almost everywhere, and hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. Write $m=\min_XF$ and $M=\max_XF$. Translating competitors gives
\[
 P_\theta[h_a]+m\leq P_{\theta,F}[h_a]\leq P_\theta[h_a]+M.
\]
Every $P_\theta[h_a]$ has supremum zero, since it lies above $h_a-\sup_Xh_a$ and below zero. The uniform $L^1$ bound for normalized potentials in \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} therefore bounds the $L^1$ norms of the $P_{\theta,F}[h_a]$ uniformly in $a$. Letting $a\uparrow1$ in the first inequality gives $P_{\theta,F}[\varphi]\leq P_{\theta,F}[u]$ almost everywhere, and by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} the inequality holds everywhere.
\end{proof}

\section{Model references and positive mass}
\label{sec:relative-envelope-room}

In this section $\theta$ is semipositive and big. Suppose that a potential $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has a competitor in a smaller background $a\theta$, $0<a<1$, that is, $P_{a\theta}(u)\not\equiv-\infty$. Then the big form $(1-a)\theta$ remains available: an envelope in this background has positive mass, and the contact property transfers this mass to $u$. To obtain such competitors for all members of a relative class, we use a reference $\varphi$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:als-model-condition}
 P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi,\qquad
 P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty
 \quad\text{for some }0<\varepsilon<1.
\end{equation}
We call such a $\varphi$ a \emph{model reference}\index{Model potential!model reference} and fix one such $\varepsilon$. Note that $\varphi=P_\theta[\varphi]\leq0$. For $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, the envelope of $u-\varphi$ belongs to the relative envelope class $\mathcal E_0=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,0)$ of the zero potential, and stretching realizes it in an arbitrarily small background. Under a bounded mass hypothesis, \cref{chap:mass-bound-volumes} uses instead the condition \eqref{eq:li-model-condition}, in which a positive lower volume replaces the condition on the smaller background; the two conditions are compared there.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-difference-envelope}
Let $\varphi$ be a model reference. If $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $u\leq\varphi$, then
\[
 w=P_\theta(u-\varphi)\in\mathcal E_0,
 \qquad w\leq0,
 \qquad w+\varphi\leq u.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\varphi\leq0$, the potential $u$ lies below the obstacle $u-\varphi$ away from the pole set of $\varphi$, so it is a competitor. Hence the envelope $w$ is not identically $-\infty$. It is at most zero, since $u-\varphi\leq0$, and it satisfies $w+\varphi\leq u$ off the poles, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. For $b\geq1$, let $h_b=Q_b(u,\varphi)$. Then, off the poles,
\[
 h_b\leq bu-(b-1)\varphi\leq b(u-\varphi).
\]
Since $\theta\geq0$, the function $b^{-1}h_b$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, and so it is a competitor for $w$. Hence $h_b\leq bw$, so $\mathcal A_b(w,0)$ is nonempty for every $b$. Since also $w\leq0$, this means exactly that $w\in\mathcal E_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smaller-background-mass-lower}
Let $0<a<1$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. If $q=P_{a\theta}(u)$ is not identically $-\infty$, then
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)\geq(1-a)^n v_-(\theta),
\]
and the mass on the left is strictly positive, even when $v_-(\theta)=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The obstacle $u-q$ is nonnegative away from the poles, so by \cref{lem:bounded-below-envelope} the envelope
$p=P_{(1-a)\theta}(u-q)$ is bounded. By the contact theorem, \cref{thm:envelope-contact}, in the semipositive background $(1-a)\theta$, its measure is carried by $D=\{p+q=u\}$, and $p+q\leq u$ everywhere. Since $p+q$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, positivity of mixed products and ordered contact imply
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_{(1-a)\theta}(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p+q)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u),
\]
where all assertions are first restricted to the complement of the poles. Writing $p=(1-a)\widetilde p$ with $\widetilde p\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^\infty(X)$ and integrating, we obtain the lower bound from the definition of $v_-(\theta)$. Finally, the bounded potential $p$ has strictly positive mass by \cref{prop:big-noncollapsing}, since $(1-a)\theta$ is big.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-uniform-mass-lower}\index{Relative envelope class!positive mass}
Let $\varphi$ be a model reference, with $\varepsilon$ as in \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}. For every $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and every $0<\delta<1-\varepsilon$, the envelope $P_{(\varepsilon+\delta)\theta}(u)$ is not identically $-\infty$. Consequently,
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)\geq(1-\varepsilon)^n v_-(\theta),
 \qquad \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)>0.
\]
The mass may be infinite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After adding a constant to $u$, we may assume $u\leq\varphi$. Let $w\in\mathcal E_0$ be as in \cref{lem:relative-difference-envelope}. Since $w\in\mathcal E_0$, the envelope $P_\theta(w/\delta)$ is not identically $-\infty$, and $\delta P_\theta(w/\delta)\leq w$. The potential
\[
 P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)+\delta P_\theta(w/\delta)
 \leq\varphi+w\leq u
\]
belongs to $\PSH(X,(\varepsilon+\delta)\theta)$, so it is a competitor for $P_{(\varepsilon+\delta)\theta}(u)$. Applying \cref{lem:smaller-background-mass-lower} with $a=\varepsilon+\delta$ and letting $\delta\downarrow0$ gives the uniform estimate. The positivity follows from the same lemma with any one such $\delta$.
\end{proof}

\section{Infinite mass inside the envelope class}

We now take the Hermitian background $\theta=\omega$ and the reference $\varphi=0$. If $X$ does not have the bounded mass property, that is, if the upper mass $v_+(\omega)$ of \cref{def:bmp} is infinite, then the series constructed in \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} has infinite mass. It splits into a smooth finite sum and a tail whose curvature is bounded below by an arbitrarily small multiple of $-\omega$. Multiplying a sufficiently late tail by the stretching factor gives a competitor for that stretch, so the series lies in the relative envelope class of zero.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:infinite-envelope-class}\index{Relative envelope class!infinite mass}
If $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, the potential $U$ in \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} can be chosen so that, in addition,
\[
 U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0),\qquad P_\omega[U]=0.
\]
The same conclusions hold for $\varepsilon U$, $0<\varepsilon\leq1$. Each of these potentials has infinite extended mass and trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Use the smooth nonpositive $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions $u_j$, $j\geq1$, of \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} and their convergent series $U=\sum_{j\geq1}2^{-j}u_j$. For each integer $N\geq1$, write
\[
 U=A_N+V_N,\qquad
 A_N=\sum_{j\leq N}2^{-j}u_j,\qquad
 V_N=\sum_{j>N}2^{-j}u_j,\qquad \delta_N=2^{-N}.
\]
The head $A_N$ is smooth, and since the finite partial sums of the tail decrease, $V_N\in\PSH(X,\delta_N\omega)$; the tail is not identically $-\infty$, because $U=A_N+V_N$ is not.

Fix $b>1$ and choose $N$ so large that $b\delta_N\leq1$. Then
\[
 h_b=bV_N+\min_X(bA_N)
\]
is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic, because $\ddc h_b\geq-b\delta_N\omega\geq-\omega$. Moreover, $h_b\leq bU$. Thus $h_b\in\mathcal A_b(U,0)$ for every $b$, and since $U\leq0$, this proves $U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0)$. For $\varepsilon U$, use the tails $\varepsilon V_N$, and choose $N$ with $b\varepsilon\delta_N\leq1$. By \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality}, the singularity envelope of each of these potentials is $P_\omega[0]=0$. Their infinite mass and trivial multiplier ideals are given by \cref{cor:bmp-characterization}.
\end{proof}

The bound on the mass in \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite} concerns the envelope fixed point itself. This example shows that potentials with the same singularity envelope as a fixed point, even with trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight, need not have finite mass.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bmp-relative-finiteness}
The following conditions on a Hermitian metric $\omega$ are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $v_+(\omega)<+\infty$;
\item every member of $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0)$ has finite extended mass;
\item every $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $P_\omega[u]=0$ has finite extended mass.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $v_+(\omega)<+\infty$, all $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions have finite extended mass by \cref{prop:bmp-finite}. If $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, \cref{prop:infinite-envelope-class} contradicts each of the other two conditions.
\end{proof}
