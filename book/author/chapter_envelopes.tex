\chapter{Envelopes and volumes of singularities}
\label{chap:envelopes}

Changing the bounded part of a potential can change its Monge--Amp\`ere mass. The lower and upper volumes record the extremal masses within a singularity type. To study singularities independently of the mass of a chosen representative, we also introduce a relative class through the existence of stretched-obstacle envelopes. Convex combinations give its stability properties and identify the ordinary envelope shared by all its members.

The main analytic tool is concentration of an envelope's measure on its contact set. It yields bounded domination and transfers positivity from a smaller background to the reference class. Positivity obtained separately for each potential is distinguished from a uniform lower volume bound. At the end of the chapter, the infinite-mass construction shows why membership in the relative envelope class alone gives no finiteness of mass. These distinctions determine the hypotheses of the prescribed-singularity equations in the next chapter.

Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ on $X$. We use the singularity order of \cref{def:singularity-order}, the envelope operators of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}, and $\theta_u=\theta+\ddc u$. Throughout this chapter, $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)$ denotes the extended non-pluripolar measure of \cref{chap:products}; it agrees with the bounded product for bounded $u$. Capacities are taken with respect to $\omega$, and $a_+=\max(a,0)$ for real $a$.

\section{Contact sets}

The envelope lies below its obstacle, but its measure is supported where the two agree. For a smooth obstacle this follows by approximating the envelope with exponential equations: their right-hand sides tend to zero wherever there is a strict gap. Quasi-continuous approximation and truncation then give the measure statement for singular obstacles.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:envelope-contact}
Let $\theta\geq0$ be smooth. If $f$ is quasi-continuous and $p=P_\theta(f)$ is a genuine potential, then $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere and
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{p<f\}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p)=0.
\]
The total extended mass is allowed to be infinite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The local envelope theorem and a countable Choquet family give $p\leq f$ outside a pluripolar set. Choose $M>\sup_Xp$ and replace $f$ by $\min(f,M)$. This does not change its envelope: $p$ itself is a competitor for the smaller obstacle. It suffices to prove support on the contact set for this smaller obstacle, because $p<M$ and therefore it has the same contact set as the original one.

Set $f_j=\max(f,-j)$ and $p_j=P_\theta(f_j)$. The constants $-j$ are admissible, so $p_j$ is bounded. \Cref{thm:bounded-contact-support} gives $p_j\leq f_j$ quasi-everywhere and
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{p_j<f_j\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p_j)=0.
\]
The sequence $p_j$ decreases and stays above $p$. Its limit is a genuine $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function, lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere, and is therefore a competitor for $p$. Hence $p_j\downarrow p$.

Fix $k$ and $C>0$. Define the bounded nonnegative quasi-continuous function
\[
 H_{k,C}=\min\{1,(f-p_k)_+\}\,
                  \min\{1,(p+C)_+\}.
\]
Values on pluripolar exceptional sets are immaterial. Where this coefficient is positive, $p_k<f$ and $p>-C$. For $j\geq k$, we then have
$p_j\leq p_k<f\leq f_j$ and $p_j\geq p>-C$. Locality and \cref{thm:bounded-contact-support} imply
\[
 \int_X H_{k,C}\,
       \mathrm{MA}_\theta(\max(p_j,-C))=0.
\]
The truncated potentials are uniformly bounded and decrease to $\max(p,-C)$. Their measures converge weakly by bounded Bedford--Taylor continuity. The uniform capacity bounds and weighted convergence in \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity} also allow testing against $H_{k,C}$. Consequently,
\[
 \int_X H_{k,C}\,
       \mathrm{MA}_\theta(\max(p,-C))=0.
\]
By locality this says that the extended measure of $p$ vanishes on
$\{p_k<f\}\cap\{p>-C\}$. These sets increase to $\{p<f\}$ off the pole set when first $k$ and then $C$ tend to infinity. Continuity from below of positive measures proves the assertion, even if the total extended mass is infinite.
\end{proof}

For a big background which is not semipositive, constants no longer supply the lower truncations used in this proof. A strict singular barrier takes their place. We state the contact theorem here for use in the rooftop inequality; its proof follows the local replacement argument in \cref{thm:big-general-envelope-contact}.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:envelope-contact-big}
Let $\theta$ be smooth and big. If $f$ is quasi-continuous, finite almost everywhere, and $p=P_\theta(f)$ is a genuine potential, then $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere and
\[
\mathbf1_{\{p<f\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)=0.
\]
The extended measure may have infinite total mass. No bounded mass or positive lower-volume hypothesis is required.
\end{theorem}

Combining either contact theorem with the ordered-contact inequality of \cref{prop:ordered-contact-general} controls rooftop measures. The strict inequality in the first term below assigns the common contact set to the second term only.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rooftop-measure}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is semipositive or big, and let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Suppose $p=P_\theta(u,v)\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. The corresponding contact theorem, \cref{thm:envelope-contact,thm:envelope-contact-big}, gives
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{p=u<v\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
      +\mathbf1_{\{p=v\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The envelope is below both obstacles; their upper semicontinuity gives this inequality everywhere. Its measure is supported on $\{p=\min(u,v)\}$ by the contact theorem. Partition this set into $\{p=u<v\}$ and $\{p=v\}$. On each part apply \cref{prop:ordered-contact-general}. All measures vanish on the pluripolar pole sets, which can be discarded throughout.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fixed-point-finite}
Let $\theta\geq0$ be smooth. Every envelope fixed point $\varphi$ satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{\varphi=0\}}\theta^n.
\]
In particular, its mass is finite, without assuming bounded mass on $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We have $\varphi\leq0$. For every $j\geq0$, the envelope $P_\theta(\varphi+j,0)$ is both at least $\varphi$, since $\varphi$ is a competitor, and at most $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$. Apply \cref{prop:rooftop-measure} to obtain
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{\varphi\leq-j\}}
          \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\varphi)
      +\mathbf1_{\{\varphi=0\}}\theta^n.
\]
One must not subtract potentially infinite measures here. Instead, restrict to $\{\varphi>-t\}$ and choose $j>t$. The first term vanishes. Letting $t\to+\infty$ proves the claim by the definition of the extended measure.
\end{proof}

\section{Bounded domination in a semipositive background}

A strict measure inequality on $\{u<v\}$ forces $u\geq v$ once every bounded potential has positive mass. The proof uses the contact set of $P_\theta(bu-(b-1)v)$ to bound its supremum from below, while any fixed gap between $u$ and $v$ would force its normalized $L^1$ norm to diverge as $b\to\infty$. Only positivity for each of these bounded envelopes is needed.

Call a smooth semipositive form $\theta$ \emph{non-collapsing on bounded potentials} if
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)>0
 \quad\hbox{for every }w\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^\infty(X).
\]
Every Hermitian form has this property by \cref{cor:bounded-mass-positive}. Bigness also supplies it, by an envelope argument.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:big-noncollapsing}
If a smooth real background $\theta$ is big, every bounded $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has strictly positive Monge--Amp\`ere mass.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $a>0$ with $\theta_\rho\geq a\omega$. Normalize $\rho\leq0$ and put
\[
 p=P_{a\omega}(u-\rho).
\]
The obstacle is finite almost everywhere and bounded below, so \cref{lem:bounded-below-envelope} gives bounded $p$. The obstacle is quasi-continuous off a pluripolar set. Contact support, applied in the Hermitian background $a\omega$, gives $\mathrm{MA}_{a\omega}(p)$ carried by
$D=\{p+\rho=u\}$, outside the pole set of $\rho$.

By \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, $p+\rho\leq u$ everywhere, and
\[
 \theta+\ddc(p+\rho)
 =(a\omega+\ddc p)+(\theta_\rho-a\omega)
 \geq a\omega+\ddc p\geq0.
\]
On common finite truncation levels, positivity of the mixed products and ordered contact give
\[
 \mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_{a\omega}(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p+\rho)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u).
\]
Exhausting these levels proves the same inequalities for the extended measures. Since $p$ is bounded, its Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere mass is positive by \cref{cor:bounded-mass-positive}. Integrating proves the assertion. No estimate uniform in $u$ is claimed.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Guedj--Lu]\label{thm:semipositive-bounded-domination}
Let $\theta\geq0$ be non-collapsing on bounded potentials. Suppose $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^\infty(X)$ and $0\leq c<1$. If
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
 \leq c\,\mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v),
\]
then $u\geq v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the conclusion failed, choose $a>0$ such that $E=\{u<v-a\}$ is nonempty. This set has positive volume by \cref{lem:strict-set-positive-volume}. Fix $C$ with $|u|,|v|\leq C$.

For $b>1$ define the bounded envelope
\[
 w_b=P_\theta\bigl(bu-(b-1)v\bigr),\qquad
 D_b=\{w_b=bu-(b-1)v\}.
\]
Constants bounded below are competitors, since $\theta\geq0$. Bounded contact support gives $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_b)$ concentrated on $D_b$. The potential
$b^{-1}w_b+(1-b^{-1})v$ lies below $u$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, and equals $u$ on $D_b$ outside a pluripolar set. Ordered contact and positivity of the mixed terms give
\[
 \mathbf1_{D_b}\left[b^{-n}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_b)
              +(1-b^{-1})^n\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)\right]
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_b}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u).
\]
Choose $b$ sufficiently large that $(1-b^{-1})^n>c$. On $D_b\cap\{u<v\}$ this forces $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(w_b)=0$. Non-collapsing gives positive total mass for $w_b$, so some mass is carried by $D_b\cap\{u\geq v\}$. At a point of that set outside the exceptional pluripolar set,
\[
 w_b=bu-(b-1)v=u+(b-1)(u-v)\geq-C.
\]
Consequently $\sup_Xw_b\geq-C$, uniformly as $b\to\infty$.

On the fixed set $E$, the obstacle bound gives
$w_b\leq C-(b-1)a$ quasi-everywhere. Hence
\[
 \int_X|w_b-\sup_Xw_b|\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq\bigl((b-1)a-2C\bigr)\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{d}V}(E)
\]
for all sufficiently large $b$. This contradicts the normalized $L^1$ bound of \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}. No uniform estimate for the masses of $w_b$ was used.
\end{proof}

The same argument as in \cref{cor:bounded-exponential-comparison} now gives exponential comparison for bounded potentials in every such semipositive background. Removing boundedness requires further control: an envelope used in the proof may then have infinite mass or fail to lie in the desired singularity class.

\section{Upper and lower volumes}

The contact arguments compare measures of particular representatives. To obtain quantities attached to a singularity type, we take the infimum and supremum of the masses of all equivalent potentials. These two extrema need not coincide in a Hermitian background.

The volumes take values in $[0,+\infty]$. Their comparison theory under bounded mass is developed in \cref{chap:mass-bound-volumes}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:singularity-volumes}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, define
\[
 \begin{split}
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
  &=\inf_{v\in\PSH(X,\theta),\ v\sim u}
       \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v),\\
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)
  &=\sup_{v\in\PSH(X,\theta),\ v\sim u}
       \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v).
 \end{split}
\]
These are the lower and upper volumes of the singularity type of $u$ in the fixed background. We write $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}(\theta)$ and $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(\theta)$ for their values at $V_\theta$.
\end{definition}

For a semipositive form define
\[
 v_-(\theta)=\inf_{v\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^\infty(X)}
                      \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v).
\]
This is $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(0)$, since the potentials of ordinary type zero are exactly the bounded potentials. The inequality $v_-(\theta)>0$ is uniform positivity over all bounded potentials, which is stronger than positivity of the mass for each individual bounded potential.

The two volumes are invariant under bounded changes of $u$ by definition. They are also invariant under the smooth gauge change $(\theta,u)\mapsto(\theta+\ddc f,u-f)$, for $f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$: the map $v\mapsto v-f$ identifies the competitor currents and their measures. Scaling gives
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{t\theta}(tu)
  =t^n\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{t\theta}(tu)
  =t^n\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\quad(t>0).
\]
\section{Relative envelope classes}

Fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ and a reference $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. We require that a potential below the singularity type of $\varphi$ still admit competitors after every finite stretching away from $\varphi$. This gives a class defined by envelopes, to which convex combinations and finite rooftops apply for arbitrary smooth backgrounds.

For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $b\geq1$, define the competitor family
\[
 \mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)=
 \left\{h\in\PSH(X,\theta):
 b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})\varphi\leq u\right\}.
\]
For $b=1$ the inequality means $h\leq u$. For $b>1$ the displayed sum of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions is defined also at poles. This notation avoids assigning a value to $bu-(b-1)\varphi$ at a common pole. When the family is nonempty, its upper envelope is denoted
\[
 Q_b(u,\varphi)=P_\theta(bu-(b-1)\varphi).
\]
The finite-sublevel bound in \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} makes this a genuine potential whenever the competitor family is nonempty. The upper-envelope theorem gives the convex inequality almost everywhere, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} makes it pointwise.

\begin{definition}\label{def:relative-envelope-class}
Define
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
 =\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\preceq\varphi,
       \ \mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)\ne\varnothing
       \text{ for every }b\geq1\}.
\]
\end{definition}

Write $\mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$. The defining competitors first give convexity and stability under bounded changes. Their comparison with the volume-defined class will require the hypotheses of \cref{chap:mass-bound-volumes}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-elementary}
The class $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$ is convex, contains all potentials equivalent to $\varphi$, and is stable under bounded changes of its members. If $u$ belongs to it, $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, $u\leq v$ and $v\preceq\varphi$, then $v$ belongs to it. In particular, it is stable under taking maxima of two members.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $u\geq\varphi-C$, use $h=\varphi-bC$ for each $b$. More generally, if $u'\geq u-C$ and $h\in\mathcal A_b(u,\varphi)$, then $h-bC\in\mathcal A_b(u',\varphi)$. The singularity-order condition persists under bounded changes. Convex combinations of two competitors give competitors for the same convex combination of their target functions. Finally a competitor for $u$ is a competitor for any larger $v$; the assumed relation $v\preceq\varphi$ supplies the other defining condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-stretch-closure}
If $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $u\leq\varphi$, then $Q_b(u,\varphi)\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ for every $b\geq1$. More generally, if $u,w\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $u\leq w$, then $Q_b(u,w)\in\mathcal E_\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $u_t=Q_t(u,\varphi)$. Fix $s\geq1$ and choose $t>bs$. The defining inequality for $u_t$ shows that
\[
 \frac bt u_t+\left(1-\frac bt\right)\varphi
 \leq bu-(b-1)\varphi.
\]
The left side is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, so it is at most $u_b$. Consequently
\[
 \frac{bs}{t}u_t+\left(1-\frac{bs}{t}\right)\varphi
 \leq s u_b-(s-1)\varphi.
\]
This supplies a competitor for every $s$. Also $u_b\leq u\leq\varphi$, which supplies the singularity-order condition. All inequalities are interpreted off the pole sets and then as inequalities between the corresponding convex combinations of canonical potentials.

For the second assertion choose a constant $C$ such that $u-C\leq\varphi$ and $w-C\leq\varphi$. Translation reduces to $u,w\leq\varphi$. The obstacle for $Q_b(u,w)$ lies above that for $Q_b(u,\varphi)$ and below $u$, because $u\leq w\leq\varphi$. Thus
\[
 Q_b(u,\varphi)\leq Q_b(u,w)\leq u\leq\varphi.
\]
Upward stability of the relative class, proved in \cref{prop:relative-elementary}, gives the conclusion. Restoring the translation does not change class membership.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-finite-rooftops}
The rooftop of any nonempty finite collection of members of $\mathcal E_\varphi$ is genuine and belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $N\geq1$ and $u_1,\ldots,u_N\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, and write $p=P_\theta(\min_i u_i)$. The case $N=1$ follows from the definition. A common constant translation reduces the remaining cases to $u_i\leq\varphi$, since rooftops commute with such translations and class membership is unchanged. For $b\geq1$, put
\[
 h_b=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Q_{Nb}(u_i,\varphi).
\]
Every summand is genuine and at most $u_i\leq\varphi$. For each $i$, the defining inequality for its summand and the bounds on the others give
\[
 b^{-1}h_b+(1-b^{-1})\varphi
 \leq (Nb)^{-1}Q_{Nb}(u_i,\varphi)
              +(1-(Nb)^{-1})\varphi
 \leq u_i.
\]
These are inequalities between convex combinations of canonical potentials, so they remain meaningful at common poles. For $b=1$, $h_1$ is a competitor for $p$; hence $p$ is genuine. For every $b\geq1$, the left side is a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic competitor below each $u_i$, so it is at most $p$. Thus $h_b\in\mathcal A_b(p,\varphi)$. Together with $p\leq u_1\preceq\varphi$, this proves $p\in\mathcal E_\varphi$.
\end{proof}

The finiteness of the family is essential: every $\varphi-j$ belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$, whereas $P_\theta(\inf_{j\geq1}(\varphi-j))\equiv-\infty$. The common-envelope constructions for sequences in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures} require additional estimates.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-envelope-equality}
If $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, then
\[
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[\varphi].
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The relation $u\preceq\varphi$ gives one inequality. For $a\in(0,1)$ choose $h_a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $a\varphi+(1-a)h_a\leq u$. Combining normalized competitors for $P_\theta[\varphi]$ and $P_\theta[h_a]$ gives
\[
 aP_\theta[\varphi]+(1-a)P_\theta[h_a]\leq P_\theta[u].
\]
Indeed, their convex combination is below zero and is more singular than $u$; taking the two upper envelopes preserves the inequality by local regularization. For every potential $h$, $P_\theta[h]$ has supremum zero, because it lies above $h-\sup_Xh$ and below zero. The normalized $L^1$ bound therefore applies uniformly to $P_\theta[h_a]$. Along a sequence $a\uparrow1$, the left-hand side tends to $P_\theta[\varphi]$ in $L^1$. A subsequence gives the inequality almost everywhere, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} makes it pointwise. This proves the other inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{Model references and positive mass}
\label{sec:relative-envelope-room}

In this section, assume that $\theta$ is semipositive and big. An envelope fixed point has finite mass by \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite}. To obtain positivity throughout its relative class, we require in addition that its singularities can be realized below a smaller background:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:als-model-condition}
 P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi,\qquad
 P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty
 \quad\text{for some }0<\varepsilon<1.
\end{equation}
Fix one such $\varepsilon$. We shall prove that every member of $\mathcal E_\varphi$ admits a competitor in background $(\varepsilon+\delta)\theta$ for each $0<\delta<1-\varepsilon$. Contact support then yields individual positivity and a uniform estimate in terms of $v_-(\theta)$. Under bounded mass, the model condition in \eqref{eq:li-model-condition} instead requires positive lower volume; the comparison of the two conditions belongs to that regime.

The smaller-background competitor for $\varphi$ leaves a positive part of $\theta$ available. The difference envelope below separates that remaining part from the singular reference. Stretching within $\mathcal E_\varphi$ then allows the difference to be realized in an arbitrarily small background. Write $\mathcal E_0=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,0)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-difference-envelope}
If $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $u\leq\varphi$, then
\[
 w=P_\theta(u-\varphi)\in\mathcal E_0,
 \qquad w\leq0,
 \qquad w+\varphi\leq u.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\varphi\leq0$, the potential $u$ is a competitor for the obstacle $u-\varphi$ away from its pole set. The envelope is genuine, bounded above by zero, and satisfies the asserted sum inequality by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. For $b\geq1$, let $h_b=Q_b(u,\varphi)$. Then
\[
 h_b\leq bu-(b-1)\varphi\leq b(u-\varphi).
\]
Semipositivity makes $b^{-1}h_b$ a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic competitor for $w$. Hence $h_b\leq bw$, supplying a competitor for $P_\theta(bw)$ for every $b$. This is exactly $w\in\mathcal E_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smaller-background-mass-lower}
If $0<a<1$ and $q=P_{a\theta}(u)$ is genuine for $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)\geq(1-a)^n v_-(\theta).
\]
The mass on the left is also strictly positive individually, even if $v_-(\theta)=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The obstacle $u-q$ is nonnegative away from the poles. The bounded-below envelope lemma makes
$p=P_{(1-a)\theta}(u-q)$ bounded. Its measure is carried by $D=\{p+q=u\}$, and $p+q\leq u$ everywhere. Positivity of mixed products and ordered contact imply
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_{(1-a)\theta}(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p+q)
 \leq\mathbf1_D\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u).
\]
All assertions are first restricted away from the poles. Integrating and writing $p=(1-a)\widetilde p$ proves the lower bound from the definition of $v_-(\theta)$. The bounded potential $p$ has strictly positive mass by \cref{prop:big-noncollapsing}, since $(1-a)\theta$ is big. This proves the individual assertion as well.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-uniform-mass-lower}
Every $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ admits a genuine $P_{(\varepsilon+\delta)\theta}(u)$ whenever $0<\delta<1-\varepsilon$. Consequently
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)\geq(1-\varepsilon)^n v_-(\theta),
 \qquad \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)>0.
\]
The first bound is uniform over the class; the second is an individual assertion. Neither asserts finiteness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Translate $u$ below $\varphi$ and use $w\in\mathcal E_0$ from \cref{lem:relative-difference-envelope}. The potential
\[
 P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)+\delta P_\theta(w/\delta)
 \leq\varphi+w\leq u
\]
has background $(\varepsilon+\delta)\theta$. It supplies the asserted competitor. Apply \cref{lem:smaller-background-mass-lower} and let $\delta\downarrow0$ for the uniform estimate. The individual conclusion follows by taking any one such $\delta$.
\end{proof}

\section{Infinite mass inside the envelope class}

Return to the Hermitian background $\omega$ and reference zero. The series in \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} has smooth finite heads and tails with arbitrarily small lower curvature bounds. Those tails supply every stretched-envelope competitor. When $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, this places an infinite-mass potential in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:infinite-envelope-class}
If $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, the potential $U$ in \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} can be chosen so that, in addition,
\[
 U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0),\qquad P_\omega[U]=0.
\]
The same conclusions hold for $\varepsilon U$, $0<\varepsilon\leq1$. Each of these potentials has infinite extended mass and trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the smooth nonpositive $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions $u_j$, $j\geq1$, of \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} and their convergent series $U=\sum_{j\geq1}2^{-j}u_j$. For each integer $N\geq1$, write
\[
 U=A_N+V_N,\qquad
 A_N=\sum_{j\leq N}2^{-j}u_j,\qquad
 V_N=\sum_{j>N}2^{-j}u_j,\qquad \delta_N=2^{-N}.
\]
The head $A_N$ is smooth. The tail is nonpositive and belongs to $\PSH(X,\delta_N\omega)$, by taking decreasing limits of its finite sums. It is therefore also $\omega$-plurisubharmonic. Since $U=A_N+V_N$ and $A_N$ is smooth on compact $X$, we have $V_N\sim U$ for every $N$. Each $V_N$ is a competitor for $P_\omega[U]$, so
\[
 V_N\leq P_\omega[U]\leq0.
\]
The tails increase to zero wherever the series for $U$ is finite, hence almost everywhere. The envelope consequently equals zero almost everywhere, and thus everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.

Fix $b>1$ and choose $N$ so large that $b\delta_N\leq1$. Then
\[
 h_b=bV_N+\min_X(bA_N)
\]
is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic: its curvature is bounded below by $-b\delta_N\omega\geq-\omega$. Moreover $h_b\leq bU$. This gives a member of $\mathcal A_b(U,0)$ for every $b$, proving the relative-class assertion. For $\varepsilon U$, use the tails $\varepsilon V_N$ and choose $N$ with $b\varepsilon\delta_N\leq1$. The infinite-mass and integrability conclusions are already proved in \cref{cor:bmp-characterization}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bmp-relative-finiteness}
The following are equivalent for a Hermitian metric $\omega$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $v_+(\omega)<+\infty$;
\item every member of $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0)$ has finite extended mass;
\item every $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $P_\omega[u]=0$ has finite extended mass.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Bounded mass gives finiteness for all $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions by \cref{prop:bmp-finite}. If it fails, \cref{prop:infinite-envelope-class} contradicts each of the other two assertions.
\end{proof}

For this example the ordinary envelope is smooth and all multiplier ideals are trivial, although the representative has infinite mass. The finite-mass conclusion of \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite} applies to the envelope fixed point itself. The next chapter solves equations with finite right-hand sides; its approximation arguments must therefore control the measures in addition to keeping the potentials in a prescribed relative class.
