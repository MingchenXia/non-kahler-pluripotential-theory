\subsection{Arbitrary curve backgrounds}
\label{subsec:arbitrary-curve-product-metrics}

The separated projection formula lets the product metric depend on curve areas rather than on positivity of the chosen forms. A zero-area curve has a unique normalized potential; this smooth contribution remains in the product potential even though its metric coordinate has disappeared.

Keep $M=Y\times C_0\times Z$, the projections $p,s,r$ and $\Psi$ from \cref{cor:nonclosed-separated-product-metric}. On each $C_i$, choose a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\sigma_i$ with nonempty plurisubharmonic class, and put $B_i=\int_{C_i}\sigma_i\geq0$. For a smooth $f\geq0$ on $Y$, set
\[
 \gamma_\sigma=\sum_i\pi_i^*\sigma_i,\qquad
 \Theta_{\sigma,f}=p^*\gamma_\sigma+(f\circ p)s^*\tau+r^*\kappa.
\]
For normalized ordinary models $q_i$ in the curve backgrounds $\sigma_i$, write
\[
 \Phi_\sigma(q)=\Psi+\sum_i q_i\circ\pi_i\circ p,
 \qquad q_{i,\mathcal I}=P_{\sigma_i}[q_i]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
Let $\mathcal S_\sigma$ be this locus. All coordinates are included: when $B_i=0$, $q_i$ is the unique normalized smooth potential $u_{\sigma_i}$ of \cref{lem:curve-background-area}.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:arbitrary-product-model-projections}\index{Envelope!arbitrary curve product backgrounds}
Every $\Phi_\sigma(q)$ is a normalized ordinary model, and
\[
 P_{\Theta_{\sigma,f}}[\Phi_\sigma(q)]_{\mathcal I}
       =\Phi_\sigma(q_{\mathcal I}).
\]
The background is not big if $f=0$ or some $B_i=0$. If $f>0$ everywhere and all $B_i>0$, it is big. It is genuinely nonclosed whenever $f$ is nonconstant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $B=Y\times C_0$, let $\pi,s_0$ be the projections and put
\[
 \gamma_{\sigma,f}=\pi^*\gamma_\sigma+(f\circ\pi)s_0^*\tau,
 \qquad w=\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i.
\]
The zero-obstacle case of \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections} gives $P_{\gamma_\sigma}[w]=w$. Replace an ordinary competitor for $w\circ\pi$ by its maximum with $w\circ\pi$. Every section $Y\times\{z\}$ is then a genuine ordinary competitor for $w$, so
\[
 P_{\gamma_{\sigma,f}}[w\circ\pi]=w\circ\pi.
\]

Use the atomic Green representatives $g_i$ constructed in the proof of \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections}, including its smooth representatives at zero area. Their sum $g=\sum_i g_i\circ\pi_i\circ\pi$ is $\gamma_{\sigma,f}$-plurisubharmonic and ideally equivalent to $w\circ\pi$. Its Hessian is a closed finite-mass countable divisor current minus a smooth form. If $w_{\mathcal I}=\sum_iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i$, then $g\sim w_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi$. By \cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination}, every ideal competitor is ordinarily dominated by $g$, hence bounded by
\[
 P_{\gamma_{\sigma,f}}[g]
    =P_{\gamma_{\sigma,f}}[w_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi]
    =w_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi.
\]
Here each ideal curve projection is an ordinary model, so the proved section argument applies to $w_{\mathcal I}$. The latter is itself an ideal competitor by pullback and addition. This proves the ideal base identity. Apply \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} to $M\to B$ and the unit divisor $B\times D$. Its induced zero obstacle is zero. The two upstairs identities and independence of the factors give the assertion and normalization.

Put $n=m+1+k$ and $\Omega=p^*\beta+s^*\tau+r^*\kappa>0$, using the positive product form $\beta$ fixed earlier. For $f=0$, the closed positive test form
\[
 T_0=p^*\left(\bigwedge_j\pi_j^*\eta_j\right)\wedge r^*(\kappa^k)
\]
satisfies $\int_M\Theta_{\sigma,0}\wedge T_0=0$ and $\int_M\Omega\wedge T_0>0$. If $B_i=0$, use instead
\[
 T_i=p^*\left(\bigwedge_{j\ne i}\pi_j^*\eta_j\right)
             \wedge s^*\tau\wedge r^*(\kappa^k).
\]
Then $\int_M\Theta_{\sigma,f}\wedge T_i=0$ for every $f$, since the only surviving integral contains $\int_{C_i}\sigma_i=B_i$, while $\int_M\Omega\wedge T_i>0$. In either case a strictly positive lower bound for a current in the background contradicts Stokes' theorem against the closed test form.

Finally, write $\sigma_i=B_i\eta_i/A_i+\ddc\chi_i$ by \cref{lem:curve-background-area}. Subtracting $\sum_i\chi_i\circ\pi_i\circ p$ gives a smooth potential whose current is strictly positive when every $B_i>0$ and $f>0$. This proves bigness in that case. The identity $\mathrm d\Theta_{\sigma,f}=p^*(\mathrm df)\wedge s^*\tau$ proves nonclosedness for nonconstant $f$.
\end{proof}

For the polar curvatures $\mu_i$ of $q_i$ and $\nu_i$ of another tuple $\widetilde q$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arbitrary-product-polar-metric}
 \rho_\sigma(\Phi_\sigma(q),\Phi_\sigma(\widetilde q))
       =\sum_i\|\mu_i-\nu_i\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation}
Write $\mathcal I_\sigma\subset\mathcal S_\sigma$ for its ideal subspace and $\mu_i^{\mathrm{na}}$ for the nonatomic part of $\mu_i$.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:arbitrary-curve-product-metric}\index{Metric completeness!arbitrary curve product backgrounds}\index{Model potential!area comparison}
The metric space $(\mathcal S_\sigma,\rho_\sigma)$ is complete and geodesic, with diameter $2\sum_i B_i$. The ideal subspace is closed, geodesically convex and equal to the metric closure of the analytic models. Ideal projection is its unique nearest-point $1$-Lipschitz retraction, at distance $\sum_i\mu_i^{\mathrm{na}}(C_i)$. Metric convergence implies $L^1(M)$ convergence for every smooth positive volume form. These assertions are independent of $f$, and the top-volume comparison expression vanishes on this locus.

For another tuple of smooth admissible backgrounds $\sigma'_i$ with areas $B'_i$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arbitrary-product-area-comparison}
 \left|\sum_i(B_i-B'_i)\right|
 \leq d_{\mathrm{GH}}(\mathcal S_\sigma,\mathcal S_{\sigma'})
 \leq\sum_i|B_i-B'_i|.
\end{equation}
The same bounds hold for their ideal subspaces. If $B'_i\leq B_i$ for every $i$, both comparisons equal $\sum_i(B_i-B'_i)$. Area rescaling, with the unique normalized potential used in each zero-area target coordinate, gives a surjective $1$-Lipschitz map commuting with ideal projection. Its fibers have diameter $2\sum_{\{i:B_i>0,\ B'_i=0\}}B_i$.

Both loci are noncompact if some $B_i>0$. If all $B_i=0$, they coincide with the singleton $\{\Psi+\sum_i u_{\sigma_i}\circ\pi_i\circ p\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Restriction to coordinate sections where the other potentials are finite determines each $q_i$ up to a constant, and normalization fixes that constant. The preceding lemma and \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} therefore identify the locus with the finite product of the curve model spaces and the sum of their polar metrics. Zero-area factors are singletons. Completeness, geodesics and the diameter formula follow coordinatewise. Under this polar identification, \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness,prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect} give closedness, geodesic convexity and the unique nearest-point ideal retraction; their distances add. Fiber integration transfers the curve $L^1$ convergence to $M$.

For analytic density, approximate each atomic polar measure by finite rational atomic measures of mass at most $B_i$. Their models in background $\sigma_i$ have the corresponding finite rational poles up to bounded remainder, by the atomic Green construction and smooth-gauge transport. Zero-area coordinates are smooth. Together with the unit pole of $D$, the positive-area poles clear denominators in local product monomials, giving analytic models in the locus. They converge in \eqref{eq:arbitrary-product-polar-metric}. Conversely, an analytic product model has genuine analytic restrictions to all coordinate curve sections with the other potentials finite and the $Z$ coordinate outside $D$, by \cref{prop:analytic-type-calculus}. Each $q_i$ consequently has finite atomic polar curvature and is an ideal model by the curve classification. Closedness proves the analytic-closure assertion.

The unit-divisor reduction gives zero upper volume for every model and every pairwise maximum in this locus. For the latter use $\Psi$ plus the pullback of the maximum of the two base potentials. Thus all three terms of the top-volume comparison expression vanish, even in the nonbig backgrounds identified by the lemma.

To compare backgrounds, put $a_i=B_i/A_i$. A template curve model with polar measure $\mu_i$ of mass at most $A_i$ is sent to $p_{\sigma_i}(a_i\mu_i)$, using the classification in \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}. At $a_i=0$ this is the unique zero-area model. This construction is an isometry from the weighted locus $\mathcal S_a$ of \cref{prop:weighted-product-polar-metric} onto $\mathcal S_\sigma$ and carries their ideal subspaces to each other. Apply \cref{prop:weighted-product-metric-comparison} with $a_i=B_i/A_i$ and $b_i=B'_i/A_i$. It gives \eqref{eq:arbitrary-product-area-comparison}, the exact ordered comparison, the contractions and their fiber diameters. In curve coordinates the contraction takes
\[
 p_{\sigma_i}(\mu)\longmapsto
 \begin{cases}
 p_{\sigma'_i}((B'_i/B_i)\mu),&B_i>0,\\
 p_{\sigma'_i}(0),&B_i=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
In the second case $B'_i=0$ by the area order. Atomic projection commutes with both operations. Irrational area ratios need not preserve individual analytic models, as in \cref{thm:curve-background-homothety}; the ideal subspaces are preserved for every ratio.

If $B_i>0$, the models with polar measure $B_i\delta_x$ for infinitely many distinct $x\in C_i$, with all other coordinates fixed at their zero-polar models, give an infinite set of ideal product models separated by $2B_i$. This excludes total boundedness of both loci. At zero total area, every curve coordinate is fixed at its unique smooth normalized potential, proving the singleton assertion.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:arbitrary-product-zero-area-collapse}\index{Model potential!smooth zero-area product limit}
Suppose smooth admissible backgrounds $\sigma_{i,j}$ converge uniformly to smooth forms $\sigma_{i,0}$ of area zero on every curve. For any nonnegative smooth auxiliary coefficients $f_j$, the diameters of $\mathcal S_{\sigma_j}$ tend to zero. For every fixed smooth positive volume form on $M$, their potentials converge in $L^1(M)$ to
\[
 \Psi+\sum_i u_{\sigma_{i,0}}\circ\pi_i\circ p,
\]
uniformly over all normalized model tuples.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The areas $B_{i,j}$ tend to zero, so the diameter formula applies. By \cref{thm:curve-zero-area-collapse}, all normalized $\sigma_{i,j}$-plurisubharmonic potentials converge uniformly over that family in $L^1(C_i)$ to $u_{\sigma_{i,0}}$. Integration along the fixed compact fibers bounds each pulled-back norm by a constant times this curve norm. The finite sum gives the asserted uniform limit on $M$. The auxiliary coefficients do not enter these potentials or their metrics.
\end{proof}

The metric comparison measures the areas of the curve backgrounds, while the limiting potential retains their smooth gauges. This describes the separated locus; extending its metric to unrestricted ordinary models remains the problem in \cref{qu:i-good-no-bmp}.
