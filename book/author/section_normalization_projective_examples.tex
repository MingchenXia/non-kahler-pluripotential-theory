\begin{example}\label{ex:normalization-distance-projective}
Put $X=\mathbb P^n$ for an integer $n\geq1$ and let $\theta$ be its Fubini--Study form, normalized by $\int_X\theta^n=1$, and let $D$ be a hyperplane. Let $s_D$ be a defining section of $\mathcal O(1)$ with its Fubini--Study metric, and choose a real $t\in(0,1)$. Then $a_D=\log|s_D|^2\leq0$ after rescaling, with
\[
 \ddc a_D=[D]-\theta,\qquad
 \theta+\ddc(ta_D)=(1-t)\theta+t[D].
\]
Put $\varphi_{t,D}=P_\theta[ta_D]$. By \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, this is a model reference of ordinary type $ta_D$. Also $ta_D\in\PSH(X,t\theta)$ and $ta_D\leq\varphi_{t,D}$, so it satisfies \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}. Off $D$ its original representative has Monge--Amp\`ere measure $(1-t)^n\theta^n$, and its non-pluripolar measure puts no mass on $D$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Invariance of mass under bounded changes in the closed background gives
\[
 m_{\varphi_{t,D}}=(1-t)^n,\qquad
 B_{\varphi_{t,D}}(G)-B_0(G)=n\log(1-t)\quad(G\in C(X,\mathbb R)).
\]
Thus the constant in \cref{prop:reference-normalization-comparison} is sharp.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
On $\mathbb P^1$, choose two distinct points $p,q$ for $D$. The corresponding references have distance zero, although one is unbounded at $p$ and bounded at $q$, while the other has the reverse behavior. They are not ordinarily equivalent. For every positive integer $m$,
\[
 \mathcal I(m\varphi_{t,p})=\mathcal O(-\lfloor mt\rfloor p),\qquad
 \mathcal I(m\varphi_{t,q})=\mathcal O(-\lfloor mt\rfloor q).
\]
These identities follow from \cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}. For $mt\geq1$ the ideals differ. Thus $\Delta$ does not separate ideal types either, and cannot determine the ordinary or ideal singularity type.

Fix a smooth test $F$ on $\mathbb P^1$, and let $w_F$ be the mean-zero solution of $\theta+\ddc w_F=\mathrm{e}^F\mathrm{d}V/Z_F$. The full solutions for $D=p,q$ are
\[
 u_{t,D}=ta_D+(1-t)w_F-\sup_X\bigl(ta_D+(1-t)w_F\bigr).
\]
Their distributional difference satisfies $\ddc(u_{t,p}-u_{t,q})=t([p]-[q])\ne0$. Alternating the two divisors therefore gives two different $L^1$ subsequential limits, although the entire scalar functional and each of its supporting sets are constant. Within this fixed one-weight family, the functional does recover $t$, since $t\mapsto(1-t)^n$ is injective.
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{ex:nonclosed-normalization-nonseparation}
On $\mathbb P^n$, $n\geq2$, let $\omega$ be the Fubini--Study form and let $f$ be a nonconstant smooth real function. Fix $0<t<1$ and put
\[
 \beta=\mathrm{e}^f\omega,\qquad \theta=\beta+t\omega,\qquad \varphi_D=t\log|s_D|^2.
\]
Every hyperplane $D$ has the same positive residual $\beta$. This form is nonclosed: $\mathrm{d}\beta=\mathrm{e}^f\mathrm{d}f\wedge\omega\not\equiv0$, since wedging a one-form with $\omega^{n-1}$ is injective and $\mathrm{d}f$ is nonzero somewhere. By \cref{thm:ordinary-divisor-normalization}, every ordinary type has the same functional $B_\beta$ on all bounded tests, hence the same supporting sets. At a smooth test the common support is the unique Gauduchon adjoint probability.

If $v_F$ is the common smooth residual solution, the full solutions are the supremum normalizations of $\varphi_D+v_F$. Two distinct hyperplanes give different poles, divisorial coefficients and multiplier ideals $\mathcal O(-\lfloor kt\rfloor D)$ for sufficiently large $k$; the ideal formula is the normal-coordinate calculation of \cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}. Their potentials differ by more than a constant because their distributional Hessian difference is $t([D_1]-[D_2])$. Alternation again has two distinct $L^1$ limits. These conclusions follow from a common residual background and make no mass-only assertion for general nonclosed backgrounds.
\end{example}
