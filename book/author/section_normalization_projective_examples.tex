\begin{example}\label{ex:normalization-distance-projective}
Let $X=\mathbb P^n$ with $n\geq1$, let $\theta$ be the Fubini--Study form, normalized by $\int_X\theta^n=1$, and let $D$ be a hyperplane. Let $s_D$ be a section of $\mathcal O(1)$ defining $D$, measured with the Fubini--Study metric and rescaled so that $a_D=\log|s_D|^2\leq0$, and choose $t\in(0,1)$. Then
\[
 \ddc a_D=[D]-\theta,\qquad
 \theta+\ddc(ta_D)=(1-t)\theta+t[D].
\]
Put $\varphi_{t,D}=P_\theta[ta_D]$. By \cref{lem:one-sided-divisor-correspondence}, applied to $s_D$ with weight $t$ and residual form $(1-t)\theta$, this envelope fixed point has the ordinary type of $ta_D$. Moreover $ta_D\in\PSH(X,t\theta)$ and $ta_D\leq\varphi_{t,D}$, so $\varphi_{t,D}$ satisfies \eqref{eq:als-model-condition} with $\varepsilon=t$. Off $D$, the potential $ta_D$ has Monge--Amp\`ere measure $(1-t)^n\theta^n$, and its non-pluripolar measure puts no mass on $D$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Since mass is invariant under bounded changes of the potential in a closed background on the K\"ahler manifold $\mathbb P^n$,
\[
 m_{\varphi_{t,D}}=(1-t)^n,\qquad
 B_{\varphi_{t,D}}(G)-B_0(G)=n\log(1-t)\quad(G\in C(X,\mathbb R)).
\]
Thus the constant in \cref{prop:reference-normalization-comparison} is sharp.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Now let $n=1$, and take for $D$ two distinct points $p,q\in\mathbb P^1$. The corresponding references have distance zero, although $\varphi_{t,p}$ is unbounded near $p$ and bounded near $q$, while $\varphi_{t,q}$ behaves in the opposite way; in particular they are not ordinarily equivalent. For every positive integer $m$,
\[
 \mathcal I(m\varphi_{t,p})=\mathcal O(-\lfloor mt\rfloor p),\qquad
 \mathcal I(m\varphi_{t,q})=\mathcal O(-\lfloor mt\rfloor q),
\]
by \cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}, and for $mt\geq1$ these ideals differ. Thus the distance $\Delta$ of \eqref{eq:normalization-distance} does not even separate multiplier-ideal types, and it determines neither the ordinary nor the ideal singularity type.

Fix a smooth real function $F$ on $\mathbb P^1$, and let $w_F$ be the solution of $\theta+\ddc w_F=\mathrm{e}^F\mathrm{d}V/Z_F$ with $\int_Xw_F\,\mathrm{d}V=0$. The normalized solutions for $D=p$ and $D=q$ are
\[
 u_{t,D}=ta_D+(1-t)w_F-\sup_X\bigl(ta_D+(1-t)w_F\bigr).
\]
Their difference satisfies $\ddc(u_{t,p}-u_{t,q})=t([p]-[q])\ne0$. Hence the sequence of solutions obtained by alternating between $D=p$ and $D=q$ has two different $L^1$ cluster points, although the scalar functional and all its supporting sets are the same at every step. Within this one-parameter family, the functional does recover $t$, since $t\mapsto(1-t)^n$ is injective.
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{ex:nonclosed-normalization-nonseparation}\index{Normalization scalar!failure to separate types}
On $\mathbb P^n$, $n\geq2$, let $\omega$ be the Fubini--Study form, normalized as in \cref{ex:normalization-distance-projective}, and let $f$ be a nonconstant smooth real function. Fix $0<t<1$ and put
\[
 \beta=\mathrm{e}^f\omega,\qquad \theta=\beta+t\omega,\qquad \varphi_D=t\log|s_D|^2.
\]
Here $s_D$ defines a hyperplane $D$ as in \cref{ex:normalization-distance-projective}, and $\theta+\ddc\varphi_D=\beta+t[D]$, so every hyperplane has the same positive residual form $\beta$. This form is not closed: $\mathrm{d}\beta=\mathrm{e}^f\mathrm{d}f\wedge\omega\not\equiv0$, since $\mathrm{d}f$ is nonzero somewhere and wedging a one-form with $\omega^{n-1}$ is injective. By \cref{thm:ordinary-divisor-normalization}, the ordinary types of all $\varphi_D$ have the same functional $B_\beta$ on bounded tests, hence the same supporting sets. At a smooth test, the common supporting probability is the Gauduchon adjoint probability of \cref{thm:normalization-gauduchon-recovery}.

If $v_F$ is the common smooth residual solution, the normalized solutions are the functions $\varphi_D+v_F-\sup_X(\varphi_D+v_F)$. Two distinct hyperplanes $D_1,D_2$ give different poles, different divisorial coefficients and, for sufficiently large $k$, different multiplier ideals $\mathcal O(-\lfloor kt\rfloor D_i)$, by the calculation in normal coordinates of \cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}. The potentials differ by more than a constant, since the difference of their complex Hessians is $t([D_1]-[D_2])$, and alternating between the two hyperplanes again produces two distinct $L^1$ limits. These conclusions come from the common residual form; for general nonclosed backgrounds we do not claim that the functional is determined by a mass.
\end{example}
