\section{Failure of recovery in a positive background}
\label{sec:envelope-boundary-example}

We construct a K\"ahler background on a ruled surface over an elliptic curve and an envelope system that is not realized in this background. The obstruction is a section $D$ of the ruling whose class is nef but contains only one closed positive current, namely $[D]$. Prescribing a logarithmic pole along one fiber in the K\"ahler background leaves the class of $D$ as the residual class. In every positive perturbation of the background, this class has smooth positive representatives, so the perturbed envelopes have only the fiber pole. In the limit, however, the only positive representative left is $[D]$, so the limiting envelope acquires $D$ as an additional pole, and the residual volume tends to zero.

In this section, $E=\C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$, and $X$ is the compact ruled surface
\[
 X=(\C_z\times\mathbb P^1_w)/\sim,
 \qquad (z,w)\sim(z+1,w),\qquad
 (z,w)\sim(z+\mathrm i,w+1).
\]
These commuting transformations define a holomorphic $\mathbb P^1$-bundle $\pi\colon X\to E$. Let $D$ be its section $w=\infty$. The function $v=w-\operatorname{Im}z$ is invariant under both transformations and descends to a smooth function on $X\setminus D$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ruled-explicit-nef}\index{Nef class!rigid divisor current}
Let $\sigma_0=(\mathrm i/2)\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z$ be the flat probability form on $E$. There is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta\in c_1(\mathcal O_X(D))$ whose class is nef and for which
\[
 \kappa=\theta+\pi^*\sigma_0
\]
is a K\"ahler form. The only closed positive current of the form $\theta+\ddc u$ is $[D]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $s_D$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal O_X(D)$, with zero divisor $D$. For a real parameter $t>0$, define a smooth Hermitian metric on this line bundle by prescribing the norm of $s_D$:
\[
 |s_D|_t^2=(1+t|v|^2)^{-1}\quad\hbox{on }X\setminus D.
\]
To check that it extends smoothly, use the coordinate $\xi=1/w$ near $D$. The squared norm of a local nonvanishing frame is
\[
 \bigl(|\xi|^2+t|1-(\operatorname{Im}z)\xi|^2\bigr)^{-1},
\]
which is smooth and positive. The prescribed norm is invariant under the transformations on the complement of $D$, so these local extensions define a global metric. Its curvature form is
\[
 \theta_t=\ddc\log(1+t|v|^2)
 \quad\hbox{on }X\setminus D.
\]
Set $\theta=\theta_1$, and let $\sigma=\sigma_0/\pi$ on $E$, where the denominator is the number $\pi$.

We now estimate the curvature from below. Put $r=|v|^2$ and let $H$ be the complex Hessian of $\log(1+tr)$ in the coordinates $(z,w)$. Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 H_{w\bar w}&=\frac{t}{(1+tr)^2},\\
 H_{z\bar w}&=\frac{\mathrm i t(1-tv^2)}{2(1+tr)^2},\\
 H_{z\bar z}&=\frac{t}{2(1+tr)}
             -\frac{t^2(\operatorname{Re}v)^2}{(1+tr)^2}.
 \end{split}
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Consequently
\[
 H_{z\bar z}-\frac{|H_{z\bar w}|^2}{H_{w\bar w}}
 =\frac{t(1-tr)}{4(1+tr)}\geq-\frac t4.
\]
Completing the square gives $\theta_t\geq-(t/4)\pi^*\sigma$ on $X\setminus D$, and by continuity this inequality extends across $D$. The restriction of $\theta_t$ to every fiber is a positive Fubini--Study form, including at infinity. Thus $\theta_t+A\pi^*\sigma$ is strictly positive off $D$ whenever $A>t/4$. At $\xi=0$, its coefficient matrix, with the common factor $\mathrm i/(2\pi)$ omitted, is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix} A&\mathrm i/2\\-\mathrm i/2&1/t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its vertical coefficient is positive and its Schur complement is $A-t/4>0$, so the form is strictly positive along $D$ as well. In particular, $\kappa=\theta+\pi^*\sigma_0$ is K\"ahler.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
The function
\[
 f_t=\log\frac{1+t|v|^2}{1+|v|^2}
\]
extends smoothly across $D$ and satisfies $\theta_t=\theta+\ddc f_t$. Letting $t\downarrow0$ in the curvature lower bound shows that the class of $\theta$ is nef in the sense of \cref{sec:nef-cartier-realizations}.

Write $\psi_D=\log|s_D|_1^2$. By the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula, $\theta+\ddc\psi_D=[D]$. Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Then $u-\psi_D$ is plurisubharmonic on $X\setminus D$. Its lift $\Phi(z,w)$ to $\C^2$ is invariant under the two deck transformations and, since $u$ is bounded above on the compact surface $X$, satisfies
\[
 \Phi(z,w)\leq C+\log\bigl(1+|w-\operatorname{Im}z|^2\bigr)
\]
for some real constant $C$. For each fixed $w$, the function $z\mapsto\Phi(z,w)$ is either identically $-\infty$ or subharmonic. It is $1$-periodic and has logarithmic upper growth, so \cref{lem:periodic-subharmonic-log-growth} makes it constant. Thus $\Phi(z,w)=\phi(w)$, where $\phi$ is a subharmonic function of $w$, not identically $-\infty$. The second deck transformation gives $\phi(w+1)=\phi(w)$, and the bound at $z=0$ gives logarithmic growth, so the same lemma makes $\phi$ constant. Therefore $u=\psi_D+C$ off $D$, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} extends this equality across $D$. Hence $\theta+\ddc u=[D]$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Fix a point $p\in E$ and let $F=\pi^{-1}(p)$. The Green-kernel construction in the proof of \cref{thm:curve-trace-realization} gives a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $g$ on $E$ with $\sigma_0+\ddc g=\delta_p$. Its pullback $\psi_F=g\circ\pi$ has a logarithmic pole of coefficient one along $F$, and
\[
 \pi^*\sigma_0+\ddc\psi_F=[F].
\]

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:positive-background-system-nonrecovery}\index{Envelope system!failure of recovery}
For the K\"ahler background $\kappa$ of \cref{lem:ruled-explicit-nef} and every $a<c$, there is a system $\boldsymbol\Gamma\in\mathscr S_{a,c}(\kappa)$ for which
\[
 \mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol\Gamma)
                         \neq\boldsymbol\Gamma.
\]
Every slice $\Gamma^\eta_\tau$, $\tau<c$, of every component has multiplier ideals
\[
 \mathcal I(t\Gamma^\eta_\tau)
                 =\mathcal O_X(-\lfloor t\rfloor F)\qquad(t>0),
\]
whereas its limiting slices have multiplier ideals
\[
 \mathcal I\bigl(t(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau\bigr)
                 =\mathcal O_X(-\lfloor t\rfloor(F+D)).
\]
In particular, on the system space $\mathscr S_{a,c}(\kappa)$, which carries no mass condition, $\mathsf E$ is not surjective and $\mathsf R$ is not injective.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each Hermitian form $\eta$, the nef approximation of \cref{lem:ruled-explicit-nef} gives a smooth function $f_\eta$ such that $\theta+\eta+\ddc f_\eta\geq0$. Then
\[
 u_\eta=\psi_F+f_\eta\in\PSH(X,\kappa+\eta).
\]
Let every slice $\Gamma^\eta_\tau$, $\tau<c$, of the component $\Gamma^\eta$ be $q_\eta=P_{\kappa+\eta}[u_\eta]_{\mathcal I}$, with cutoff $c$. The multiplier ideals of $q_\eta$ are those of $\psi_F$ and do not depend on the smooth function $f_\eta$. Hence the compatibility condition follows by comparing these ideals in the larger background, and the constant curves form a system with the required parameters.

Let $q$ be its limiting slice under $\mathsf R$, and let $\nu(q,F)$ be its generic Lelong number along $F$. Each $q_\eta$ has generic Lelong number one along $F$, so \cref{lem:lelong-lone-semicontinuity}, applied to a decreasing cofinal sequence, gives $\nu(q,F)\geq1$. By \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal},
\[
 \kappa+\ddc q-[F]\geq0.
\]
By the Poincar\'e--Lelong identity for $\psi_F$, this current equals $\theta+\ddc(q-\psi_F)$, where the difference $q-\psi_F$ is first taken as a locally integrable function. Since the current is positive, this difference has a canonical $\theta$-plurisubharmonic representative, and by \cref{lem:ruled-explicit-nef} it differs from $\psi_D$ by a constant. Hence
\[
 q=\psi_F+\psi_D+C.
\]
The divisors $F$ and $D$ are smooth and meet transversely, since one is a fiber and the other a section. A direct integrability calculation in local product coordinates therefore gives the stated ideals for every real $t>0$. In particular, the Lelong number at a generic point of $D$ has increased from zero to one, and \cref{prop:hermitian-system-realization-criterion} shows that recovery fails.

Finally, since $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$, the system $\boldsymbol\Gamma$ and the realized system $\mathsf E\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma\neq\boldsymbol\Gamma$ have the same image under $\mathsf R$. Thus $\mathsf R$ is not injective, and $\boldsymbol\Gamma$ is not in the image of $\mathsf E$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonrecovery-zero-residual-volume}\index{Envelope system!zero-volume boundary}
Let $q_\eta$ be the common slice of the component $\Gamma^\eta$ constructed in the preceding proof. Along the closed perturbations $\eta=\epsilon\kappa$, with real $\epsilon>0$,
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\kappa+\epsilon\kappa}(q_{\epsilon\kappa})
 =2\epsilon\int_X\theta\wedge\kappa
                  +\epsilon^2\int_X\kappa^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
The limiting slice has zero non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The potential $u_\eta=\psi_F+f_\eta$ has analytic singularities, so \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} gives $q_\eta\sim u_\eta\sim\psi_F$. Removing the divisorial component $[F]$ gives a positive current in the background $\theta+\eta$, whose global potential $w_\eta=q_\eta-\psi_F$ is bounded by this ordinary equivalence.

Off $F$, plurifine locality identifies the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of $q_\eta$ with $(\theta+\eta+\ddc w_\eta)^2$. This bounded product gives no mass to $F$ by \cref{prop:bounded-products}. For closed $\eta=\epsilon\kappa$, Stokes' theorem gives its total mass as $\int_X(\theta+\epsilon\kappa)^2$.

The normal bundle of $D$ is trivial. Indeed, the transformation $w\mapsto w+1$ becomes $\xi\mapsto\xi/(1+\xi)$, whose derivative at $\xi=0$ is one, and the other transformation fixes $w$. Hence $\int_X\theta^2=D^2=0$, and expanding the square gives the formula. The limiting potential has curvature current $[F]+[D]$, which vanishes away from the pluripolar set $F\cup D$; its non-pluripolar measure is therefore zero.
\end{proof}
