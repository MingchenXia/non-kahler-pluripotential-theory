\subsubsection{Traces of minimizing metrics}
\label{sec:warped-minimizer-trace-limits}

Uniform limits of the base potentials do not bound the trace of the minimizing balanced metrics. Their cofactor formula contains the square of the reciprocal vertical coefficient. Keep the finite-face sequence of \cref{prop:warped-endpoint-potential-limits}, and let $\phi_0$ be the uniform limit of its base potentials. Put $A_j=b_j\kappa+\ddc\phi_j$, and write $G_j=\gamma_j^m$ for the actual minimizers in \cref{thm:warped-gauduchon-volume}. Use $h=\kappa+w$ for their trace.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:warped-minimizer-trace}\index{Gauduchon metric!finite trace boundary}
Let $r>1$ be the codimension-to-order ratio at the finite face. After discarding finitely many terms, write $L_j=s_j(f+\epsilon_j)$ with $s_j>0$, $s_j\to s_0>0$ and $\epsilon_j\to0^+$. Here $f=a$, $s_j=c_j$ at the first face, and $f=M-a$, $s_j=-c_j$ at the second. Set $J_j=\int_B\sigma/L_j^2$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:warped-minimizer-trace-mass}
 G_j=\frac{\sigma}{nL_j^2}+mA_j^{m-1}\wedge w,
 \qquad t_j:=\int_Xh\wedge G_j=\frac{J_j}{n}+mb_j^{m-1}.
\end{equation}
For $1<r<2$, $t_j\asymp\epsilon_j^{r-2}$; for $r=2$, $t_j\asymp\log(1/\epsilon_j)$. For $r>2$, the entire sequence converges weakly to the finite positive closed current
\[
 G_0=\frac{\sigma}{nL_0^2}+mA_0^{m-1}\wedge w,
 \qquad A_0=b\kappa+\ddc\phi_0,
\]
and $t_j\to t_0=\int_Xh\wedge G_0>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the unit surface, $H(c_j,e_j)=nb_j^m$, so $A_j^m=\sigma/(nL_j)$. Substitute this in \eqref{eq:warped-balanced-cofactor}. Closed Stokes on $B$ gives $\int_B\kappa\wedge A_j^{m-1}=b_j^{m-1}$, proving the trace identity. The stated endpoint factorization uses $\epsilon_j=e_j/c_j$ at the first face and $\epsilon_j=e_j/(-c_j)-M$ at the second; the coefficient limits make $s_j\to s_0>0$.

On each extreme tube, $f\asymp\operatorname{dist}(Z_i)^{k_i}$. The reciprocal-square integral is therefore comparable to
\[
 \int_0^{\varrho_0}\frac{\varrho^{\operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb R}Z_i-1}}{(\varrho^{k_i}+\epsilon_j)^2}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho,
\]
where $\varrho$ is normal radius, $\varrho_0>0$ is small and $r_i=\operatorname{codim}_{\mathbb R}Z_i/k_i$. Splitting at $\varrho=\epsilon_j^{1/k_i}$ gives order $\epsilon_j^{r_i-2}$ for $r_i<2$, order $\log(1/\epsilon_j)$ for $r_i=2$, and a finite limit for $r_i>2$. The finite sum is governed by $r=\min_i r_i$. Since $b_j$ and $s_j$ have positive finite limits, these estimates prove the trace alternatives.

If $r>2$, $f^{-2}$ is integrable and dominates $L_j^{-2}$ up to a fixed constant. Hence $\sigma/L_j^2\to\sigma/L_0^2$ in total variation. \Cref{prop:warped-endpoint-potential-limits} gives weak convergence of $A_j^{m-1}\wedge w$: wedge its $(m-1)$st current limit with $w$, whose square is zero. This proves $G_j\rightharpoonup G_0$, positivity and closedness. Pairing with $h$ gives the trace limit, which is positive by the displayed reciprocal-square term.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:warped-normalized-test-limits}\index{Gauduchon metric!concentrating trace}\index{Gauduchon current!warped fiber limit}
Suppose $1<r\leq2$, and put $T_j=G_j/t_j$. Every weak cluster limit has the form $p_B^*\nu$, where $\nu$ is a probability measure supported on the extreme components with minimal ratio $r$. Such a limit is positive and closed, has trace one relative to $h$, and satisfies $\theta_0\wedge p_B^*\nu=0$. If those components reduce to a single point $p$, the entire sequence converges to the integration current $[\{p\}\times C]$.

The trace-normalized smooth metrics $\eta_j=t_j^{-1/m}\gamma_j$ satisfy
\[
 \int_X\theta_j\wedge\eta_j^m=D_\mu(\eta_j)
                  =\frac{nb_j^m}{t_j}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Both quantities have order $\epsilon_j^{2-r}$ when $1<r<2$, and order $1/\log(1/\epsilon_j)$ when $r=2$. Their quotient remains one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\nu_j=\sigma/(J_jL_j^2)$. These are base probability measures, and
\[
 T_j=\frac{J_j}{nt_j}p_B^*\nu_j
                   +\frac m{t_j}A_j^{m-1}\wedge w.
\]
The first coefficient tends to one. The second positive current has trace $mb_j^{m-1}/t_j\to0$, hence converges weakly to zero. Base probability compactness and weak continuity of submersion pullback describe every cluster limit. Away from small extreme tubes the density numerator is uniformly bounded, while $J_j\to\infty$; that region receives limiting mass zero. The tube integrals at ratios greater than $r$ are $o(J_j)$ by the preceding estimates, so only minimal-ratio components retain mass.

For a limit $T=p_B^*\nu$, base degree gives $\kappa\wedge T=0$, while $L_0w\wedge T=0$ because $L_0$ vanishes on the support of $\nu$. Its trace is one since $\int_Cw=1$. The base top-degree measure is closed, so its pullback is closed as well. If the support is the single point $p$, every cluster probability is $\delta_p$; compactness then gives whole-sequence convergence, and $p_B^*\delta_p$ is the stated fiber integration current.

The minimizing quotient is one on $\mathscr S$, and both its numerator and denominator before trace normalization equal $nb_j^m$. Each scales by $t_j^{-1}$. The trace estimates give their limits and rates.
\end{proof}

In the projective strip of \cref{ex:warped-projective-strip}, both ratios are two and each extreme set is a single point. The two normalized minimizing tests therefore converge to the corresponding curve-fiber currents, with reciprocal-logarithmic numerator and denominator decay. This is current convergence; it does not give a pointwise limit of the metrics.
