\subsection{Entropy for a prescribed ordinary type}

Return to the standing setting: $\theta$ is semipositive and big, and $\varphi$ is a model reference, with $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ as in \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}. The part $(1-\varepsilon)\theta$ of the background left over by the smaller-background envelope gives a uniform lower bound for the density norm, while comparison in a larger Hermitian background gives the upper bound. Together they extend the entropy estimates to the prescribed type.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:prescribed-dual-entropy}
For every $q>1$ there is a finite real $a_q$, and there is a finite real $A$, such that
\[
 a_q+\frac1q\operatorname{Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu)
 \leq H_\varphi(\nu)
 \leq A+n\operatorname{Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu)
\]
for every Borel probability measure $\nu$, including infinite values. Thus $H_\varphi(\nu)$ is finite exactly when $\nu$ has finite entropy, and uniformly bounded dual values are equivalent to uniformly bounded entropies. For fixed $\theta$ and $\varepsilon$, the constants can be chosen independently of the model reference $\varphi$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $G\in C(X)$, let $u\sim\varphi$ solve the equation with density $c\mathrm{e}^G$, where $c=\mathrm{e}^{B_\varphi(G)}$. The lower bound $b_q>0$ of \cref{lem:relative-density-lower-bound} for the $L^q$ norm of the density gives $c\|\mathrm{e}^G\|_q\geq b_q$, hence
\[
 B_\varphi(G)\geq\log b_q-\frac1q
                         \log\int_X\mathrm{e}^{qG}\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]

For the upper bound, choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta$, and let $B_{\Omega,0}$ and $H_{\Omega,0}$ denote its normalization scalar and dual functional for the zero reference. Let $v$ be a bounded solution for $G$ in this background; being bounded, it has a smaller-background envelope $P_{\delta\Omega}(v)\geq\inf_Xv$ for every $0<\delta<1$. Moreover $u\in\PSH(X,\Omega)$, $u\preceq v$, and positivity gives
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Omega(u)
       \geq\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
       =c\mathrm{e}^G\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
If $c>\mathrm{e}^{B_{\Omega,0}(G)}$, the singular strict domination principle, applied in the background $\Omega$ to $v$ and $u+t$ for every real $t$, would give $v\geq u+t$ for all $t$, which is impossible. Hence $B_\varphi\leq B_{\Omega,0}$, and therefore $H_\varphi\leq H_{\Omega,0}$; \cref{prop:hermitian-dual-entropy-bounds} now gives the upper bound.

The lower scalar estimate gives the lower bound with $a_q=\log b_q$, exactly as in the proof of \cref{prop:hermitian-dual-entropy-bounds}, and the finite-domain and bounded-family assertions follow by taking $q=2$. Finally, \cref{lem:relative-density-lower-bound} is uniform in the reference for fixed $\theta$ and $\varepsilon$, and the upper bound uses only $\Omega$, which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

This gives compactness when the reference varies. Let the model references $\varphi_j$ satisfy the smaller-background condition with the same $\varepsilon$, let $G_j\in L^\infty(\mathrm{d}V)$ satisfy $\|G_j\|_\infty\leq M$ for a fixed constant $M$, and choose $\nu_j\in\mathcal A_{\varphi_j}(G_j)$. With $\Omega$ as in the proof,
\[
 H_{\varphi_j}(\nu_j)
 =B_{\varphi_j}(G_j)+\int_XG_j\,\mathrm{d}\nu_j
 \leq B_{\Omega,0}(0)+2M.
\]
The uniform lower estimate of \cref{cor:prescribed-dual-entropy} bounds their entropies, and the Hermitian upper estimate then places all $\nu_j$ in a single finite sublevel set of $H_{\Omega,0}$. Hence \cref{thm:normalization-dual-compactness}, applied in the background $\Omega$, gives weak compactness and uniform integrability of their densities, and every weak limit has finite entropy. Identifying such a limit as a supporting probability for a limiting reference requires further information.
