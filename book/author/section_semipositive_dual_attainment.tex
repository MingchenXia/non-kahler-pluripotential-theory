\subsection{A smooth maximizing form with an analytic degeneracy set}
\label{subsec:semipositive-dual-setting}

We now allow the smooth maximizing form to degenerate. Where it is strictly positive, the argument of \cref{cor:smooth-dual-exact-equation} still determines the solution current and gives the equation. If the degeneracy set is a proper analytic subset, neither side of the equation charges it, so the equation holds on all of $X$. Uniqueness follows by passing to the maximum of two threshold subsolutions and applying \cref{lem:ordered-analytic-rigidity}.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation}\index{Gauduchon duality!semipositive attainment}
Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero, and put $b=b_\omega(f)$. Suppose that $D_{f^{1/n}}$ has a smooth semipositive maximizer $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$ that is strictly positive on $X\setminus Z$, where $Z$ is a proper analytic subset of $X$. Then $b>0$, and every threshold subsolution $u$ is smooth on $X\setminus Z$ and satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)=bf\,\mathrm{d}V
\]
on $X$. In particular a threshold solution exists. The function $f^{1/n}$ has a smooth nonnegative representative on $X\setminus Z$, and any two threshold subsolutions differ by a constant.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $p=f^{1/n}$. For a smooth semipositive form $T$ with coefficient matrix $P'$ relative to $\omega^n$, \eqref{eq:weak-gaud-functional} gives $k_T\,\mathrm{d}V=f_0(\det P')^{1/n}\omega^n$, so that $k_T=f_0^{1-n}(\det P')^{1/n}$ is a continuous function of that matrix. In particular $k_T$ is bounded, so $D_p(T)<+\infty$, and since $T$ maximizes, \eqref{eq:integrable-current-supremum} gives $\rho_\omega(p)=D_p(T)<+\infty$, that is, $b>0$. Let $u$ be any threshold subsolution, which exists by \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}, and put $A=\omega+\ddc u$. Fix a smooth strictly positive $T_0\in\mathscr G_\omega$, and for real $\varepsilon>0$ set $T_\varepsilon=(T+\varepsilon T_0)/(1+\varepsilon)$. Each $T_\varepsilon$ is a smooth strictly positive member of $\mathscr G_\omega$, so \cref{prop:threshold-trace-saturation} gives
\[
 A\wedge T_\varepsilon\geq b^{1/n}p k_{T_\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
By the formula for $k_T$ above, $k_{T_\varepsilon}\to k_T$ uniformly on the compact manifold $X$. Since $p\in L^1$, passing to the limit gives
\[
 A\wedge T\geq b^{1/n}p k_T\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
The left side has mass one by Stokes' theorem and $\ddc T=0$, and the right side has mass $b^{1/n}D_p(T)=1$ because $T$ maximizes. Their difference is positive and has mass zero, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semipositive-dual-trace-equality}
 A\wedge T=b^{1/n}p k_T\,\mathrm{d}V.
\end{equation}

On $U=X\setminus Z$ write $T=\gamma^{n-1}$, where $\gamma$ is smooth and strictly positive, and set $a=(bf\,\mathrm{d}V/\gamma^n)^{1/n}$. The trace equality becomes $A\wedge\gamma^{n-1}=a\gamma^n$ on $U$. On relatively compact subsets of $U$, it controls every coefficient measure of $A$ by an $L^n$ density. The trace inequalities for all Hermitian forms and the matrix arithmetic--geometric mean inequality, used exactly as in the proof of \cref{cor:smooth-dual-exact-equation}, force $A=a\gamma$ on $U$; this uses only the positivity of $\gamma$ on $U$, since the Hermitian test forms needed there are global forms obtained by extension from smaller coordinate charts.

The local regularity lemma \cref{lem:smooth-conformal-current} makes $u$ and $a$ smooth on $U$. Taking the top power proves the equation on $U$, and $f^{1/n}=b^{-1/n}a(\gamma^n/\mathrm{d}V)^{1/n}$ is smooth and nonnegative there. Both $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)$ and $f\,\mathrm{d}V$ put no mass on the pluripolar set $Z$. Hence the equality holds on $X$.

It remains to prove uniqueness. Let $v$ be another threshold subsolution and put $w=\max(u,v)$. By \cref{prop:ordered-contact-general},
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(w)\geq
 \mathbf1_{\{u\geq v\}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)
 +\mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(v)
 \geq bf\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Here the pole sets carry no mass for any of the measures involved. Thus $w$ is also a threshold subsolution, and the preceding argument gives the same smooth current $\omega+\ddc u=\omega+\ddc v=\omega+\ddc w=a\gamma$ on $U$.

We apply \cref{lem:ordered-analytic-rigidity} to $u\leq w$ and to $v\leq w$: their complex Hessians agree on $X\setminus Z$, so $w-u$ and $w-v$ are constant. Hence $u-v$ is constant.
\end{proof}
