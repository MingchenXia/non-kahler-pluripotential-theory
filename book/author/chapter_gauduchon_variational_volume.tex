\chapter{A Gauduchon variational volume}
\label{chap:gauduchon-variational-volume}

\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, which we call the \emph{background}, and let $\mu$ be a smooth strictly positive probability form on $X$. If $\theta+\ddc v>0$ for some smooth real function $v$, there is exactly one constant $c_\theta(\mu)>0$ for which the Monge--Amp\`ere equation $(\theta+\ddc u)^n=c_\theta(\mu)\mu$ has a smooth solution $u$ with $\theta+\ddc u>0$. When $\theta$ is closed, Stokes' theorem gives $c_\theta(\mu)=\int_X\theta^n$, independently of $\mu$. For a nonclosed background the total mass $\int_X(\theta+\ddc u)^n$ can depend on the potential, and the constant is the one number that the equation attaches to the pair $(\theta,\mu)$. By \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}, its $n$th root is the infimum of the quotients $\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}/D_\mu(\gamma)$ over Gauduchon metrics $\gamma$. For every pseudoeffective background this infimum is the root volume $\lambda_\mu(\theta)$, and $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta)=\lambda_\mu(\theta)^n$ is the Gauduchon variational volume of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume}. We keep the notation $D_\mu$, $\mathscr P$, $\Omega_\theta$ and $c_\theta(\mu)$ of \cref{sec:gaud-volume-definition}, where the elementary properties of $\lambda_\mu$, its dependence on $\mu$ and its extrema over $\mu$ are proved. This chapter studies the volume as a function of the background.

The root volume is concave. On the positive cone, a minimizing Gauduchon metric computes its derivative, and the Hessian of its negative logarithm defines a Riemannian metric on background classes in each finite-dimensional family. Invariant forms on compact quotients of complex Lie groups and warped products with a curve show that the unit-volume hypersurface of this metric may be complete or may acquire a boundary at finite distance.

For $n\geq2$, the same quotient computes the subsolution threshold $b_\omega(f)$ for a Hermitian background $\omega$ and an integrable density $f$. Allowing positive $\ddc$-closed currents as tests makes this dual problem compact. For continuous densities the maximizing currents represent exactly the supporting probabilities of the normalization functional; for integrable densities, a uniform tail condition passes the determinant integral to weak limits and again gives a maximizing current. A maximizing test that is a smooth strictly positive form gives more: equality in the trace inequality determines the solution current and forces an exact smooth equation. The exact equation persists when the maximizing form degenerates along a proper analytic set, whereas a threshold solution with a point pole can admit no smooth maximizing test at all. If the solution forms $\omega+\ddc v$ are uniformly comparable to $\omega$, their Gauduchon multipliers and the determinant densities of the corresponding exact maximizing currents are bounded above and below uniformly, without bounds on derivatives of the complex Hessian. The determinant measures of Gauduchon currents do not depend on the auxiliary Hermitian form. When a Hermitian form $\beta$ satisfies $\ddc(\beta^j)=0$ for $1\leq j\leq n-1$, the normalized Gauduchon currents of every $\beta+\ddc u>0$, their determinant measures and a sharp bound for them depend only on $\beta$; examples on a flat torus show that these data cannot detect a loss of ellipticity, and that exact maximizers for varying densities can concentrate.

The last part of the chapter concerns the boundary of the positive cone and the lower and upper Monge--Amp\`ere masses, which are the extrema of the volume over $\mu$. At a nef background the variational volume is the limit of the smooth equation constants of nearby positive backgrounds. On a compact K\"ahler manifold, along a path of backgrounds ending at a nef point, a closed nef core of positive top intersection keeps the root volume positive up to the endpoint, and the decrease of the root volume is measured by a subtraction cost; this cost is also computed on warped products whose endpoint is not big and for a residual on a ruled surface. A projective radial model gives positive variational volume without bigness, even at the finite maximal time of a Chern--Ricci flow; along tests approaching the bigness threshold its determinant denominators collapse, as they must for every such background. On threefolds, curvature conditions characterize the smooth potentials of extremal mass, and they give the upper mass of invariant backgrounds on compact Lie-group quotients. A threefold with zero lower mass shows that positivity for each smooth density need not be uniform over densities; it is a primitive elliptic bundle, and on every such bundle the upper mass is computed exactly and separated potentials keep a positive mass. On an elliptic threefold, by contrast, a nonnegative sum of curvature squares gives a bounded cubic energy identity and hence a positive lower bound for every potential, even when the manifold admits no pluriclosed metric. Finally, nef degeneration gives a further contrast with closed K\"ahler classes: intersection polynomials are replaced by fractional, logarithmic or oscillating decay, and a single blowup can change its order.

Pseudoeffective and big backgrounds are those of \cref{sec:gaud-volume-definition}. Throughout the chapter, $\theta$ is called \emph{nef} if, for a Hermitian form $h$ and every $\epsilon>0$, there is a smooth real $f_\epsilon$ with $\theta+\ddc f_\epsilon\geq-\epsilon h$; this is the condition of \cref{sec:nef-cartier-realizations} for the affine class of $\theta$ in \cref{sec:affine-model-space}. Like pseudoeffectivity and bigness, nefness does not require $\theta$ to be closed and is unchanged by a smooth $\ddc$ change of $\theta$.

\section{The equality case of concavity and the first variation}

Since $\lambda_\mu$ is an infimum of linear functions, each minimizing Gauduchon metric at $\theta$ defines a linear functional that supports $\lambda_\mu$ at $\theta$. If equality holds in the concavity inequality along a segment, a metric minimizing at an interior point must minimize at both endpoints, and \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula} then makes the two endpoint solution forms proportional. Differentiating the smooth equation shows that the supporting functional is the derivative of $\lambda_\mu$. For $\theta\in\mathscr P$ we write $\gamma_\theta$ for a minimizing Gauduchon metric, which exists by \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}; variation directions are smooth real $(1,1)$-forms.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gaud-volume-concavity-equality}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!concavity and equality}
Let $\theta_0,\theta_1\in\mathscr P$ and $0<s<1$. Equality holds in the concavity inequality
\[
 \lambda_\mu((1-s)\theta_0+s\theta_1)
 \geq(1-s)\lambda_\mu(\theta_0)+s\lambda_\mu(\theta_1)
\]
if and only if, for some $r>0$ and some smooth real $f$,
\[
 \theta_1=r\theta_0+\ddc f.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $n\geq2$ and that equality holds. Take a minimizing Gauduchon metric for the middle background $(1-s)\theta_0+s\theta_1$. Its quotient at the middle background is the same convex combination of its quotients at $\theta_0$ and $\theta_1$, and each of these is at least the corresponding infimum. Equality therefore forces this metric to minimize both endpoint quotients.

By \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}, the solution forms $\Omega_i=\Omega_{\theta_i}$, with constants $c_i=c_{\theta_i}(\mu)$ for $i=0,1$, are then both conformal to this metric. Their volume forms are $c_0\mu$ and $c_1\mu$, so their pointwise proportionality factor is the constant $r=(c_1/c_0)^{1/n}$. Hence $\Omega_1=r\Omega_0$, which gives the stated smooth $\ddc$ relation. Conversely, if that relation holds, invariance under smooth $\ddc$ changes and homogeneity give equality.

When $n=1$, the function is $\theta\mapsto\int_X\theta$. Take $r=\int_X\theta_1/\int_X\theta_0$. The difference $\theta_1-r\theta_0$ has integral zero, so the Green operator of the compact curve writes it as $\ddc f$ with $f$ smooth. Thus the same equivalence holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-volume-first-variation}
The function $\lambda_\mu$ is smooth along smooth finite-dimensional families in $\mathscr P$. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\chi$ and $n\geq2$, its derivative at $\theta$ is
\[
 (\mathrm{d}\lambda_\mu)_\theta(\chi)
 =\frac{\displaystyle\int_X\chi\wedge\gamma_\theta^{n-1}}
        {D_\mu(\gamma_\theta)},
\]
where $\gamma_\theta$ is any minimizing Gauduchon metric. In dimension one the derivative is $\int_X\chi$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a smooth potential $u_*$ with $\theta+\ddc u_*>0$. Backgrounds $\theta_t$ near $\theta$ then have the positive representatives $\theta_t+\ddc u_*$. The implicit-function argument in the proof of \cref{prop:smooth-background-potential-stability} applies to this family and shows that the solution and its constant depend smoothly on the parameters. Adding $u_*$ to these solution potentials and fixing the mean gives the normalized solutions for $\theta_t$, so $\lambda_\mu(\theta_t)=c_{\theta_t}(\mu)^{1/n}$ is smooth.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
To compute the derivative, write $\lambda=\lambda_\mu(\theta)$ and $\Omega=\Omega_\theta$, and write the minimizing metric as $\gamma_\theta=\mathrm{e}^g\Omega$ with $g$ smooth and real, as \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula} permits. Along $\theta_t=\theta+t\chi$ for small real $t$, put $\Omega_t=\theta_t+\ddc u_t$ and $\lambda_t=\lambda_\mu(\theta_t)$, and let dots denote derivatives at $t=0$. Differentiating $\Omega_t^n=\lambda_t^n\mu$ gives
\[
 (\chi+\ddc\dot u)\wedge\Omega^{n-1}
                   =\lambda^{n-1}\dot\lambda\,\mu.
\]
We multiply by $\mathrm{e}^{(n-1)g}$, so that $\Omega^{n-1}$ becomes $\gamma_\theta^{n-1}$, and integrate. The term with $\ddc\dot u$ vanishes by the Gauduchon condition, and $\int_X\chi\wedge\gamma_\theta^{n-1}=\lambda^{n-1}\dot\lambda\int_X\mathrm{e}^{(n-1)g}\mu$. On the other hand, $\gamma_\theta^n=\mathrm{e}^{ng}\lambda^n\mu$, so that
\[
 D_\mu(\gamma_\theta)
       =\lambda^{n-1}\int_X \mathrm{e}^{(n-1)g}\mu.
\]
Dividing the two identities proves the derivative formula. In dimension one the formula follows directly from the definition.
\end{proof}

\section{Nef limits and nonattainment}
\label{sec:gaud-volume-nef-limit}

We return to arbitrary dimension $n\geq1$. If $\theta$ is nef and $h$ is a Hermitian form, then the backgrounds $\theta+\epsilon h$ lie in $\mathscr P$ for every $\epsilon>0$, directly from the definition of nefness; their volumes are therefore scalars of smooth equations. As $\epsilon$ decreases to zero, the infimum over Gauduchon metrics commutes with the decreasing limit of the background. Thus the boundary value is determined by smooth equations, even when no metric attains it.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gaud-volume-nef-limit}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!nef limit}
For every nef background $\theta$ and every Hermitian form $h$,
\[
 \lambda_\mu(\theta)
     =\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\lambda_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h),
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta)
     =\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}c_{\theta+\epsilon h}(\mu).
\]
Both quantities on the right decrease as $\epsilon\downarrow0$, and their limits do not depend on $h$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $0<\epsilon'<\epsilon$ the difference of the two backgrounds is the positive form $(\epsilon-\epsilon')h$, so the monotonicity in \cref{prop:gaud-volume-elementary} shows that $\lambda_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h)$ decreases as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Its limit is therefore the infimum over $\epsilon>0$. Infima over $\epsilon>0$ and over Gauduchon metrics commute, and for a fixed metric the quotient is affine in $\epsilon$. Hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 \inf_{\epsilon>0}\lambda_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h)
 &=\inf_\gamma\inf_{\epsilon>0}
   \frac{\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
                +\epsilon\int_Xh\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}
        {D_\mu(\gamma)}\\
 &=\lambda_\mu(\theta).
 \end{split}
\]
Here the second equality holds because the coefficient of $\epsilon$ is positive, so the inner infimum is the value at $\epsilon=0$. This proves the first identity, and its right side is the definition of $\lambda_\mu(\theta)$, which does not involve $h$. The second identity follows by raising the first to the $n$th power, since $\lambda_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h)^n=c_{\theta+\epsilon h}(\mu)$ by \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}.
\end{proof}

If $X$ is K\"ahler and $\theta$ is closed and nef, choose $h$ K\"ahler. Each perturbed solution form is then closed and cohomologous to $\theta+\epsilon h$, so integrating its equation and applying Stokes' theorem gives
\[
 c_{\theta+\epsilon h}(\mu)=\int_X(\theta+\epsilon h)^n.
\]
Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$, we obtain $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta)=\int_X\theta^n$ for every closed nef $\theta$ on a compact K\"ahler manifold and every $\mu$. We refer to this as the \emph{closed nef formula}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zero-gaud-volume-attainment}
Suppose $\theta$ is pseudoeffective and $\lambda_\mu(\theta)=0$. The infimum in \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} is attained if and only if $\theta=\ddc f$ for a smooth real function $f$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $D_\mu(\gamma)>0$, a metric $\gamma$ attaining the value zero satisfies $\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}=0$. By \cref{lem:zero-gauduchon-degree}, including its dimension-one case, $\theta$ is then smoothly $\ddc$-exact. Conversely, if $\theta=\ddc f$, Stokes' theorem makes every quotient zero, so every admissible metric attains the infimum.
\end{proof}

For example, let $X$ be the ruled surface and $D$ the section of \cref{lem:ruled-explicit-nef}. The class $c_1(\mathcal O_X(D))$ is nef, has zero square, and contains the nonzero positive current $[D]$. By the closed nef formula its volume is zero. A smooth $\ddc$-exact representative would make $[D]$ itself $\ddc$-exact, which is impossible because $\int_D\kappa>0$ for a K\"ahler form $\kappa$. By \cref{prop:zero-gaud-volume-attainment}, no Gauduchon metric minimizes the defining quotient.

\section{Volume and the bigness threshold}
\label{sec:gaud-volume-bigness-threshold}

In this section $X$ is again an arbitrary connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$. The root volume and the threshold of the Gauduchon criterion for bigness divide the same pairing by different quantities. Positive volume can therefore fail to imply bigness only if these denominators separate along tests approaching the threshold, and the following proposition isolates this degeneration. For the radial background of the next section it occurs, by \cref{prop:radial-localized-balanced-tests}.

Fix a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$ and a smooth strictly positive probability form $\mu$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth pseudoeffective real $(1,1)$-form. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, abbreviate the pairings by
\[
 A_\theta(\gamma)=\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1},
 \qquad A_h(\gamma)=\int_Xh\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\]
The threshold $\kappa_h(\theta)$ of \cref{cor:gauduchon-threshold} is the infimum of the quotient $A_\theta/A_h$ over Gauduchon metrics, and it is positive exactly when $\theta$ is big; we call it the \emph{bigness threshold}. The root volume $\lambda_\mu(\theta)$ is the infimum of $A_\theta/D_\mu$, with the determinant denominator $D_\mu(\gamma)$ of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume}.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-positive-volume-collapse}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!collapse}
Let $h$, $\theta$ and $\mu$ be as above, and let $(\gamma_j)$ be a sequence of Gauduchon metrics.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose $n\geq2$, $\lambda_\mu(\theta)>0$, and $\theta$ is not big. If
\[
 A_h(\gamma_j)=1,\qquad A_\theta(\gamma_j)\longrightarrow0,
\]
then $D_\mu(\gamma_j)\to0$. Such sequences exist.
\item More generally, fix a real number $\delta>0$. If $A_h(\gamma_j)=1$ and
\[
 A_\theta(\gamma_j)\longrightarrow\kappa_h(\theta),
 \qquad \liminf_jD_\mu(\gamma_j)\geq\delta>0,
\]
then
\[
 \kappa_h(\theta)\geq\delta\lambda_\mu(\theta).
\]
In particular, positive root volume then implies bigness.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\theta$ is not big. Since $\theta$ is pseudoeffective, the threshold criterion of \cref{cor:gauduchon-threshold} gives $\kappa_h(\theta)=0$, so there are Gauduchon metrics whose quotients $A_\theta/A_h$ tend to zero. Multiplying a Gauduchon metric by a positive constant multiplies $A_\theta$, $A_h$ and $D_\mu$ by the same factor; for $n\geq2$ this factor can be chosen to make $A_h=1$, without changing either quotient. This gives the sequences in the first assertion. By the definition of the root volume, every Gauduchon metric satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-collapse-basic}
 \lambda_\mu(\theta)D_\mu(\gamma)\leq A_\theta(\gamma).
\end{equation}
Dividing by the positive root volume proves the first assertion. Applying the same inequality to the sequence in the second assertion and taking lower limits gives $\delta\lambda_\mu(\theta)\leq\kappa_h(\theta)$. If the root volume is positive, then $\kappa_h(\theta)>0$, and the threshold criterion gives bigness.
\end{proof}

The quotient $D_\mu/A_h$ is always bounded above: \eqref{eq:gaud-collapse-basic} with $\theta=h$ gives
\[
 D_\mu(\gamma)\leq\lambda_\mu(h)^{-1}A_h(\gamma),
\]
where $\lambda_\mu(h)>0$ by \cref{cor:gaud-volume-big-positive}.
