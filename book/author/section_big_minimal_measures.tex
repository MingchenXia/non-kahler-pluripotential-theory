\section{Continuity for smoothed data}
\label{sec:big-measure-smoothing}

Smooth the measure by the coordinate convolution of \cref{subsec:psh-measure-smoothing}. Its integral estimates, combined with the higher-moment bound, give convergence against the original measure. The resulting self-measure error then yields capacity convergence, and a first-moment tail estimate identifies the Monge--Amp\`ere limit.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:big-smoothed-measure-continuity}
Let $\nu$ be a non-pluripolar probability measure with $A_\theta(\nu)<\infty$, and let $\nu_j$ be its smooth approximations from \eqref{eq:measure-smoothing-duality}. Fix a constant $C>0$ and let $v_j,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Suppose
\[
 v_j\simeq V,\qquad \sup_Xv_j=0,\qquad
 \mu_{v_j}\leq C\nu_j,
 \qquad v_j\longrightarrow v\text{ in }L^1(X).
\]
Then $v\in\mathcal E_V$, convergence also holds in $L^1(\nu)$ and in capacity, and
\[
 \mu_{v_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_v.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The limit is genuine and normalized, and $v_j,v\leq V\leq0$. The smoothing lemma gives the uniform estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothed-first-moment}
 \int_X(-v_j)\,\mu_{v_j}
 \leq C\int_X(-v_j)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_j\leq C'.
\end{equation}
In particular the relative first moments $\int_X(V-v_j)\mu_{v_j}$ are bounded. \Cref{prop:big-relative-first-moment}, with fixed reference $V$, puts $v$ in $\mathcal E_V$.

\Cref{prop:big-minimal-higher-moment}, applied to the fixed measure $\nu$, gives uniform integrability of $v_j$ with respect to $\nu$. For each fixed $T$, the bounded functions $\max(v_j,-T)$ have a common lower curvature bound in a Hermitian background $\Omega\geq\theta$ and converge in smooth-volume $L^1$ to $\max(v,-T)$. \Cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals} gives their $L^1(\nu)$ convergence. Uniform integrability, followed by $T\to\infty$, gives $v_j\to v$ in $L^1(\nu)$. The limit is integrable because $A_\theta(\nu)<\infty$. The truncations need not be $\theta$-plurisubharmonic; they are used only in the larger Hermitian background.

The smoothing lemma now yields
\[
 \int_X|v_j-v|\,\mu_{v_j}
 \leq C\int_X|v_j-v|\,\mathrm{d}\nu_j\longrightarrow0.
\]
The self-measure convergence theorem \cref{thm:big-relative-self-measure-convergence} proves convergence in capacity. Finally \eqref{eq:smoothed-first-moment} gives the raw sublevel estimate
\[
 \sup_j\mu_{v_j}(\{v_j\leq-T\})\leq C'/T.
\]
Their masses are bounded by $M_V$. The undamped continuity theorem \cref{thm:general-background-damping} therefore gives the asserted weak convergence.
\end{proof}

\section{General measures in an arbitrary big background}
\label{sec:big-minimal-general-measures}

The smoothing argument solves the equations for measures with finite $A_\theta$. A bounded carrier representation then reduces an arbitrary finite non-pluripolar measure to increasing bounded densities. Their solution measures have one fixed finite dominator, so \cref{thm:big-relative-fixed-continuity} passes to the limit.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:big-minimal-general-measures}
For every finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu$ with $\mu(X)>0$, the following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $\lambda>0$ there is a unique $u\in\mathcal E_V$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu.
\]
\item There are $v\in\mathcal E_V$ with $\sup_Xv=0$ and a unique scalar $c>0$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)=c\mu.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First suppose $\mu$ is a probability measure and $A_\theta(\mu)<\infty$. Let $\mu_j$ be its smooth probability approximations. The minimal ordinary-type $L^p$ theorems \cref{thm:big-minimal-exponential,thm:big-minimal-normalized} apply to their smooth, nonzero densities.

For the normalized equation they give $v_j\simeq V$, $\sup_Xv_j=0$, with $\mu_{v_j}=c_j\mu_j$. The mass interval gives $\ell_V\leq c_j\leq M_V$. Extract $c_j\to c>0$ and $v_j\to v$ in $L^1(X)$. The preceding proposition applies, so $v\in\mathcal E_V$ and $\mu_{v_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_v$. The right sides converge weakly to $c\mu$, proving the equation.

For the positive-exponent equation take $u_j\simeq V$ satisfying $\mu_{u_j}=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}\mu_j$. Write $a_j=\sup_Xu_j$ and $v_j=u_j-a_j$. The mass interval and Jensen's inequality give
\[
 \ell_V\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda a_j},\qquad
 M_V\geq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda a_j}\int_X\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_j}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_j
       \geq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda a_j-\lambda A_\theta(\mu_j)}.
\]
Thus $a_j$ is bounded above and below. After extraction $a_j\to a$, $v_j\to v$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\mu_{v_j}\leq C\mu_j$. The preceding proposition gives $v\in\mathcal E_V$, convergence in $L^1(\mu)$ and weak convergence of the Monge--Amp\`ere measures. Set $u=v+a$. The smoothing lemma identifies the right sides as $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$. This proves the equation and membership in $\mathcal E_V$.

The same conclusions hold for any nonzero finite measure whose normalization has finite $A_\theta$: scaling the measure only changes the positive scalar or translates the positive-exponent solution by a constant.

Now let $\mu$ be an arbitrary finite non-pluripolar measure of positive mass. The bounded-carrier decomposition \cref{lem:bounded-measure-carrier}, applied to $\omega$, writes
\[
 \mu=f\nu,\qquad \nu=\mathrm{MA}_\omega(\psi),\qquad
 \psi\in\PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^\infty(X),\qquad
 0\leq f\in L^1(\nu).
\]
For $\mu_k=\min(f,k)\nu$, we have $\mu_k(X)>0$. By \cref{lem:bounded-carrier-integrability}, its normalization has finite $A_\theta$ for each fixed $k$.

For the normalized equation the preceding case gives $v_k\in\mathcal E_V$, $\sup_Xv_k=0$, and $\mu_{v_k}=c_k\mu_k$, with
\[
 \frac{\ell_V}{\mu(X)}\leq c_k\leq\frac{M_V}{\mu_1(X)}.
\]
Extract $c_k\to c>0$ and a genuine $L^1$ limit $v$ of $v_k$. All their Monge--Amp\`ere measures are bounded by the fixed finite non-pluripolar measure $M_V\mu/\mu_1(X)$. The fixed-dominator theorem \cref{thm:big-relative-fixed-continuity} gives $v\in\mathcal E_V$ and weak convergence to $\mu_v$. Since $c_k\mu_k\to c\mu$ in total variation, $\mu_v=c\mu$.

For the positive-exponent equation let $u_k\in\mathcal E_V$ solve $\mu_{u_k}=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_k}\mu_k$. Exponential order gives $u_{k+1}\leq u_k$, because $\mu_k$ increases. Also
\[
 \ell_V\leq\mu_{u_k}(X)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_k}\mu(X),
 \qquad \mu_{u_k}\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_1}\mu.
\]
The lower bound on the suprema makes the decreasing limit $u$ genuine. The same fixed-dominator theorem puts it in $\mathcal E_V$ and gives weak convergence of the left sides. Dominated convergence with respect to $\nu$, using the integrable bound $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_1}f$, gives total-variation convergence of the right sides to $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$.

Apply \cref{cor:big-relative-orders} with reference $V$ in both directions to obtain uniqueness of the exponential solution and of the positive scalar.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:metric-pvp-minimal-volume}
If $X$ has both the bounded mass and positive volume properties, then the preceding theorem applies to every smooth big $\theta$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\theta_\rho\geq a\omega$ for some $a>0$. Then $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta-a\omega)$. Volume addition, followed by monotonicity of singularities, gives
\[
 0<a^n v_-(\omega)
 =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{a\omega}(0)
 \leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\rho)
 \leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(V_\theta).
\]
For the addition step the big background is $a\omega$ and the added positive current is $(\theta-a\omega)_\rho$. Bounded mass supplies finiteness of the upper volume.
\end{proof}

To prescribe a model reference $\varphi$, we will project these minimal-class solutions. The weighted addition argument first provides the operation needed to enlarge their measures.
