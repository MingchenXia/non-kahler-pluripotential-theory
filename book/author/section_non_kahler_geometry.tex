\section{Closed positivity and geometric equations}
\label{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems}

A strict closed current places the manifold in Fujiki's class, whereas a strict nonclosed current preserves the torsion of its background. This distinction connects the volume-to-bigness question to geometric structure and limits what a scalar potential equation can prove. The external geometric inputs used below have their exact scopes in \cref{sec:history-geometry}.

\subsection{Nef classes, bigness and the Fujiki class}

The existence of a closed K\"ahler current characterizes Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$. The transcendental Grauert--Riemenschneider conjecture asks whether a closed nef real $(1,1)$-class $\alpha$ with
\[
 \int_X\alpha^n>0
\]
is big. Under these hypotheses this is equivalent to $X\in\mathcal C$; the exact statements and sources are recorded in \cref{sec:history-geometry}.

The mass obstruction is precise. Under the same nefness and positive top-intersection hypotheses, one has
\[
 \alpha\text{ is big}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_+(h)<\infty
\]
for a Hermitian metric $h$. There is also a characterization of $\mathcal C$ by bounded mass and positivity of an upper class volume for some closed Bott--Chern class. That volume differs from $\mathcal V_\mu$; both results are specified in \cref{sec:history-geometry}.

The Hopf threefold tests the mass hypothesis but does not refute this conjecture: a counterexample would also need a closed nef class with positive top self-intersection. A proof must explain why that closed positivity forces bounded mass in the geometry at hand.

The elliptic threefold of \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass} supplies the complementary test: it has bounded mass, but no big closed class. Its entire nef cone is the nonnegative span of two smooth rank-one classes, and every top self-intersection is zero, by \cref{prop:elliptic-bott-chern-cones}. Hence this lower-mass example also leaves the geometric conjecture untouched. The exact boundary calculation in \cref{prop:elliptic-boundary-volume} describes its degeneration without identifying a nonclosed metric with a closed class.

The volume of this book separates two possible steps:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dp-two-bridges}
 \int_X\alpha^n>0
 \quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
 \mathcal V_\mu(\alpha)>0
 \quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
 \alpha\text{ is big}.
\end{equation}
Neither arrow is established here in the unrestricted compact complex setting. The second is the closed special case of \cref{conj:nef-gaud-volume-big}. On a compact K\"ahler manifold both follow from the usual nef volume formula and the classical nef-and-big criterion. Without closedness, positivity of $\mathcal V_\mu$ cannot characterize $\mathcal C$: every Hermitian metric $h$ already satisfies $\mathcal V_\mu(h)>0$, on every compact complex manifold.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:closed-nef-gaud-bridge}
Let $\alpha$ be closed and nef with $\int_X\alpha^n>0$. Prove a positive lower bound for $\lambda_\mu(\alpha)$, and rule out the collapse in \cref{prop:gaud-positive-volume-collapse} for a sequence minimizing the bigness threshold of $\alpha$.
\end{problem}

The relevant test metrics can be normalized by $\int_Xh\wedge\gamma^{n-1}=1$. A positive lower bound for $D_\mu(\gamma)$ along one threshold-minimizing sequence would finish the second step. The Gauduchon equation can change the volume density while retaining an Aeppli class. Since $\alpha$ is closed, it also retains the numerator paired against $\alpha$. The missing estimate is control of the trace normalization and determinant denominator after that change. Merely prescribing a volume density does not give this estimate.

\subsection{Positive lower volume and concentration}

Positive lower mass is an endpoint integrability property of the Gauduchon tests. It is independent of finite upper mass: the threefold in \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass} has $v_-(h)=0$ for every Hermitian metric and admits a balanced metric with finite upper mass, while the normalized Hopf metric has $v_-=6$ and $v_+=\infty$.

For $n\geq2$, use the normalized current cone $\mathscr G_h$ and determinant measure of \eqref{eq:weak-gaud-functional}, writing
\[
 \Gamma_0(T)=k_T\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad T\in\mathscr G_h.
\]
By \cref{thm:gaud-determinant-endpoint},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pvp-endpoint-geometric}
 v_-(h)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{T\in\mathscr G_h}
       \|k_T\|_{L^{n/(n-1)}(\mathrm{d}V)}<\infty.
\end{equation}
The exact supremum is $v_-(h)^{-1/n}$, including the value $+\infty$. Uniform estimates for every smaller exponent $1<q<n/(n-1)$ hold without either volume property. The lower-mass example violates the endpoint already for smooth strictly positive tests. Thus positivity, $\ddc T=0$ and normalized trace alone do not control concentration at that exponent.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:geometric-gaud-endpoint}
Find geometric conditions beyond the metric identities in \cref{cor:geometric-determinant-bound} that imply the endpoint estimate in \eqref{eq:pvp-endpoint-geometric}, and identify how they exclude the lower-mass degeneration of \cref{sec:vanishing-lower-mass}.
\end{problem}

\Cref{cor:geometric-determinant-bound} proves the endpoint bound when a metric has its first two powers $\ddc$-closed, including all compact K\"ahler manifolds and all compact complex surfaces. For a fixed smooth strictly positive density, \cref{thm:weak-gaud-compact-duality} gives one smooth maximizing current with bounded determinant density. However, \cref{prop:gaud-determinant-concentration} produces maximizing sequences in fixed K\"ahler classes with divergent moments at every higher exponent. Exact maximizers and maximizing sequences therefore require different estimates.

The balanced condition and bounded upper mass are insufficient, even together. The explicit identity \eqref{eq:lower-mass-vertical-energy} relates the failing lower bound to a vertical Dirichlet integral on an elliptic fibration. Meanwhile the horizontal curvature forms $\alpha,\beta$ of \cref{sec:vanishing-lower-mass} have a positive exact product $\alpha\wedge\beta$, which excludes pluriclosed metrics and closed K\"ahler currents. These calculations supply geometric information about this example without giving a classification of all lower-mass degeneration.

The equation consequence is exact for a Hermitian background: \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-solvability} characterizes positive lower mass by solvability for all finite non-pluripolar measures. When it vanishes, even a positive $L^1$ density bounded away from zero can admit no positive scaled subsolution. The remaining equation questions concern specified measures or additional geometric hypotheses, and separately the effect of the exponential weight.

\subsection{Null loci and the singularities of the Chern--Ricci flow}

Return to the general manifold $X$ of dimension $n$. At a finite maximal time $\tau$ of the Chern--Ricci flow, the limiting background is nef. To identify its regular region by a null locus, one first needs bigness. The dual currents detect failure of strict positivity, but their determinant degeneration does not by itself locate an analytic exceptional set. Write $\omega_0$ for the initial metric and $\theta_\tau=\omega_0-\tau\operatorname{Ric}(\omega_0)$, with the Ricci convention of \cref{cor:chern-ricci-current-lifetime}.

For a big smooth background $\theta$, the non-Hermitian locus $E_{\mathrm{nH}}(\theta)$ is the intersection of the singular sets of its strict currents with \emph{almost analytic singularities}; one such current attains the intersection. Here the potential is locally a positive multiple of the logarithm of an ideal norm up to a bounded remainder, and that remainder becomes smooth after subtracting the divisorial logarithms on a suitable log resolution. These are the precise singularities used by the theorem recorded in \cref{sec:history-geometry}.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For nef and big $\theta$, define
\[
 \operatorname{Null}(\theta)
 =\bigcup_{\substack{V\subset X\text{ irreducible analytic}\\
                     \dim V>0,\ \theta|_V\text{ not big}}}V.
\]
Bigness on a singular subvariety has its intrinsic analytic meaning. The qualitative equality $E_{\mathrm{nH}}(\theta)=\operatorname{Null}(\theta)$ holds for nef and big smooth backgrounds on arbitrary compact complex manifolds. The numerical criterion in \cref{sec:history-geometry} has additional hypotheses: bounded mass on $X$ and positive lower volume on its positive-dimensional submanifolds.

The known flow theorem recorded in \cref{sec:history-geometry} identifies finite-time Chern--Ricci singularities with the null locus under positive limiting lower volume, for arbitrary initial metrics in dimension two, and for Guan--Li initial metrics in higher dimensions. Here Guan--Li means $\ddc\omega_0=0$ and $\mathrm{d}\omega_0\wedge \dc\omega_0=0$. These hypotheses must not be silently replaced by positivity of $\mathcal V_\mu$.

The current calculation in \cref{cor:chern-ricci-current-lifetime} applies to arbitrary initial metrics. The normalized annihilating currents of $\theta_\tau$ satisfy
\[
 \int_X\theta_\tau\wedge T=0.
\]
If $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta_\tau)>0$, their determinant-weighted measures vanish by \cref{prop:nef-annihilator-determinant}. This is a precise degeneracy statement, but it does not imply that their traces are concentrated on analytic sets. A smooth positive semidefinite matrix of deficient rank can be defined on an open set.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:chern-ricci-gaud-singularities}
Suppose a Chern--Ricci flow has finite maximal time $\tau$ and $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta_\tau)>0$. Can one prove that $\theta_\tau$ is big and that the flow converges smoothly to a positive metric locally on $X\setminus E_{\mathrm{nH}}(\theta_\tau)$, without a bounded mass hypothesis? How do the annihilating Gauduchon currents describe the resulting degeneracy?
\end{problem}

The first assertion is a case of the volume-to-bigness question. Once bigness of $\theta_\tau$ is known, the parabolic theorem recorded in \cref{sec:history-geometry} supplies convergence as currents and smooth local convergence off $E_{\mathrm{nH}}(\theta_\tau)$, by parabolic estimates. Combined with nefness and the qualitative null-locus theorem, this identifies the analytic exceptional set. Thus the missing step in this route is the proposed numerical criterion for bigness. The local envelope continuity and homogeneous replacement arguments of \cref{thm:big-continuous-envelope-contact} supply an elliptic foundation away from a strict barrier's poles; they are not a reconstruction of those parabolic estimates.

Completeness requires an additional metric estimate. In \cref{thm:incomplete-divisorial-metric}, the unique finite-energy solution of a divisorial exponential equation in a big closed class is incomplete on every nonempty connected open subset of its ample locus. Its minimal divisorial singularity cancels the prescribed pole. This resolves the unrestricted big-class completeness question negatively; it does not decide completeness under nefness or under the compatibility conditions of the geometric smoothing problem discussed in \cref{sec:history-completeness}. In particular it does not settle \cref{qu:chern-ricci-gaud-singularities}, whose limiting background is nef.

For integrable poles weakened by logarithmic factors, \cref{cor:divisorial-exponential-completeness} gives a sufficient condition in terms of the density, the potential and the tangential determinant. The proof controls normal length and applies to arbitrary Hermitian backgrounds. The torus models in \cref{ex:critical-complete-divisor,ex:supercritical-incomplete-divisor} attain its critical exponent and show its sharpness, even with a K\"ahler background and full mass. Their pole coefficients need not extend smoothly and positively across the divisor; this distinction preserves the separate scope of the geometric smoothing question.

The regularity of the residual also matters. By \cref{thm:holder-current-distance}, a H\"older residual on a compact manifold gives finite distance to its analytic singular set wherever it defines a smooth positive metric off that set. Continuity alone does not suffice: the prescribed radial densities in \cref{thm:radial-endpoints} have bounded continuous minimal remainders and complete regular ends when $n<d\leq2n$. These examples concern their specified densities; they do not establish the flow compatibility in \cref{qu:chern-ricci-gaud-singularities}.

\subsection{Balanced and pluriclosed metrics}

The balanced--pluriclosed conjecture asks whether the coexistence of these two metric structures forces a compact complex manifold to be K\"ahler. The balanced metric $\beta$ and pluriclosed metric $\omega$ may differ; a single metric satisfying both conditions is already K\"ahler. The source and flow formulation are given in \cref{sec:history-geometry}.

A pluriclosed manifold in $\mathcal C$ is K\"ahler, by the result recorded there. It would therefore suffice to construct a closed K\"ahler current from the two metrics. The scalar potential equation alone cannot produce the required closedness.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For every distributional potential $u$,
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\omega+\ddc u)=\mathrm{d}\omega.
\]
Thus if the starting pluriclosed metric is nonclosed, no potential in its fixed background makes it K\"ahler. The finite-volume equation, including its smooth solution, does not change this differential datum. Similarly, a conformal Gauduchon representative need not be balanced. Positivity and a scalar normalization cannot be used to replace either missing closedness condition.

The balanced form $\beta^{n-1}$ pairs trivially with exact real two-forms and can therefore give a conserved trace along a flow. Under $c_1(X)=0$ in real cohomology, the flow conjecture in \cref{sec:history-geometry} proposes long-time existence and convergence of pluriclosed flow to a K\"ahler metric when a balanced metric exists. The invariant examples recorded there use the conserved trace. For general metrics, turning that integral into a pointwise bound remains the analytic difficulty.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:balanced-pluriclosed-program}
Use the coexistence of a balanced metric and a pluriclosed metric to construct closed positive data, or to control a geometric flow that can change the torsion. Determine what estimate beyond the finite balanced trace prevents metric degeneration. Can determinant estimates for Gauduchon currents contribute to that estimate?
\end{problem}

For families with a controlled balanced trace and determinant density, the remaining analytic target is a local ellipticity estimate. The scalar trace identity supplies no such estimate for arbitrary families.

\subsection{Morse inequalities and the choice of volume}

Subtracting a nef class has a controlled volume cost in the closed K\"ahler theory. For closed nef classes $\alpha,\beta$ on a compact K\"ahler manifold, the transcendental Morse inequality is
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}(\alpha-\beta)
 \geq\int_X\alpha^n-n\int_X\alpha^{n-1}\wedge\beta
\]
where $\operatorname{Vol}$ is the usual class volume. Its source and the resulting differentiability and cone-duality theorems are recorded in \cref{sec:history-geometry}; their proofs are not reconstructed here. To formulate an analogue on an arbitrary compact complex manifold, one must first choose the volume being estimated.

For nonclosed backgrounds, the displayed intersection expression is not generally invariant under changing representatives. Also, the upper class volume and $\mathcal V_\mu$ have different definitions and different possible finiteness behavior. A Hermitian analogue must first specify which invariant it estimates.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
The first-variation formula gives a finite quantity to investigate. Fix smooth real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta,\eta$ and $t>0$. Suppose $\theta_s=\theta-s\eta$ has a smooth positive representative for $0\leq s\leq t$, and let $\gamma_s$ be a minimizing Gauduchon metric for its smooth equation. By \cref{prop:gaud-volume-first-variation} and the fundamental theorem of calculus,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-volume-subtraction-cost}
 \lambda_\mu(\theta-t\eta)
 =\lambda_\mu(\theta)
   -\int_0^t
       \frac{\int_X\eta\wedge\gamma_s^{n-1}}
            {D_\mu(\gamma_s)}\,\mathrm{d}s.
\end{equation}
The quotient is unchanged by rescaling the minimizer. This identity is valid on the stated positive segment and uses no upper Monge--Amp\`ere mass bound. A uniform upper bound on its integrand gives a lower bound for the remaining root volume. \Cref{thm:closed-core-subtraction} extends the identity to a genuine nef endpoint and bounds its improper integral when a closed nef positive-volume core is supplemented by a semipositive form. The fixed tests retain the prescribed density, and \eqref{eq:closed-core-differential-cost} isolates the correction involving $\ddc B$. The endpoint is big by the closed core; \cref{ex:nonclosed-nef-subtraction} gives a nonclosed example with an explicit strict current.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:hermitian-morse-program}
Find geometric hypotheses controlling the cost in \eqref{eq:gaud-volume-subtraction-cost} up to a nef endpoint beyond the closed-core regime of \cref{thm:closed-core-subtraction}. Determine when positive root volume forces bigness without that core. For closed backgrounds, compare the estimate with classical Morse inequalities; for nonclosed backgrounds, retain the differential data and the dependence on $\mu$.
\end{problem}

The singularity and divisorial constructions in the book become relevant at this stage: they record how much positivity can be spent on imposed poles, while the residual Gauduchon volume stays finite. They do not yet give the moving intersection product or the birational volume formula of the closed theory. Recovering such geometric information is a further objective, with the finite-divisor and Hopf calculations serving as explicit tests.
