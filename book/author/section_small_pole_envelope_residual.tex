Ordinary projection can change the singularity type. At a sufficiently small compact pole set, it nevertheless preserves the full residual measure. Bounded mass controls the local approximations in every degree; the lower degrees carry no mass on that set.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual}\index{Residual mass!ordinary envelope}
Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on $X$, and let $S\subset X$ be compact of Hausdorff dimension zero. Suppose $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ is locally bounded off $S$, has pole set $S$, and has positive lower singularity volume. Put $p=P_\omega[u]$. For $v=u,p$, the bounded truncations define a finite positive full measure
\[
 \Phi_v=\lim_{k\to\infty}
                 (\omega+\ddc\max(v,-k))^n,
 \qquad \mathbf1_S\Phi_p=\mathbf1_S\Phi_u.
\]
On each coordinate chart, the residual restriction equals $\mathbf1_S(\ddc(v+q))^n_{\mathrm{full}}$ for any fixed smooth Euclidean correction $q$ making $v+q$ plurisubharmonic there. It is independent of that correction. The full measures of $P_\omega(u+m,0)$ converge weakly to $\Phi_p$ as $m\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $u\leq0$ and set $p_m=P_\omega(u+m,0)$ for positive integers $m$. Then
\[
 u\leq p_m\leq\min(u+m,0),\qquad p_m\uparrow p
\]
with canonical regularization of the limit. Thus $p_m\sim u$, while $p_m,p$ are locally bounded off $S$. The model assertion follows from \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}. By \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}, the increasing convergence is in capacity. On compact subsets off $S$ the potentials have common bounded ranges, so all bounded local products converge there, by \cref{sec:psh-local-capacity}.

For any $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ locally bounded off $S$, put $v_k=\max(v,-k)$. Fixed local Euclidean corrections and \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products} give full weak limits in every degree,
\[
 W_j(v)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\ddc v_k)^j,
 \qquad
 \Phi_v=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\omega+\ddc v_k)^n.
\]
Expand in the corrected closed positive powers and smooth forms. The expressions patch because the approximating bounded currents are global. They are finite order-zero currents on compact $X$, and $\Phi_v$ is positive. Bounded Stokes gives $\int_XW_n(v)=0$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-pole-full-total}
 J(v):=\Phi_v(X)
 =\int_X\omega^n+
       \sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj
              \int_XW_j(v)\wedge\omega^{n-j}.
\end{equation}
The measure $\Phi_v$ equals $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)$ off $S$. Every lower corrected closed power has zero coefficient mass on $S$, by \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}. Thus only the top closed power contributes to $\mathbf1_S\Phi_v$; this identifies it with the local residual measure and proves independence of the smooth correction. These assertions are local on each chart and require no single chart containing $S$. Also $J(v)\leq v_+(\omega)$, by the bounded truncation masses.

We need uniform local trace bounds for the closed powers of $p_m$. Fix a relatively compact coordinate neighborhood and write $\kappa=\ddc|z|^2$. Choose $B>0$ so that $B\kappa\geq\omega$ there, and $A>1$ so that $A\omega-B\kappa\geq\kappa$. For $p_{m,k}=\max(p_m,-k)$, the closed current
$T_{m,k}=B\kappa+\ddc p_{m,k}$ is positive and
\[
 Q_{m,k}:=A\omega+\ddc p_{m,k}\geq T_{m,k}+\kappa.
\]
Expanding against the positive smooth difference gives, for $1\leq j\leq n$,
\[
 \binom nj T_{m,k}^j\wedge\kappa^{n-j}
       \leq Q_{m,k}^n,
 \qquad
 \int_XQ_{m,k}^n\leq A^n v_+(\omega).
\]
The last bound uses the bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic potential $p_{m,k}/A$. Passing $k\to\infty$ gives uniform trace bounds for every full closed power of $\ddc(p_m+B|z|^2)$, independently of $m$.

For $j<n$, every subsequential weak limit of these closed positive powers gives $S$ zero coefficient mass. Off $S$ the bounded capacity convergence identifies it with the corresponding full power of $p+B|z|^2$. That power also gives $S$ zero mass, so the limit is unique. Expanding back in the smooth correction and using a finite coordinate cover yields
\[
 W_j(p_m)\longrightarrow W_j(p),\qquad 1\leq j<n.
\]
Hence \eqref{eq:small-pole-full-total} gives $J(p_m)\to J(p)$.

Let $R=\mathbf1_S\Phi_u$. Apply bounded residual invariance in \cref{lem:small-pole-residual-invariance} on each chart, after a common correction. This gives $\mathbf1_S\Phi_{p_m}=R$ for every $m$. Their total masses are bounded by $v_+(\omega)$, so take a weak subsequential limit $\eta$. Since $\Phi_{p_m}-R$ is positive, $\eta-R$ is positive. Off $S$, the limit equals $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(p)$, and its total mass is $J(p)$. The measure $\Phi_p$ has the same total mass and the same restriction off $S$, so
\[
 \Phi_p(S)=\eta(S)\geq R(S).
\]
On each chart, $u\leq p$ and the residual-order part of \cref{lem:small-pole-residual-invariance} give $\mathbf1_S\Phi_p\leq R$. The last display forces equality of their total masses, hence equality of these positive measures. It also gives $\mathbf1_S\eta=R$. Equality off $S$ now implies $\eta=\Phi_p$. Every weak subsequential limit is therefore $\Phi_p$, proving the asserted full-measure convergence of the rooftops.
\end{proof}
