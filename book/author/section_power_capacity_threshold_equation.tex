\section{Power capacity bounds and threshold equations}
\label{sec:power-capacity-threshold}

The Hermitian form $h$ and the probability volume form $\mathrm dV$ are as before. The root-tail condition of \cref{prop:root-uniform-tails} controls the scalar threshold. A power bound for one density's measure supplies the missing control of its approximating potentials: the capacity estimates give a common bound, and stability against the solution measure gives uniform convergence.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:power-capacity-critical-equation}\index{Subsolution!threshold under capacity domination}\index{Density space!capacity dominated roots}
Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm dV)$ have positive integral. Suppose that $A,\alpha>0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:datum-power-capacity}
 \int_Ef\,\mathrm dV\leq A\operatorname{Cap}_h(E)^{1+\alpha}
 \qquad(E\subset X\text{ Borel}).
\end{equation}
There is $C>0$, depending only on $(X,h),A,\alpha$, such that every measurable $0\leq g\leq f$ with positive integral satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-capacity-critical-bound}
 b_h(g)\geq C\left(\int_Xg\,\mathrm dV\right)^{-\alpha/(1+\alpha)},
 \qquad
 \rho_h(g^{1/n})\leq C^{-1/n}
        \left(\int_Xg\,\mathrm dV\right)^{\alpha/(n(1+\alpha))}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $f^{1/n}\in\mathcal D_h^0$. There is $\phi\in\PSH(X,h)\cap C(X)$, with $\sup_X\phi=0$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-capacity-exact-threshold}
 \mathrm{MA}_h(\phi)=b_h(f)f\,\mathrm dV,
\end{equation}
where $b_h(f)>0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the coefficient and root tails.} Put $g_j=\min(g,j)$ and let $\phi_j$ be normalized continuous solutions of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(\phi_j)=c_jg_j\,\mathrm dV,
 \qquad c_j=c_h(g_j),\qquad \sup_X\phi_j=0,
\]
given by \cref{thm:kn-continuous}. The definition \eqref{eq:individual-truncated-normalizer} gives $c_j\downarrow b_h(g)$. Their measures satisfy a power capacity bound with constant $c_jA$.

Apply \cref{lem:thin-sublevel-capacity,lem:power-backwards-capacity}, with $h$ in place of $\omega$, to $u=\phi_j$ and $v=0$, with $H=1$ and $\varepsilon=1/2$; let $C_1$ be the resulting constant $C_H$, and $C_\alpha$ the constant in \eqref{eq:power-sublevel-lower}. Fix $D>0$ with $2D<\delta_{1/2}$, and put $U_D=\{\phi_j<\inf_X\phi_j+D\}$. The backward estimate \eqref{eq:power-sublevel-lower} and the thin-sublevel estimate \eqref{eq:thin-capacity} with $s=t=D$ give
\[
 D\leq C_\alpha(C_1c_jA)^{1/n}
                   \operatorname{Cap}_h(U_D)^{\alpha/n},
 \qquad
 D^n\operatorname{Cap}_h(U_D)
       \leq C_1c_j\int_Xg_j\,\mathrm dV.
\]
Eliminating $\operatorname{Cap}_h(U_D)$ yields
\[
 c_j^{1+\alpha}\left(\int_Xg_j\,\mathrm dV\right)^\alpha\geq c>0,
\]
with $c$ depending only on $(X,h)$, $A$ and $\alpha$. Letting $j\to\infty$ proves the lower bound for $b_h(g)$. Since $\rho_h(g^{1/n})=b_h(g)^{-1/n}$ by definition, this lower bound is equivalent to the root estimate; by \eqref{eq:integrable-gauduchon-root}, the root estimate bounds every Gauduchon test of $g^{1/n}$. Apply it to $g=f\mathbf1_{\{f>K^n\}}$, for which $g^{1/n}=f^{1/n}\mathbf1_{\{f^{1/n}>K\}}$, whenever this density is nonzero; the zero density has root functional zero. Since the mass of $g$ tends to zero as $K\to\infty$, the tails of $f^{1/n}$ tend to zero in both $L^n$ and $\rho_h$. The case $g=f$ shows that $f^{1/n}\in\mathcal D_h$, and \cref{prop:root-uniform-tails} gives $f^{1/n}\in\mathcal D_h^0$.

\emph{Step 2: uniform convergence of a subsequence.} Take $g=f$ in Step 1, so that $g_j=f_j$ and $b_h(f)>0$. Since $c_j\leq c_1$, the measures satisfy
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(\phi_j)(E)
       \leq Ac_1\operatorname{Cap}_h(E)^{1+\alpha},
\]
and the first estimate of Step 1 bounds $\operatorname{Cap}_h(U_D)$ below by a positive constant independent of $j$. Since $U_D=\{\phi_j<\inf_X\phi_j+D\}$, the tail estimate \eqref{eq:global-capacity-tail}, applied with $h$ in place of $\omega$, gives one $M\geq1$ with $-M\leq\phi_j\leq0$ for all $j$.

By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, extract a subsequence converging in $L^1(\mathrm dV)$ and almost everywhere. Since $f\,\mathrm dV$ is a fixed finite absolutely continuous measure and the potentials have a common bound, dominated convergence gives
\[
 \int_X|\phi_j-\phi_k|\,\mathrm{MA}_h(\phi_j)
 \leq c_1\int_X|\phi_j-\phi_k|f\,\mathrm dV\longrightarrow0
\]
along this subsequence as both indices tend to infinity. The same bound holds with $\mathrm{MA}_h(\phi_k)$ in place of $\mathrm{MA}_h(\phi_j)$. Applying \cref{prop:power-capacity-one-sided-stability} with $H=M$ to the pairs $(u,v)=(\phi_j,\phi_k)$ and $(\phi_k,\phi_j)$ shows that the subsequence is uniformly Cauchy. Its limit $\phi$ is continuous, $h$-plurisubharmonic by distributional positivity, and has supremum zero.

\emph{Step 3: the limit equation.} Uniform convergence implies convergence in capacity, so the continuity of bounded products recalled in \cref{sec:psh-local-capacity,sec:hermitian-capacity} gives $\mathrm{MA}_h(\phi_j)\rightharpoonup\mathrm{MA}_h(\phi)$ along the subsequence. On the other hand, $c_jf_j\to b_h(f)f$ in $L^1(\mathrm dV)$. The two limits prove \eqref{eq:power-capacity-exact-threshold}.
\end{proof}

The capacity assumption concerns the fixed datum and implies the root-tail condition. When $v_-(h)>0$, which always holds for $n\leq2$ by \cref{cor:lower-mass-dimension}, the root tails and the lower bound $b_h(g)\geq v_-(h)/\int_Xg\,\mathrm dV$ already follow from \cref{prop:root-uniform-tails,prop:individual-normalizer-lp}, and \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-exact-threshold} gives an exact threshold equation for every integrable density; the capacity assumption then provides a continuous solution. Whether root tails alone suffice for the exact equation when $v_-(h)=0$ remains part of \cref{qu:individual-equation-threshold}; whether every positive-threshold root has these tails is the question following \cref{prop:root-uniform-tails}.
