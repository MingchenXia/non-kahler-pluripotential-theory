\section{Chern-connection regularization}
\label{sec:psh-chern-regularization}\index{Regularization!Chern connection}

Convolution in local coordinates does not directly produce global approximants with a controlled lower curvature bound. Demailly's regularization instead averages over the fibers of the tangent bundle, using the Chern connection of $\omega$, and a Kiselman-type minimum of these averages controls the loss of positivity. The classical curvature estimate for this minimum holds in a Hermitian background, while the $L^1$ and gradient bounds for the averages are elementary.

Fix $C_0>0$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $-C_0\leq u\leq0$. Distances, gradients and norms of tensors are taken with respect to the Riemannian metric associated with $\omega$.

For small $t>0$, let $R_tu$ be Demailly's regularization of $u$ formed with the Chern connection of $\omega$. It averages $u$ over the image of the ball of radius $t$ in each tangent space, with a fixed smooth nonnegative radial kernel of integral one. The map used for this purpose is a smooth realization of the holomorphic part of the formal fiberwise expansion of the exponential map; it need not be holomorphic in the fiber. The following classical estimate controls the Kiselman-type transform of these averages, including its mixed base--fiber terms; see \cref{sec:history-psh} for the sources.

\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:chern-kiselman-input}
There are constants $A,K>0$ and $t_*>0$, depending only on $(X,\omega)$ and $C_0$, such that $R_tu$ is smooth for $0<t<t_*$,
\[
 -C_0\leq R_tu\leq0,\qquad
 R_tu+Kt^2\downarrow u\quad\text{as }t\downarrow0.
\]
For $0<\delta<t_*$ and $c>0$, the bounded function
\[
 \Phi_{c,\delta}
   =\inf_{0<t\leq\delta}
       \left\{R_tu+K(t^2-\delta^2)+K(t-\delta)
                         -c\log(t/\delta)\right\}
\]
is quasi-plurisubharmonic and satisfies
\[
 \omega+\ddc\Phi_{c,\delta}\geq-(Ac+2K\delta)\omega.
\]
At every point, the infimum is attained at some $t>0$.
\end{lemma}

For the background $\omega$, this lemma is the only input we take from the classical theory. The linear term $K(t-\delta)$ is needed in the Hermitian case, where it absorbs the mixed base--fiber terms in the complex Hessian of the regularization. Since $-c\log(t/\delta)$ tends to $+\infty$ as $t\downarrow0$ while $u$ is bounded, the infimum is attained away from zero. We need neither smoothness of the minimizing radius nor smoothness of the transform across the region where this radius equals $\delta$.

Next we prove two elementary estimates for the averaging operators.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:chern-smoothing-integral}
For all sufficiently small $t>0$,
\[
 \|R_tu-u\|_{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq Ct^2,
 \qquad \|\nabla R_tu\|_\infty\leq C/t.
\]
The constants are uniform for $-C_0\leq u\leq0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $z$ be local coordinates on $X$ and $\xi\in\mathbb C^n$ the fiber variable with respect to a smooth orthonormal frame of the tangent bundle. Write $\rho(|\xi|^2)$ for the fixed radial averaging kernel, normalized with respect to Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{d}\xi$ and supported in $|\xi|\leq1$. In these coordinates the averaging map $F$ gives
\[
 R_tu(z)=\int u(F(z,t\xi))\rho(|\xi|^2)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,
 \qquad F(z,0)=z.
\]
The map $F$ is smooth in these variables, and its fiber differential at zero is an isomorphism. Let $R_t^*$ be the formal adjoint of $R_t$ with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$, so that $\int_X(R_ta)b\,\mathrm{d}V=\int_Xa\,R_t^*b\,\mathrm{d}V$. We first claim that
\[
 R_t^*1=1+O(t^2)
\]
uniformly. To see this, insert a smooth partition of unity in the variable $z$, each term being compactly supported in a chart on which the frame is defined. For fixed $\xi$, change variables $x=F(z,t\xi)$ on a neighborhood of the support of a term. The inverse map and its Jacobian have uniform Taylor expansions in $t$. The term of order zero in the transformed density is the chosen partition function at $x$, and the term of order one is linear in $\xi$, so it integrates to zero against the even radial kernel. The remaining density is bounded by $Ct^2\mathrm{d}V(x)$, since the second derivatives of all maps, partition functions and volume coefficients are bounded on these finitely many compact charts. Summing over the partition functions proves the claim.

Consequently,
\[
 \left|\int_X(R_tu-u)\,\mathrm{d}V\right|
  =\left|\int_Xu(R_t^*1-1)\,\mathrm{d}V\right|\leq Ct^2.
\]
By \cref{lem:chern-kiselman-input}, $R_tu-u\geq-Kt^2$. A real function $a\geq-Kt^2$ satisfies $|a|\leq a+2Kt^2$; applying this to $a=R_tu-u$ and integrating gives the first estimate.

For the derivative bound, change variables in the fiber to write $R_tu(z)=\int_Xu(x)k_t(z,x)\,\mathrm{d}V(x)$. In local coordinates the smooth kernel $k_t$ is supported where $|x-z|\leq Ct$ and satisfies
\[
 |\nabla_z k_t(z,x)|\leq Ct^{-2n-1}.
\]
Indeed, the inverse fiber map and its Jacobian have uniformly bounded derivatives, and differentiating the rescaled radial argument introduces at most one factor $t^{-1}$ in addition to $t^{-2n}$. Since the kernel is smooth with compact support, there are no boundary terms. Integration over a ball of volume at most $Ct^{2n}$ gives $\int|\nabla_zk_t|\,\mathrm{d}V\leq C/t$, and multiplication by the uniform bound for $u$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The general curvature modulus}
\label{subsec:general-curvature-modulus}

Let $\theta$ now be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^\infty(X)$. We use the same averaging operators and the same metric.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
We first state the classical input for this background. The classical Chern-connection regularization theorem applies to the closed current $\ddc u$ with the smooth, possibly nonclosed, lower bound $-\theta$; the curvature of the Chern connection on the tangent bundle is bounded by a fixed multiple of $\omega$. The theorem gives a constant $K_0>0$ such that
\[
 R_tu+K_0t^2\downarrow u.
\]
To state its curvature estimate, use local coordinates $z$ on $X$ and a complex radius variable $w$, and put $t=|w|$. Write $\zeta\in T_z^{1,0}X$ and $\eta\in\mathbb C$ for the base and radius components of a tangent vector, and set $\lambda(z,t)=t\partial_t(R_tu(z)+K_0t^2)$. Then $\lambda$ is nonnegative and increasing in $t$. Moreover, there are constants $A,C>0$ and a function $\epsilon_0(t)\geq0$ with $\epsilon_0(t)\downarrow0$ as $t\downarrow0$ with the following property: the complex Hessian of $(z,w)\mapsto R_{|w|}u(z)$ is bounded below by the base form $-\theta-A\lambda(z,t)\omega-\epsilon_0(t)\omega$, evaluated on $\zeta$, minus mixed and vertical error terms bounded by $C(|\zeta||\eta|+|\eta|^2)$. This is the classical regularization estimate for a smooth lower bound.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:general-kiselman-modulus}
For $K$ sufficiently large, there is an increasing function $\epsilon(\delta)\geq0$ tending to zero at zero such that, for every small $\delta>0$ and every $c>0$, the function
\[
 \Phi_{c,\delta}
   =\inf_{0<t\leq\delta}
      \{R_tu+K(t^2-\delta^2)+K(t-\delta)-c\log(t/\delta)\}
\]
is bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic and satisfies
\[
 \theta+\ddc\Phi_{c,\delta}
                       \geq-(Ac+\epsilon(\delta))\omega.
\]
The infimum is attained away from zero. Moreover, $R_tu+Kt^2\downarrow u$ as $t\downarrow0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take $K\geq K_0$ and put $H(z,t)=R_tu(z)+Kt^2+Kt$. As a function of $\log t$, $H$ is increasing and strictly convex, because $R_tu+K_0t^2$ has nonnegative increasing logarithmic derivative and the added terms have positive second logarithmic derivative. Its logarithmic derivative $\widetilde\lambda=t\partial_tH$ is at least $\lambda$.

The complex Hessian of $K|w|$ has a positive vertical term proportional to $K/|w|$. For sufficiently small $|w|=t$, this term absorbs the vertical error and, by the inequality
\[
 C|\zeta||\eta|
       \leq C' t|\zeta|^2+\frac{K}{8t}|\eta|^2,
\]
also the mixed error. After increasing the constants and replacing $\lambda$ by the larger $\widetilde\lambda$, we find that the complex Hessian of $(z,w)\mapsto H(z,|w|)$ is bounded below by the base form
\[
 -\theta-(A\widetilde\lambda+\epsilon(t))\omega,
\]
where $\epsilon$ is an increasing majorant of $\epsilon_0(t)+C't$.

Since $u$ is bounded, the term $-c\log(t/\delta)$ tends uniformly to $+\infty$ as $t\downarrow0$, so the minimum occurs on a compact interval of radii away from zero. Strict convexity in $\log t$ makes the minimizing radius unique. At an interior minimum $\widetilde\lambda=c$, while at the upper endpoint $t=\delta$ one has $\widetilde\lambda\leq c$. No differentiability of the minimizing radius is needed.

In more detail, localize near a point of $X$ and fix $a>0$. By continuity of the minimizers and of $\widetilde\lambda$, there is an interval of radii containing the minimizers at all nearby points on which $\widetilde\lambda\leq c+a$. If the minimum is attained at the upper endpoint, keep the upper endpoint $\delta$; an open interval approaching it has the same infimum. Because of the uniform logarithmic term, the lower endpoint can be chosen below all minimizers, and since $\widetilde\lambda$ is increasing, the bound $\widetilde\lambda\leq c+a$ holds throughout the chosen interval. The local Kiselman minimum principle for functions depending only on $|w|$, applied after a local quadratic correction, now gives the lower curvature bound with error $A(c+a)+\epsilon(\delta)$, up to an arbitrarily small error from freezing the smooth base forms. Letting these errors and $a$ tend to zero proves the asserted current inequality and the quasi-plurisubharmonicity. Finally, $K\geq K_0$ preserves the monotone convergence of $R_tu+Kt^2$ to $u$.
\end{proof}

The classical theorem only gives some modulus $\epsilon$; we do not claim that $\epsilon(\delta)$ is linear in $\delta$. The cancellation and kernel arguments in the proof of \cref{lem:chern-smoothing-integral} still give
\[
 \|R_tu-u\|_{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq Ct^2,
 \qquad \|\nabla R_tu\|_\infty\leq C/t,
\]
because the averaging map is unchanged, $u$ is bounded, and these arguments use only the monotone lower bound $R_tu-u\geq-Kt^2$.


For continuous potentials, the approximants of \cref{prop:bounded-almost-regularization} converge uniformly.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:continuous-potential-smooth-loss}\index{Regularization!continuous potentials}
Let $\alpha$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\alpha)\cap C^0(X)$. There are smooth real functions $u_j$ and numbers $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ such that $u_j\to u$ uniformly and
\[
 \alpha+\ddc u_j\geq-\varepsilon_j\omega.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The continuous function $u$ is bounded, so \cref{prop:bounded-almost-regularization}, applied with $\theta=\alpha$, gives smooth functions $u_j\downarrow u$ and numbers $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ with $\alpha+\varepsilon_j\omega+\ddc u_j\geq0$. The continuous functions $u_j$ decrease to the continuous function $u$ on the compact manifold $X$, so the convergence is uniform by Dini's theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A linear error for a smooth background}

Throughout this subsection, $\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is big. By \cref{thm:resolvable-regularization-input} and the remark following it, there is a strict potential $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, that is, $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\varepsilon\omega$ for some $\varepsilon>0$, which is smooth outside an analytic pole set and has a smooth remainder after resolution. Put $\Omega=X\setminus\{\rho=-\infty\}$. For normalized potentials that are locally bounded on $\Omega$, the next lemma improves the curvature error of \cref{lem:general-kiselman-modulus} to a linear one.

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-background-regularization-rate}
Let $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $\sup_Xa=0$, and suppose that $a$ is locally bounded on $\Omega$. There are constants $A,K,C>0$ such that the averaging operators $R_t$ of \cref{lem:chern-kiselman-input} satisfy
\[
 R_ta+Kt^2\downarrow a,\qquad
 \|R_ta-a\|_{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq Ct^2.
\]
For small $\delta>0$ and $c>0$, the transform on $\Omega$
\[
 \Phi_{c,\delta}
 =\inf_{0<t\leq\delta}
 \{R_ta+K(t^2-\delta^2)+K(t-\delta)-c\log(t/\delta)\}
\]
extends canonically to $X$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-background-transform-rate}
 a-K(\delta^2+\delta)\leq\Phi_{c,\delta}\leq R_\delta a\leq0,
 \qquad \theta+\ddc\Phi_{c,\delta}\geq-(Ac+C\delta)\omega.
\end{equation}
At each point of $\Omega$ the infimum is attained at a positive radius. On every compact set $K_1\Subset\Omega$, for sufficiently small $t$, $\|\nabla R_ta\|_{L^\infty(K_1)}\leq C_{K_1}/t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the classical expansion.} Apply the classical Chern-connection regularization expansion to the closed current $\ddc a$ and the smooth lower bound $-\theta$. It gives monotonicity and logarithmic convexity: for a constant $K_0>0$, the function $\lambda(x,t)=t\partial_t(R_ta(x)+K_0t^2)$ is nonnegative and increasing in $t$. Instead of inferring a rate from the unspecified modulus in that theorem, we estimate the error terms of the expansion directly, using the smoothness of $\theta$.

Choose the auxiliary curvature form to be $A\omega$, with $A$ large enough that the Chern curvature plus $A\omega\otimes\operatorname{Id}$ is nonnegative on every decomposable tensor. Equivalently, after absorbing the normalization constants, the Chern curvature coefficients $R_{j\bar k\ell\bar m}$ in a unitary frame satisfy
\[
 \sum R_{j\bar k\ell\bar m}\xi_j\bar\xi_k\zeta_\ell\bar\zeta_m
 \geq-A|\xi|^2|\zeta|^2
\]
for all tangent vectors $\xi,\zeta$. Such an $A$ exists by compactness. Pairing this inequality with the nonnegative eigenvalues of a positive complex Hessian bounds its curvature contraction from below by $-A$ times its trace, with no further error term.

\emph{Step 2: a frozen quadratic correction.} Fix $x\in X$ and a complex radius variable $w\ne0$, and put $t=|w|$. Write $\xi\in T_x^{1,0}X$ and $\tau\in\mathbb C$ for the base and radius components of a tangent vector. In a coordinate chart of uniform size about $x$, write $\beta=\ddc|z|^2$ and choose a quadratic function $q_{x,t}$ with
\[
 \ddc q_{x,t}=\theta(x)+Bt\beta,
\]
where the constant $B$ is chosen so large that $a+q_{x,t}$ is plurisubharmonic on a ball of radius of order $t$ containing the support of the averages at $x$ and at nearby points. This is possible by the bound $\|\theta(y)-\theta(x)\|\leq C|y-x|$ on such a ball. For the Hessian calculation at $(x,w)$, the quadratic function is held fixed; its coefficients and all needed derivatives are uniformly bounded.

\emph{Step 3: the error terms.} By normalized compactness, $\|a\|_{L^1}$ is bounded. On each fixed larger chart, add one fixed strictly plurisubharmonic quadratic function $Q$ to $a$. For the trace measure $\mu=\ddc(a+Q)\wedge\beta^{n-1}$, the local Lelong mass inequality and a bound for the mass of a fixed larger ball give $\mu(B(x,t))\leq Ct^{2n-2}$. The coefficients of $\partial\bar\partial(a+q_{x,t})$ are controlled in total variation by those of $\ddc(a+Q)$ plus a uniformly bounded smooth form. Write $|\partial\bar\partial(a+q_{x,t})|$ for the sum of the total variations of its coefficient measures. The rescaled averaging kernels are supported in $B(x,Ct)$ and bounded by a constant times $t^{-2n}$, so their integrals against this measure are bounded by
\[
 C t^{-2n}|\partial\bar\partial(a+q_{x,t})|(B(x,Ct))
 \leq C't^{-2}.
\]
Thus the terms of the expansion with a coefficient $O(t^3)$ contribute $O(t)$, and the remainder of the expansion of order two is $O(t)$ as well. The mixed and vertical contributions are bounded by $C(|\xi||\tau|+|\tau|^2)$. By the choice of $A$, the curvature term is bounded below by $-A\lambda(x,t)|\xi|^2$; here the radial term of the same expansion identifies the relevant mass with $\lambda$ up to $O(t^2)$. Subtracting the regularization of $q_{x,t}$ restores $-\theta(x)$: its base Hessian differs from $\ddc q_{x,t}$ by $O(t)$, its mixed and vertical Hessians are bounded, and its radial logarithmic derivative is $O(t^2)$. The extra term $Bt\beta$ also costs only $O(t)$. For a real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha$, write $\alpha[v]^2$ for its associated Hermitian quadratic form evaluated on $v$. Consequently, for a fixed constant $C_1>0$, the complex Hessian of $(x,w)\mapsto R_{|w|}a(x)$ satisfies
\[
 \ddc_{x,w}R_{|w|}a[\xi,\tau]^2
 \geq-\theta_x[\xi]^2-(A\lambda(x,t)+Ct)|\xi|^2
       -C_1(|\xi||\tau|+|\tau|^2).
\]
All constants are independent of $x,t,c,\delta$ and uniform for normalized $a$. In particular, setting $\xi=0$ in the same expansion gives a uniform lower bound for the vertical Hessian. Adding a fixed multiple of $|w|^2$ makes the function subharmonic in $w$, which gives the logarithmic convexity and monotonicity of Step~1 with a uniform constant $K_0$.

\emph{Step 4: the minimum.} Take $K\geq K_0$ and add $K(t^2+t)$ to the regularization. The positive vertical Hessian of $K|w|$ absorbs the mixed term by the inequality
\[
 C_1|\xi||\tau|\leq C't|\xi|^2+\frac{K}{8t}|\tau|^2,
\]
and it absorbs the remaining bounded vertical error for small $t$. The logarithmic derivative of the corrected function is at least $\lambda$. The Kiselman minimum argument with an upper endpoint, as in the proof of \cref{lem:general-kiselman-modulus}, therefore gives the last inequality in \eqref{eq:smooth-background-transform-rate}, now with error $C\delta$. On compact subsets of $\Omega$, local boundedness of $a$ makes the term $-c\log(t/\delta)$ dominate uniformly as $t\downarrow0$, so that the minimum is attained away from zero.

\emph{Step 5: the remaining bounds.} The other bounds in \eqref{eq:smooth-background-transform-rate} follow from the monotonicity of $R_ta+Kt^2$ and from evaluation at $t=\delta$. In particular, the transform is bounded above near the analytic set $X\setminus\Omega$. The removable singularity theorem therefore extends it across this set, and local freezing and passage to the limit preserve the exact smooth lower bound for its Hessian. Its lower bound $a-K(\delta^2+\delta)$ also extends, since quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are determined by their values off an analytic set.

The adjoint computation in the proof of \cref{lem:chern-smoothing-integral} gives $R_t^*1=1+O(t^2)$. It only needs $a\in L^1$, since
\[
 \left|\int_X(R_ta-a)\,\mathrm{d}V\right|
 \leq Ct^2\|a\|_1.
\]
Together with $R_ta-a\geq-Kt^2$, this proves the stated $L^1$ estimate. Finally, near $K_1$ the smoothing kernel only samples a fixed larger compact subset of $\Omega$, on which $a$ is bounded. The kernel derivative estimate in the proof of \cref{lem:chern-smoothing-integral} then gives the bound $C_{K_1}/t$.
\end{proof}
