\section{The adjoint equation for Gauduchon volume}
\label{sec:gauduchon-volume-adjoint}

As for the Monge--Amp\`ere equation, solving the linearized Gauduchon volume equation requires a compatibility condition, given by an adjoint probability measure. Taking the $(n-1)$st root multiplies the trace linearization by the factor $n/(n-1)$, and the first-order term of the ansatz introduces a drift term. We compute the linearized operator at any potential with $\Gamma_u>0$, and then express its adjoint condition as an equation for a positive $(1,1)$-form. Throughout this section we put $K=\gamma^{n-2}$.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-volume-linearization}\index{Adjoint measure!Gauduchon volume equation}
On the open set of smooth potentials $u$ with $\Gamma_u>0$, the derivative of
\[
 \mathcal G(u)=\log\frac{\eta_u^n}{\gamma^n}
\]
is the real elliptic operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-volume-linearization}
 \mathcal L_uv=
 \frac{n}{n-1}\frac{\eta_u\wedge\mathcal D_\gamma v}{\eta_u^n}.
\end{equation}
It has no zeroth-order term, and its kernel consists of the constants. There is a unique smooth positive function $\rho_u$, normalized by $\int_X\rho_u\eta_u^n=1$, such that the probability measure
\[
 \mathrm{d}\mu_u^\gamma=\rho_u\eta_u^n
\]
satisfies $\int_X\mathcal L_uv\,\mathrm{d}\mu_u^\gamma=0$ for every smooth real $v$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a smooth path of potentials $u(t)$, write $\eta=\eta_{u(t)}$ and $\Gamma=\Gamma_{u(t)}$. Differentiating $\eta^{n-1}=\Gamma$ gives
$(n-1)\dot\eta\wedge\eta^{n-2}=\dot\Gamma$, where $\dot\Gamma=\mathcal D_\gamma\dot u$. Wedging with $\eta$ and dividing by $\eta^n$, we obtain
\[
 \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\log\eta^n
 =\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{\eta\wedge\dot\Gamma}{\eta^n},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:gaud-volume-linearization}.

Only the second-order term $\ddc v\wedge\gamma^{n-2}$ of $\mathcal D_\gamma v$ contributes to the principal symbol. For a nonzero complex covector $\zeta$ of type $(1,0)$, the form
\[
 \eta_u\wedge\mathrm i\zeta\wedge\overline\zeta
                       \wedge\gamma^{n-2}
\]
is a positive volume form. Hence the principal symbol is positive definite, and by compactness the operator is uniformly elliptic for each fixed $u$. The remaining term is of first order, and constants are annihilated, so the strong maximum principle gives $\ker\mathcal L_u=\mathbb R$.

The Fredholm and positivity arguments in the proof of \cref{thm:gauduchon} apply to this real elliptic operator with first-order terms and no zeroth-order term. Its index is zero by deformation to a Laplacian, so its formal adjoint with respect to $\eta_u^n$ has a one-dimensional kernel spanned by a smooth strictly positive function. Normalizing its integral to one gives $\rho_u$ and proves uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
The adjoint condition can be expressed as an equation for the positive $(1,1)$-form $\rho_u\eta_u$. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $A$, set
\[
 \mathcal A_\gamma(A)
 =\ddc A\wedge\gamma^{n-2}
 +\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}
              \partial A\wedge\bar\partial(\gamma^{n-2})\right).
\]
This is a real form of top degree, and $\mathcal A_\gamma$ is the formal adjoint of $\mathcal D_\gamma$ under the pairing of $(1,1)$-forms with $(n-1,n-1)$-forms, as the proof below shows.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-volume-form-adjoint}
The function $\rho_u$ of \cref{prop:gaud-volume-linearization} is characterized by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-volume-form-adjoint}
 \mathcal A_\gamma(\rho_u\eta_u)=0,
 \qquad \int_X\rho_u\eta_u^n=1.
\end{equation}
When $n=2$, its first equation is $\ddc(\rho_u\eta_u)=0$.
When $\eta_u=\gamma$, the function $\rho_u$ is constant and
$\mathrm{d}\mu_u^\gamma=\gamma^n/\int_X\gamma^n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first prove that, for every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $A$ and every smooth real function $v$ on $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-volume-integration}
 \int_X A\wedge\mathcal D_\gamma v
 =\int_X v\,\mathcal A_\gamma(A).
\end{equation}
Write $c=\mathrm i/(2\pi)$. Since $A,K,v$ are real, expanding the real part gives
\[
 \mathcal D_\gamma v
 =c\,\partial\bar\partial v\wedge K
  +\frac c2\bigl(\partial v\wedge\bar\partial K
                   -\bar\partial v\wedge\partial K\bigr).
\]
Integrating the first term by parts twice gives
$\int_Xv\,c\partial\bar\partial(A\wedge K)$,
and integrating each first-order term by parts once gives the correction
\[
 \int_Xv\,\frac c2
 \bigl(-\partial A\wedge\bar\partial K
       +\bar\partial A\wedge\partial K
       -2A\wedge\partial\bar\partial K\bigr).
\]
Finally,
\[
 \partial\bar\partial(A\wedge K)
 =\partial\bar\partial A\wedge K
   -\bar\partial A\wedge\partial K
   +\partial A\wedge\bar\partial K
   +A\wedge\partial\bar\partial K.
\]
Adding these contributions cancels the terms containing $A\wedge\partial\bar\partial K$ and leaves $\mathcal A_\gamma(A)$. This proves \eqref{eq:gaud-volume-integration}. Applied to $A=\rho_u\eta_u$, it shows by \eqref{eq:gaud-volume-linearization} that $\int_X\mathcal L_uv\,\mathrm{d}\mu_u^\gamma$ is a fixed positive multiple of $\int_Xv\,\mathcal A_\gamma(\rho_u\eta_u)$, so the adjoint condition is equivalent to \eqref{eq:gaud-volume-form-adjoint}.

For $n=2$, we have $K=1$, which gives the first special case. For the second, the product rule gives
\[
 \mathcal A_\gamma(\gamma)
 =\frac1{n-1}\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0.
\]
By uniqueness, $\rho_u$ is consequently the constant $(\int_X\gamma^n)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

For $n>2$ the equation for $\rho_u\eta_u$ involves the first derivatives of $\gamma^{n-2}$, so the fixed auxiliary metric $\gamma$ remains part of the adjoint equation; it is not simply the Gauduchon condition for $\eta_u$.
