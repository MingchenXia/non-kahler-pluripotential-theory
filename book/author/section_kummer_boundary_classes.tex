\section{Nonrigid classes on a quotient resolution}
\label{sec:kummer-boundary-classes}

In contrast with \cref{ex:irrational-torus-rigidity}, an irrational nef boundary ray can admit many smooth semipositive representatives. On a suitable two-dimensional torus, such representatives come from semipositive forms depending on a single real coordinate, with an irrational lattice direction making the ray irrational. If these forms vanish near the fixed points of the involution $z\mapsto-z$, they pass smoothly to the resolution of the quotient, a Kummer surface.

Let $A=\mathbb C^2/\Lambda$ be a complex two-torus, let $Y=A/\{\pm1\}$, and let $r\colon X\to Y$ be the minimal resolution. The fixed points of the involution are the sixteen points of $A[2]=(\frac12\Lambda)/\Lambda$, and their images are the sixteen singular points of $Y$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kummer-resolution-kahler}\index{Kummer resolution}
The surface $X$ is compact K\"ahler. Its exceptional set consists of sixteen disjoint projective lines with self-intersection $-2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Blow up the sixteen fixed points on $A$ and take the quotient by the lifted involution. Near one exceptional line, a blowup chart has coordinates $(u,v)$ with $z_1=u$ and $z_2=uv$, and the involution is $(u,v)\mapsto(-u,v)$. The quotient chart has smooth coordinates $(s,v)=(u^2,v)$. On the other chart, $v'=1/v$ and $s'=v^2s$. These charts describe the total space of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)$ near its zero section. The quotient is therefore smooth and resolves the double point, with the stated exceptional curve. Doing this at all fixed points gives the compact surface $X$.

Choose a flat K\"ahler form on $A$ that equals $\ddc\rho$, where $\rho=|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2$, on disjoint balls centered at the fixed points. Away from those points it descends to $Y$. On the resolution chart,
\[
 \rho=|s|(1+|v|^2),\qquad
 \rho^2=|s|^2(1+|v|^2)^2.
\]
Choose a smooth function $F$ on $[0,+\infty)$ with $F(\rho)=c\rho^2$ near zero, where $c>0$, with $F'>0$ on $(0,+\infty)$ and $F''\geq0$, and with $F(\rho)=\rho-C$ for $\rho\geq\delta$, for a sufficiently small $\delta>0$. Such a function is obtained by choosing $F'$ nondecreasing, linear near zero and equal to one beyond $\delta$. Replace the flat form in each ball by $\ddc F(\rho)$. This agrees with the flat form near the boundary of the ball and extends smoothly across the exceptional line. The resulting closed form $\eta$ is positive off the exceptional set, while along it $\eta$ is positive in the normal direction and zero in the tangent direction.

To restore positivity in the tangent direction, choose a smooth cutoff $\chi$ equal to one for $\rho\leq\delta$ and zero for $\rho\geq2\delta$. For $s\ne0$, set
\[
 q_v=\chi(\rho)\log\rho-\log|s|.
\]
Near $s=0$ this equals $\log(1+|v|^2)$, so it extends smoothly. On the overlap of the two charts, $q_{v'}-q_v=-2\log|v|$ is pluriharmonic, so their $\ddc$ forms agree. They define a smooth closed form $R$ supported in the resolved ball, positive in the tangent direction along the exceptional line and semipositive where $\chi=1$. Hence $\eta+\varepsilon R$ is positive where $\chi=1$, including along the line. On the compact transition annulus $\eta$ is already positive, so $\eta+\varepsilon R$ remains positive there for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, and outside the ball $R=0$. Adding these corrections in all the disjoint balls produces a K\"ahler form on $X$.
\end{proof}

Fix now $a\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q$ and take for $\Lambda$ the lattice generated by
\[
 \lambda_1=(1,0),\quad \lambda_2=(a,1),\quad
 \lambda_3=(\mathrm i,0),\quad \lambda_4=(0,\mathrm i).
\]
Write $z_j=x_j+\mathrm i y_j$. Since $y_1$ takes integer values on $\Lambda$, the coordinate $y_1\bmod\mathbb Z$ defines a map from $A$ to the circle $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. Call a smooth even function $h\colon\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\to[0,+\infty)$ \emph{admissible} if it has integral one and vanishes near $\frac12\mathbb Z$; such functions are obtained from even pairs of nonnegative bumps supported away from that set. For an admissible $h$, put
\[
 \theta_h=h(y_1)\,\mathrm dx_1\wedge\mathrm dy_1.
\]

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kummer-nonrigid-irrational}\index{Nef class!irrational nonrigid class}\index{Semipositive form!quotient resolution}
The forms $\theta_h$ descend and extend by zero to smooth semipositive closed forms $T_h$ on $X$. They all represent one nonzero nef Bott--Chern class $\alpha$ with $\alpha^2=0$. This class is not rigid, that is, it contains more than one closed positive current, and its image in real de Rham cohomology spans an irrational ray. Each $T_h$ vanishes on a neighborhood of the exceptional set.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The form $\theta_h$ is closed, because $\mathrm dh(y_1)$ is proportional to $\mathrm dy_1$, and it is semipositive. Since $h$ is even, $\theta_h$ is invariant under the involution. At every fixed point, $y_1$ belongs to $\frac12\mathbb Z$ modulo $\mathbb Z$, so the form vanishes on a neighborhood of that point. Its descent to the complement of the singular points of $Y$ therefore extends by zero across their resolved neighborhoods. This proves smoothness and the asserted vanishing.

Let $h_1,h_2$ be admissible and put $k=h_1-h_2$. This function is even, has mean zero and vanishes near $\frac12\mathbb Z$. Choose $f$ with
\[
 f'(y)=4\pi\int_0^y k(s)\,\mathrm ds,
 \qquad f''=4\pi k.
\]
The derivative $f'$ is periodic and odd, so its mean is zero, and $f$ can be chosen periodic and even. Evenness and the zero mean of $k$ give $\int_0^{1/2}k=0$, hence $f'=0$ near $\frac12\mathbb Z$. Therefore $f(y_1)$ descends, and it extends by constants near each exceptional curve to a smooth function $G$ on $X$. Since
\[
 \ddc f(y_1)=\frac{f''(y_1)}{4\pi}
                    \,\mathrm dx_1\wedge\mathrm dy_1
 \quad\text{on }A,
 \qquad\text{we have}\qquad
 \ddc G=T_{h_1}-T_{h_2}.
\]
Thus all the forms $T_h$ have the same Bott--Chern class $\alpha$. Distinct admissible functions give distinct forms, so $\alpha$ is not rigid. The forms are semipositive and have square zero, so $\alpha^2=0$. By \cref{lem:kummer-resolution-kahler}, adding any positive multiple of a K\"ahler form gives a K\"ahler representative of a nearby class; therefore $\alpha$ is nef.

By the mean-one condition, the periods of $\theta_h$ on the real two-tori generated by $(\lambda_1,\lambda_3)$ and by $(\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$ are $1$ and $a$, respectively. Translate both cycles by $(0,\mathrm i/4)$. The translated cycles lie in $\{y_2=1/4\}$ modulo $\mathbb Z$ and avoid the sixteen fixed points, and their periods are unchanged because the form is closed. Their images in $X$ lie in the complement of the exceptional curves, which is doubly covered without ramification by $A\setminus A[2]$. The first period shows that $\alpha\ne0$. If a nonzero multiple $b\alpha$ had a rational de Rham class, then evaluating it on these cycles would make both $b$ and $ba$ rational, contradicting $a\notin\mathbb Q$. Finally, since $\alpha^2=0$, Stokes' theorem shows that $\alpha$ itself has no K\"ahler representative, so it lies on the boundary of the nef cone.
\end{proof}
