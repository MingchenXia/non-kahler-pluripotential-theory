Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a domain, $n\geq1$, and let $b$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$. If potentials approach $b$ from below in local capacity, every limit of their Monge--Amp\`ere measures must dominate $(\ddc b)^n$. Truncation at a fixed distance below $b$ provides a common bound, and plurifine locality keeps the measure outside the small set where the truncation changes the potential.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:capacity-limit-measure-floor}\index{Monge--Ampere product@Monge--Amp\`ere product!capacity limit order}
Let $b,u_j$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$ with $u_j\leq b$ and $u_j\to b$ in local capacity. Then, for every nonnegative $f\in C_c(\Omega)$,
\[
 \liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_\Omega f(\ddc u_j)^n
      \geq\int_\Omega f(\ddc b)^n.
\]
In particular, every vague subsequential limit of $(\ddc u_j)^n$ dominates $(\ddc b)^n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First suppose that $f$ is supported in $B'$, where $B'\Subset B\Subset\Omega$ are concentric balls. Put $v_j=\max(u_j,b-1)$ and $E_j=\{u_j\leq b-1\}$. The functions $v_j$ lie between $b-1$ and $b$ and tend to $b$ in local capacity. Because of their common bounds, the continuity of bounded products recalled in \cref{sec:psh-local-capacity} gives $(\ddc v_j)^n\to(\ddc b)^n$ vaguely on $B$. With $R=\sup_B b-\inf_B b+1$, the capacity bound for bounded potentials gives
\[
 (\ddc v_j)^n(E_j\cap\operatorname{supp}f)
 \leq R^n\operatorname{cap}(E_j\cap\operatorname{supp}f,B)
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
where the limit holds because $E_j\subset\{|u_j-b|>1/2\}$. On the plurifine open Borel set $\{u_j>b-1\}$ we have $v_j=u_j$, so \cref{thm:bt-input} shows that the two measures agree there. By positivity,
\[
 \int f(\ddc u_j)^n
 \geq\int f(\ddc v_j)^n
       -\|f\|_\infty(\ddc v_j)^n(E_j\cap\operatorname{supp}f).
\]
Taking the lower limit proves the assertion for such $f$. For a general $f$, use a finite nonnegative partition of unity subordinate to a cover of its support by such balls. Applying the inequality along a vaguely convergent subsequence proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bounded-background-cluster-sets}\label{thm:zero-local-cluster-sets}\index{Monge--Ampere product@Monge--Amp\`ere product!cluster sets at a bounded background}\index{Monge--Ampere product@Monge--Amp\`ere product!full cluster sets}
Assume that $\Omega$ is bounded and $n\geq2$. For a set $F\subset\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F$ is vaguely closed and every $\mu\in F$ satisfies $\mu\geq(\ddc b)^n$;
\item there are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_j\leq b$ on $\Omega$, converging to $b$ in $L^p(\Omega)$ for every $1\leq p<\infty$ and in local capacity, whose full vague cluster set is $F$:
\[
 F=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal M_+(\Omega):\ (\ddc u_{j_k})^n\longrightarrow\mu
        \text{ vaguely for some }j_1<j_2<\cdots\right\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The empty set is allowed. In the second condition, the $u_j$ can be chosen with $u_j-b$ bounded, and smooth if $b$ is smooth.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In a metric space, the full cluster set of a sequence is the intersection of the closures of its tails: a point of this intersection can be approximated within $1/k$ at an index larger than the preceding one, successively for $k=1,2,\ldots$. Hence, if the second condition holds, $F$ is closed by \cref{lem:local-vague-metric}, and \cref{lem:capacity-limit-measure-floor} gives the measure inequality.

Conversely, assume the first condition. If $F$ is nonempty, choose a dense sequence $\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots$ in $F$, repetitions allowed; this is possible because a subset of a separable metric space is separable. Concatenating the finite lists $(\mu_1)$, $(\mu_1,\mu_2)$, $(\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)$, and so on, we obtain a sequence $(\nu_j)$ in $F$ in which every $\mu_k$ appears arbitrarily late. Hence every point of $F$ is the limit of a subsequence, and since $F$ is closed, no other subsequential limit occurs. If $F$ is empty, take $\nu_j=(\ddc b)^n+j\delta_a$ for a fixed $a\in\Omega$. A nonnegative compactly supported test function equal to one at $a$ has integral at least $j$ against $\nu_j$, so this sequence has no vaguely convergent subsequence. In either case, $\nu_j-(\ddc b)^n$ is a positive Radon measure.

Let $(f_i)$ and $d_v$ be as in \cref{lem:local-vague-metric}. For each $j$, perform the $j$-th step of the proof of \cref{thm:local-prescribed-excess} with the excess $\nu_j-(\ddc b)^n$ in place of $\nu$: approximate it against $f_1,\ldots,f_j$ by a finite positive atomic measure with errors less than $1/j$, and add its atoms successively to $b$ by \cref{prop:local-one-atom-addition}. As in that proof, $u_j\leq b$, $u_j-b$ is bounded, $u_j$ is smooth when $b$ is smooth, and
\[
 \|u_j-b\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,p}/j,
 \qquad
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i\bigl((\ddc u_j)^n-\nu_j\bigr)\right|
       <2/j\quad(i\leq j).
\]
The capacity estimates in that proof do not depend on the measure, so $u_j\to b$ in local capacity. Neither convergence of the $\nu_j$ nor a common local mass bound for them is needed.

The error in the first $j$ coordinates and the tail of the series yield
\[
 d_v\bigl((\ddc u_j)^n,\nu_j\bigr)\leq 2/j+2^{-j}\longrightarrow0.
\]
By the remark following \cref{lem:local-vague-metric}, the two sequences of measures have the same limits along corresponding subsequences. Hence their full vague cluster sets coincide.
\end{proof}

For $b=0$, the first condition says only that $F$ is vaguely closed: every vaguely closed subset of $\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$, including the empty set, is the full vague cluster set of smooth bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_j\leq0$ tending to zero in every $L^p(\Omega)$, $1\leq p<\infty$, and in local capacity. On the unit ball, the function $b=|z|^2-1$ has nonzero Monge--Amp\`ere measure. For every point $a$ of the ball, the theorem allows the cluster sets $\{(\ddc b)^n\}$, $\{(\ddc b)^n+\delta_a\}$ and the empty set, but it excludes $\{0\}$; this exclusion relies on the convergence in local capacity. In dimension one, by contrast, $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ convergence alone forces $\ddc u_j\to\ddc b$ in the sense of distributions, hence vaguely by positivity, so the full cluster set is always $\{\ddc b\}$; the freedom in the theorem begins in dimension two.
