\extrachap{Conventions}

Unless otherwise stated, $X$ denotes a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$. A Hermitian form is a smooth real strictly positive $(1,1)$-form. The letter $\omega$ is reserved for such a form, while $\theta$ denotes an arbitrary smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Whenever $\theta$ or $\omega$ is assumed to be closed, this is stated explicitly.

We follow the normalizations of \cite{SGPT}:
\[
 \dc=\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}(\bar\partial-\partial),
 \qquad \ddc=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial.
\]
Thus, on a disk in $\mathbb C$ with coordinate $z$, $\ddc\log|z|^2$ is the unit Dirac measure at the origin. Multiplier ideals of a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ are defined with the weight $\exp(-u)$, and Monge--Amp\`ere masses carry no factor $1/n!$.
