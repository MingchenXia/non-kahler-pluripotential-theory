\section{Partial envelopes on a weighted compact set}
\label{sec:hermitian-partial-envelopes}

The envelope entering the source theorem can be defined before any mass or section-growth assumption. In this section $\theta$ is any smooth real background with $\PSH(X,\theta)\ne\varnothing$, $K\subseteq X$ is closed and non-pluripolar, and $w\in C(K,\mathbb R)$. An inequality on $K$ holds quasi-everywhere if its exceptional set is pluripolar. We use the local facts that a countable union of pluripolar sets is pluripolar, and that the regularized supremum of a locally upper-bounded family of plurisubharmonic functions differs from its pointwise supremum only on a pluripolar set. These are standard local results recalled in \cite{SGPT}; smooth corrections give the same statements for a family with a common smooth curvature lower bound.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:compact-obstacle-upper-bound}
There is a constant $C=C(\theta,K,w)$ such that every $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfying $a\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$ has $\sup_X a\leq C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise choose such functions $a_j$ with
\[
 M_j:=\sup_Xa_j\geq 2^j+\max_Kw.
\]
The normalized functions $v_j=a_j-M_j\leq0$ have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms by \cref{chap:compactness}. Choose a Hermitian form $\omega$ and $A>0$ with $\theta\leq A\omega$. The partial sums
\[
 s_N=\sum_{j=1}^N2^{-j}v_j
\]
decrease, have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms, and satisfy $\ddc s_N\geq-A\omega$. Thus their limit is a genuine quasi-plurisubharmonic function $s$; the $L^1$ bound excludes the identically $-\infty$ alternative in the local decreasing-limit theorem.

Let $N_j\subseteq K$ be a pluripolar exceptional set for the $j$-th obstacle inequality. On $K\setminus\bigcup_jN_j$ one has $v_j\leq-2^j$ for every $j$, hence $s=-\infty$. The set where a genuine quasi-plurisubharmonic function is $-\infty$ is pluripolar. Together with the countable union of the $N_j$, it would contain all of $K$, contradicting the hypothesis on $K$.
\end{proof}

For $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-partial-i-envelope}
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)
 &=\Bigl(\sup\bigl\{a\in\PSH(X,\theta):
       a\preceq_{\mathcal I}\varphi,\\
 &\hspace{52pt}a\leq w\text{ quasi-everywhere on }K\bigr\}\Bigr)^*.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $a\preceq_{\mathcal I}\varphi$ means inclusion of multiplier ideals at every positive weight. It is the same order as in \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-partial-i-envelope}
The function $E=E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)$ is a genuine $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function and the largest member of its defining family. If $q=Q_\theta[\varphi]$, then
\[
 q+\min_Kw\leq E\leq q+C,
\]
where $C$ is any upper bound from \cref{lem:compact-obstacle-upper-bound}. In particular, $E$ has the ordinary singularity type of $q$, and
\[
 E\sim_{\mathcal I}\varphi.
\]
No positivity of Monge--Amp\`ere mass or bounded mass property is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The projection theorem gives $q\leq0$ and $q\sim_{\mathcal I}\varphi$. Thus $q+\min_Kw$ belongs to the defining family. The lemma bounds the entire family above by $C$, so its regularized supremum is genuine and gives the lower inequality.

For each competitor $a$, the upper bound $\sup_Xa\leq C$ and \cref{prop:p-below-i,prop:i-envelope-operations} give
\[
 a-C\leq a-\sup_Xa\leq Q_\theta[a]
                    \leq Q_\theta[\varphi]=q.
\]
Taking regularized suprema proves $E\leq q+C$.

The two bounds show that $E$ and $q$ differ by a bounded amount. They therefore have the same multiplier ideals, and these equal those of $\varphi$.

It remains to check the obstacle after regularization. Choose a countable subfamily by Choquet's lemma, and replace it by successive finite maxima. Finite maxima remain admissible, by the same valuative maximum formula and by taking the finite union of the obstacle's exceptional sets. Let $a_j$ be the resulting increasing sequence. Outside a countable union of pluripolar subsets of $K$, every $a_j$ is at most $w$. The unregularized limit is therefore at most $w$ there. The regularized limit equals $E$ and changes this limit only on a further pluripolar set, by the local supremum theorem. Thus $E\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$.

We have proved that $E$ is a member of its defining family. It dominates each competitor by construction, so it is the largest member; in particular the unregularized supremum in \eqref{eq:hermitian-partial-i-envelope} already equals $E$ at every point.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{25\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:partial-envelope-calculus}
The envelope depends only on the multiplier-ideal class of $\varphi$. It is increasing in the obstacle and in the multiplier-ideal order of $\varphi$. For $w,w'\in C(K)$ and $c\in\mathbb R$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w+c)
       &=E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)+c,\\
 \bigl|E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)
       -E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w')\bigr|
       &\leq\|w-w'\|_{C(K)}.
 \end{split}
\]
The second line means the two corresponding pointwise inequalities, including at poles. For $0<t<1$, backgrounds $\theta_i$, potentials $\varphi_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i)$ and obstacles $w_i\in C(K)$, put $\theta_t=(1-t)\theta_0+t\theta_1$, and similarly define $\varphi_t,w_t$. Then
\[
 (1-t)E_{\theta_0,K}[\varphi_0]_{\mathcal I}(w_0)
       +tE_{\theta_1,K}[\varphi_1]_{\mathcal I}(w_1)
 \leq E_{\theta_t,K}[\varphi_t]_{\mathcal I}(w_t).
\]
For a smooth real $f$, changing the background and obstacle gives the exact identity
\[
 E_{\theta+\ddc f,K}[\varphi-f]_{\mathcal I}(w-f|_K)
       =E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)-f.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equality or inclusion of the defining families proves the first two assertions. Adding a constant gives a bijection of competitor families, proving translation. Apply monotonicity to $w'\pm\|w-w'\|_{C(K)}$ to obtain the two inequalities in the second line.

For concavity, the left side is $\theta_t$-plurisubharmonic and obeys the obstacle $w_t$ outside the union of two pluripolar exceptional sets. Each of its summands has the multiplier-ideal class of its prescribed singularity by the theorem. Addition and positive scaling in \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus} give the multiplier-ideal class of $\varphi_t$. Thus the left side is an admissible competitor on the right. Finally, $a\mapsto a-f$ is a bijection of the two families in the gauge identity, and regularization commutes with subtraction of a smooth function.
\end{proof}

This supplies the partial-envelope calculus for a general Hermitian background. Identifying a limiting partial kernel measure with a Monge--Amp\`ere measure of this envelope requires further analysis. In particular, the order and obstacle arguments above do not prove a contact-support theorem or any mass identity. The quotient theorem below makes such an identification through the K\"ahler theory on its base.
