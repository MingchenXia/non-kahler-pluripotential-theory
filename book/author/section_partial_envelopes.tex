\section{Partial envelopes on a weighted compact set}
\label{sec:hermitian-partial-envelopes}

Two constraints act on limits of partial Fubini--Study potentials. The continuous weight is an obstacle on the set where sections are measured, and the selecting potential imposes a multiplier-ideal constraint on the whole manifold. The partial envelope is the largest potential satisfying both. Since an obstacle on a non-pluripolar compact set bounds every competitor globally, the partial envelope lies below a translate of the multiplier-ideal envelope of the selecting potential; conversely, a translate of that envelope lying below the obstacle is a competitor. The partial envelope therefore differs from the multiplier-ideal envelope by a bounded function.

Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, let $K\subseteq X$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $w\in C(K,\mathbb R)$. Recall that \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} characterizes the order $\preceq_{\mathcal I}$ by Lelong numbers on local modifications. As in \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}, an inequality holds quasi-everywhere if it holds outside a pluripolar set. The \emph{partial envelope}\index{Envelope!partial} of $\varphi$ with obstacle $w$ on $K$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian-partial-i-envelope}
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)
 &=\Bigl(\sup\bigl\{a\in\PSH(X,\theta):
       a\preceq_{\mathcal I}\varphi,\\
 &\hspace{52pt}a\leq w\text{ quasi-everywhere on }K\bigr\}\Bigr)^*.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-partial-i-envelope}
The function $E=E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, and it is the largest member of its defining family. Let $q=P_\theta[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}$, and let $C$ be a constant as in \cref{lem:compact-obstacle-upper-bound}, so that $\sup_Xa\leq C$ for every $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $a\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$. Then
\[
 q+\min_Kw\leq E\leq q+C.
\]
In particular, $E\sim q$ and
\[
 E\sim_{\mathcal I}\varphi.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection}, $q\leq0$ and $q\sim_{\mathcal I}\varphi$. Hence $q+\min_Kw$ belongs to the defining family. \Cref{lem:compact-obstacle-upper-bound} bounds the entire family above by $C$, so its regularized supremum $E$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, and $E\geq q+\min_Kw$.

For the upper bound, let $a$ be a member of the family. Since $\sup_Xa\leq C$, \cref{prop:p-below-i,prop:i-envelope-operations} give
\[
 a-C\leq a-\sup_Xa\leq P_\theta[a]_{\mathcal I}
                    \leq P_\theta[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}=q.
\]
Taking the regularized supremum over $a$ proves $E\leq q+C$.

The two bounds show that $E-q$ is bounded. Hence $E$ and $q$ have the same multiplier ideals, and these are the multiplier ideals of $\varphi$.

It remains to show that $E$ itself satisfies the obstacle condition, which is not automatic after regularization. By Choquet's lemma, $E$ is the regularization of the supremum of a countable subfamily. Replace its members by their successive finite maxima. Finite maxima are again members of the family: the multiplier-ideal order is preserved by \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}, and a finite union of pluripolar exceptional sets is pluripolar. We obtain an increasing sequence $a_j$ in the family whose limit has regularization $E$. Outside a countable union of pluripolar subsets of $K$, every $a_j$ is at most $w$, and hence so is $\lim_ja_j$. By the local upper-envelope theorem recalled in \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}, $E$ differs from $\lim_ja_j$ only on a pluripolar set. Thus $E\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$.

We have shown that $E$ belongs to its defining family. It dominates every member by construction, so it is the largest member. In particular, the supremum in \eqref{eq:hermitian-partial-i-envelope} equals $E$ at every point even before regularization.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{19\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:partial-envelope-calculus}
The partial envelope depends only on the multiplier-ideal type of $\varphi$, and it is increasing in the obstacle and in $\varphi$ with respect to $\preceq_{\mathcal I}$. For $w,w'\in C(K)$ and $c\in\mathbb R$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w+c)
       &=E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)+c,\\
 \bigl|E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)
       -E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w')\bigr|
       &\leq\|w-w'\|_{C(K)}.
 \end{split}
\]
The second line stands for the two corresponding pointwise inequalities, which make sense also at the poles. Let $\theta_0,\theta_1$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$, and let $\varphi_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i)$ and $w_i\in C(K)$ for $i=0,1$. For $0<t<1$ put $\theta_t=(1-t)\theta_0+t\theta_1$, and define $\varphi_t$ and $w_t$ in the same way. Then
\[
 (1-t)E_{\theta_0,K}[\varphi_0]_{\mathcal I}(w_0)
       +tE_{\theta_1,K}[\varphi_1]_{\mathcal I}(w_1)
 \leq E_{\theta_t,K}[\varphi_t]_{\mathcal I}(w_t).
\]
Finally, for a smooth real function $f$, changing the background and the obstacle gives the identity
\[
 E_{\theta+\ddc f,K}[\varphi-f]_{\mathcal I}(w-f|_K)
       =E_{\theta,K}[\varphi]_{\mathcal I}(w)-f.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first two assertions hold because the defining families are equal or nested. Adding a constant $c$ is a bijection between the families for $w$ and $w+c$, which proves the translation rule. Applying monotonicity and translation to $w'-\|w-w'\|_{C(K)}\leq w\leq w'+\|w-w'\|_{C(K)}$ gives the two inequalities in the second line.

For concavity, the left-hand side is $\theta_t$-plurisubharmonic, and it lies below $w_t$ outside the union of two pluripolar exceptional sets. By \cref{thm:hermitian-partial-i-envelope}, its two summands have the multiplier-ideal types of $\varphi_0$ and $\varphi_1$, so the addition and scaling rules of \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus} show that the left-hand side has the multiplier-ideal type of $\varphi_t$. It is therefore a member of the family defining the right-hand side. Finally, $a\mapsto a-f$ is a bijection between the two families in the last identity, and regularization commutes with subtraction of a continuous function.
\end{proof}

In the next section the partial envelope bounds every limit of partial Fubini--Study potentials. The point is to show that both constraints, the multiplier-ideal order and the obstacle on $K$, survive $L^1$ limits.
