\subsection{The boundary volume and section growth}
\label{sec:elliptic-boundary-volume}

Although no class $a\{\alpha\}+b\{\beta\}$ is big, its small Hermitian perturbations have positive Gauduchon variational volume $\mathcal V_\mu$ (\cref{def:gaud-variational-volume}). For the volume form of the balanced metric, the zero potential solves the normalized Monge--Amp\`ere equation for every positive combination of $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $D$, so the volume is an explicit cubic polynomial in the perturbation parameter. Its order of vanishing at the nef boundary records how many of the two horizontal coefficients vanish.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-boundary-volume}\index{Elliptic fibration!boundary volume}
On the elliptic threefold, let $a,b\geq0$, put $\theta=a\alpha+b\beta$, and let $\mu_0=\omega^3/(24\pi)$. Then for every $\epsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-boundary-volume}
 \mathcal V_{\mu_0}(\theta+\epsilon\omega)
       =24\pi\epsilon(a+\epsilon)(b+\epsilon).
\end{equation}
In particular, $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta)=0$ for every smooth positive probability measure $\mu$. Let $q$ be the number of positive coefficients among $a,b$. Then the degeneration orders of \cref{prop:nef-degeneration-orders} are
\[
 \underline a(\theta)=\overline a(\theta)=3-q,
\]
for every smooth positive probability measure and every Hermitian perturbation direction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The forms $\alpha,\beta,D$ are semipositive of rank one, so their squares vanish, and their sum $\omega$ is strictly positive. Thus
\[
 \Omega_\epsilon=\theta+\epsilon\omega
       =(a+\epsilon)\alpha+(b+\epsilon)\beta+\epsilon D>0.
\]
Since $\omega^3=6\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge D$ and $\int_X\omega^3=24\pi$, the wedge expansion gives
\[
 \Omega_\epsilon^3
   =6\epsilon(a+\epsilon)(b+\epsilon)\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge D
   =24\pi\epsilon(a+\epsilon)(b+\epsilon)\mu_0.
\]
Hence the zero potential solves the smooth normalized equation for $\Omega_\epsilon$ and $\mu_0$. By \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}, the scalar of this equation is $\mathcal V_{\mu_0}(\Omega_\epsilon)$, which proves \eqref{eq:elliptic-boundary-volume}. The continuity of the volume at nef classes in \cref{thm:gaud-volume-nef-limit} and comparison of smooth densities then give $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta)=0$.

As $\epsilon\downarrow0$, the leading term of \eqref{eq:elliptic-boundary-volume} is $24\pi ab\epsilon$ if $a$ and $b$ are both positive, $24\pi(a+b)\epsilon^2$ if exactly one of them is positive, and $24\pi\epsilon^3$ if both vanish. The logarithmic quotients defining the orders therefore have limits $1,2,3$, respectively. Their independence of the density and of the perturbing metric is part of \cref{prop:nef-degeneration-orders}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
The same number $q$ governs the growth of sections. For nonnegative integers $a,b$, the line bundle $L=\pi^*\mathcal O_B(a,b)$ satisfies
\[
 h^0(X,L^m)=(am+1)(bm+1),
\]
since the descent along compact fibers and the monomial count in the proof of \cref{prop:elliptic-selected-section-descent} also apply when one or both integers vanish. The growth degree of these spaces is exactly $q$, whereas the boundary order of the smooth curvature form $a\alpha+b\beta$ of $L$ is $3-q$. In this explicit family, the boundary order thus records the loss of algebraic growth dimension, even though the three-dimensional Monge--Amp\`ere measures in the unperturbed background vanish.

Formula \eqref{eq:elliptic-boundary-volume} concerns one fixed smooth density; it does not assert that a total mass is conserved over all potentials. Indeed, by \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass} every Hermitian form on $X$, in particular each $\Omega_\epsilon$, has zero lower mass, while its upper mass is finite because $X$ has the bounded mass property (\cref{def:bmp,prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric}). Whether comparable identifications hold beyond such explicit families is part of \cref{qu:nef-degeneration-exponent}.
