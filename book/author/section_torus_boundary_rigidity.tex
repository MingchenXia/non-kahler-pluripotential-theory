\section{Rigid boundary classes on a torus}
\label{sec:torus-boundary-rigidity}

A nef class with zero top intersection can have a smooth semipositive representative and still leave little freedom to vary that representative. On a torus, the kernel of a constant form determines this freedom. It also determines whether adding a small fixed positive form collapses the metric to a point. The direction of approximation matters for this relation.

Let $X=V/\Lambda$ be a complex torus, where $V\simeq\mathbb C^n$ and $\Lambda$ is a full lattice. Let $\theta$ be a translation-invariant semipositive real $(1,1)$-form of rank $1\leq r<n$, and fix a translation-invariant K\"ahler form $\kappa$. The class $\alpha=\{\theta\}_{\mathrm{BC}}$ is nef and has $\int_X\theta^n=0$. It is a boundary class: a K\"ahler representative would have positive top intersection, contrary to Stokes' theorem. Call $\alpha$ \emph{rigid} if it contains exactly one closed positive current. Put $K=\ker\theta\subset V$, and let $H\subset X$ be the closure of its image. This is a connected real subtorus; it need not be complex. Diameters below use the real metric $g_\eta(v,w)=\eta(v,Jw)$ associated with a positive form $\eta$.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:flat-torus-radial-rigidity}\index{Nef class!rigid torus boundary}\index{Metric degeneration!dense kernel leaves}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\alpha$ is rigid;
\item $H=X$;
\item $\operatorname{diam}(X,\theta+t\kappa)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$.
\end{enumerate}
In the first case the unique current is $\theta$. The third condition is independent of the chosen translation-invariant K\"ahler form $\kappa$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $H=X$. Write a positive current in $\alpha$ as $T=\theta+\ddc u$, with $u$ quasi-plurisubharmonic and in $L^1(X)$, using \cref{lem:bc-one-one-current-description}. For a constant real vector $v\in K$, the coefficient $T(v,Jv)$ is a positive Radon measure. Its total integral against Haar volume is zero: $\theta(v,Jv)=0$, and the remaining coefficient is a constant derivative of $u$. Hence that coefficient vanishes. Write $D_v$ for differentiation along $v$. Since $K$ is complex, this gives
\[
 (D_v^2+D_{Jv}^2)u=0
 \quad\text{as a distribution for every }v\in K.
\]
For a real linear functional $\ell$ with $\ell(\Lambda)\subset\mathbb Z$, let $\widehat u(\ell)$ be the Fourier coefficient at the character $\exp(2\pi\mathrm i\ell)$. The displayed identity gives
\[
 \bigl(\ell(v)^2+\ell(Jv)^2\bigr)\widehat u(\ell)=0.
\]
A nonzero character cannot vanish on $K$ when its image is dense. Thus every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of $u$ is zero. Convolution with a smooth approximate identity shows that $u$ is constant, so $T=\theta$.

If $H\ne X$, a nontrivial character of the real torus $X/H$ gives a nonzero $\ell$ annihilating $K$. Put $\phi=\cos(2\pi\ell)$. The form $\ddc\phi$ annihilates $K$ in both arguments, since $K$ is complex. On a fixed complement of $K$, $\theta$ is positive definite and $\ddc\phi$ is uniformly bounded. Consequently $\theta\pm\varepsilon\ddc\phi$ are semipositive for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. They are distinct: a nonconstant smooth function on a compact torus cannot have zero $\ddc$. They represent the same class, proving nonrigidity.

For the metric assertion, suppose $H=X$ and fix $\delta>0$. Density and compactness give finitely many vectors $k_1,\ldots,k_N\in K$ whose images form a $\delta$-net in the flat $\kappa$ metric. Put $R=\max_j|k_j|_\kappa$. A straight path to the image of $k_j$ has $(\theta+t\kappa)$-length $\sqrt t\,|k_j|_\kappa$. For $0<t\leq1$ these metrics are bounded above by a fixed multiple of $\kappa$. Joining this path to a point within $\delta$ therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(X,\theta+t\kappa)
       \leq\sqrt t\,R+C\delta.
\]
Translation invariance makes the supremum of distances from zero equal to the diameter. First let $t\downarrow0$ at this fixed net, then let $\delta\downarrow0$.

If $H\ne X$, choose $\ell$ as above. Its differential annihilates $K$, so $|\mathrm d\ell(v)|\leq L|v|_\theta$ for some $L>0$. Since $\theta+t\kappa\geq\theta$, the surjective circle map $\ell\bmod\mathbb Z$ is $L$-Lipschitz for every $t>0$. Two points mapping to opposite points of the unit-length circle have distance at least $1/(2L)$. Thus the diameters cannot tend to zero. This completes all equivalences.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:irrational-torus-rigidity}\index{Nef class!irrational rigid torus class}
On $X=(\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z))^2$, for $a\in\mathbb R$ set
\[
 \theta_a=\frac{\mathrm i}{2}
       (\mathrm dz_2-a\,\mathrm dz_1)
       \wedge(\mathrm d\bar z_2-a\,\mathrm d\bar z_1).
\]
Its class is rigid exactly when $a$ is irrational. In that case its ray is irrational and every family $\theta_a+t\kappa$ as above collapses to a point in diameter. For rational $a$ the class is nonrigid and these diameters have a positive lower bound.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The kernel is the complex line $z_2=a z_1$. Write $z_j=x_j+\mathrm i y_j$. An integral character has the form
\[
 \ell=m_1x_1+m_2x_2+n_1y_1+n_2y_2,
 \qquad m_j,n_j\in\mathbb Z.
\]
It annihilates the kernel exactly when $m_1+a m_2=n_1+a n_2=0$. For irrational $a$ only the zero character does so, hence the kernel image is dense. For rational $a$, a nonzero such character exists. Apply \cref{thm:flat-torus-radial-rigidity}. The periods of $\theta_a$ on the coordinate two-tori in $(x_2,y_2)$ and $(x_1,y_2)$ are respectively $1$ and $-a$. If a nonzero multiple of its class were rational, the first period would make that multiple rational and the second would make $a$ rational. This proves the ray assertion.
\end{proof}

The fixed positive direction in the theorem cannot be replaced by arbitrary convergence of K\"ahler forms, even when all the metrics are flat and Ricci-flat.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:unrestricted-torus-collapse}\index{Metric degeneration!unrestricted class convergence}
On the same square torus let $\theta_0=\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\mathrm dz_2\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_2$ and, for $t>0$, put
\[
 \beta_t=\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\left[
    (t\,\mathrm dz_1+\mathrm dz_2)
      \wedge(t\,\mathrm d\bar z_1+\mathrm d\bar z_2)
       +t^2\mathrm dz_2\wedge\mathrm d\bar z_2\right].
\]
These are flat Ricci-flat K\"ahler forms, $\beta_t\to\theta_0$ smoothly, and
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(X,\beta_t)\leq t.
\]
The limiting class $\{\theta_0\}_{\mathrm{BC}}$ is nonrigid.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}t^2&t\\t&1+t^2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with determinant $t^4>0$. Its coefficients are constant, giving flatness and Ricci-flatness. Take representatives $z_1,z_2$ with real and imaginary parts in $[-1/2,1/2]$, and choose a Gaussian integer $k$ nearest to $-z_1-z_2/t$. The displacement $(z_1+k,z_2)$ represents the same torus point and has squared metric norm
\[
 |t(z_1+k)+z_2|^2+t^2|z_2|^2
       \leq t^2/2+t^2/2=t^2.
\]
Its straight path proves the distance bound from zero; translation proves the diameter bound. For $0<|s|<1/\pi$, the form $\theta_0+\ddc(s\cos(2\pi x_2))=(1-\pi s\cos(2\pi x_2))\theta_0$ is another semipositive representative, proving nonrigidity. The coefficient matrix of $\beta_t-\theta_0$ has eigenvalues $t^2\pm t$, so this difference is indefinite for $0<t<1$.
\end{proof}
