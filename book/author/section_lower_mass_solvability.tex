\section{Lower mass and solvability for all measures}
\label{sec:lower-mass-solvability}

For a Hermitian background $h$, solvability for every finite non-pluripolar measure is equivalent to positive lower mass. When $v_-(h)=0$, smooth densities with rapidly decreasing normalizers can be summed to produce a density admitting no positive scaled subsolution. Strict domination supplies the bound that makes this construction work. In the remaining sections, $h$ denotes a Hermitian form on $X$.

For nonzero $0\leq q\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ with $p>1$, let $c_h(q)>0$ be the scalar in its continuous equation, supplied by \cref{thm:kn-continuous,prop:vanishing-exponent}. For the measure $\nu=q\,\mathrm{d}V$ we also write $c_h(\nu)=c_h(q)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-normalizer-subsolution}\label{lem:smooth-normalizer-subsolution-bound}
If $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ and $a>0$ satisfy
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)\geq aq\,\mathrm{d}V,
\]
then $a\leq c_h(q)$. Consequently, for nonzero nonnegative bounded densities $q,r$,
\[
 q\leq r\ \Longrightarrow\ c_h(q)\geq c_h(r),
 \qquad c_h(tq)=t^{-1}c_h(q)\quad(t>0).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be a continuous solution for $q$. If $a>c_h(q)$, put $\tau=c_h(q)/a<1$. Every translate $v+A$ admits a genuine envelope in background $h/2$, and $u\preceq v+A$. The inequality
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(v+A)=c_h(q)q\,\mathrm{d}V
       \leq\tau\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)
\]
and \cref{thm:singular-strict-domination} give $v+A\geq u$ for every real $A$. Letting $A\to-\infty$ at a point where $u$ is finite is a contradiction.

If $q\leq r$, a continuous solution for $r$ is a subsolution for $c_h(r)q\,\mathrm{d}V$, giving the order inequality. Scalar uniqueness gives the homogeneity. The comparison used only boundedness of $v$, so its first conclusion also holds for any nonnegative density that has a bounded solution with scalar $c_h(q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lp-normalizer-continuity}
Fix $p>1$. If nonzero nonnegative densities $q_j,q\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ satisfy $q_j\to q$ in $L^p$, then $c_h(q_j)\to c_h(q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Gauduchon trace inequality bounds $c_h(q_j)$ above, since $q_j^{1/n}\to q^{1/n}$ in $L^n$ and every fixed positive trace test has a positive integral at $q$. The lower-density estimate of \cref{thm:lp-potential-estimate} bounds the scalars away from zero. Normalized continuous solutions consequently have uniformly bounded $L^p$ densities. By \cref{thm:lp-uniform-compactness}, every subsequence has a further subsequence on which the potentials converge uniformly and the scalars converge to some $c>0$. Bounded product continuity identifies the limit equation as $\mathrm{MA}_h(u)=cq\,\mathrm{d}V$. Scalar uniqueness gives $c=c_h(q)$, proving convergence of the original sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lower-mass-obstructing-measure}\index{Non-pluripolar measure!solvability obstruction}
Suppose $v_-(h)=0$. There is a probability measure $\mu=f\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ and $f\geq m>0$ almost everywhere such that no $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ and $a>0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-mass-impossible-subsolution}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)\geq a\mu.
\end{equation}
In particular, the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=c\mu$ has no solution for any $c>0$, even without a prescribed singularity type or a finite-mass requirement on the candidate potential.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema}, there are smooth strictly positive probability measures $\nu_j=f_j\,\mathrm{d}V$ such that
\[
 c_h(\nu_j)\leq 2^{-2j}\qquad(j\geq1).
\]
Set
\[
 \mu=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\nu_j,
 \qquad f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}f_j.
\]
Monotone convergence gives $\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}V=1$, so $f\in L^1$. Also $f\geq\tfrac12\min_X f_1=:m>0$ almost everywhere. Absolute continuity with respect to smooth volume makes $\mu$ non-pluripolar.

If \eqref{eq:lower-mass-impossible-subsolution} held, then for every $j$
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)\geq a2^{-j}\nu_j.
\]
\Cref{lem:bounded-normalizer-subsolution} would imply $a2^{-j}\leq c_h(\nu_j)\leq2^{-2j}$. Thus $a\leq2^{-j}$ for all $j$, contradicting $a>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:positive-lower-mass-solvability}
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $v_-(h)>0$.
\item Every nonzero finite positive non-pluripolar measure $\mu$ admits $c>0$ and $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ such that
$\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=c\mu$.
\item For every probability density $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ bounded below by a positive constant, there are $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ and $a>0$ such that
$\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)\geq af\,\mathrm{d}V$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the reference zero, $P_h[0]=0$ and $P_{\varepsilon h}(0)=0$ for every $0<\varepsilon<1$. The form $h$ is semipositive and big. Thus (1) implies (2) by \cref{thm:als-general-measure}. Condition (2) implies (3), since an absolutely continuous measure is non-pluripolar. The preceding theorem proves the contrapositive of (3) implies (1).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lower-mass-lone-instability}
Assume $v_-(h)=0$, and let $\mu=f\,\mathrm{d}V$ be the measure constructed above. Then $f\notin L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ for every $p>1$. Moreover, for every nonnegative probability density $g\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ and every $0<t<1$, the probability density
\[
 g_t=(1-t)g+tf
\]
admits no positive scaled subsolution as in \eqref{eq:lower-mass-impossible-subsolution}. Nevertheless $\|g_t-g\|_{L^1}\leq2t$. In particular, densities with this obstruction are dense among probability densities in $L^1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $f\in L^p$ for some $p>1$, \cref{thm:kn-continuous} would give a continuous solution of $\mathrm{MA}_h(u)=cf\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $c>0$, contradicting the theorem. Since $g_t\,\mathrm{d}V\geq t\mu$, a positive scaled subsolution for $g_t\,\mathrm{d}V$ would give one for $\mu$. Finally
$\|g_t-g\|_1=t\|f-g\|_1\leq2t$.
\end{proof}

Smooth strictly positive probability densities are also dense in $L^1$ and always admit a smooth normalized solution. Thus ordinary $L^1$ approximation alone cannot preserve solvability when the lower mass vanishes. The obstruction concerns the concentration of the density; it occurs even when that density is bounded below by a positive constant. It is realized on the compact threefold of \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}.

This argument does not exclude unbounded solutions of the exponential equation. If $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$ with $\lambda>0$, the factor $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ need not have a positive lower bound. It does exclude bounded solutions for the measure above. Determining whether the exponential equation can absorb this concentration, and characterizing individual measures that admit a normalized solution when $v_-(h)=0$, are distinct questions.
