\subsection{Full cluster sets}
\label{subsec:zero-local-cluster-sets}\label{subsec:bounded-background-cluster-sets}

\Cref{thm:local-prescribed-excess} prescribes one limit of the Monge--Amp\`ere measures, but a single sequence of potentials can have many subsequential limits. On a bounded domain in dimension at least two, the sets of all subsequential limits for potentials that converge from below in local capacity to a locally bounded potential $b$ are exactly the vaguely closed sets of measures dominating $(\ddc b)^n$ (\cref{thm:bounded-background-cluster-sets}); for $b=0$, every vaguely closed set occurs. For a domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, write $\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$ for the set of positive Radon measures on $\Omega$, including zero and measures of infinite total mass, with the vague topology defined by integration against $C_c(\Omega)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-vague-metric}\index{Vague convergence!metric}
There are real functions $f_i\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ such that
\[
 d_v(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{i\geq1}2^{-i}
 \frac{\left|\int_\Omega f_i(\alpha-\beta)\right|}
      {1+\left|\int_\Omega f_i(\alpha-\beta)\right|}
\]
is a metric inducing the vague topology on $\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$. This metric space is separable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the functions $f_i$ as at the beginning of the proof of \cref{thm:local-prescribed-excess}, which does not use the boundedness of the domain, and call the maps $\alpha\mapsto\int_\Omega f_i\,\mathrm d\alpha$ the coordinates. As shown there, positive measures that converge to a fixed measure in every coordinate converge vaguely. The same argument, applied near a fixed measure, shows that $\alpha\mapsto\int_\Omega g\,\mathrm d\alpha$ is continuous for the topology generated by the coordinates: bound the relevant cutoff coordinate on a neighborhood, choose one smooth test that is uniformly close to $g$, and shrink the neighborhood using the coordinate of that test. Thus the coordinates generate exactly the vague topology, and they separate measures.

The function $s\mapsto s/(1+s)$ is increasing and subadditive on $[0,\infty)$. Applying it to the triangle inequalities for the coordinates gives the triangle inequality for $d_v$, and $d_v$ is positive on distinct measures because the coordinates separate them. Each coordinate is continuous for this metric, and the tails of the series are uniformly small, so the metric induces exactly the topology generated by the coordinates.

Finally, the finite atomic measures with nonnegative rational weights at points of a fixed countable dense subset of $\Omega$ form a countable dense family. Indeed, the proof of \cref{thm:local-prescribed-excess} approximates every measure in finitely many coordinates by a finite positive atomic measure; small changes of the locations and weights of its atoms change these coordinates arbitrarily little, and the remaining tail of the metric is arbitrarily small.
\end{proof}

The metric controls limits along corresponding subsequences: if $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ and one of the sequences converges vaguely to a fixed measure along a subsequence, then, by the triangle inequality, so does the other. Without common local mass bounds, however, $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ need not imply that $\int g(\alpha_j-\beta_j)\to0$ for every $g\in C_c(\Omega)$. For example, fix $a\in\Omega$, a unit vector $e$, and numbers $r_j\downarrow0$ with $a+r_je\in\Omega$, and put
\[
 \alpha_j=r_j^{-1/2}\delta_{a+r_je},\qquad
 \beta_j=r_j^{-1/2}\delta_a.
\]
For every smooth compactly supported test, the difference of the two integrals is $O(r_j^{1/2})$, so $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ because the tails of the series are small. On the other hand, if $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ equals one near $a$, the continuous test function $g(z)=\chi(z)|z-a|^{1/2}$ gives $\int g(\alpha_j-\beta_j)=1$ for all large $j$.
