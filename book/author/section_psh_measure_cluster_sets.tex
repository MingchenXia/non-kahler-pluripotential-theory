\subsection{Full cluster sets at the zero potential}
\label{subsec:zero-local-cluster-sets}

\Cref{thm:local-prescribed-excess} prescribes one limit of the Monge--Amp\`ere measures, but a single sequence of potentials can have many subsequential limits. In fact, every vaguely closed set of measures is the set of all subsequential limits for a suitable sequence of potentials tending to zero. For a domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, write $\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$ for the set of positive Radon measures on $\Omega$, including zero and measures of infinite total mass, with the vague topology defined by integration against $C_c(\Omega)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-vague-metric}\index{Vague convergence!metric}
There are real functions $f_i\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ such that
\[
 d_v(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{i\geq1}2^{-i}
 \frac{\left|\int_\Omega f_i(\alpha-\beta)\right|}
      {1+\left|\int_\Omega f_i(\alpha-\beta)\right|}
\]
is a metric inducing the vague topology on $\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$. This metric space is separable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose relatively compact open sets $U_\ell$ exhausting $\Omega$, with $\overline U_\ell\subset U_{\ell+1}$. For each $\ell$, choose a countable uniformly dense subset of $C_0(U_{\ell+1};\mathbb R)$ consisting of smooth compactly supported functions, extended by zero to $\Omega$; such families exist because $C_0(U_{\ell+1};\mathbb R)$ is separable and its elements can be approximated uniformly by smooth functions. Add a nonnegative smooth compactly supported cutoff function equal to one near $\overline U_{\ell+1}$, and enumerate the union of all these functions as $(f_i)$.

Call the maps $\alpha\mapsto\int_\Omega f_i\,\mathrm d\alpha$ the coordinates. If positive measures converge to a fixed measure in every coordinate, the cutoff coordinates give common mass bounds on each $\overline U_{\ell+1}$, and uniform approximation then gives convergence against every $g\in C_c(\Omega)$. The same argument, applied near a fixed measure, shows that $\alpha\mapsto\int_\Omega g\,\mathrm d\alpha$ is continuous for the topology generated by the coordinates: bound the relevant cutoff coordinate on a neighborhood, choose one smooth test that is uniformly close to $g$, and shrink the neighborhood using the coordinate of that test. Thus the coordinates generate exactly the vague topology, and they separate measures.

The function $s\mapsto s/(1+s)$ is increasing and subadditive on $[0,\infty)$. Applying it to the triangle inequalities for the coordinates gives the triangle inequality for $d_v$, and $d_v$ is positive on distinct measures because the coordinates separate them. Each coordinate is continuous for this metric, and the tails of the series are uniformly small, so the metric induces exactly the topology generated by the coordinates.

Finally, the finite atomic measures with nonnegative rational weights at points of a fixed countable dense subset of $\Omega$ form a countable dense family. To approximate a measure in finitely many coordinates, partition a compact neighborhood of the supports of the corresponding tests into sets on which all these tests have small oscillation, place an atom in each set of nonzero mass, and then perturb its location and weight. Only the finite mass of that compact neighborhood is used, and the remaining tail of the metric is arbitrarily small.
\end{proof}

The metric controls limits along corresponding subsequences: if $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ and one of the sequences converges vaguely to a fixed measure along a subsequence, then, by the triangle inequality, so does the other. Without common local mass bounds, however, $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ need not imply that $\int g(\alpha_j-\beta_j)\to0$ for every $g\in C_c(\Omega)$. For example, fix $a\in\Omega$, a unit vector $e$, and numbers $r_j\downarrow0$ with $a+r_je\in\Omega$, and put
\[
 \alpha_j=r_j^{-1/2}\delta_{a+r_je},\qquad
 \beta_j=r_j^{-1/2}\delta_a.
\]
For every smooth compactly supported test, the difference of the two integrals is $O(r_j^{1/2})$, so $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ because the tails of the series are small. On the other hand, if $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ equals one near $a$, the continuous test function $g(z)=\chi(z)|z-a|^{1/2}$ gives $\int g(\alpha_j-\beta_j)=1$ for all large $j$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:zero-local-cluster-sets}\index{Monge--Ampere product@Monge--Amp\`ere product!full cluster sets}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq2$, be a bounded domain. A set $F\subset\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$ is vaguely closed if and only if there are smooth bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_j\leq0$ on $\Omega$, tending to zero in $L^p(\Omega)$ for every $1\leq p<\infty$ and in local capacity, whose full vague cluster set is $F$:
\[
 F=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal M_+(\Omega):\ (\ddc u_{j_k})^n\longrightarrow\mu
        \text{ vaguely for some }j_1<j_2<\cdots\right\}.
\]
The empty set is allowed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In a metric space, the full cluster set of a sequence is the intersection of the closures of its tails: a point of this intersection can be approximated within $1/k$ at an index larger than the preceding one, successively for $k=1,2,\ldots$. The full cluster set is therefore closed, and \cref{lem:local-vague-metric} proves necessity.

For sufficiency, suppose first that $F$ is nonempty and closed. A subset of a separable metric space is separable, so we may choose a dense sequence $\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots$ in $F$, repetitions allowed. Concatenating the finite lists $(\mu_1)$, $(\mu_1,\mu_2)$, $(\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)$, and so on, we obtain a sequence $(\nu_j)$ in which every $\mu_k$ appears arbitrarily late. Hence every point of $F$ is the limit of a subsequence, and since $F$ is closed, no other subsequential limit occurs. If $F$ is empty, take $\nu_j=j\delta_a$ for a fixed $a\in\Omega$. A nonnegative compactly supported test function equal to one at $a$ has integral $j$ against $\nu_j$, so this sequence has no vaguely convergent subsequence.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
In either case, let $(f_i)$ and $d_v$ be as in \cref{lem:local-vague-metric}, and perform the finite atomic construction from the proof of \cref{thm:local-prescribed-excess} separately for each $j$, with background zero, target $\nu_j$ and tests $f_1,\ldots,f_j$. Thus we approximate $\nu_j$ in these coordinates by a finite atomic measure with error less than $1/j$. If this measure has $N_j>0$ atoms, apply \cref{prop:local-one-atom-addition} successively with $t=\tau=(jN_j)^{-1}$; if it has none, take $u_j=0$. The resulting smooth bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_j\leq0$ satisfy
\[
 \|u_j\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,p}/j,
 \qquad
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i\bigl((\ddc u_j)^n-\nu_j\bigr)\right|
       <2/j\quad(i\leq j).
\]
On every fixed pair of concentric balls $B'\Subset B\Subset\Omega$, the bounds of that proposition for test measures $(\ddc w)^n$, with $w\in\PSH(B)$ and $0\leq w\leq1$, also sum to $C_{B',B,\Omega}/j$. Dividing by a positive threshold and taking the supremum over $w$ gives convergence in local capacity. These estimates require neither convergence of the targets $\nu_j$ nor a common local mass bound for them.

Splitting the series defining the metric after its first $j$ terms gives
\[
 d_v\bigl((\ddc u_j)^n,\nu_j\bigr)\leq 2/j+2^{-j}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently, the two sequences of measures have the same limits along corresponding subsequences, and hence the same full cluster set $F$.
\end{proof}

For subharmonic functions on a planar domain, by contrast, $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ convergence to zero forces $\ddc u_j\to0$ in the sense of distributions, and positivity, tested against compactly supported cutoff functions, then gives vague convergence to zero. The freedom in the theorem therefore begins in dimension two.
