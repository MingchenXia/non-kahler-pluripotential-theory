\section{Exponential equations for an individual density}
\label{sec:individual-exponential-equations}

We return to the Hermitian background $h$ and a fixed nonzero density $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, and consider, for $\lambda>0$, the exponential equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:individual-exponential-equation}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mathrm{d}V,
 \qquad u\in\PSH(X,h).
\end{equation}
A \emph{solution} is a function $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ satisfying \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation}, and an \emph{exponential subsolution} at exponent $\lambda$ is a function $s\in\PSH(X,h)$ with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(s)\geq\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}f\,\mathrm{d}V$. For the truncations $f_k=\min(f,k)$, \cref{thm:nguyen-exp} gives continuous solutions $u_k$ of the truncated equations, and these decrease as $k$ increases. Strict domination shows that every exponential subsolution is a common lower bound for the $u_k$. Hence, as soon as one subsolution exists, the decreasing limit is not identically $-\infty$, and \cref{thm:exponential-closedness} shows that it solves \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation}; it is the greatest subsolution. A positive threshold $b_h(f)$ provides exponential subsolutions for every exponent, and the normalized greatest solutions then recover $b_h(f)$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!greatest exponential solution}
Fix $\lambda>0$, and suppose that \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation} has an exponential subsolution $s\in\PSH(X,h)$. For $k\geq1$, let $u_k$ be the continuous solution of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(u_k)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_k}f_k\,\mathrm{d}V,
 \qquad f_k=\min(f,k),
\]
given by \cref{thm:nguyen-exp}. Then $u_k$ decreases to a solution $u_\lambda$ of \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation}, and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u_\lambda)$ has finite mass. Moreover, $u_\lambda$ is the greatest exponential subsolution: every exponential subsolution $v$ at exponent $\lambda$ satisfies $v\leq u_\lambda$. In particular, $u_\lambda$ is the greatest solution of \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $f_k\leq f_{k+1}$, \cref{lem:exp-order} gives $u_{k+1}\leq u_k$.

We first show that every exponential subsolution $v$ lies below each $u_k$. Let $t>0$. On the set $\{u_k<v-t\}$,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(u_k)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_k}f_k\,\mathrm{d}V
 \leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}f\,\mathrm{d}V
 \leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v-t).
\]
Since $u_k$ is bounded, $P_{h/2}(u_k)\geq\inf_Xu_k$ is not identically $-\infty$, and $v-t\preceq u_k$. Strict domination, \cref{thm:singular-strict-domination}, therefore gives $u_k\geq v-t$. Letting $t\downarrow0$ gives $u_k\geq v$.

In particular $s\leq u_k\leq u_1$ for every $k$, so the decreasing limit $u_\lambda\geq s$ is not identically $-\infty$ and belongs to $\PSH(X,h)$. By \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}, $u_k\to u_\lambda$ in capacity. Choose a constant $A\geq\sup_Xu_1$. The nonpositive functions $u_k-A$ satisfy
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u_k-A)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda(u_k-A)}\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f_k\,\mathrm{d}V,
\]
and $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f_k\to\mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. \Cref{thm:exponential-closedness}, applied with $\mu=\mathrm{d}V$ and constant exponents, shows that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u_\lambda)$ is finite and that $u_\lambda$ solves \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation}. Finally, every exponential subsolution lies below all $u_k$, hence below $u_\lambda$, which is itself a subsolution.
\end{proof}

Being the greatest subsolution, $u_\lambda$ does not depend on the approximation used to construct it. The equation may have other, smaller solutions with different singularities. If $f\in L^p$ for some $p>1$, the continuous solution of \cref{thm:nguyen-exp} is a subsolution and therefore lies below $u_\lambda$. Thus $u_\lambda$ is bounded, and bounded exponential comparison (\cref{cor:bounded-exponential-comparison}) shows that it coincides with the continuous solution.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential}
Suppose $b=b_h(f)>0$. Then \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation} has a greatest solution $u_\lambda$ for every $\lambda>0$. If $v\in\PSH(X,h)$ satisfies $\sup_Xv=0$ and
$\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v)\geq bf\,\mathrm{d}V$, then
\[
 u_\lambda\geq v+\lambda^{-1}\log b.
\]
Put
\[
 M_\lambda=\sup_Xu_\lambda,\qquad
 v_\lambda=u_\lambda-M_\lambda,\qquad
 c_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda M_\lambda}.
\]
Then, as $\lambda\downarrow0$,
\[
 c_\lambda\longrightarrow b_h(f),\qquad
 \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f\longrightarrow b_h(f)f
           \quad\text{in }L^1(\mathrm{d}V).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}, a function $v$ as in the statement exists. Since $v\leq0$, the function $s=v+\lambda^{-1}\log b$ satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(s)\geq bf\,\mathrm{d}V
       \geq\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}f\,\mathrm{d}V,
\]
so it is an exponential subsolution. \Cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution} gives the greatest solution $u_\lambda$ and the bound $u_\lambda\geq s$; in particular, $c_\lambda\geq b$.

Set $g_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda}f$. By \cref{lem:normalized-exponential-npp}, applied to the finite measure $f\,\mathrm{d}V$, we have $g_\lambda\to f$ in $L^1$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$. Each $g_\lambda$ is nonzero, since $v_\lambda$ is finite almost everywhere. The equation for $u_\lambda$ reads $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v_\lambda)=c_\lambda g_\lambda\,\mathrm{d}V$, so \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}, applied to the density $g_\lambda$, gives
\[
 c_\lambda\leq b_h(g_\lambda).
\]
The upper semicontinuity of the threshold (\cref{thm:individual-threshold-semicontinuity}) therefore gives
\[
 b\leq\liminf_{\lambda\downarrow0}c_\lambda
 \leq\limsup_{\lambda\downarrow0}c_\lambda
 \leq b_h(f)=b.
\]
This proves $c_\lambda\to b$. Since $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f=c_\lambda g_\lambda$ and $g_\lambda\to f$ in $L^1$, it also proves $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f\to bf$ in $L^1$.
\end{proof}

Since $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v_\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f\,\mathrm{d}V$, the Monge--Amp\`ere measures of the normalized greatest solutions converge in total variation to $b_h(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V$. This answers the questions about the scalar and the measure raised in \cref{sec:zero-exponent-topology}; the potentials $v_\lambda$ themselves are harder to control. Under positive lower mass, \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-exact-threshold} shows that every $L^1$-convergent sequence $v_{\lambda_j}$ with $\lambda_j\downarrow0$ converges in capacity to a solution of $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v)=b_h(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V$. For $L^p$ data with $p>1$, \cref{prop:symmetric-equation-clusters} gives uniform precompactness. In both cases, convergence of the whole family $(v_\lambda)$ requires that all its cluster limits coincide. This holds when the dual problem for $f^{1/n}$ over Gauduchon currents, studied in \cref{sec:integrable-gauduchon-currents}, has a smooth strictly positive maximizer: the whole family then converges uniformly (\cref{cor:smooth-dual-exponential-limit}). If the maximizer is smooth and semipositive, and strictly positive outside a proper analytic set, \cref{prop:semipositive-dual-exponential-limit} gives $L^1$ convergence of the whole family and a fixed lower barrier, and \cref{cor:semipositive-dual-lp-limit} strengthens this to uniform convergence for $L^p$ data with $p>1$.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For a general positive threshold, the corollary gives the lower bound
\[
 v_\lambda\geq v-\lambda^{-1}\log(c_\lambda/b_h(f))
\]
for every normalized threshold subsolution $v$. The next subsection estimates the scalar error $\log(c_\lambda/b_h(f))$ when $f^{1/n}$ can be approximated by bounded functions in the norm of $\mathcal D_h$. When $b_h(f)=0$, \cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution} still applies whenever an exponential subsolution exists; examples are constructed in \cref{subsec:zero-threshold-product-exponential}.

\subsection{Exponential equations at zero threshold}
\label{subsec:zero-threshold-product-exponential}

When $b_h(f)=0$, no positive multiple of $f\,\mathrm{d}V$ is dominated by a Monge--Amp\`ere measure. An exponential subsolution $s$ only needs $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(s)\geq\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}f\,\mathrm{d}V$, however, and the factor $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}$ is small where $s$ is very negative. A density concentrating near the pole of a singular potential can therefore admit exponential subsolutions although its threshold vanishes. We construct such densities on the products of \cref{sec:zero-lower-mass-product-equations}.

We keep the notation of that section: $X=X_0\times Y$ with $Y=\mathbb P^k$, the background $H=h_0+\kappa$, the constants $N$, $C$ and $V_0$, the probability volume form $\mu=V_0^{-1}h_0^m\wedge\kappa^k$ with density $R=\mu/\mathrm{d}V$, and the potential $u_\alpha$ with density $q_\alpha$ from \cref{lem:projective-sublogarithmic-density}, for a fixed $0<\alpha<1$. On $Y\setminus\{o\}$ put $L=1-\psi\geq1$, so that $u_\alpha=1-L^\alpha$, and $t=\mathrm e^{1-L}$. As functions of $L$, define
\[
 a=\alpha L^{\alpha-1},\qquad Q=t+(1-t)a,\qquad
 D=t(1-a)+(1-t)\alpha(1-\alpha)L^{\alpha-2},
\]
\[
 W=kQ^{k-1}D,\qquad
 B_\tau=q_\alpha\mathrm e^{\tau(L^\alpha-1)}\quad(\tau>0),\qquad
 b_0=(1-\alpha)^k.
\]
Here $Q$ is the function $Q(r)$ of \cref{lem:projective-sublogarithmic-density}, written in terms of $L$, and $D=-\mathrm{d}Q/\mathrm{d}L$. The weight $B_\tau$ is chosen so that $\mathrm e^{\tau u_\alpha}B_\tau=q_\alpha$ off $o$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projective-exponential-barrier-growth}
As functions of $L$,
\[
 q_\alpha=Q^{k-1}D\,\mathrm e^{k(L-1)},\qquad q_\alpha\geq b_0,
\]
and $W(L)\sim k\alpha^k(1-\alpha)L^{-1-k(1-\alpha)}$ as $L\to+\infty$.
For every $\tau>0$, $\mathrm e^{\tau(L^\alpha-1)}W(L)\to+\infty$ as $L\to+\infty$. In particular, $B_\tau\kappa^k$ has infinite mass on every punctured neighborhood of $o$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In the notation of the proof of \cref{lem:projective-sublogarithmic-density}, $t=r/(1+r)$ and $s(r)=L$. Since the mass of $q_\alpha\kappa^k$ on $\{r_1<r<r_2\}$ is $Q(r_2)^k-Q(r_1)^k$, this measure has radial distribution $-\mathrm{d}(Q^k)/\mathrm{d}L=W(L)\,\mathrm{d}L$. The Fubini--Study measure $\kappa^k$ corresponds to $Q=t$, so its radial distribution is $k\mathrm e^{-k(L-1)}\mathrm{d}L$. Both measures are invariant under the unitary group of the chart, so $q_\alpha$ is the ratio of the two distributions, which is the stated formula. The lower bound $q_\alpha\geq b_0$ follows from $\kappa+\ddc u_\alpha\geq(1-\alpha)\kappa$ off $o$. As $L\to+\infty$, $t$ tends to zero exponentially, so $Q\sim\alpha L^{\alpha-1}$ and $D\sim\alpha(1-\alpha)L^{\alpha-2}$; this gives the asymptotics of $W$. Consequently,
\[
 \log\bigl(\mathrm e^{\tau(L^\alpha-1)}W(L)\bigr)=\tau L^\alpha-[1+k(1-\alpha)]\log L+O(1)\longrightarrow+\infty,
\]
and the mass of $B_\tau\kappa^k$ on $\{L>T\}$, which equals $\int_T^\infty\mathrm e^{\tau(L^\alpha-1)}W(L)\,\mathrm{d}L$, is infinite for every $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:product-zero-threshold-exponential-criterion}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!zero threshold criterion}
Let $f\geq0$ be a nonzero integrable function on $X$, and put $F=f/R$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\lambda>0$ and $F\leq AB_\lambda$ almost everywhere for a constant $A>0$, then
\[
 s_\lambda=u_\alpha+\lambda^{-1}\log(CV_0/A)
\]
is an exponential subsolution at exponent $\lambda$, of mass $CV_0$, and the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mathrm{d}V$ has a greatest solution, whose mass is finite and positive.
\item Suppose that $F$ is locally essentially bounded on $X\setminus(X_0\times\{o\})$ and
\[
 \lim_{T\to+\infty}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in X_0,\ L(y)>T}
 \frac{\log^+(F(x,y)/q_\alpha(y))}{L(y)^\alpha}=0,
\]
where $\log^+r=\log\max(1,r)$ for $r\geq0$.
Then the hypothesis of (1), and hence its conclusion, holds for every $\lambda>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) By \cref{thm:zero-lower-mass-product-equation}, $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u_\alpha)=CV_0q_\alpha\mu$. Off the pole fiber $X_0\times\{o\}$ we have $\mathrm e^{\lambda u_\alpha}B_\lambda=q_\alpha$ and $f\,\mathrm{d}V=F\mu\leq AB_\lambda\mu$, hence
\[
 \mathrm e^{\lambda s_\lambda}f\,\mathrm dV
 \leq(CV_0/A)\mathrm e^{\lambda u_\alpha}AB_\lambda\mu
 =CV_0q_\alpha\mu=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(s_\lambda),
\]
and both measures vanish on the pole fiber. Thus $s_\lambda$ is an exponential subsolution, and its mass is $CV_0$, since adding a constant does not change the Monge--Amp\`ere measure. \Cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution} now gives the greatest solution $u$ and the finiteness of its mass. The mass $\int_X\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mathrm{d}V$ is positive because $u$ is finite almost everywhere and $f$ has positive integral.

(2) Fix $\lambda>0$. By hypothesis, there is $T$ such that $F/q_\alpha\leq\mathrm e^{(\lambda/2)L^\alpha}$ almost everywhere on $\{L>T\}$. Since $\mathrm e^{(\lambda/2)L^\alpha}\leq\mathrm e^{\lambda}\mathrm e^{\lambda(L^\alpha-1)}$ for $L\geq1$, we have $F\leq\mathrm e^{\lambda}B_\lambda$ there. On the complement $\{L\leq T\}$, which is compact and disjoint from the pole fiber, $F$ is essentially bounded, while $B_\lambda\geq q_\alpha\geq b_0>0$. Increasing the constant, we obtain $F\leq AB_\lambda$ almost everywhere on $X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:zero-threshold-solvable-concentration}\index{Monge--Ampere density@Monge--Amp\`ere density!solvable concentration}\index{Subsolution!zero threshold exponential equation}
Let $F_j$, $j\geq1$, be smooth strictly positive probability densities on $X_0$ with respect to $\mathrm dV_0=V_0^{-1}h_0^m$, whose normalization scalars on $X_0$, relative to $h_0$ and $\mathrm dV_0$, satisfy $c_{h_0}(F_j)\leq2^{-2j}/C$. There are smooth strictly positive probability densities $g_j$ on $Y$ with respect to $\kappa^k$ such that the probability densities
\[
 f_j=RF_jg_j,\qquad f=\sum_{j\geq1}2^{-j}f_j
\]
on $X$ satisfy $c_H(f_j)\leq2^{-2j}$ and $b_H(f)=0$. The function $f$ is finite everywhere, smooth off the pole fiber $X_0\times\{o\}$, has integral one and is bounded below by a positive constant. For every $\lambda>0$, the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mathrm{d}V$ has an exponential subsolution and a greatest solution, of finite mass, and every solution is unbounded.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $w_j=2^{-j}$ and $M_j=\max_{X_0}F_j$.

\emph{Step 1: a smooth baseline.} Fix $C^j$ norms $\|\cdot\|_{C^j}$ on $X_0$, computed in a fixed finite atlas, so that they increase with $j$. Choose $0<\varepsilon_j\leq1/2$ with
\[
 w_j\varepsilon_jM_j\leq b_0 2^{-j-2},\qquad
 w_j\varepsilon_j\|F_j\|_{C^j}\leq2^{-j}.
\]
Then $E=\sum_jw_j\varepsilon_jF_j$ converges in every $C^l$ norm, so $E$ is smooth on $X_0$, and $0<E\leq b_0/4$.

\emph{Step 2: bumps escaping to the pole.} Choose a smooth function $\chi\colon\mathbb R\to[0,1]$, supported in $[0,1]$, positive on $(0,1)$ and flat at $0$ and $1$. Starting with $T_0=0$, choose inductively $T_j\geq2$ with $T_j>T_{j-1}+2$ and
\[
 Z_j=\int_{T_j}^{T_j+1}\chi(L-T_j)
       \mathrm e^{(L^\alpha-1)/j}W(L)\,\mathrm dL
       \geq2w_jM_j.
\]
This is possible because, by \cref{lem:projective-exponential-barrier-growth} with $\tau=1/j$, the factor $\mathrm e^{(L^\alpha-1)/j}W(L)$ is uniformly large on sufficiently distant intervals. On the annulus $\{T_j\leq L\leq T_j+1\}$ put $\beta_j=\chi(L-T_j)B_{1/j}/Z_j$, extend $\beta_j$ by zero, and set $g_j=\varepsilon_j+(1-\varepsilon_j)\beta_j$. Since $\chi$ is flat at the endpoints, $\beta_j$ is smooth; it vanishes near $o$ and near the hyperplane $\{L=1\}$. Its integral against $\kappa^k$ is one, so $g_j$ is a smooth strictly positive probability density with respect to $\kappa^k$. Moreover,
\[
 w_jM_j(1-\varepsilon_j)\beta_j\leq B_{1/j}/2.
\]

\emph{Step 3: the scalars $c_H(f_j)$.} By \cref{thm:tw-smooth} on $Y$, there is a smooth $\phi_j$ with $\kappa+\ddc\phi_j>0$ and $(\kappa+\ddc\phi_j)^k=g_j\kappa^k$; the scalar is one because both sides have mass one. On $X_0$, the same theorem gives a smooth $v_j$ with $h_0+\ddc v_j>0$ and $(h_0+\ddc v_j)^m=c_{h_0}(F_j)F_j\,\mathrm dV_0$. Since only one term of the binomial expansion survives for dimensional reasons,
\[
 (H+\ddc(v_j+\phi_j))^N
 =C c_{h_0}(F_j)F_jg_j\mu
 =C c_{h_0}(F_j)f_j\,\mathrm dV.
\]
Uniqueness of the scalar gives $c_H(f_j)=C c_{h_0}(F_j)\leq2^{-2j}$.

\emph{Step 4: properties of $f$.} By Tonelli's theorem, $\int_Xf\,\mathrm dV=\sum_jw_j=1$. The density of $f\,\mathrm{d}V$ with respect to $\mu$ is
\[
 F=f/R=\sum_jw_jF_jg_j=E+\sum_jw_j(1-\varepsilon_j)F_j\beta_j.
\]
The annuli are disjoint and escape to the pole, so only finitely many bumps meet any compact subset of $Y\setminus\{o\}$; hence $F$, and therefore $f$, is smooth off the pole fiber. On the pole fiber all bumps vanish and $F=E$, so $f$ is finite everywhere. Also $f\geq w_1f_1\geq\frac12\min_Xf_1>0$. If $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u)\geq af\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $a>0$, then $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u)\geq aw_jf_j\,\mathrm{d}V$, and \cref{lem:bounded-normalizer-subsolution} gives
\[
 aw_j\leq c_H(f_j)\leq2^{-2j},\quad\text{that is,}\quad a\leq2^{-j},
 \quad\text{for every }j.
\]
This is impossible, so $b_H(f)=0$ by \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}.

\emph{Step 5: exponential subsolutions.} Fix $\lambda>0$, and choose $J$ with $1/j\leq\lambda$ for $j\geq J$. Since $L^\alpha-1\geq0$, we have $B_{1/j}\leq B_\lambda$ for these $j$. As the bumps have disjoint supports, Step 2 bounds their total contribution $\sum_{j\geq J}w_j(1-\varepsilon_j)F_j\beta_j$ by $B_\lambda/2$. The baseline satisfies $E\leq b_0/4\leq B_\lambda/4$, because $B_\lambda\geq q_\alpha\geq b_0$. The remaining finitely many bumps are bounded by the constant
\[
 K_\lambda=\max_Y\sum_{j<J}w_j(1-\varepsilon_j)M_j\beta_j,
\]
and $K_\lambda\leq(K_\lambda/b_0)B_\lambda$. Hence $F\leq A_\lambda B_\lambda$ with $A_\lambda=3/4+K_\lambda/b_0$, and \cref{thm:product-zero-threshold-exponential-criterion} gives the exponential subsolution $u_\alpha+\lambda^{-1}\log(CV_0/A_\lambda)$ and the greatest solution. Finally, a bounded solution $u$ would satisfy $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u)\geq\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\inf_Xu}f\,\mathrm{d}V$, a positive scaled subsolution, contradicting $b_H(f)=0$. Hence every solution is unbounded.
\end{proof}

The density just constructed also satisfies the criterion in part (2) of \cref{thm:product-zero-threshold-exponential-criterion}: on the $j$th annulus, $F/q_\alpha\leq1/4+\frac12\mathrm e^{(L^\alpha-1)/j}$, and away from all annuli the ratio is at most $1/4$. Approaching the pole through the annuli forces $j\to\infty$, so the required limit is zero. Since $b_H(f)=0$, \cref{prop:individual-normalizer-lp} also shows that $f$ belongs to no $L^p$ with $p>1$.

Densities $F_j$ as in the theorem exist whenever $v_-(h_0)=0$, by \cref{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema,thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}. For instance, on the elliptic threefold, \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass} gives smooth functions $v_\ell$ with $h_0+\ddc v_\ell>0$ and masses $\delta_\ell=\int_{X_0}(h_0+\ddc v_\ell)^3\to0$. The probability densities $F_\ell=(h_0+\ddc v_\ell)^3/(\delta_\ell\,\mathrm dV_0)$ satisfy $c_{h_0}(F_\ell)=\delta_\ell$ by uniqueness of the scalar, and a subsequence with $\delta_{\ell_j}\leq2^{-2j}/C$ gives the required densities. Thus the construction applies to this threefold times $\mathbb P^k$ for every $k\geq1$. With the balanced metric of \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric}, $V_0=24\pi$, so the explicit subsolutions have mass $CV_0$, equal to $96\pi$ on the fourfold ($k=1$) and $240\pi$ on the fivefold ($k=2$). These values concern the subsolutions; the greatest solutions have their own finite positive masses.

In this construction the projective components $g_j$ are chosen after the densities $F_j$, to fit them. We do not know whether the exponential equation is solvable for every prescribed concentration family of this kind, and we have no necessary criterion for solvability at zero threshold.

\subsection{Solvable exponents and collapse at zero threshold}

We return to a general Hermitian form $h$ on $X$ and a nonzero density $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm dV)$. Let $\Lambda_h(f)$ be the set of exponents $\lambda>0$ for which \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation} has a solution. By \cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution}, $\lambda\in\Lambda_h(f)$ if and only if the equation has an exponential subsolution at exponent $\lambda$.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:solvable-exponents-upper-interval}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!solvable exponents}
If $\lambda_0\in\Lambda_h(f)$, then $[\lambda_0,+\infty)\subset\Lambda_h(f)$. Consequently, $\Lambda_h(f)$ is empty, or equals $(a,+\infty)$ for some $a\geq0$, or equals $[a,+\infty)$ for some $a>0$. If $b_h(f)>0$, then $\Lambda_h(f)=(0,+\infty)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $u$ be a solution at $\lambda_0$, and put $s=u-\max(0,\sup_Xu)$. Then $s\leq\min(u,0)$, so for $\lambda\geq\lambda_0$,
\begin{align*}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(s)
 &=\mathrm e^{\lambda_0u}f\,\mathrm dV\\
 &\geq\mathrm e^{\lambda_0s}f\,\mathrm dV
 \geq\mathrm e^{\lambda s}f\,\mathrm dV.
\end{align*}
Thus $s$ is an exponential subsolution at every $\lambda\geq\lambda_0$, and \cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution} gives a solution. Hence $\Lambda_h(f)$ contains $[\lambda_0,+\infty)$ for each of its points $\lambda_0$. A nonempty subset of $(0,+\infty)$ with this property is determined by its infimum $a$ and by whether $a$ belongs to it, which gives the stated forms. If $b_h(f)>0$, \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential} gives $\Lambda_h(f)=(0,+\infty)$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zero-threshold-exponential-mass-collapse}\index{Normalization scalar!zero threshold collapse}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!zero threshold exponential limit}
Suppose $b_h(f)=0$. Let $\lambda_j>0$ decrease to zero, and let $u_j$ be any solution of \eqref{eq:individual-exponential-equation} at exponent $\lambda_j$. Put
\[
 M_j=\sup_Xu_j,\qquad v_j=u_j-M_j,\qquad
 c_j=\mathrm e^{\lambda_jM_j}.
\]
Then
\begin{gather*}
 c_j\longrightarrow0,\qquad \lambda_jM_j\longrightarrow-\infty,\\
 \|\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u_j)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\|\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v_j)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{gather*}
Moreover, $\mathrm e^{\lambda_ju_j}\to0$ uniformly on $X$, where the exponential is zero on the pole set, and $u_j\to-\infty$ uniformly, that is, $\sup_Xu_j\to-\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $g_j=\mathrm e^{\lambda_jv_j}f$. By \cref{lem:normalized-exponential-npp}, applied to the finite non-pluripolar measure $f\,\mathrm dV$, $g_j\to f$ in $L^1$; each $g_j$ is nonzero, since $v_j$ is finite almost everywhere. Since the Monge--Amp\`ere measure is unchanged by adding constants,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(v_j)=c_jg_j\,\mathrm dV,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad 0<c_j\leq b_h(g_j)
\]
by \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}. The upper semicontinuity of the threshold, \cref{thm:individual-threshold-semicontinuity}, gives $\limsup_jc_j\leq b_h(f)=0$. Since $0\leq g_j\leq f$, the total masses are at most $c_j\|f\|_{L^1}$ and tend to zero. Moreover $\lambda_jM_j=\log c_j\to-\infty$, and $0\leq\mathrm e^{\lambda_ju_j}\leq\mathrm e^{\lambda_jM_j}=c_j$. Finally, once $\lambda_j<1$ and $\log c_j<0$, we have $M_j=\lambda_j^{-1}\log c_j\leq\log c_j$, so $M_j\to-\infty$.
\end{proof}

For the density of \cref{thm:zero-threshold-solvable-concentration}, solutions exist for every positive exponent, so the collapse applies as $\lambda\downarrow0$ to every choice of solutions, in particular to the greatest ones. By contrast, the subsolutions of \cref{thm:product-zero-threshold-exponential-criterion} keep the mass $CV_0$ for every exponent, while the masses of the solutions above them tend to zero: on a nonclosed background, the order of two potentials does not give an order of their total Monge--Amp\`ere masses. Whether the normalized potentials converge in capacity, and which equation a normalized cluster limit satisfies at exponent zero, are not decided by this argument. Positive endpoints of $\Lambda_h(f)$ are governed instead by the compactness criterion below.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\subsection{Positive endpoints and one-sided limits}

For $\lambda\in\Lambda_h(f)$, write $U_\lambda$ for the greatest solution given by \cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution}. When the exponents stay above a fixed $\tau>0$, the solution of a bounded truncated equation at exponent $\tau$ is a common upper barrier for all subsolutions. Together with the maximality of greatest solutions, this barrier gives an order estimate between $U_{\lambda_1}$ and $U_{\lambda_2}$, which controls the positive endpoint of $\Lambda_h(f)$ and the one-sided limits of $U_\lambda$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive-exponent-uniform-upper-barrier}\index{Subsolution!common upper barrier}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!positive exponent order}
For every $\tau>0$, there is a nonnegative $t_\tau\in\PSH(X,h)\cap C(X)$ such that every exponential subsolution at an exponent $\lambda\geq\tau$ lies below $t_\tau$. Put $K_\tau=\sup_Xt_\tau$. If $\tau\leq\lambda_1\leq\lambda_2$ belong to $\Lambda_h(f)$, then
\[
 U_{\lambda_2}\geq U_{\lambda_1}
             -K_\tau\frac{\lambda_2-\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}.
\]
Consequently, $\lambda\mapsto U_\lambda+K_\tau\log(\lambda/\tau)$ is nondecreasing on $\Lambda_h(f)\cap[\tau,+\infty)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $q=\min(f,1)$, a nonzero bounded density. By \cref{thm:nguyen-exp}, there is a continuous $w\in\PSH(X,h)$ with $\mathrm{MA}_h(w)=\mathrm e^{\tau w}q\,\mathrm dV$. Put $t_\tau=w+\max(0,-\inf_Xw)$. Then $t_\tau\geq0$ and $t_\tau\geq w$, so for $\lambda\geq\tau$,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(t_\tau)=\mathrm e^{\tau w}q\,\mathrm dV
       \leq\mathrm e^{\lambda t_\tau}f\,\mathrm dV.
\]
Let $u$ be an exponential subsolution at an exponent $\lambda\geq\tau$, and let $\epsilon>0$. On $\{t_\tau<u-\epsilon\}$,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(t_\tau)
       \leq\mathrm e^{\lambda(u-\epsilon)}f\,\mathrm dV
       \leq\mathrm e^{-\lambda\epsilon}
                  \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u-\epsilon).
\]
The bounded function $t_\tau$ satisfies $P_{h/2}(t_\tau)\not\equiv-\infty$, and $u-\epsilon\preceq t_\tau$. Thus \cref{thm:singular-strict-domination} gives $t_\tau\geq u-\epsilon$, and letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives $t_\tau\geq u$.

For the order estimate, put $\delta=K_\tau(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)/\lambda_2$. By the first part, $U_{\lambda_1}\leq t_\tau\leq K_\tau$, so $(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)U_{\lambda_1}\leq\lambda_2\delta$, that is, $\lambda_1U_{\lambda_1}\geq\lambda_2(U_{\lambda_1}-\delta)$. Since the Monge--Amp\`ere measure is unchanged by adding constants,
\begin{align*}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(U_{\lambda_1}-\delta)
   &=\mathrm e^{\lambda_1U_{\lambda_1}}f\,\mathrm dV\\
   &\geq\mathrm e^{\lambda_2(U_{\lambda_1}-\delta)}f\,\mathrm dV.
\end{align*}
Thus $U_{\lambda_1}-\delta$ is an exponential subsolution at $\lambda_2$, and the maximality of $U_{\lambda_2}$ gives $U_{\lambda_2}\geq U_{\lambda_1}-\delta$. Finally, $1-x^{-1}\leq\log x$ for $x\geq1$; with $x=\lambda_2/\lambda_1$ this gives $\delta\leq K_\tau\log(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)$, which proves the monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-exponent-endpoint-attainment}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!positive endpoint}
Suppose $\Lambda_h(f)\ne\varnothing$ and $a=\inf\Lambda_h(f)>0$. Then $a\in\Lambda_h(f)$ if and only if there are exponents $\lambda_j>a$ decreasing to $a$ and solutions $u_j$ at exponent $\lambda_j$ with $\inf_j\sup_Xu_j> -\infty$.

If $a\notin\Lambda_h(f)$, then every sequence of solutions $u_j$ at exponents $\lambda_j>a$ decreasing to $a$ satisfies
\begin{gather*}
 \sup_Xu_j\longrightarrow-\infty,\qquad
 \mathrm e^{\lambda_ju_j}\longrightarrow0\ \text{uniformly on }X,\\
 \|\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u_j)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
                \longrightarrow0,
\end{gather*}
where the exponential is zero on the pole sets.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $a\in\Lambda_h(f)$, let $u_a$ be a solution at $a$, and put $s=u_a-\max(0,\sup_Xu_a)$. As in the proof of \cref{prop:solvable-exponents-upper-interval}, $s$ is an exponential subsolution at every $\lambda\geq a$, so $U_\lambda\geq s$. Hence the greatest solutions at any exponents $\lambda_j\downarrow a$ have suprema at least $\sup_Xs>-\infty$.

Conversely, let $\lambda_j$ and $u_j$ be as in the statement. The lemma with $\tau=a$ gives the common upper bound $u_j\leq K_a$. Write $M_j=\sup_Xu_j$. After extraction, $M_j\to M\in\mathbb R$ and, by \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, $u_j-M_j\to v$ in $L^1$ for some $v\in\PSH(X,h)$. Then $u_j\to u=v+M$ in $L^1$ and, after a further extraction, almost everywhere. Since $a\leq\lambda_j\leq\lambda_1$, $u_j\leq K_a$ and $K_a\geq0$, the densities $\mathrm e^{\lambda_ju_j}f$ are bounded by $\mathrm e^{\lambda_1K_a}f$, and dominated convergence gives
\[
 \mathrm e^{\lambda_ju_j}f\longrightarrow\mathrm e^{au}f
              \quad\text{in }L^1(\mathrm dV).
\]
\Cref{lem:density-subsolution-lone-closure} now gives $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)\geq\mathrm e^{au}f\,\mathrm dV$. Thus $u$ is an exponential subsolution at $a$, and \cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution} gives a solution at $a$.

Finally, suppose $a\notin\Lambda_h(f)$, and let $u_j$ be solutions at exponents $\lambda_j\downarrow a$. If $\sup_Xu_j$ did not tend to $-\infty$, a subsequence would satisfy the condition just shown to imply $a\in\Lambda_h(f)$. Hence $M_j\to-\infty$. Once $M_j<0$, since $\lambda_j\geq a>0$,
\[
 0\leq\mathrm e^{\lambda_ju_j}\leq\mathrm e^{\lambda_jM_j}\leq\mathrm e^{aM_j}
                 \longrightarrow0,
\]
and the total mass $\int_X\mathrm e^{\lambda_ju_j}f\,\mathrm dV$ is at most $\mathrm e^{aM_j}\|f\|_{L^1}$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:greatest-exponential-right-continuity}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!right continuity}\index{Convergence!greatest exponential solutions}
For every $a\in\Lambda_h(f)$, the greatest solutions satisfy
\[
 U_\lambda\longrightarrow U_a
       \quad\text{in }L^1\text{ and in capacity}\quad(\lambda\downarrow a),
\]
and their Monge--Amp\`ere measures converge in total variation to $\mathrm e^{aU_a}f\,\mathrm dV$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $K=K_a$ be the constant of the lemma with $\tau=a$, and let $\lambda_j>a$ decrease to $a$. By the lemma, the functions
\[
 V_j=U_{\lambda_j}+K\log(\lambda_j/a)
\]
decrease and satisfy $V_j\geq U_a$. Their limit $V$ therefore belongs to $\PSH(X,h)$ and satisfies $V\geq U_a$. Dominated convergence between the integrable bounds $U_a$ and $V_1$ gives $V_j\to V$ in $L^1$, and \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity} gives convergence in capacity. Since the added constants tend to zero, $U_{\lambda_j}\to V$ in both senses.

The nonpositive functions $U_{\lambda_j}-K$ satisfy
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(U_{\lambda_j}-K)=\mathrm e^{\lambda_j(U_{\lambda_j}-K)}\,\mathrm e^{\lambda_jK}f\,\mathrm dV,
\]
and $\mathrm e^{\lambda_jK}f\to\mathrm e^{aK}f$ in $L^1$. \Cref{thm:exponential-closedness} therefore gives $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(V)=\mathrm e^{aV}f\,\mathrm dV$, together with total-variation convergence of the measures $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(U_{\lambda_j})$. Hence $V$ is a solution at $a$, so $V\leq U_a$ by maximality, while $V\geq U_a$ by construction. Thus $V=U_a$. Since every sequence of exponents converging to $a$ from above has a decreasing subsequence, this proves the assertions.
\end{proof}

The monotonicity of the corrected family $U_\lambda+K_\tau\log(\lambda/\tau)$ in \cref{lem:positive-exponent-uniform-upper-barrier} also controls the approach from the left. The left limit solves the equation at the limiting exponent, but maximality only places it below the greatest solution there.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:greatest-exponential-left-limit}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!left limit}
Suppose $\Lambda_h(f)\ne\varnothing$, and put $a_0=\inf\Lambda_h(f)$. For every $a>a_0$, there is $L_a\in\PSH(X,h)$ such that
\[
 U_\lambda\longrightarrow L_a
       \quad\text{in }L^1\text{ and in capacity}\quad(\lambda\uparrow a).
\]
It satisfies
\[
 L_a\leq U_a,\qquad
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(L_a)=\mathrm e^{aL_a}f\,\mathrm dV.
\]
The equation measures converge in total variation to this finite measure. The left limit does not depend on the auxiliary exponent or on the sequence of exponents used to construct it in the proof.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $a>a_0$, there is $\tau\in\Lambda_h(f)$ with $\tau<a$, and \cref{prop:solvable-exponents-upper-interval} gives $[\tau,+\infty)\subset\Lambda_h(f)$. Put $K=K_\tau$ from \cref{lem:positive-exponent-uniform-upper-barrier} and
\[
 V_\lambda=U_\lambda+K\log(\lambda/\tau)
       \qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda_h(f),\ \lambda\geq\tau).
\]
By \cref{lem:positive-exponent-uniform-upper-barrier}, this family is nondecreasing in $\lambda$. For a sequence $\lambda_j\uparrow a$ with $\tau\leq\lambda_j<a$, its members lie between the integrable potentials $V_\tau$ and $V_a$. The upper-envelope construction in \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} gives
\[
 V^-_a=\left(\sup_{\tau\leq\lambda<a}V_\lambda\right)^*
        =\left(\lim_jV_{\lambda_j}\right)^*\in\PSH(X,h).
\]
Indeed, since the family is monotone, every such sequence has the same pointwise supremum, and regularization changes this supremum only on a pluripolar set. Dominated convergence between the integrable bounds gives $L^1$ convergence to $V^-_a$, and \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity} gives convergence in capacity. Since the added constants converge, set
\[
 L_a=V^-_a-K\log(a/\tau).
\]
Then $U_{\lambda_j}\to L_a$ in both topologies and $L_a\leq U_a$; the latter inequality survives regularization because $V_a$ is upper semicontinuous.

By the lemma, $U_{\lambda_j}\leq t_\tau\leq K$, so the potentials $U_{\lambda_j}-K$ and their limit $L_a-K$ are nonpositive. As in the proof of \cref{prop:greatest-exponential-right-continuity}, \cref{thm:exponential-closedness}, applied with exponents tending to $a>0$ and densities $\mathrm e^{\lambda_jK}f\to\mathrm e^{aK}f$ in $L^1$, proves the equation for $L_a$ and the total-variation convergence.

Every sequence of exponents converging to $a$ from the left has an increasing subsequence. If convergence of the potentials in either topology, or total-variation convergence of the equation measures, failed, there would be such a sequence staying outside a fixed neighborhood of the proposed limit, and its increasing subsequence would contradict the preceding argument. This proves the full left limit and measure convergence. Repeating the construction with another $\tau$ gives the same $L^1$ limit, and quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere. Thus the auxiliary choice does not change $L_a$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:greatest-exponential-countable-jumps}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!countable jumps}
Suppose $\Lambda_h(f)\ne\varnothing$, put $a_0=\inf\Lambda_h(f)$, and let $L_a$ be the left limit of \cref{prop:greatest-exponential-left-limit}. The set
\[
 D=\{a>a_0:L_a\ne U_a\}
\]
is at most countable. At every $a>a_0$ with $a\notin D$, the greatest solutions are continuous in $L^1$ and in capacity as $\lambda\to a$, and their equation measures converge in total variation to $\mathrm e^{aU_a}f\,\mathrm dV$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $a_0<a_1<a_2$, choose $\tau\in\Lambda_h(f)$ with $\tau<a_1$, and use the corrected family $V_\lambda$ from the preceding proof on $[a_1,a_2]$. Its integrals
\[
 I(\lambda)=\int_XV_\lambda\,\mathrm dV
\]
form a bounded nondecreasing real function on $[a_1,a_2]$. For $a\in(a_1,a_2]$, the $L^1$ convergence in \cref{prop:greatest-exponential-left-limit} gives
\[
 I(a)-I(a-)=\int_X(U_a-L_a)\,\mathrm dV.
\]
If $L_a\ne U_a$, this quantity is positive: the integrable nonnegative difference $U_a-L_a$ cannot vanish almost everywhere, since quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere. For finitely many such points in $(a_1,a_2]$, the sum of their jumps is at most $I(a_2)-I(a_1)$. Consequently, for each positive integer $\ell$ there are only finitely many jumps of size at least $1/\ell$ in $(a_1,a_2]$, and hence at most countably many positive jumps. Covering $(a_0,+\infty)$ by countably many intervals $(a_1,a_2]$ with rational endpoints proves the assertion about $D$.

If $a\notin D$, the left limit is $U_a$, and \cref{prop:greatest-exponential-right-continuity} supplies the same right limit in both topologies and for the equation measures. Splitting an arbitrary approaching sequence into its two sides proves the stated two-sided convergence.
\end{proof}

We do not know whether the set $D$ can be nonempty. We also know neither an intrinsic criterion on $f$ for $a\in\Lambda_h(f)$, nor whether a positive infimum $a\notin\Lambda_h(f)$ can occur. These one-sided limits concern greatest solutions at positive exponents; they do not identify limits of arbitrary solutions with other singularities or supply a zero-exponent limit.
