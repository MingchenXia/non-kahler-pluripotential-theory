\chapter{Bounded products and non-pluripolar measures}
\label{chap:products}

A non-closed form has no local potential, but a bounded potential still has a well-defined Monge--Amp\`ere measure. The construction expands the non-closed smooth part separately from the local plurisubharmonic part. Passing to unbounded functions requires a further step: the resulting measure need not have finite mass. At the end of the chapter, we show that this failure can occur even when the full current has integrable coefficients and is approximated by smooth currents in the coefficient $L^1$ norm.

\section{Local products}

We recall the following input from local pluripotential theory, developed in the source book \cite{SGPT}.

\begin{theorem}[Bedford--Taylor]
\label{thm:bt-input}
If $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a domain in $\C^n$, their mixed product
$\ddc p_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc p_k$ is a positive closed current of order zero. It is symmetric, multilinear in its current factors, and continuous under simultaneous locally bounded decreasing approximation. It does not charge pluripolar sets and is local in the plurifine topology.
\end{theorem}

Here locality means equality of the restricted currents on a plurifine open Borel set where the potentials agree. The plurifine topology is the topology generated locally by plurisubharmonic functions. It is finer than the ordinary topology and, for example, makes the strict superlevel sets of a quasi-plurisubharmonic function open.

Let $u$ be locally bounded and $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. On a relatively compact coordinate ball choose $q=A|z|^2$ so that $p=u+q$ is plurisubharmonic. Set $\beta=\theta-\ddc q$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-expansion}
 (\theta+\ddc u)^k
 :=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}(\ddc p)^j\wedge\beta^{k-j}.
\end{equation}
Each summand is defined by \cref{thm:bt-input} and multiplication by a smooth form. The signed terms in this expansion should not individually be assumed positive.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-products}
The construction \eqref{eq:bounded-expansion}, and its mixed version, is independent of the local correction $q$ and of the decomposition of each current into a smooth form and a $\ddc$-potential. It defines global products of currents with locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials. The products are symmetric, multilinear, plurifinely local, and give no mass to pluripolar sets. If every factor is positive, their mixed product is positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the overlap of two coordinate balls, the two corrected potentials differ by a smooth function. The multilinear rule
$\ddc(p+f)=\ddc p+\ddc f$, valid for bounded local products by smooth approximation, and the binomial formula identify the two expansions. The same argument handles a smooth change of the decomposition of a current. Any two potential decompositions of the same $(1,1)$-current do differ smoothly: their difference has smooth $\ddc$, and taking its local Euclidean trace gives smoothness by elliptic regularity. Symmetry, multilinearity and locality then follow term by term. Wedges of bounded Bedford--Taylor products with smooth forms have locally finite variation and do not charge pluripolar sets, so these properties also pass to the finite expansion.

It remains to prove positivity; it does not follow from the signs of the summands. This is local. Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and a local positive constant form $\kappa$. After shrinking a neighborhood of a chosen point, each smooth background $\theta_i$ admits a constant, hence closed, form $\theta_i'$ such that
\[
 -\varepsilon\kappa\leq\theta_i-\theta_i'
 \leq\varepsilon\kappa.
\]
If $T_i=\theta_i+\ddc u_i\geq0$, then
$S_i=\theta_i'+\varepsilon\kappa+\ddc u_i$ is positive and closed there, with a bounded local plurisubharmonic potential. Moreover
\[
 T_i+2\varepsilon\kappa
 =S_i+(\theta_i-\theta_i'+\varepsilon\kappa).
\]
All terms on the right are positive, and the second term is smooth. Expanding the mixed product therefore gives a sum of positive currents. Although the neighborhoods depend on $\varepsilon$, they cover the original coordinate ball. The product of the $T_i+2\varepsilon\kappa$ is thus positive throughout that ball. By multilinearity it converges weakly to the product of the $T_i$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, proving positivity.
\end{proof}

The local expansion also gives continuity along uniformly locally bounded decreasing sequences, by applying \cref{thm:bt-input} to the corrected potentials and then summing the finitely many terms. These constructions are discussed in \cite[Section 1]{KN15} and \cite[Section 2.1]{BGL25}. No bounded mass property is needed here. The mass of each fixed bounded product is finite on compact $X$.

\section{Maxima and truncations}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:max-psh}
If $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\max(u,v)\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Consequently, if $\theta\geq0$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\max(u,-t)$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic for every real $t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The maximum is quasi-plurisubharmonic by a common local smooth correction. To verify its precise lower Hessian bound, use the constant form $\theta'$ from the preceding proof. Write $\theta'+\varepsilon\kappa=\ddc r$ locally. Then $u+r$ and $v+r$ are plurisubharmonic, hence so is their maximum. It follows that
\[
 \theta+2\varepsilon\kappa+\ddc\max(u,v)\geq0
\]
on each such neighborhood. Let $\varepsilon\to0$. For the last assertion, constants belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$ when $\theta\geq0$.
\end{proof}

For a general smooth $\theta$, the final hypothesis cannot be dropped: a constant is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic precisely when $\theta\geq0$. Thus constant truncations of $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions are not an automatic device in a merely big, non-semipositive background.

\section{An extended measure without a mass bound}

For the moment let $\theta\geq0$. Subtract a constant so that $u\leq0$, and put
\[
 u_t=\max(u,-t),\qquad D_t=\{u>-t\},\qquad
 \nu_t=\mathbf1_{D_t}(\theta+\ddc u_t)^n.
\]
The sets $D_t$ are Borel and plurifinely open.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:extended-measure}
The positive measures $\nu_t$ increase with $t$. Their increasing limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extended-ma}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
 :=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathbf1_{\{u>-t\}}
       (\theta+\ddc\max(u,-t))^n
\end{equation}
is a countably additive positive Borel measure, vanishes on pluripolar sets, and is unchanged by adding a constant to $u$. It is allowed to have infinite total mass. If it has finite mass, it is the non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of $u$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $s>t$, then $D_t\subset D_s$ and $u_s=u_t$ on $D_t$. Plurifine locality gives
$\mathbf1_{D_t}\nu_s=\nu_t$. Positivity proves monotonicity. An increasing limit of measures is a measure, by monotone convergence applied to the sums in countable additivity. Every $\nu_t$ vanishes on pluripolar sets, hence so does the limit.

On each set $\{u>-t\}$, two sufficiently deep truncations, including truncations made after adding a constant, have potentials differing by that constant. Their Hessians and restricted measures therefore agree. These sets exhaust the complement of the pole set, which is discarded by the definition. This proves independence of the additive normalization.

For finite mass, the construction is precisely the extension of the bounded local products by zero across the pole set, which defines the non-pluripolar measure.
\end{proof}

The word \emph{extended} keeps a mathematical distinction visible. A positive Borel measure of infinite total mass on a compact space is not a Radon measure in the sense of a locally finite measure. We cannot treat it as a current of order zero on all of $X$.

\section{Arbitrary smooth backgrounds}

The construction can be made without semipositivity of $\theta$. The point is to prove positivity only after restricting to the sets where truncation leaves the potential unchanged.

Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega$ with $\theta\leq\Omega$. Every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\Omega)$. Thus $u_t=\max(u,-t)$ is a bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic function, and the signed product $(\theta+\ddc u_t)^n$ is defined by \cref{prop:bounded-products}. It need not be positive on all of $X$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:restricted-positive-product}
With $D_t=\{u>-t\}$, one has
\[
 0\leq\mathbf1_{D_t}(\theta+\ddc u_t)^n
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_t}(\Omega+\ddc u_t)^n.
\]
The same local positivity assertion holds for mixed products, on a common set where all the truncations leave their potentials unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work in a coordinate ball with a fixed positive constant form $\kappa$, and fix $\epsilon>0$. Around any point choose a smaller neighborhood and a constant real form $\theta'$ such that
\[
 -\epsilon\kappa\leq\theta-\theta'\leq\epsilon\kappa.
\]
Take a smooth local potential $r$ for $\theta'+\epsilon\kappa$. Then $u+r$ is plurisubharmonic. On a relatively compact smaller neighborhood choose $k>t+\sup|r|$ and set
\[
 h_k=\max(u+r,-k)-r.
\]
It is locally bounded and equals $u_t=u$ on $D_t$. Moreover,
\[
 \theta+\ddc h_k+2\epsilon\kappa
 =\ddc\max(u+r,-k)+(\theta-\theta'+\epsilon\kappa)
\]
is a sum of a positive closed bounded-potential current and a smooth positive form. Its bounded products are positive. Plurifine locality transfers this positivity to the restriction of $(\theta+\ddc u_t+2\epsilon\kappa)^n$ to $D_t$.

These neighborhoods cover the coordinate ball. Let $\epsilon\downarrow0$. The product with the fixed bounded potential $u_t$ is polynomial in $\epsilon$, with coefficients of locally finite variation. Consequently convergence holds in local total variation, also after restriction to $D_t$. This proves the first inequality. Apply the same construction to each factor to obtain mixed positivity.

For the second inequality, expand the difference of the two powers by the telescoping identity, using the smooth positive difference $\Omega-\theta$. Every summand is a mixed product of positive factors on $D_t$, as just proved. The difference is therefore nonnegative there.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}
Let $\theta$ be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Formula \eqref{eq:extended-ma}, with signed bounded products before restriction, defines a positive non-pluripolar Borel measure. It is independent of additive normalization, is allowed infinite mass, and satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Omega(u)
 \quad\text{whenever }\Omega\text{ is Hermitian and }\theta\leq\Omega.
\]
It is finite on every $D_t$ and is sigma-finite. The construction is intrinsic to the positive current $\theta+\ddc u$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding lemma gives positivity and the comparison for every truncation level. Locality gives compatibility of the restrictions as in \cref{prop:extended-measure}. Their increasing limit is therefore a positive measure, and comparison passes to this limit. Each restricted measure has finite mass because it is dominated by a bounded $\Omega$-product. The sets $D_t$ exhaust the complement of the discarded pole set, proving sigma-finiteness.

For intrinsicness, consider another smooth decomposition $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$, $u'=u-f$. On each set where both potentials are bounded below, sufficiently deep truncations agree with their untruncated potentials. The two bounded-potential currents then agree on that set, and plurifine locality gives equality of the restricted products. Such sets exhaust the complement of the common pole set, since $f$ is smooth and bounded. This proves the assertion; changes of additive normalization are a special case. Any two smooth decompositions of the same current differ in this way by the elliptic-regularity argument in \cref{prop:bounded-products}.
\end{proof}

For finite mass, this is the usual non-pluripolar product. The restricted construction is the mechanism in \cite[Proposition 2.1]{BGL25}. Allowing an extended measure removes the need for a global variation bound at this stage. It does not remove the need for such a bound when a global current is required.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ordered-contact-general}
Let $\theta$ be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form and let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $u\leq v$. Then
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u=v\}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{u=v\}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(v).
\]
No finiteness of either extended mass is assumed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first prove a local bounded version. Let $a\leq b$ be bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on a coordinate domain and suppose
$\gamma+\ddc a\geq0$ and $\gamma+\ddc b\geq0$ for a smooth form $\gamma$. Set
\[
 m_\delta=\max(a+\delta,b),\qquad \delta>0.
\]
Since $b\leq m_\delta\leq b+\delta$ and $m_\delta\downarrow b$, decreasing continuity gives weak convergence of the positive bounded-product measures to that of $b$. On the plurifine open set $\{a+\delta>b\}$, which contains $\{a=b\}$, locality and invariance under constants identify their measures with that of $a$. Therefore, for every compact $K\subset\{a=b\}$, the upper bound for weak limits on compact sets gives
\[
 \int_K(\gamma+\ddc a)^n
 =\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\int_K(\gamma+\ddc m_\delta)^n
 \leq\int_K(\gamma+\ddc b)^n.
\]
Inner regularity on any Borel subset of the equality set proves the bounded assertion. This argument uses positivity, so we do not apply it directly to signed global truncation products.

To treat $u,v$, fix a coordinate ball with a constant positive form $\kappa$, a level $t>0$, and $\epsilon>0$. On smaller neighborhoods freeze $\theta$ to a constant form $\theta'$ with
$-\epsilon\kappa\leq\theta-\theta'\leq\epsilon\kappa$. Choose a quadratic function $r$ with
$\ddc r=\theta'+\epsilon\kappa$. Then $u+r$ and $v+r$ are plurisubharmonic. For a large integer $k$ put
\[
 a_k=\max(u+r,-k)-r,\qquad
 b_k=\max(v+r,-k)-r.
\]
They are bounded on relatively compact neighborhoods, satisfy $a_k\leq b_k$, and
\[
 \theta+2\epsilon\kappa+\ddc a_k
 =\ddc\max(u+r,-k)+(\theta-\theta'+\epsilon\kappa)\geq0,
\]
with the same inequality for $b_k$. Apply the bounded assertion with
$\gamma=\theta+2\epsilon\kappa$.

On $D_t=\{u>-t\}$, both $u$ and $v$ are above $-t$. Taking $k>t+\sup|r|$ makes $a_k=u$ and $b_k=v$ on $D_t$. Restrict the bounded inequality to $\{u=v\}\cap D_t$ and use locality. It gives the desired contact inequality for background $\theta+2\epsilon\kappa$ on that set. The smaller neighborhoods cover the original ball, so the restricted inequality holds throughout it.

Now choose a larger Hermitian background $\Omega\geq\theta$ and fixed bounded truncations $u_t=\max(u,-t)$, $v_t=\max(v,-t)$. On $D_t$, the products just used equal the restrictions of
$(\theta+\ddc u_t+2\epsilon\kappa)^n$ and
$(\theta+\ddc v_t+2\epsilon\kappa)^n$. These are polynomials in $\epsilon$ whose coefficients have locally finite variation. They converge in local total variation as $\epsilon\downarrow0$, including after restriction to $\{u=v\}\cap D_t$. This proves the inequality for background $\theta$ on each finite level. Finally let $t\to+\infty$. The common pole set has zero extended mass, and the levels exhaust its complement in the equality set.
\end{proof}

\section{The bounded mass hypothesis}

\begin{definition}\label{def:bmp}
For a Hermitian form $\omega$, set
\[
 v_+(\omega)=
 \sup_{v\in\PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^\infty(X)}
       \int_X(\omega+\ddc v)^n.
\]
The manifold has the \emph{bounded mass property} if $v_+(\omega)<+\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bmp-metric}
The bounded mass property is independent of the Hermitian metric. Moreover, the supremum in \cref{def:bmp} is unchanged if only smooth potentials are used.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $\eta\leq C\omega$. If $v$ is a bounded $\eta$-plurisubharmonic function, then $v/C$ is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic and
\[
 0\leq\eta+\ddc v
 \leq C\bigl(\omega+\ddc(v/C)\bigr).
\]
By positivity of mixed bounded products,
$\int_X(\eta+\ddc v)^n\leq C^n v_+(\omega)$.
Comparison in the other direction proves metric independence.

For the last assertion apply \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization}. The smooth approximants decrease and remain uniformly bounded, so continuity of bounded products gives convergence of their total masses. The reverse inequality between the two suprema is immediate. This also recovers the comparison recorded after \cite[Definition 1.16]{BGL25}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bmp-finite}
Assume $v_+(\omega)<+\infty$. If $0\leq\theta\leq C\omega$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)(X)\leq C^n v_+(\omega).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every constant truncation $u_t$ is bounded and $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. Apply the comparison of positive forms from \cref{prop:bmp-metric} to $u_t$, and then restrict to $D_t$. The bound is independent of $t$, so it passes to the increasing limit.
\end{proof}

For arbitrary smooth backgrounds, including non-semipositive ones, the comparison in \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma} gives the same finite-mass conclusion under bounded mass, after enlarging the Hermitian metric. The finite mixed-current construction of \cite[Theorem 2.2]{BGL25} also uses a uniform variation bound before removing the restrictions. Such a bound is unnecessary for an extended positive measure, but is essential for viewing the result as a global current.

The counterexample of Xia--Zhang \cite{XZ26} shows that \cref{def:bmp} is a genuine restriction in dimension three. Smooth solvability of an equation with a fixed density, established in the next part, therefore coexists with failure of a uniform mass bound over all potentials.

\section{Small exponential weights}

First, \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives, for normalized potentials $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $\sup_Xv=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-lone}
 \int_X|v|\,\mathrm{d}V\leq K_\omega.
\end{equation}
Changing the smooth volume form only changes the constant. The genuine decreasing-limit consequence is \cref{cor:decreasing-limit}. The normalized estimate is also recorded in \cite[Proposition 6.2]{Dinew16}.

Second, local Skoda integrability implies that every quasi-plurisubharmonic function $v$ on compact $X$ admits some exponent $a(v)>0$ for which $e^{-a(v)v}$ is integrable. Indeed, apply the local theorem to a plurisubharmonic correction near each point, and then take the minimum exponent over a finite cover. The local theorem is recalled in \cite[Section 1]{Zeriahi01}. This assertion is qualitative: the exponent may initially depend on $v$.

The compactness estimate and this standard local input imply the uniform version needed here.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:uniform-exponential}
There are constants $\alpha>0$ and $C<+\infty$, depending on $(X,\omega,\mathrm{d}V)$, such that
\[
 \int_Xe^{-\alpha v}\,\mathrm{d}V\leq C
 \quad\hbox{for all }v\in\PSH(X,\omega),\quad \sup_Xv=0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose no such pair exists. Set $a_j=2^{-j}$ and choose normalized potentials $v_j\leq0$ with
\[
 \int_X\exp\!\left(-\frac{a_j}{j}v_j\right)\mathrm{d}V\geq2^j.
\]
The partial sums $V_N=\sum_{j\leq N}a_jv_j$ decrease, are $\omega$-plurisubharmonic, and have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms by \eqref{eq:normalized-lone}. Their limit $V$ is therefore $\omega$-plurisubharmonic. Since all summands are nonpositive, $V\leq a_jv_j$ for every $j$.

Fix $p>0$. For every $j\geq1/p$,
\[
 \int_Xe^{-pV}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq\int_Xe^{-pa_jv_j}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq\int_Xe^{-(a_j/j)v_j}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq2^j.
\]
Thus $e^{-pV}$ is nonintegrable for every $p>0$, contradicting the qualitative Skoda input applied to $V$.
\end{proof}

\section{Infinite mass with trivial multiplier ideals}

The failure of bounded mass has a consequence for individual singularities, rather than only for sequences of smooth potentials.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:infinite-mass-witness}
If $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, there is $U\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, $U\leq0$, such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(U)(X)=+\infty,
 \qquad
 \int_Xe^{-pU}\,\mathrm{d}V<+\infty\quad\hbox{for every }p>0.
\]
In particular, $\mathcal I(pU)=\mathcal O_X$ for every $p>0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $a_j=2^{-j}$. By \cref{prop:bmp-metric}, choose smooth $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions $u_j$ with $\sup_Xu_j=0$ and
\[
 M_j:=\int_X\omega_{u_j}^n\geq2^{j(n+1)}.
\]
The partial sums $U_N=\sum_{j\leq N}a_ju_j$ decrease and satisfy
\[
 \omega+\ddc U_N
 =\sum_{j\leq N}a_j\omega_{u_j}
     +\left(1-\sum_{j\leq N}a_j\right)\omega\geq0.
\]
Their $L^1$ norms are bounded by \eqref{eq:normalized-lone}, so their limit $U$ is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic and nonpositive.

Fix $j$ and write $v_j=U-a_ju_j$. Taking limits in the remaining partial sums gives
$(1-a_j)\omega+\ddc v_j\geq0$. For $t>0$, put
\[
 v_{j,t}=\max(v_j,-t),\qquad W_{j,t}=a_ju_j+v_{j,t}.
\]
The first is a bounded potential for the positive background $(1-a_j)\omega$, so $W_{j,t}$ is bounded and $\omega$-plurisubharmonic.

On $D_t=\{U>-t\}$, the inequality $u_j\leq0$ gives
$v_j=U-a_ju_j\geq U>-t$. Thus $W_{j,t}=U=\max(U,-t)$ there. Plurifine locality and positivity of bounded mixed products yield
\begin{align*}
 \mathbf1_{D_t}\omega_{\max(U,-t)}^n
 &=\mathbf1_{D_t}
   \bigl(a_j\omega_{u_j}
        +(1-a_j)\omega+\ddc v_{j,t}\bigr)^n\\
 &\geq a_j^n\mathbf1_{D_t}\omega_{u_j}^n.
\end{align*}
The sets $D_t$ exhaust $X\setminus\{U=-\infty\}$. This pole set is pluripolar, hence has zero smooth volume; the smooth measure $\omega_{u_j}^n$ gives it zero mass. Letting $t\to+\infty$, we obtain
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(U)(X)
 \geq a_j^nM_j\geq2^j.
\]
Since $j$ is arbitrary, the mass is infinite.

For integrability, fix $p>0$ and take $N$ so large that
$p\delta\leq\alpha$, where $\delta=\sum_{j>N}a_j=2^{-N}$ and $\alpha$ is from \cref{lem:uniform-exponential}. Write
$U=A_N+V_N$ with $A_N=\sum_{j\leq N}a_ju_j$ smooth.
For a finite tail ending at $m>N$, Jensen's inequality, with the unused weight assigned to zero, gives
\begin{align*}
 \exp\!\left(-p\sum_{N<j\leq m}a_ju_j\right)
 &\leq\sum_{N<j\leq m}\frac{a_j}{\delta}
                  e^{-p\delta u_j}
       +1-\sum_{N<j\leq m}\frac{a_j}{\delta}.
\end{align*}
Because $u_j\leq0$ and $p\delta\leq\alpha$, the integrals of the exponentials on the right are uniformly bounded. Integrate and let $m\to+\infty$ by monotone convergence to obtain $\int_Xe^{-pV_N}\mathrm{d}V<+\infty$. Multiplication by the bounded smooth function $e^{-pA_N}$ proves $\int_Xe^{-pU}\mathrm{d}V<+\infty$.

The constant germ $1$ is consequently integrable against each weight $e^{-pU}$, so every multiplier ideal in the statement is the whole structure sheaf.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bmp-characterization}
The bounded mass property is equivalent to finiteness of
$\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(u)(X)$ for every $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$.
If bounded mass fails, potentials of infinite extended mass with all multiplier ideals trivial can approach zero in $L^1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows from \cref{prop:bmp-finite,thm:infinite-mass-witness}. For the second, use $\varepsilon U$, $0<\varepsilon\leq1$, with $U$ as above. It is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic and has all the required exponential integrability. On $D_t=\{U>-t\}$ its truncation at level $\varepsilon t$ is $\varepsilon\max(U,-t)$, and
\[
 \omega+\ddc\bigl(\varepsilon\max(U,-t)\bigr)
 =\varepsilon\omega_{\max(U,-t)}+(1-\varepsilon)\omega.
\]
Positivity of mixed products gives a lower bound of $\varepsilon^n$ times the truncation measure for $U$. Its limiting mass is therefore infinite. Finally, $\|\varepsilon U\|_{L^1}\to0$.
\end{proof}

In particular, the manifold in \cite{XZ26} has a potential that is $\mathcal I$-equivalent to a smooth function while its extended mass is infinite. Thus the multiplier ideals alone cannot control the mass of a representative. A Hermitian characterization of $\mathcal I$-goodness must specify a finite-mass domain or a different global invariant; \cref{chap:hermitian-igood} returns to this problem.
