\subsection{Adding a prescribed local measure}
\label{subsec:local-prescribed-excess}

The concentration in \cref{prop:smooth-dirac-concentration} can be produced on top of a given bounded potential $b$ and repeated, so as to add an arbitrary prescribed measure to $(\ddc b)^n$. Adding a perturbation $h$ to $b$ creates the mixed terms $(\ddc b)^{n-r}\wedge(\ddc h)^r$. A mixed Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimate controls them, provided that the lower-degree currents of $h$ are kept small.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ridge-lower-degree-limit}
Fix the data $n,U,v,\varepsilon,R,\eta,\chi_\eta$ of \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge}, and write $h_A$ for the smooth plurisubharmonic function $h$ of that lemma, regarded as a function of $A$. Put $D=\{0\}\times U$, and write $[D]$ for the current of integration over this hypersurface. Then, for every $1\leq r\leq n$,
\[
 (\ddc h_A)^r\longrightarrow
       \varepsilon[D]\wedge(\ddc v)^{r-1}
 \quad\text{as currents on }\mathbb C\times U,\ A\to\infty.
\]
The product on the right is integration of the restriction of the smooth form $(\ddc v)^{r-1}$ over $D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $S=\ddc v$, $q=\varepsilon\log(|\zeta|^2/R^2)$ and $T_A=v-A-q$. The rank-one calculation in the proof of \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge} gives
\[
 (\ddc h_A)^r=\chi_\eta'(T_A)^rS^r
       +r\chi_\eta'(T_A)^{r-1}\chi_\eta''(T_A)
                   \,\mathrm dT_A\wedge\dc T_A\wedge S^{r-1}
\]
off $D$; since $h_A$ is smooth and $D$ has measure zero, this formula determines the current. Fix a smooth compactly supported test form. On the projection of its support to $U$, the function $v$ and its derivatives are bounded. Hence the support of $\chi_\eta'(T_A)$ lies in $\{|\zeta|\leq s_A\}$, where $s_A=C\exp(-A/(2\varepsilon))$ and $C$ depends only on this compact set and the fixed data. The coefficients involving $v$ and $\chi_\eta$ are bounded at the fixed parameters, so the first summand has pairing $O(s_A^2)$. In the second summand, the term with two derivatives of $v$ also has pairing $O(s_A^2)$, and the two cross terms have pairing $O(s_A)$: the derivative of $q$ in the $\zeta$ direction has size $O(|\zeta|^{-1})$, and its area integral over the disc $\{|\zeta|\leq s_A\}$ is $O(s_A)$.

It remains to integrate the term containing $\mathrm dq\wedge\dc q$. The logarithmic change of the radial variable used for \eqref{eq:smooth-ridge-transverse-integral} leaves the factor $\varepsilon$ and the probability weight
\[
 r\chi_\eta'(t)^{r-1}\chi_\eta''(t)\,\mathrm dt,
 \qquad -\eta\leq t\leq\eta.
\]
Only the component of the test form in the $z'$ directions enters this term, and on the shrinking circles its coefficients tend uniformly to their values at $\zeta=0$. Integration in the angle therefore gives, in the limit, exactly the pairing with $\varepsilon[D]\wedge S^{r-1}$. This proves the convergence in every degree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-mixed-cln-trace}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a domain, let $K$ and $L$ be compact subsets of $\Omega$ with $K$ contained in the interior $L^\circ$ of $L$, and put $\beta=\ddc|z|^2$. Let $b$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$, and let $T$ be a smooth positive closed $(r,r)$-form, $1\leq r<n$. Then
\[
 \int_K(\ddc b)^{n-r}\wedge T
 \leq C_{K,L,n}(1+\|b\|_{L^\infty(L)})^{n-r}
                     \int_L T\wedge\beta^{n-r}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $M=1+\|b\|_{L^\infty(L)}$. Cover $K$ by finitely many balls whose larger concentric balls have closures in $L^\circ$. Replacing $b$ by $b+M$, which has the same Hessian and takes values in $[0,2M]$ on $L$, we may assume $0\leq b\leq2M$ there. For nested cutoff functions $\psi$, integration by parts against closed forms gives
\[
 \int\psi\ddc b\wedge W
       =\int b\ddc\psi\wedge W
       \leq CM
                   \int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi}\beta\wedge W,
\]
where $W$ is $T$ multiplied by the remaining positive closed factors. Apply this successively to the $n-r$ Hessians of $b$. For bounded $b$, the integrations by parts are justified by local decreasing regularization and \cref{thm:bt-input}, with one common bound on the range. Summing over the finite cover gives the asserted constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:local-one-atom-addition}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq2$, be a bounded domain, let $b\in\PSH(\Omega)\cap L^\infty(\Omega)$, $a\in\Omega$ and $c>0$. Given finitely many $f_i\in C_c(\Omega)$ and numbers $\tau,t>0$, there is a smooth bounded plurisubharmonic function $h\leq0$ on $\Omega$ such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \|h\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,p}t\quad(1\leq p<\infty),\\
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i\bigl((\ddc(b+h))^n-(\ddc b)^n\bigr)
                         -cf_i(a)\right|<\tau\quad\text{for every }i.
 \end{gathered}
\]
For concentric balls $B'\Subset B\Subset\Omega$ and every $w\in\PSH(B)$ with $0\leq w\leq1$, it also satisfies
\[
 \int_{B'}|h|(\ddc w)^n\leq C_{B',B,\Omega}t.
\]
These constants are independent of $b,a,c$, the test functions and the tolerances.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We take for $h$ a function from \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge} whose top-degree measure approximates $c\delta_a$ and whose lower-degree currents are small; \cref{lem:local-mixed-cln-trace} then controls the mixed terms.

Write $a=(a_1,a')$ and $m=n-1$, and fix $R=2\sup_\Omega|z_1|+2$. For $\delta>0$, put $H_\delta(z')=\log(|z'-a'|^2+\delta^2)$. By the radial calculation in the proof of \cref{prop:smooth-dirac-concentration}, $(\ddc H_\delta)^m$ is a probability measure tending to $\delta_{a'}$ as $\delta\downarrow0$. Extend every $f_i$ by zero outside $\Omega$, and choose $\delta$ such that
\[
 \left|c\int_{\mathbb C^m}f_i(a_1,z')(\ddc H_\delta)^m
                  -cf_i(a)\right|<\tau/3\quad\text{for all }i.
\]
Choose compact sets $K,L\subset\Omega$ with $K\subset L^\circ$ and $K$ containing the supports of all $f_i$, and a nonnegative smooth cutoff function $\psi$ with compact support in $\Omega$, equal to one near $L$. Put $\beta=\ddc|z|^2$. Expanding the top power of $\ddc(b+h)$, summing the bounds of \cref{lem:local-mixed-cln-trace} with their binomial coefficients and the norms $\|f_i\|_\infty$, we find $\gamma>0$ such that the mixed terms contribute less than $\tau/3$ to every test integral whenever $h$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic and
\[
 \int_L(\ddc h)^r\wedge\beta^{n-r}<\gamma,
 \qquad 1\leq r<n.
\]

Fix $0<\eta\leq t/2$. Apply \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge} on $\mathbb C\times\mathbb C^m$ with transverse variable $\zeta=z_1-a_1$ and $v=c^{1/m}\varepsilon^{-1/m}H_\delta$. For $r<n$, \cref{lem:ridge-lower-degree-limit} gives
\[
 (\ddc h_A)^r\longrightarrow
 \varepsilon^{(n-r)/(n-1)}c^{(r-1)/(n-1)}
               [\{z_1=a_1\}]\wedge(\ddc H_\delta)^{r-1}
\]
as $A\to\infty$. For the fixed $\delta$, the integrals of these limits against $\psi\beta^{n-r}$ are finite, and their exponents of $\varepsilon$ are positive. Choose $0<\varepsilon\leq t/2$ so that each of them is less than $\gamma/2$. With these parameters fixed, choose $A$ so large that the integrals $\int_L(\ddc h_A)^r\wedge\beta^{n-r}$ are less than $\gamma$, that $v-A\leq0$ on the projection of $\Omega$ to the $z'$ variables, and that
\[
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i(\ddc h_A)^n
   -c\int_{\mathbb C^m}f_i(a_1,z')(\ddc H_\delta)^m\right|<\tau/3
 \quad\text{for all }i.
\]
The last choice follows directly from \eqref{eq:smooth-ridge-transverse-integral}, because the circles there shrink uniformly over the projected supports of the tests and $\varepsilon(\ddc v)^m=c(\ddc H_\delta)^m$.

Set $h=h_A-\eta$. As in the proof of \cref{prop:smooth-dirac-concentration}, $h$ is smooth, bounded and nonpositive on $\Omega$, with
\[
 \varepsilon\log(|z_1-a_1|^2/R^2)-\eta\leq h\leq0.
\]
The logarithm has $L^p(\Omega)$ norm bounded uniformly for $a_1$ in the projection of $\Omega$ to the first coordinate: after translation, its one-variable integral is bounded by polar integration on one fixed disc. This uniform bound, the weighted Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimate and the range estimate \eqref{eq:local-cln-range} give the asserted bound for the integrals over $B'$. Since $\varepsilon+\eta\leq t$, both estimates hold with constants depending only on $\Omega$, $p$, $B'$ and $B$.

Finally, multilinearity of bounded products gives
\[
 (\ddc(b+h))^n=(\ddc b)^n+(\ddc h)^n
       +\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\binom nr
                     (\ddc b)^{n-r}\wedge(\ddc h)^r.
\]
The choice of $\delta$, the choice of $A$ for the top-degree term and the estimate of the mixed terms each contribute less than $\tau/3$ to every test integral. This proves the required inequalities, with one choice of $h$ for all finite $p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:local-prescribed-excess}\index{Monge--Ampere product@Monge--Amp\`ere product!prescribed excess measure}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq2$, be a bounded domain, let $b\in\PSH(\Omega)\cap L^\infty(\Omega)$, and let $\nu$ be a positive Radon measure on $\Omega$. There are bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_j\leq b$ on $\Omega$ such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \|u_j-b\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\longrightarrow0\quad(1\leq p<\infty),\\
 (\ddc u_j)^n\longrightarrow(\ddc b)^n+\nu
                    \quad\text{vaguely}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The same sequence converges to $b$ in local capacity. If $b$ is smooth, every $u_j$ can be chosen smooth. The measure $\nu$ may have infinite total mass.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose relatively compact open sets exhausting $\Omega$, each with closure contained in the next one. On each of these sets, take a countable family of smooth compactly supported functions that is uniformly dense in the space of continuous functions vanishing at its boundary, and add a nonnegative smooth compactly supported cutoff function equal to one on its closure. Enumerate the union of these families as $(f_i)$. If positive measures converge against every $f_i$ to a fixed positive Radon measure, they converge vaguely: the cutoff functions give common local mass bounds, and uniform approximation then handles every continuous compactly supported test function.

For each $j$, approximate $\nu$ against $f_1,\ldots,f_j$ by a finite positive atomic measure
\[
 \nu_j=\sum_{s=1}^{N_j}c_{j,s}\delta_{a_{j,s}},\qquad
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i(\nu_j-\nu)\right|<j^{-1}\quad(i\leq j).
\]
Such an approximation uses only the finite mass of $\nu$ on a compact neighborhood of the supports of these tests: a finite partition on which all the tests have small oscillation provides the atoms and their positive weights. Atoms of weight zero are omitted. If $N_j=0$, set $u_j=b$. Otherwise start with $b_{j,0}=b$ and apply \cref{prop:local-one-atom-addition} successively to $b_{j,s-1}$, $a_{j,s}$ and $c_{j,s}$, with the tests $f_1,\ldots,f_j$ and $\tau=t=(jN_j)^{-1}$. This gives perturbations $h_{j,s}$, and we define
\[
 b_{j,s}=b_{j,s-1}+h_{j,s},\qquad u_j=b_{j,N_j}.
\]
Every intermediate potential is bounded and plurisubharmonic, so the proposition applies at each step; the choice at the next step may depend on the bound of the current potential, and each perturbation serves all finite $p$ at once. Summing the norm estimates yields $\|u_j-b\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,p}/j$, and since every $h_{j,s}\leq0$, we have $u_j\leq b$. Summing the measure errors gives
\[
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i\bigl((\ddc u_j)^n-(\ddc b)^n-\nu\bigr)\right|
       <2/j\quad(i\leq j).
\]
By the choice of the family $(f_i)$, this proves the asserted vague limit, also when $\nu(\Omega)=\infty$.

For fixed concentric balls $B'\Subset B\Subset\Omega$, the perturbations are nonpositive, so $|u_j-b|=\sum_s|h_{j,s}|$. The last estimate of \cref{prop:local-one-atom-addition} bounds the integral of this function against $(\ddc w)^n$ over $B'$ by $C_{B',B,\Omega}/j$, for every $w\in\PSH(B)$ with $0\leq w\leq1$. Restricting to the set where $|u_j-b|$ exceeds a fixed threshold, dividing by the threshold and taking the supremum over $w$ proves convergence in local capacity. If $b$ is smooth, the finite sums defining $u_j$ are smooth as well.
\end{proof}

The construction takes place in a bounded Euclidean domain, without prescribed boundary values and without a common bound on the approximating potentials. These freedoms allow concentration even under convergence in local capacity; a fixed global Hermitian background imposes further constraints.
