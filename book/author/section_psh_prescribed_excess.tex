\subsection{Adding a prescribed local measure}
\label{subsec:local-prescribed-excess}

We now construct the potentials of \cref{prop:smooth-dirac-concentration} on top of a given locally bounded potential $b$, and repeat the construction so as to add an arbitrary positive measure to $(\ddc b)^n$. Adding a perturbation $h$ to $b$ creates the mixed terms $(\ddc b)^{n-r}\wedge(\ddc h)^r$. A mixed Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimate controls them, provided that the lower-degree currents of $h$ are kept small.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ridge-lower-degree-limit}
Fix the data $n,U,v,\varepsilon,R,\eta,\chi_\eta$ of \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge}, and write $h_A$ for the smooth plurisubharmonic function $h$ of that lemma, regarded as a function of $A$. Put $D=\{0\}\times U$, and write $[D]$ for the current of integration over this hypersurface. Then, for every $1\leq r\leq n$,
\[
 (\ddc h_A)^r\longrightarrow
       \varepsilon[D]\wedge(\ddc v)^{r-1}
 \quad\text{as currents on }\mathbb C\times U,\ A\to\infty.
\]
The product on the right is integration of the restriction of the smooth form $(\ddc v)^{r-1}$ over $D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $S=\ddc v$, $q=\varepsilon\log(|\zeta|^2/R^2)$ and $T_A=v-A-q$. The rank-one calculation in the proof of \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge} gives
\[
 (\ddc h_A)^r=\chi_\eta'(T_A)^rS^r
       +r\chi_\eta'(T_A)^{r-1}\chi_\eta''(T_A)
                   \,\mathrm dT_A\wedge\dc T_A\wedge S^{r-1}
\]
off $D$; since $h_A$ is smooth and $D$ has measure zero, this formula determines the current. Fix a smooth compactly supported test form. On the projection of its support to $U$, the function $v$ and its derivatives are bounded. Hence the support of $\chi_\eta'(T_A)$ lies in $\{|\zeta|\leq s_A\}$, where $s_A=C\exp(-A/(2\varepsilon))$ and $C$ depends only on this compact set and the fixed data. The coefficients involving $v$ and $\chi_\eta$ are bounded at the fixed parameters, so the first summand has pairing $O(s_A^2)$. In the second summand, the term with two derivatives of $v$ also has pairing $O(s_A^2)$, and the two cross terms have pairing $O(s_A)$: the derivative of $q$ in the $\zeta$ direction has size $O(|\zeta|^{-1})$, and its area integral over the disk $\{|\zeta|\leq s_A\}$ is $O(s_A)$.

It remains to integrate the term containing $\mathrm dq\wedge\dc q$. The logarithmic change of the radial variable used for \eqref{eq:smooth-ridge-transverse-integral} leaves the factor $\varepsilon$ and the probability weight
\[
 r\chi_\eta'(t)^{r-1}\chi_\eta''(t)\,\mathrm dt,
 \qquad -\eta\leq t\leq\eta.
\]
Only the component of the test form in the $z'$ directions enters this term, and on the shrinking circles its coefficients tend uniformly to their values at $\zeta=0$. Integration in the angle therefore gives, in the limit, exactly the pairing with $\varepsilon[D]\wedge S^{r-1}$. This proves the convergence in every degree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-mixed-cln-trace}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a domain, let $K$ and $L$ be compact subsets of $\Omega$ with $K$ contained in the interior $L^\circ$ of $L$, and put $\beta=\ddc|z|^2$. Let $b$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$, and let $T$ be a smooth positive closed $(r,r)$-form, $1\leq r<n$. Then
\[
 \int_K(\ddc b)^{n-r}\wedge T
 \leq C_{K,L,n}(1+\|b\|_{L^\infty(L)})^{n-r}
                     \int_L T\wedge\beta^{n-r}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $M=1+\|b\|_{L^\infty(L)}$. Cover $K$ by finitely many balls whose larger concentric balls have closures in $L^\circ$. Replacing $b$ by $b+M$, which has the same Hessian and takes values in $[0,2M]$ on $L$, we may assume $0\leq b\leq2M$ there. For nested cutoff functions $\psi$, integration by parts against closed forms gives
\[
 \int\psi\ddc b\wedge W
       =\int b\ddc\psi\wedge W
       \leq CM
                   \int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi}\beta\wedge W,
\]
where $W$ is $T$ multiplied by the remaining positive closed factors. Apply this successively to the $n-r$ Hessians of $b$. For bounded $b$, the integrations by parts are justified by local decreasing regularization and \cref{thm:bt-input}, with one common bound on the range. Summing over the finite cover gives the asserted constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:local-one-atom-addition}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq2$, be a bounded domain, let $b$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$, and let $a\in\Omega$ and $c>0$. Given finitely many $f_i\in C_c(\Omega)$ and numbers $\tau,t>0$, there is a smooth bounded plurisubharmonic function $h\leq0$ on $\Omega$ such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \|h\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,p}t\quad(1\leq p<\infty),\\
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i\bigl((\ddc(b+h))^n-(\ddc b)^n\bigr)
                         -cf_i(a)\right|<\tau\quad\text{for every }i.
 \end{gathered}
\]
For concentric balls $B'\Subset B\Subset\Omega$ and every $w\in\PSH(B)$ with $0\leq w\leq1$, it also satisfies
\[
 \int_{B'}|h|(\ddc w)^n\leq C_{B',B,\Omega}t.
\]
These constants are independent of $b,a,c$, the test functions and the tolerances.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We take for $h$ a function from \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge} whose top-degree measure approximates $c\delta_a$ and whose lower-degree currents are small; \cref{lem:local-mixed-cln-trace} then controls the mixed terms.

Write $a=(a_1,a')$ and $m=n-1$, and fix $R=2\sup_\Omega|z_1|+2$. For $\delta>0$, put $H_\delta(z')=\log(|z'-a'|^2+\delta^2)$. With $s=|z'-a'|^2$, the complex Hessian of $H_\delta$ has the eigenvalue $(s+\delta^2)^{-1}$ with multiplicity $m-1$ in the directions orthogonal to $z'-a'$ and the eigenvalue $\delta^2(s+\delta^2)^{-2}$ in the radial direction. Taking the determinant and integrating in polar coordinates gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 (\ddc H_\delta)^m=
 \frac{m!}{\pi^m}\frac{\delta^2}{(|z'-a'|^2+\delta^2)^{m+1}}
                       \,\mathrm d\lambda_{2m},\\
 \int_{|z'-a'|<\rho}(\ddc H_\delta)^m
       =\left(\frac{\rho^2}{\rho^2+\delta^2}\right)^m,
 \end{gathered}
\]
where $\lambda_{2m}$ is Lebesgue measure. These probability measures tend to $\delta_{a'}$ as $\delta\downarrow0$, since their mass outside each ball about $a'$ tends to zero. Extend every $f_i$ by zero outside $\Omega$, and choose $\delta$ such that
\[
 \left|c\int_{\mathbb C^m}f_i(a_1,z')(\ddc H_\delta)^m
                  -cf_i(a)\right|<\tau/3\quad\text{for all }i.
\]
Choose compact sets $K,L\subset\Omega$ with $K\subset L^\circ$ and $K$ containing the supports of all $f_i$, and a nonnegative smooth cutoff function $\psi$ with compact support in $\Omega$, equal to one near $L$. Put $\beta=\ddc|z|^2$. Expanding the top power of $\ddc(b+h)$, summing the bounds of \cref{lem:local-mixed-cln-trace} with their binomial coefficients and the norms $\|f_i\|_\infty$, we find $\gamma>0$ such that the mixed terms contribute less than $\tau/3$ to every test integral whenever $h$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic and
\[
 \int_L(\ddc h)^r\wedge\beta^{n-r}<\gamma,
 \qquad 1\leq r<n.
\]

Fix $0<\eta\leq t/2$. Apply \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge} on $\mathbb C\times\mathbb C^m$ with transverse variable $\zeta=z_1-a_1$ and $v=c^{1/m}\varepsilon^{-1/m}H_\delta$. For $r<n$, \cref{lem:ridge-lower-degree-limit} gives
\[
 (\ddc h_A)^r\longrightarrow
 \varepsilon^{(n-r)/(n-1)}c^{(r-1)/(n-1)}
               [\{z_1=a_1\}]\wedge(\ddc H_\delta)^{r-1}
\]
as $A\to\infty$. For the fixed $\delta$, the integrals of these limits against $\psi\beta^{n-r}$ are finite, and their exponents of $\varepsilon$ are positive. Choose $0<\varepsilon\leq t/2$ so that each of them is less than $\gamma/2$. With these parameters fixed, choose $A$ so large that the integrals $\int_L(\ddc h_A)^r\wedge\beta^{n-r}$ are less than $\gamma$, that $v-A\leq0$ on the projection of $\Omega$ to the $z'$ variables, and that
\[
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i(\ddc h_A)^n
   -c\int_{\mathbb C^m}f_i(a_1,z')(\ddc H_\delta)^m\right|<\tau/3
 \quad\text{for all }i.
\]
The last choice follows from \eqref{eq:smooth-ridge-transverse-integral} and the uniform continuity of the $f_i$: as $A\to\infty$, the radii of the circles there tend to zero uniformly for $z'$ in the projection of $\Omega$, while $\varepsilon(\ddc v)^m=c(\ddc H_\delta)^m$ has mass $c$.

Set $h=h_A-\eta$, a smooth plurisubharmonic function. In the notation of \cref{lem:smooth-logarithmic-ridge}, now $q=\varepsilon\log(|z_1-a_1|^2/R^2)$ and $T=v-A-q$. The convolution satisfies $0\leq\chi_\eta-\max(\cdot,0)\leq\eta$, and $q+\max(T,0)=\max(q,v-A)$. On $\Omega$, both branches of this maximum are nonpositive, by the choices of $R$ and $A$, and the second is bounded below. Hence $h$ is bounded on $\Omega$, and
\[
 q-\eta\leq h\leq0,
 \qquad\text{so}\qquad
 |h|\leq\varepsilon\bigl|\log(|z_1-a_1|^2/R^2)\bigr|+\eta.
\]
The logarithm has $L^p(\Omega)$ norm bounded uniformly for $a_1$ in the projection of $\Omega$ to the first coordinate: after translation, its one-variable integral is bounded by polar integration on one fixed disk. This gives the $L^p$ bound. The weighted Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimate, applied to this nonpositive plurisubharmonic logarithm, and the range estimate \eqref{eq:local-cln-range} for the constant term give the bound for the integrals over $B'$. Since $\varepsilon+\eta\leq t$, both estimates hold with constants depending only on $\Omega$, $p$, $B'$ and $B$.

Finally, multilinearity of bounded products gives
\[
 (\ddc(b+h))^n=(\ddc b)^n+(\ddc h)^n
       +\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\binom nr
                     (\ddc b)^{n-r}\wedge(\ddc h)^r.
\]
The choice of $\delta$, the choice of $A$ for the top-degree term and the estimate of the mixed terms each contribute less than $\tau/3$ to every test integral. This proves the required inequalities, with one choice of $h$ for all finite $p$.
\end{proof}

For a domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, write $\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$ for the set of positive Radon measures on $\Omega$, including zero and measures of infinite total mass, with the vague topology defined by integration against $C_c(\Omega)$. This topology is metrizable.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-vague-metric}\index{Vague convergence!metric}
There are real functions $f_i\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ such that
\[
 d_v(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{i\geq1}2^{-i}
 \frac{\left|\int_\Omega f_i(\alpha-\beta)\right|}
      {1+\left|\int_\Omega f_i(\alpha-\beta)\right|}
\]
is a metric inducing the vague topology on $\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$. The finite atomic measures with nonnegative rational weights at points of a fixed countable dense subset of $\Omega$ are dense in this metric space; in particular, it is separable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose relatively compact open sets $\Omega_k$ exhausting $\Omega$, each with closure contained in the next one. On each $\Omega_k$, take a countable family of functions in $C_c^\infty(\Omega_k)$ that is uniformly dense in the space of continuous functions on $\overline\Omega_k$ vanishing on $\partial\Omega_k$, and add a function $\chi_k\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with $0\leq\chi_k\leq1$ and $\chi_k=1$ on $\overline\Omega_k$. Enumerate the union of these families as $(f_i)$, and call the maps $\alpha\mapsto\int_\Omega f_i\,\mathrm d\alpha$ the coordinates.

Fix $\alpha\in\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$, $g\in C_c(\Omega)$ and $0<\epsilon\leq1$. Choose $k$ with $\operatorname{supp}g\subset\Omega_k$ and a member $f_i$ of the family of $\Omega_k$ with $|g-f_i|<\epsilon$. For every $\beta\in\mathcal M_+(\Omega)$,
\[
 \left|\int_\Omega g\,\mathrm d(\beta-\alpha)\right|
 \leq\epsilon\int_\Omega\chi_k\,\mathrm d(\alpha+\beta)
     +\left|\int_\Omega f_i\,\mathrm d(\beta-\alpha)\right|.
\]
On the set where the coordinates of $\chi_k$ and $f_i$ differ from those of $\alpha$ by less than $\epsilon$, the right-hand side is at most $\epsilon(2\int\chi_k\,\mathrm d\alpha+2)$. Thus integration against $g$ is continuous for the topology generated by the coordinates. Since the coordinates are vaguely continuous, they generate exactly the vague topology, and they separate measures.

The function $s\mapsto s/(1+s)$ is increasing and subadditive on $[0,\infty)$. Applying it to the triangle inequalities for the coordinates gives the triangle inequality for $d_v$, and $d_v$ is positive on distinct measures because the coordinates separate them. Each coordinate is continuous for this metric, and the tails of the series are uniformly small, so the metric induces exactly the topology generated by the coordinates.

For density, fix $\alpha$ and $j$, and let $K\subset\Omega$ be a compact set containing the supports of $f_1,\ldots,f_j$. Partition $K$ into finitely many Borel sets $E_s$ of diameter less than $\eta$, and choose points $a_s$ of the dense subset within distance $\eta$ of $E_s$ and rational weights $c_s\geq0$ with $\sum_s|c_s-\alpha(E_s)|<\eta$. If $\eta$ is small, the measure $\sum_sc_s\delta_{a_s}$ has coordinates $i\leq j$ arbitrarily close to those of $\alpha$, by uniform continuity of $f_1,\ldots,f_j$ and finiteness of $\alpha(K)$. The tail of the series beyond $j$ is at most $2^{-j}$, which proves density.
\end{proof}

The metric controls limits along corresponding subsequences: if $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ and one of the sequences converges vaguely to a fixed measure along a subsequence, then, by the triangle inequality, so does the other. Without common local mass bounds, however, $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ need not imply that $\int g(\alpha_j-\beta_j)\to0$ for every $g\in C_c(\Omega)$. For example, fix $a\in\Omega$, a unit vector $e$, and numbers $r_j\downarrow0$ with $a+r_je\in\Omega$, and put
\[
 \alpha_j=r_j^{-1/2}\delta_{a+r_je},\qquad
 \beta_j=r_j^{-1/2}\delta_a.
\]
For every smooth compactly supported test, the difference of the two integrals is $O(r_j^{1/2})$, so $d_v(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\to0$ because the tails of the series are small. On the other hand, if $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ equals one near $a$, the continuous test function $g(z)=\chi(z)|z-a|^{1/2}$ gives $\int g(\alpha_j-\beta_j)=1$ for all large $j$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:local-prescribed-excess}\index{Monge--Ampere product@Monge--Amp\`ere product!prescribed excess measure}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq2$, be a bounded domain, let $b$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$, and let $\nu$ be a positive Radon measure on $\Omega$. There are plurisubharmonic functions $u_j\leq b$ on $\Omega$, with $u_j-b$ bounded, such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \|u_j-b\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\longrightarrow0\quad(1\leq p<\infty),\\
 (\ddc u_j)^n\longrightarrow(\ddc b)^n+\nu
                    \quad\text{vaguely}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The same sequence converges to $b$ in local capacity. If $b$ is smooth, every $u_j$ can be chosen smooth.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $(f_i)$ and $d_v$ be as in \cref{lem:local-vague-metric}. For each $j$, the density of finite atomic measures in that lemma gives a finite positive atomic measure
\[
 \nu_j=\sum_{s=1}^{N_j}c_{j,s}\delta_{a_{j,s}},\qquad
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i(\nu_j-\nu)\right|<j^{-1}\quad(i\leq j).
\]
Atoms of weight zero are omitted. If $N_j=0$, set $u_j=b$. Otherwise start with $b_{j,0}=b$ and apply \cref{prop:local-one-atom-addition} successively to $b_{j,s-1}$, $a_{j,s}$ and $c_{j,s}$, with the tests $f_1,\ldots,f_j$ and $\tau=t=(jN_j)^{-1}$. This gives perturbations $h_{j,s}$, and we define
\[
 b_{j,s}=b_{j,s-1}+h_{j,s},\qquad u_j=b_{j,N_j}.
\]
Every intermediate potential is locally bounded and plurisubharmonic, so the proposition applies at each step, and each perturbation serves all finite $p$ at once. The difference $u_j-b=\sum_sh_{j,s}$ is bounded and nonpositive, and summing the norm estimates yields $\|u_j-b\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,p}/j$. Summing the measure errors gives
\[
 \left|\int_\Omega f_i\bigl((\ddc u_j)^n-(\ddc b)^n-\nu\bigr)\right|
       <2/j\quad(i\leq j).
\]
Hence $d_v\bigl((\ddc u_j)^n,(\ddc b)^n+\nu\bigr)\leq2/j+2^{-j}$, and \cref{lem:local-vague-metric} proves the asserted vague limit, also when $\nu(\Omega)=\infty$.

For fixed concentric balls $B'\Subset B\Subset\Omega$, the perturbations are nonpositive, so $|u_j-b|=\sum_s|h_{j,s}|$. The last estimate of \cref{prop:local-one-atom-addition} bounds the integral of this function against $(\ddc w)^n$ over $B'$ by $C_{B',B,\Omega}/j$, for every $w\in\PSH(B)$ with $0\leq w\leq1$. Restricting to the set where $|u_j-b|$ exceeds a fixed threshold, dividing by the threshold and taking the supremum over $w$ proves convergence in local capacity. If $b$ is smooth, the finite sums defining $u_j$ are smooth as well.
\end{proof}

The construction takes place in a bounded Euclidean domain, without prescribed boundary values and without a common bound on the approximating potentials. These freedoms allow concentration even under convergence in local capacity; a fixed global Hermitian background imposes further constraints.
