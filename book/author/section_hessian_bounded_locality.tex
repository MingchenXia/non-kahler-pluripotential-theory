\section{Locality and maxima of bounded potentials}
\label{sec:hessian-bounded-locality}

Where one bounded admissible potential is strictly larger than another,
the maximum has the same positive Hessian current as the larger
potential. These strict comparison sets need not be open.
Quasicontinuity gives cutoffs whose products with the approximating
currents converge, allowing smooth locality to pass to these sets.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{hessian:bounded-strict-locality}\index{Hessian measure!locality}
Let $u,v$ be bounded admissible, put $w=\max\{u,v\}$, and fix
$1\leq p\leq m$. For any bounded admissible $t_2,\ldots,t_p$,
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}Q_p(w,t_2,\ldots,t_p)
       =\mathbf1_{\{u>v\}}Q_p(u,t_2,\ldots,t_p).
\]
The equality is coefficientwise as currents with measure
coefficients. The corresponding equality on $\{v>u\}$ follows
by exchanging $u,v$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose smooth uniformly bounded decreasing approximations of all
the potentials by \cref{hessian:bounded-decreasing-approximation}.
Construct $w_j$ by \cref{hessian:regularized-maximum}. Work on
one compact coordinate test set. Fix $\delta>0$ and a smooth
$\chi_\delta\colon\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ that vanishes for
$x\leq\delta/2$ and equals one for $x\geq\delta$.
For $j$ large enough that $\epsilon_j<\delta/2$, ordinary smooth
locality gives
\[
 \chi_\delta(u_j-v_j)Q_p(w_j,t_2^j,\ldots,t_p^j)
       =\chi_\delta(u_j-v_j)Q_p(u_j,t_2^j,\ldots,t_p^j).
\]
Indeed, wherever the weight is nonzero, $w_j=u_j$ on an open
neighborhood. The equality elsewhere follows from its zero weight.

Both sequences of positive currents converge by
\cref{hessian:joint-decreasing-products}; every coefficient variation
is uniformly dominated by $C c_m^*$ by
\cref{hessian:positive-capacity-domination}. The potentials and their
limits are quasicontinuous by \cref{hessian:rough-coefficient-capacity}.
Apply \cref{hessian:weighted-measure-convergence} with $s=m$ and
$F(x,y)=\chi_\delta(x-y)$ to the real and imaginary parts of
each coefficient. Passing to the limit gives the same identity
with $u,v,w,t_2,\ldots,t_p$.
As $\delta\downarrow0$, $\chi_\delta(x)\to\mathbf1_{\{x>0\}}$
for every real $x$. Dominated convergence against
the fixed finite coefficient variations on the test set gives
the asserted restriction. A partition of unity proves it globally.
\end{proof}

On the contact set, a positive constant shift gives a strict comparison
before the limit is taken. Decreasing the shift then recovers that
set by dominated convergence for a fixed measure.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{hessian:bounded-maximum-measure}\index{Hessian measure!maximum inequality}
Let $u,v$ be bounded admissible. Fix $1\leq r\leq m$ and bounded admissible
$z_1,\ldots,z_{m-r}$. Define the positive Radon measure
\[
 \mu_r(t)=Q_m(\underbrace{t,\ldots,t}_{r},z_1,\ldots,z_{m-r}).
\]
For $w=\max\{u,v\}$,
\[
 \mu_r(w)\geq\mathbf1_{\{u\geq v\}}\mu_r(u)
                  +\mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mu_r(v).
\]
The inequality with $u,v$ exchanged also holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Symmetry and \cref{hessian:bounded-strict-locality}, applied to the
$r$ slots one at a time, give
$\mu_r(w)=\mu_r(v)$ on $\{u<v\}$ and $\mu_r(w)=\mu_r(u)$ on $\{u>v\}$.
To include the contact set, put $w_\epsilon=\max\{u+\epsilon,v\}$.
Strict locality and invariance under addition of constants give
\[
 \mu_r(w_\epsilon)\geq
                   \mathbf1_{\{u+\epsilon>v\}}\mu_r(u).
\]
The invariance follows from the smooth products and their joint
decreasing limits. As $\epsilon\downarrow0$,
$w_\epsilon\downarrow w$, so the left side converges weakly to
$\mu_r(w)$ by \cref{hessian:joint-decreasing-products}. Against any nonnegative continuous compactly supported
test function, the right side converges by dominated convergence
for the fixed measure $\mu_r(u)$ to
$\mathbf1_{\{u\geq v\}}\mu_r(u)$. This proves the measure inequality
on the contact set as well as on $\{u>v\}$. On its disjoint
complement $\{u<v\}$, the equality proved first gives
$\mu_r(w)=\mu_r(v)$. Restricting the positive measures to these two Borel sets
and adding gives the assertion. Exchanging $u,v$ gives its other
form.
\end{proof}

For bounded admissible $v$, write $H_m(v)=Q_m(v,\ldots,v)$ for the
positive Radon measure of \cref{hessian:bounded-shifted-positive}
with all potentials equal to $v$. For continuous $v$ this is the
measure $H_m(v)$ of \cref{sec:hessian-continuous-products}.
Convex rescaling supplies a positive background term in this measure.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{hessian:convex-background-term}\index{Hessian measure!convex rescaling}
For bounded admissible $v$, $0\leq\alpha\leq1$ and a real constant
$b$, the function $v_\alpha=\alpha v+b$ is bounded admissible and
\[
 H_m(v_\alpha)\geq\alpha^m H_m(v)+(1-\alpha)^m\omega^n.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take smooth decreasing uniformly bounded admissible $v_j\downarrow v$
by \cref{hessian:bounded-decreasing-approximation}. Cone convexity makes $\alpha v_j+b$ admissible because
\[
 \omega+\ddc(\alpha v_j+b)
        =\alpha(\omega+\ddc v_j)+(1-\alpha)\omega.
\]
For $\alpha>0$, an upper test $q$ for $\alpha v+b$ gives the
upper test $(q-b)/\alpha$ for $v$. The same convex combination
of forms therefore proves admissibility of the limit. For
$\alpha=0$ the potential is constant. The pointwise decreasing
limit has the displayed canonical affine values.
Expand the smooth top product. Every mixed term is positive by
polarized G\aa rding positivity. Keeping the two extreme terms gives
\[
 H_m(\alpha v_j+b)\geq\alpha^m H_m(v_j)
                                    +(1-\alpha)^m\omega^n.
\]
Joint decreasing convergence from
\cref{hessian:joint-decreasing-products} passes this inequality
against each nonnegative continuous test to the asserted inequality
of positive Radon measures.
\end{proof}

The weighted limits also pass integration by parts against metric
derivatives. A truncation just above the infimum then excludes zero
Hessian mass for a bounded potential.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{hessian:bounded-torsion-weight}\index{Torsion!weighted bounded products}
Put $k=m-1$ and $\ell=n-m$. For bounded admissible
$t_1,\ldots,t_k$, let
$T=R_k(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\wedge\omega^\ell$.
Then $\ddc T$ is a signed Radon measure. If bounded admissible
$t_i^j\downarrow t_i$ have a common bound, these measures converge
weakly, also after multiplication by any continuous function of
finitely many uniformly bounded decreasing admissible potentials.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The subset definition and the torsion formulas in
\cref{hessian:bounded-current-torsion} give, in distributions,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \ddc T={}&\ddc\omega\wedge
 \left(\sum_{a=1}^kR_{k-1}^{\widehat a}\omega^\ell
                       +\ell R_k\omega^{\ell-1}\right)\\
 &+2\,\mathrm d\omega\wedge\dc\omega\wedge
       \sum_{a<b}R_{k-2}^{\widehat a\widehat b}\omega^\ell\\
 &+2\ell\,\mathrm d\omega\wedge\dc\omega\wedge
       \sum_{a=1}^kR_{k-1}^{\widehat a}\omega^{\ell-1}\\
 &+\ell(\ell-1)\,\mathrm d\omega\wedge\dc\omega\wedge
       R_k\omega^{\ell-2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The sum with two hats is absent when $k=1$, and the last term is
absent when $\ell=1$. Every factor $R_r$ has degree $r\leq k<m$,
so its coefficients are signed measures. Expanding each $R_r$
over subsets expresses the displayed measure as fixed smooth forms
wedged with raw currents $D_p$ of degrees $p\leq m-1$.
\Cref{hessian:rough-coefficient-capacity} therefore bounds its
variation locally by $C c_{m-1}^*$, uniformly under common bounds
on the potentials. Joint convergence follows from
\cref{hessian:joint-decreasing-products} applied to this expansion.
All the canonical weights are quasicontinuous for $c_{m-1}^*$, by
\cref{hessian:rough-coefficient-capacity}.
\Cref{hessian:weighted-measure-convergence}, with $s=m-1$,
thus proves the weighted assertion coefficientwise on a finite
coordinate cover. A partition of unity gives it on $X$.
Even the term involving $R_k\ddc(\omega^\ell)$ contains only a
current of degree $m-1$, so the argument requires no coefficient
estimate in degree $m$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{hessian:bounded-nonvanishing}\index{Hessian measure!nonvanishing for bounded potentials}
Every bounded admissible potential $u$ has
\[
 \int_X H_m(u)>0.
\]
No differential identities for powers of $\omega$ are required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose instead that $\int_XH_m(u)=0$. Since $H_m(u)$ is positive,
$H_m(u)=0$. Fix $s>0$, to be chosen below, set
$c=\inf_Xu+s$, and put $w=\max\{u,c\}$.
The upper semicontinuity of $u$ makes
$U=\{u<c\}$ open, and the definition of the infimum makes it
nonempty. Moreover $0\leq w-u\leq s$ and this difference is
zero outside $U$. The maximum is bounded admissible with its
canonical pointwise values by \cref{hessian:regularized-maximum}.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For $0\leq q\leq m$, define the positive Radon measures
\[
 \mu_q=Q_m(\underbrace{u,\ldots,u}_{q},
                    \underbrace{w,\ldots,w}_{m-q}).
\]
Thus $\mu_m=H_m(u)=0$. For $q<m$, let $T_q$ be the sum of
$m-q$ currents with $q+r$ slots equal to $u$ and
$m-q-1-r$ slots equal to $w$, for $0\leq r<m-q$, each of degree
$m-1$ and wedged with $\omega^{n-m}$. In smooth notation this is
\[
 T_q=\sum_{r=0}^{m-q-1}
       (\omega+\ddc u)^{q+r}
       (\omega+\ddc w)^{m-q-1-r}\omega^{n-m}.
\]
Here each displayed product denotes the symmetric shifted current
$R_{m-1}$ of \eqref{eq:hessian-bounded-shifted-current}, wedged with
$\omega^{n-m}$.

Choose uniformly bounded smooth admissible $u_j\downarrow u$ by
\cref{hessian:bounded-decreasing-approximation}, and the
regularized maxima $w_j=M_{\epsilon_j}(u_j,c)\downarrow w$,
where $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$, as in
\cref{hessian:regularized-maximum}.
Both lists have a common bound. Write $\mu_q^j$ and $T_q^j$ for the
smooth forms defined by $u_j,w_j$ in place of $u,w$. The polynomial
identity $\mu_q^j-\mu_m^j=\ddc(w_j-u_j)\wedge T_q^j$ and
Stokes' theorem on the compact manifold give
\[
 \int_X(\mu_q^j-\mu_m^j)
              =\int_X(w_j-u_j)\ddc T_q^j.
\]
Joint decreasing convergence in
\cref{hessian:joint-decreasing-products} passes the left side to
$\int_X(\mu_q-\mu_m)=\int_X\mu_q$.
\Cref{hessian:bounded-torsion-weight}, with $F(x,y)=y-x$ and the
decreasing lists $u_j,w_j$, passes the right side to its limit. Hence
\[
 \int_X\mu_q=\int_X(w-u)\ddc T_q.
\]
The difference of the two decreasing sequences need not itself
decrease; the continuous two-variable weight is essential here.

On the open set $U$, the potential $w$ is constant. Locality of
the raw recursion therefore sets every factor $\ddc w$ to zero
there, so that its shifted factors become $\omega$.
The formula in the preceding lemma expresses $\ddc T_q|_U$ as
finitely many smooth metric-derivative forms wedged with powers
of $\omega+\ddc u$ of degrees at most $m-1$.
Use smooth admissible approximations to $u$ and the constant $c$ in
these local expressions; their limits are the same restricted currents.
For those smooth approximations, the absolute mixed-form
estimate \cref{hessian:absolute-mixed-forms} bounds a term with $r$
factors $\omega+\ddc u_j$ by the positive trace
$C(\omega+\ddc u_j)^r\wedge\omega^{n-r}$. In the joint limit, this
trace becomes $C(\omega+\ddc u)^r\wedge\omega^{n-r}$, which equals
$C\mu_r$ on $U$. Against compactly supported nonnegative tests in $U$, weak
convergence and lower semicontinuity of total variation preserve
the estimate. Exhaustion gives the measure inequality
\[
 \mathbf1_U|\ddc T_q|
                 \leq C\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\mathbf1_U\mu_r.
\]
Here $C$ depends only on $\omega,m,n$, not on $u$ or $s$; a fixed
finite coordinate cover bounds all the smooth metric coefficients.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Since $0\leq w-u\leq s$ and $w-u$ vanishes outside $U$, positivity
of $\mu_q$ and this measure inequality give
\[
 \int_U\mu_q\leq\int_X\mu_q
            \leq Cs\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\int_U\mu_r
                 \qquad(0\leq q<m).
\]
Summing these $m$ inequalities gives
$(1-mCs)\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\int_U\mu_r\leq0$. Since $C$ does not
depend on $s$, we may choose $s>0$ with $mCs<1$, which forces
$\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\int_U\mu_r=0$.
But locality gives $\mu_0=\omega^n$ on the nonempty open set $U$,
whose volume is positive. This contradiction proves the result.
\end{proof}
