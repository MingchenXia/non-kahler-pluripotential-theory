\subsubsection{A regularization defect along a complex disk}
\label{sec:affine-contact-defect}
\index{Contact set!regularization defect}

Raw contact with a constant obstacle can persist at every positive growth parameter while regularization raises the value at a point. A transverse complex disk prevents a local plurisubharmonic barrier from separating that point from the compact set, but does not remove this defect.

On the complex plane let $D$ be the open unit disk and put
\[
 p_j=2+2^{-j},\qquad r_j=\exp(-16\,2^j),\qquad
 E=\overline D\cup\{2\}\cup\bigcup_{j\geq1}\overline D(p_j,r_j).
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:planar-contact-barrier}
The set $E$ is compact and every neighborhood of each of its points contains an open disk. The canonical subharmonic function
\[
 h(\zeta)=\sum_{j\geq1}2^{-j}\log|\zeta-p_j|+2\log2
\]
has logarithmic growth at most one, satisfies $h(2)=0$, and is at most $-16+2\log2$ on every small disk $\overline D(p_j,r_j)$. The function
\[
 v(\zeta)=\frac{8\log^+|\zeta|+h(\zeta)-4\log2}{9}
\]
is subharmonic with the same growth bound, is nonpositive on $E\setminus\{2\}$, and satisfies $v(2)=4\log2/9$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The bound $r_j<2^{-j-3}$ separates the small disks and their centers accumulate only at $2$. It proves compactness; at the accumulating point any neighborhood contains a sufficiently small disk, and elsewhere a disk interior approaches the point.

To identify the series canonically, write $\chi(\zeta)=\log(1+|\zeta|^2)$ on $\C$ and $\theta=\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}/2$ on $\mathbb P^1$. By \cref{lem:affine-projective-growth}, each
\[
 q_j=\log|\zeta-p_j|-\chi/2
\]
extends to a genuine $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function. Cauchy--Schwarz gives $q_j\leq\log4$, while its value at infinity is zero. Thus $0\leq\sup q_j\leq\log4$, and \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} bounds $\|q_j-\log4\|_{L^1}$ uniformly. The sums
\[
 w_N=\sum_{j=1}^N2^{-j}(q_j-\log4)
\]
decrease, remain $\theta$-plurisubharmonic and have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms. Their genuine limit $w$ exists by \cref{cor:decreasing-limit}. Since the partial weights tend to one,
\[
 h=w+\chi/2+\log4+2\log2
\]
on the affine chart. This proves subharmonicity and the growth bound, and $\sum j2^{-j}=2$ gives $h(2)=0$.

On the $j$th small disk, all distances to the other centers are less than one, so
\[
 h\leq2^{-j}\log r_j+2\log2=-16+2\log2.
\]
On $\overline D$ all distances to the centers are less than four, hence $h\leq4\log2$ and $v\leq0$. On each small disk $|\zeta|<4$, so $9v\leq14\log2-16<0$. Its growth bound and value at $2$ follow directly from the formula.
\end{proof}

Now take $n\geq2$ and $K=E\times\overline D^{\,n-1}\subset\C^n$. For a non-pluripolar compact $L$, write $V_{\delta,L}$ and $F_{\delta,L}$ for the raw envelope and its regularization with zero obstacle on $L$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:affine-transverse-contact-defect}
The compact $K$ is non-pluripolar in every neighborhood of every point. For $\delta>0$ and $a=(2,w)$, $w\in\overline D^{\,n-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-contact-defect}
 V_{\delta,K}|_K=0,\qquad
 F_{\delta,K}(a)\geq\frac{4\delta}{9}\log2>0.
\end{equation}
For every $r>0$, the same lower bound holds for $F_{\delta,K\cap\overline B(a,r)}(a)$. At $a_0=(2,0,\ldots,0)$, every local plurisubharmonic function $f$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transverse-psh-no-jump}
 f(a_0)\leq\limsup_{K\setminus\{a_0\}\ni z\to a_0}f(z).
\end{equation}
Thus $K$ is non-plurithin at $a_0$, although the regularized envelope has no contact there at any positive growth parameter.
\end{proposition}
\index{Envelope!non-thin contact defect}
\begin{proof}
Every neighborhood contains an open product of disk pieces, by the preceding lemma and the interiors of the remaining disk factors. This proves local non-pluripolarity, also for each positive-radius compact piece.

Put $b(\zeta)=\log|\zeta-2|-\log3$. Since $|\zeta-2|\leq3$ on $E$, one has $b\leq0$ there and $b(2)=-\infty$. For $\varepsilon>0$ the genuine plurisubharmonic function
\[
 u_\varepsilon(z)=\frac{v(z_1)+\varepsilon b(z_1)}{1+\varepsilon}
\]
belongs to $\mathcal L_1$ and meets the zero obstacle at every point of $K$, including $z_1=2$. Thus $u_\varepsilon\leq F_{1,K}$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives $v(z_1)\leq F_{1,K}$ off that hyperplane. \Cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} extends this inequality everywhere. The value $v(2)$ gives the lower bound. Constants give $V_{\delta,K}|_K=0$, and scaling of the competitor family gives $F_{\delta,K}=\delta F_{1,K}$. Passing to a smaller compact only increases its envelope, proving the local assertion.

Finally, if $f(a_0)$ is finite, restrict $f$ to the complex line varying the second coordinate with the others fixed. A small disk in this line lies in $K$ and has center $a_0$. A strict jump at its center contradicts the subharmonic circular mean inequality. If $f(a_0)=-\infty$, \eqref{eq:transverse-psh-no-jump} is automatic.
\end{proof}
