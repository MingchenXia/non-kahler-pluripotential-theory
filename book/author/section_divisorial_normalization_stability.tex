\section{Divisorial types and moving sections}\label{sec:divisorial-normalization-stability}

Removing an actual divisorial part identifies an ordinary singularity class with bounded potentials in its residual background. The scalar therefore sees the residual form. Convergence of full potentials also needs control of the sections defining the divisors.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Fix finitely many holomorphic line bundles $L_r$ on $X$ with smooth metrics $h_r$, nonzero sections $s_r$, and weights $t_r>0$. Put
\[
 \varphi=\sum_r t_r\log|s_r|_{h_r}^2,\qquad
 \Gamma=\sum_r t_r\operatorname{div}(s_r),\qquad
 \beta=\theta-\sum_r t_rc_1(L_r,h_r),
\]
and assume $\beta>0$. The empty family is allowed, with $\varphi=0$ and $\beta=\theta$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ordinary-divisor-normalization}
There is an exact correspondence
\[
 \{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\sim\varphi\}
 =\{\varphi+w:w\in\PSH(X,\beta)\cap L^\infty(X)\},
\]
and for each corresponding pair,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary-divisor-measure}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\varphi+w)=(\beta+\ddc w)^n.
\end{equation}
For every bounded measurable $F$, the equation in this ordinary type has a unique scalar and a unique solution modulo constants. Its scalar functional satisfies
\[
 B_{\theta,[\varphi]}(F)=B_\beta(F).
\]
Its dual and supporting sets consequently coincide with $H_\beta$ and $\mathcal A_\beta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Poincar\'e--Lelong gives $\theta+\ddc\varphi=[\Gamma]+\beta$, so every potential on the right is admissible and has type $\varphi$. Conversely, an ordinary-type competitor $u$ has exactly the divisorial coefficients of $\Gamma$. By \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal},
\[
 \theta+\ddc u-[\Gamma]\geq0.
\]
The locally integrable difference $w=u-\varphi$ therefore has a $\beta$-plurisubharmonic representative. It is bounded off the divisor by ordinary equivalence; the local submean property preserves both bounds across the divisor. Equality almost everywhere gives $u=\varphi+w$ for the quasi-plurisubharmonic representatives.

Off the divisor the two currents agree. Locality identifies their products there. The extended measure on the left gives the divisor zero mass, and the bounded product on the right is finite and gives analytic sets zero mass by \cref{sec:psh-bounded-products}. This proves \eqref{eq:ordinary-divisor-measure}. The bounded equation in background $\beta$ has a continuous solution and a unique scalar by \cref{thm:kn-continuous,prop:vanishing-exponent}. Its density is bounded above and below by positive constants, so \cref{cor:bounded-positive-density-uniqueness} gives uniqueness modulo constants. The correspondence transfers these assertions to the entire ordinary type and identifies the functionals.
\end{proof}

In particular the background comparison and adjoint stability of \cref{thm:geometric-normalization-comparison,cor:geometric-adjoint-stability} apply whenever the positive residuals satisfy the relative form inequalities. The divisor locations do not enter those estimates. This assertion concerns ordinary types; a model-envelope interpretation additionally requires bounded equivalence of the envelope and the displayed reference.

For a fixed positive residual $\beta$, uniformly bounded $F_j\to F$ in $L^1$ give uniform convergence of the normalized residual solutions. Indeed, scalar continuity gives uniformly bounded equation densities converging in every finite $L^p$. The compactness theorem \cref{thm:lp-uniform-compactness} gives uniform subsequential convergence, bounded-product continuity identifies the limiting equation, and \cref{cor:bounded-positive-density-uniqueness} identifies every limit. Subtracting the means gives the same conclusion for mean-zero normalization.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:moving-divisorial-potential-stability}\index{Divisor!moving sections}
Keep the line bundles and metrics fixed. Let nonzero sections $s_{jr}$ converge to $s_r\ne0$ in fixed finite-dimensional spaces of global sections, and let the positive weights satisfy $t_{jr}\to t_r>0$. Define $\varphi_j,\theta_j$ from these data and positive residuals $\beta_j$ by the preceding formulas. Suppose either that $\beta_j\to\beta>0$ and $F_j\to F$ in $C^\infty$, or that $\beta_j=\beta>0$ and the tests converge in $L^1$ with a common $L^\infty$ bound. Then $\varphi_j\to\varphi$ in $L^1$, and the ordinary-type solutions normalized by $\sup_Xu_j=0$ converge in $L^1$ to the similarly normalized limiting solution. For mean-zero residual solutions $v_j$, one also has
\[
 \sup_X(\varphi_j+v_j)\longrightarrow\sup_X(\varphi+v).
\]
The residuals converge smoothly in the first regime and uniformly in the second.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The residual convergence follows from \cref{prop:smooth-background-potential-stability} and the preceding compactness argument. The fixed metrics and bounded weights give a common smooth lower bound for $\ddc\varphi_j$. The convergent sections give a common upper bound for $\varphi_j$ and convergence at a point outside all limiting divisors, so their suprema are bounded above and below. By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in $L^1$. Off the limiting divisors, convergence of the sections and weights gives local uniform convergence to $\varphi$. Since those divisors have zero volume, every $L^1$ cluster equals $\varphi$. Thus $\varphi_j\to\varphi$ in $L^1(X)$.

Put $C_j=\sup_X(\varphi_j+v_j)$ and $C=\sup_X(\varphi+v)$. The sums converge in $L^1$ and have a common smooth lower Hessian bound, since $\ddc v_j\geq-\beta_j$ and the residual backgrounds are uniformly bounded. Applying \cref{lem:qpsh-hartogs} at maximizing points gives $\limsup C_j\leq C$. A maximizing point of $\varphi+v$ lies off its poles. Evaluation there, using local uniform convergence, gives $\liminf C_j\geq C$.

It follows that $u_j=\varphi_j+v_j-C_j\to\varphi+v-C$ in $L^1$. For an empty divisor family the same assertion is just residual convergence and convergence of its supremum.
\end{proof}
