\section{Selection by a vanishing exponential parameter}
\label{sec:vanishing-parameter-selection}

The zero-exponent equation can have several normalized solutions. The positive-exponent family nevertheless selects one: its scalar functional supplies a family of integral normalizations, and the selected potential is the greatest critical subsolution satisfying all of them. To prove greatestness, we use supporting probabilities for a second scalar functional, obtained by varying the forcing in the subsolution inequality.

In this section $\theta$ is an arbitrary smooth big real $(1,1)$-form. Fix a strict analytic barrier $\rho\leq0$ as in \cref{sec:big-minimal-comparison}, and put $\Omega=X\setminus\{\rho=-\infty\}$. Fix a nonzero $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$, with $1<p<\infty$. For a nonzero nonnegative density $g$ in some $L^q$, $q>1$, let $c(g)>0$ be the scalar in \cref{thm:big-minimal-normalized}. Its solutions have ordinary type $V_\theta$ and are continuous on $\Omega$ by \cref{thm:big-minimal-holder}. Write $\mathrm{MA}_\theta$ for the extended measure. On $\Omega$ this is the locally bounded product for every potential of minimal type, and its extension gives zero mass to the analytic complement.

Put $c=c(f)$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-probabilities}
 \begin{aligned}
 \kappa(r)&=\log c-\log c(\mathrm{e}^r f),\\
 \mathcal P_f&=\left\{\nu\in\mathcal P(X):
       \int_X r\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq\kappa(r)
       \text{ for every }r\in C(X)\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
When $\theta$ is semipositive and $f=\mathrm{e}^F$ with $F\in C(X)$, this is the supporting set $\mathcal A_0(F)$. The definition above also allows $f$ to vanish and $\theta$ to be nonsemipositive.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:vanishing-exponential-selection}
Let $u_\lambda$ be the solution of minimal type of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_\lambda)
       =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad \lambda>0.
\]
The set $\mathcal P_f$ is nonempty and weakly compact, and every potential of minimal type is integrable against all its measures. As $\lambda\downarrow0$,
\[
 w_\lambda=u_\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\log c
\]
converges locally uniformly on $\Omega$ and in $L^1(X)$ to a potential $w$ of minimal type, continuous on $\Omega$. It is the greatest potential $s$ of minimal type, continuous on $\Omega$, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-class}
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(s)\geq cf\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad
 \int_Xs\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq0\quad(\nu\in\mathcal P_f).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(w)=cf\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad
 \max_{\nu\in\mathcal P_f}\int_Xw\,\mathrm{d}\nu=0.
\]
Thus $u_\lambda-\sup_Xu_\lambda$ converges to $w-\sup_Xw$ in the same two topologies. For a Hermitian $\theta$, one may take $\Omega=X$, and both potential convergences are uniform.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{A norm defined by the critical scalar}

The root of the density is the variable in which the mixed product inequality becomes a triangle inequality. This yields a continuous lattice norm even though its definition supplies no initial quantitative estimate.

Fix $1<q<p$ and set $\ell=nq$. On real $L^\ell(\mathrm{d}V)$ define
\[
 N(a)=
 \begin{cases}
 c(|a|^n)^{-1/n},&a\ne0,\\
 0,&a=0.
 \end{cases}
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:critical-scalar-norm}
The function $N$ is a lattice norm. There is $C_q<\infty$, depending only on $X,\theta,\mathrm{d}V,q$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-scalar-norm-bound}
 N(a)\leq C_q\|a\|_\ell,\qquad
 |N(a)-N(b)|\leq C_q\|a-b\|_\ell.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for nonzero $g,h\geq0$ in $L^q$,
\[
 |c(g)^{-1/n}-c(h)^{-1/n}|
       \leq C_q\|g-h\|_q^{1/n}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If a potential $v$ of minimal type satisfies $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)\geq d g\,\mathrm{d}V$, then $d\leq c(g)$. Otherwise comparison with a critical solution for $g$ contradicts \cref{cor:big-barrier-orders}. Hence $g\leq h$ implies $c(g)\geq c(h)$. Scalar uniqueness also gives $c(tg)=c(g)/t$ for $t>0$. These facts prove lattice monotonicity and absolute homogeneity of $N$; positivity follows from $0<c(g)<\infty$.

For nonzero $a,b\geq0$, put $A=N(a)$ and $B=N(b)$. Choose critical potentials $v_a,v_b$ with measures $(a/A)^n\mathrm{d}V$ and $(b/B)^n\mathrm{d}V$. On every relatively compact subset of $\Omega$, \eqref{eq:interior-minkowski-background} gives
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta\left(\frac{A v_a+B v_b}{A+B}\right)
       \geq\left(\frac{a+b}{A+B}\right)^n\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Both potentials are locally bounded there. Exhaustion of $\Omega$ gives the global inequality, since its complement has zero smooth volume. The averaged potential has minimal type, so scalar order gives $N(a+b)\leq A+B$. The zero cases follow from homogeneity. For real functions, use $|a+b|\leq|a|+|b|$.

If the first bound in \eqref{eq:critical-scalar-norm-bound} failed, homogeneity would give $a_j\geq0$ with $\|a_j\|_\ell\leq2^{-j}$ and $N(a_j)>j$. Their sum $a=\sum_j a_j$ belongs to $L^\ell$ by the triangle inequality in $L^\ell$ and monotone convergence. But $a\geq a_j$ would imply $N(a)>j$ for every $j$, contradicting finiteness of $N(a)$. The reverse triangle inequality gives the second bound. Apply it to $g^{1/n},h^{1/n}$ and use $|g^{1/n}-h^{1/n}|^n\leq|g-h|$ to obtain the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Norming probabilities and integral bounds}

Set $a=f^{1/n}$ and $A=N(a)=c^{-1/n}$. The probabilities in \eqref{eq:selection-probabilities} come from positive functionals norming $N$ at $a$. Their representation will control integrals of unbounded potentials as well as bounded logarithmic perturbations.

With $\ell'=\ell/(\ell-1)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-norming-face}
 \mathcal H_a=\left\{\eta\in L^{\ell'}(\mathrm{d}V):
 \eta\geq0,\ \int_Xa\eta\,\mathrm{d}V=A,\
 \int_Xb\eta\,\mathrm{d}V\leq N(b)\text{ for every }b\in L^\ell\right\}.
\end{equation}

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:selection-norming-probabilities}
The set $\mathcal H_a$ is nonempty and weakly compact in $L^{\ell'}$. The function
\[
 \kappa(r)=n\log\frac{N(a\mathrm{e}^{r/n})}{A}
\]
is convex, increasing and $1$-Lipschitz on $C(X)$, with $\kappa(0)=0$ and $\kappa(r+b)=\kappa(r)+b$ for constants $b$. Its subdifferential at zero is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-face-representation}
 \mathcal P_f=\{\nu_\eta:\eta\in\mathcal H_a\},\qquad
 \mathrm{d}\nu_\eta=A^{-1}a\eta\,\mathrm{d}V.
\end{equation}
If $z$ is measurable and $az\in L^\ell$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-weighted-moment}
 \sup_{\nu\in\mathcal P_f}\int_X|z|\,\mathrm{d}\nu
       \leq C_q A^{-1}\|az\|_\ell.
\end{equation}
If also $a\mathrm{e}^{tz/n}\in L^\ell$ and $N(a\mathrm{e}^{tz/n})=A$ for some $t>0$, then $\int_Xz\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq0$ for every $\nu\in\mathcal P_f$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Hahn--Banach gives a linear functional norming $N$ at $a$ and dominated by $N$. By \eqref{eq:critical-scalar-norm-bound} it is continuous in $L^\ell$, and hence is represented by $\eta_0\in L^{\ell'}$. For $b\geq0$, testing on $\operatorname{sgn}(\eta_0)b$ gives
\[
 \int_Xb|\eta_0|\,\mathrm{d}V\leq N(b).
\]
Since $a\geq0$ and $\int_Xa\eta_0\,\mathrm{d}V=A=N(a)$, equality holds also with $|\eta_0|$. Positivity extends domination to real $b$, so $|\eta_0|\in\mathcal H_a$. The defining conditions are weakly closed and give $\|\eta\|_{\ell'}\leq C_q$. Reflexivity proves weak compactness.

For nonzero nonnegative $b_0,b_1$, apply weighted arithmetic--geometric mean to $b_j/N(b_j)$, then use monotonicity and the triangle inequality:
\[
 N(b_0^{1-t}b_1^t)\leq N(b_0)^{1-t}N(b_1)^t,\qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\]
Taking $b_j=a\mathrm{e}^{r_j/n}$ proves convexity of $\kappa$. Order and the constant translation rule follow from those of $N$, and together imply its Lipschitz bound.

Every $\nu_\eta$ is a probability and satisfies
\[
 \frac{N(a\mathrm{e}^{r/n})}{A}
       \geq\int_X\mathrm{e}^{r/n}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_\eta.
\]
Jensen's inequality gives $\kappa(r)\geq\int_Xr\,\mathrm{d}\nu_\eta$, proving one inclusion in \eqref{eq:selection-face-representation}. The map $\eta\mapsto\nu_\eta$ is weakly continuous, since $ar\in L^\ell$ for $r\in C(X)$.

Conversely, let $\nu\in\mathcal P_f$ and put $L(r)=\int_Xr\,\mathrm{d}\nu$. Differentiating the exponential curve at zero in $L^\ell$ gives
\[
 N(a\mathrm{e}^{tr/n})
       =N(a)+\frac{t}{n}N'_+(a;ar)+o(t),\qquad t\downarrow0.
\]
Here the one-sided derivative of the continuous convex norm exists, and the error follows from its Lipschitz bound. The supporting inequality, used with $r$ and $-r$, therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-face-extension}
 |A L(r)|\leq C_q\|ar\|_\ell,\qquad
 A L(r)\leq N'_+(a;ar)\leq N(a+ar)-A.
\end{equation}
Thus $T(ar)=A L(r)$ is well defined and continuous on these products. They are dense in
\[
 S_a=\{b\in L^\ell:b=0\text{ almost everywhere on }\{a=0\}\}:
\]
approximate $b/a$ in $L^\ell(a^\ell\mathrm{d}V)$ by continuous functions. If $ar\geq0$, then $ar=ar_+$ almost everywhere. The first inequality in \eqref{eq:selection-face-extension} gives $L(r-r_+)=0$, so $T(ar)\geq0$. Positive continuous approximations in the weighted space show that the extension of $T$ to $S_a$ is positive.

Represent this extension by $\eta\geq0$ in $L^{\ell'}$, chosen zero on $\{a=0\}$. Since $T(a)=A$, extending the second inequality in \eqref{eq:selection-face-extension} and replacing its argument $b$ by $b-a$ gives $T(b)\leq N(b)$ on $S_a$. For general $b\in L^\ell$,
\[
 \int_Xb\eta\,\mathrm{d}V
 \leq N(b\mathbf1_{\{a>0\}})\leq N(b).
\]
Thus $\eta\in\mathcal H_a$, and its probability agrees with $\nu$ on all continuous tests. This proves the representation and weak compactness of $\mathcal P_f$.

The bound \eqref{eq:selection-weighted-moment} is H\"older's inequality. Under the last hypothesis, the norming inequality gives $\int_X\mathrm{e}^{tz/n}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq1$. Since $z$ is integrable by that bound, Jensen's inequality implies $\int_Xz\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq0$.
\end{proof}

These estimates apply uniformly to supremum-normalized $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potentials $v$. Indeed \cref{lem:uniform-exponential} gives uniform finite moments, after enlarging $\theta$ to a Hermitian background, and H\"older's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-psh-moments}
 \int_X |v|^{nq} f^q\,\mathrm{d}V
 \leq \|f\|_p^q
       \left(\int_X|v|^{nqp/(p-q)}\,\mathrm{d}V\right)^{(p-q)/p}.
\end{equation}
Consequently \eqref{eq:selection-weighted-moment} gives a uniform bound for $\int_X|v|\,\mathrm{d}\nu$ over $\nu\in\mathcal P_f$. Constant translates are integrable as well. The representation also shows that these probabilities charge neither $\{f=0\}$ nor $X\setminus\Omega$.

\subsection{Bounds for the centered potentials}

Choose a critical potential $\psi\leq0$, with $\sup_X\psi=0$ and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(\psi)=cf\,\mathrm{d}V$. The integral normalizations prevent the centered family from drifting upward by constants.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:selection-centered-bounds}
There is $K<\infty$, independent of $0<\lambda\leq1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-centered-bounds}
 \psi\leq w_\lambda,\qquad
 0\leq k_\lambda:=\sup_Xw_\lambda\leq K,\qquad
 \psi-K\leq v_\lambda:=w_\lambda-k_\lambda\leq V_\theta.
\end{equation}
Moreover $\int_Xw_\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq0$ for every $\nu\in\mathcal P_f$. The family $w_\lambda$ is relatively compact for locally uniform convergence on $\Omega$; every such cluster also converges in $L^1(X)$ to a potential of minimal type.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\psi\leq0$, its measure dominates $c\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\psi}f\,\mathrm{d}V$. Exponential order in \cref{cor:big-barrier-orders} gives $\psi\leq w_\lambda$, hence $k_\lambda\geq0$. For each fixed $\lambda$, the density $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda w_\lambda}f$ lies in $L^p$, and its critical scalar is $c$. Therefore
\[
 N(a\mathrm{e}^{\lambda w_\lambda/n})=A.
\]
The moment estimate above makes $aw_\lambda\in L^\ell$, so \cref{prop:selection-norming-probabilities} gives all the asserted integral inequalities. Fix one $\nu\in\mathcal P_f$. Then
\[
 k_\lambda\leq-\int_Xv_\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\nu
       \leq C_q A^{-1}\|a v_\lambda\|_\ell\leq K
\]
by \eqref{eq:selection-psh-moments}. Normalization gives $v_\lambda\leq V_\theta$, and subtracting $k_\lambda$ from the lower bound proves \eqref{eq:selection-centered-bounds}.

Since $\psi\sim V_\theta$, the normalized potentials lie in a fixed reference interval. Their actual equation densities
\[
 G_\lambda=c\mathrm{e}^{\lambda w_\lambda}f
       \quad\text{satisfy}\quad
 \|G_\lambda\|_p\leq c\mathrm{e}^K\|f\|_p.
\]
Thus \cref{thm:big-minimal-holder} gives one H\"older modulus on every fixed compact subset of $\Omega$. Its constants depend only on the fixed reference interval, this density bound, the geometry, barrier and compact subset. Adding $k_\lambda\in[0,K]$ preserves equicontinuity. Diagonal compactness gives locally uniform subsequences. The bound $|w_\lambda|\leq|\psi|+K$ and the zero volume of $X\setminus\Omega$ give global $L^1$ convergence along them. Positivity passes in distributions, and the same bounds preserve minimal type. The limiting representative agrees with the local uniform limit on $\Omega$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
In particular,
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_\lambda}
       =c\mathrm{e}^{\lambda k_\lambda}\longrightarrow c.
\]
The uniform density bound above includes this scalar. An upper $L^p$ bound on arbitrarily rescaled data alone would not bound the normalized potentials: on $\mathbb P^1$, with $\theta=\ddc\log(1+|z|^2)$,
\[
 \psi_\epsilon=\log\frac{|z|^2+\epsilon^2}{1+|z|^2}
\]
is smooth and $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, with supremum zero and infimum $\log\epsilon^2$. If $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(\psi_\epsilon)=g_\epsilon\,\mathrm{d}V$, the data $g_\epsilon/\|g_\epsilon\|_p$ have norm one but scalar $\|g_\epsilon\|_p$ and the same potentials. This rescaling does not bound the actual equation densities $g_\epsilon$.

\subsection{Scalar envelopes of subsolutions}

To compare a cluster potential with every member of \eqref{eq:selection-class}, fix a point $x\in\Omega$ and vary a continuous forcing. Let $\mathcal S$ be the convex class of potentials of minimal type that are continuous on $\Omega$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-scalar-envelope}
 \mathcal F_{\lambda,x}(r)=
 \sup\{s(x):s\in\mathcal S,\
       \mathrm{MA}_\theta(s)\geq
              c\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s+r}f\,\mathrm{d}V\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:selection-forcing-probability}
The functional $\mathcal F_{\lambda,x}$ is finite, concave, decreasing and $\lambda^{-1}$-Lipschitz on $C(X)$. It satisfies
\[
 \mathcal F_{\lambda,x}(0)=w_\lambda(x),\qquad
 \mathcal F_{\lambda,x}(r+b)
       =\mathcal F_{\lambda,x}(r)-b/\lambda.
\]
There is a probability $\nu_{\lambda,x}$ such that, for every $s$ admissible in \eqref{eq:selection-scalar-envelope},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-forcing-support}
 \lambda s(x)+\int_Xr\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\lambda,x}
       \leq\lambda w_\lambda(x).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Exponential order makes $w_\lambda$ the greatest admissible potential for $r=0$. If $m=\min_Xr$ and $M=\max_Xr$, then $w_\lambda-M/\lambda$ is admissible for $r$, and translating any admissible $s$ by $m/\lambda$ makes it admissible for zero forcing. Hence
\[
 w_\lambda(x)-M/\lambda\leq\mathcal F_{\lambda,x}(r)
       \leq w_\lambda(x)-m/\lambda.
\]
The local mixed inequality on $\Omega$ and arithmetic--geometric mean show that a convex combination of potentials admissible for $r_0,r_1$ is admissible for the same combination of the forcings. Approximating the two suprema at $x$ proves concavity. Inclusion of the admissible classes proves order; constant translation proves the displayed covariance and the Lipschitz bound.

Apply the Hahn--Banach supporting-functional construction in the proof of \cref{thm:weak-adjoint-probabilities} to $-\lambda\mathcal F_{\lambda,x}$ at zero. This is a finite continuous convex functional, increasing and with constant translation slope one. The same order and constant tests make its subgradient a probability. The supporting inequality and the definition of the supremum give \eqref{eq:selection-forcing-support}. Attainment of the supremum is unnecessary.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[\cref{thm:vanishing-exponential-selection}]
The representation and moment bound prove the assertions about $\mathcal P_f$. The centered bounds give precompactness. We identify every cluster potential.

Fix $x\in\Omega$. For $r\in C(X)$, choose a critical potential $\psi_r$ with supremum zero for the density $\mathrm{e}^r f$. Then
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(\psi_r)
       =c\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa(r)+r}f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Since $\psi_r\leq0$, the potential $\psi_r-\kappa(r)/\lambda$ is admissible in \eqref{eq:selection-scalar-envelope}. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-forcing-limit}
 \int_Xr\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\lambda,x}
       \leq\kappa(r)+\lambda\bigl(w_\lambda(x)-\psi_r(x)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For fixed $x,r$, the values $w_\lambda(x)$ are uniformly bounded and $\psi_r(x)$ is finite. Every weak cluster of $\nu_{\lambda,x}$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$ therefore belongs to $\mathcal P_f$.

Let $s$ satisfy \eqref{eq:selection-class}. The function $-\lambda s$ is lower semicontinuous and bounded below on $X$, with the value $+\infty$ allowed. Choose finite continuous $r_j\uparrow-\lambda s$, by metric inf-convolution and truncation. Since $r_j\leq-\lambda s$, the potential $s$ is admissible for every $r_j$. Inequality \eqref{eq:selection-forcing-support} bounds their integrals from above. Monotone convergence after subtracting a common lower bound proves that $s$ is integrable against $\nu_{\lambda,x}$ and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection-pointwise-comparison}
 w_\lambda(x)\geq s(x)-\int_Xs\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\lambda,x}.
\end{equation}

Take a sequence $\lambda_j\downarrow0$ with $w_{\lambda_j}\to z$ locally uniformly on $\Omega$ and in $L^1(X)$. The relative bounds preserve minimal type and local continuity. Local bounded-product continuity identifies the limit equation, since $c\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j w_{\lambda_j}}f\to cf$ in $L^p$ by dominated convergence and the common bound $w_\lambda\leq K$. Thus $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(z)=cf\,\mathrm{d}V$ on $\Omega$, and then on $X$ because both sides ignore its analytic complement.

For each $\nu\in\mathcal P_f$, the bound $|w_\lambda|\leq|\psi|+K$ has a $\nu$-integrable right side. These measures charge only $\Omega$. Dominated convergence in $\int_Xw_\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq0$ gives $\int_Xz\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq0$. Hence $z$ belongs to the selection class.

For any other $s$ in that class and fixed $x\in\Omega$, extract a further subsequence with $\nu_{\lambda_j,x}\rightharpoonup\nu\in\mathcal P_f$. The potential $s$ is upper semicontinuous and bounded above on $X$, so
\[
 \limsup_j\int_Xs\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\lambda_j,x}
       \leq\int_Xs\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq0.
\]
For an unbounded $s$, this is Portmanteau applied first to $\max(s,-M)$, followed by $M\to\infty$; the final integral is finite by \eqref{eq:selection-weighted-moment}. Passing to the limit in \eqref{eq:selection-pointwise-comparison} gives $z(x)\geq s(x)$. This holds for every $x\in\Omega$ and extends to all of $X$ by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. Every cluster is therefore the same greatest element $w$. Precompactness proves convergence of the whole family.

The maximum of $\int_Xw\,\mathrm{d}\nu$ over $\mathcal P_f$ is attained: under \eqref{eq:selection-face-representation} this is the weakly continuous functional $\eta\mapsto A^{-1}\int_Xaw\eta\,\mathrm{d}V$ on $\mathcal H_a$, since $aw\in L^\ell$. If the maximum were negative, a small positive constant translate of $w$ would still satisfy \eqref{eq:selection-class}, contrary to greatestness. Thus it is zero.

Finally $k_\lambda\to\sup_Xw$. Indeed, from any subsequence choose a further one with $k_\lambda\to k$. Then $v_\lambda=w_\lambda-k_\lambda$ converges in $L^1$ to $w-k$; \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives $\sup_X(w-k)=0$. Thus $k=\sup_Xw$ for every scalar cluster. Subtracting the constants proves the claimed normalized convergence. If $\theta$ is Hermitian, take $\rho=0$ and $\Omega=X$, so local uniform convergence is uniform.
\end{proof}

Together with \cref{prop:big-exponential-clusters}, the theorem also gives convergence of the normalized potentials uniformly in differences on $\Omega$ and in every local $C^\beta$ topology stated there: compactness in those topologies and uniqueness of the $L^1$ limit force every subsequence to have the same limit. Selection of this limit does not imply uniqueness of all normalized solutions of the critical equation.
