\subsection{Models over a curve}
\label{subsec:curve-fibration-polar-models}

The polar metric on a curve also describes models on a compact
manifold that maps onto it with connected fibers, including
critical fibers. A background pulled back from the curve has no
vertical curvature, so every potential descends. Gauduchon
volumes of the fiber divisors are constant, with their
multiplicities included. The normalized polar trace therefore
recovers the curve metric without choosing a product structure.

Let $p:X\to C$ be a surjective holomorphic map with connected
fibers, where $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of
dimension $n\geq2$ and $C$ is a connected compact Riemann surface.
Fix a smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\beta$ on $C$, put
$A=\int_C\beta$ and $\theta=p^*\beta$. For
$v\in\PSH(C,\beta)$ write
\[
 T_v=\beta+\ddc v,\qquad
 \mu_v=\mathbf1_{\{v=-\infty\}}T_v.
\]
Thus $\mu_v$ is the polar curvature of the curve potential.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback}\index{Envelope!curve map}
For $v\in\PSH(C,\beta)$ and $U=v\circ p$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_\theta[U]&=P_\beta[v]\circ p,\\
 P_\theta[U]_{\mathcal I}&=P_\beta[v]_{\mathcal I}\circ p.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Pullback is a bijection between the ordinary model spaces and
between their ideal subspaces. Every $U\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has zero
extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure. In particular all ordinary types
in this background have zero upper and lower singularity volumes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{curve-map:potential-descent,curve-map:ideal-types},
ordinary and ideal competitor families correspond under pullback.
The map $p$ is open: on a line through any point its nonconstant
holomorphic germ is open. Hence upper regularization commutes
with pullback, even at a critical point. These observations
prove both envelope identities and the model assertions.

For the mass assertion, $\max(U,-j)=\max(v,-j)\circ p$. Locally
choose a smooth $a$ with $\ddc a=\beta$. The bounded subharmonic
function $a+\max(v,-j)$ has smooth decreasing local approximants.
Their pullbacks have complex Hessian rank at most one, including
at critical points, so their $n$th powers vanish. Bounded product
continuity proves that every bounded truncation has zero top
product. The non-pluripolar measure is therefore zero. Descent
applies to every representative of every ordinary type, proving
the volume assertion.
\end{proof}

Fix a Gauduchon form $\gamma$ on $X$ and a smooth positive
area-one form $\eta$ on $C$. For a finite positive measure
$\mu$ of mass $M$, let $q_\mu$ be the solution normalized by
$\int_Cq_\mu\,\eta=0$ to
\[
 \ddc q_\mu=\mu-M\eta.
\]
The curve Green kernel of \cref{sec:curve-trace-realization}
gives this quasi-subharmonic solution. Define the positive current
\[
 p^*\mu=M p^*\eta+\ddc(q_\mu\circ p),\qquad
 L_\gamma(\mu)=p^*\mu\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\]
Locally this is the pullback of a subharmonic potential of $\mu$;
changing that potential adds a harmonic function and does not
change the current. Extend both operators to signed measures by
linearity. Write $F_s=p^{-1}(s)$ for the fiber divisor with its
scheme multiplicities.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{fibration:gauduchon-fiber-volume}\index{Gauduchon metric!fiber divisor volume}
There is a positive constant $\kappa_\gamma$ such that, for every
finite signed measure $\mu$ and every Borel set $B\subset C$,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 p_*L_\gamma(\mu)=\kappa_\gamma\mu,\qquad
 \|L_\gamma(\mu)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
               =\kappa_\gamma\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}},\\
 L_\gamma(\mathbf1_B\mu)
            =\mathbf1_{p^{-1}(B)}L_\gamma(\mu),\qquad
 \int_{F_s}\gamma^{n-1}=\kappa_\gamma\quad(s\in C).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The last integral includes the multiplicities of the fiber.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The positive $(0,0)$-current $S=p_*(\gamma^{n-1})$ on $C$ satisfies
$\ddc S=p_*\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0$. A harmonic distribution is smooth,
so $S$ is a constant $\kappa_\gamma$. On the complement of the
critical values, smooth fiber integration identifies it with
the positive integral of $\gamma^{n-1}$ on a regular fiber.
Thus $\kappa_\gamma>0$.

First let $\mu$ be positive with mass $M>0$. Choose smooth
positive measures $\mu_j$ with the same mass and
$\mu_j\rightharpoonup\mu$. Their mean-zero solutions $q_j$ have
a common lower curvature bound $-M\eta$. Normalized compactness
bounds $\sup_Cq_j$: their mean-zero condition makes this
supremum the negative mean of their supremum-zero normalization.
Hence $(q_j)$ is precompact in $L^1(C)$. Every limit has mean
zero and Hessian $\mu-M\eta$. Two such limits differ by a
harmonic function, hence by a constant, which their means force
to be zero. Therefore $q_j\to q_\mu$ in $L^1(C)$.

By \cref{curve-map:lone-pullback}, $q_j\circ p\to q_\mu\circ p$
in $L^1(X)$. Thus $p^*\mu_j\to p^*\mu$ as currents and their
smooth Hermitian traces converge weakly. For smooth $\mu_j$,
the definition of $S$ gives
$p_*L_\gamma(\mu_j)=\kappa_\gamma\mu_j$. Passing to the limit
proves the identity for $\mu$. The case $M=0$ is zero, and
linearity proves the signed identity.

For a positive $\mu$, split it into $\mathbf1_B\mu$ and
$\mathbf1_{C\setminus B}\mu$. Their traces are positive; the
pushforward identity shows that they are respectively carried
by $p^{-1}(B)$ and its complement. Additivity then gives the
restriction identity. Apply the same argument to the positive
and negative Jordan parts of a signed measure. Their disjoint
Borel carriers pull back to disjoint carriers, so their traces
are the Jordan parts upstairs. The pushforward identity gives
the asserted total-variation norm.

Finally the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula in the book normalization
gives $p^*\delta_s=[F_s]$, including every zero multiplicity of
a local equation of the fiber. Its trace has total mass
$\kappa_\gamma$, by the pushforward identity with $\mu=\delta_s$.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal M(\theta)$ be the ordinary model space. For
$U=v\circ p\in\mathcal M(\theta)$, its polar trace measure is
\[
 \lambda_U^\gamma
 =\mathbf1_{\{U=-\infty\}}
       (\theta+\ddc U)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 =L_\gamma(\mu_v).
\]
The last identity follows from the Borel restriction formula of
\cref{fibration:gauduchon-fiber-volume}, including at critical
fibers and for a nonatomic polar measure. Define
\[
 \rho_\theta(U,V)=\kappa_\gamma^{-1}
                  \|\lambda_U^\gamma-\lambda_V^\gamma\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{fibration:polar-model-metric}\index{Metric completeness!curve map}
The metric $\rho_\theta$ is independent of the Gauduchon form
$\gamma$. Pullback is an isometry from the complete geodesic
curve model space onto $(\mathcal M(\theta),\rho_\theta)$, which
has diameter $2A$. The ideal models form a closed geodesically
convex subspace equal to the metric closure of the models with
analytic singularities. Ideal projection is its unique
nearest-point retraction and is $1$-Lipschitz. If $U=v\circ p$ is
a model, its distance to that subspace is the nonatomic polar
mass of $v$.

Metric convergence implies $L^1(X)$ convergence for every smooth
positive volume form. The upper-volume comparison on
$\mathcal M(\theta)$ vanishes identically.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Borel restriction identity of \cref{fibration:gauduchon-fiber-volume}
applied to $T_v$ and $\{v=-\infty\}$ gives
$\lambda_U^\gamma=L_\gamma(\mu_v)$. Its variation identity yields
\[
 \rho_\theta(v\circ p,w\circ p)
       =\|\mu_v-\mu_w\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=d_\beta(v,w).
\]
Together with \cref{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback}, this proves
independence and an isometry onto the entire model space.
Completeness and geodesics follow from
\cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}. The mass bound gives diameter
at most $2A$, and the models of $A\delta_s$ and $A\delta_t$ for
distinct base points attain it.

Ideal projection commutes with pullback by the envelope identity.
Thus \cref{prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect} gives its unique
nearest-point property, the Lipschitz estimate and the distance
formula. By \cref{curve-map:analytic-types}, analytic models
correspond in both directions. Every analytic model is ideal by
\cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}. The analytic curve models of
positive curve mass are dense in the ideal subspace by
\cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}; their pullbacks have zero
top-degree mass. This proves the asserted analytic closure.
Closedness and geodesic convexity follow from those of atomic
polar measures.

Curve metric convergence gives base $L^1$ convergence by
\cref{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence}, and
\cref{curve-map:lone-pullback} then gives convergence upstairs.
Finally the envelope lemma makes all top masses zero, including
that of each pairwise maximum. This makes the upper-volume
comparison zero.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{fibration:all-obstacle-criterion}\index{Envelope!all-obstacle curve map criterion}
Let $U=v\circ p$ and $R=P_\theta[U]_{\mathcal I}$. The following
are equivalent: $P_\theta[U]=P_\theta[U]_{\mathcal I}$;
$\mu_v$ is atomic; $U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,R)$;
and the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $U$ agree below every
smooth real obstacle on $X$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $r=P_\beta[v]_{\mathcal I}$. The envelope lemma gives
$R=r\circ p$ and makes the first condition equivalent to
$P_\beta[v]=r$. By \cref{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion},
this is equivalent to atomicity and to
$v\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(C,\beta,r)$.

For each $b\geq1$, descent and surjectivity give a bijection
between all stretching competitors satisfying
$b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})r\leq v$ and their upstairs counterparts.
Only convex combinations occur, so the correspondence remains
valid at poles. It identifies the two relative-class conditions.
Then \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality below
every smooth obstacle on $X$, including obstacles that do not
descend. Conversely that equality includes the zero obstacle.
\end{proof}

The top-degree measure is zero for every potential, yet the
first polar trace separates every pair of models. Here the
one-dimensional quotient supplies all the singularity data.

\subsection{Hopf surface fibers and unbounded ambient mass}
\label{subsec:hopf-surface-fibration}

The complete model space of a curve submersion can occur on a
manifold whose ambient Hermitian masses are unbounded. Blowing
up the elliptic base locus of the projection $[z_0:z_1]$ on the
scalar Hopf threefold turns this projection into a submersion
with Hopf surface fibers. The rank-one background then retains
only the curve singularities,
while the smooth potentials of the Hopf threefold still force
unbounded mass in a positive ambient background.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{fibration:unbounded-hopf-threefold}\index{Hopf manifold!surface fibration}\index{Metric completeness!Hopf surface fibration}\index{Bounded mass property!curve submersion counterexample}
There is a connected compact complex threefold $Z$ with a
surjective holomorphic submersion $p:Z\to\mathbb P^1$ whose
fibers are scalar Hopf surfaces. The manifold $Z$ does not have
the bounded mass property. For the pullback
$\theta=p^*\omega_{\FS}$ of the Fubini--Study form of area one,
the entire ordinary model space has the complete geodesic polar
metric of \cref{fibration:polar-model-metric}, with diameter two.
Its ideal subspace is the analytic closure, with the unique
nearest-point, $1$-Lipschitz ideal retraction. All potentials
in this background have zero extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure,
yet it contains analytic ideal models at polar distance one.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Put $\lambda=\mathrm e^{-1}$ and consider the vector bundle
\[
 \mathcal V=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)
                  \oplus\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}.
\]
Let $\mathcal V^\times$ be its total space with the zero section
removed, and define
\[
 Z=\mathcal V^\times/\langle v\mapsto\lambda v\rangle.
\]
The bundle projection descends to a holomorphic map
$p:Z\to\mathbb P^1$.
A Hermitian norm on the bundle satisfies
$\|\lambda^kv\|=\lambda^k\|v\|$. The action is free and proper:
on compact subsets of $\mathcal V^\times$ the norms have positive
lower and finite upper bounds, so only finitely many powers can
move one such subset to meet another. Thus the quotient is a
complex threefold. The annulus bundle
$\{\lambda\leq\|v\|\leq1\}$ is compact over $\mathbb P^1$ and
meets every orbit, so $Z$ is compact. Bundle charts identify
$p^{-1}(U)$ with
\[
 U\times\bigl((\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\})/
                    \langle w\mapsto\lambda w\rangle\bigr).
\]
Hence $p$ is a holomorphic submersion with connected compact
Hopf surface fibers, and $Z$ is connected.

The tautological inclusion into $\mathbb C^2$ identifies
$\mathcal V^\times$ with the incidence manifold
\[
 \left\{([s:t],z)\in\mathbb P^1\times
      (\mathbb C^3\setminus\{0\}):\ t z_0=s z_1\right\}.
\]
The last coordinate $z_2$ comes from the trivial summand. This
is the blowup of $\mathbb C^3\setminus\{0\}$ along
$L=\{z_0=z_1=0,\ z_2\ne0\}$: in the chart $s\ne0$, write $a=t/s$ and
$z_1=az_0$, while in the chart $t\ne0$ write $b=s/t$ and
$z_0=bz_1$. These are the two usual blowup charts of the ideal
$(z_0,z_1)$. The scalar contraction preserves the incidence
relation and commutes with projection to $z$. It therefore
descends to a modification
\[
 \mu:Z\longrightarrow
 H=(\mathbb C^3\setminus\{0\})/
                \langle z\mapsto\lambda z\rangle.
\]
The map is an isomorphism away from the elliptic curve
$L/\langle\lambda\rangle$. Local quotient charts show that it
is its blowup; in particular it has degree one.

Take the Gauduchon form $\omega$ and smooth potentials $V_j$ on
$H$ from \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}. Since $Z$ is compact, a
sufficiently large multiple $\Omega$ of any Hermitian form satisfies
$\Omega-\mu^*\omega>0$. Put $W_j=V_j\circ\mu$. Then
\[
 \Omega+\ddc W_j
 =\Omega-\mu^*\omega+\mu^*(\omega+\ddc V_j)>0.
\]
Positivity of smooth mixed products and the degree-one change
of variables off the exceptional divisor give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_Z(\Omega+\ddc W_j)^3
 &\geq\int_Z\mu^*(\omega+\ddc V_j)^3\\
 &=\int_H(\omega+\ddc V_j)^3\longrightarrow+\infty.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The exceptional divisor and its image have zero measure for
smooth top-degree forms, so they do not affect the equality.
Every $W_j$ is smooth and bounded, so $v_+(\Omega)=+\infty$.
By \cref{prop:bmp-metric}, $Z$ does not have the bounded mass
property.

Apply \cref{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback,fibration:polar-model-metric} to $p$ and the area-one form
$\omega_{\FS}$. This gives all the assertions about the full
model space. To see the stated distance explicitly, fix
$x\in\mathbb P^1$ and let $g_x$ be its normalized Green model,
with $\omega_{\FS}+\ddc g_x=\delta_x$. The models $0$ and
$g_x\circ p$ are ordinary and ideal, and the latter has a
logarithmic pole of coefficient one along the smooth reduced
fiber $p^{-1}(x)$. Thus both have analytic singularities. Their
base polar measures are $0$ and $\delta_x$, so their polar
distance is one, although their top-degree measures vanish.
\end{proof}
