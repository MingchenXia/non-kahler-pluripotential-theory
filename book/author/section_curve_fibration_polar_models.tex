\subsection{Models over a curve}
\label{subsec:curve-fibration-polar-models}

The polar metric on a curve also describes models on a compact
manifold that maps onto it with connected fibers, including
critical fibers. A background pulled back from the curve has no
vertical curvature, so every potential descends. Gauduchon
volumes of the fiber divisors are constant, with their
multiplicities included. The normalized polar trace therefore
recovers the curve metric without choosing a product structure.

Let $p\colon X\to C$ be a surjective holomorphic map with connected
fibers, where $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of
dimension $n\geq2$ and $C$ is a connected compact Riemann surface.
Fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta$ on $C$, put
$A=\int_C\beta$ and $\theta=p^*\beta$, and choose a smooth
positive area-one form $\eta$ on $C$. For
$v\in\PSH(C,\beta)$ write
\[
 T_v=\beta+\ddc v,\qquad
 \mu_v=\mathbf1_{\{v=-\infty\}}T_v.
\]
Thus $\mu_v$ is the polar curvature of the curve potential.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback}\index{Envelope!curve map}\index{Model potential!arbitrary curve background pullback}
The class $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is nonempty if and only if $A\geq0$.
For $v\in\PSH(C,\beta)$ and $U=v\circ p$, one has
\[
 P_\theta[U]=P_\beta[v]\circ p,\qquad
 P_\theta[U]_{\mathcal I}=P_\beta[v]_{\mathcal I}\circ p.
\]
Pullback is a bijection between the ordinary model spaces and
between their ideal subspaces. Every potential in this background
has zero extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure, and every ordinary type
has zero upper and lower singularity volumes.

If $A=0$ and $\beta=\ddc h$ with $h$ smooth, the only normalized potential is
$-h\circ p+\min_Ch$. It is smooth and is the unique ordinary and
ideal model.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{curve-map:potential-descent}, every potential descends,
even for this possibly indefinite background. Thus nonemptiness
is equivalent to the curve condition in
\cref{lem:curve-background-area}. Descent preserves and reflects
the ordinary order and the supremum, and \cref{curve-map:ideal-types}
preserves and reflects the all-weight ideal order. Pullback is
therefore a bijection between the competitor families of the
envelopes upstairs and on $C$. The map $p$ is open: in coordinates
centered at $x$ and $p(x)$, its restriction to a complex line on
which the first nonzero homogeneous term of $p$ does not vanish is
a nonconstant function of one variable. Upper regularization
therefore commutes with pullback, proving the envelope identities
and the model bijections.

Write $\beta=A\eta+\ddc h$ with $h$ smooth, using
\cref{lem:curve-background-area}. For $v\in\PSH(C,\beta)$, put
$w=v+h\in\PSH(C,A\eta)$. Then
\[
 \theta+\ddc(v\circ p)
   =A p^*\eta+\ddc(w\circ p).
\]
When $A>0$, put $W=w\circ p$. Its bounded truncations are
$\max(W,-j)=\max(w,-j)\circ p$. Locally choose a smooth $a$
with $\ddc a=A\eta$. The bounded subharmonic function
$a+\max(w,-j)$ has smooth decreasing local approximants. Their
pullbacks have complex Hessian rank at most one, including at
critical points, so their $n$th powers vanish. Bounded product
continuity makes each truncation have zero top product, and
hence $W$ has zero extended measure in the semipositive
background $Ap^*\eta$.
The current invariance in
\cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma} gives zero extended measure in
the original decomposition as well.

When $A=0$, \cref{lem:curve-background-area} gives $v=-h+c$. Its pullback is
smooth with zero curvature current, so its top product is zero.
Surjectivity gives the stated normalization. Every potential
differs from this one by a constant, and both envelopes fix its
normalized representative. Since descent applies to every
representative of every ordinary type, both singularity volumes
are zero in all cases.
\end{proof}

Henceforth assume $A\geq0$. Fix a Gauduchon form $\gamma$ on $X$. For a finite positive measure
$\mu$ on $C$ of mass $M$, let $q_\mu$ be the solution normalized by
$\int_Cq_\mu\,\eta=0$ to
\[
 \ddc q_\mu=\mu-M\eta.
\]
The curve Green kernel of \cref{sec:curve-trace-realization}
gives this quasi-subharmonic solution. Define the positive current
\[
 p^*\mu=M p^*\eta+\ddc(q_\mu\circ p),\qquad
 L_\gamma(\mu)=p^*\mu\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\]
Locally this is the pullback of a subharmonic potential of $\mu$;
changing that potential adds a harmonic function and does not
change the current. Extend both operators to signed measures by
linearity. Write $F_s=p^{-1}(s)$ for the fiber divisor with its
scheme multiplicities.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{fibration:gauduchon-fiber-volume}\index{Gauduchon metric!fiber divisor volume}
There is a positive constant $\kappa_\gamma$ such that, for every
finite signed measure $\mu$ and every Borel set $B\subset C$,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 p_*L_\gamma(\mu)=\kappa_\gamma\mu,\qquad
 \|L_\gamma(\mu)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
               =\kappa_\gamma\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}},\\
 L_\gamma(\mathbf1_B\mu)
            =\mathbf1_{p^{-1}(B)}L_\gamma(\mu),\qquad
 \int_{F_s}\gamma^{n-1}=\kappa_\gamma\quad(s\in C).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The last integral includes the multiplicities of the fiber.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The positive $(0,0)$-current $S=p_*(\gamma^{n-1})$ on $C$ satisfies
$\ddc S=p_*\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0$. A harmonic distribution is smooth,
so $S$ is a constant $\kappa_\gamma$. On the complement of the
critical values, smooth fiber integration identifies it with
the positive integral of $\gamma^{n-1}$ on a regular fiber.
Thus $\kappa_\gamma>0$.

First let $\mu$ be positive with mass $M>0$. Choose smooth
positive measures $\mu_j$ with the same mass and
$\mu_j\rightharpoonup\mu$. Their mean-zero solutions $q_j$ have
a common lower curvature bound $-M\eta$. Normalized compactness
bounds $\sup_Cq_j$: their mean-zero condition makes this
supremum the negative mean of their supremum-zero normalization.
Hence $(q_j)$ is precompact in $L^1(C)$. Every limit has mean
zero and Hessian $\mu-M\eta$. Two such limits differ by a
harmonic function, hence by a constant, which their means force
to be zero. Therefore $q_j\to q_\mu$ in $L^1(C)$.

By \cref{curve-map:lone-pullback}, $q_j\circ p\to q_\mu\circ p$
in $L^1(X)$. Thus $p^*\mu_j\to p^*\mu$ as currents, and the measures
$L_\gamma(\mu_j)$ converge weakly to $L_\gamma(\mu)$. For smooth $\mu_j$,
the definition of $S$ gives
$p_*L_\gamma(\mu_j)=\kappa_\gamma\mu_j$. Passing to the limit
proves the identity for $\mu$. The case $M=0$ is zero, and
linearity proves the signed identity.

For a positive $\mu$, split it into $\mathbf1_B\mu$ and
$\mathbf1_{C\setminus B}\mu$. Their traces are positive; the
pushforward identity shows that they are respectively carried
by $p^{-1}(B)$ and its complement. Additivity then gives the
restriction identity. Apply the same argument to the positive
and negative Jordan parts of a signed measure. Their disjoint
Borel carriers pull back to disjoint carriers, so their traces
are the Jordan parts upstairs. The pushforward identity gives
the asserted total-variation norm.

Finally the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula, with our normalization of $\ddc$,
gives $p^*\delta_s=[F_s]$, including every zero multiplicity of
a local equation of the fiber. Its trace has total mass
$\kappa_\gamma$, by the pushforward identity with $\mu=\delta_s$.
\end{proof}

For $v\in\PSH(C,\beta)$, the definition of the pullback measure
gives $p^*T_v=\theta+\ddc(v\circ p)$: in the smooth decomposition
$\beta=A\eta+\ddc h$, both sides are
$A p^*\eta+\ddc((v+h)\circ p)$.
Let $\mathcal M(\theta)$ be the ordinary model space. For
$U=v\circ p\in\mathcal M(\theta)$, its polar trace measure is
\[
 \lambda_U^\gamma
 =\mathbf1_{\{U=-\infty\}}
       (\theta+\ddc U)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 =L_\gamma(\mu_v).
\]
The last identity follows from $p^*T_v=\theta+\ddc U$, the equality
$\{U=-\infty\}=p^{-1}(\{v=-\infty\})$ and the Borel restriction formula of
\cref{fibration:gauduchon-fiber-volume}, including at critical
fibers and for a nonatomic polar measure. Define
\[
 \rho_\theta(U,V)=\kappa_\gamma^{-1}
                  \|\lambda_U^\gamma-\lambda_V^\gamma\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{fibration:polar-model-metric}\index{Metric completeness!curve map}
The metric $\rho_\theta$ is independent of the Gauduchon form
$\gamma$. Pullback is an isometry from the complete geodesic
curve model space $(\mathcal M(\beta),d_\beta)$ of
\cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} onto
$(\mathcal M(\theta),\rho_\theta)$, which has diameter $2A$. The ideal models form a closed geodesically
convex subspace equal to the metric closure of the models with
analytic singularities. Ideal projection is its unique
nearest-point retraction and is $1$-Lipschitz. If $U=v\circ p$ is
a model, its distance to that subspace is the nonatomic polar
mass of $v$.

Metric convergence implies $L^1(X)$ convergence for every smooth
positive volume form. The upper-volume comparison on
$\mathcal M(\theta)$ vanishes identically. At $A=0$, $\mathcal M(\theta)$ is the
one-point metric space of the unique smooth model.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the Borel restriction and
total-variation identities of
\cref{fibration:gauduchon-fiber-volume} to get
\[
 \lambda_{v\circ p}^\gamma=L_\gamma(\mu_v),\qquad
 \rho_\theta(v\circ p,w\circ p)
       =\|\mu_v-\mu_w\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=d_\beta(v,w).
\]
The envelope bijection and
\cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} give the isometry,
completeness and geodesics. Polar measures have mass at most
$A$, so their mutual distance is at most $2A$. For $A>0$, the
models of $A\delta_s$ and $A\delta_t$, $s\ne t$, attain this
bound. For $A=0$, \cref{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback} gives
the singleton.

Ideal projection corresponds to the atomic part of the polar
measure by the same curve classification. If
$\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{at}}+\mu_{\mathrm{diff}}$ is the decomposition of a
polar measure into its atomic and nonatomic parts, and $\nu$ is atomic,
the measures $\mu_{\mathrm{diff}}$ and
$\mu_{\mathrm{at}}-\nu$ are mutually singular. Therefore
\[
 \|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
  =\mu_{\mathrm{diff}}(C)
     +\|\mu_{\mathrm{at}}-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
The minimum is attained exactly at $\nu=\mu_{\mathrm{at}}$.
Taking the atomic part does not increase total variation, giving
the Lipschitz estimate. Atomic polar measures of mass at most
$A$ form a closed convex subset of the polar-measure space.

For $A>0$, the smooth-gauge model bijection in the proof of
\cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} preserves analytic
singularities in both directions: its envelope differs by a
bounded function from the smoothly transported analytic model,
by \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge}. Thus the analytic density in
\cref{cor:curve-model-completeness} carries from $A\eta$ to
$\beta$. The analytic descent equivalence of
\cref{curve-map:analytic-types} and the envelope bijection then
give the stated closure upstairs. At zero area the unique smooth
model is already analytic and ideal.

Curve metric convergence implies $L^1(C)$ convergence by
\cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}, and
\cref{curve-map:lone-pullback} gives convergence upstairs.
Finally all top-degree masses, including those of pairwise
maxima, vanish by \cref{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback}.
The upper-volume comparison therefore vanishes.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{fibration:all-obstacle-criterion}\index{Envelope!all-obstacle curve map criterion}
For $v\in\PSH(C,\beta)$, let $U=v\circ p$ and $R=P_\theta[U]_{\mathcal I}$. The following
are equivalent: $P_\theta[U]=P_\theta[U]_{\mathcal I}$;
$\mu_v$ is atomic; $U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,R)$;
and the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $U$ agree below every
smooth real obstacle on $X$. At zero area all four conditions hold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $r=P_\beta[v]_{\mathcal I}$. The envelope bijection gives
$R=r\circ p$. By \cref{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion},
base envelope equality is equivalent to atomic polar curvature
and to $v\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(C,\beta,r)$.
For each stretching parameter $b\geq1$, descent and surjectivity
identify competitors $g$ satisfying
$b^{-1}g+(1-b^{-1})r\leq v$ with their upstairs counterparts.
This uses only convex combinations and remains valid at poles.
Thus the relative-class conditions correspond. Apply
\cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} for equality below every
smooth obstacle on $X$; the converse includes the zero obstacle.
The zero-area assertion follows from the smooth singleton.
\end{proof}

The top-degree measure is zero for every potential, yet the
first polar trace separates every pair of models. Here the
one-dimensional quotient supplies all the singularity data.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\subsection{Finite curve factors}
\label{subsec:finite-curve-factors}

Keep $X$ and $C$ as above, but now allow $p\colon X\to C$ to have
disconnected fibers. By \cref{curve-map:component-factor}, it has
the component factorization
\[
 X\xrightarrow{q}C'\xrightarrow{r}C,
 \qquad p=r\circ q,
\]
where $q$ is surjective and holomorphic with connected fibers,
$C'$ is a connected compact Riemann surface, and $r$ is finite
and holomorphic of degree $d\geq1$. Let $\beta$ be any smooth
real $(1,1)$-form on $C$,
put $A=\int_C\beta$, and set $\sigma=r^*\beta$ and
$\theta=p^*\beta=q^*\sigma$.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{fibration:finite-curve-factor}\index{Metric completeness!finite curve factor}
The class $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is nonempty exactly when $A\geq0$.
For $A\geq0$, the metric $\rho_\theta$ of
\cref{fibration:polar-model-metric}, formed with $q$ and normalized
by the Gauduchon volume of its fibers, makes the entire ordinary
$\theta$-model space isometric to the curve model space in
background $\sigma$, whose area is $dA$. Its diameter is $2dA$,
and it has the analytic closure, ideal retraction, convergence and
all-obstacle properties of
\cref{fibration:polar-model-metric,fibration:all-obstacle-criterion}. Every potential has zero extended top-degree
measure and every ordinary type has zero upper and lower
singularity volumes. At $A=0$ the model space is a smooth singleton.

If $\kappa_q^\gamma$ and $\kappa_p^\gamma$ are the constants
defined by the pushforwards of $\gamma^{n-1}$ to $C'$ and $C$,
then
\[
 \kappa_p^\gamma=d\kappa_q^\gamma.
\]
For $A\geq0$, normalizing the same polar-trace variation by
$\kappa_p^\gamma$ gives $d^{-1}$ times the $q$-normalized metric,
with diameter $2A$. This rescaled metric is also independent of
$\gamma$. For a model $U=v\circ q$ with polar measure $\mu_v$,
\[
 p_*\lambda_U^\gamma=\kappa_q^\gamma r_*\mu_v.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Away from the finitely many critical values, $r$ is a covering
with $d$ sheets. Change of variables on these sheets gives
\[
 \int_{C'}r^*\alpha=d\int_C\alpha
\]
for every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha$; the branch points
and their inverse images have smooth area zero. In particular
$\int_{C'}\sigma=dA$. The envelope lemma
\cref{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback}, applied to $q$, gives
nonemptiness exactly for $dA\geq0$, equivalent to $A\geq0$,
and proves the zero-mass and zero-area assertions. In this
nonempty case, apply
\cref{fibration:polar-model-metric,fibration:all-obstacle-criterion}
to $q$ and the smooth background $\sigma$, which may vanish at
branch points or be indefinite.

\Cref{fibration:gauduchon-fiber-volume}, applied to $q$, gives
$q_*(\gamma^{n-1})=\kappa_q^\gamma$. For a smooth test form
$\alpha$ on $C$, the projection formula and the covering
calculation yield
\[
 \int_Xp^*\alpha\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
  =\kappa_q^\gamma\int_{C'}r^*\alpha
  =d\kappa_q^\gamma\int_C\alpha.
\]
Thus $p_*(\gamma^{n-1})$ is the constant $d\kappa_q^\gamma$.
The ratio of the two normalizations is $d^{-1}$, giving every
metric assertion. Finally
$q_*\lambda_U^\gamma=\kappa_q^\gamma\mu_v$, so composing
pushforwards proves the last identity.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{fibration:disconnected-polar-pushforward}\index{Descent!disconnected curve fibers}
Let $X=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, let $q$ be its first
projection and let $r\colon\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1$ be
$r([z_0:z_1])=[z_0^2:z_1^2]$. Put $p=r\circ q$ and
$\theta=p^*\omega_{\FS}$, with $\int_{\mathbb P^1}\omega_{\FS}=1$.
There are two distinct analytic ideal $\theta$-models $U_+,U_-$
which do not descend through $p$. Their $q$-normalized polar
distance is four and their $p$-normalized distance is two, yet
\[
 p_*\lambda_{U_+}^\gamma=p_*\lambda_{U_-}^\gamma
\]
for every Gauduchon form $\gamma$. Thus the pushforward of polar
trace to the original base does not separate the models.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The finite map $r$ has degree two and is branched at zero and
infinity. In the affine coordinate it is $r(z)=z^2$, so the
smooth semipositive form $\sigma=r^*\omega_{\FS}$ has area two.
For $s\in\{1,-1\}$, set
\[
 v_s(z)=2\log|z-s|^2-\log(1+|z|^4)-\log8.
\]
This extends smoothly across infinity and has curvature
$\sigma+\ddc v_s=2\delta_s$. The inequality
$|z-s|^4\leq(|z|+1)^4\leq8(1+|z|^4)$, with equality at
$z=-s$, shows that its supremum is zero.
The model of $2\delta_s$ from
\cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} has precisely
this total curvature, since its polar measure already has the
full mass two. Harmonic uniqueness and normalization identify
it with $v_s$. Write $v_+=v_1$ and $v_-=v_{-1}$. Both have
analytic singularities, hence are ideal by
\cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}. The pullback bijection of
\cref{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback}, applied to $q$, makes
$U_\pm=v_\pm\circ q$ analytic ideal $\theta$-models.

The function $U_+$ is $-\infty$ on $q^{-1}(1)$ and finite on
$q^{-1}(-1)$, although both divisors are mapped by $p$ to the
same base point $1$. It therefore cannot descend through $p$;
the same argument with the divisors exchanged applies to $U_-$.
The variation of $2\delta_1-2\delta_{-1}$ is four, and
\cref{fibration:finite-curve-factor} gives the two distances and
\[
 p_*\lambda_{U_\pm}^\gamma
   =2\kappa_q^\gamma\delta_1.
\]
The upstairs polar traces are distinct, while this common
pushforward loses which component carries the pole.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hopf surface fibers and unbounded ambient mass}
\label{subsec:hopf-surface-fibration}

The complete model space of a curve submersion can occur on a
manifold whose ambient Hermitian masses are unbounded. Blowing
up the elliptic base locus of the projection $[z_0:z_1]$ on the
scalar Hopf threefold turns this projection into a submersion
with Hopf surface fibers. The rank-one background then retains
only the curve singularities,
while the smooth potentials of the Hopf threefold still force
unbounded mass in a positive ambient background.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{fibration:unbounded-hopf-threefold}\index{Hopf manifold!surface fibration}\index{Metric completeness!Hopf surface fibration}\index{Bounded mass property!curve submersion counterexample}
There is a connected compact complex threefold $Z$ with a
surjective holomorphic submersion $p\colon Z\to\mathbb P^1$ whose
fibers are scalar Hopf surfaces. The manifold $Z$ does not have
the bounded mass property. For the pullback
$\theta=p^*\omega_{\FS}$ of the Fubini--Study form of area one,
the entire ordinary model space has the complete geodesic polar
metric of \cref{fibration:polar-model-metric}, with diameter two.
Its ideal subspace is the analytic closure, with the unique
nearest-point, $1$-Lipschitz ideal retraction. All potentials
in this background have zero extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure,
yet the model space contains analytic ideal models at polar
distance one.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Put $\lambda=\mathrm e^{-1}$ and consider the vector bundle
\[
 \mathcal V=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)
                  \oplus\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}.
\]
Let $\mathcal V^\times$ be its total space with the zero section
removed, and define
\[
 Z=\mathcal V^\times/\langle v\mapsto\lambda v\rangle.
\]
The bundle projection descends to a holomorphic map
$p\colon Z\to\mathbb P^1$.
A Hermitian norm on the bundle satisfies
$\|\lambda^kv\|=\lambda^k\|v\|$. The action is free and proper:
on compact subsets of $\mathcal V^\times$ the norms have positive
lower and finite upper bounds, so only finitely many powers can
move one such subset to meet another. Thus the quotient is a
complex threefold. The annulus bundle
$\{\lambda\leq\|v\|\leq1\}$ is compact over $\mathbb P^1$ and
meets every orbit, so $Z$ is compact. Bundle charts identify
$p^{-1}(U)$ with
\[
 U\times\bigl((\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\})/
                    \langle w\mapsto\lambda w\rangle\bigr).
\]
Hence $p$ is a holomorphic submersion with connected compact
Hopf surface fibers, and $Z$ is connected.

The tautological inclusion into $\mathbb C^2$ identifies
$\mathcal V^\times$ with the incidence manifold
\[
 \left\{([s:t],z)\in\mathbb P^1\times
      (\mathbb C^3\setminus\{0\}):\ t z_0=s z_1\right\}.
\]
The last coordinate $z_2$ comes from the trivial summand. This
is the blowup of $\mathbb C^3\setminus\{0\}$ along
$L=\{z_0=z_1=0,\ z_2\ne0\}$: in the chart $s\ne0$, write $a=t/s$ and
$z_1=az_0$, while in the chart $t\ne0$ write $b=s/t$ and
$z_0=bz_1$. These are the two usual blowup charts of the ideal
$(z_0,z_1)$. The scalar contraction preserves the incidence
relation and commutes with projection to $z$. It therefore
descends to a modification
\[
 \pi\colon Z\longrightarrow
 H=(\mathbb C^3\setminus\{0\})/
                \langle z\mapsto\lambda z\rangle.
\]
The map is an isomorphism away from the elliptic curve
$L/\langle\lambda\rangle$. Local quotient charts show that $\pi$
is the blowup of $H$ along this curve; in particular, $\pi$ has
degree one.

Take the Gauduchon form $\omega$ and smooth potentials $V_j$ on
$H$ from \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}. Since $Z$ is compact, a
sufficiently large multiple $\Omega$ of any Hermitian form satisfies
$\Omega-\pi^*\omega>0$. Put $W_j=V_j\circ\pi$. Then
\[
 \Omega+\ddc W_j
 =\Omega-\pi^*\omega+\pi^*(\omega+\ddc V_j)>0.
\]
Positivity of smooth mixed products and the degree-one change
of variables off the exceptional divisor give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_Z(\Omega+\ddc W_j)^3
 &\geq\int_Z\pi^*(\omega+\ddc V_j)^3\\
 &=\int_H(\omega+\ddc V_j)^3\longrightarrow+\infty.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The exceptional divisor and its image have zero measure for
smooth top-degree forms, so they do not affect the equality.
Every $W_j$ is smooth and bounded, so $v_+(\Omega)=+\infty$.
By \cref{prop:bmp-metric}, $Z$ does not have the bounded mass
property.

Apply \cref{fibration:curve-envelope-pullback,fibration:polar-model-metric} to $p$ and the area-one form
$\omega_{\FS}$. This gives all the assertions about the full
model space. To see the stated distance explicitly, fix
$x\in\mathbb P^1$ and let $g_x$ be its normalized Green model,
with $\omega_{\FS}+\ddc g_x=\delta_x$. The models $0$ and
$g_x\circ p$ are ordinary and ideal, and the latter has a
logarithmic pole of coefficient one along the smooth reduced
fiber $p^{-1}(x)$. Thus both have analytic singularities. Their
base polar measures are $0$ and $\delta_x$, so their polar
distance is one, although their top-degree measures vanish.
\end{proof}
