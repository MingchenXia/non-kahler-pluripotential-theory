\section{Multiplier ideals and logarithmic poles}
\label{sec:psh-local-ideals}

For a logarithmic pole along a submanifold, weighted integrability of a holomorphic germ is determined by its vanishing order in the normal directions. For general singularities, the valuative characterization of multiplier ideals and the strong openness theorem, both recalled in \cite{SGPT}, make the comparison of multiplier ideals compatible with maxima, sums and increasing limits.

In local coordinates $(z,w)\in\mathbb C^d\times\mathbb C^k$, with $d\geq0$ and $k\geq1$, let $Y=\{w=0\}$ and denote its ideal by $\mathcal I_Y$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}
If a quasi-plurisubharmonic function has the form $u=c\log|w|^2+O(1)$ with $c>0$ and locally bounded remainder, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathcal I(tu)=\mathcal I_Y^{\max\{\lfloor tc\rfloor-k+1,0\}}.
\]
The zeroth power is the full structure sheaf.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The bounded remainder changes the weight only by bounded positive factors. Expand a nonzero holomorphic germ $g(z,w)$ in homogeneous polynomials in $w$, and let $a$ be the smallest degree whose term does not vanish identically. On smaller product neighborhoods, $|g(z,w)|\leq C|w|^a$. Hence $a+k>tc$ suffices for weighted integrability, since the corresponding radial integral in $\rho=|w|$ is
\[
 \int_0^\varepsilon \rho^{2a-2tc+2k-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho<+\infty.
\]
Conversely, let $z$ be a point near the origin at which the term of degree $a$ does not vanish identically in $w$. Integration of $|g(z,w)|^2$ over the spheres $|w|=\rho$ gives a positive leading term of order $\rho^{2a}$, so the radial integral in $w$ diverges when $a+k\leq tc$, logarithmically in the case of equality. Such points $z$ form a nonempty open set in every sufficiently small neighborhood, and Fubini's theorem shows that $g$ is not weighted-integrable near the origin. For $d=0$ this is the radial calculation at a point. Thus $g$ is weighted-integrable exactly when $a\geq\lfloor tc\rfloor-k+1$, that is, when $g$ lies in the power of $\mathcal I_Y$ given in the statement.
\end{proof}

Cutoff functions allow us to insert small isolated logarithmic poles into any Hermitian background.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:small-isolated-log-poles}
Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on $X$, and fix distinct points $p_1,\ldots,p_N$ of $X$. There are constants $C_j>0$ such that, whenever $a_j>0$ and $\sum_jC_ja_j<1$, there is $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, smooth off these points, with
\[
 u=a_j\log|z|^2+O(1)\quad\hbox{near }p_j,\qquad
 \omega+\ddc u\geq\left(1-\sum_jC_ja_j\right)\omega.
\]
Here $z$ denotes local coordinates centered at $p_j$. The function can be chosen to vanish outside arbitrarily small disjoint coordinate neighborhoods, with the constants depending on those neighborhoods.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose disjoint coordinate balls centered at the points $p_j$ and smooth cutoffs $\chi_j$ supported in them and equal to one near their centers. The function $\chi_j\log|z|^2$, extended by zero, is plurisubharmonic near its pole and smooth elsewhere, so its complex Hessian is bounded below by $-C_j\omega$. Set $u=\sum_ja_j\chi_j\log|z|^2$. These lower bounds give the asserted current inequality, and the remaining properties follow from the disjointness of the supports.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:local-i-inputs}\index{Multiplier ideal!strong openness}
Let $u,v$ be plurisubharmonic functions near a point of $\mathbb C^n$. By a \emph{local modification} we mean a modification $\pi$, with smooth source, of a neighborhood of this point.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The inclusions $\mathcal I(ku)\subseteq\mathcal I(kv)$ for every real $k>0$ are equivalent to the inequalities $\nu(u\circ\pi,y)\geq\nu(v\circ\pi,y)$ at every point $y$ of the source of every local modification $\pi$. Likewise, equality of the multiplier ideals at every positive weight is equivalent to equality of all these Lelong numbers.
\item If $p_j$ is an increasing sequence of plurisubharmonic functions converging almost everywhere to a plurisubharmonic function $p$, then, for each point $x$ and each $\lambda>0$,
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda p_j)_x=\mathcal I(\lambda p)_x
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }j.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

In the first item, the neighborhoods may be shrunk as necessary; we never need to extend a local modification to a modification of a compact manifold. The second item is the increasing-limit form of the strong openness theorem used in \cite{SGPT}. Since adding a smooth function changes neither multiplier ideals nor Lelong numbers, both items apply to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions after the local correction of \cref{prop:local-correction}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hermitian-i-calculus}\index{Multiplier ideal!cancellation}
Let $u,u',v,v'$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$. For any quasi-plurisubharmonic $h$, one has
\[
 u+h\sim_{\mathcal I}v+h
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u\sim_{\mathcal I}v.
\]
If $u\sim_{\mathcal I}v$ and $u'\sim_{\mathcal I}v'$, then
\[
 \max(u,u')\sim_{\mathcal I}\max(v,v'),\qquad
 u+u'\sim_{\mathcal I}v+v',\qquad
 a u\sim_{\mathcal I}a v\quad(a>0).
\]
\end{lemma}
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Fix a coordinate neighborhood $U\subset X$ and a modification $\pi:Y\to U$ with smooth source. At every $y\in Y$, the elementary rules for the Lelong numbers of maxima, sums and positive multiples give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \nu(\max(u,u')\circ\pi,y)
   &=\min\{\nu(u\circ\pi,y),\nu(u'\circ\pi,y)\},\\
 \nu((u+u')\circ\pi,y)
   &=\nu(u\circ\pi,y)+\nu(u'\circ\pi,y),\\
 \nu(a u\circ\pi,y)&=a\nu(u\circ\pi,y).
 \end{aligned}
\]
These rules are unaffected by smooth local corrections. Since $u\sim_{\mathcal I}v$ and $u'\sim_{\mathcal I}v'$, the first item of \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} shows that the right-hand sides do not change when $u,u'$ are replaced by $v,v'$. The converse direction of the same item, applied on each coordinate neighborhood, gives the three asserted equivalences.

For the cancellation rule, suppose that $u+h\sim_{\mathcal I}v+h$. By the first item of \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}, the pullbacks $(u+h)\circ\pi$ and $(v+h)\circ\pi$ have the same Lelong numbers. The common summand $h\circ\pi$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, so its Lelong numbers are finite, and subtracting them shows that $u\circ\pi$ and $v\circ\pi$ have the same Lelong numbers. Hence $u\sim_{\mathcal I}v$ by the same item. The reverse implication is the addition rule proved above.
\end{proof}

Let $v,w$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on an open set, and let $Y$ be a complex submanifold on which $v|_Y$ is not identically $-\infty$. The restriction and subadditivity theorems give the two inclusions recalled in \cite{SGPT} and \cite[Section 15]{DemAM11}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-local-ideal-inputs}
 \mathcal I(v|_Y)\subseteq\mathcal I(v)\mathcal O_Y,
 \qquad
 \mathcal I(v+w)\subseteq\mathcal I(v)\mathcal I(w).
\end{equation}
The first follows from the local Ohsawa--Takegoshi extension theorem. For the second, the weighted Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on a product is the Hilbert tensor product of the two weighted spaces, so the multiplier ideal of $v(x)+w(y)$ on the product is generated by products of generators of $\mathcal I(v)$ and $\mathcal I(w)$; the first inclusion, applied to the diagonal, then gives the second. These local results for plurisubharmonic functions extend to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, in the weight convention of this book, by smooth corrections.

Multiplier ideals commute with pullback by holomorphic submersions. Here the extension $\mathcal I\mathcal O_Z$ of an ideal sheaf $\mathcal I$ on $Y$ is the ideal sheaf on $Z$ generated by the pullbacks of its local sections.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback}\index{Multiplier ideal!submersion pullback}
Let $p:Z\to Y$ be a holomorphic submersion of complex manifolds and let $u\in\QPSH(Y)$. For every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathcal I(tu\circ p)
       =\mathcal I(tu)\mathcal O_Z.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work in product coordinates $(z,\zeta)$ with $p(z,\zeta)=z$. Write $\alpha$ for a multi-index in the fiber variables and $\mathrm{d}\lambda_\zeta$ for their Euclidean volume. A germ in the ideal on the right is a finite sum of bounded holomorphic multiples of pullbacks of germs that are square integrable against $\mathrm{e}^{-tu(z)}$. Fubini's theorem shows that it is weighted-integrable on a sufficiently small product neighborhood.

Conversely, let $F(z,\zeta)$ be a weighted-integrable holomorphic germ and expand it in the fiber variables,
\[
 F(z,\zeta)=\sum_\alpha f_\alpha(z)\zeta^\alpha.
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
The coefficient estimate for a holomorphic function on a polydisk gives, on smaller product neighborhoods,
\[
 |f_\alpha(z)|^2\leq C_\alpha
       \int |F(z,\zeta)|^2\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_\zeta.
\]
Multiplying by $\mathrm{e}^{-tu(z)}$ and integrating in $z$ shows that each coefficient germ $f_\alpha$ belongs to $\mathcal I(tu)$. Let $J$ be the extension of this ideal to the local ring $R$ of holomorphic germs on the product. Every finite Taylor sum of $F$ belongs to $J$, and the remainder of total fiber degree at least $N$ belongs to $(\zeta)^N$. Thus the class of $F$ in $R/J$ lies in $(\zeta)^N(R/J)$ for every $N$. By the Krull intersection theorem for the finitely generated module $R/J$ over the Noetherian local ring $R$, the intersection of these submodules is zero. Hence $F\in J$. Smooth corrections and smooth positive volume densities do not change the integrability conditions.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{fibration:ramified-ideal-pullback}\index{Multiplier ideal!ramified pullback}
Exact multiplier-ideal pullback can fail at a critical fiber.
On a polydisk in $\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq1$, let
$f(z_0,\ldots,z_{n-1})=z_0^2$ and put
$v(t)=\tfrac12\log|t|^2$. At the origin,
\[
 \mathcal I(v)=\mathcal O,\qquad
 \mathcal I(v\circ f)=(z_0)
             \ne\mathcal I(v)\mathcal O.
\]
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
On the curve the coefficient is $1/2$, so the multiplier ideal
is the zeroth power of the maximal ideal. Upstairs
$v\circ f=\log|z_0|^2$ has coefficient one along the smooth
divisor $\{z_0=0\}$. Apply \cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}
in codimension one to get its first ideal power.
\end{proof}

This calculation concerns a fixed weight. For a surjective
holomorphic map from a connected compact manifold to a curve,
with connected fibers, pullback still preserves and reflects
all-weight ideal types by \cref{curve-map:ideal-types}.
