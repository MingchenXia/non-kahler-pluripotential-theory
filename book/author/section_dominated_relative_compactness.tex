\section{A common envelope after extraction}

\label{sec:relative-common-envelope-setting}
Retain the data of \cref{sec:relative-general-setting} and put $\kappa=(1-\varepsilon)^n v_-(\theta)>0$. Write $Q_b(a,w)=P_\theta(ba-(b-1)w)$ for $b\geq1$, as in \cref{sec:relative-finite-domination}. All sequences are indexed by positive integers.

A prescribed ordinary-type barrier is not needed when the Monge--Amp\`ere measures are all bounded by one finite measure. The lower-volume estimate can construct a barrier in the relative envelope class after extraction. We formulate exactly the extraction statement used in the convergence proof.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dominated-relative-rooftop-extraction}
Assume \eqref{eq:relative-positive-lower-volume}. Suppose $u_j\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, $u_j\to u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\mu_{u_j}\leq\nu$ for a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$. There is a subsequence, denoted again by $u_j$, such that
\[
 h=P_\theta\left(\inf_j u_j\right)\in\mathcal E_\varphi.
\]
In particular $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The envelope identity \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality} gives $P_\theta[u_j]=\varphi$. Since $u_j-\sup_Xu_j\leq P_\theta[u_j]$, Hartogs' lemma supplies a constant $C$ with $u_j\leq\varphi+C$ for all $j$. A common translation reduces to $u_j\leq\varphi\leq0$, and then $u\leq\varphi$. By \cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals},
\[
 I_j=\int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{u_j}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Extract so that $I_j\leq2^{-j}$ and $u_j\to u$ almost everywhere for smooth volume. This extraction is made once.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For $N\geq j$ put $p_{j,N}=P_\theta(u_j,\ldots,u_N)$. These are in $\mathcal E_\varphi$ by \cref{prop:relative-finite-rooftops}. Partition their contact sets by the first index at which the minimum is attained. Ordered contact on these disjoint pieces gives both
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-rooftop-common-domination}
 \mu_{p_{j,N}}\leq\nu,
 \qquad
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p_{j,N}}
 \leq\sum_{\ell=j}^N I_\ell\leq2^{1-j}.
\end{equation}
The first bound has no factor $N$: each disjoint piece is bounded by the restriction of the same measure $\nu$ to that piece.

Fix a real number $B\geq1$ and set $r_{j,N}=Q_B(p_{j,N},\varphi)$. It belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$, so $\mu_{r_{j,N}}(X)\geq\kappa$. Put $D_{j,N}=\{B^{-1}r_{j,N}+(1-B^{-1})\varphi=p_{j,N}\}$. Contact support and positivity give
\[
 \mu_{r_{j,N}}\leq B^n\mathbf1_{D_{j,N}}\mu_{p_{j,N}}
 \leq B^n\nu,
\]
On this contact set, since $\varphi\leq0$,
$\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}\leq \mathrm{e}^{r_{j,N}/B}$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-rooftop-noncollapse-test}
 \int_X\mathrm{e}^u\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{r_{j,N}}
 \leq B^n2^{1-j}
      +B^n\nu(X)\exp\!\left(\frac{\sup_X r_{j,N}}B\right).
\end{equation}
On the other hand choose $K>0$ with $B^n\nu(\{u\leq-K\})<\kappa/2$. This is possible since $u$ is genuine and $\nu$ does not charge its pole set. The preceding measure domination and lower mass bound imply
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{e}^u\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{r_{j,N}}\geq \mathrm{e}^{-K}\kappa/2.
\]
Choose $j$ so large that $B^n2^{1-j}<\mathrm{e}^{-K}\kappa/4$. Formula \eqref{eq:relative-rooftop-noncollapse-test} then bounds $\sup_Xr_{j,N}$ from below independently of $N$. The sequence decreases in $N$ and has the common upper bound $\varphi$. Its limit $R$ is therefore genuine. It satisfies, for every $\ell\geq j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-tail-stretched-competitor}
 B^{-1}R+(1-B^{-1})\varphi\leq u_\ell,
 \qquad R\leq\varphi.
\end{equation}
The second inequality follows already from $r_{j,N}\leq p_{j,N}\leq\varphi$.

It remains to include the finitely many earlier terms and to establish every defining envelope condition. Fix $b\geq1$ and apply the preceding construction with $B=2b$. Let $j$ and $R$ be as above. If $j>1$, let $p_0=P_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_{j-1})$ and $S=Q_{2b}(p_0,\varphi)$. Both are genuine by the finite-rooftop and stretching lemmas, and $S\leq\varphi$. For $j=1$ set $S=\varphi$. The potential $H=(R+S)/2$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. For a tail index use \eqref{eq:relative-tail-stretched-competitor} and $S\leq\varphi$; for a prefix index use its counterpart for $S$ and $R\leq\varphi$. In either case,
\[
 b^{-1}H+(1-b^{-1})\varphi\leq u_\ell
 \quad\hbox{for every }\ell\geq1.
\]
For $b=1$ this first proves that $h=P_\theta(\inf_\ell u_\ell)$ is genuine. For general $b$ the left side is a competitor for $h$, so the same inequality with $h$ in place of every $u_\ell$ supplies the defining $b$-condition. Also $h\leq\varphi$. Thus $h\in\mathcal E_\varphi$.

Finally $h\leq u_j$ for all $j$, hence $h\leq u\leq\varphi$ by the almost-everywhere limit and canonical representatives. Upward stability proves $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$. Restoring the common translation proves the lemma as stated.
\end{proof}

The prefix and tail step matters: extracting a subsequence of finite rooftops does not allow one to rename the number of their obstacles. The proof above fixes a summable error sequence first, proves a lower bound uniform in the finite endpoint $N$, and then restores every term of that extracted sequence. It makes no assertion about the rooftop of the entire original sequence before extraction.

Once a common lower envelope is available, a single integral criterion gives convergence in capacity. Its proof uses zero domination and allows infinite masses.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-common-envelope-criterion}
Suppose $u_j\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and, for a fixed $h\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and constant $C$,
\[
 h\leq u_j\leq\varphi+C,\qquad
 u_j\longrightarrow u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\quad\hbox{in }L^1(X).
\]
If
\[
 I_j:=\int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mu_{u_j}\longrightarrow0,
\]
then $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $u_j\to u$ in capacity. No upper mass bound is part of this criterion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The bounds pass to $u$ almost everywhere along an extraction and then to canonical representatives. Upward stability gives $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$. Translate the entire family and its bounds by a common constant to make all potentials nonpositive.

It suffices to obtain capacity convergence along a further extraction of every subsequence. Extract so that $I_j\leq2^{-j}$ and $u_j\to u$ almost everywhere for smooth volume. Form
\[
 p_{j,N}=P_\theta(u_j,\ldots,u_N),\qquad
 p_j=P_\theta\left(\inf_{\ell\geq j}u_\ell\right).
\]
Every envelope lies above $h$ and below $\varphi+C$, so is genuine and belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$. For the finite envelope, partition its contact set by the first index $\ell$ with $p_{j,N}=u_\ell$. Ordered contact on each part gives
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mu_{p_{j,N}}
 \leq\sum_{\ell=j}^N I_\ell\leq2^{1-j}.
\]
Only positive integrals are used here, even if a rooftop has infinite mass.

As $N\to\infty$, $p_{j,N}\downarrow p_j$. As $j\to\infty$, the $p_j$ increase to a genuine canonical limit $p$. Both convergences hold in capacity by monotone truncation with the fixed lower bound $h$. Also $h\leq p\leq u\leq\varphi+C$; the upper inequality follows first from almost-everywhere convergence of the extracted $u_j$. Thus $p\in\mathcal E_\varphi$.

Apply \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} first as $N\to\infty$ and then as $j\to\infty$, with the bounded quasi-continuous weights displayed above. The weights converge in capacity because the exponential is Lipschitz on a common upper half-line. We obtain
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^p-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mu_p=0,
 \qquad \mu_p(\{p<u\})=0.
\]
The second equality follows off the poles, which the extended measures ignore. The relative zero-domination lemma gives $p=u$, without a finiteness hypothesis on $\mu_p$.

Finally $U_j=(\sup_{\ell\geq j}u_\ell)^*$ decreases to $u$ by Hartogs' lemma and the almost-everywhere extraction. Monotone capacity convergence applies to $p_j\uparrow u$ and $U_j\downarrow u$. The inequalities $p_j\leq u_j\leq U_j$ prove capacity convergence on this extraction, and hence on the original sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{Dominated continuity}

Use the manifold, backgrounds, reference, measure notation and positive constant $\kappa$ of \cref{sec:relative-common-envelope-setting}. Capacity is computed with the fixed Hermitian form $\omega$, and $L^1(X)$ uses smooth positive volume.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity}
Under \eqref{eq:relative-positive-lower-volume}, suppose $u_j\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, $u_j\to u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\mu_{u_j}\leq\nu$ for a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$. Then
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_\varphi,
 \qquad u_j\longrightarrow u\text{ in capacity},
 \qquad \mu_{u_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_u\leq\nu.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with any subsequence. By \cref{lem:dominated-relative-rooftop-extraction}, a further subsequence has a common lower envelope $h\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, and $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$. The envelope identity and Hartogs' lemma give a common upper bound $u_j\leq\varphi+C$.

After a common translation, assume $u_j,u\leq0$. The fixed non-pluripolar integration lemma gives
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{u_j}
 \leq\int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mathrm{d}\nu
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \cref{prop:relative-common-envelope-criterion} proves capacity convergence along this extraction. For the raw measures, the common lower bound gives
\[
 \mu_{u_j}(\{u_j\leq-T\})
 \leq\nu(\{h\leq-T\})\longrightarrow0
 \quad\hbox{uniformly in }j.
\]
The limit on the right uses finiteness of $\nu$ and its vanishing on the pole set of the genuine $h$. The raw-tail theorem \cref{thm:undamped-tail-continuity} now gives weak convergence to $\mu_u$. Weak limits preserve domination by the fixed finite $\nu$. Every original subsequence has a further subsequence with these conclusions and the same limit $u$, so both convergences hold for the original sequence.
\end{proof}

The argument uses $v_-(\theta)>0$ only to prevent the stretched comparison envelopes from collapsing. It never uses $v_+(\theta)<+\infty$.
