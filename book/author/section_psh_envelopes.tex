\section{Obstacle envelopes}
\label{sec:obstacle-envelopes}\index{Envelope!obstacle}

Given an obstacle function, one looks for the largest $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function lying below it. The local upper-envelope theorem constructs this envelope as soon as the competitors have a common upper bound, and normalized compactness provides such a bound for an obstacle that is finite almost everywhere or prescribed on a non-pluripolar compact set. Letting the obstacles $\min(u+C,0)$ increase with $C$ produces the envelope $P_\theta[u]$ of the ordinary singularity type of $u$.

A smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ is called \emph{big} if there are $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $\delta>0$ such that
\[
 \theta+\ddc\rho\geq\delta\omega.
\]
Here $\omega$ is any Hermitian form; changing it changes $\delta$ but not the condition. The definition does not require $\theta$ to be closed.\index{Big form}

Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. For an extended-real function $f$ on $X$, defined at least outside a pluripolar set (the \emph{obstacle}), write
\[
 P_\theta(f)=\left(\sup\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):v\leq f
                      \ \hbox{q.e.}\}\right)^*.
\]
As in \cref{sec:psh-local-capacity}, q.e.\ (quasi-everywhere) means outside a pluripolar set. The functions $v$ in the supremum are called \emph{competitors}; the empty supremum is $-\infty$. For obstacles $f_1,\ldots,f_N$, we write $P_\theta(f_1,\ldots,f_N)=P_\theta(\min_if_i)$; when the $f_i$ belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$, this is their \emph{rooftop envelope}. For an upper semicontinuous obstacle, the pointwise constraint $v\leq f$ gives the same competitors: by the local submean inequality and upper semicontinuity of $f$, a quasi-plurisubharmonic function lying below $f$ almost everywhere lies below it everywhere.

If the family of competitors is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, then $P_\theta(f)\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. To apply the local upper-envelope theorem while keeping the exact lower bound $-\theta$, fix a constant positive form $\kappa$ in local coordinates. For each $\epsilon>0$, shrink the coordinate neighborhood and choose a constant form $\theta'$ with $-\epsilon\kappa\leq\theta-\theta'\leq\epsilon\kappa$. A smooth function $r$ with $\ddc r=\theta'+\epsilon\kappa$ makes every competitor plus $r$ plurisubharmonic, so the local envelope theorem gives $\theta+\ddc P_\theta(f)\geq-2\epsilon\kappa$ there. Letting $\epsilon$ tend to zero proves the exact bound. The same local theorem shows that the regularization changes the supremum only on a pluripolar set.

The following observation gives the upper bound for an unbounded obstacle. Suppose that $f\leq A$, for a real constant $A$, on a Borel set $E$ with $\operatorname{Vol}(E)=\int_E\mathrm{d}V>0$, where $\mathrm{d}V$ is the volume form fixed in \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}. If $h$ is a competitor, put $\widetilde h=h-\sup_Xh$. By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness},
\[
 (\sup_Xh-A)\operatorname{Vol}(E)
 \leq\int_E|\widetilde h|\,\mathrm{d}V\leq C_\theta
 \quad\hbox{whenever }\sup_Xh>A.
\]
Thus $\sup_Xh\leq A+C_\theta/\operatorname{Vol}(E)$ for every competitor. In particular, the competitors for a measurable obstacle that is finite almost everywhere have a common upper bound: apply this to a sublevel set $\{f\leq A\}$ of positive volume.

An obstacle bound on a non-pluripolar compact set also bounds every competitor globally, even when that set has zero smooth volume.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:compact-obstacle-upper-bound}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $K\subseteq X$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $w\in C(K,\mathbb R)$. There is a constant $C=C(\theta,K,w)$ such that every $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfying $a\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$ has $\sup_X a\leq C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise there are such functions $a_j$ with
\[
 M_j:=\sup_Xa_j\geq 2^j+\max_Kw.
\]
The normalized functions $v_j=a_j-M_j\leq0$ have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms by \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}. Choose $A>0$ with $\theta\leq A\omega$. The partial sums
\[
 s_N=\sum_{j=1}^N2^{-j}v_j
\]
decrease, have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms, and satisfy $\ddc s_N\geq-A\omega$. By \cref{cor:decreasing-limit}, their limit $s$ is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function; in particular, $s$ is not identically $-\infty$.

Let $N_j\subseteq K$ be a pluripolar set outside which $a_j\leq w$ on $K$. On $K\setminus\bigcup_jN_j$ one has $v_j\leq-2^j$ for every $j$, hence $s=-\infty$. The pole set $\{s=-\infty\}$ of the quasi-plurisubharmonic function $s$ is pluripolar, and so is the countable union of the $N_j$. Their union would contain all of $K$, contradicting the hypothesis on $K$.
\end{proof}

Suppose that $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is nonempty. Then the envelope of the zero obstacle,
\[
 V_\theta=P_\theta(0),
\]
belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$: the competitors are bounded above by zero, and they include $u-\sup_Xu$ for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. In particular,
\[
 u-\sup_Xu\leq V_\theta\qquad(u\in\PSH(X,\theta)).
\]
A potential $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has \emph{minimal singularities} if $u\preceq v$ for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.\index{Minimal singularity} By the last inequality, $V_\theta$ has minimal singularities, and every potential $v$ with minimal singularities satisfies $v\sim V_\theta$. If $\theta\geq0$, then $0$ is a competitor, and hence $V_\theta=0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-below-envelope}
Let $\eta$ be a smooth semipositive real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. If an obstacle $f$ is finite almost everywhere and bounded below quasi-everywhere by a constant, then $P_\eta(f)$ is a bounded $\eta$-plurisubharmonic function.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\eta\geq0$, constants are $\eta$-plurisubharmonic, so the constant lower bound of $f$ is a competitor. The finite-sublevel argument above gives a common upper bound for all competitors. Hence $P_\eta(f)$ is an $\eta$-plurisubharmonic function lying between these two constants.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:continuous-obstacle-envelope}
If $f\in C^0(X)$, then $P_\omega(f)$ is continuous. Moreover,
\[
 \|P_\omega(f)-P_\omega(g)\|_\infty\leq\|f-g\|_\infty
 \quad(f,g\in C^0(X)).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The constant $\min_Xf$ is a competitor, so $p=P_\omega(f)$ is bounded. The local envelope theorem gives $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere, and continuity of $f$ makes this inequality hold everywhere. By \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization}, there are smooth $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions $v_j\downarrow p$. Fix $\epsilon>0$. The continuous functions $v_j-f$ decrease to a function that is at most zero, so $v_j\leq f+\epsilon$ everywhere for all large $j$; otherwise the decreasing compact sets $\{v_j-f\geq\epsilon\}$ would have nonempty intersection. Hence $v_j-\epsilon$ is a competitor, and
\[
 p\leq v_j\leq p+\epsilon.
\]
Thus $v_j\to p$ uniformly, which proves continuity. The last inequality follows from the monotonicity of $P_\omega$ in the obstacle and the identity $P_\omega(g+c)=P_\omega(g)+c$ for constants $c$, applied with $c=\|f-g\|_\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:singularity-envelope}\index{Envelope!ordinary singularity}\index{Model potential!envelope fixed point}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, set
\[
 P_\theta[u]
 =\left(\lim_{C\to+\infty}P_\theta(u+C,0)\right)^*
 =\left(\sup\{h\in\PSH(X,\theta):h\preceq u,\ h\leq0\}\right)^*.
\]
We call $u$ an \emph{envelope fixed point} if $P_\theta[u]=u$.
For $F\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$, replacing the constraint $h\leq0$ by $h\leq F$ defines $P_{\theta,F}[u]$.
\end{definition}

The two expressions agree, because the family in the second one is the increasing union over $C$ of the families defining $P_\theta(u+C,0)$. They do not change if zero is replaced by $V_\theta$, since every $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function below zero lies below $V_\theta$. The competitors include $u-\sup_Xu$ and are bounded above by zero, so $P_\theta[u]\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $u-\sup_Xu\leq P_\theta[u]\leq0$. In particular, $u\preceq P_\theta[u]$; this does not assert that $P_\theta[u]-u$ is bounded.

Directly from the definition, $P_\theta[u]$ depends only on the ordinary singularity type of $u$, and
\[
 u\preceq v\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[v].
\]
Replacing the condition $h\preceq u$ by $h\sim_{\mathcal I}u$ gives the multiplier-ideal envelope $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ of \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope}. This is a different operator; its relation to $P_\theta[u]$ is studied in \cref{chap:hermitian-igood}.

For a bounded obstacle in a big background, exceptional sets in the competitor inequality can be removed by a small singular perturbation.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:big-pointwise-competitor}
Let $\theta$ be big, let $f$ be a bounded function on $X$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. Then there are $u_t\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, $t>0$, such that $u_t\leq f$ everywhere and $u_t\to u$ almost everywhere as $t\downarrow0$.
\end{lemma}
\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\kappa\omega$ for some $\kappa>0$. The exceptional set $E=\{u>f\}$ is pluripolar. The global form of Josefson's theorem on compact complex manifolds gives a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\chi$ with $\chi=-\infty$ on $E$; see \cite[Theorem 1.1.5]{SGPT}. After rescaling and adding a constant, we may assume that $\chi\leq0$ and $\ddc\chi\geq-\omega$. Subtract a constant from $\rho$ so that $\rho\leq\inf_X f$. With $s=t/\kappa$, put
\[
 u_t=\frac{u+t\chi+s\rho}{1+s}.
\]
All three functions in the numerator are finite almost everywhere, so $u_t$ is not identically $-\infty$. Moreover,
\[
 \theta+\ddc u_t
 =\frac{\theta+\ddc u+t\ddc\chi+s(\theta+\ddc\rho)}{1+s}
 \geq0.
\]
Outside $E$, both $u$ and $\rho$ are at most $f$, while $\chi\leq0$, so $u_t\leq f$. On $E$ the numerator is $-\infty$. Finally, $u_t\to u$ wherever all three functions are finite, which is a set of full measure.
\end{proof}

A potential with minimal singularities stays away from the poles of a strictly positive potential with analytic singularities, in the following sense.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:interior-proper-gap}
Let $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have analytic singularities with smooth remainder and satisfy $\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\varepsilon\omega$ for some $\varepsilon>0$. Set $\Omega=\{\rho>-\infty\}$. If $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has minimal singularities and is continuous on $\Omega$, then $\varphi-\rho$ is continuous, bounded below, and proper on $\Omega$: all its sublevel sets are compact in $\Omega$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Adding constants to $\rho$ and $\varphi$ does not affect the assertions, so we may assume that $\rho\leq0$ and $\sup_X\varphi=0$. Since $\varphi$ has minimal singularities, there is a constant $C$ with $\varphi\geq V_\theta-C$. Choose $M\geq0$ with $\theta\geq-M\omega$. If $a\in(0,1)$ is sufficiently close to one, then
\[
  \theta+\ddc(a\rho)
  =a(\theta+\ddc\rho)+(1-a)\theta
  \geq\bigl(a\varepsilon-(1-a)M\bigr)\omega>0.
\]
Thus $a\rho$ is a competitor for $V_\theta$, and therefore
\[
  0\geq\varphi\geq V_\theta-C\geq a\rho-C,
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-gap-proper}
  \varphi-\rho\geq(1-a)(-\rho)-C.
\end{equation}
By hypothesis $\varphi$ is continuous on $\Omega$, and $\rho$ is smooth there because it has analytic singularities with smooth remainder. Hence $\varphi-\rho$ is continuous on $\Omega$, and by \eqref{eq:interior-gap-proper} it is bounded below. Since $\rho\to-\infty$ at $X\setminus\Omega$, the sets $\{\rho\geq-c\}$ are compact subsets of $\Omega$, and \eqref{eq:interior-gap-proper} places every sublevel set of $\varphi-\rho$ in one of them. Being closed in $\Omega$, each sublevel set is therefore compact.
\end{proof}
