\section{Compactness with a common lower barrier}

We return to a semipositive big background $\theta$. Convergence of potentials in $L^1$ does not by itself imply convergence of their Monge--Amp\`ere measures. We give a criterion for convergence in capacity when the potentials are bounded above and stay above a reference admitting a smaller-background envelope, up to a fixed constant. This common lower barrier ensures that all finite and infinite rooftop envelopes of the sequence are $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. A small error integral of each potential against its own Monge--Amp\`ere measure bounds the corresponding error of the rooftops by a summable tail. Weighted lower semicontinuity passes this bound to the limit, and singular domination identifies the limit of the lower envelopes with the $L^1$ limit. Squeezing the sequence between lower and upper envelopes then gives convergence in capacity, and a domination of the measures also controls their mass on deep sublevels.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:common-barrier-criterion}\label{prop:lower-barrier-criterion}\index{Barrier!common lower bound}
Let $\theta\geq0$ be big. Suppose $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfies $P_{\delta\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty$ for some $0<\delta<1$. Let $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy, for a fixed $C$,
\[
 \varphi-C\leq u_j\leq C,\qquad u_j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1(X),
 \qquad u\preceq\varphi.
\]
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-barrier-self-error}
 I_j:=\int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,
               \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
then $u_j\to u$ in capacity.
\end{theorem}

The condition $u\preceq\varphi$ holds if $u_j\leq\varphi+C$ for all $j$. The approximants themselves need not have the singularity type of $\varphi$; they may be bounded while $\varphi$ is unbounded.

\begin{proof}
The canonical representative of the limit satisfies $\varphi-C\leq u\leq C$, hence $u\sim\varphi$. It suffices to show that every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in capacity. We may therefore assume that $I_j\leq2^{-j}$ and that $u_j\to u$ almost everywhere. For $N\geq j$ define
\[
 p_{j,N}=P_\theta(\min\{u_j,\ldots,u_N\}),\qquad
 p_j=P_\theta\left(\inf_{\ell\geq j}u_\ell\right).
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
All these envelopes lie between $\varphi-C$ and $C$. The contact theorem and ordered contact give an inequality for the finite rooftops. Partition the contact set of $p_{j,N}$ according to the first index $\ell$ for which $p_{j,N}=u_\ell$. On the corresponding piece, the measure of $p_{j,N}$ is bounded by that of $u_\ell$, and $\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}=\mathrm{e}^{u_\ell}$. Therefore
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,
            \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p_{j,N})
 \leq\sum_{\ell=j}^N I_\ell\leq2^{1-j}.
\]
Only positive measures enter this argument, so it applies also when their total masses are infinite.

As $N\to\infty$, $p_{j,N}$ decreases to $p_j$. Indeed, the limit lies below every finite minimum, hence below the infimum quasi-everywhere, and every competitor for $p_j$ lies below every finite rooftop. The sequence $p_j$ increases, and its canonical limit $p$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. By \cref{lem:monotone-psh-capacity}, both monotone limits hold in capacity. Applying \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} to the bounded nonnegative weights $|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^u|$, first in $N$ and then in $j$, we obtain
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^p-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p)=0.
\]
The inequalities $p_j\leq u_\ell$ for all $\ell\geq j$ imply $p\leq u$, first almost everywhere and then for the canonical representatives. Since $\varphi-C\leq p\leq u$, also $p\sim u\sim\varphi$. Off the pole sets, $\mathrm{e}^p<\mathrm{e}^u$ exactly on $\{p<u\}$, so $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p)(\{p<u\})=0$. Since $P_{\delta\theta}(\varphi)-C$ is a competitor for $P_{\delta\theta}(p)$, the latter is not identically $-\infty$. \Cref{thm:singular-strict-domination}, applied with coefficient zero, gives $p\geq u$, and hence $p=u$.

Finally, by Hartogs' lemma and the almost-everywhere convergence, $U_j=(\sup_{\ell\geq j}u_\ell)^*$ decreases to $u$. The inequalities $p_j\leq u_j\leq U_j$, together with the convergence in capacity of both monotone sequences, prove the claim.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:common-barrier-continuity}
In the setting of \cref{thm:common-barrier-criterion}, suppose either
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)\leq\nu$ for a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$; or
\item $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)\leq A\omega_{w_j}^n$, where $A$ is fixed and $w_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ are uniformly bounded and converge in capacity to a bounded $w$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $u_j\to u$ in capacity, all the measures $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)$ and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$ have finite mass, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)
 \rightharpoonup\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In the first case, \cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals}, applied to the exponentials $\mathrm{e}^{u_j}$, proves \eqref{eq:common-barrier-self-error}. In the second case we use \cref{lem:moving-bounded-integrals} instead, since these exponentials are bounded and have a common lower bound for their curvature. In both cases the criterion gives convergence in capacity.

The masses are uniformly bounded by those of the dominating measures. For the deep sublevels we use $u_j\geq\varphi-C$, which gives $\{u_j<-T\}\subset\{\varphi<C-T\}$. In the first case the masses of these sublevels are at most $\nu(\{\varphi<C-T\})$, which tends to zero as $T\to\infty$ because $\nu$ does not charge the pole set of $\varphi$. In the second case \cref{lem:bounded-mixed-capacity} bounds them by $C'\operatorname{Cap}_\omega(\{\varphi<C-T\})$, which also tends to zero. The tail criterion \cref{thm:undamped-tail-continuity}, in the general-background form of \cref{thm:general-background-damping}, now gives finiteness of the limiting measure and weak convergence.
\end{proof}

When no common lower barrier is available, one has to be constructed in a relative envelope class; this is done in \cref{sec:general-measure-relative}.
