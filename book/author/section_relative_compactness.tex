\section{Compactness with a common lower barrier}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ on $X$. Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form; additional positivity hypotheses are stated in each result. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\eta)$, put $\eta_u=\eta+\ddc u$ and $\mathrm{MA}_\eta(u)=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\eta(u)$, the extended product of \cref{chap:products}. For bounded potentials this is the bounded product. Use the obstacle envelope $P_\theta$ of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}; a star denotes upper semicontinuous regularization. Write $u\preceq v$ if $u\leq v+C$ for some real $C$, and $u\simeq v$ if both inequalities hold. Norms and almost-everywhere limits without another named measure use $\mathrm{d}V$. Capacity means $\operatorname{Cap}_\omega$ of \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity}.

An existence proof usually produces convergence in $L^1$ before it produces convergence in capacity. We now bridge these topologies when all potentials stay within a fixed bounded distance of one reference. The proof keeps the local bounded estimates separate from the singular domination argument. Neither bounded mass nor a uniform positive lower mass is assumed.

We first specify one further standard local input. Cegrell's fixed-measure convergence lemma says that a uniformly bounded sequence of plurisubharmonic functions converging in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ converges in $L^1(\nu)$ on compact subsets for each finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$ there. The usual formulation allowing extraction gives the same conclusion: apply it to every subsequence, extract almost-everywhere convergence for smooth volume, and use the subsequence criterion for convergence. This is the classical fixed-measure argument used in \cite[Section 11.1.2]{GZ17}; see also the explicit extraction in \cite[Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.2]{KN22}. We retain this local lemma as a standard input. The Hermitian arguments below are proved here.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:fixed-npp-integrals}
Let $\nu$ be a finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$. If uniformly bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $a_j,a$ have a common smooth lower curvature bound and $a_j\to a$ in $L^1(X)$, then
\[
 \int_X|a_j-a|\,\mathrm{d}\nu\longrightarrow0.
\]
If instead $u_j,u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have a common upper bound and $u_j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$, then, for every $b>0$,
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{bu_j}-\mathrm{e}^{bu}|\,\mathrm{d}\nu\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the first statement add a fixed smooth strictly plurisubharmonic correction on each of finitely many coordinate balls. The resulting functions are uniformly bounded and plurisubharmonic. Apply the local input on smaller compact balls covering $X$ and sum the integrals; the corrections cancel in the difference.

For the second statement choose $A\omega\geq\theta$. If $u_j\leq C$, the convex chain rule gives
$\ddc \mathrm{e}^{bu_j}\geq-bA\mathrm{e}^{bC}\omega$, also for unbounded $u_j$ by bounded truncation. Thus $\mathrm{e}^{bu_j}$ are uniformly bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions in one lower curvature class. Their $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ convergence follows from the Lipschitz bound $b\mathrm{e}^{bC}$ for the exponential on $(-\infty,C]$. Apply the first statement. We assign the value zero to the exponential at a pole.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-mixed-capacity}
Fix a Hermitian metric $\omega$, a finite $A\geq0$ and an integer $0\leq k\leq n$. All positive mixed measures
\[
 \omega_{v_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{v_k}\wedge\omega^{n-k},
 \qquad \|v_i\|_\infty\leq A,
 \quad v_i\in\PSH(X,\omega),
\]
are bounded above on Borel sets by $C_A\operatorname{Cap}_\omega$. In particular their total masses are bounded. Their integrals against uniformly bounded functions tending to zero in capacity tend uniformly to zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Translate the $v_i$ so that $0\leq v_i\leq2A$, and put
$q=(v_1+\cdots+v_k)/B$, where $B\geq\max\{2k,2kA,1\}$.
Then $0\leq q\leq1$ and
\[
 \omega_q=B^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^k\omega_{v_i}+(1-k/B)\omega.
\]
All coefficients are positive. Expanding $\omega_q^n$ bounds the indicated mixed product by a constant multiple of $\omega_q^n$. The definition of capacity proves the claim. The case $k=0$ follows by testing with zero. For a bounded function $z$, split its integral into $\{|z|>t\}$ and its complement; first use capacity convergence for fixed $t>0$, and then let $t\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:moving-bounded-integrals}
Let $a_j,a,w_j,w\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be uniformly bounded. If $a_j\to a$ in $L^1(X)$ and $w_j\to w$ in capacity, then, for $0\leq k\leq n$,
\[
 \int_X|a_j-a|\,\omega_{w_j}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
The same conclusion holds when the $a_j$ have any fixed smooth lower curvature bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We give the torsion calculation behind \cite[Lemma 2.3]{KN22}. By the subsequence criterion, we may first extract $a_j\to a$ almost everywhere for smooth volume. The upper tail envelopes
$A_j=(\sup_{\ell\geq j}a_\ell)^*$ decrease to $a$: the local Hartogs lemma gives the upper inequality for the limit, and almost-everywhere convergence gives the reverse one. Bounded monotone capacity convergence therefore implies
\[
 \alpha_j:=\max(a_j,a)\longrightarrow a\quad\text{in capacity}.
\]
The part $\alpha_j-a$ of $|a_j-a|=(\alpha_j-a)+(\alpha_j-a_j)$ tends to zero against the moving products by \cref{lem:bounded-mixed-capacity}.

Put $h_j=\alpha_j-a_j\geq0$. We compare its integrals for two indices $j,\ell$. The case $k=0$ is the assumed $L^1$ convergence. For $k\geq1$, put $z=w_j-w_\ell$ and
\[
 T=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\omega_{w_j}^{s}\wedge
       \omega_{w_\ell}^{k-1-s}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.
\]
The sum includes $s=0$. Factorization and compact Stokes give
\begin{align*}
 &\int_Xh_j\bigl(\omega_{w_j}^{k}-\omega_{w_\ell}^{k}\bigr)
                      \wedge\omega^{n-k}\\
 &\hspace{20mm}=\int_Xh_j\ddc z\wedge T
 =\int_Xz\,\ddc(h_jT).
\end{align*}
Here the full product rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moving-measure-torsion}
 \ddc(h_jT)=\ddc h_j\wedge T+\mathrm{d}h_j\wedge\dc T
             -\dc h_j\wedge \mathrm{d}T+h_j\ddc T.
\end{equation}
The last term is retained. It need not vanish on a Hermitian manifold.

We estimate the four terms. Since $\ddc h_j=\omega_{\alpha_j}-\omega_{a_j}$, the absolute contribution of the first is at most
\[
 \int_X|z|(\omega_{\alpha_j}+\omega_{a_j})\wedge T.
\]
It tends to zero uniformly as $j,\ell\to\infty$ by \cref{lem:bounded-mixed-capacity}, since $w_j-w_\ell\to0$ in capacity. Differentiating each factor shows
$\mathrm{d}T=\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge R$ and $\dc T=\dc\omega\wedge R$, where $R$ is a positive sum of mixed products of degree $(n-2,n-2)$. Moreover $\ddc T$ is a sum of terms containing $\ddc\omega$ and mixed products of degree $n-2$, or $\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\dc\omega$ and mixed products of degree $n-3$. Terms with negative degree are absent. These smooth torsion factors are bounded in absolute value by fixed multiples of $\omega^2$ and $\omega^3$. As $h_j$ is uniformly bounded, the contribution of $h_j\ddc T$ also tends uniformly to zero by the same capacity estimate.

For the two gradient terms, the pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for a positive mixed product gives, for example,
\[
 \left|\int_Xz\,\mathrm{d}h_j\wedge\dc\omega\wedge R\right|^2
 \leq C\left(\int_X|z|\,\mathrm{d}h_j\wedge\dc h_j\wedge\omega\wedge R\right)
          \left(\int_X|z|\,\omega^2\wedge R\right).
\]
The second factor tends uniformly to zero. The first is uniformly bounded. For completeness, if $p$ is any of the uniformly bounded potentials and $S$ any positive mixed product of degree $n-1$ occurring here, then
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{d}p\wedge\dc p\wedge S
 =\frac12\int_Xp^2\ddc S-\int_Xp\,\ddc p\wedge S.
\]
Expand $\ddc S$ with all its torsion terms as above, and write $\ddc p=\omega_p-\omega$. Boundedness of $p$ and \cref{lem:bounded-mixed-capacity} bound both integrals uniformly. Finally,
$\mathrm{d}h_j\wedge\dc h_j\leq2\mathrm{d}\alpha_j\wedge\dc\alpha_j+2\mathrm{d}a_j\wedge\dc a_j$
as positive quadratic forms. This proves the asserted bound. The other gradient term has the same estimate. In dimension one $T=1$ and all torsion terms in \eqref{eq:moving-measure-torsion} vanish.

These bounded integration-by-parts and energy identities may first be written for smooth decreasing regularizations in the exact Hermitian background. The local Bedford--Taylor chain rule, its gradient products and quasi-continuity give the displayed identities for bounded potentials. Equivalently they follow from the bounded current calculus of \cref{chap:products}. Only the common bound on the potentials enters the estimates.

We have shown that the difference of the two $h_j$ integrals tends uniformly to zero when $j,\ell$ are large. For a fixed sufficiently large $\ell$, \cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals} gives
\[
 \int_Xh_j\,\omega_{w_\ell}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Then let $\ell\to\infty$. Together with the estimate for $\alpha_j-a$, this proves the result. If the $a_j$ have a larger fixed lower curvature bound, enlarge $\omega$ to $\Omega$ so that they are $\Omega$-plurisubharmonic. Apply the proved statement with $\Omega$; positivity bounds the original mixed measures by their $\Omega$ counterparts.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weighted-extended-lsc}\label{lem:big-weighted-lsc}
Let $\theta$ be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Suppose $u_j,u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have a common upper bound and $u_j\to u$ in capacity. If $b_j,b$ are nonnegative uniformly bounded quasi-continuous functions and $b_j\to b$ in capacity, then
\[
 \int_Xb\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\liminf_j\int_Xb_j\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j).
\]
Both sides may be infinite. No mass or bigness assumption is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Translate the potentials below zero and choose a Hermitian metric $\Omega\geq\theta$. For $T>0$, let $g_T:[-\infty,0]\to[0,1]$ be a continuous nondecreasing cutoff, zero on $[-\infty,-2T]$, one on $[-T,0]$, increasing to one at finite points. Put $v_j=\max(u_j,-2T)$ and $v=\max(u,-2T)$. These are uniformly bounded $\Omega$-plurisubharmonic functions and converge in capacity. Expand $(\theta+\ddc v_j)^n$ using $\theta+\ddc v_j=(\Omega+\ddc v_j)+(\theta-\Omega)$. Each smooth factor is bounded by a fixed power of $\Omega$ up to a constant. Thus the signed terms have total variations controlled by positive bounded mixed measures, and \cref{lem:bounded-mixed-capacity} gives a common capacity bound for fixed $T$. Bounded Bedford--Taylor continuity gives weak convergence term by term. It also permits the weights $b_jg_T(v_j)$: their differences from the limiting weight tend to zero in capacity, and the common capacity bound makes their integrals tend to zero. For the fixed limiting weight, remove a set of arbitrarily small capacity, extend continuously from its complement, and control the discarded part by the same bound. Locality and the vanishing cutoff on the truncation level give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_Xb_jg_T(u_j)\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)
 &=\int_Xb_jg_T(v_j)(\theta+\ddc v_j)^n\\
 &\longrightarrow
 \int_Xbg_T(u)\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u).
 \end{split}
\]
The first integral is nonnegative and bounded above by the corresponding uncut integral, although the bounded product before multiplication may be signed. Let $T\to\infty$ and use monotone convergence.
\end{proof}

Monotone capacity convergence also needs no mass estimate. A decreasing sequence with a genuine limit has that limit as a common lower bound. An increasing sequence of genuine potentials with a common upper bound has its first term as a lower bound and a genuine upper-regularized limit. Choose a Hermitian $\Omega\geq\theta$ and truncate at $-T$. Bounded monotone capacity convergence applies to the truncations. The deep sublevels of the fixed lower bound have capacity tending to zero, so letting $T\to\infty$ proves convergence for the original sequence.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:common-barrier-criterion}
Let $\theta\geq0$ be big. Suppose $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ admits a genuine $P_{\delta\theta}(\varphi)$ for some $0<\delta<1$. Let $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy, for a fixed $C$,
\[
 \varphi-C\leq u_j\leq\varphi+C,\qquad u_j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1(X).
\]
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-barrier-self-error}
 I_j:=\int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,
               \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
then $u_j\to u$ in capacity. No bound on the total Monge--Amp\`ere masses is part of this criterion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The canonical limit lies between $\varphi-C$ and $\varphi+C$. It suffices to prove capacity convergence after further extraction from an arbitrary subsequence. Choose an extraction with $I_j\leq2^{-j}$ and almost-everywhere convergence for smooth volume. For $N\geq j$ define
\[
 p_{j,N}=P_\theta(u_j,\ldots,u_N),\qquad
 p_j=P_\theta\left(\inf_{\ell\geq j}u_\ell\right).
\]
All these envelopes lie between $\varphi-C$ and $\varphi+C$. The contact theorem and ordered contact give a finite-rooftop inequality: partition the contact set according to the first index $\ell$ for which $p_{j,N}=u_\ell$. On that piece the measure of $p_{j,N}$ is bounded by that of $u_\ell$, and $\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}=\mathrm{e}^{u_\ell}$. Therefore
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,
            \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p_{j,N})
 \leq\sum_{\ell=j}^N I_\ell\leq2^{1-j}.
\]
This uses only positive measures, including when their total masses are infinite.

As $N\to\infty$, $p_{j,N}\downarrow p_j$; the limit is the envelope of the infimum because every competitor is below every finite rooftop. The sequence $p_j$ increases to a genuine canonical limit $p$. Both passages hold in capacity by the preceding monotone observation. Apply \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} to the bounded nonnegative weights $|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^u|$, first in $N$ and then in $j$. We obtain
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^p-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p)=0.
\]
The inequalities $p_j\leq u_\ell$ for all $\ell\geq j$ imply $p\leq u$, first almost everywhere and then for canonical representatives. Also $p\simeq u\simeq\varphi$. Off their poles, $\mathrm{e}^p<\mathrm{e}^u$ exactly on $\{p<u\}$, so
$\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p)(\{p<u\})=0$.
The competitor $P_{\delta\theta}(\varphi)-C$ makes $P_{\delta\theta}(p)$ genuine. Apply \cref{thm:singular-strict-domination} with coefficient zero to obtain $p\geq u$, and hence $p=u$.

Finally $U_j=(\sup_{\ell\geq j}u_\ell)^*$ decreases to $u$ by Hartogs' lemma and the almost-everywhere extraction. The inequalities
$p_j\leq u_j\leq U_j$, together with the monotone capacity convergence of both outer sequences, prove the claim.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower-barrier-criterion}
In \cref{thm:common-barrier-criterion}, one may replace the two bounds on $u_j$ by
\[
 \varphi-C\leq u_j\leq C
\]
provided the $L^1$ limit satisfies $u\preceq\varphi$. In particular the approximants may be bounded while $\varphi$ is unbounded.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The $L^1$ limit satisfies $u\geq\varphi-C$, so the additional hypothesis implies $u\simeq\varphi$. Repeat the finite rooftop construction in the theorem. Every $p_{j,N}$ remains genuine and lies between $\varphi-C$ and $C$, which is enough for contact support and for the bounded exponential weights. The resulting inequality is still
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,
             \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p_{j,N})\leq2^{1-j}.
\]
The limits $p_j$ exist by the common lower bound, and $p_j\leq u$ follows from $p_j\leq u_\ell$ for every $\ell\geq j$ and almost-everywhere convergence. Thus
$\varphi-C\leq p_j\leq u$, and all $p_j$ and their increasing limit $p$ have the same ordinary type as $\varphi$. Monotone capacity convergence and weighted lower semicontinuity give
$\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p)(\{p<u\})=0$. Singular domination applies to $(p,u)$ exactly as before, so $p=u$. The upper tail envelopes decrease to $u$, and the same sandwich gives capacity convergence. No singularity-type assertion was needed for the finite rooftops or for the individual approximants.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:common-barrier-continuity}
Under the geometric and common-barrier hypotheses of \cref{thm:common-barrier-criterion}, suppose either
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)\leq\nu$ for a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$; or
\item $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)\leq A\omega_{w_j}^n$, where $A$ is fixed and $w_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ are uniformly bounded and converge in capacity to a bounded $w$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $u_j\to u$ in capacity, all the measures have finite mass, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)
 \rightharpoonup\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In the first case \cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals} applied to exponentials proves \eqref{eq:common-barrier-self-error}. In the second case use \cref{lem:moving-bounded-integrals}, since the exponentials are bounded and have a common lower curvature bound. The criterion gives capacity convergence in both cases.

The masses are uniformly bounded by the corresponding dominating measures. For the deep tails use $u_j\geq\varphi-C$. In the first case their masses are at most $\nu(\{\varphi<C-T\})$, which tends to zero because $\nu$ ignores the pole set of $\varphi$. In the second case \cref{lem:bounded-mixed-capacity} bounds them by
$C'\operatorname{Cap}_\omega(\{\varphi<C-T\})$, which also tends to zero. Apply the raw-tail continuity theorem \cref{thm:undamped-tail-continuity} in its general-background form. It gives finiteness and weak convergence for the limiting measure as well.
\end{proof}

This reconstructs the common ordinary type case of \cite[Theorem 4.1]{ALS26}. Its reference need not be an envelope fixed point; the smaller-background condition is enough. A model reference becomes useful when an a priori estimate supplies the two uniform barriers. If such a lower barrier is unavailable, constructing one in a relative envelope class is an additional problem, treated separately in the general-measure theorem below.
