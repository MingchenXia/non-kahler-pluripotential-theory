\section{Entropy bounds}

For a Hermitian background and the zero reference, a lower bound for the $L^q$ norm of the density in the equation and an upper bound coming from a Gauduchon trace place $B_0$ between two logarithmic integrals. Dualizing gives two-sided entropy estimates, which identify the set where $H_0$ is finite.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hermitian-dual-entropy-bounds}
Let $\theta=\omega$ be a Hermitian form, and let $\varphi=0$. There are real constants $A$ and, for every $q>1$, $a_q$ such that every Borel probability measure $\nu$ satisfies
\[
 a_q+\frac1q\operatorname{Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu)
 \leq H_0(\nu)
 \leq A+n\operatorname{Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu).
\]
The inequalities include the value $+\infty$. Thus $H_0(\nu)<\infty$ if and only if $\nu$ has finite entropy, and a family of probability measures has uniformly bounded values of $H_0$ if and only if its entropies are uniformly bounded.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In dimension one, $\omega$ is a K\"ahler form, and \cref{prop:normalization-dual-entropy} gives $H_0=\log\int_X\omega+\operatorname{Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}$; since $\operatorname{Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}\geq-\log\mathrm{d}V(X)$, the assertions follow. Suppose that $n\geq2$.

For $G\in C(X)$, apply the lower bound for $\|F\|_q$ in \cref{thm:lp-potential-estimate} to a bounded solution with density $F=\mathrm{e}^{B_0(G)+G}$. It gives a constant $c_q>0$, independent of $G$, such that
\[
 B_0(G)\geq\log c_q-\frac1q\log\int_X\mathrm{e}^{qG}\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
For the upper bound, fix a smooth Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, rescaled so that $\int_X\omega\wedge\gamma^{n-1}=1$, and write
\[
 k_\gamma=\left(\frac{\gamma^n}{\mathrm{d}V}\right)^{(n-1)/n},
 \qquad m=\min_X k_\gamma>0.
\]
For smooth $G$, the trace bound \eqref{eq:gauduchon-c-bound} gives
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{B_0(G)/n}\int_X\mathrm{e}^{G/n}k_\gamma\,\mathrm{d}V\leq1.
\]
Uniform approximation and continuity of $B_0$ extend this inequality to continuous $G$. Consequently
\[
 B_0(G)\leq-n\log m-n\log\int_X\mathrm{e}^{G/n}\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]

Taking the defining suprema in the two scalar bounds and applying \cref{lem:entropy-variational} with $F=qG$ and $F=G/n$, respectively, proves both inequalities for every probability measure, including infinite values, with $a_q=\log c_q$ and $A=-n\log m$. The finite-domain and bounded-family assertions follow by taking $q=2$.
\end{proof}
