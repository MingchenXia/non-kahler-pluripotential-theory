\section{The mass determined by the functional}\label{sec:normalization-mass-quotient}

In a closed background, the information lost by the scalar functional can be described exactly: for the model references below, two functionals agree precisely when the masses agree. This also gives examples in which the supporting probabilities stay fixed while the potentials have different limits.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
In this section $X$ is a compact K\"ahler manifold and $\theta$ is any smooth closed big real $(1,1)$-form. Let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$, have small unbounded locus in the sense of \cref{sec:psh-singularity-types}, and have positive mass
\[
 m_\varphi=\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(\varphi)>0.
\]
Write $B_{\theta,\varphi}$ and $\mathcal A_{\theta,\varphi}$ for the normalization scalar and the supporting sets of the equation \eqref{eq:relative-normalization} in the ordinary type of $\varphi$, and put $Z_F=\int_X\mathrm{e}^F\,\mathrm{d}V$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:normalization-mass-quotient}
For every such pair $(\theta,\varphi)$ and every bounded measurable $F$, the equation in the ordinary type of $\varphi$ has a unique constant and a solution unique modulo constants. Its normalization scalar is
\[
 B_{\theta,\varphi}(F)=\log m_\varphi-\log Z_F.
\]
For continuous real $F$, its supporting set is
\[
 \mathcal A_{\theta,\varphi}(F)=\{\mathrm{e}^F\mathrm{d}V/Z_F\}.
\]
For two such pairs on the same $X$, with the same $\mathrm{d}V$ and possibly in different classes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalization-mass-distance}
 \sup_{F\in L^\infty}|B_{\theta,\varphi}(F)-B_{\theta',\psi}(F)|
 =|\log m_\varphi-\log m_\psi|.
\end{equation}
Thus an invariant of these pairs is determined by the whole functional if and only if it is constant on every fiber of the map $(\theta,\varphi)\mapsto m_\varphi$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Existence follows from \cref{cor:big-model-lp-type}. Indeed, a K\"ahler manifold has the bounded mass property by \cref{lem:kahler-modification-bounded-mass}, and since in a closed background all potentials of the ordinary type of $\varphi$ have the same mass $m_\varphi>0$, the reference satisfies the positive-volume model condition \eqref{eq:li-model-condition}. Uniqueness modulo constants is the classical theorem on equations with prescribed singularities in closed big classes, which applies under the model and small-unbounded-locus hypotheses; see the notes in \cref{sec:history-normalization}.

Every potential of the ordinary type of $\varphi$ has mass $m_\varphi$, so integrating the equation forces $cZ_F=m_\varphi$, which is the scalar formula. The supporting set follows from it as for \eqref{eq:normalization-closed-formula}. Subtracting the scalar formulas gives \eqref{eq:normalization-mass-distance}. Finally, $m_\varphi=\mathrm{d}V(X)\exp B_{\theta,\varphi}(0)$, so the functional and the mass determine each other, and an invariant factors through either one exactly when it is constant on the fibers of the mass map.
\end{proof}

For an ordinary divisorial type whose residual form $\beta$ is positive and closed, \cref{thm:ordinary-divisor-normalization} gives the same formula with mass $\int_X\beta^n$, since Stokes' theorem for bounded products in a closed background fixes the mass of every bounded $\beta$-plurisubharmonic function. This computation does not require the real weighted sum of divisors to have a model representative. If $\beta_j\to\beta$ uniformly, the masses converge by continuity of $\beta\mapsto\int_X\beta^n$; under the two-sided bounds $(1-\varepsilon_j)\beta\leq\beta_j\leq(1+\varepsilon_j)\beta$ one has the sharper inequalities $(1-\varepsilon_j)^n\int\beta^n\leq\int\beta_j^n\leq(1+\varepsilon_j)^n\int\beta^n$.
