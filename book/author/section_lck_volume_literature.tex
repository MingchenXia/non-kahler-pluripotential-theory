\section{Prescribed volume on Vaisman manifolds}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, with complex structure $J$. For a Hermitian metric $\omega$ and a real one-form $\lambda$, $\lambda^\sharp$ is the vector field metrically dual to $\lambda$. Lee classes $[\lambda]$ lie in real degree-one de Rham cohomology; the LCK and Vaisman conditions are those of \cref{def:lck}.

The Vaisman case has a prescribed-volume theorem, but its symmetry assumptions are essential. We record the precise statement from the original paper \cite[Theorem 4.1]{OV24Vaisman}, including Lee and anti-Lee invariance of the volume form.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lck-vaisman-volume}
Let $(X,\omega,\lambda)$ be a compact Vaisman manifold, with $\lambda\not\equiv0$, and let $\mu$ be a smooth positive volume form invariant under both the Lee field $\lambda^\sharp$ and the anti-Lee field $J\lambda^\sharp$. Assume
\[
 \int_X\mu=\int_X\omega^n.
\]
There is a unique Vaisman metric $\omega'$ with Lee class $[\lambda]$ and $(\omega')^n=\mu$.
\end{theorem}

The existence assertion uses the transverse Calabi--Yau theorem for the canonical foliation; we retain that theorem as an external input. The symmetry condition makes contraction of $\mu$ along the two distinguished vector fields into a basic transverse volume form. It cannot be removed: the Lee and anti-Lee fields of a Vaisman metric are Killing and hence preserve its volume, and their directions are determined by the complex structure. Thus an arbitrary smooth positive volume form is not admissible.

The basic uniqueness argument can be given independently of the transverse existence theory.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lck-basic-uniqueness}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a foliation on a connected compact manifold $M$ with transverse complex dimension $m\geq1$. Let $\eta_0,\eta_1$ be transverse K\"ahler forms and let $v$ be a smooth real basic function, meaning that it is constant on local leaves. Compute $\ddc v$ in transverse complex coordinates. Suppose
\[
 \eta_1=\eta_0+\ddc v,
 \qquad \eta_1^m=\eta_0^m.
\]
Then $v$ is constant and $\eta_1=\eta_0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The function $v$ is constant on each local plaque. On every transverse coordinate chart it satisfies
\[
 \ddc v\wedge\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\eta_1^j\wedge\eta_0^{m-1-j}=0.
\]
This is a linear elliptic equation with no zeroth-order term. Let $c=\max_Mv$. At a point where $v=c$, the strong maximum principle on a connected transverse chart makes $v$ equal to $c$ throughout that chart. Basicness makes it equal to $c$ on the corresponding foliation chart. Hence $\{v=c\}$ is nonempty, open and closed. Connectedness proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Chern--Ricci flatness and Vaisman rigidity}

Let $(X,J)$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$. For a Hermitian metric $\omega$, use the normalized Chern--Ricci form $\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega)=-\ddc\log\omega^n$, whose class is the first Bott--Chern class $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)$. Vanishing of this form is unaffected by its constant normalization. A strictly LCK metric $\omega$ has the Lee form $\lambda$ of \cref{def:lck}.

A later rigidity theorem uses exactly the Gauduchon condition discussed above. A compact complex manifold is called Calabi--Yau in the Bott--Chern sense if $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)=0$. This is a condition on the ordinary first Bott--Chern class; it is not the vanishing of the twisted class of an LCK form.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lck-chern-ricci-rigidity}
A compact strictly LCK metric that is both Gauduchon and Chern--Ricci flat is Vaisman.
\end{theorem}

The hypotheses concern the same metric. Vanishing of $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)$ gives a Chern--Ricci-flat representative in each Hermitian conformal class: if a smooth real function $h$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega)=\ddc h$ in this normalization, then
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\mathrm{e}^{h/n}\omega)
 =\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega)-\ddc h=0.
\]
Gauduchon's theorem gives a Gauduchon representative in the same conformal class. These two representatives need not coincide. The equation that makes them coincide is an additional condition, so the rigidity theorem does not establish the following conjecture from \cite{BO25LCK}.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:lck-calabi-yau-vaisman}
A compact complex manifold with $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)=0$ that admits a strictly LCK metric admits a Vaisman metric.
\end{conjecture}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
This formulation concerns existence of a Vaisman metric on the manifold, not parallelness of every LCK metric on it. It suggests a concrete equation problem: can one arrange both Chern--Ricci flatness and the Gauduchon condition inside an appropriate family of LCK structures? A general Hermitian prescribed-volume theorem alone does not preserve that family.
