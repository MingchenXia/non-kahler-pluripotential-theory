\subsection{Invariant volumes on Vaisman manifolds}
\label{subsec:lck-vaisman-volume}

The flows of the Lee and anti-Lee vector fields preserve a Vaisman metric. For a prescribed volume invariant under these flows, uniqueness for the smooth Hermitian equation forces the potential to be constant along them. Stokes' theorem in the transverse directions then fixes the normalizing constant, and changing the Lee form by the differential of the potential produces a Vaisman metric with the prescribed volume.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lck-vaisman-volume}\index{Vaisman metric!prescribed volume}
Let $\omega$ be a Vaisman metric on $X$ with Lee form $\lambda$, and let $\mu$ be a smooth positive volume form invariant under the flows of both the Lee field $\lambda^\sharp$ and the anti-Lee field $J\lambda^\sharp$. Assume
\[
 \int_X\mu=\int_X\omega^n.
\]
Then there is a unique Vaisman metric $\omega'$ with Lee class $[\lambda]$ and $(\omega')^n=\mu$.
\end{theorem}

We first describe the transverse geometry and the integration of transverse forms. Let $g$ be the Riemannian metric of $\omega$. Since $\lambda$ is parallel and not identically zero, it has constant squared norm $s=|\lambda|_g^2>0$. Write
\[
 A=s^{-1}\lambda^\sharp,\qquad B=JA,\qquad
 \tau=\frac{s}{4\pi},\qquad \eta=\kappa_\lambda.
\]
Thus $\lambda(A)=1$ and $\lambda(B)=0$. Since $\nabla\lambda=0$, the identity \eqref{eq:lck-complex-connection} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vaisman-normalized-form}
 \begin{split}
 \tau\omega&=\eta+\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c},\\
 \eta(U,JV)&=\frac1{4\pi}\bigl(sg(U,V)-\lambda(U)\lambda(V)-\lambda(JU)\lambda(JV)\bigr).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
To obtain these identities, use $\lambda^{\mathrm c}=-\lambda\circ J/(4\pi)$ and antisymmetrize $\nabla\lambda^{\mathrm c}$; the terms containing $\nabla\lambda$ vanish. In particular, $\eta$ is a closed semipositive $(1,1)$-form whose kernel is the span $\mathcal F$ of $A$ and $B$.

The field $A$ is parallel, and the same connection formula yields
\[
 \nabla_U B=\tfrac12\bigl(-JU+\lambda(U)B+\lambda(JU)A\bigr),
 \qquad \nabla_AJ=0.
\]
The first identity shows that $B$ is a Killing field and that $[A,B]=0$. The second shows that $A$ is holomorphic, and integrability of $J$ then shows that $B=JA$ is holomorphic. Their flows preserve $g$, $J$, $\lambda$ and $\eta$. Consequently $\mathcal F$ is a holomorphic foliation by complex curves, and $\eta$ induces a K\"ahler form on each local transverse space. A form is called \emph{horizontal} if its contractions with $A$ and $B$ vanish.

A smooth function $v$ is \emph{basic} for $\mathcal F$ if $Av=Bv=0$. For such a function, $\mathrm{d}v$, $\dc v$ and $\ddc v$ are horizontal and invariant under both flows; for $\ddc v$ this follows from Cartan's formula. Put $m=n-1$ and $\eta_v=\eta+\ddc v$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vaisman-basic-mass}
 \int_X\eta_v^m\wedge\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}
 =\int_X\eta^m\wedge\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\eta_v^m-\eta^m=\ddc v\wedge T$, where $T=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\eta_v^j\wedge\eta^{m-1-j}$ is closed and horizontal. Since $\mathrm{d}(\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c})=\lambda\wedge\eta$, the differential of $\dc v\wedge T\wedge\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}$ is $\ddc v\wedge T\wedge\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}$ plus a term containing the horizontal form $\dc v\wedge T\wedge\eta$ of degree $2m+1$, which vanishes because the transverse real dimension is $2m$. Stokes' theorem therefore proves \eqref{eq:vaisman-basic-mass}.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
The following transverse maximum principle identifies the metric once its scale has been fixed.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lck-basic-uniqueness}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a foliation of a connected compact manifold $M$ with a transverse holomorphic structure of complex dimension $m\geq1$. Let $\eta_0,\eta_1$ be transverse K\"ahler forms, and let $v$ be a smooth real basic function, with $\ddc v$ computed in transverse holomorphic coordinates. Suppose
\[
 \eta_1=\eta_0+\ddc v,
 \qquad \eta_1^m=\eta_0^m.
\]
Then $v$ is constant and $\eta_1=\eta_0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The function $v$ is constant on each local plaque. On every transverse coordinate chart it satisfies
\[
 \ddc v\wedge\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\eta_1^j\wedge\eta_0^{m-1-j}=0.
\]
This is a linear elliptic equation without zeroth-order term. Let $c=\max_Mv$. If $v=c$ at some point, the strong maximum principle on a connected transverse chart shows that $v=c$ throughout that chart, and since $v$ is basic, $v=c$ on the corresponding foliation chart. Hence $\{v=c\}$ is nonempty, open and closed, and connectedness of $M$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[of \cref{thm:lck-vaisman-volume}]
Replacing $\omega$ by $\tau\omega$ and $\mu$ by $\tau^n\mu$ does not change the Lee form or the assertion, and it makes the new constant $\tau$ equal to one. We may therefore assume $\tau=1$, so that $\omega=\eta+\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}$. Write $\mu=f\omega^n$; since $\mu$ and $\omega^n$ are invariant under both flows, the positive smooth function $f$ is basic.

\emph{Existence.} By \cref{thm:tw-smooth}, there are a unique smooth function $u$ and a unique constant $b$ such that
\[
 (\omega+\ddc u)^n=\mathrm{e}^b f\omega^n,
 \qquad \omega+\ddc u>0,\qquad \sup_Xu=0.
\]
Pullback by the flow of $A$ or of $B$ preserves the equation and the normalization, so uniqueness shows that $u$ is basic. Hence $\eta_u=\eta+\ddc u$ is a transverse K\"ahler form, and
\[
 (\omega+\ddc u)^n=n\eta_u^m\wedge\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}.
\]
Integrating over $X$ and using \eqref{eq:vaisman-basic-mass} and $\int_X\mu=\int_X\omega^n$ gives $b=0$.

Set $\lambda_u=\lambda-\mathrm{d}u$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vaisman-reconstructed-metric}
 \omega_u'=\eta_u+\lambda_u\wedge\lambda_u^{\mathrm c}.
\end{equation}
Here $\eta_u=\kappa_{\lambda_u}$ has kernel $\mathcal F$, while $\lambda_u(A)=1$ and $\lambda_u(B)=0$; together these facts give $\omega_u'>0$. Direct differentiation gives $\mathrm{d}\omega_u'=\lambda_u\wedge\omega_u'$. Since $u$ is basic, $A$ is a holomorphic Killing field for the Riemannian metric $g_u'$ of $\omega_u'$, and \eqref{eq:vaisman-reconstructed-metric} gives
\[
 g_u'(A,\cdot)=\frac1{4\pi}\lambda_u.
\]
Since $\lambda_u$ is closed, $\nabla^{g_u'}A$ is symmetric, whereas the Killing equation makes it skew-symmetric. Thus $A$ and $\lambda_u$ are parallel, so $\omega_u'$ is Vaisman with Lee class $[\lambda]$.

Finally, $\eta_u^m$ has top transverse degree, and every term of $\lambda_u\wedge\lambda_u^{\mathrm c}-\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}$ contains a horizontal one-form. Therefore
\[
 (\omega_u')^n
 =n\eta_u^m\wedge\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}
 =(\omega+\ddc u)^n=\mu.
\]

\emph{Uniqueness.} Let $\widehat\omega$ be another Vaisman solution, with Lee form $\widehat\lambda=\lambda+\mathrm{d}v$, and denote by $\widehat s,\widehat\tau,\widehat A,\widehat B,\widehat\eta$ the corresponding objects for $\widehat\omega$. The form $\eta+\widehat\eta$ is de Rham exact and semipositive. Its top power is nonnegative and has zero integral, so it vanishes everywhere, and $\eta+\widehat\eta$ has a nonzero kernel at every point. The kernel of a sum of two semipositive Hermitian forms is the intersection of their kernels. Since both kernels are complex lines, they coincide, and both metrics define the same foliation $\mathcal F$.

The nowhere vanishing holomorphic vector field $A-\mathrm iB$ trivializes the holomorphic tangent bundle of $\mathcal F$. The analogous field $\widehat A-\mathrm i\widehat B$ is a holomorphic multiple of it, and the multiple is constant because $X$ is compact. Write $\widehat A=aA+bB$ with $a,b\in\mathbb R$. Since $\widehat\lambda(\widehat A)=1$ and $\widehat\lambda(\widehat B)=0$,
\[
 \widehat A v=1-a,\qquad \widehat B v=b.
\]
Both fields are complete and $v$ is bounded, so $v$ cannot grow linearly along their flows; hence these constants vanish, and $a=1$, $b=0$. Thus $\widehat A=A$, $\widehat B=B$, and $v$ is basic.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
From \eqref{eq:vaisman-normalized-form} we obtain the contraction formulas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vaisman-volume-contraction}
 \iota_B\iota_A\widehat\omega^n
 =\frac{n}{4\pi\widehat\tau^n}\widehat\eta^m,
 \qquad
 \iota_B\iota_A(\omega_u')^n=\frac{n}{4\pi}\eta_u^m.
\end{equation}
Since the two volume forms are equal, $\widehat\eta^m=\widehat\tau^n\eta_u^m$. Since $\widehat\eta=\eta-\ddc v$ and $v$ is basic, integrating against $\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}$ and applying \eqref{eq:vaisman-basic-mass} twice gives $\widehat\tau^n=1$. Thus $\widehat\tau=1$ and $\widehat\eta^m=\eta_u^m$. Since $\widehat\eta-\eta_u=-\ddc(v+u)$, \cref{prop:lck-basic-uniqueness} shows that $v+u$ is constant. It follows that $\widehat\lambda=\lambda_u$, $\widehat\eta=\eta_u$ and $\widehat\omega=\omega_u'$.
\end{proof}

\section{Chern--Ricci flatness and Vaisman rigidity}

Return to an LCK metric $\omega$ on $X$ with Lee form $\lambda$. If $\omega$ is Gauduchon, then $\lambda$ is harmonic by \cref{prop:lck-gauduchon}. If $\omega$ is moreover Chern--Ricci flat, the Riemannian Ricci tensor can be expressed through $\lambda$ and its covariant derivative, and combining the contracted Bianchi identity with the Bochner formula forces this derivative to vanish.

We write $\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}=\operatorname{Ric}/(2\pi)$ for the normalized Chern--Ricci form, which represents the first Bott--Chern class $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)$, with $\operatorname{Ric}$ as in \cref{subsec:chern-ricci-maximal-time}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lck-chern-ricci-rigidity}\index{Chern--Ricci curvature!LCK rigidity}
A compact strictly LCK metric that is both Gauduchon and Chern--Ricci flat is Vaisman.
\end{theorem}

\input{author/section_lck_chern_ricci_proof}

In the theorem, both conditions are imposed on the same metric. If $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)=0$, each conformal class of Hermitian metrics contains a Chern--Ricci-flat metric: if a smooth real function $h$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega)=\ddc h$, then
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\mathrm{e}^{h/n}\omega)
 =\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega)-\ddc h=0.
\]
By Gauduchon's theorem, the same conformal class also contains a Gauduchon metric. The rigidity theorem applies when these two metrics coincide, and arranging this coincidence is one approach to the following conjecture.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:lck-calabi-yau-vaisman}
If $X$ satisfies $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)=0$ and admits a strictly LCK metric, then $X$ admits a Vaisman metric.
\end{conjecture}

The analytic problem is to achieve Chern--Ricci flatness and the Gauduchon condition simultaneously within a family of LCK metrics. The conjecture asks for one Vaisman metric; it does not assert that the Lee form of every LCK metric on $X$ is parallel.
