\section{Prescribed volume on Vaisman manifolds}

A Vaisman metric has two distinguished real vector fields: the Lee field $\lambda^\sharp$, metrically dual to its Lee form, and the anti-Lee field $J\lambda^\sharp$. Their flows preserve the metric. Prescribing volume within this class therefore leads to a Monge--Amp\`ere equation transverse to their canonical foliation, with a volume form invariant under both flows.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lck-vaisman-volume}
Let $(X,\omega,\lambda)$ be a compact Vaisman manifold, and let $\mu$ be a smooth positive volume form invariant under both the Lee field $\lambda^\sharp$ and the anti-Lee field $J\lambda^\sharp$. Assume
\[
 \int_X\mu=\int_X\omega^n.
\]
There is a unique Vaisman metric $\omega'$ with Lee class $[\lambda]$ and $(\omega')^n=\mu$.
\end{theorem}

We use the prescribed-volume theorem as a standard input. Its proof combines the transverse Calabi--Yau theorem with reconstruction of a Vaisman metric from its transverse K\"ahler form and Lee class. The invariance of $\mu$ makes its contraction along the two distinguished vector fields a basic transverse volume form. Moreover, the Lee field of a compact Vaisman manifold is determined by the complex structure up to a constant multiplier. Since this field and its $J$-transform are Killing for every Vaisman metric, the invariance condition is necessary.

The uniqueness argument for the transverse equation is elementary once the difference of the two transverse forms has a basic potential.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lck-basic-uniqueness}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a foliation on a connected compact manifold $M$ with transverse complex dimension $m\geq1$. Let $\eta_0,\eta_1$ be transverse K\"ahler forms and let $v$ be a smooth real basic function, meaning that it is constant on local leaves. Compute $\ddc v$ in transverse complex coordinates. Suppose
\[
 \eta_1=\eta_0+\ddc v,
 \qquad \eta_1^m=\eta_0^m.
\]
Then $v$ is constant and $\eta_1=\eta_0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The function $v$ is constant on each local plaque. On every transverse coordinate chart it satisfies
\[
 \ddc v\wedge\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\eta_1^j\wedge\eta_0^{m-1-j}=0.
\]
This is a linear elliptic equation with no zeroth-order term. Let $c=\max_Mv$. At a point where $v=c$, the strong maximum principle on a connected transverse chart makes $v$ equal to $c$ throughout that chart. Basicness makes it equal to $c$ on the corresponding foliation chart. Hence $\{v=c\}$ is nonempty, open and closed. Connectedness proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Chern--Ricci flatness and Vaisman rigidity}

For an LCK metric in Gauduchon gauge, the Lee form is harmonic. Chern--Ricci flatness then expresses the Riemannian Ricci tensor in terms of that form and its covariant derivative. Combining the contracted Bianchi identity with the Bochner formula forces the derivative to vanish.

Use $\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}=\operatorname{Ric}/(2\pi)$ for the normalized Chern--Ricci form representing $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)$. A manifold with $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)=0$ is called \emph{Calabi--Yau in the Bott--Chern sense}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lck-chern-ricci-rigidity}
A compact strictly LCK metric that is both Gauduchon and Chern--Ricci flat is Vaisman.
\end{theorem}

The hypotheses concern the same metric. Vanishing of $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)$ gives a Chern--Ricci-flat representative in each Hermitian conformal class: if a smooth real function $h$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega)=\ddc h$ in this normalization, then
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\mathrm{e}^{h/n}\omega)
 =\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega)-\ddc h=0.
\]
Gauduchon's theorem gives a Gauduchon representative in the same conformal class. The rigidity theorem applies when these representatives coincide. Arranging this coincidence is an approach to the following conjecture.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:lck-calabi-yau-vaisman}
A compact complex manifold with $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(X)=0$ that admits a strictly LCK metric admits a Vaisman metric.
\end{conjecture}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
This formulation concerns existence of a Vaisman metric on the manifold, not parallelness of every LCK metric on it. It suggests a concrete equation problem: can one arrange both Chern--Ricci flatness and the Gauduchon condition inside an appropriate family of LCK structures? A general Hermitian prescribed-volume theorem alone does not preserve that family.
