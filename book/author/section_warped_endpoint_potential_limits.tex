\subsubsection{Limits of the base potentials}
\label{sec:warped-endpoint-potential-limits}

The base potentials used in \cref{thm:warped-gauduchon-volume} to construct the minimizing balanced metrics converge uniformly at each finite end, and the limit solves the endpoint equation \eqref{eq:warped-continuous-endpoint-equation}. Both the compactness and the uniqueness needed here concern only the equation on $B$, whose background is closed.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:warped-endpoint-potential-limits}\index{Gauduchon metric!boundary potential limits}\index{Compactness!warped endpoint potentials}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:warped-unit-volume-completion}, let $\theta_j=b_j\kappa+L_jw\in\mathscr S$ converge in coefficients to a boundary form $\theta_0=b\kappa+L_0w$ at a finite end. Normalize the smooth base potentials $\phi_j$ of \cref{thm:warped-gauduchon-volume} by $\sup_B\phi_j=0$ and put $u_j=\phi_j\circ p_B$. Then the $u_j$ converge uniformly on $X$ to a continuous solution $u_0$ of \eqref{eq:warped-continuous-endpoint-equation} pulled back from $B$. For every $1\leq k\leq n$,
\[
 (\theta_j+\ddc u_j)^k\rightharpoonup
             (\theta_0+\ddc u_0)^k.
\]
Moreover, $u_0$ is the unique bounded solution of \eqref{eq:warped-continuous-endpoint-equation} that is pulled back from $B$ and has supremum zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $v_j=c_j/e_j$ and $\psi_j=\phi_j/b_j$. On $\mathscr S$ the equation for $\phi_j$ on $B$ becomes
\[
 (\kappa+\ddc\psi_j)^m=F_{v_j}\sigma,
 \qquad F_v=\frac1{Q(v)(1+va)},\qquad\sup_B\psi_j=0.
\]
At the end where $\theta_0=\theta_+(b)$, $v_j\to\infty$. Set $\epsilon=1/v$ and $H_\epsilon=\int_B\sigma/(a+\epsilon)$. Then $F_v=[H_\epsilon(a+\epsilon)]^{-1}$ and $H_\epsilon\to H_a$. At the end where $\theta_0=\theta_-(b)$, $v_j\to-1/M$. With $f=1-a/M$ and $t=v+1/M>0$, the denominator of $F_v$ is $Q(v)(f+ta)$, and $Q(v)\to H_-$. Thus the respective limits of $F_{v_j}$ are
\[
 F_+=\frac1{H_a a},\qquad F_-=\frac1{H_-(1-a/M)}.
\]
Choose $1<p<r$, where $r>1$ is the ratio $r_0$ or $r_M$ of that end. Integration over tubes as in the proof of \cref{prop:warped-continuous-endpoint-equation} gives $a^{-1}\in L^p(B,\sigma)$, respectively $f^{-1}\in L^p(B,\sigma)$. Near the first end $F_v\leq C/a$, and near the second $F_v\leq C/f$. Since the extreme sets have volume zero, dominated convergence gives $F_{v_j}\to F_\pm$ in $L^p(B,\sigma)$, and each limiting density has integral one. Since $\sigma/\kappa^m$ is smooth and strictly positive on compact $B$, the densities of the equations also have a common bound in $L^p(B,\kappa^m)$.

We apply \cref{thm:lp-uniform-compactness} to the fixed background $\kappa$: every subsequence of $(\psi_j)$ has a uniformly convergent further subsequence. Continuity of bounded products under uniform convergence identifies its limit with a normalized solution of $(\kappa+\ddc\psi_0)^m=F_\pm\sigma$. Since $\kappa$ is closed, it satisfies the Guan--Li condition, so \cref{uni-thm:GL-intro} makes this solution unique. Every cluster limit is consequently the same, which proves uniform convergence of the whole sequence. Since $b_j\to b>0$, we obtain $u_j\to u_0=(b\psi_0)\circ p_B$ uniformly.

To prove convergence of the products, note that on each coordinate ball the convergence of the background coefficients provides a common smooth function $q$, a quadratic polynomial, such that all $u_j+q$ are plurisubharmonic. These corrected potentials are uniformly bounded and converge uniformly. The continuity of bounded products under convergence in capacity (\cref{sec:psh-local-capacity}) gives weak convergence of all their products, with uniform local mass bounds. Expanding the products as in \eqref{eq:bounded-expansion}, and using smooth convergence of the remaining background factors, we obtain the asserted convergence of currents. In top degree, $(\theta_j+\ddc u_j)^n=\mu$ for all $j$, so the limit solves the endpoint equation.

For uniqueness, let $v=\chi\circ p_B$ be a bounded solution pulled back from $B$, with $\sup_B\chi=0$. The current $A_\chi=b\kappa+\ddc\chi$ on $B$ is positive, as one sees by pairing the pulled-back current $\theta_0+\ddc v$ with $w$ and with pullbacks of positive forms on $B$. The product expansion gives $nL_0A_\chi^m\wedge w=\sigma\wedge w$, which determines $A_\chi^m=\sigma/(nL_0)$ off the extreme set. That density already has total mass $H_0/n=b^m$, whereas mass invariance for the closed background $b\kappa$ gives $\int_BA_\chi^m=b^m$. Positivity therefore excludes any mass of $A_\chi^m$ on the extreme set. Thus $\chi/b$ satisfies the same normalized equation on $B$ as $\psi_0$, and \cref{uni-thm:GL-intro} proves the asserted uniqueness.
\end{proof}

The proposition concerns the limit of the base potentials constructed above. It does not describe solutions of the endpoint equation that are not pulled back from $B$, nor does it give a pointwise limit of the balanced metrics $\gamma_j=L_j^{-1/m}(b_j\kappa+\ddc\phi_j+L_jw)$.
