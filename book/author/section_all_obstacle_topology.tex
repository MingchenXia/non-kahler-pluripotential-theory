\subsection{All smooth obstacles}
\label{subsec:all-obstacle-topology}

We return to the Hermitian background $\omega$ on $X$. By \cref{prop:ordinary-topology-gauge-failure}, a smooth change of background need not preserve convergence when the obstacle is kept at zero. Requiring convergence below every smooth obstacle gives a topology that is stable under such changes. The dyadic approximation still determines its analytic closure, which is now described by equality of the two envelopes below every obstacle.

Let $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$ be the topology of the uniformity generated by all the uniformities $\tau_{\omega,F}$ with $F\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$. Thus an entourage involves finitely many distances between obstacle envelopes, together with finitely many of the controlled ideal relations and divisorial distances defined above.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:all-obstacle-uniformity}\index{Singularity type!all smooth obstacles}
A countable family $\{F_k\}_{k\geq1}\subset C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$ that is dense for the uniform norm suffices for the obstacle coordinates of $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$. For smooth $\chi$ with $\omega'=\omega+\ddc\chi>0$, the map $[u]\mapsto[u-\chi]$ is a uniform isomorphism between $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$ and $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_{\omega'}$. For $s>0$, positive scaling gives the corresponding uniform isomorphism onto $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_{s\omega}$. This topology is not $T_0$ on ordinary classes.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
For either envelope operator, the obstacle comparison gives
\[
 \|P_{\omega,F}[u]-P_{\omega,G}[u]\|_{L^1(\mathrm dV)}
 \leq\|F-G\|_\infty.
\]
Hence the two envelope distances for a pair $[u],[v]$ differ by at most $2\|F-G\|_\infty$, uniformly in the pair. The space $C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$ contains a countable subset that is dense for the uniform norm: approximate each member of a countable dense subset of $C(X)$ by smooth functions with errors $1/m$, $m\geq1$. Given an entourage, we approximate each of its finitely many obstacles by a member of the family and shrink the radii accordingly; this proves that the family generates the same uniformity. The divisorial coordinates still range over all fixed compact modifications.

The transport in \cref{prop:ordinary-topology-background-transport} relabels the entire family of smooth obstacles by $F\mapsto F-\chi$, or by $F\mapsto sF$. These are bijections of the family, which proves both isomorphisms.

For nonseparation, use the unbounded function $u=1-\sqrt{1-w}$ of \cref{prop:ordinary-topology-nonseparation}. It has trivial multiplier ideals and zero divisorial Lelong numbers, and $\delta w\preceq u$ for $0<\delta\leq1$. Fix $F$ and put $q=P_\omega(F)$, the envelope of the obstacle $F$ in the sense of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}. Since $\omega>0$, the constant $\min_XF$ is a competitor, so $q$ is bounded and $q\leq F$. The function
\[
 h_\delta=(1-\delta)q+\delta w-\delta\|F\|_\infty
\]
is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic, lies below $F$, and satisfies $h_\delta\preceq u$. Thus $h_\delta\leq P_{\omega,F}[u]\leq q$. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ off the pole set of $w$ and applying \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} gives $P_{\omega,F}[u]=q=P_{\omega,F}[0]$. Since $u$ and $0$ have the same multiplier ideals, their ideal envelopes below $F$ also agree. Every generating entourage therefore has identical sections at $[u]$ and $[0]$, although these ordinary classes differ.
\end{proof}

Below a smooth obstacle, the ideal envelope is the ordinary envelope of the ideal model obtained with the zero obstacle. Equality of the two envelopes below every obstacle therefore reduces to a question about ordinary envelopes alone.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection}\index{Envelope!ideal projection with an obstacle}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and put $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. For every smooth real $F$,
\[
 P_{\theta,F}[q]=P_{\theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}\sim q.
\]
If $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,q)$, then also
\[
 P_{\theta,F}[u]=P_{\theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
 \quad\text{for every smooth real }F.
\]
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Set $q_F=P_{\theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Translation of competitors gives
\[
 q+\min_XF\leq q_F\leq q+\max_XF,
\]
so $q_F\sim q$. The identity
$q_F-F=P_{\theta+\ddc F}[u-F]_{\mathcal I}$ and \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection} give $q_F\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. Since $q_F\leq F$ and $q_F\sim q$, it is a competitor for $P_{\theta,F}[q]$. Conversely, the maximum of a competitor for $P_{\theta,F}[q]$ with a sufficiently low translate of $q$ is a competitor for $P_{\theta,F}[q]_{\mathcal I}$, and this ideal envelope equals $q_F$ because $q\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. These bounds give
\[
 q_F\leq P_{\theta,F}[q]\leq P_{\theta,F}[q]_{\mathcal I}=q_F.
\]
The last assertion follows from \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality}, which gives $P_{\theta,F}[u]=P_{\theta,F}[q]$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:all-obstacle-analytic-closure}\index{Analytic singularity!closure for all obstacles}
The closure and the sequential closure of $\mathcal A_\omega$ in $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$ are both equal to
\[
 \mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega
 =\{[u]:P_{\omega,F}[u]=P_{\omega,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
                      \text{ for every smooth }F\}.
\]
It suffices to test equality on the countable dense family above. The dyadic sequence $b_j$ of \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density} converges to $[u]$ in this topology if and only if $[u]\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a smooth obstacle $F$ and put $\theta_F=\omega+\ddc F$. Translation by $F$ identifies the competitor families, so that
\[
 P_{\omega,F}[v]=P_{\theta_F}[v-F]+F,\qquad
 P_{\omega,F}[v]_{\mathcal I}=P_{\theta_F}[v-F]_{\mathcal I}+F
\]
for every $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. If $v$ has analytic singularities, so has $v-F$, and \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, applied to $v-F$ in the background $\theta_F$, gives $P_{\omega,F}[v]=P_{\omega,F}[v]_{\mathcal I}$. In particular, analytic classes lie in $\mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$.

The functions $b_j-F$ belong to $\PSH(X,\theta_F)$ and satisfy $u-F\preceq b_j-F$, hence $u-F\preceq_{\mathcal I}b_j-F$. By the divisorial estimate in the proof of \cref{thm:ordinary-topology-analytic-closure}, which holds for every local divisor $E$ over $X$, $\nu_E(b_j)\to\nu_E(u)$. \Cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence}, applied in the background $\theta_F$ with all $\epsilon_j=0$, therefore gives
\[
 P_{\omega,F}[b_j]=P_{\omega,F}[b_j]_{\mathcal I}
       \longrightarrow P_{\omega,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
       \quad\text{in }L^1.
\]
The controlled ideal relations and the divisorial distances on fixed modifications do not involve $F$, and they converge by the proof of \cref{thm:ordinary-topology-analytic-closure}. Consequently the dyadic sequence converges in $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$ exactly when $P_{\omega,F}[u]=P_{\omega,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$ for every $F$, by uniqueness of $L^1$ limits and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.

For each $F$, equality of the two $L^1$ envelope coordinates is a closed condition. Since analytic classes satisfy all these equalities, the closure of the analytic classes under nets lies in $\mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$, and the dyadic sequence proves the converse. Finally, the uniform obstacle bound in the proof of \cref{prop:all-obstacle-uniformity} extends equality from a dense family to every smooth obstacle.
\end{proof}

Taking $F=0$ gives $\mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega\subseteq\mathcal G_\omega$. For the reverse inclusion, let $q=P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$. By \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection}, it suffices to show that $P_{\omega,F}[u]=P_{\omega,F}[q]$ for every $F$, and membership $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,q)$ is sufficient for this. Under bounded mass and positive lower volume of $q$, this membership is equivalent to equality of the envelopes below zero, by \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization}. On curves the two closures coincide even at zero mass, by \cref{prop:curve-all-obstacle-topology}. We do not know whether the reverse inclusion holds in general. The following subsections prove equality for explicit classes of potentials in higher dimension, including types of zero volume. The reduction along a divisor in a fibration gives such classes; in one of them the base potential lies in the required relative envelope class and the ambient manifold fails the bounded mass property (\cref{ex:zero-volume-unbounded-mass-fibration}). Although $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega$ removes the dependence on a fixed obstacle, it still has incomplete curve families and is strictly weaker than polar total variation on ordinary curve models, by \cref{prop:curve-all-obstacle-incomplete}. On the product and fibration model loci constructed below, complete metrics come instead from polar curvature.

Before turning to examples, we record how the envelopes and the relative envelope classes behave under finite unramified coverings; this will be used for fibrations with disconnected fibers. Envelopes below a continuous real obstacle are defined by the same competitor families as below a smooth one. Recall from \cref{def:relative-envelope-class} that $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$ means that $u\preceq\varphi$ and that, for every $a\in(0,1)$, there is a \emph{stretching competitor} $h\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $a\varphi+(1-a)h\leq u$; here $a=1-b^{-1}$ in the notation of that definition. Pullback of competitors and the maximum over the fibers of the covering identify the envelopes upstairs and downstairs, and the same fiber maximum transfers stretching competitors from the covering space to the base.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite-cover-envelope-transport}\index{Envelope!finite unramified cover}\index{Relative envelope class!finite unramified cover}
Let $r\colon Y\to X$ be a finite unramified holomorphic covering between connected compact complex manifolds. Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $u,\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and put $\theta'=r^*\theta$ and $u'=u\circ r$. For every $F\in C(X,\mathbb R)$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_{\theta',F\circ r}[u']&=P_{\theta,F}[u]\circ r,\\
 P_{\theta',F\circ r}[u']_{\mathcal I}
   &=P_{\theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}\circ r.
 \end{split}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 u'\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\theta',\varphi\circ r)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $v\in\PSH(Y,\theta')$, define
\[
 v_{\max}(x)=\max_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}v(y).
\]
Over a sufficiently small ball in $X$, this is the finite maximum of the functions obtained by composing $v$ with the local inverse branches of $r$. Each of them is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, so $v_{\max}\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ by \cref{lem:max-psh}. If $v\preceq u'$, then $v_{\max}\preceq u$. If $v\sim_{\mathcal I}u'$, a local biholomorphic change of variables makes each branch multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u$, and the maximum rule in \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus} gives $v_{\max}\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. The obstacle bound $v\leq F\circ r$ gives $v_{\max}\leq F$.

Thus every competitor upstairs is dominated by the pullback of a corresponding competitor downstairs. Conversely, pullback of a competitor downstairs preserves the background inequality, the ordinary order or multiplier-ideal equivalence, and the obstacle bound. These two correspondences between the competitor families give the envelope identities; upper regularization commutes with $r$, since $r$ is locally biholomorphic.

Since $r$ is surjective, it reflects the ordinary order: $u'\preceq\varphi\circ r$ if and only if $u\preceq\varphi$. Fix $a\in(0,1)$. Pullback takes any stretching competitor $h$ with $a\varphi+(1-a)h\leq u$ to a stretching competitor upstairs. Conversely, if $v\in\PSH(Y,\theta')$ satisfies
\[
 a\varphi\circ r+(1-a)v\leq u',
\]
then taking the maximum over the fibers gives $a\varphi+(1-a)v_{\max}\leq u$; this also holds at poles, because the convex sums are defined there. Applying the definition of the relative class for every $a$ proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}
