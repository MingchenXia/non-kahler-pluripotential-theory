\subsection{Intermediate currents of a shrinking sequence}\label{subsec:shrinking-defect-currents}

We formulate the result for a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, since it will be applied with different metrics in \cref{chap:hopf}. Consider smooth functions
\[
 v_j=s_ju_j,\qquad u_j\in\PSH(X,h),\qquad 0<s_j\longrightarrow0,
\]
and suppose that
\[
 (h+\ddc v_j)^n\rightharpoonup h^n+\sigma.
\]
The lower bound $h+\ddc v_j\geq(1-s_j)h$ shows that $\sigma$ is positive. Although $\ddc v_j\to0$ in coefficient total variation, its intermediate powers can have nonzero limits, and these limits determine $\sigma$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:shrinking-intermediate-currents}
\index{Mass defect!intermediate currents}
After passage to a common subsequence, the positive forms
\[
 H_j=\ddc v_j+s_jh=s_j(h+\ddc u_j)
\]
satisfy $H_j^k\rightharpoonup K_k$ for $1\leq k\leq n$. The currents $K_k$ are positive and closed, and $K_1=K_n=0$. For every smooth real $(n-k,n-k)$-form $\eta$ with $\mathrm{d}\eta=0$ or $\ddc\eta=0$,
\[
 \int_X K_k\wedge\eta=0.
\]
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shrinking-defect-current-decomposition}
 \sigma=\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\binom nk h^{n-k}\wedge K_k.
\end{equation}
If $\vartheta$ is a smooth closed semipositive $(1,1)$-form on $X$, then $\sigma$ is supported on $\{\vartheta^n=0\}$. In dimension three the same conclusion holds for every smooth semipositive $(1,1)$-form $\vartheta$ with $\ddc\vartheta=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The masses of the weakly convergent measures $(h+\ddc v_j)^n$ are bounded. For $s_j<1/2$, positivity in the expansion
\[
 (h+\ddc v_j)^n
 =\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk(1-s_j)^{n-k}h^{n-k}\wedge H_j^k
\]
gives a uniform bound for each $\int_Xh^{n-k}\wedge H_j^k$. In local unitary coordinates, the coefficient matrix of the smooth positive form $H_j^k$ on exterior powers is positive semidefinite, so its entries are bounded in absolute value by its trace, up to a dimensional constant. The trace bounds therefore bound every coefficient in total variation, and weak compactness of measures gives a common subsequence and positive limits $K_k$.

For $n\geq2$, choose a Gauduchon form $\gamma$. By \cref{prop:gauduchon-trace},
\[
 \int_X H_j\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 =s_j\int_Xh\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\]
Since $\gamma$ and $h$ are equivalent smooth metrics, it follows that $H_j\to0$ in coefficient total variation. For $n=1$ the same conclusion follows from $\int_XH_j=s_j\int_Xh$. Thus $K_1=0$ in all dimensions. Since $\mathrm{d}H_j=s_j\mathrm{d}h$,
\[
 \mathrm{d}(H_j^k)=ks_j\mathrm{d}h\wedge H_j^{k-1}\longrightarrow0
\]
as currents, because the preceding trace bounds control multiplication by the fixed smooth form $\mathrm{d}h$. Hence every $K_k$ is closed.

The smooth form $(\ddc v_j)^k$ is both $\mathrm{d}$-exact and $\ddc$-exact:
\[
 (\ddc v_j)^k
 =\mathrm{d}\bigl(\dc v_j\wedge(\ddc v_j)^{k-1}\bigr)
 =\ddc\bigl(v_j(\ddc v_j)^{k-1}\bigr).
\]
Its integral against either kind of test form $\eta$ in the statement is therefore zero by Stokes' formula. Expanding $\ddc v_j=H_j-s_jh$ gives
\[
 \int_X\eta\wedge H_j^k
 =-\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\binom kr(-s_j)^{k-r}
      \int_X\eta\wedge h^{k-r}\wedge H_j^r
 =O(s_j).
\]
Each integral on the right is bounded by the trace bound for $H_j^r$. Taking $k=n$ and $\eta=1$ shows that the positive measures $H_j^n$ tend to zero in total variation, which proves $K_n=0$; the other vanishing integrals follow by weak convergence. Finally, letting $j\to\infty$ in the positive expansion above, the terms with $k=0$ tend to $h^n$ and those with $k=1$ and $k=n$ vanish in the limit, which proves \eqref{eq:shrinking-defect-current-decomposition}.

For closed semipositive $\vartheta$, apply the vanishing integrals to $\eta=\vartheta^{n-k}$. The measure $K_k\wedge\vartheta^{n-k}$ is positive and has zero mass. On every relatively compact open set where $\vartheta>0$, it dominates a positive multiple of the trace measure of $K_k$, so $K_k$ vanishes there. The decomposition gives the asserted support. In dimension three only $K_2$ occurs, and the same argument applies to the $\ddc$-closed test form $\vartheta$ itself.
\end{proof}

In dimensions at most two the decomposition forces $\sigma=0$. A threefold carrying a pluriclosed Hermitian metric, that is, a Hermitian form $\vartheta$ with $\ddc\vartheta=0$, has no nonzero defect $\sigma$ of this form either. In higher dimensions, the vanishing integrals impose necessary conditions on each intermediate current; they do not by themselves produce a smooth sequence with prescribed intermediate limits.
