\section{Semipositive metrics in a four-point blowup family}
\label{sec:four-point-metric-nonextension}\index{Semipositive metric!failure of extension}\index{Blowup!four-point family}

The preceding constructions keep the manifold fixed. For a family of compact manifolds and a line bundle on the total space, one may ask whether a semipositive singular metric on the restriction of the bundle to a special fiber is the restriction of a semipositive metric over a neighborhood of that fiber. In a family of blowups of the projective plane at four points, the answer is negative. The obstruction comes from compact curves on the nearby fibers on which the bundle has degree zero: the curvature of a semipositive metric vanishes on such a curve, and this persists on a component of the limiting curve in the central fiber. It forces the restricted metric to have a logarithmic singularity of coefficient one at a point of that component, whereas the prescribed metric has coefficient one half there. Varying these curves also identifies exactly which central metrics extend.

Let $\Delta$ be a disk with coordinate $t$, and let $[x:y:z]$ be homogeneous coordinates on $\mathbb P^2$. Blow up $\mathbb P^2\times\Delta$ along the four disjoint sections
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_1(t)&=[1:0:0],&\qquad p_2(t)&=[0:1:0],\\
 p_3(t)&=[1:1:t],&\qquad p_4(t)&=[0:0:1],
 \end{aligned}
\]
write $\pi\colon\mathcal X\to\Delta$ for the resulting family and $X_t=\pi^{-1}(t)$ for its fibers. Let $H$ be the pullback of the hyperplane line bundle, let $E_i$ be the exceptional divisor over $p_i$, and put
\[
 L=2H-E_1-E_2-E_3-E_4,\qquad L_t=L|_{X_t}.
\]
We use additive notation for tensor products of line bundles and also for their divisor classes, and we write $K_{X_t}$ for the canonical line bundle. A singular Hermitian metric $h$ has squared norm $|e|_h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi}$ in a local holomorphic frame $e$; the function $\varphi$ is its local \emph{weight}, and its curvature is $\ddc\varphi$. The metric is \emph{semipositive} if its local weights are plurisubharmonic. For an effective divisor with local equation $w$, the \emph{divisor metric} on the associated line bundle is the singular metric for which the canonical section has norm one; its weight is $\log|w|^2$ and its curvature is the integration current along the divisor.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:four-point-family-adjoint-vanishing}
The family is smooth and projective with rational surface fibers, and $L$ is effective on $\mathcal X$. For every $t\ne0$, $L_t$ is generated by global sections, has square zero and is nef. For every integer $m\geq1$,
\[
 H^0(X_t,K_{X_t}+mL_t)=0\qquad(t\ne0).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Near one of the sections, coordinates centered on it identify the blowup with $\operatorname{Bl}_0(\mathbb C^2)\times\Delta$. Since the four centers are disjoint, these charts prove smoothness and the submersion property. The blowup of the projective morphism $\mathbb P^2\times\Delta\to\Delta$ is projective and proper, and each fiber $X_t$ is the blowup of $\mathbb P^2$ at the four points $p_i(t)$.

The quadratic forms
\[
 F_1=x(z-ty),\qquad F_2=y(z-tx)
\]
vanish along all four sections and therefore lift to sections of $L$. They are nonzero, which proves that $L$ is effective. For $t\ne0$, their common zeros in $\mathbb P^2$ are exactly the four points. In suitable affine coordinates $(u,v)$ centered at $p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4$, their linear initial terms are
\[
 (v-tu,-tu),\quad(-tu,v-tu),\quad(v-tu,v),\quad(u,v).
\]
Each pair is linearly independent. Hence, after the exceptional divisors are subtracted, the transformed sections have no common zero on an exceptional curve. The two transformed sections therefore generate $L_t$, and $L_t$ has nonnegative degree on every irreducible curve.

On a fiber, $H^2=1$, $H\cdot E_i=0$, and $E_i\cdot E_j=-\delta_{ij}$. Hence $L_t^2=0$, and $K_{X_t}=-3H+\sum_iE_i$ gives $K_{X_t}\cdot L_t=-2$. A nonzero section of $K_{X_t}+mL_t$ would have an effective zero divisor whose intersection with the nef line bundle $L_t$ is $-2<0$, a contradiction. This argument also excludes a nowhere-vanishing section, whose zero divisor would have intersection zero.
\end{proof}

On $X_0$, let $\ell$ be the strict transform of the line $\{z=0\}$, which passes through $p_1(0)$, $p_2(0)$ and $p_3(0)$. In additive notation,
\[
 \ell=H-E_1-E_2-E_3,\qquad
 A=2L_0-\ell=3H-E_1-E_2-E_3-2E_4.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:four-point-central-semipositive-metric}
The line bundle $A$ is generated by global sections. There is a semipositive metric $h_0$ on $L_0$ whose weight along $\ell$ is
\[
 \varphi_0=\tfrac12\log|w|^2+\text{a smooth function},
\]
where $w$ is a local equation of $\ell$. It is smooth off $\ell$ and has analytic singularities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider the cubic forms
\[
 x^2z,\qquad xyz,\qquad y^2z,\qquad xy(x-y).
\]
Their common zero set in $\mathbb P^2$ consists of the four central points. In affine coordinates at $p_1$, $p_2$ and $p_3$, their linear initial terms include $(z,y)$, $(z,x)$ and $(z,x-y)$, respectively, while at $p_4$ their quadratic initial terms include $x^2,xy,y^2$. These have no common zero on the corresponding exceptional projective lines. After the simple exceptional factors at $p_1,p_2,p_3$ and the double factor at $p_4$ are removed, the transformed cubics therefore generate $A$.

Their Fubini--Study metric $h_A$ is smooth and semipositive. Let $h_\ell$ be the divisor metric on $\mathcal O(\ell)$. Since $2L_0=A+\ell$, the square root of $h_A\otimes h_\ell$ defines a metric $h_0$ on $L_0$, with
\[
 \Theta_{h_0}=\tfrac12\Theta_{h_A}+\tfrac12[\ell]\geq0.
\]
The stated form of the weight and of its singularities follows directly.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:four-point-conic-degeneration}\index{Curvature!degree-zero curve}
The strict transforms of the conics
\[
 F_1-2F_2=z(x-2y)+txy=0
\]
form a surface $\Gamma\subset\mathcal X$. For $t\ne0$, its fiber $C_t$ is a smooth compact rational curve with $L_t\cdot C_t=0$. The central fiber of $\Gamma$ is $\ell+q$, where $q$ is the strict transform of the line $\{x=2y\}$. The curves $\ell$ and $q$ meet transversely at one point $p$, and $L_0\cdot q=1$. Near every point of $q\setminus\{p\}$, $\Gamma$ is smooth and $\pi|_\Gamma$ is a submersion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The symmetric matrix of the quadratic form has determinant $-t/2$, so the conic is smooth for $t\ne0$. It passes simply through all four centers, so its strict transform is a smooth rational curve of class $L_t$, and its degree against $L_t$ is $L_t^2=0$.

At $t=0$ the conic is the union of the lines $\{z=0\}$ and $\{x=2y\}$. The second line avoids $p_1,p_2,p_3$ and passes through $p_4$. Each component has multiplicity one at every center it contains, so the central fiber of $\Gamma$ contains no additional exceptional component. The two lines meet at $[2:1:0]$, transversely and away from every center. Since $q=H-E_4$, we have $L_0\cdot q=1$.

Away from the centers, the defining equation at $t=0$ has nonzero derivative in the directions of $\mathbb P^2$ at every point of $q\setminus\{p\}$, which proves the assertion there. Near $p_4$, use the blowup chart $z=1$, $x=r$, $y=rs$. After the exceptional factor is removed, the equation becomes
\[
 1-2s+trs=0.
\]
At the central point of $q\cap E_4$ we have $r=0$ and $s=1/2$, and the derivative in $s$ is $-2$. Thus $(r,t)$ are local coordinates on $\Gamma$ there. These two cases cover $q\setminus\{p\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:four-point-semipositive-metric-nonextension}\index{Singularity type!metric extension}
There is no semipositive metric on $L$ over an open neighborhood of $X_0$ whose local weights, restricted to $X_0$, satisfy
\[
 \psi\geq\varphi_0-C
\]
in compatible local frames, for a constant $C$. In particular, $h_0$ is neither the restriction of a semipositive metric on such a neighborhood nor of the same singularity type as such a restriction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that such a metric exists. Since $\pi$ is proper, we may shrink $\Delta$ so that the whole family lies in its domain: otherwise points outside the neighborhood over $t_j\to0$ would have a limit point on $X_0$ outside it. The lower bound makes the restricted weight finite at every point of $q\setminus\{p\}$, because $\varphi_0$ is smooth there. Hence the restrictions of the weight to $\Gamma$ and to $q$ are not identically $-\infty$.

Near a point of $q\setminus\{p\}$, choose a product chart $(\zeta,t)$ on $\Gamma$, as given by \cref{lem:four-point-conic-degeneration}, and a holomorphic frame of $L$. The restricted weight $u(\zeta,t)$ is plurisubharmonic and locally integrable. By Fubini's theorem, for almost every small $t\ne0$ its restriction to $C_t$ is not identically $-\infty$, so the metric restricts to $C_t$. The curvature of this restriction is a positive measure on the compact curve $C_t$ of total mass $L_t\cdot C_t=0$, so it vanishes. Thus $u(\cdot,t)$ is harmonic for almost every $t$. By \cref{lem:psh-harmonic-slice-persistence} below, whose proof does not use this theorem, the central slice $u(\cdot,0)$ is harmonic or identically $-\infty$, and it is finite. Hence the restricted weight is harmonic near every point of $q\setminus\{p\}$.

The restricted metric on $L_0|_q$ therefore has curvature supported at $p$. This curvature is a positive measure of total mass $L_0\cdot q=1$, so it is the Dirac measure $[p]$. In a coordinate $w$ on $q$ centered at $p$, the restricted weight is therefore $\log|w|^2$ plus a harmonic function, because their difference has zero distributional Laplacian. Since $\ell$ and $q$ meet transversely, $\varphi_0|_q=\tfrac12\log|w|^2+O(1)$. The difference $\psi|_q-\varphi_0|_q$ thus tends to $-\infty$ at $p$, contradicting the assumed lower bound.
\end{proof}

The obstruction concerns the prescribed singularity type. Since $L$ is effective on the total space, the divisor metric of the zero divisor of a nonzero section of $L$ is a semipositive metric on $\mathcal X$, with a different restriction to $X_0$.

\subsection{The image of metric restriction}
\label{subsec:four-point-metric-restriction-image}\index{Semipositive metric!restriction image}\index{Pencil!metric extension}

The degree-zero conics also determine exactly which central metrics extend. We say that a semipositive metric $h$ on $L_0$ \emph{extends} if there is a semipositive metric on $L$ over a neighborhood of $X_0$ whose local weights restrict to local weights of $h$; in particular, these restrictions are not identically $-\infty$. The following lemma is the slice argument used in the proof of \cref{thm:four-point-semipositive-metric-nonextension}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:psh-harmonic-slice-persistence}
Let $u$ be a plurisubharmonic function on a product of disks $D_\zeta\times D_t$ with $0\in D_t$, and suppose that $u$ is not identically $-\infty$. Suppose that $u(\cdot,t)$ is harmonic for almost every $t$. Then $u(\cdot,0)$ is harmonic or identically $-\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $u$ is locally integrable, Fubini's theorem gives $\partial_\zeta\partial_{\bar\zeta}u=0$ in the sense of distributions. On smaller product disks, form the averages
\[
 u_\varepsilon(\zeta)=\frac1{\pi\varepsilon^2}
       \int_{|t|<\varepsilon}u(\zeta,t)\,\mathrm{d}A(t),
\]
where $\mathrm{d}A$ is planar area. Local upper bounds for $u$ and the reverse Fatou lemma give upper semicontinuity, and Fubini's theorem gives the submean inequality in $\zeta$. The averages are locally integrable, since $u$ is integrable on compact product disks. Thus each $u_\varepsilon$ is subharmonic with zero distributional Laplacian, hence harmonic.

Since $u$ is subharmonic in $t$, the disk averages decrease to $u(\zeta,0)$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. A decreasing limit of harmonic functions is harmonic or identically $-\infty$: if the values are bounded below at one point, Harnack's inequality, applied to the differences from the first function, bounds the differences on compact subdisks, and compactness of harmonic functions gives a harmonic limit. This proves the alternative on every smaller disk, and hence on $D_\zeta$.
\end{proof}

Let $f\colon X_0\to\mathbb P^1$ be the morphism defined by the pencil of lines through $p_4=p_4(0)$, that is, by $[x:y:z]\mapsto[x:y]$, let $a=x/y$ be the affine coordinate on $\mathbb P^1$, and put $P=f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:four-point-pencil-reference}
The pencil $f$ is a holomorphic map with connected fibers, $P=H-E_4$, and $L_0=\mathcal O(\ell)+P$. Let $h_\ell$ be the divisor metric on $\mathcal O(\ell)$, for which the canonical section $s_\ell$ satisfies $|s_\ell|_{h_\ell}=1$ away from $\ell$, and let $h_{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini--Study metric on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$, with curvature $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$. Then the metric
\[
 h_*=h_\ell\otimes f^*h_{\mathrm{FS}}
\]
on $L_0$ extends to a semipositive metric on $L$ over the whole family. Its curvature is $[\ell]+f^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First blow up $p_4$ in $\mathbb P^2$. The map $[x:y]$ then becomes holomorphic: in the chart $x=r$, $y=rs$ with $z=1$, it is $[1:s]$, and the other chart covers the remaining direction. Its fibers are the strict transforms of the lines through $p_4$, and the pullback of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$ is $H-E_4$. Next blow up the other three central points, which lie in the directions $a=\infty,0,1$, respectively. Each affected fiber becomes the union of its strict transform and one exceptional line meeting it, while every other fiber remains a projective line. This proves connectedness of the fibers and the formula for $P$. The equality $L_0=\mathcal O(\ell)+P$ follows from the divisor classes.

In this identification, the restrictions of $F_1,F_2$ to $X_0$ are $s_\ell\otimes f^*x$ and $s_\ell\otimes f^*y$, where $x$ and $y$ are regarded as sections of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$. Indeed, at $t=0$ the forms are $xz$ and $yz$; the pullback of $\{z=0\}$ is $\ell+E_1+E_2+E_3$, and removing the four exceptional factors from the transforms of $xz$ and $yz$ leaves exactly these sections. In a local frame of $L$, the weight $\log(|F_1|^2+|F_2|^2)$ defines a semipositive metric, since a change of frame multiplies both coefficients by the same nonvanishing holomorphic function. On every fiber $X_t$ with $t\ne0$ the two sections have no common zero, so the weight is not identically $-\infty$ there. On the central fiber the weight is the sum of the divisor weight of $\ell$ and the pullback of the Fubini--Study weight, which proves the claim and the curvature formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:four-point-variable-conics}
For $a\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$, the strict transforms of the conics
\[
 F_1-aF_2=z(x-ay)+t(a-1)xy=0
\]
have smooth rational fibers of $L_t$-degree zero for $t\ne0$. Their central fiber is $\ell+q_a$, where $q_a=f^{-1}(a)$ is a smooth projective line of $L_0$-degree one. The two components meet transversely at $p_a=[a:1:0]$, away from the blowup centers. Along $q_a\setminus\{p_a\}$, the surface formed by these conics is smooth and its projection to the disk is a submersion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The symmetric matrix of the quadratic form has determinant $-a(a-1)t/4$, so the conic is smooth for $t\ne0$; it passes simply through the four centers. Its strict transform has class $L_t$, so its degree is $L_t^2=0$. At $t=0$ the conic splits into the two lines $\{z=0\}$ and $\{x=ay\}$. Since $a$ is finite and $a\notin\{0,1\}$, the line $\{x=ay\}$ does not pass through the other three central points, so no additional exceptional component occurs, and $q_a=H-E_4$ has degree one against $L_0$. The two lines meet transversely at $p_a$, away from all centers.

Away from the centers, the central equation has nonzero derivative in the directions of $\mathbb P^2$ along $q_a\setminus\{p_a\}$. At the point of $q_a$ on $E_4$, the chart $z=1$, $x=r$, $y=rs$ gives, after the exceptional factor is removed,
\[
 1-as+t(a-1)rs=0.
\]
At $r=t=0$ and $s=1/a$, the derivative in $s$ is $-a\ne0$. These charts prove the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:four-point-metric-restriction-image}
The semipositive metrics on $L_0$ that extend to semipositive metrics on $L$ over a neighborhood of $X_0$ are exactly the metrics
\[
 h_\ell\otimes f^*h_B,
\]
where $h_B$ ranges over all semipositive singular Hermitian metrics on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$. Every such central metric extends over the whole family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Necessity.} Let $h$ be a semipositive metric on $L_0$ that extends. By properness we may shrink the disk so that the extending metric is defined on the whole family. On the locus where $f$ is smooth, local integrability and Fubini's theorem show that for almost every $a\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$ the restriction of the weights of $h$ to $q_a$ is not identically $-\infty$. Fix such an $a$. On each local product chart of the conic surface of \cref{lem:four-point-variable-conics} along $q_a\setminus\{p_a\}$, the weight of the extending metric is not identically $-\infty$, since its central slice is not. By Fubini's theorem, the metric restricts to almost every nearby compact conic, and on such a conic its curvature is a positive measure of degree zero, hence zero. \Cref{lem:psh-harmonic-slice-persistence} therefore makes the central slice of the weight harmonic on each chart; the alternative $-\infty$ is excluded by the choice of $a$.

Consequently, the curvature of $h|_{q_a}$ is supported at $p_a$. It is a positive measure of degree one, so it equals $[p_a]$, which is also the curvature of $h_*|_{q_a}$. The difference of the two weights is a globally defined harmonic function on the projective line $q_a$: near $p_a$, subtracting $\log|w|^2$ from either weight leaves a harmonic function, so the difference extends across $p_a$. It is therefore constant. Thus the weight difference $u$ between $h$ and $h_*$ is constant along almost every fiber of $f$, away from $\ell$.

Over every point of the base there are local holomorphic sections of $f$ avoiding $\ell$ and the exceptional curves: over the finite chart use $[a:1:1]$, and near infinity use $[1:b:1]$ with $b=1/a$. If $e_B$ is a local frame of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$, then $s_\ell\otimes f^*e_B$ is a nonvanishing holomorphic frame of $L_0$ along these sections. Composing the weight of $h$ in this frame with a section defines a subharmonic weight on the base. It is not identically $-\infty$, because on almost every fiber the weight is harmonic away from $p_a$. Two choices of section give the same function at almost every base point, because $u$ is constant on those fibers, and hence everywhere, since subharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere. Changes of the base frame give the transition law of a metric. These weights therefore define a semipositive metric $h_B$ on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$. Fubini's theorem on local product charts, together with the fact that $\ell$ and the exceptional fibers have zero volume, shows that $h$ and $h_\ell\otimes f^*h_B$ agree almost everywhere on $X_0$. By \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, their local plurisubharmonic weights agree everywhere, including on the three exceptional fibers. This proves necessity.

\emph{Sufficiency.} Let $h_B$ be a semipositive metric on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$, and represent it by a homogeneous weight $V$ on $\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\}$, with coordinates $(\xi_1,\xi_2)$ and $a=\xi_1/\xi_2$. In the chart $\xi_2\ne0$, if $\phi_B$ is the weight of $h_B$ in the frame given by the section $\xi_2$, set
\[
 V(\xi_1,\xi_2)=\phi_B(\xi_1/\xi_2)+\log|\xi_2|^2.
\]
The other chart gives the same function, by the transition law of the metric. The function $V$ is plurisubharmonic and satisfies $V(\lambda\xi_1,\lambda\xi_2)=V(\xi_1,\xi_2)+\log|\lambda|^2$ for $\lambda\ne0$. The weight difference between $h_B$ and $h_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is bounded above on the compact base, so
\[
 V(\xi_1,\xi_2)\leq\log(|\xi_1|^2+|\xi_2|^2)+C.
\]
Hence $V$ extends plurisubharmonically across the origin, with value $-\infty$ there, by \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}. In each local frame of $L$, the function $V(F_1,F_2)$ is plurisubharmonic, and by homogeneity it transforms as the weight of a metric. Its restriction to each fiber $X_t$ with $t\ne0$ is not identically $-\infty$: there the two generating sections define a nonconstant map onto $\mathbb P^1$, and the weight of $h_B$ is not identically $-\infty$. On the central fiber, the factorization in \cref{lem:four-point-pencil-reference} and homogeneity give exactly the divisor weight of $\ell$ plus the pullback of the weight of $h_B$. This is the required extension over $\mathcal X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:four-point-least-singular-extendable}\index{Singularity type!least singular extendable metric}
Among all semipositive metrics on $L_0$ that extend, $h_*$ has the least singular weights: every such metric has weights $\psi$ satisfying $\psi\leq\psi_*+C$ in compatible local frames, where $\psi_*$ is the weight of $h_*$; and $h_*$ itself extends. The assertion concerns metrics that extend, not all semipositive metrics on $L_0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:four-point-metric-restriction-image}, such a metric has the form $h_\ell\otimes f^*h_B$. Its weight difference from $h_*$ is the pullback of the global quasi-plurisubharmonic difference between the weights of $h_B$ and $h_{\mathrm{FS}}$, which is bounded above on $\mathbb P^1$. This proves the inequality. That $h_*$ extends is \cref{lem:four-point-pencil-reference}.
\end{proof}
