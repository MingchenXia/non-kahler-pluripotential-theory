\section{Polar curvature}
\label{sec:psh-polar-invariance}

The polar curvature of $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ is the restriction of the current $\theta+\ddc u$ to the pole set $\{u=-\infty\}$. A bounded change of the potential preserves more than its Lelong numbers: it preserves the whole polar curvature. Indeed, a local energy estimate puts the bounded difference of two potentials in a Sobolev space, while pole sets admit smooth test functions of arbitrarily small energy, and these test functions show that no coefficient measure of the Hessian of the difference charges the pole set. Polar curvature is closed even when the pole set is not closed, so a local elliptic equation recovers it from its scalar Hermitian trace.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bounded-measure-laplacian-energy}
Let $U\subset\mathbb R^d$ be open and let $w\in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. If $\Delta w$ is a locally finite signed Radon measure, then $w\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a smooth function $0\leq\psi\leq1$ with compact support in $U$, and a relatively compact open neighborhood $V$ of its support. Mollify $w$ inside $V$ with a standard mollifier $\rho_\varepsilon$, writing $w_\varepsilon=w*\rho_\varepsilon$ and $\nu_\varepsilon=(\Delta w)*\rho_\varepsilon$. For small $\varepsilon$, integration by parts and Young's inequality give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int\psi^2|\nabla w_\varepsilon|^2
 &=-\int\psi^2w_\varepsilon\nu_\varepsilon
       -2\int\psi w_\varepsilon\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla w_\varepsilon\\
 &\leq \|w\|_{L^\infty(V)}|\Delta w|(V)
       +\tfrac12\int\psi^2|\nabla w_\varepsilon|^2
       +2\int w_\varepsilon^2|\nabla\psi|^2.
 \end{split}
\]
Absorbing the middle term on the right, we obtain a bound for $\int\psi^2|\nabla w_\varepsilon|^2$ that is independent of $\varepsilon$. Taking $\psi=1$ on a prescribed smaller compact set, weak compactness in $W^{1,2}$ and convergence of the mollifications in $L^2$ identify the weak limit with $w$. This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pole-small-energy-tests}\index{Pluripolar set!smooth energy tests}
Let $v$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on a complex manifold, let $U$ be a coordinate ball with compact closure in a larger chart, and let $K\subset U\cap\{v=-\infty\}$ be compact. For every open $O\subset U$ containing $K$, there are real $\varphi_j\in C^\infty_c(O)$ with $0\leq\varphi_j\leq1$, equal to one near $K$, such that $\|\varphi_j\|_{W^{1,2}(U)}\to0$. The norm uses the Euclidean coordinates.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adding a smooth quadratic function and subtracting a constant, we obtain a negative plurisubharmonic function $h$ on $U$ with the same pole set as $v$. Put $h_M=\max(h,-M)$ for $M\geq1$, and choose $\psi\in C^\infty_c(O)$ with $0\leq\psi\leq1$ and $\psi=1$ near $K$. On a fixed compact neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp}\psi$, the positive measures $\Delta h_M$ have uniformly bounded mass: choose a nonnegative smooth function $\chi$ with compact support in $U$, equal to one on this neighborhood, and use
\[
 \int\chi\,\Delta h_M=\int h_M\Delta\chi,
 \qquad |h_M|\leq|h|\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U).
\]
Apply the integration-by-parts estimate of the preceding proof to mollifications of $h_M$. Their absolute values are at most $M$, and their squares are at most $M$ times their absolute values. The uniform bounds for the mass of the Laplacians and for the local $L^1$ norms therefore give, after passing to the weak limit in $W^{1,2}$,
\[
 \int\psi^2|\nabla h_M|^2\leq CM,
 \qquad
 \left\|\frac{-\psi h_M}{M}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(U)}^2\leq\frac{C}{M}.
\]
Here $C$ depends on the fixed cutoffs and the local $L^1$ norm of $h$, but not on $M$. The second inequality also uses $h_M^2\leq M|h|$ in the terms containing $\psi$ and $\nabla\psi$.

The Sobolev function $-\psi h_M/M$ takes values between zero and one. It equals one on a neighborhood of $K$, since $h$ is upper semicontinuous and therefore $\{h<-M\}$ is an open neighborhood of $K$. Extend it by zero and mollify with a sufficiently small radius: the support stays in $O$, the function remains equal to one on a smaller neighborhood of $K$, and its Sobolev norm does not increase. Letting $M\to\infty$ gives the required smooth functions.
\end{proof}

For a current $T$ of order zero and a Borel set $B$, we write $\mathbf1_BT$ for the restriction of $T$ to $B$, taken coefficientwise on the Radon coefficient measures of $T$. These restrictions commute with smooth changes of coordinates.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bounded-type-polar-invariance}\index{Singularity type!polar curvature invariance}\index{Polar curvature!bounded changes}
Let $u,v$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on a complex manifold such that every point has a neighborhood on which $u-C\leq v\leq u+C$ for some constant $C$. Then $u$ and $v$ have the same pole set $S=\{u=-\infty\}=\{v=-\infty\}$, and
\[
 \mathbf1_S\ddc u=\mathbf1_S\ddc v.
\]
Consequently, for any smooth real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta,\beta$,
\[
 \mathbf1_S(\theta+\ddc u)=\mathbf1_S(\beta+\ddc v).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two local inequalities show that the pole sets agree. Off $S$ put $w=u-v$, and give $w$ arbitrary bounded values on $S$. Since $S$ has Lebesgue measure zero, this defines the locally bounded distribution $u-v$. The coefficient measures of $\ddc w$ are locally finite, since after a smooth correction the complex Hessians of $u$ and $v$ are positive currents of order zero. Taking the Euclidean trace shows that $\Delta w$ is a signed Radon measure, so \cref{lem:bounded-measure-laplacian-energy} gives $w\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

Consequently, the real and imaginary parts of the coefficient measures of $\ddc w$ are continuous linear functionals on $W^{1,2}_0$ of a smaller ball, since a second distributional derivative of $w$ is a first derivative of its $L^2$ gradient. Let $\nu$ be any one of these real signed measures, with Jordan decomposition $\nu=\nu^+-\nu^-$. Choose a compact set $K$ contained in the intersection of $S$ with a positive set of a Hahn decomposition, so that $\nu^-(K)=0$. For $\epsilon>0$, take an open neighborhood $O$ of $K$ in the ball with $\nu^-(O)<\epsilon$. The test functions $\varphi_j$ of \cref{lem:pole-small-energy-tests}, applied to $u$, satisfy
\[
 \nu^+(K)\leq\int\varphi_j\,\mathrm d\nu^+
       =\langle\nu,\varphi_j\rangle
          +\int\varphi_j\,\mathrm d\nu^-
       \leq|\langle\nu,\varphi_j\rangle|+\epsilon.
\]
By continuity on the Sobolev space, the first term tends to zero. Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ and using inner regularity gives $\nu^+(S)=0$ locally, and the same argument applied to $-\nu$ gives $\nu^-(S)=0$. Thus all coefficient measures of $\ddc w$ vanish on $S$, which proves the first identity. Since smooth forms also give $S$ zero mass, the second identity follows.
\end{proof}

For closed positive currents, local potentials turn equality of the scalar traces into an elliptic equation. If both currents are carried by a Lebesgue null set, the resulting smooth difference vanishes.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:closed-null-current-trace}\index{Closed positive current!trace on a null set}
Let $T,S$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, both carried coefficientwise by a Borel set of Lebesgue measure zero. For any Hermitian form $\eta$,
\[
 T\wedge\eta^{n-1}=S\wedge\eta^{n-1}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T=S.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a small coordinate ball, take plurisubharmonic local potentials $a,b$ with $T=\ddc a$ and $S=\ddc b$, by \cite[Chapter III, Proposition 1.19, p.~135]{DemCADG}. Put $h=a-b\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The trace identity gives $\ddc h\wedge\eta^{n-1}=0$ in the sense of distributions. Dividing by the smooth positive volume form $\eta^n$ gives a scalar second-order operator with smooth coefficients whose principal matrix is a positive multiple of the inverse of the Hermitian matrix of $\eta$; it is uniformly elliptic on every smaller ball. Local elliptic regularity therefore makes $h$ smooth. Thus $T-S=\ddc h$ is smooth and carried by a Lebesgue null set, so its coefficient measures vanish. This proves equality locally, and hence globally.
\end{proof}

Pushforward by a proper map commutes with restriction to the inverse image of a Borel set. Hence polar curvature is compatible with modifications, even when the pole set is not closed.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:polar-curvature-pushforward}\index{Polar curvature!pushforward}
Let $\pi:Y\to X$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Put $S=\{u=-\infty\}$ and
\[
 T=\theta+\ddc u,\qquad
 T_Y=\pi^*\theta+\ddc(u\circ\pi).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-curvature-pushforward}
 \pi_*\bigl(\mathbf1_{\pi^{-1}(S)}T_Y\bigr)=\mathbf1_ST.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}, $u\circ\pi\in\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$. Change of variables on the locus where $\pi$ is an isomorphism, whose complement has Lebesgue measure zero in both manifolds, gives $\pi_*(u\circ\pi)=u$ as distributions and $\pi_*\pi^*\theta=\theta$ as currents. Since pushforward by a proper map commutes with $\ddc$, we get $\pi_*T_Y=T$.

For any Borel set $B\subset X$, the coefficient measures of $\pi_*T_Y$ are pushforwards of the coefficient measures of $T_Y$, multiplied by the smooth coefficients that arise from pulling back a test form. Restriction therefore gives $\pi_*(\mathbf1_{\pi^{-1}(B)}T_Y)=\mathbf1_B\pi_*T_Y$. Applying this to the Borel set $S$ proves \eqref{eq:polar-curvature-pushforward}; closedness of the restricted currents is not used.
\end{proof}

The restriction of a closed positive current to a closed complete pluripolar set is closed; the classical proof uses bounded cutoff functions. Since these cutoff functions converge pointwise to the indicator function of the complement of the pole set, the argument also applies to pole sets that are not closed.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}\index{Polar curvature!closedness}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, let $u$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on $X$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Then
\[
 \operatorname{Pol}_\theta(u)
       :=\mathbf1_{\{u=-\infty\}}(\theta+\ddc u)
\]
is a closed positive $(1,1)$-current. We call it the \emph{polar curvature} of $u$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Locally, adding a smooth quadratic function to $u$ and subtracting a constant gives a negative plurisubharmonic function $a$ with pole set $S=\{u=-\infty\}$. Put $\Theta=\ddc a$. Since smooth forms give $S$ zero mass, the polar current equals $\mathbf1_S\Theta$ and is positive. In dimension one it is closed for degree reasons.

Now suppose that $n\geq2$. Choose a smooth convex nondecreasing function $\chi$ on $\mathbb R$ with $\chi(t)=0$ for $t\leq1/2$ and $\chi(1)=1$, and a smooth function $0\leq\vartheta\leq1$ that vanishes near zero and equals one near one. Set
\[
 w_k=\chi(\mathrm{e}^{a/k}),\qquad k\geq1.
\]
These functions are bounded and plurisubharmonic, with $0\leq w_k\leq1$. They vanish on $S$ and tend to one at every point outside $S$. Hence $\vartheta(w_k)\Theta$ converges locally, coefficientwise in total variation, to $\mathbf1_{X\setminus S}\Theta$. Moreover, $\vartheta'(w_k)$ vanishes on $S$ and tends to zero everywhere.

Fix a constant strongly positive $(n-2,n-2)$-form $\beta$ and put $\Xi=\Theta\wedge\beta$. The bounded Bedford--Taylor chain rule gives
\[
 \mathrm d\bigl(\vartheta(w_k)\Xi\bigr)
       =\vartheta'(w_k)\,\mathrm dw_k\wedge\Xi.
\]
Indeed, approximate $w_k$ on a smaller ball by a decreasing sequence of smooth plurisubharmonic functions. Dominated convergence gives convergence of the corresponding $\vartheta$-weighted currents, while the convergence of bounded Bedford--Taylor energies and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality give convergence of the differentiated expressions; this proves the chain rule for $w_k$. For a smooth compactly supported test one-form $\alpha$, choose a nonnegative smooth cutoff $\tau$ equal to one near its support. Since $w_k\geq0$ and $\Xi$ is closed and positive, bounded integration by parts gives
\[
 2\int\tau\,\mathrm dw_k\wedge\dc w_k\wedge\Xi
       \leq\int\tau\,\ddc(w_k^2)\wedge\Xi
       =\int w_k^2\ddc\tau\wedge\Xi\leq C.
\]
Here $C$ depends only on the local mass of $\Xi$ and the fixed cutoff. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for a positive current now yields
\[
 \bigl|\langle\vartheta'(w_k)\,\mathrm dw_k\wedge\Xi,
                    \alpha\rangle\bigr|^2
       \leq C_\alpha\int_{\operatorname{supp}\alpha}
                 |\vartheta'(w_k)|^2\,\Xi\wedge\kappa
       \longrightarrow0,
\]
where $\kappa$ is the Euclidean form; the limit follows by dominated convergence with respect to the fixed locally finite trace measure. Hence $\mathrm d(\vartheta(w_k)\Xi)\to0$, and $\mathbf1_{X\setminus S}\Theta\wedge\beta$ is closed. Since constant strongly positive forms span all constant forms of bidegree $(n-2,n-2)$, the current $\mathbf1_{X\setminus S}\Theta$ is closed. Subtracting it from $\Theta$ shows that $\mathbf1_S\Theta$ is closed. The local currents agree on overlaps and give the asserted global current.
\end{proof}
