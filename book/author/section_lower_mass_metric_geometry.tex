\subsection{A balanced metric and a finite upper mass}
\label{sec:lower-mass-metric-geometry}

The quotient threefold has a balanced metric with finite upper mass, despite the vanishing of its lower mass. A direct calculation expresses the loss of mass as vertical Dirichlet energy along the elliptic fibers. It also shows why potentials separated into a periodic radial part and a base part cannot realize the degeneration. Put $D=\mathrm{d}t\wedge\dc t$, so that the metric already constructed is $\omega=\alpha+\beta+D$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric}\index{Balanced metric!finite upper mass}\index{Elliptic fibration!vanishing lower mass}
On the threefold of \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}, the Hermitian form $\omega=\alpha+\beta+D$ satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\omega^2)=0,\qquad \ddc\omega=2\alpha\wedge\beta\geq0,
 \qquad \int_X\omega^3=24\pi.
\]
For every smooth real function $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-mass-vertical-energy}
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^3
 =24\pi-6\int_X\mathrm{d}u\wedge\dc u\wedge\alpha\wedge\beta.
\end{equation}
Consequently $v_+(\omega)=24\pi$. In particular $X$ has the bounded mass property, while $v_-(h)=0$ for every Hermitian form $h$ on $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Although $t$ is defined on the covering space, both $\mathrm{d}t$ and $\dc t$ descend. The Fubini--Study potentials give
\[
 B:=\ddc t=\beta-\alpha.
\]
The forms $\alpha,\beta$ are closed, and $\alpha^2=\beta^2=0$, since each is pulled back from a curve. As $D^2=0$, we obtain
\[
 \mathrm{d}D=-\mathrm{d}t\wedge B,\qquad
 \ddc D=-B^2=2\alpha\wedge\beta,
\]
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\omega^2)
 =-2(\alpha+\beta+D)\wedge\mathrm{d}t\wedge(\beta-\alpha)=0.
\]
In the last identity, the term involving $D$ has a repeated $\mathrm{d}t$, and
$(\alpha+\beta)\wedge(\beta-\alpha)=0$. Thus $\omega$ is balanced, hence Gauduchon, and the formula for $\ddc\omega$ follows.

On a fiber use $\zeta=a+\mathrm{i}b$ with both real periods $2\pi$. There $t=2a+$ a constant, so
\[
 D|_E=\frac1\pi\,\mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}b,
 \qquad \int_ED=4\pi.
\]
The equality $\omega^3=6\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge D$, fiber integration and the integral-one normalization of each Fubini--Study form give $\int_X\omega^3=24\pi$.

Apply the Gauduchon threefold identity \eqref{eq:threefold-mass-dirichlet} with $\ddc\omega=2\alpha\wedge\beta$ to obtain \eqref{eq:lower-mass-vertical-energy}. Its last integral is nonnegative. Thus every smooth strictly $\omega$-plurisubharmonic mass is at most $24\pi$, and the constant potential attains this value. Bounded exact-background regularization, as in \cref{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema}, identifies the same supremum over bounded potentials. Metric independence in \cref{prop:bmp-metric} proves bounded mass on $X$. The assertion about the lower mass is \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}.
\end{proof}

For an admissible smooth $u$, equality in the upper-mass bound holds precisely when $u$ is constant along every elliptic fiber. Indeed, $\alpha\wedge\beta$ is a positive volume form in the base directions, so the nonnegative integral in \eqref{eq:lower-mass-vertical-energy} vanishes exactly when the fiber derivative of $u$ vanishes. The fibers are connected, and smooth local sections then show that $u$ is the pullback of a smooth function on $\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1$. For the sequence with masses tending to zero, that same integral instead tends to $4\pi$.

Even after adding an arbitrary base potential, a periodic function of $t$ has a positive mass gap. The sharp gap follows from the bounds on its first two derivatives imposed by positivity.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-radial-mass}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!radial gap}
Let $\pi:X\to B=\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1$ be the elliptic fibration above,
and put $L=4\pi$. Let $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be smooth and $L$-periodic. Among smooth real potentials of the form
\[
 u=f(t)+\pi^*v,\qquad f(s+L)=f(s),\qquad v\in C^\infty(B,\R),
 \qquad \omega+\ddc u>0,
\]
the infimum of $\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^3$ is $8$. Every such mass is
strictly greater than $8$, and this infimum is approached already with $v=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $p=f'$. It is $L$-periodic and has mean zero. The chain rule gives
\[
 \omega+\ddc u
 =(1-p)\alpha+(1+p)\beta+\pi^*\ddc v+(1+p')D.
\]
Positivity on the vertical line implies $p'>-1$. On the complex plane
$\ker\partial t$, the projection to the base is an isomorphism and $D$
vanishes. For each fixed value of $t$ modulo $L$, every base point has a
point above it with that value. Thus, writing $\alpha_B,\beta_B$ for the
two Fubini--Study forms on $B$, the form
$(1-p)\alpha_B+(1+p)\beta_B+\ddc v$ is positive on $B$ for each value of
$p$. Integration over the two rulings gives $1-p>0$ and $1+p>0$.
Thus $|p|<1$ and $p'>-1$; when $v=0$, these inequalities also suffice
for strict positivity.

In a logarithmic fiber coordinate $x+\mathrm{i}y$, both real periods are
$2\pi$, while $t=2x+$ a constant and
$D|_E=\pi^{-1}\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y$. Hence
$\int_E F(t)D=\int_0^L F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$ for every $L$-periodic $F$.
Only the vertical derivative of $u$ contributes to
\eqref{eq:lower-mass-vertical-energy}. Fiber integration therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-radial-mass}
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^3
 =6\left(L-\int_0^L p(s)^2\,\mathrm{d}s\right).
\end{equation}

Suppose first that $p\not\equiv0$, and put
$A=\max p>0$, $-B_0=\min p<0$ and $r=A+B_0<2$.
The function $G(s)=(A-s)(s+B_0)$ is nonnegative on $[-B_0,A]$.
Choose a forward interval $I$ of length at most $L$ from a maximum of
$p$ to a subsequent minimum. Since $p'\geq-1$, we have
$G(p)\geq-G(p)p'$. Using the zero mean of $p$, we obtain
\[
 \begin{split}
 AB_0L-\int_0^L p^2\,\mathrm{d}s
 &=\int_0^L G(p)\,\mathrm{d}s
 \geq-\int_I G(p)p'\,\mathrm{d}s\\
 &=\int_{-B_0}^{A}G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{r^3}{6}.
 \end{split}
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
As $AB_0\leq r^2/4$, it follows that
\[
 \int_0^L p^2\,\mathrm{d}s
 \leq\frac{Lr^2}{4}-\frac{r^3}{6}<L-\frac43.
\]
The strict inequality follows because the middle expression is strictly
increasing for $0<r\leq2$, as $L>2$. It also holds when $p=0$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:elliptic-radial-mass} now gives the strict lower bound $8$.

To prove sharpness, put $a=(L-2)/2$ and define an $L$-periodic function
$p_0$ by
\[
 p_0(s)=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&0\leq s<a,\\
  1-(s-a),&a\leq s<a+2,\\
  -1,&a+2\leq s<L.
 \end{cases}
\]
It has mean zero, $|p_0|\leq1$ and
$\int_0^L p_0^2\,\mathrm{d}s=L-4/3$.
Its distributional derivative is $-1$ on the descending interval, zero
on the two constant intervals, and has a positive atom at the period
join. Thus $p_0'\geq-1$ as distributions on the circle.
Let $\rho_\varepsilon$ be a nonnegative smooth periodic approximate
identity of integral one, and set
$p_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)(p_0*\rho_\varepsilon)$ for
$0<\varepsilon<1$. Then $p_\varepsilon$ is smooth, has mean zero, and
\[
 |p_\varepsilon|\leq1-\varepsilon,
 \qquad p_\varepsilon'\geq-1+\varepsilon,
 \qquad p_\varepsilon\longrightarrow p_0\quad\hbox{in }L^2.
\]
Its primitive $f_\varepsilon$ is smooth and periodic, so
$u_\varepsilon=f_\varepsilon(t)$ is admissible. Substitution in
\eqref{eq:elliptic-radial-mass} proves that its mass tends to $8$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower-mass-no-pluriclosed}
The threefold $X$ admits neither a pluriclosed Hermitian metric nor a closed K\"ahler current.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The nonzero smooth semipositive $(2,2)$-form $\alpha\wedge\beta$ is $\ddc$-exact by the preceding calculation. If a Hermitian metric $\eta$ satisfied $\ddc\eta=0$, Stokes would give
\[
 0<\int_X\eta\wedge\alpha\wedge\beta
   =\frac12\int_X\eta\wedge\ddc\omega
   =\frac12\int_X\omega\wedge\ddc\eta=0,
\]
a contradiction. The strict inequality holds because $\eta$ is positive in the vertical direction at every point.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
If a closed positive current $T$ dominated $\varepsilon\omega$ for some $\varepsilon>0$, positivity and current Stokes would likewise give
\[
 0<\varepsilon\int_X\omega\wedge\alpha\wedge\beta
 \leq\int_XT\wedge\alpha\wedge\beta
 =\frac12\int_X\omega\wedge\ddc T=0.
\]
This excludes a closed K\"ahler current.
\end{proof}

The example is therefore outside the Fujiki class, by the characterization recalled in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems}. It shows that a balanced metric does not guarantee positive lower mass, even together with bounded upper mass. In contrast, the Hopf threefold has positive lower mass and infinite upper mass. The two properties are logically independent. Their simultaneous failure on the products in \cref{cor:both-mass-properties-fail} is a third regime. In particular, bounded mass alone cannot replace the positive lower-mass hypothesis in a theorem asserting solvability for every non-pluripolar measure.
