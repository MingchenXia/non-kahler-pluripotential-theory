\section{Moving measures and relative compactness}

The integral criterion also applies to varying Bedford--Taylor measures. Capacity convergence of their bounded potentials gives convergence of the integration error; a common relative lower envelope controls the tails of the solution measures.

\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-moving-measure-continuity}
Under the geometric and common-envelope hypotheses of \cref{prop:relative-common-envelope-criterion}, fix $A\geq0$ and a finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$. Let $\psi_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be uniformly bounded and converge in capacity to a bounded $\psi$. Suppose
\[
 \mu_{u_j}\leq A\omega_{\psi_j}^n+\nu.
\]
Then $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, $u_j\to u$ in capacity, $\mu_u$ is finite, and
\[
 \mu_{u_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_u.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Translate so that $u_j,u\leq0$. The functions $\mathrm{e}^{u_j},\mathrm{e}^u$ are uniformly bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions with a common lower curvature bound. They converge in smooth-volume $L^1$, since the exponential is Lipschitz on $(-\infty,0]$. \Cref{lem:moving-bounded-integrals,lem:fixed-npp-integrals} give
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\omega_{\psi_j}^n\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mathrm{d}\nu\longrightarrow0.
\]
The integration error tends to zero, so \cref{prop:relative-common-envelope-criterion} gives capacity convergence and class membership.

The bounded reference measures have uniformly bounded mass and satisfy a common capacity estimate by \cref{lem:bounded-mixed-capacity}. For a constant $B$ independent of $j,T$,
\[
 \mu_{u_j}(\{u_j<-T\})
 \leq B\operatorname{Cap}_\omega(\{h<-T\})
       +\nu(\{h<-T\})\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly in $j$. The first term tends to zero because $h$ is a genuine quasi-plurisubharmonic function, and the second because $\nu$ is finite and ignores its pole set. The total masses are uniformly bounded by the assumed domination. \Cref{thm:undamped-tail-continuity} gives finiteness of $\mu_u$ and the asserted weak convergence.
\end{proof}
