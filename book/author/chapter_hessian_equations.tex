\chapter{Complex Hessian equations}
\label{chap:hessian-equations}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$,
let $\omega$ be a smooth positive Hermitian form, and fix $2\leq m<n$.
The complex Hessian operator
$(\omega+\ddc u)^m\wedge\omega^{n-m}$ replaces the determinant by an
intermediate symmetric polynomial. Its admissible cone allows negative
eigenvalues, so the product calculus for plurisubharmonic potentials
from \cref{chap:framework} no longer applies directly.

The problem is to recover smoothness from a continuous admissible weak
solution with smooth positive density depending on the potential.
A proper exponential equation supplies the smooth approximations.
The essential estimate controls their complex Hessians without using
derivatives of the prescribed approximate potential. Uniform convergence
then defines continuous mixed products, and obstacle envelopes give the
domination principle that identifies the auxiliary limit. A concave
uniformly elliptic extension brings this limit within classical interior
regularity theory. The determinant case is treated in
\cref{thm:bounded-hermitian-smoothness}; the argument here concerns the
intermediate Hessian degrees.

Bounded admissible potentials are reached by decreasing rather than
uniform approximation. Their recursive currents are first constructed
in a fixed order. Sobolev control of the first Hessian coefficients then
gives symmetry and simultaneous decreasing convergence for two factors.
Local Hessian capacities then control bounded weights against the
remaining coefficient measures. An exact smooth energy variation
proves their quasicontinuity and gives symmetry and simultaneous
decreasing convergence through every Hessian degree.
For the positive shifted products, this control also gives convergence
after multiplication by a bounded decreasing admissible weight.
Continuous functions of decreasing weights also converge against these
currents, which gives locality and the measure inequality for a maximum.
