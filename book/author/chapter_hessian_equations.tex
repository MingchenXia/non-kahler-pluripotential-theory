\chapter{Complex Hessian equations}
\label{chap:hessian-equations}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$,
let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form, and fix $2\leq m<n$.
The complex Hessian operator
$(\omega+\ddc u)^m\wedge\omega^{n-m}$ replaces the determinant by an
intermediate symmetric polynomial. Its admissible cone allows negative
eigenvalues, so the product calculus for plurisubharmonic potentials
from \cref{chap:framework} no longer applies directly. The cone, the
normalized symmetric functions $\sigma_j$ and the admissible
potentials are fixed in \cref{sec:hessian-cone}.

For a continuous admissible weak solution whose smooth positive density
depends on the potential, the difficulty is to obtain derivative
estimates before smoothness is known. Proper exponential equations
associate a smooth solution with each smooth admissible approximation
of the weak solution. A Liouville theorem reduces their gradient bound
to a trace bound (\cref{sec:hessian-liouville}), and the trace estimate
of \cref{sec:hessian-trace} is independent of derivatives of the
prescribed approximation. The resulting complex-Hessian bound therefore
survives passage to the limit (\cref{sec:hessian-complex-limits}).
Uniform convergence defines the continuous mixed products, while
obstacle envelopes give the domination principle that identifies the
limit with the original solution (\cref{sec:hessian-continuous-products}).
A concave uniformly elliptic extension then supplies classical interior
regularity (\cref{sec:hessian-continuous-regularity}). The determinant
case is treated in \cref{thm:bounded-hermitian-smoothness}; the argument
here concerns the intermediate Hessian degrees.
Uniform stability also constructs solutions for strictly increasing
exponents, by contraction between constant barriers.

Bounded admissible potentials require decreasing approximation
(\cref{subsec:hessian-bounded-approximation}). The recursion of
\cref{sec:hessian-bounded-currents} first gives currents with measure
coefficients below degree $m$. Sobolev capacity controls first
Hessians but not products of two
(\cref{sec:hessian-sobolev-two-factors}); local Hessian capacities
control multiplication by the discontinuous potentials in all degrees
(\cref{sec:hessian-smooth-capacity}).
Quasicontinuity and weighted convergence make the products symmetric
and allow all approximation indices to tend to their limits together.
At degree $m$, the positive product after wedging with $\omega^{n-m}$
is a Radon measure.

These weighted limits pass locality and the maximum inequality to
bounded potentials (\cref{sec:hessian-bounded-locality}). In global
comparison (\cref{sec:hessian-mass-comparison}), the maximum inequality
leaves a signed total-mass difference. The complex Hessians of the
background powers determine when this difference vanishes for every
bounded admissible list. On small sublevel sets, a modified comparison
estimate absorbs the torsion error on arbitrary Hermitian backgrounds.
It yields strict domination, hence nonvanishing of the Hessian
measure of every bounded potential, bounded stability for proper
equations and smoothness for their solutions with smooth data between
constant barriers.
