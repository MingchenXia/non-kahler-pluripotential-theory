\section{Current tests for smooth positivity}
\label{sec:smooth-cone-current-duality}

Lamari's duality, \cref{thm:lamari}, characterizes pseudoeffective backgrounds by their pairings with Gauduchon metrics. Smooth positivity, and hence nefness, is characterized in the same way by pairing with all positive $\ddc$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-currents. By the Hahn--Banach theorem, the minimum of these pairings over normalized currents is exactly the largest $a$ such that $\theta+\ddc u>ah$ for a smooth function $u$, and the nef boundary is where this minimum vanishes. The full set of currents is needed here: the closure of the smooth tests used in Lamari's duality detects pseudoeffectivity and bigness instead. The minimizing currents at the nef boundary appear again at the maximal time of the Chern--Ricci flow, and positive variational volume forces their determinant-weighted measures to vanish.

Let $X$ again be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$. Fix a Hermitian metric $h$, and let $\mathscr G_h$ be the set of normalized Gauduchon currents of \cref{sec:gauduchon-linear-duality}, that is, positive $\ddc$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-currents $T$ with $\int_Xh\wedge T=1$. This set is weakly compact for $n\geq2$. For $n=1$, elliptic regularity and the maximum principle give $\mathscr G_h=\{1/\int_Xh\}$, so compactness holds in this case as well.
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$, define
\[
 m_h(\theta)=\min_{T\in\mathscr G_h}\int_X\theta\wedge T.
\]
The minimum exists by weak compactness, and it is finite since $-Ch\leq\theta\leq Ch$ for some constant $C$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:smooth-cone-current-duality}\index{Cone duality!smooth forms and currents}
The background $\theta$ has a smooth positive representative if and only if $m_h(\theta)>0$. Moreover, $\theta$ is nef if and only if $m_h(\theta)\geq0$, and
\[
 m_h(\theta)=\sup\{a\in\mathbb R:\ \exists u\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R),
                                  \ \theta+\ddc u>ah\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\theta+\ddc u\geq ah$ with $a>0$ and $u$ smooth, then integration by parts gives $\int_X\theta\wedge T\geq a$ for every $T\in\mathscr G_h$. Since a smooth positive representative satisfies such a bound for some $a>0$ by compactness, this proves one implication.

For the converse, work in the real Fr\'echet space of smooth real $(1,1)$-forms. Let $\mathcal K$ be its open convex cone of strictly positive forms, and let $\mathcal L=\ddc C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$. If the affine subspace $\theta+\mathcal L$ is disjoint from $\mathcal K$, the Hahn--Banach separation theorem for an open convex set and a disjoint affine subspace gives a nonzero continuous real linear functional $T$ with
\[
 T(\theta+\ell)\leq T(\eta)\qquad(\ell\in\mathcal L,\ \eta\in\mathcal K).
\]
Replacing $\ell$ by arbitrary real multiples shows that $T(\mathcal L)=0$. Scaling a fixed $\eta\in\mathcal K$ to zero shows that $T(\theta)\leq0$, while scaling it to infinity shows that $T(\eta)\geq0$. Since every smooth semipositive form is a limit of strictly positive forms, $T$ is a positive current, and positivity makes it a current of order zero. The equality $T(\mathcal L)=0$ says precisely that $\ddc T=0$. Since $T\ne0$ and $h>0$, the trace $T(h)$ is positive, and normalizing $T$ gives an element of $\mathscr G_h$ with nonpositive pairing against $\theta$. This contradicts $m_h(\theta)>0$ and proves the converse.

Applying the equivalence just proved to $\theta-ah$, and using $m_h(\theta-ah)=m_h(\theta)-a$, shows that the set in the supremum is exactly $(-\infty,m_h(\theta))$. Finally, $\theta$ is nef if and only if $\theta+\varepsilon h$ has a smooth positive representative for every $\varepsilon>0$, that is, if and only if $m_h(\theta)+\varepsilon>0$ for all such $\varepsilon$, or equivalently $m_h(\theta)\geq0$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For $n\geq2$, let $\mathscr C_h\subseteq\mathscr G_h$ be the weak closure of the smooth strictly positive normalized Gauduchon forms, and let $\kappa_h(\theta)$ be the linear positivity threshold of \cref{cor:gauduchon-threshold}, which is the minimum of $\int_X\theta\wedge T$ over $T\in\mathscr C_h$, by \cref{sec:gauduchon-linear-duality}. The inclusion $\mathscr C_h\subseteq\mathscr G_h$ gives
$m_h(\theta)\leq\kappa_h(\theta)$.
The larger test set detects smooth positivity and nefness, whereas the weak closure of the smooth tests detects the existence of positive and of strictly positive currents in the class, that is, pseudoeffectivity and bigness. The inequality can be strict, as \cref{ex:gauduchon-current-smoothing-obstruction} shows.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nef-annihilator-determinant}
Let $\theta$ be nef with $\lambda_\mu(\theta)>0$. If $T\in\mathscr G_h$ satisfies $\int_X\theta\wedge T=0$, then the determinant-weighted measure of $T$, computed as in \eqref{eq:weak-gaud-functional} with reference metric $h$ and with $\mu$ in place of $\mathrm e^F\mathrm dV$, vanishes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In dimension one the hypotheses cannot hold, since
$\int_X\theta\wedge T=\lambda_\mu(\theta)/\int_Xh>0$.
Suppose $n\geq2$, and write $D_\mu(T)$ for the total mass of this determinant-weighted measure; for $T=\gamma^{n-1}$ it is the denominator $D_\mu(\gamma)$ of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume}. If $\eta\in\mathscr P$, its solution form $\Omega$ satisfies $\Omega^n=\lambda_\mu(\eta)^n\mu$, so $\Omega/\lambda_\mu(\eta)$ is an admissible test in the infimum of \cref{lem:weak-gaud-determinant-dual}. Together with Stokes' theorem, this gives
\[
 \int_X\eta\wedge T=\int_X\Omega\wedge T
              \geq\lambda_\mu(\eta)D_\mu(T).
\]
Apply this to $\eta=\theta+\varepsilon h$. The left side equals $\varepsilon$, while \cref{thm:gaud-volume-nef-limit} gives $\lambda_\mu(\eta)\to\lambda_\mu(\theta)>0$. Hence $D_\mu(T)=0$. The determinant-weighted measure is positive and has total mass zero, so it vanishes.
\end{proof}

Thus, at a nef background of positive volume, every annihilating current in $\mathscr G_h$ has a coefficient matrix of deficient rank almost everywhere with respect to its trace measure. This alone does not force the trace measure to be supported on an analytic subset.

\subsection{Maximal time of the Chern--Ricci flow}
\label{subsec:chern-ricci-maximal-time}

The Chern--Ricci flow deforms a Hermitian metric by $\partial_t\omega=-\operatorname{Ric}(\omega)$. Since the Chern--Ricci forms of any two Hermitian metrics differ by a $\ddc$-exact form, a solution starting at $\omega_0$ stays in the affine family $\omega_0-t\operatorname{Ric}(\omega_0)$ modulo $\ddc$ of smooth functions. It can therefore exist only as long as this family has smooth positive representatives. Conversely, a positive representative at a later time provides a uniformly positive reference path, and parabolic estimates exclude an earlier singularity. The current criterion of \cref{thm:smooth-cone-current-duality} then computes the lifetime.

Let $\omega_0$ be the initial Hermitian metric. For a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$ we use the Chern--Ricci form
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}(\omega)=-\mathrm{i}\partial\bar\partial\log\omega^n
                          =-2\pi\ddc\log\omega^n,
\]
where $\omega^n$ is identified with its coefficient in local holomorphic coordinates; a change of coordinates changes $\log\omega^n$ by a pluriharmonic function and does not affect the form. This normalization fixes the time scale of the flow $\partial_t\omega=-\operatorname{Ric}(\omega)$. Put $\chi=\operatorname{Ric}(\omega_0)$ and $\theta_t=\omega_0-t\chi$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:chern-ricci-maximal-time}\index{Chern--Ricci flow!maximal time}
The Chern--Ricci flow starting from $\omega_0$ has a unique maximal smooth solution on $[0,\tau)$, where
\[
 \tau=\sup\{t\geq0:\ \theta_t\text{ has a smooth positive representative}\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Call a time $t\geq0$ admissible if $\theta_t$ has a smooth positive representative. The admissible times form an interval containing zero, since convex combinations of positive representatives at two times are positive representatives at the intermediate times, and the set is open because positivity is an open condition. We show that the flow exists on $[0,t_1)$ for every admissible $t_1>0$ and that it cannot exist beyond $\tau$.

\emph{Step 1: the scalar equation.} Fix an admissible $t_1>0$, and choose a smooth real function $a$ with $\theta_{t_1}+\ddc a>0$. The reference forms
\[
 \gamma_t=\theta_t+\frac t{t_1}\ddc a
          =\left(1-\frac t{t_1}\right)\omega_0
             +\frac t{t_1}(\theta_{t_1}+\ddc a),\qquad 0\leq t\leq t_1,
\]
are uniformly positive, with all derivatives uniformly bounded. Put
\[
 \Omega=\mathrm{e}^{a/(2\pi t_1)}\omega_0^n.
\]
Then $\dot\gamma_t=-\operatorname{Ric}(\Omega)$, where the Ricci form of a volume form is defined by the same local expression. Consider the scalar equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-ricci-scalar-flow}
 \dot u=2\pi\log\frac{(\gamma_t+\ddc u)^n}{\Omega},
 \qquad \gamma_t+\ddc u>0,\qquad u(0)=0.
\end{equation}
Applying $\ddc$ shows that $\omega(t)=\gamma_t+\ddc u(t)$ solves the metric flow. Conversely, for a solution $\omega(t)$ of the metric flow, the function $u(t)=2\pi\int_0^t\log(\omega(s)^n/\Omega)\,\mathrm ds$ satisfies $\omega(t)=\gamma_t+\ddc u(t)$ and solves \eqref{eq:chern-ricci-scalar-flow}. The linearization of \eqref{eq:chern-ricci-scalar-flow} is $\partial_t-2\pi\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\ddc$, a strictly parabolic operator, so the classical short-time existence theory for smooth strictly parabolic equations gives a solution. The difference of two solutions satisfies a linear parabolic equation whose principal coefficients, the averages of the inverse metrics along the segment between the two solutions, are positive; the maximum principle therefore proves uniqueness.

Suppose that the maximal time $t_*$ of this solution satisfies $t_*<t_1$. We derive estimates independent of $t<t_*$ that allow us to continue the solution.

\emph{Step 2: bounds for $u$ and $\dot u$.} For a large constant $C$, the functions $Ct$ and $-Ct$ are a supersolution and a subsolution of \eqref{eq:chern-ricci-scalar-flow}, respectively: at a spatial extremum of $u$ the complex Hessian has the appropriate sign, and $\gamma_t^n/\Omega$ is bounded above and away from zero. The maximum principle gives $|u|\leq Ct_1$.

Set $v=\dot u$ and $\mathcal L=2\pi\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\ddc$. Differentiation gives
\[
 (\partial_t-\mathcal L)v=2\pi\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\dot\gamma_t,
 \qquad
 (\partial_t-\mathcal L)u=v-2\pi n+2\pi\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\gamma_t.
\]
Since $\gamma_t$ is affine in time, these identities imply
\begin{align*}
 (\partial_t-\mathcal L)(t v-u-2\pi nt)
     &=-2\pi\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\omega_0<0,\\
 (\partial_t-\mathcal L)((t_1-t)v+u+2\pi nt)
     &=2\pi\operatorname{tr}_{\omega(t)}\gamma_{t_1}>0.
\end{align*}
By the maximum principle, the first expression is bounded above by its initial value zero, and the second is bounded below by $\min_Xt_1v(0)$. The bound for $u$ and $t_1-t\geq t_1-t_*>0$ give a lower bound for $v$. The first inequality gives an upper bound for $v$ on $[t_*/2,t_*)$, and the smooth solution bounds it on $[0,t_*/2]$. In particular, $\omega(t)^n/\Omega=\mathrm{e}^{v/(2\pi)}$ is bounded above and away from zero.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\emph{Step 3: a parabolic trace inequality.} We next bound the trace, keeping track of the time derivative of the reference metric. In the coordinates of \cref{lem:hermitian-trace-inequality}, write $g(t)$ for the matrix of $\gamma_t$ and, as in that lemma, $h=g+(u_{i\bar j})$ for the matrix of $\omega(t)$. Put
\[
 S=\operatorname{tr}_g h,\qquad B=\operatorname{tr}_h g,
 \qquad F=\log\frac{\Omega}{\gamma_t^n},
\]
so that $\log(\det h/\det g)=v/(2\pi)+F$. The family $g(t)$ has uniformly bounded Chern torsion and curvature, so at each fixed time \cref{lem:hermitian-trace-inequality} applies with uniform constants. Direct differentiation of $S$ gives
\[
 \partial_t\log S
   =\frac{\Delta_g v+\operatorname{tr}_g\dot g}{S}
       -\frac{\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}\dot g\,g^{-1}h)}{S}.
\]
In $(\partial_t-\mathcal L)\log S$, the term $\Delta_gv/S$ cancels the corresponding term in the lower bound for $\mathcal L\log S$ given by the lemma. With $\tau_k=\sum_iT_{ik}^i$ the torsion trace of $g(t)$, as in the lemma, we obtain on $\{S\geq1\}$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chern-ricci-parabolic-trace}
 (\partial_t-\mathcal L)\log S
 \leq-\frac{4\pi}{S^2}\operatorname{Re}(h^{k\bar q}\tau_k S_{\bar q})+CB+C.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\Delta_gF$ and $\operatorname{tr}_g\dot g$ are uniformly bounded, $S^{-1}\leq B/n^2$, and $-Cg\leq\dot g\leq Cg$ bounds the last quotient above. These bounds account for all the terms coming from the time dependence of the reference metric.

\emph{Step 4: the trace bound.} Choose a constant $C_0$ such that $1\leq s=u+C_0\leq D$ on the whole time interval, and maximize
\[
 Q=\log S-Au+s^{-1}
\]
on $X\times[0,t_0]$, where $t_0<t_*$ and the constant $A\geq1$ will be fixed independently of $t_0$. If the maximum is attained at time zero, the initial data bound $Q$. At a maximum with positive time, $\partial\log S=(A+s^{-2})\partial u$. Since $|\tau|_h^2\leq CB$, Young's inequality bounds the absolute value of the first term on the right of \eqref{eq:chern-ricci-parabolic-trace} by
\[
 2\pi s^{-3}|\partial u|_h^2
       +\frac{C(A+1)^2s^3}{S^2}B.
\]
If $S^2\leq(A+1)^2D^3$, the trace is already bounded at the maximum. Otherwise the last coefficient is at most $C$, and
\[
 \begin{split}
 0\leq(\partial_t-\mathcal L)Q
 &\leq CB+C-(A+s^{-2})(v-2\pi n+2\pi B)
                       -2\pi s^{-3}|\partial u|_h^2\\
 &\leq(C-2\pi A)B+C(A+1).
 \end{split}
\]
Choosing $A$ with $2\pi A>C+1$ bounds $B$ at the maximum. In terms of the eigenvalues, $S\leq n(\det h/\det g)B^{n-1}$ for $n\geq2$, and $S=\det h/\det g$ for $n=1$, so the determinant bound of Step~2 bounds $S$ at the maximum in every case. The bound for $u$ transfers this estimate from $Q$ to $S$ everywhere, uniformly in $t_0$, and the lower bound for the determinant then bounds the smallest eigenvalue away from zero. Thus $\omega(t)$ and $\gamma_t$ are uniformly equivalent for $t<t_*$.

\emph{Step 5: higher estimates and continuation.} Uniform ellipticity now gives the higher estimates by classical local parabolic regularity. In a coordinate ball, write the logarithmic determinant as a concave function of the complex part of the real Hessian. On the compact set of positive matrices just obtained, its supporting affine functions have uniformly positive and bounded slopes, and their infimum extends the operator to a concave, uniformly elliptic function of all real symmetric matrices. The dependence on space and time enters only through the smooth family $g(t)$ and the fixed density $\Omega$. The parabolic Evans--Krylov estimate gives uniform $C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}$ bounds away from the initial time, and the smooth solution covers a fixed initial interval. Differentiating the equation and applying parabolic Schauder estimates then bounds all spatial and time derivatives uniformly. This is the parabolic counterpart of the local argument used in \cref{cor:classical-higher-estimate}.

The bounds for the time derivatives show that $u(t)$ converges in every spatial $C^k$ norm as $t\uparrow t_*$. By uniform ellipticity, the smooth limit still has a strictly positive solution form, and short-time existence with this limit as initial datum extends the solution beyond $t_*$, a contradiction. Hence the flow exists up to every admissible time $t_1$, and by uniqueness these solutions patch together on $[0,\tau)$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\emph{Step 6: maximality.} Integrating the metric flow gives
\[
 \omega(t)=\theta_t+\ddc\left(2\pi\int_0^t
                   \log\frac{\omega(s)^n}{\omega_0^n}\,\mathrm{d}s\right).
\]
Hence every time at which a smooth positive solution exists is admissible, which proves maximality.
\end{proof}

Combining the continuation theorem with the current criterion gives a numerical description of the lifetime.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:chern-ricci-current-lifetime}
Let
\[
 b=\max_{T\in\mathscr G_{\omega_0}}\int_X\chi\wedge T.
\]
Then the maximal smooth existence time is
\[
 \tau=\begin{cases}1/b,&b>0,\\+\infty,&b\leq0.\end{cases}
\]
If $\tau<\infty$, the limiting background $\theta_\tau$ is nef and there is a current $T_*\in\mathscr G_{\omega_0}$ with $\int_X\theta_\tau\wedge T_*=0$. If moreover $\lambda_\mu(\theta_\tau)>0$, every such annihilating current has zero determinant-weighted measure.
\end{corollary}
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
For $t\geq0$, the normalization of the test currents gives the identity
\[
 m_{\omega_0}(\theta_t)=1-tb.
\]
\Cref{thm:smooth-cone-current-duality,thm:chern-ricci-maximal-time} give the formula for $\tau$. When $b>0$, the maximum defining $b$ is attained by weak compactness, and a maximizer $T_*$ annihilates $\theta_\tau$. The same identity gives $m_{\omega_0}(\theta_\tau)=0$, hence nefness. The last assertion is \cref{prop:nef-annihilator-determinant}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Along the flow, $\omega(t)=\theta_t+\ddc\varphi_t$ with a smooth potential $\varphi_t$. Consequently, for every fixed $T\in\mathscr G_{\omega_0}$,
\[
 \int_X\omega(t)\wedge T
       =1-t\int_X\chi\wedge T.
\]
These traces describe the obstruction at the maximal time without any bound on the total Monge--Amp\`ere mass. They do not locate the singularities of the flow, nor do they give smooth convergence away from an analytic set; such geometric questions require local estimates in addition to these scalar pairings.
