\subsection{Polar curvature in higher dimension}
\label{subsec:polar-first-current-obstruction}

On a curve, the polar curvature records every logarithmic pole. In higher dimension, the restriction of the curvature current to the pole set no longer does: it vanishes for an isolated logarithmic pole, although such a pole changes the multiplier ideals. The curve metric therefore cannot be extended simply by replacing the polar measure with this restricted current.

Here $X$ has dimension $n\geq2$, and $\omega$ is a Hermitian form on $X$. For $p\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, the \emph{first polar current} of $p$ is the closed positive current
\[
 \operatorname{Pol}_1(p)=\operatorname{Pol}_\omega(p)
      =\mathbf1_{\{p=-\infty\}}(\omega+\ddc p)
\]
of \cref{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:isolated-pole-polar-current-blindness}\index{Model potential!polar current obstruction}\index{Multiplier ideal!isolated pole}
For every $x\in X$, there is $a_0>0$ such that, for each rational $a\in(0,a_0)$, there is an ideal model $p_a$ with analytic singularities and pole set $\{x\}$ satisfying
\[
 p_a=a\log|z|^2+O(1)\quad\text{near }x,\qquad
 \operatorname{Pol}_1(p_a)=0.
\]
The models $p_a$ are pairwise ordinarily inequivalent and have pairwise distinct families of multiplier ideals $\mathcal I(tp_a)$, $t>0$, whereas also $\operatorname{Pol}_1(0)=0$. More precisely, with $\mathfrak m_x$ the maximal ideal at $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isolated-pole-model-ideals}
 \mathcal I(tp_a)_x
       =\mathfrak m_x^{\max\{\lfloor ta\rfloor-n+1,0\}}
       \qquad(t>0).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose the cutoff potentials $u_a$ of \cref{lem:small-isolated-log-poles} for the single point $x$, with $a_0$ smaller than the reciprocal of the constant in that lemma, and put $p_a=P_\omega[u_a]$. By \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, $p_a$ is an ideal model with analytic singularities, ordinarily equivalent to $u_a$. It is locally bounded away from $x$, and its only pole has the stated logarithmic coefficient. Distinct coefficients give unbounded differences near $x$. Formula \eqref{eq:isolated-pole-model-ideals} follows from \cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal} with codimension $n$, and its exponents distinguish any two coefficients at sufficiently large weights.

It remains to show that the current has no coefficient mass at $x$. In coordinates centered at $x$, let $\beta=\ddc|z|^2$ and let
\[
 \nu=(\omega+\ddc p_a)\wedge\beta^{n-1}.
\]
This is a positive measure. Choose smooth cutoff functions $0\leq\chi_\varepsilon\leq1$, equal to one on the ball of radius $\varepsilon$ and supported in the ball of radius $2\varepsilon$, with $-C\varepsilon^{-2}\beta\leq\ddc\chi_\varepsilon\leq C\varepsilon^{-2}\beta$; their complex Hessians are supported in the annulus between the two balls. Since $\beta$ is closed, distributional integration by parts gives
\[
 \int\chi_\varepsilon\,\mathrm d\nu
 =\int\chi_\varepsilon\omega\wedge\beta^{n-1}
       +\int p_a\,\ddc\chi_\varepsilon\wedge\beta^{n-1}.
\]
The logarithmic bound for $p_a$ on the annulus and the Euclidean volume of the annulus therefore give
\[
 0\leq\nu(\{x\})\leq\int\chi_\varepsilon\,\mathrm d\nu
 \leq C_a\bigl(\varepsilon^{2n}
        +\varepsilon^{2n-2}(1+|\log\varepsilon|)\bigr)
       \longrightarrow0.
\]
This is where $n\geq2$ is used. By positivity, the coefficient measures of $\omega+\ddc p_a$ are bounded by a constant multiple of its trace measure $\nu$, so they have no mass at $x$. Hence the restriction to the pole set is zero, as asserted. The zero potential is an ordinary and an ideal model because $\omega>0$, and its pole set is empty.
\end{proof}

Consequently, no comparison that depends only on the fixed background and on $\operatorname{Pol}_1$ separates these analytic ideal models, and a complete topology in higher dimension must involve singularity data of higher codimension.

A modification can turn the isolated pole into a divisor. For a modification $\pi\colon Y\to X$, pullback maps $\PSH(X,\omega)$ to $\PSH(Y,\pi^*\omega)$ by \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}, and we define
\[
 \operatorname{Pol}_\pi(p)
 =\mathbf1_{\{p\circ\pi=-\infty\}}
       (\pi^*\omega+\ddc(p\circ\pi)).
\]

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:exceptional-polar-current-detects-pole}\index{Model potential!exceptional polar curvature}
Fix $x$ and the models $p_a$ of \cref{prop:isolated-pole-polar-current-blindness}. Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be the blowup at $x$, with exceptional divisor $E$, and let $\eta$ be a Hermitian form on $Y$. Then
\[
 \operatorname{Pol}_\pi(p_a)=a[E].
\]
For $\mu_a=\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(p_a)\wedge\eta^{n-1}$ and any two admissible coefficients $a,b$,
\[
 \|\mu_a-\mu_b\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       =|a-b|\int_E\eta^{n-1}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On relatively compact blowup charts, with $z=(s,sw)$, one has $p_a\circ\pi=a\log|s|^2+O(1)$ along $E=\{s=0\}$. Thus the pole set is $E$ and the generic Lelong number along it is $a$. By the proof of \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal}, the restriction of $\pi^*\omega+\ddc(p_a\circ\pi)$ to $E$ is $a[E]$. Consequently $\mu_a=a[E]\wedge\eta^{n-1}$. The positive measure $[E]\wedge\eta^{n-1}$ has mass $\int_E\eta^{n-1}>0$, which proves the formula for the total variation.
\end{proof}

A fixed modification detects isolated poles only over the set where it is not an isomorphism, so countably many modifications still miss the poles at all points outside a countable union of nowhere dense analytic sets.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:countable-modification-polar-blindness}\index{Model potential!countable modification obstruction}
For every countable family of smooth modifications $\pi_j\colon Y_j\to X$, there are pairwise ordinarily inequivalent ideal $\omega$-models $p_a$ with analytic singularities and with pairwise distinct families of multiplier ideals, such that
\[
 \operatorname{Pol}_{\pi_j}(p_a)=0
       \qquad\text{for every }j\text{ and every }a.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each $j$, choose a proper closed analytic subset $S_j\subset X$ over whose complement $\pi_j$ is an isomorphism. Each $S_j$ is nowhere dense, so the Baire category theorem gives a point $x\notin\bigcup_jS_j$. Apply \cref{prop:isolated-pole-polar-current-blindness} at this point.

Since $x\notin S_j$, the only pole of $p_a\circ\pi_j$ is the point $y_j=\pi_j^{-1}(x)$, and $\pi_j$ maps a neighborhood of $y_j$ biholomorphically onto a neighborhood of $x$. Near $y_j$, the current $\pi_j^*\omega+\ddc(p_a\circ\pi_j)$ is the pullback of $\omega+\ddc p_a$ by this biholomorphism, so $\operatorname{Pol}_{\pi_j}(p_a)$ is the pullback of $\operatorname{Pol}_1(p_a)=0$. The same proposition gives the ordinary inequivalence and the distinct multiplier ideals.
\end{proof}
