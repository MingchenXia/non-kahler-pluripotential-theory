\subsection{Dini moduli and limits of distances}
\label{sec:dini-metric-distance}

The annular argument also applies to a continuous potential whose modulus need not be a power. The condition concerns the square root of that modulus. It supplies uniform distance estimates for families even when their H\"older constants are not controlled.

Retain a coordinate domain $U\subset\C^n$ and its Euclidean form. Let now $v\in C^0(U)$, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, and assume $T=\theta+\ddc v\geq0$. On the nested charts under consideration, choose a nondecreasing function $m:(0,r_0)\to[0,+\infty)$ with $m(r)\to0$ such that
\[
 |v(x)-v(y)|\leq m(|x-y|),\qquad |x-y|<r_0.
\]
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dini-distance-modulus}
 D(r)=r+\int_0^r\frac{\sqrt{m(t)}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t.
\end{equation}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:dini-current-distance}\index{Dini modulus!metric distance}\index{Metric completion!Dini potential}
Assume $D(r_0/2)<+\infty$, and suppose that $T$ defines a smooth strictly positive metric $\eta$ on $U\setminus A$ for a proper analytic subset $A$. On a sufficiently small inner chart there are constants $C,r_1>0$ such that
\[
 d_\eta^U(x,y)\leq C D(8|x-y|),
 \qquad x,y\notin A,\quad |x-y|<r_1.
\]
The constants depend on the charts and an upper bound for $|\theta|$, and the displayed modulus records the dependence on $v$. Each point of $A$ in the inner chart has a unique completion limit. If these hypotheses hold in a finite chart cover of a compact connected manifold, the intrinsic metric completion is a compact continuous metric quotient of the manifold. Deleting a nonempty $A$ makes the regular metric incomplete.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $d=2n$ and $\mu=T\wedge\omega_{\mathrm E}^{n-1}$. The same cutoff calculation as in \cref{thm:holder-current-distance}, with a constant subtracted from $v$, gives
\[
 \mu(B(x,s))\leq C\bigl(s^d+s^{d-2}m(2s)\bigr).
\]
It uses only the bound for $\theta$; closedness of $\theta$ is unnecessary. If $\tau$ is the Euclidean trace of $\eta$, then $\tau\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq C\mu$ on the regular locus. Polar integration bounds the average lengths of segments from a regular point $x$ over $B(x,R)$ by
\[
 C\int_{B(x,R)}\sqrt{\tau(\zeta)}
                      |\zeta-x|^{1-d}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(\zeta).
\]
On an annulus of outer radius $s$, Cauchy--Schwarz and the trace bound make its contribution at most
\[
 Cs^{1-d}s^{d/2}
       \bigl(s^d+s^{d-2}m(2s)\bigr)^{1/2}
 \leq C\bigl(s+\sqrt{m(2s)}\bigr).
\]
For $s_j=2^{-j}R$, monotonicity gives
\[
 \sum_{j\geq0}\sqrt{m(2s_j)}
 \leq\frac1{\log2}\int_0^{4R}\frac{\sqrt{m(t)}}t\,\mathrm{d}t.
\]
The intervals $[2s_j,4s_j]$ have disjoint interiors, which proves the inequality without a doubling condition on $m$. The average length is therefore at most $CD(4R)$.

Average the two segments through a ball of radius $|x-y|/2$ about the midpoint of $x,y$, using $R=2|x-y|$ for each endpoint. Its volume is comparable to $R^d$, so the same bound supplies a broken path of length at most $CD(8|x-y|)$. The analytic avoidance argument in the proof of \cref{thm:holder-current-distance} makes almost every such path avoid $A$. Nested charts keep it inside $U$.

The integrability in \eqref{eq:dini-distance-modulus} implies $D(r)\to0$. Hence any two regular sequences approaching the same point are mutually Cauchy and define one completion point. The completion and compact-quotient argument of \cref{thm:holder-current-distance} applies with this modulus. A local positive lower metric bound near a regular point prevents a sequence approaching $A$ from converging there. This also proves incompleteness when $A$ is nonempty.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dini-distance-rates}\index{Dini modulus!logarithmic distance rate}
For $0<\beta\leq1$, a modulus $m(r)\leq Cr^\beta$ gives $d_\eta^U(x,y)\leq C'|x-y|^{\beta/2}$. If instead
\[
 m(r)\leq C(-\log r)^{-a},\qquad a>2,
\]
then for sufficiently close regular points,
\[
 d_\eta^U(x,y)\leq C'(-\log|x-y|)^{1-a/2}.
\]
These are sufficient bounds; no necessity of the Dini condition is asserted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Integrating the square root of the power modulus gives a multiple of $r^{\beta/2}$. For the logarithmic modulus, the substitution $s=-\log t$ gives
\[
 \int_0^r\frac{(-\log t)^{-a/2}}t\,\mathrm{d}t
 =\frac{(-\log r)^{1-a/2}}{a/2-1}.
\]
In either case the term $r$ in $D(r)$ is bounded by the asserted rate for small $r$. Apply the theorem; the fixed dilation of the argument changes only the constants.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dini-family-distance-limits}\index{Metric distance!uniform pseudometric limit}
Let $X$ be a compact connected complex manifold and $\omega_0$ a Hermitian metric. For each $j$, let $T_j=\theta_j+\ddc v_j\geq0$, where $\theta_j$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, $v_j$ is continuous, and $T_j$ is a smooth strictly positive metric off a proper analytic set $A_j$. Assume
\[
 |\theta_j|_{\omega_0}\leq\Lambda,\qquad
 |v_j(x)-v_j(y)|\leq m(d_{\omega_0}(x,y)),
\]
with one fixed $\Lambda$ and one nondecreasing modulus $m$ tending to zero such that the function in \eqref{eq:dini-distance-modulus} satisfies $D(r_0/2)<+\infty$. Pull back each completed distance to $X$ by its continuous completion map, and denote it by $d_j$. Their diameters are uniformly bounded. A subsequence converges uniformly on $X\times X$ to a continuous pseudometric. If $T_j\geq c\omega_0$ on the regular loci for one $c>0$, the limit is a metric and dominates $\sqrt c\,d_{\omega_0}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A finite fixed chart cover and the theorem give common constants $C,r_1>0$ such that
\[
 d_j(x,y)\leq CD(Cd_{\omega_0}(x,y)),
 \qquad d_{\omega_0}(x,y)<r_1.
\]
For a pair meeting $A_j$, take regular pairs tending to it in the background topology. The completed distance and $D$ are continuous on these pairs, so the same estimate holds on $X\times X$. No uniform bound on the geometry of the analytic sets enters the constants. Divide a background minimizing geodesic into a uniformly bounded number of short pieces. The triangle inequality gives a uniform diameter bound. It also gives
\[
 |d_j(x,y)-d_j(x',y')|
 \leq d_j(x,x')+d_j(y,y'),
\]
so the distance functions are equicontinuous. Arzel\`a--Ascoli supplies $d_{j_k}\to d_\infty$ in the uniform norm on $X\times X$. Nonnegativity, symmetry, zero diagonal and the triangle inequality pass to the limit. The lower metric bound gives $d_j\geq\sqrt c\,d_{\omega_0}$ first on regular pairs and then on all pairs, proving the last assertion.

Degeneration is possible without that lower bound. For $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$, take $\theta_j=\varepsilon_j\omega_0$ and $v_j=0$, with $A_j=\varnothing$. These satisfy the hypotheses with $m(r)=r^2$, and $d_j=\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}\,d_{\omega_0}$ converges uniformly to zero.
\end{proof}
