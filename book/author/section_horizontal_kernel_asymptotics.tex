\section{Horizontal poles over projective space}
\label{sec:horizontal-kernel-asymptotics}

For a hyperplane in projective space, the descended vanishing conditions give binomial kernels. Their large-deviation rate is the obstacle envelope of the original upstairs singularity. Concentration of evaluation sections then identifies the limiting measure for every smooth integration volume, improving the preceding weak limit to total variation.

Specialize \cref{sec:horizontal-center-constraints} to
$B=\mathbb P^q$, $A=\mathcal O_B(k)$ and $Z=\{Z_0=0\}$, where $q,r,k$ are positive integers and $0<c<k$. Use the Fubini--Study metric on $A$, with curvature $\theta=k\omega_B$ and $\int_B\omega_B^q=1$. Retain the submersion $\pi:X\to B$, its connected $r$-dimensional fibers, and the selecting singularity \eqref{eq:horizontal-center-singularity} along a section $Y$ over $Z$. Write
\[
 R=\frac{|Z_0|^2}{\sum_{i=0}^q|Z_i|^2},\qquad
 a=\frac ck,\qquad D_c=\{R\geq a\},\qquad
 C_c=\frac{(k-c)^q}{q!}.
\]
For any smooth positive probability volume $\nu_X$, fiber integration gives
$\nu_B=\pi_*\nu_X=w\omega_B^q$ with $w>0$ smooth. Let $B_m^B$ and $B_m^X=B_m^B\circ\pi$ be the kernels in the unweighted norms on the descended spaces $V_m$. Put $M=\max_Bw$.

The selecting potential need not be $\pi^*\theta$-plurisubharmonic. Its domination envelope in this curvature background is
\[
 \mathcal P(\varphi)=
 \left(\sup\{u\in\PSH(X,\pi^*\theta):u\leq0,\ u\preceq\varphi\}\right)^*.
\]
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-projective-envelope}
 u_c(R)=
 \begin{cases}
 -k\left(a\log\dfrac aR+(1-a)\log\dfrac{1-a}{1-R}\right),&0<R<a,\\
 0,&a\leq R\leq1,\\
 -\infty,&R=0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:horizontal-projective-kernels}\index{Bergman kernel!horizontal pole}
The envelope satisfies $\mathcal P(\varphi)=u_c\circ\pi$. With
$\ell_m=\lfloor mc\rfloor$ and $t_m=\max\{\ell_m-r,0\}$,
\[
 N_m=\dim V_m=\binom{km-t_m+q}{q}
       =C_cm^q+O(m^{q-1}).
\]
The intrinsic growth degree is $q<\dim X$. For every $m\geq1$, in the extended-value sense,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-kernel-lower}
 \frac1m\log B_m^X\geq\mathcal P(\varphi)-\frac{\log M}{m}.
\end{equation}
These potentials converge to $\mathcal P(\varphi)$ globally in $L^1$ for every smooth positive volume and locally uniformly off $\pi^{-1}(Z)$. In the normalization of \cref{sec:partial-selected-space}, the kernel measures converge in total variation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-kernel-tv}
 \begin{split}
 \frac{q!}{m^q}B_m^B\nu_B&\longrightarrow\mu_c:=\mathbf1_{D_c}\theta^q,\\
 \frac{q!}{m^q}B_m^X\nu_X&\longrightarrow
       \Lambda_{\nu_X}:=\frac{k^q\mathbf1_{D_c}\circ\pi}{w\circ\pi}\nu_X.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The limiting masses are $(k-c)^q$, and $\pi_*\Lambda_{\nu_X}=\mu_c$. Dividing the unscaled kernel measures by $N_m$ gives the probability limits $\mu_c/(k-c)^q$ and $\Lambda_{\nu_X}/(k-c)^q$. Normalization by $m^{q+r}$ gives zero in total variation.

For every sequence of intermediate spaces $W_m\subseteq U_m\subseteq V_m$, with the same restricted norm, the lower bound and all these convergences remain valid. Their dimensions are $C_cm^q+O(m^{q-1})$; dimension normalization uses $\dim U_m$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The domination envelope}

The base envelope can be computed on the affine chart $Z_0\ne0$. Set $z_i=Z_i/Z_0$, $s=|z|^2$, $l=k-c$, $s_0=l/c$, and
$C=k\log k-c\log c-l\log l$. The reference weight is $\phi_A=k\log(1+s)$, and put
\[
 F_0(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 k\log(1+s),&s\leq s_0,\\
 l\log s+C,&s\geq s_0.
 \end{cases}
\]
As a function of $\log s$, it is increasing and convex: its derivative increases to $l$ and then stays constant. Thus it is plurisubharmonic. The branches match to first order, and the tangent-line inequality gives $F_0\leq\phi_A$.
On a chart $Z_j\ne0$, $j\geq1$, with $x=Z_0/Z_j$ and the other ratios denoted by $\zeta$, its transformed weight near $Z$ is
\[
 F_j=c\log|x|^2+l\log(1+|\zeta|^2)+C.
\]
It therefore defines a global plurisubharmonic metric weight with a divisor pole of coefficient $c$ and a smooth remainder.

For any competing plurisubharmonic weight $v\leq\phi_A$ with $v\leq c\log|x|^2+O(1)$ near $Z$, the obstacle gives $v_0\leq F_0$ on $s\leq s_0$. On each complex line $z=t\xi$, $|\xi|=1$, either $v_0(t\xi)\equiv-\infty$ or
$v_0(t\xi)-l\log|t|^2$ is subharmonic on $|t|>\sqrt{s_0}$ and bounded above near infinity. In the coordinate $1/t$, the puncture is removable. The maximum principle bounds this difference by $C$. Varying $\xi$ proves $v\leq F$ globally. Consequently $F=\phi_A+u_c$ is the divisor obstacle envelope, and its contact set is $D_c$.

By \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion}, every $\pi^*\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential is $v\circ\pi$ for a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic $v$. Restriction to the local section $w_1=\cdots=w_r=0$ shows that
$v\circ\pi\preceq\varphi$ implies $v\leq c\log|x|^2+O(1)$.
Conversely this divisor bound and
$|x|^2\leq|x|^2+\sum_j|w_j|^2$ give the domination near $Y$. A finite cover of $Y$ gives one constant there; on the compact complement of a smaller neighborhood, $\varphi$ is bounded below and $v\leq0$. Thus one constant works on $X$. The competitor families correspond, proving $\mathcal P(\varphi)=u_c\circ\pi$. Its pole fills $\pi^{-1}(Z)$, although $\varphi$ is finite on $\pi^{-1}(Z)\setminus Y$.

\subsection{Binomial kernels}

For a fixed integer $j\geq0$, set $d=km$, $t_{m,j}=\max\{\ell_m-j,0\}$, and
$\mathcal H_m^{(j)}=H^0(B,\mathcal O(d)(-t_{m,j}Z))$.
The choices $j=0,r$ give $W_m,V_m$. Under the reference norm for $\nu_0=\omega_B^q$, their kernels are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-binomial-kernel}
 B_{m,j}^0(R)=A_dQ_{m,j}(R),\qquad
 A_d=\binom{d+q}{q},\qquad
 Q_{m,j}(R)=\mathbb P\{\operatorname{Bin}(d,R)\geq t_{m,j}\}.
\end{equation}
Apply the monomial norm formula \eqref{eq:projective-monomial-norm} in degree $d$. Coordinate circle invariance makes the monomials orthogonal, and selection retains those with exponent of $Z_0$ at least $t_{m,j}$. The multinomial identity, summed over the other exponents, gives \eqref{eq:horizontal-binomial-kernel}. Counting these monomials gives
$\dim\mathcal H_m^{(j)}=\binom{d-t_{m,j}+q}{q}=C_cm^q+O(m^{q-1})$.

Write
$D(v\mid R)=v\log(v/R)+(1-v)\log((1-v)/(1-R))$ and $b=\lfloor da\rfloor$.
If $R\geq b/d$, a binomial mode $\min\{d,\lfloor(d+1)R\rfloor\}$ is at least $b$, so the tail above $t_{m,j}\leq b$ is at least $1/(d+1)$. If $0<R<b/d$, the mass at $b$ for parameter $v=b/d$ is a largest mass, hence at least $1/(d+1)$. For parameter $R$, that mass is at least
$(d+1)^{-1}\exp(-dD(v\mid R))$.
Since
\[
 \frac{\partial D(v\mid R)}{\partial v}
       =\log\frac{v(1-R)}{R(1-v)}\geq0\quad(v\geq R),
\]
it is at least $(d+1)^{-1}\exp(-dD(a\mid R))$.
Together with $u_c\leq0$, these cases give
$Q_{m,j}\geq(d+1)^{-1}\exp(mu_c)$, including $b=0$ and $R=1$.
Since $A_d\geq d+1$, the reference kernels satisfy $m^{-1}\log B_{m,j}^0\geq u_c$; at $R=0$ this holds in the extended sense.

For fixed $0<R<a$, Stirling's formula at the first retained index $t_{m,j}$ gives logarithmic rate $-kD(a\mid R)$. Ratios of successive binomial masses above that index are eventually bounded by a number less than one, because $t_{m,j}/d\to a>R$. The whole tail has the same logarithmic rate. For $R>a$, the binomial variance estimate gives $Q_{m,j}\to1$. At $R=a$, the lower mode bound and $Q_{m,j}\leq1$ give logarithmic limit zero. The functions $m^{-1}\log Q_{m,j}(R)$ increase with $R$, and their limit $u_c$ is continuous on each $[\delta,1]$. A finite partition on which the oscillation of $u_c$ is small upgrades pointwise convergence to uniform convergence there.

The event that the first $t_{m,j}$ trials succeed gives
\[
 c\log R\leq\frac{t_{m,j}}m\log R
       \leq\frac1m\log Q_{m,j}(R)\leq0.
\]
Since $\log R$ is integrable against every smooth volume, dominated convergence proves global $L^1$ convergence of the relative weights. The added term $m^{-1}\log A_d$ tends to zero. For the measures, the variance estimate gives $Q_{m,j}\to\mathbf1_{D_c}$ away from $R=a$. The marginal of $R$ is $q(1-R)^{q-1}\,\mathrm{d}R$: apply the radial computation in the proof of \cref{thm:hopf-point-potential-limit} to $1-R$. Thus the boundary has zero volume. Since $q!A_d/m^q\to k^q$ and these constants are bounded, dominated convergence proves total-variation convergence to $\mu_c$ and
\[
 \mu_c(B)=k^q\int_a^1q(1-R)^{q-1}\,\mathrm{d}R=(k-c)^q.
\]

\subsection{Changing the integration volume}

Fix a positive continuous $w$ on $B$, and put $\delta=\min_Bw>0$ and $M=\max_Bw$. Comparing norms gives
\[
 M^{-1}B_{m,j}^0\leq B_{m,j}^w\leq\delta^{-1}B_{m,j}^0.
\]
This proves the weight lower bound and both weight convergences. To identify the measure limit, more than comparability is needed.

Let $\Pi_m$ be the reference $L^2$ orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal H_m^{(j)}$. In a unit frame at $x$, write $F_{m,x}$ for the evaluation vector of $H^0(B,\mathcal O(d))$ and $K_{m,x}=\Pi_mF_{m,x}$. Formula \eqref{eq:horizontal-binomial-kernel} gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \|F_{m,x}\|_0^2=A_d,\qquad
 \|K_{m,x}\|_0^2=A_dQ_{m,j}(R(x)),\\
 \|F_{m,x}-K_{m,x}\|_0^2=A_d(1-Q_{m,j}(R(x))).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The full coherent section satisfies
\[
 |F_{m,x}(y)|_{h_A^m}^2=A_d^2\tau(x,y)^d,\qquad
 \tau([Z],[W])=\frac{|\langle Z,W\rangle|^2}{|Z|^2|W|^2}.
\]
The measure $A_d^{-1}|F_{m,x}|^2\nu_0$ is a probability measure concentrating at $x$: outside any neighborhood of $x$, $\tau\leq\rho<1$ and its mass is at most $A_d\rho^d\to0$. Thus continuity of $w$ gives
$\|(w-w(x))F_{m,x}\|_0=o(\sqrt{A_d})$ for each fixed $x$.

On $\mathcal H_m^{(j)}$, let $A_ms=\Pi_m(ws)$. Its quadratic form is the weighted norm, so $\delta I\leq A_m\leq MI$, and
\[
 B_{m,j}^w(x)=\langle A_m^{-1}K_{m,x},K_{m,x}\rangle_0.
\]
For $R(x)>a$, the preceding norm identities give
$\|F_{m,x}-K_{m,x}\|_0=o(\sqrt{A_d})$. It follows that
$\|(A_m-w(x)I)K_{m,x}\|_0=o(\sqrt{A_d})$.
Using $\|A_m^{-1}\|\leq\delta^{-1}$ yields
\[
 B_{m,j}^w(x)=w(x)^{-1}B_{m,j}^0(x)+o(A_d)
       \qquad(R(x)>a).
\]
For $R(x)<a$, the upper kernel comparison gives $m^{-q}B_{m,j}^w(x)\to0$.
Finally $0\leq q!m^{-q}wB_{m,j}^w\leq(M/\delta)q!A_d/m^q$ is uniformly bounded. Dominated convergence away from the null set $R=a$ proves
$q!m^{-q}B_{m,j}^w w\nu_0\to\mu_c$ in total variation.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:horizontal-projective-kernels}]
The exact space identity is \cref{thm:horizontal-center-descent}; its projective dimension follows from the monomial count. The envelope computation and the two endpoint kernel calculations prove the downstairs assertions. Isometric descent gives $B_m^X=B_m^B\circ\pi$.
For $f_c=k^q\mathbf1_{D_c}/w$, fiber integration gives
\[
 \int_X\left|\frac{q!}{m^q}B_m^X-f_c\circ\pi\right|\nu_X
 =\int_B\left|\frac{q!}{m^q}B_m^B-f_c\right|\nu_B.
\]
This proves the upstairs total-variation limit and its pushforward. The same integration transfers $L^1$ convergence; any other smooth positive volume on compact $X$ is comparable to $\nu_X$. Local uniform convergence pulls back off $\pi^{-1}(Z)$. The dimension asymptotic gives the probability limits, and $N_m/m^{q+r}=O(m^{-r})$ proves the zero limit in ambient degree.

For $W_m\subseteq U_m\subseteq V_m$, monotonicity gives $B_{W,m}\leq B_{U,m}\leq B_{V,m}$. By \cref{lem:kernel-codimension-comparison}, the positive difference of the $q$-normalized $U_m$ and $W_m$ measures has total mass at most $q!(\dim V_m-\dim W_m)/m^q=O(m^{-1})$. The two endpoint weights give the lower bound and local uniform convergence by sandwiching. Their $L^1$ errors bound the intermediate error, proving global convergence as well.
\end{proof}

The base limit is independent of $\nu_X$. If
$\nu_X(\mathrm{d}b,\mathrm{d}x)=\nu_B(\mathrm{d}b)\lambda_b(\mathrm{d}x)$ is its disintegration, then
$\Lambda_{\nu_X}(\mathrm{d}b,\mathrm{d}x)=\mu_c(\mathrm{d}b)\lambda_b(\mathrm{d}x)$.
Thus the lifted limit retains exactly the conditional fiber probabilities on $D_c$.
Equality of two lifts implies equality of those probabilities for $\mu_c$-almost every $b$: test against $(g\circ\pi)f$, with $g\in C(B)$ and $f$ in a countable dense subset of $C(X)$, and then take one common exceptional null set. The converse follows directly by integration.

This dependence occurs in the product example of \cref{ex:horizontal-center-sharp-error}. Keep its weight $\varphi=c\log(R_B+R_F)$ and let
$\eta_f=k\omega_B+bf\omega_F$, where $f>0$ is smooth on $\mathbb P^r$ and $b\min f>c$. Then
$\eta_f+\ddc\varphi\geq(k-c)\omega_B+(bf-c)\omega_F>0$, and its normalized volume and limit are
\[
 \nu_{X,f}=\omega_B^q\otimes\frac{f^r\omega_F^r}{\int_{\mathbb P^r}f^r\omega_F^r},
 \qquad
 \Lambda_{\nu_{X,f}}=\mu_c\otimes\frac{f^r\omega_F^r}{\int_{\mathbb P^r}f^r\omega_F^r}.
\]
A nonconstant $f$ changes the lift. For $r\geq2$, it also makes $\eta_f$ nonclosed: $\mathrm{d}\eta_f=b\,\mathrm{d}f\wedge\omega_F$, and wedging by $\omega_F^{r-2}$ reduces its vanishing to the injectivity of $\xi\mapsto\xi\wedge\omega_F^{r-1}$ on one-forms. For $r=1$ this derivative vanishes by degree.

The spaces, envelope and kernels are unchanged by varying an admissible Hermitian background with $\pi,A,h_A,\varphi,\nu_X$ fixed. Moving $p$ in $Y=Z\times\{p\}$ also leaves them unchanged. For $mc\geq r+1$, however, the constant germ fails integrability at the old center and becomes integrable there after the move. Thus all kernels together need not determine the original multiplier-ideal type.
