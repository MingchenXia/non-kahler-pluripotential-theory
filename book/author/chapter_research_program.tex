Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$.

A uniform Gauduchon trace bound makes a family of positive currents relatively compact, but its limits can lose the Monge--Amp\`ere mass or singularity data needed for an equation. Even convergence of currents with integrable coefficients and convergence of their traces in total variation can coexist with infinite determinant mass, by \cref{prop:integrable-infinite-mass,cor:strong-linear-infinite-mass}. The envelope class can also contain infinite-mass potentials, by \cref{prop:goodness-infinite-mass}. Passing from these linear bounds to an exact equation therefore requires additional control at the nonlinear limit.

Strict positivity and preservation of singularities impose further requirements. A positive variational volume must produce one strict current to prove bigness. An envelope realizing divisorial bounds must avoid acquiring extra orders to recover the prescribed type. The examples below test these requirements separately: bounded upper mass, a positive scalar and an exact divisorial profile cannot replace one another.

\section{Threshold equations and singular limits}

The equation problem has two separate steps: construct a subsolution at the optimal coefficient, then pass to a limit without leaving excess Monge--Amp\`ere mass. Fix a Hermitian form $h$ and a smooth positive probability volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. We use the density threshold $b_h(f)$ and root space $\mathcal D_h$ of \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution,prop:individual-density-banach}.

For semipositive big backgrounds, \cref{thm:als-general-measure} solves both the positive-exponent and normalized equations under its model condition and positive lower mass. For arbitrary smooth big backgrounds, \cref{thm:big-model-exponential,thm:big-model-normalized} give both equations under bounded mass and positive lower model-reference volume. The minimal ordinary-type $L^p$ theorem in \cref{sec:big-minimal-existence} needs neither metric mass property; under the second regime, \cref{cor:big-model-lp-type} also recovers the prescribed ordinary model type.

Under bounded mass, \cref{thm:big-varying-model-closure,thm:big-varying-equation-stability} also permit the ordinary model references to converge in $d_\theta$, with a common positive lower-volume bound. For densities converging in $L^1$ of a fixed non-pluripolar measure, the positive-exponent potentials converge in capacity and their measures in total variation. At exponent zero the scalars and measures converge, while the normalized potentials satisfy the limiting equation along each $L^1$ convergent subsequence. Identifying a single potential requires an additional uniqueness or selection argument.

Removing these mass hypotheses means replacing their uses in compactness. The semipositive proof constructs a common lower envelope using finite auxiliary contact measures, then obtains the moving-measure limit by capacity convergence and sublevel tails. The arbitrary-background proof uses finite upper relative volumes to construct a rooftop between classes and maximize the weighted measure. In either case the solution measure must be shown finite. The formal existence of the envelopes alone does not supply this step.

\subsection{Individual density thresholds}

The distinction between all measures and one fixed density is essential. \Cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-solvability} characterizes solvability for every finite non-pluripolar measure by $v_-(h)>0$. When it vanishes, \cref{thm:lower-mass-obstructing-measure} gives an $L^1$ probability density, bounded below by a positive constant, which admits no positive scaled subsolution. Such obstructing densities are $L^1$ dense, although every smooth strictly positive density is solvable. \Cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass} realizes this obstruction on a compact threefold.

For an individual nonnegative $L^1$ density, the bounded-truncation normalization constants decrease to $b_h(f)$. The upper-envelope and damped-continuity argument attains this coefficient with a subsolution, but does not prove an exact equation or finite mass. Smooth tests alone miss this threshold, by \cref{thm:smooth-tests-insufficient}. The Gauduchon formula in \cref{thm:integrable-gauduchon-formula} makes it upper semicontinuous in $L^1$. Its positive domain is described by density roots in $\mathcal D_h$, independently of the Hermitian metric. For $p\in\mathcal D_h$, the sum norm $\|p\|_{L^n}+\rho_h(p)$ makes this space Banach. Positive lower mass makes its domain all of $L^n$; finite upper mass is equivalent to completeness for $\rho_h$ alone, by \cref{prop:individual-density-banach,prop:individual-threshold-backgrounds,prop:individual-root-completeness}.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:individual-equation-threshold}\index{Subsolution!exact threshold equation problem}
Suppose $v_-(h)=0$. For a nonzero $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ with $b_h(f)>0$, does there exist $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=b_h(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V?
\]
Determine the singularity or envelope class in which such an equation should be posed.
\end{problem}

When $v_-(h)>0$, \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-exact-threshold} gives an exact solution in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ for every nonzero nonnegative $L^1$ density. Finite-measure comparison identifies its scalar, and dominated continuity gives capacity convergence along every $L^1$-convergent subsequence of normalized greatest exponential solutions. This includes the Hopf example without bounded mass. For $L^p$ densities, $p>1$, the continuous equation and \cref{prop:individual-normalizer-lp} give the answer without positive lower mass.

For the data in \cref{qu:individual-equation-threshold}, \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential} gives greatest exponential solutions, convergence of their scalars to $b_h(f)$ and total-variation convergence of their measures. Capacity convergence of the normalized potentials is the missing step. Under the root-tail condition of \cref{prop:root-uniform-tails}, \cref{prop:exponential-root-tail-modulus} quantifies the scalar and measure errors; a rate linear in the exponent needs additional control. Positivity and integrability of $f$ alone do not ensure a positive threshold. Exponential subsolutions when $b_h(f)=0$, and an extension of the density criterion to arbitrary non-pluripolar measures, are separate questions.

\subsection{Dual attainment and exact equations}

A maximizing Gauduchon current can convert the threshold inequality into equality, provided its regularity or its weighted moments prevent loss in the limit. For density roots in the closed order ideal $\mathcal D_h^0$ of \cref{prop:root-uniform-tails}, \cref{thm:integrable-current-attainment,prop:integrable-threshold-adjoints} give attainment and represent all supporting probabilities. These probabilities are absolutely continuous, and the trace errors along smooth maximizing sequences tend to zero in total variation by \cref{prop:threshold-trace-saturation}. Uniform integrability along just one maximizing sequence already gives attainment and convergence of its determinant-weighted measures, by \cref{prop:selected-gauduchon-attainment}. That weaker hypothesis does not give the representation of all supporting probabilities.

In dimension at least two, a smooth strictly positive maximizer forces every threshold subsolution to be smooth and exact, with uniqueness modulo constants, and makes the density root smooth and nonnegative; see \cref{cor:smooth-dual-exact-equation}. For a closed background, \cref{cor:kahler-smooth-dual-attainment} characterizes smooth dual attainment by smooth strict positivity of the density. The strict positivity conclusion fails for nonclosed backgrounds on every compact complex manifold of dimension at least two, by \cref{ex:nonclosed-dual-zero-density}.

\Cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation} also allows a smooth semipositive maximizer which is strictly positive outside a proper analytic set $Z$. It gives an exact global equation, smoothness off $Z$ and uniqueness of all threshold subsolutions modulo constants. The proof takes the maximum of two subsolutions and uses a Gauduchon integral to eliminate their divisorial difference; no singularity type is prescribed. The positive pole-divisor classes are linearly independent by \cref{cor:semipositive-dual-divisor-independence}. Write $b=b_h(f)$ and, for the exponential parameter $\lambda>0$, let $u_\lambda$ be the greatest exponential solution and put $c_\lambda=\exp(\lambda\sup_Xu_\lambda)$. In this regime \cref{prop:semipositive-dual-exponential-limit} gives whole-family normalized $L^1$ convergence, a fixed lower barrier and, for a fixed finite constant $C$, the estimate $b\leq c_\lambda\leq b\mathrm{e}^{C\lambda}$. A genuine smaller-background envelope of the threshold solution gives capacity convergence by \cref{cor:semipositive-dual-capacity-limit}; $L^p$ data with $p>1$ give a continuous solution and uniform convergence by \cref{cor:semipositive-dual-lp-limit}. \Cref{ex:semipositive-projective-maximizer} has a smooth solution but a nondifferentiable density root on $Z$. For general $L^1$ data, automatic genuineness of that envelope and regularity across $Z$ remain open here.

The moment criterion \cref{thm:gauduchon-moment-threshold} uses no regularity of a maximizing current. Uniform first moments of normalized quasi-plurisubharmonic functions against one smooth maximizing sequence give a fixed lower barrier for the exponential solutions. Their normalized cluster limits lie below the greatest normalized threshold subsolution $\Phi_f$. If $P_{\delta h}(\Phi_f)$ is genuine for some $0<\delta<1$, the lower-barrier criterion gives subsequential capacity convergence and an exact threshold equation, without either mass property. An entropy bound on the test probabilities supplies both the first moments and the selected-sequence uniform integrability above. By \cref{prop:hermitian-dual-entropy-bounds}, such an entropy bound is equivalent to a uniform bound on these probabilities for $H_0$, the convex dual of the zero-reference normalization defined in \cref{chap:normalization}. It supplies no smaller-background envelope.

Thus singular-current attainment alone does not settle the equation. At zero lower mass, it is still unknown here whether root tails suffice, or even whether $\mathcal D_h^0=\mathcal D_h$. \Cref{prop:root-tail-continuity} characterizes the tail condition by continuity under uniformly bounded logarithmic perturbations tending to zero in measure. This is an order-interval statement, not unrestricted $L^1$ continuity of the threshold.

Capacity convergence is essential here. The finite atomic curve models of \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} converge in $L^1$ to a smooth positive-mass potential while their non-pluripolar measures vanish. The existence arguments obtain stronger convergence from their measure bounds; a carrier representation alone, even in a prescribed type, does not provide it.

\subsection{Regularity of singular solutions}

Regularity estimates supply the compactness needed to select a limit as the exponential parameter vanishes. For bounded solutions, \cref{thm:local-big-bounded-continuity} gives continuity wherever a fixed strict barrier is locally bounded. In an arbitrary smooth big background, \cref{thm:nonclosed-interior-smoothness} makes a locally H\"older solution with minimal singularities smooth where a strict analytic-singularity barrier and the positive density are smooth. Global $L^p$ control supplies the H\"older premise by \cref{thm:big-minimal-holder,cor:big-lp-interior-smoothness}.

For a fixed ordinary type above the strict barrier, \cref{cor:big-reference-uniform-convergence} gives compactness for uniform convergence of differences under common reference bounds and a uniform $L^p$ density bound. The reference need not have minimal singularities, so this compactness does not by itself make the potentials regular. In the minimal type, the scalar and density convergence of \cref{prop:big-exponential-clusters} identifies each normalized exponential cluster equation.

For a compact K\"ahler manifold and a smooth positive global volume form, \cref{thm:big-class-ample-smoothness} proves smoothness of the full-mass solution on the ample locus of every big class, including non-nef classes. Smoothness of the density only on this open set does not imply global $L^p$ integrability. On an arbitrary smooth-density open subset, it does not even imply continuity of a bounded minimal solution: \cref{thm:compact-local-density-discontinuity} gives a compact K\"ahler counterexample with constant density on a ball. Under local closedness, \cref{thm:smooth-boundary-trace-regularity} gives a sufficient condition in terms of smooth pointwise boundary traces. The resolved logarithmic types of \cref{thm:resolved-type} admit H\"older remainders when their residual form is positive. For exceptional minimal poles, \cref{thm:minimal-blowup-data,thm:iterated-blowups} instead give a H\"older remainder on the target and quantify the Jacobian loss in integrability. Neither result covers arbitrary prescribed types or derives a global H\"older bound from density information confined to the regular locus. At a density zero, even smooth data need not give a smooth residual: \cref{prop:exceptional-density-obstruction,ex:power-blowup-family} give a necessary vanishing order and show that it is not sufficient. The corresponding questions beyond these resolved and explicit geometric cases remain open here.

\subsection{Uniqueness and selection}

Zeros of the density can separate regions where the linearized equation is elliptic. The issue is whether a difference of solutions can vary between these regions. Throughout this discussion $\omega$ is Hermitian, $f$ is smooth and nonnegative, and potentials are normalized by their supremum.

The counterexample in \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness} shows that a smooth nonnegative density can have two distinct smooth normalized solutions even on $\mathbb P^3$. The further construction in \cref{thm:numa-thin-zero-set} has uncountably many smooth normalized solutions and a density positive off two real hypersurfaces. By modifying the background, \cref{thm:quadratic-density-nonuniqueness} achieves exactly quadratic vanishing on those hypersurfaces. Thus neither zero smooth volume of the zero set nor finite-order vanishing suffices for uniqueness. All the backgrounds are strictly Hermitian and have bounded mass. The degeneration causing nonuniqueness lies in the density and the solution forms.

This places a necessary restriction on any geometric use of the equation. A construction that assigns a canonical potential to arbitrary nonnegative data must specify a selection procedure and prove that it is well defined. Existence in the ordinary minimal singularity type does not provide such a selection. The unique scalar, and the corresponding variational volume for strictly positive data, remain meaningful without uniqueness of every potential. On a compact Hermitian surface, \cref{uni-cor:dense-lowdim} proves uniqueness for $C^2$ potentials and continuous density positive on a dense set, with no differential condition on the metric. In dimension three, \cref{uni-thm:sobolev} instead uses a sign condition on $\ddc\omega$. The cofactor identity in \eqref{eq:mixed-uniqueness-defect} and the signed quadratic-difference argument provide two distinct integration mechanisms.

When $\{f>0\}$ is connected and dense, \cref{thm:connected-positive-uniqueness} proves that two bounded normalized solutions with the same positive scalar coincide. The result applies to $cf\,\mathrm{d}V$ by absorbing the smooth positive ratio $\mathrm{d}V/\omega^n$ into the density. All the hypersurface counterexamples here have three positive-density components, including the analytic example in \cref{uni-thm:analytic-example}. They do not refute \cref{conj:continuous-gaud-adjoint}, which concerns strictly positive densities $\mathrm{e}^F$ with $F$ continuous.

Selection by the exponential equation is settled for every fixed nonzero $L^p$ density, $p>1$. The norming probabilities in \cref{thm:vanishing-exponential-selection} select the greatest critical subsolution satisfying their integral normalizations. In a Hermitian background the normalized solutions converge uniformly as the exponent tends to zero; in an arbitrary smooth big background convergence is in $L^1(X)$ and locally uniform off the strict barrier's pole set. The selected limit preserves the symmetries of the data by \cref{prop:symmetric-equation-clusters}. For the projective example, \cref{thm:numa-selected-limit} identifies it with the explicit symmetric $C^{1,1}$ solution of \cref{prop:numa-radial-continuum}. Selection therefore coexists with nonuniqueness of the normalized critical equation. The remaining questions concern merely integrable data and more general singular reference types.

For a nonzero density at positive threshold in dimension at least two, \cref{cor:smooth-dual-exact-equation,cor:smooth-dual-exponential-limit} give uniqueness of the normalized threshold solution and uniform convergence of the whole normalized positive-exponent family whenever a smooth strictly positive maximizing Gauduchon current exists. The construction in \cref{ex:nonclosed-dual-zero-density} satisfies this condition even though the density vanishes on an open set. Thus the size of the zero set alone does not determine uniqueness; the dual condition also involves the background.

\section{Positive Gauduchon volume and bigness}

The Gauduchon volume tests a background through smooth metrics, whereas bigness asks for one potential making that background strictly positive. Fix a smooth positive probability form $\mu$ and retain $h$. The question is whether positivity of the fixed-density volume of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} supplies such a potential at a nef boundary point.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:nef-gaud-volume-big}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!bigness conjecture}
Let $\theta$ be a nef smooth real $(1,1)$-background on a compact complex manifold. Then
\[
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \theta\text{ is big}.
\]
Here $\mu$ is any smooth strictly positive probability measure.
\end{conjecture}

The choice of $\mu$ does not affect positivity, by \cref{prop:gaud-volume-density-comparison}. Taking an infimum over all smooth probability densities would change the question: that infimum vanishes for the big Hermitian backgrounds of \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}. Bigness implies the stated positivity, since pseudoeffectivity of $\theta-ah$ gives $\lambda_\mu(\theta)\geq a\lambda_\mu(h)>0$. In dimension one the converse follows from the integral formula. For a closed nef class on a compact K\"ahler manifold, $\mathcal V_\mu$ is its top self-intersection, and the equivalence is the nef-and-big criterion recalled in \cite{SGPT}. \Cref{thm:closed-core-subtraction} treats a nonclosed endpoint on a compact K\"ahler manifold when it is a closed nef positive-volume core plus a semipositive form. The remaining implication concerns arbitrary nef smooth backgrounds without that structure.

\section{Closed and twisted classes}

A volume attached to a closed Bott--Chern class must survive changes of representative. A volume equation for an LCK form must instead retain its Lee class and twisted differential. The examples below show where ordinary Hermitian perturbations fail to preserve these requirements. We use the Lee operator $D_\lambda$ and the map $j_X$ of \eqref{eq:lck-gauge-operators} and \cref{sec:lee-gauduchon-cone}.

The loci of \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci} distinguish small negative errors for closed currents from strict closed positivity. Any proposed numerical restricted-volume construction in the general Hermitian setting must first pass \cref{prop:bc-zero-class-infinite-error-volume}: an unrestricted supremum over smooth Hermitian-error representatives is already infinite in the zero class on the Hopf threefold. The failure occurs at each fixed error size. Taking the error to zero afterwards cannot recover a class invariant from that expression.

The global subsolution threshold also constrains divisorial singularities. For a subsolution at this coefficient, \cref{prop:threshold-divisorial-constraint} places every smooth maximizing Gauduchon sequence in the annihilator of each component class in the limit. If one such sequence has uniformly integrable determinant-weighted densities, \cref{cor:semipositive-divisor-exclusion} excludes classes with suitable smooth semipositive representatives, including the hyperplane divisor on the Hopf threefold. For a nef component class, \cref{cor:nef-divisor-threshold-volume} forces its fixed-density variational volume to vanish. Root tails suffice for both conclusions. Characterizing the remaining possible components requires a realization theorem. The smooth-test closure can be smaller than the full current set, as \cref{ex:gauduchon-current-smoothing-obstruction} shows.

For closed classes on manifolds in class $\mathcal C$, \cref{sec:finite-restricted-invariants} supplies a finite resolution-independent restricted invariant. Its positivity is equivalent to bigness on the resolved subvariety, and for a nef ambient class its zero-invariant union is the null locus. For an actual divisorial trace with bounded remainder, \cref{sec:resolved-trace-masses} computes the individual mass and turns positive residual mass into a strict current more singular than the trace. Preserving its bounded-difference type requires more: \cref{ex:positive-trace-bounded-type-obstruction} shows the obstruction even on a projective surface. A corresponding construction for general nonclosed backgrounds, and its relation to intrinsic multiplier-ideal traces beyond this residual regime, remain open.

The LCK setting gives a separate set of equation questions. By \cref{cor:lck-no-closed-big-class}, a compact strictly LCK manifold has no big ordinary Bott--Chern class. Its positive LCK form belongs instead to a twisted class, whose representatives are described using $D_\lambda$. The coupled system \eqref{eq:lck-coupled-equation} identifies the additional conformal equation imposed by Gauduchon's condition. The uniqueness problem \cref{q:lck-gauduchon-volume} concerns that system with a fixed Lee class and twisted class; the ordinary-potential nonuniqueness examples do not decide it.

Two geometric questions use the Lee form in different ways. The pseudoeffectivity conjecture \cref{conj:lck-lee-psef} requires positivity against all Gauduchon tests, extending the positive pairing obtained from the LCK metric itself. The Calabi--Yau conjecture \cref{conj:lck-calabi-yau-vaisman} asks for a Vaisman metric on the manifold. The proved rigidity theorem \cref{thm:lck-chern-ricci-rigidity} applies when one metric already satisfies both Chern--Ricci flatness and the Gauduchon condition. Arranging those conditions simultaneously is the remaining geometric existence issue.

The Lee--Gauduchon cone makes one part of this geometry invariant under bimeromorphic changes. Its dual is the cone of degree-one classes whose image under $j_X$ is pseudoeffective; the strict inequalities exclude $\ker j_X$. The full duality and modification argument are proved in \cref{sec:lee-gauduchon-cone}. A nonzero positive class in this image obstructs balanced metrics throughout the bimeromorphic class. The Hopf calculation gives a positive half-ray, despite unbounded Monge--Amp\`ere masses. Thus this cohomological invariant addresses a geometric obstruction while leaving the nonlinear estimates for the scalar equation as a separate task. The converse to its balanced-metric consequence fails: \cref{ex:lee-calabi-eckmann} has zero Lee--Gauduchon cone and no strongly Gauduchon metric.

\section{Degeneration orders and finite-distance boundaries}

Approaching a nef background through positive ones gives two kinds of boundary data: the rate at which volume vanishes and the distance traveled in the Hessian metric of \cref{sec:gauduchon-metric-geometry}. They need not detect the same boundary points.

The orders of \cref{prop:nef-degeneration-orders} are independent of the smooth probability measure and the Hermitian perturbation direction, but they need not coincide. On a fixed product of elliptic curves, \cref{prop:nef-oscillatory-orders} realizes every pair $(1+\alpha,1+\beta)$ with $0<\alpha<\beta<1$, as well as irrational common orders. \Cref{prop:nef-diagonal-fractional-and-zero} gives zero volume with decay slower than every positive power. Equality and integrality therefore require additional geometry, as in the closed K\"ahler case governed by numerical dimension.

Under every modification both orders can only increase, by \cref{prop:nef-modification-volume}. A single blowup can increase them strictly or remove positive limiting volume while both remain zero. Positive-current subtraction gives the same one-sided comparison when the residual stays nef. Perturbative minimal multiplicities vanish for nef backgrounds, whereas the actual minimal boundary current in \cref{cor:nef-boundary-minimal-removal} has a nonzero divisor whose removal changes the order. The remaining questions in \cref{qu:nef-degeneration-exponent} concern equality and geometric restrictions on these behaviors.

The background-class metric yields a different kind of boundary information. On a finite-dimensional cone it splits as a product of the logarithmic volume direction and a unit-volume hypersurface. Consequently a path whose volume tends to zero or infinity has infinite length. Yet the blowup example in \cref{sec:gauduchon-metric-geometry} has a finite-distance boundary with positive nef volume. It follows that completing this metric cone cannot simply mean adjoining all nef classes, or declaring every nef boundary infinitely far away.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:gaud-metric-boundary}
For a specified finite-dimensional cone of smooth background classes, describe the metric completion of its unit-volume hypersurface. Which boundary points admit a current representative, and what estimates on the minimizing Gauduchon metrics distinguish finite from infinite distance?
\end{problem}

The cone must be specified in this question. Allowing additional directions can shorten path distances. The finite-dimensional metric calculations do not establish a distance on the entire infinite-dimensional quotient.

\section{Weak Gauduchon adjoints}

Differentiating the smooth equation produces a Gauduchon adjoint probability. At a continuous logarithmic density, supporting probabilities of the concave normalization functional provide a weak replacement. In the background and reference regimes of \cref{chap:normalization}, the set $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ is nonempty, convex, weakly compact and absolutely continuous. Uniqueness of this probability would make the scalar functional differentiable even where a classical linearized equation is unavailable.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:continuous-gaud-adjoint}\index{Adjoint measure!continuous-density conjecture}
For a Hermitian background $\omega$, the nonsingular reference $\varphi=0$, and every real $F\in C(X)$, the set $\mathcal A_0(F)$ consists of a single probability measure.
\end{conjecture}

Equivalently, the concave normalization functional is G\^ateaux differentiable at every continuous logarithmic density in this regime. The conjecture holds for smooth $F$ by the linearized equation, and in the closed K\"ahler setting by the explicit normalization. It would imply that smooth approximations of $F$ have a unique weak limit of their Gauduchon adjoint probabilities, by \cref{prop:weak-adjoint-stability}.

A direct bounded-potential result is \cref{prop:bounded-adjoint-persistence}. If every smooth positive potential perturbation of $\omega$ is Gauduchon, normalized Monge--Amp\`ere measures extend the smooth adjoint probabilities and converge under capacity convergence of uniformly bounded potentials. This applies on every Gauduchon surface. It does not define a weak linearized operator or settle approximation by unbounded potentials in a general background.

There is also a partial result from Baire category: \cref{thm:generic-adjoint-uniqueness} proves uniqueness on a dense $G_\delta$ of continuous logarithmic densities, in every fixed background and reference regime of \cref{chap:normalization}. At those densities all choices of approximating supporting probabilities have the same weak limit. The assertion is generic in the uniform topology; it does not eliminate exceptional continuous densities.

For $n\geq2$, the compact current formulation gives a more precise target. By \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints}, the supporting probabilities are exactly the normalized determinant-weighted measures of maximizing Gauduchon currents. Thus the conjecture asks whether these weighted measures agree for all maximizers. \Cref{cor:gaud-adjoint-lq} already bounds their densities uniformly in every $L^q$, $1<q<n/(n-1)$, on bounded sets of $F$; the endpoint is available if $v_-(\omega)>0$. These estimates give compactness, but do not prove uniqueness. For a fixed smooth strictly positive density the maximizing current is already unique, smooth and strictly positive. The example in \cref{prop:gaud-determinant-concentration} shows that a sequence approaching its value can nevertheless have unbounded determinant moments above the critical exponent. Neither statement resolves uniqueness for merely continuous $F$. Another approach would show directly that the upper and lower directional derivatives of $B_0$ agree.

Relative inequalities between positive residual forms already give uniform convergence of the whole scalar functional, by \cref{thm:geometric-normalization-comparison}. Under uniformly bounded $L^1$ changes of the tests, \cref{cor:geometric-adjoint-stability} gives uniformly integrable supports and weak $L^1$ cluster limits in the limiting supporting set. Smooth-test adjoints together with the scalar at zero determine the functional by \eqref{eq:adjoint-functional-reconstruction}. These statements leave the conjectured uniqueness at every continuous test unchanged.

For a singular reference or a degenerate background, the same uniqueness question remains meaningful with the hypotheses of \cref{chap:normalization}. A further geometric interpretation requires a well-defined weak linearization. For a smooth background $\theta$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, write $\theta_u=\theta+\ddc u$, and let $\rho$ be a measurable weight. The expression $\ddc(\rho\theta_u^{n-1})$ is not a definition when multiplication of the product by $\rho$ has not been justified.

\section{Singularity topology}

A topology for singularities must retain information that weak convergence of currents can lose. Upper-volume deficits do so when bounded mass and positive lower volume provide uniform control. On curves, polar curvature gives a complete description even at zero volume. The remaining problem is to extend that control to higher-dimensional degenerations and to backgrounds with unbounded mass.

\subsection{Weak convergence and singularity data}

The complete Gauduchon trace metric induces the $L^1$ topology on normalized potentials. The elementary ideal-envelope metric uses the same topology on canonical ideal representatives. Its completion can acquire points that are not representatives of their own ideal classes: on a curve, \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} approximates every normalized potential by ideal envelopes with finite logarithmic poles and zero non-pluripolar mass. In particular, these envelopes can converge to a smooth potential with strictly positive curvature. The convergence is not in capacity, since damped continuity would then preserve their zero measures.

A positive mass bound does not repair this failure. In \cref{prop:positive-mass-ideal-discontinuity}, analytic ideal envelopes of one fixed positive mass converge in the elementary ideal metric to the smooth model $0$, whose mass is larger. Nor can the normalization functional recover the lost data. On the closed model domain of \cref{thm:normalization-mass-quotient}, it records exactly the mass. The positive-residual examples in \cref{ex:normalization-distance-projective,ex:nonclosed-normalization-nonseparation} have identical functionals and supporting sets but different poles, multiplier ideals and normalized potential limits, including in genuinely nonclosed backgrounds.

An order bound gives more control than weak convergence alone. Under a one-sided ideal bound, \cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence} identifies the envelope limit from divisorial Lelong numbers. It gives the criterion for $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ along ordered analytic approximations, including dyadic regularization. Fixed-weight ideal inclusions as in \cref{cor:fixed-weight-envelope-convergence} supply the required divisorial convergence. Without the order bound, convergence of every divisorial number together with $L^1$ convergence is insufficient, by \cref{ex:divisorial-convergence-envelope-failure}. These tests still make sense when upper volume is infinite. In contrast, analytic classes are dense throughout the elementary ideal metric in a Hermitian background, by \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density}, so density for that metric cannot characterize envelope equality.

Finite damped measures control nonlinear limits throughout the potential space, including infinite-mass potentials. Their weak continuity already follows from capacity convergence. Requiring that continuity in addition to capacity convergence therefore imposes no further condition on multiplier ideals.

The uniform topology $\tau_{\omega,0}$ in \cref{sec:ordinary-type-topology} combines both envelope coordinates with controlled ideal inclusions and full divisorial variation on each fixed compact model. Its analytic closure and sequential analytic closure are exactly the ordinary classes satisfying $P_\omega[u]=P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$, by \cref{thm:ordinary-topology-analytic-closure}. This answers the analytic-characterization question for every Hermitian background, including classes represented by infinite-mass potentials. The topology is not $T_0$ on ordinary classes. Smooth-gauge transport is exact when the obstacle changes with the background. With zero obstacles fixed, it is discontinuous already on curves, even within a positive-mass cutoff, by \cref{prop:ordinary-topology-gauge-failure}. The all-obstacle uniformity of \cref{subsec:all-obstacle-topology} is invariant under smooth background changes. Its analytic closure requires envelope equality at every smooth obstacle. On curves this is the same analytic locus, but \cref{prop:curve-all-obstacle-incomplete} shows that the topology remains incomplete and strictly weaker than polar total variation. In higher dimension, membership in the relative envelope class of the ideal envelope suffices, by \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection}. Under bounded mass and positive lower ideal volume, \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization} makes zero-obstacle equality sufficient. For every admissible smooth curve background, \cref{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion} identifies zero-obstacle equality, all-obstacle equality and relative-envelope membership in the ideal reference. For separated products, \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections} computes both envelopes coordinatewise under separated smooth obstacles, including smooth curve backgrounds of zero area. For the same sums, \cref{prop:separated-product-all-obstacle-criterion} identifies zero-obstacle equality with equality at every smooth obstacle, including mixed obstacles and zero-area factors. \Cref{cor:auxiliary-product-all-obstacle-criterion} preserves this equivalence through the nonclosed auxiliary coefficient and unit divisor. \Cref{cor:general-auxiliary-all-obstacle-criterion} extends it to arbitrary compact complex auxiliary factors with a semipositive vertical term, without an ambient mass bound. For divisorial fibrations over curves, \cref{cor:fibration-component-cover} gives the same equivalence at zero model volume even with whole-fiber poles or disconnected fibers, by reducing obstacles on the cover of fiber components. In \cref{ex:zero-volume-unbounded-mass-fibration}, the ambient background fails bounded mass, while the base potential belongs to the relative envelope class needed for all-obstacle equality. The unrestricted zero-lower-volume implication, including backgrounds with unbounded mass, remains unresolved.

\subsection{Volume comparison and analytic approximation}

In this subsection, assume bounded mass and fix a smooth big background $\theta$. The comparison $d_\theta$ of \cref{sec:model-volume-comparison} measures upper-volume deficits between ordinary models. A common positive lower-volume bound converts a small deficit into a stretching estimate. The resulting quantitative $L^1$ bound and three-point modulus give separation and Cauchy completeness in \cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness}; the cutoff must be closed, as \cref{ex:strict-volume-cutoff-incomplete} shows. The positive lower-volume condition also prevents the comparison from identifying distinct ordinary models: \cref{ex:zero-volume-comparison-collapse} gives such a pair at zero volume in positive closed backgrounds in every dimension at least two, and in positive nonclosed backgrounds in dimensions at least three. Rooftop continuity supplies monotone lower bounds, which give capacity convergence by \cref{prop:volume-rooftop-continuity,thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}.

Analytic approximation then detects precisely the ideal models. \Cref{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation} corrects analytic regularizations within the fixed background; their ordinary models converge to the ideal envelope, and their limiting comparison with the original potential equals its upper-volume excess. Conversely, \cref{thm:analytic-model-volume-criterion} makes ideal models closed under this convergence at positive lower volume. Although $d_\theta$ fails the triangle inequality even on projective analytic models, \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric} constructs a complete metric with the same convergent sequences. Its Cauchy criterion also controls reciprocal lower volumes, excluding approach to zero volume. The ideal models form a closed complete subspace and are the completion of the positive-volume analytic models, by \cref{cor:positive-ideal-metric-completion}.

Monotone limits explain where positivity enters. For decreasing ordinary models in backgrounds decreasing uniformly to a smooth big form, \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} gives lower-volume continuity even when the limiting volume is zero. Positive limiting lower volume also gives upper-volume and Lelong continuity. For increasing sequences in a fixed background, \cref{thm:increasing-envelope-volume-continuity} gives ordinary-envelope and volume continuity under that positivity condition. For arbitrary decreasing potentials with positive-lower-volume limit, \cref{prop:decreasing-envelope-volume-criterion} identifies envelope convergence with upper-volume convergence. Likewise, \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization} identifies ordinary and ideal envelope equality with upper-volume equality when the ideal envelope has positive lower volume.

The comparison is compatible with changes of background as long as these controls persist. Smooth gauges preserve it after ordinary projection, by \cref{prop:model-comparison-gauge}. The fixed-potential limit in \cref{cor:ordinary-envelope-background-limit} retains lower volume and, at positive lower volume, the ordinary envelope. The uniform estimate \eqref{eq:vanishing-background-comparison} allows the curvature errors in analytic regularization to tend to zero, as in \cref{thm:analytic-approximation-curvature-error}. For two-sided perturbations, convex correction gives admissible transported models and a uniform comparison independent of the chosen strict reference at the level of limiting convergence. This yields the analytic criterion of \cref{thm:two-sided-model-transport,thm:two-sided-analytic-criterion}. These arguments do not cover loss of bigness or the higher-dimensional zero-volume boundary.

\subsection{The curve boundary}

On a curve, polar curvature determines the ordinary model and its atomic part determines the ideal model. \Cref{thm:curve-model-volume-distance} identifies $d_\theta$ with total variation of polar curvature, including at zero non-pluripolar mass. All ordinary models form a complete geodesic space, and the ideal models form the closed subspace obtained by completing positive-mass analytic models, by \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}. On positive-mass ideal models, this agrees with the source topology described in \cref{thm:curve-atomic-source-metric,cor:curve-ideal-source-completeness}. For the source metric, this space is not totally bounded. Thus its completeness has different content from the compact completion obtained from weak trace convergence.

Atomic extraction is a $1$-Lipschitz retraction and selects the unique nearest ideal model. The distance to the analytic closure is exactly the diffuse polar mass, by \cref{prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect}. The zero-mass model of \cref{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-model} has trivial multiplier ideals but positive distance from that closure. Total-variation convergence implies $L^1$ and capacity convergence even at zero mass, by \cref{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence}; the moving pole of \cref{ex:moving-pole-capacity-not-variation} shows that capacity convergence does not imply total-variation convergence.

Background degeneration is also explicit on curves. For smooth backgrounds of positive area, \cref{thm:curve-background-homothety} gives compatible area-rescaling correspondences, including for zero-mass models. Their normalized representatives vary uniformly in $L^1$ and capacity under uniform convergence of the backgrounds, by \cref{cor:curve-varying-background-continuity}. When the limiting area is zero, \cref{thm:curve-zero-area-collapse} makes every normalized potential converge to the unique limiting potential, uniformly over the admissible families. The unscaled model-space diameters tend to zero.

The joint space of \cref{thm:joint-curve-model-completeness} includes the smooth backgrounds as coordinates. It is complete for smooth background convergence and polar total variation, with analytic closure exactly the ideal locus, even at zero area. Its restriction to each fiber is the established curve topology. The higher-dimensional realization of compatible singularity data and the replacement for finite upper-volume comparisons remain unresolved.

In dimension at least two, the first curvature current can have zero restriction to the pole set for distinct analytic ideal models. \Cref{prop:isolated-pole-polar-current-blindness} proves this with isolated logarithmic poles and distinguishes their all-weight ideals. The blowup at the pole detects its coefficient on the exceptional divisor, but every fixed countable family of modifications misses some such models, by \cref{cor:exceptional-polar-current-detects-pole,cor:countable-modification-polar-blindness}.

Gauduchon trace normalizes the comparison over modifications in \cref{prop:gauduchon-polar-comparison}. It separates analytic models and recovers the complete curve topology up to a constant. Bounded equivalence preserves the polar currents by \cref{prop:bounded-type-polar-invariance}, so smooth-gauge transport preserves this comparison after ordinary projection by \cref{cor:polar-model-gauge-transport}. Polar curvature is closed for every pole set by \cref{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}. Equality of the scalar traces therefore recovers the full polar currents on each modification, by \cref{cor:closed-pole-trace-recovery}. Arbitrary independent choices of the Gauduchon forms can nevertheless fail to recover the positive-volume topology, even for analytic ideal models on $\mathbb P^2$, by \cref{prop:gauduchon-choice-topology-obstruction}. The coordinate space is complete by \cref{prop:polar-trace-coordinate-completeness}. For comparison-Cauchy sequences, \cref{prop:closed-pole-compatible-limits} gives compatible weak current limits carried by the pole set of one genuine potential. Realization of those currents by an ordinary model remains a further problem.

Even using every modification cannot make this first polar comparison a topology with the required separation and analytic closure. On every compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension at least two, \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness} constructs a nonzero ordinary model whose polar currents vanish on every modification. Its positive non-pluripolar volume is strictly below that of the zero model, while all its multiplier ideals are trivial. In fact, \cref{cor:diffuse-product-exact-volume} gives a complete ordered interval with volumes $A-Mt^n$ and distances $M|s^n-t^n|$, entirely in any prescribed cutoff below $A=\int_X\omega^n$. Every first polar coordinate is zero on this interval, and only its zero endpoint is ideal. Thus every choice of Gauduchon forms collapses distinct models, and the polar-comparison closure of the analytic models contains nonideal models. A complete extension must retain further singularity information.

On ideal models, $\tau_{\theta,0}$ is the atomic total-variation topology and its canonical metric includes the zero-mass boundary, by \cref{thm:curve-ordinary-type-topology}. On general ordinary models it is weaker than polar total variation: diffuse polar curvature can concentrate into an atom in the envelope limit, producing the Cauchy sequence with no ordinary-type limit in \cref{prop:curve-ordinary-topology-incomplete}. Thus analytic characterization on the whole ordinary space and a complete extension of the model topology are separate requirements.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:singularity-topology-program}\index{Singularity type!topology problem}
Extend the complete metrizable topology of \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric} on ordinary models beyond bounded mass and positive lower volume, retaining analytic closure exactly at the ideal models. Preserve the smooth-gauge and two-sided background compatibility of \cref{sec:model-background-comparison,sec:two-sided-model-backgrounds}, and treat background limits losing bigness or a uniform positive lower-volume bound. Recover the complete polar-variation topology of \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness} on all curve models, and determine whether the higher-dimensional extension is completely metrizable.
\end{problem}

The extension must retain the singularity information lost by weak limits. The class of the infinite-mass envelope-good potential in \cref{prop:goodness-infinite-mass} is already covered by $\tau_{\omega,0}$, while its canonical envelopes have finite mass. The curve result shows that zero volume alone need not prevent completeness; in higher dimension the stretching argument still needs a positive lower-volume bound. On the restricted divisorial product locus of \cref{prop:divisorial-product-polar-metric}, a complete metric nevertheless comes from polar curvature on the curve base, with analytic closure exactly at the ideal models. The top-degree volume comparison vanishes throughout this locus, so it cannot supply that metric. \Cref{cor:nonclosed-product-polar-metric} gives the same restricted metric and analytic closure in a positive nonclosed projective background. \Cref{cor:degenerate-product-polar-metric} preserves this locus, metric and ideal projection when the second curve coefficient vanishes and the limiting background is not big. \Cref{prop:separated-product-polar-metric} gives a complete sum metric on the locus induced by several independent curve models, with the same ideal-projection and analytic-closure properties. \Cref{cor:nonclosed-separated-product-metric} preserves that several-curve locus and metric through a nonclosed auxiliary coefficient and its nonbig zero limit. \Cref{prop:weighted-product-polar-metric} gives compatible weighted metrics when the curve coefficients vary, discarding zero coordinates and proving uniform $L^1$ collapse when all curve areas vanish. \Cref{prop:weighted-product-metric-comparison} gives quantitative comparison across active sets, including the exact distance for ordered coefficients. \Cref{prop:arbitrary-curve-product-metric,cor:arbitrary-product-zero-area-collapse} extends this product construction to arbitrary smooth admissible curve backgrounds and identifies its limiting singleton. \Cref{prop:general-auxiliary-product-metric} preserves this restricted complete metric through arbitrary compact complex auxiliary factors and semipositive vertical terms, independently of ambient bounded mass.

\section{Trace realization and recovery}

An envelope realizing divisorial lower bounds can have orders larger than those prescribed. For a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $X$ and a connected compact submanifold $Y\subset X$ with $\nu(u,Y)=0$, the trace profile and envelope of \eqref{eq:trace-profile} and \eqref{eq:trace-profile-envelope} therefore give a realization problem. \Cref{thm:restricted-analytic-envelope-limit} constructs the envelope in the original restricted background and identifies it as the $L^1$ limit of the restricted analytic envelopes. \Cref{thm:trace-envelope-modification} gives exact pullback compatibility for the inherited data and convergence under vanishing Hermitian perturbations on any compact model. Exact realization asks for equality in the divisorial inequalities. When $Y$ has bounded mass and positive lower mass, \cref{cor:mass-bound-exact-trace} proves this equality in sufficiently large backgrounds. The K\"ahler target case also follows directly from the closed theory in \cref{thm:kahler-target-exact-trace}. Analytic ambient singularities have exact ordinary restrictions. \Cref{thm:curve-fibration-exact-trace} realizes all orders in higher dimension when the data reduce to an ambient curve problem, with an ordinary type independent of a sufficiently large background. In a larger Hermitian background, \cref{thm:trace-profile-model-envelope} supplies positive finite envelope mass for general data.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:exact-trace-program}
If $Y$ fails at least one of the two mass properties, can the Hermitian background $\Omega$ in \cref{thm:trace-profile-model-envelope} be chosen so that
\[
 \nu\bigl(\mathcal T_\Omega(u;Y)\circ\pi,E\bigr)
       =\tau_{Y,u}(E)
\]
for every prime divisor $E$ on every smooth modification $\pi$ of $Y$? If so, determine a background-compatible ordinary or ideal singularity class of this realization, and prove its addition and approximation properties.
\end{problem}

The quantified equality is stronger than realizing the divisorial part on one model. \Cref{cor:divisorial-current-ordinary-type} gives a canonical ordinary type for that part by combining its Gauduchon mass bound with a smooth current decomposition. It does not determine higher-codimensional trace data on further modifications. The exceptional-divisor formula in \cref{thm:trace-exceptional-difference} describes the discrepancy between the canonical types on two models, including a cocycle and approximation in linear mass. \Cref{ex:exceptional-trace-with-exact-potential} shows that a nonzero exceptional term is compatible with an exact trace potential: the term records information absent from the original model's divisorial part. Increasing the background ensures a competitor with controlled curvature; it does not automatically exclude additional divisorial orders. The curve-fibration proof reduces every further-model order to a one-variable multiplicity. Under the two mass properties, \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} controls Lelong numbers through the upper-volume deficit and a positive lower volume. Extending that control beyond these mass conditions remains the missing step.

Even exact realization does not control the mass of all ideally equivalent representatives. In \cref{prop:exact-trace-infinite-representatives}, a product with the Hopf threefold has one smooth positive extended measure and one infinite-mass representative with exactly the same ideals and trace numbers. A proposed volume or continuity characterization must specify which representative or mass domain it uses.

\subsection{Recovery of compatible divisor systems}

Prescribing finitely many poles leaves a residual smooth background. If that background has a positive representative, \cref{thm:divisorial-residual-equation} solves the residual equation for a smooth strictly positive probability measure. Every threshold subsolution dominating the prescribed divisor differs from this solution by a constant. A solution in the prescribed ordinary type consequently has the same scalar and a smooth remainder. The difficulty in passing to compatible data on all modifications is to keep this positivity and exact singularity information uniformly.

Passing to all modifications introduces two further requirements. A smooth positive probability measure can pull back to a density that vanishes on the exceptional divisor. The smooth dual formula cannot be applied to that density without proving a degenerate extension. The residual classes can be compared explicitly: \cref{cor:affine-trace-exceptional-difference} subtracts the effective exceptional Bott--Chern class while retaining the prescribed exterior derivative. The coefficient approximants converge in linear trace mass, but no nonlinear volume transformation follows from this formula. Neither the degenerate equation issue nor the volume issue is resolved by assigning an intersection number to a nonclosed form.

The envelope-system construction in \cref{chap:hermitian-envelope-systems} gives an order adjunction and an exact criterion for recovery: the perturbation limit must retain the prescribed ideal type. Under bounded mass and bigness, \cref{thm:positive-volume-system-recovery} supplies a sufficient condition using only the initial slices: their lower singularity volumes must have a positive limit as the perturbation vanishes. Concavity then gives positive lower volume at every finite parameter, and stretching preserves all local divisorial orders. The ruled-surface example has zero residual volume and shows why unrestricted recovery fails.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:positive-system-recovery}
For a smooth big background on a manifold without bounded mass, find a condition on a compatible Hermitian envelope system that forces recovery of its ideal type at every finite parameter. It should recover the positive limiting lower-volume criterion of \cref{thm:positive-volume-system-recovery} when bounded mass holds. Specify the invariant and uniform estimates that replace the finite upper-volume deficit, and their behavior under modifications.
\end{problem}

For fixed finite divisor support, \cref{prop:finite-divisorial-equation-limits} gives a positive-cone case: if the coefficients converge and the limiting residual background still has a smooth positive representative, the normalized smooth remainders converge smoothly. The singular equation solutions converge in capacity, their measures converge in total variation, and their logarithmic coefficients remain exact on every modification. Multiplier ideals can nevertheless jump at an integer coefficient. For moving sections in fixed finite-dimensional spaces, positive limiting weights and residuals give the full-potential stability of \cref{thm:moving-divisorial-potential-stability}, in its two stated regimes. Recovery beyond the bounded-mass positive-volume regime, degeneration of residual positivity, and estimates uniform over infinitely many models still require additional arguments.

\section{Sections, kernels and the dimension of growth}

A Hermitian form on the manifold does not become the curvature of a holomorphic line bundle unless it is closed. The algebraic part of the theory must therefore keep its closed curvature data and its analytic background separate. The weighted spaces of sections, their multiplicative variant, and their intrinsic flag bodies do so. Their growth dimension can be strictly less than the complex dimension of the manifold.

For partial kernels, the general upper envelope bound is proved. A matching lower bound is available in the explicit regimes below, while the general problem remains open. \Cref{thm:fujiki-partial-kernel-limit} gives the full-dimensional kernel measure limit on a manifold with a K\"ahler modification and a positive-mass closed-curvature potential. The proof retains the relative canonical bundle: its Jacobian factor corrects both ideal integrability and the Hilbert norm. This geometric assumption implies BMP; the birational transformation identities themselves do not require it. On a holomorphic submersion with descended data, the source equilibrium theorem on the base gives a different exact limit upstairs. Write $m$ for the positive integer tensor power of the selecting line bundle on the Hopf $n$-fold. The Hopf example has growth degree $n-1$, and normalizing by $m^n$ would erase it. Adding the vertical Hermitian form recovers a nonzero measure, with the factor computed in \eqref{eq:hopf-partial-equilibrium}.

Isolated point poles which do not descend are treated in \cref{thm:isolated-pole-kernel-limit}: their projected jet conditions differ from the base multiplier ideals by only finitely many orders. For a single point on the Hopf manifold, \cref{thm:hopf-point-potential-limit} proves the potential lower asymptotic as well as total-variation convergence of the kernel measures. Moving the pole along its fiber leaves every selected space unchanged. These limits therefore recover the projected equilibrium, while the original Hermitian multiplier-ideal type can be different.

\Cref{thm:horizontal-projective-kernels} gives a further regime: a logarithmic pole along a section over a hyperplane, with arbitrary positive-dimensional compact connected fibers. The growth degree is the base dimension. Its partial potentials attain the domination envelope of the original upstairs singularity, and the kernel measures converge in total variation for every smooth positive norm volume. The base limit is fixed by the line-bundle metric; its lift retains precisely the conditional fiber probabilities.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:partial-kernel-lower-program}
Identify hypotheses on a weighted section series and its intrinsic growth dimension under which the partial Fubini--Study potentials attain the envelope lower bound and the normalized kernel measures converge. Relate the limiting measure to the Hermitian background while retaining the closed curvature and the growth dimension of the series.
\end{problem}

Beyond the isolated-point, horizontal projective and descended regimes proved above, this question includes two tasks: producing enough sections with the required ideal constraints, and controlling the resulting measure. A Bergman upper bound supplies neither. In a non-Moishezon setting, ambient-degree normalization may already force every kernel measure to vanish, independently of singularity theory.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Locality of H\"older regularity and regularity of weighted extremals under a uniform density-in-capacity condition provide specific cases of the regularity problem; see \cref{sec:history-regularity}. These hypotheses do not cover every singular envelope or settle the lower-asymptotic problem above.

The finite-codimension comparison suggests a more specific question on a fibration. It asks for an analytic description of the section constraints after descent.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:fiber-selected-approximation}
Let $\pi:X\to B$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion of connected compact complex manifolds with connected fibers, where $B$ is K\"ahler of dimension $q\geq1$. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$, a holomorphic line bundle $A$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ on $B$, and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. Set $\theta=c_1(A,h)$. For positive integers $m$, identify
\[
 V_m=H^0\bigl(X,\pi^*A^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\varphi)\bigr)
          \subseteq H^0(B,A^m)
\]
by holomorphic descent. Find geometric or analytic conditions that produce $\psi\in\PSH(B,\theta)$ of positive non-pluripolar mass such that, for all sufficiently large $m$,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 W_m=H^0\bigl(B,A^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\psi)\bigr)\subseteq V_m,\\
 \dim V_m-\dim W_m=o(m^q).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Determine which features of the singularities of $\varphi$ along the fibers suffice for this approximation.
\end{problem}

For descended continuous weights and probability norms, \cref{lem:kernel-codimension-comparison} and the base equilibrium theorem would then give the kernel-measure and determinant-functional limits. They do not by themselves give convergence of the potentials. The finite isolated-point case in \cref{thm:isolated-pole-kernel-limit} supplies the stronger error $O(m^{q-1})$, under its stated hypothesis on the projected poles. \Cref{thm:horizontal-center-descent} gives the same error for a logarithmic singularity along a section over a smooth base divisor, under the curvature margin stated there. Its base reference is explicitly divisorial, and \cref{ex:horizontal-center-sharp-error} proves sharpness of the error order. The general problem does not assert that every Hermitian-positive selecting weight admits such a base reference.

\subsection{Growing vanishing orders on curves}

Fix an integer $r\geq1$ and nonnegative integers $q_m$, indexed by positive integers $m$. The model in \cref{sec:degenerate-selected-kernels} treats the smooth weight $|z|^{2r}$ and selecting ideals $(z^{q_m})$ on $\mathbb C$. When $q_m\to\infty$, the kernel vanishes at the natural rescaled limit, but has a Gaussian transition at the larger radius $(q_m/(rm))^{1/(2r)}$. For $q_m=\lfloor m\lambda\rfloor$ with a real $\lambda>0$, the limiting kernel measure omits the pole atom of the limiting envelope current. For a compact curve with the curvature and local expansion in \cref{thm:compact-growing-jets}, that theorem transfers the Gaussian transition to global sections when $q_m=o(m^{1/(2r+1)})$. The next problem concerns larger growing orders.

\Needspace{27\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:growing-jets-localization}
Let $C$ be a connected compact Riemann surface, let $x\in C$, and let $(L,h)$ be a smooth Hermitian holomorphic line bundle whose curvature is semipositive and strictly positive on $C\setminus\{x\}$. Fix an integer $r\geq1$. Suppose that in a local coordinate $z$ centered at $x$ and a holomorphic frame $e$ one has
\[
 |e|_h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\phi},\qquad
 \phi(z)=|z|^{2r}+O(|z|^{2r+1}).
\]
Let $\nu$ be a smooth positive volume form with local expression $a(z)\,\mathrm{d}A$ and $a(0)=1$, where $\mathrm{d}A$ is Euclidean area. For positive integers $m$, choose integers $q_m\to\infty$ with $q_m=o(m)$. Give
\[
 V_m=H^0(C,L^m\otimes\mathfrak m_x^{q_m})
\]
the norm $\|s\|^2=\int_C|s|_{h^m}^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu$, where $\mathfrak m_x$ is the ideal sheaf of $x$. Write $B_m=\sum_j|s_{m,j}|_{h^m}^2$ for an orthonormal basis $(s_{m,j})$ of $V_m$, with value zero when $V_m=0$. Determine the growth ranges of $q_m$ for which, for each fixed real $\tau$, every sequence of points $x_m$ in the coordinate neighborhood satisfying
\[
 m|z(x_m)|^{2r}=q_m/r+\tau\sqrt{q_m/r}
\]
obeys
\[
 \frac{\pi B_m(x_m)}{mr^2|z(x_m)|^{2r-2}}
 \longrightarrow
 \Phi(\tau):=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}
                         \mathrm{e}^{-s^2/2}\,\mathrm{d}s.
\]
When this transition law fails, determine how further terms of the local weight change its center and scale.
\end{problem}

For the data of this problem, put $R_m=(q_m/(rm))^{1/(2r)}$. The range $q_m=o(m^{1/(2r+1)})$ is settled by \cref{thm:compact-growing-jets}: on every fixed multiple of the disc of radius $R_m$, the local weight error satisfies $mR_m^{2r+1}=q_mR_m/r=o(1)$. Its proof constructs global sections with the growing vanishing condition. Extending the range requires control of the weight remainder at the transition scale, rather than only a fixed-order kernel limit. Higher-dimensional analogues and transfer through Hermitian fibrations remain separate questions.

\section{Equations on singular spaces}

On a resolution of a singular space, a pulled-back Hermitian form degenerates and the pulled-back density can vanish or become singular. Existence upstairs must therefore be combined with an estimate that permits descent. \Cref{app:hermitian-spaces} provides potentials, envelopes, ideal equivalence and extended measures on reduced irreducible compact unibranch spaces, retaining the original nonclosed background. On projective cones, \cref{thm:cone-logarithmic-equation,thm:cone-bounded-equation} now supply explicit equations in full ordinary logarithmic classes and in the closed bounded class, for the prescribed angular and radial data. The logarithmic construction includes determinant-preserving nonclosed deformations. Its full solution descends even when the smooth residual is nonconstant on the exceptional fiber. The continuity results discussed in \cref{sec:history-regularity} use additional geometric and integrability hypotheses, notably log terminal singularities.

A conformal Gauduchon metric, uniformly comparable to the original metric, is available on the regular locus of a compact reduced irreducible Hermitian space of dimension at least two; see \cref{sec:history-regularity}. This existence theorem is not reconstructed in the appendix and does not by itself supply the nonlinear equations asked for below.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:singular-space-equations-program}
Beyond the projective-cone constructions of \cref{sec:projective-cone-equations}, determine geometric and measure conditions on compact unibranch Hermitian spaces that give existence and uniqueness in specified singularity classes. Identify estimates ensuring finite mass, descent and continuity, and their behavior under common refinements.
\end{problem}

In the cone examples, the pulled-back density vanishes on the exceptional divisor, and the logarithmic remainder solves an equation in a strictly positive background. The closed bounded endpoint instead uses an energy which is coercive off that divisor. More general spaces require a replacement for these two controls. The linear trace construction also needs a separate singular-space formulation; there is no smooth elliptic Green operator across the singular locus without additional analysis.
