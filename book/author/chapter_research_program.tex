\label{chap:research-program}

\predisplaypenalty=10000

Throughout this chapter $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$. The Hermitian forms, volume forms and backgrounds needed for each problem are fixed in the section where the problem is stated.

Many arguments in this book begin with linear control. A uniform bound on Gauduchon traces makes a family of positive currents relatively compact, and Gauduchon duality expresses scalar invariants through optimization over test currents. Most of the questions below arise because such linear control does not determine the nonlinear data of a limit, namely its Monge--Amp\`ere mass and its singularities. Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on $X$ that fails the bounded mass property of \cref{def:bmp}. By \cref{prop:integrable-infinite-mass,cor:strong-linear-infinite-mass}, currents $\omega+\ddc u$ with integrable coefficients can converge in the coefficient $L^1$ norm, with Gauduchon trace measures converging in total variation, while their determinant masses are infinite. By \cref{prop:goodness-infinite-mass}, even the relative envelope class $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0)$ of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class} contains potentials of infinite mass. Passing from linear bounds to an exact equation therefore requires additional control of the nonlinear limit.

Two further requirements recur. Positivity of a scalar invariant does not imply strict positivity: the smooth semipositive background on a projective manifold in \cref{thm:radial-positive-volume-nonbig} has positive Gauduchon variational volume but is not big. Likewise, an envelope realizing prescribed divisorial lower bounds may acquire larger orders, and then it does not recover the prescribed singularity type. Thus the mass of a limiting solution, strict positivity of a current, and exactness of its divisorial orders have to be checked in their respective arguments.

\section{Threshold equations and singular limits}

Solving a Monge--Amp\`ere equation with singular data involves two separate steps: one constructs a subsolution at the optimal coefficient, and then passes to a limit of approximate solutions without losing Monge--Amp\`ere mass. Throughout this section, $h$ is a Hermitian form on $X$ and $\mathrm{d}V$ a smooth positive probability volume form. We write $v_-(h)$ and $v_+(h)$ for the lower and upper masses of $h$ (\cref{def:bmp}), $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)$ for the extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure of $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ (\cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}), which may have infinite mass, and $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ for the relative envelope class with reference $0$. For a nonzero nonnegative density $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, the density threshold $b_h(f)$ is the largest $a\geq0$ for which some $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)\geq af\,\mathrm{d}V$ (\cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}); such a $u$ with $a=b_h(f)$ is called a threshold subsolution. Finally, $\mathcal D_h$ is the space of density roots $p\in L^n(\mathrm{d}V)$ whose root norm $\rho_h(p)=b_h(|p|^n)^{-1/n}$ is finite (\cref{prop:individual-density-banach}).

In two regimes the equations are solved for arbitrary finite non-pluripolar measures. For a semipositive big background $\theta$ with positive lower mass and a model reference satisfying \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}, \cref{thm:als-general-measure} solves both the equation with a positive exponent and the normalized equation. For an arbitrary smooth big background $\theta$, \cref{thm:big-model-exponential,thm:big-model-normalized} solve both equations under bounded mass and positive lower volume of the model reference. The existence theorem for $L^p$ densities in the minimal ordinary singularity type, in \cref{sec:big-minimal-existence}, requires neither mass property; in the second regime, \cref{cor:big-model-lp-type} also places the solutions for $L^p$ densities in the prescribed ordinary model type.

Under bounded mass the reference may also vary. Suppose the ordinary model references converge for the volume comparison $d_\theta$ of \cref{sec:model-volume-comparison} and have a common positive lower bound for their lower volumes, and the densities converge in $L^1$ of a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure. Then, by \cref{thm:big-varying-model-closure,thm:big-varying-equation-stability}, for a fixed positive exponent the solutions converge in capacity and their Monge--Amp\`ere measures converge in total variation. At exponent zero the scalars and the measures converge, and every $L^1$-convergent subsequence of normalized solutions converges to a solution of the limiting equation; singling out one limit potential requires a further uniqueness or selection argument.

To remove these mass hypotheses, one must replace each place where they enter the compactness arguments. The proof in the semipositive regime uses finite auxiliary contact measures to construct a common lower envelope, and then obtains the limit for a moving measure from convergence in capacity and control of deep sublevel tails. The proof for an arbitrary big background uses finite upper relative volumes to construct a rooftop between classes and to maximize the weighted measure. In either case one must still prove that the measure of the solution is finite; the formal existence of the envelopes does not give this.

\subsection{Individual density thresholds}

One must distinguish solvability for all measures from solvability for one fixed density. By \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-solvability}, the normalized equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=c\mu$ has a solution in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ for every nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu$ exactly when $v_-(h)>0$. When $v_-(h)=0$, \cref{thm:lower-mass-obstructing-measure} produces a probability density in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, bounded below by a positive constant, that admits no positive scaled subsolution. Such obstructing densities are dense in $L^1$, although every smooth strictly positive density is solvable. The compact threefold of \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass} has $v_-(h)=0$ for every Hermitian form $h$, so the obstruction does occur.

For one nonnegative density $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, the threshold $b_h(f)$ is the decreasing limit of the normalization constants of the bounded truncations $\min(f,k)$, see \eqref{eq:individual-truncated-normalizer}. An argument with upper envelopes and damped continuity shows that a subsolution attains this coefficient, but it proves neither an exact equation nor finiteness of the mass. Smooth comparison tests alone do not detect the threshold, by \cref{thm:smooth-tests-insufficient}. On the other hand, the Gauduchon formula of \cref{thm:integrable-gauduchon-formula} expresses the root norm as a supremum of linear tests, which makes $b_h$ upper semicontinuous in $L^1$. The densities with positive threshold are those whose roots $f^{1/n}$ lie in $\mathcal D_h$, and this space does not depend on the Hermitian form (\cref{prop:individual-threshold-backgrounds}). With the norm $\|p\|_{L^n}+\rho_h(p)$ it is a Banach space; it equals $L^n$ when $v_-(h)>0$, and it is complete for $\rho_h$ alone exactly when $v_+(h)<\infty$, by \cref{prop:individual-density-banach,prop:individual-root-completeness}.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:individual-equation-threshold}\index{Subsolution!exact threshold equation problem}
Let $h$ be a Hermitian form on $X$ with zero lower mass, $v_-(h)=0$, and let $\mathrm{d}V$ be a smooth positive probability volume form. Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero, with positive density threshold $b_h(f)>0$. Does there exist $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=b_h(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V?
\]
Determine the singularity type or envelope class in which such an equation should be posed.
\end{problem}

The hypothesis $v_-(h)=0$ is what makes the problem open. For $\lambda>0$, write $u_\lambda$ for the greatest solution of the exponential equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mathrm{d}V$, when it exists (\cref{thm:individual-exponential-subsolution}). When $v_-(h)>0$, \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-exact-threshold} solves the threshold equation in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ for every nonzero nonnegative $L^1$ density: comparison of finite measures identifies the scalar, and dominated continuity shows that every $L^1$-convergent subsequence of the normalized greatest exponential solutions $u_\lambda-\sup_Xu_\lambda$ converges in capacity. This covers the Hopf example of \cref{chap:hopf}, which has positive lower mass but not bounded mass. For $L^p$ densities with $p>1$, the continuous solution of the normalized equation and \cref{prop:individual-normalizer-lp} answer the question without any lower mass assumption.

At zero lower mass, \cref{thm:zero-lower-mass-product-equation} solves the threshold equation with finite mass for explicit densities on a split product with projective space; these densities are integrable but belong to no $L^p$ with $p>1$. There the background $h$ is a nonclosed Hermitian form, and the canonical solutions $u$ belong to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$; moreover, for every $0<\delta<1$, the envelope $P_{\delta h}(u)$ of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}, the largest $\delta h$-plurisubharmonic function below $u$, is not identically $-\infty$. More generally, \cref{thm:closed-factor-integrable-equation} solves the exact finite-mass equation for every nonzero integrable density from a closed K\"ahler factor, with the appropriate change of reference volume. The threshold is the split background's mass divided by the integral of the factor density. On the product of the elliptic threefold with $\mathbb P^2$, this gives threshold $240\pi$ for every probability density from that factor. An arbitrary density on the product need not have this form, and the general problem remains open.

For product measures from a projective-line factor, \cref{prop:nonpolar-curve-product-equation} gives an exact finite-mass equation for every non-polar factor probability. In the separated sector, boundedness and continuity are exactly those of its logarithmic potential. When that potential is finite and continuous, \cref{thm:continuous-potential-product-threshold} identifies the attained coefficient over all genuine subsolutions, constructs the greatest exponential solution for every positive exponent, and gives whole-family uniform selection with error $O(\lambda)$. The Sobolev measure trace supplies existence, and bounded comparison extends greatestness to singular competitors. Weighted circle probabilities in \cref{prop:frostman-product-measures} give measures singular with respect to smooth volume. The zero-lower-mass specialization retains the factor hypothesis. These product criteria do not classify arbitrary measures on a fixed Hermitian manifold.

A different geometric condition applies directly to singular measures on a fixed Hermitian manifold. The probability $\rho$ of \eqref{eq:fixed-spherical-probability} is chosen from a coordinate ball before the datum. If $\nu$ is finite, nonzero and has bounded density with respect to $\rho$, \cref{prop:fixed-spherical-reference,thm:fixed-spherical-exponential} give a strict H\"older scaled subsolution and a unique bounded exponential solution for every positive exponent. This includes bounded densities on one of the spheres and needs neither a product decomposition nor positive lower mass. It is a sufficient condition; it does not classify arbitrary individual measures or identify a homogeneous threshold coefficient.

For the data of \cref{qu:individual-equation-threshold}, \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential} shows that the greatest exponential solutions $u_\lambda$ exist for every $\lambda>0$, that the scalars $c_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_\lambda}$ converge to $b_h(f)$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$, and that the measures $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f\,\mathrm{d}V$ converge in total variation. What is missing in general is convergence in capacity of the normalized potentials. If $f^{1/n}$ lies in the subspace $\mathcal D_h^0$ of density roots with uniformly vanishing tails (\cref{prop:root-uniform-tails}), \cref{prop:exponential-root-tail-modulus} gives a modulus for the errors of the scalars and of the measures; a rate linear in the exponent would require further control.

Positivity and integrability of $f$ do not make the threshold positive, and exponential equations can be solvable even when it vanishes. For zero threshold, \cref{thm:product-zero-threshold-exponential-criterion} gives a sufficient growth condition in the radial variable, and \cref{thm:zero-threshold-solvable-concentration} constructs a single strictly positive probability density with $b_h(f)=0$ whose exponential equation has, for every positive exponent, a greatest solution of finite mass; all its solutions are unbounded. In that construction the projective factors are chosen after the collapsing sequence on the base.

For this density, \cref{prop:zero-threshold-exponential-mass-collapse} shows that any choice of exact exponential solutions has total mass tending to zero as the exponent decreases to zero; the argument says nothing about convergence of the normalized potentials. In general, the set $\Lambda_h(f)$ of positive exponents for which the exponential equation is solvable is an upper interval, by \cref{prop:solvable-exponents-upper-interval}. A necessary criterion on the density for solvability at zero threshold, solvability for an arbitrary prescribed concentration family, and an extension to arbitrary non-pluripolar measures are open.

Suppose that $a=\inf\Lambda_h(f)$ is positive. By \cref{prop:positive-exponent-endpoint-attainment}, $a$ belongs to $\Lambda_h(f)$ if and only if there are exact solutions at exponents decreasing to $a$ whose suprema are bounded below; if $a\notin\Lambda_h(f)$, the total masses of every such sequence of solutions tend to zero. The common upper barrier of \cref{lem:positive-exponent-uniform-upper-barrier} also shows that the greatest solutions become monotone in the exponent after a logarithmic correction. Consequently, by \cref{prop:greatest-exponential-right-continuity}, they are continuous from the right in $L^1$ and in capacity at every exponent in $\Lambda_h(f)$, and their Monge--Amp\`ere measures converge in total variation. At every interior exponent, \cref{prop:greatest-exponential-left-limit} gives a left limit in $L^1$ and in capacity that solves the equation at that exponent and lies below the greatest solution; the equation measures converge in total variation. By \cref{prop:greatest-exponential-countable-jumps}, these potentials can differ at only countably many exponents. Whether a positive endpoint can fail to belong to $\Lambda_h(f)$, or an actual jump from the left can occur, remains open.

\subsection{Dual attainment and exact equations}

For the current formulation of the dual problem, suppose $n\geq2$. By Gauduchon duality, the threshold is determined by a maximization problem over test currents. Let $\mathscr G_h$ be the compact convex set of normalized Gauduchon currents, the positive $(n-1,n-1)$-currents $T$ with $\ddc T=0$ and $\int_Xh\wedge T=1$ (\cref{sec:gauduchon-linear-duality}), and let $k_T$ be the determinant density of $T$ (\cref{thm:gaud-determinant-subcritical}). For $0\leq p\in\mathcal D_h$, the root norm $\rho_h(p)$ is the supremum of $D_p(T)=\int_Xp\,k_T\,\mathrm{d}V$ over $T\in\mathscr G_h$. A maximizing current can turn the threshold inequality into an equality, provided its regularity, or control of its weighted moments, prevents loss of mass in the limit. For roots in the closed order ideal $\mathcal D_h^0$, \cref{thm:integrable-current-attainment,prop:integrable-threshold-adjoints} show that the supremum is attained and represent all supporting probabilities of the threshold functional by maximizing currents. These probabilities are absolutely continuous, and by \cref{prop:threshold-trace-saturation} the trace errors along smooth maximizing sequences tend to zero in total variation. Uniform integrability along a single maximizing sequence already gives attainment and convergence of the determinant-weighted measures of that sequence (\cref{prop:selected-gauduchon-attainment}), but it does not give the representation of all supporting probabilities.

Regularity of a maximizer has strong consequences. Let $n\geq2$ and let $f$ be a nonzero density with $b_h(f)>0$. If $D_{f^{1/n}}$ has a smooth strictly positive maximizer, then $f^{1/n}$ is smooth and nonnegative, and every threshold subsolution is smooth and solves the equation exactly, uniquely up to constants (\cref{cor:smooth-dual-exact-equation}). For a K\"ahler background, \cref{cor:kahler-smooth-dual-attainment} shows that such a maximizer exists exactly when $f$ is smooth and strictly positive. The strict positivity fails for nonclosed backgrounds: on every compact complex manifold of dimension at least two, \cref{ex:nonclosed-dual-zero-density} gives a smooth strictly positive maximizer for a density vanishing on an open set.

\Cref{thm:semipositive-dual-exact-equation} weakens the hypothesis to a smooth semipositive maximizer that is strictly positive outside a proper analytic subset $Z\subset X$. It still gives an exact equation on all of $X$, smoothness of the solutions off $Z$, and uniqueness of threshold subsolutions up to constants. The proof takes the maximum of two subsolutions and uses a Gauduchon integral to eliminate the divisorial part of their difference; no singularity type is prescribed in advance. The prime divisors along which the threshold solution has positive Lelong number have linearly independent Bott--Chern classes, by \cref{cor:semipositive-dual-divisor-independence}. In this regime write $b=b_h(f)$ and $c_\lambda=\exp(\lambda\sup_Xu_\lambda)$ for the greatest exponential solutions $u_\lambda$, $\lambda>0$. By \cref{prop:semipositive-dual-exponential-limit}, the whole family of normalized solutions converges in $L^1$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$, it has a fixed lower barrier, and $b\leq c_\lambda\leq b\mathrm{e}^{C\lambda}$ for a finite constant $C$ independent of $\lambda$. If the envelope $P_{\delta h}(u_0)$ of the normalized threshold solution $u_0$ is not identically $-\infty$ for some $0<\delta<1$, the convergence also holds in capacity (\cref{cor:semipositive-dual-capacity-limit}); for $L^p$ data with $p>1$ the threshold solution is continuous and the convergence is uniform (\cref{cor:semipositive-dual-lp-limit}). In \cref{ex:semipositive-projective-maximizer} the solution is smooth, but the root $f^{1/n}$ is not differentiable on $Z$. For general $L^1$ data we do not know whether $P_{\delta h}(u_0)\not\equiv-\infty$ holds automatically, nor how regular the solution is across $Z$.

The moment criterion of \cref{thm:gauduchon-moment-threshold} requires no regularity of a maximizing current. Suppose that the supremum-normalized $h$-plurisubharmonic functions have uniformly bounded first moments against the determinant-weighted probabilities of one smooth maximizing sequence. Then the exponential solutions have a fixed lower barrier, and their normalized cluster limits lie below the greatest normalized threshold subsolution $\Phi_f$ of \cref{prop:greatest-normalized-threshold}. If, in addition, $P_{\delta h}(\Phi_f)\not\equiv-\infty$ for some $0<\delta<1$, the lower-barrier criterion gives convergence in capacity along subsequences and an exact threshold equation, without either mass property. An entropy bound on these test probabilities implies both the bound on first moments and the uniform integrability along one sequence used above. By \cref{prop:hermitian-dual-entropy-bounds}, such an entropy bound is equivalent to a uniform bound for $H_0$, the convex dual of the normalization functional with zero reference (\cref{chap:normalization}). An entropy bound does not, however, give the condition on $P_{\delta h}(\Phi_f)$.

Attainment by a singular current therefore does not settle the equation by itself. The roots from a closed factor form a closed subspace of $\mathcal D_h^0$ isomorphic to $L^n$ on that factor, by \cref{prop:closed-factor-density-roots}; some of these roots belong to no higher $L^r$ space, and their tails decay only as a power of $\log K$. At zero lower mass we do not know in general whether the root-tail condition $f^{1/n}\in\mathcal D_h^0$ suffices for an exact threshold equation, or even whether $\mathcal D_h^0=\mathcal D_h$. By \cref{prop:root-tail-continuity}, the tail condition is equivalent to continuity of the threshold under logarithmic perturbations $\mathrm{e}^{F_j}f$ with $F_j$ uniformly bounded and tending to zero in measure. This is continuity on order intervals, not continuity of the threshold under arbitrary $L^1$ perturbations.

Convergence in capacity cannot be replaced by $L^1$ convergence in these arguments. On a curve, the ideal envelopes with finitely many logarithmic poles in \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} have zero non-pluripolar mass, yet they converge in $L^1$ to a smooth potential of positive mass. The existence theorems obtain the stronger convergence from their measure bounds; representing the measure by a carrier, even within a prescribed singularity type, does not provide it.

\subsection{Regularity of singular solutions}

Regularity estimates provide the compactness needed to single out a limit as the exponential parameter tends to zero. In this subsection $\theta$ is a smooth big real $(1,1)$-form, and $\rho$ is the strict barrier with analytic singularities fixed in \cref{sec:big-minimal-comparison}, with pole set $Z$ and regular locus $\Omega=X\setminus Z$. For bounded solutions with $L^p$ density, $p>1$, \cref{thm:local-big-bounded-continuity} gives continuity wherever $\rho$ is locally bounded. A locally H\"older continuous solution with minimal singularities is smooth on $\Omega$ when the density is smooth and positive there (\cref{thm:nonclosed-interior-smoothness}), and global $L^p$ control of the density gives the H\"older hypothesis (\cref{thm:big-minimal-holder,cor:big-lp-interior-smoothness}).

For a fixed reference $\eta$ with $\rho\leq\eta\leq0$, \cref{cor:big-reference-uniform-convergence} shows that the potentials $u$ with $\eta-C_0\leq u\leq\eta$ and uniformly bounded $L^p$ densities form a compact family for uniform convergence of differences on $\Omega$. Since the reference need not have minimal singularities, this compactness does not by itself make the potentials regular. In the minimal type, the convergence of scalars and densities in \cref{prop:big-exponential-clusters} identifies the equation satisfied by every cluster limit of the normalized exponential solutions.

Smoothness of the density on an open set is not enough. On a compact K\"ahler manifold with a smooth positive volume form, \cref{thm:big-class-ample-smoothness} proves that the full-mass solution is smooth on the ample locus of every big class, nef or not. Smoothness of the density only on the ample locus, however, does not imply global $L^p$ integrability. On an arbitrary open set where the density is smooth, it does not even imply continuity of a bounded solution with minimal singularities: \cref{thm:compact-local-density-discontinuity} gives a compact K\"ahler counterexample with constant density on a ball. When the background has smooth local potentials, \cref{thm:smooth-boundary-trace-regularity} gives a sufficient condition in terms of smooth boundary traces on small balls. Among prescribed singularity types, the resolved logarithmic types of \cref{thm:resolved-type} admit H\"older remainders when their residual form is positive, while for exceptional minimal poles \cref{thm:minimal-blowup-data,thm:iterated-blowups} give a H\"older remainder on the target and quantify the loss of integrability caused by the Jacobian. Neither result covers arbitrary prescribed types, and neither derives a global H\"older bound from information on the density confined to the regular locus. At a zero of the density, even smooth data need not give a smooth residual: \cref{prop:exceptional-density-obstruction,ex:power-blowup-family} give a necessary vanishing order and show that it is not sufficient. Beyond these resolved and explicit geometric cases, the regularity questions are open.

For the nonlogarithmic projective types of \cref{sec:radial-residual-regularity}, the central density criterion gives residual regularity across the pole and smoothly through density zeros. Perturbing the same singular reference as an everywhere radial obstacle instead gives a globally $C^{1,1}$ residual, zero near the pole, with open free regions of zero measure (\cref{thm:singular-radial-envelope,cor:singular-radial-envelope-mass}). Fixed-support bounded $C^2$ obstacle families satisfy the derivative stability of \cref{thm:singular-radial-uniform-stability}, and \cref{prop:singular-radial-full-product} transfers the full envelope to any fixed positive Hermitian product factor. These explicit ordinary-type regimes do not cover general singular references or ideal weighted envelopes.

\subsection{Uniqueness and selection}

Where the density vanishes, the linearized equation degenerates, and the zero set can separate regions where it is elliptic. The question is whether the difference of two solutions can vary from one such region to another. Throughout this subsection $\omega$ is a Hermitian form on $X$, $f$ is smooth and nonnegative, and potentials are normalized by $\sup_Xu=0$.

On $\mathbb P^3$, \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness} gives a nonclosed Hermitian form and a smooth nonnegative density with two distinct smooth normalized solutions; there the density vanishes on an open set. The construction of \cref{thm:numa-thin-zero-set} has uncountably many smooth normalized solutions for a density that is positive off two disjoint real hypersurfaces, and after a modification of the background, \cref{thm:quadratic-density-nonuniqueness} achieves exactly quadratic vanishing on those hypersurfaces. Thus neither a zero set of zero volume nor vanishing of finite order guarantees uniqueness. In all these examples the background is a Hermitian form with bounded mass; the degeneracy responsible for nonuniqueness lies in the density and in the solution forms.

These examples restrict any geometric use of the equation. A construction assigning a canonical potential to arbitrary nonnegative data must specify a selection procedure and prove that it is well defined; existence in the minimal ordinary singularity type does not provide one. The scalar, which is unique, and for strictly positive data the corresponding variational volume, remain meaningful without uniqueness of the potential. In low dimension uniqueness holds under additional conditions. On a compact Hermitian surface, \cref{uni-cor:dense-lowdim} proves uniqueness for $C^2$ potentials when the common density is continuous and positive on a dense set, with no differential condition on the metric; in dimension three, \cref{uni-thm:sobolev} uses instead a sign condition on $\ddc\omega$. The two arguments rest on different integration identities: the cofactor identity \eqref{eq:mixed-uniqueness-defect} and a signed estimate for the quadratic difference.

When $\{f>0\}$ is connected and dense, \cref{thm:connected-positive-uniqueness} shows that two bounded normalized solutions with the same positive scalar coincide; the theorem applies to the measure $cf\,\mathrm{d}V$ after the smooth positive ratio $\mathrm{d}V/\omega^n$ is absorbed into the density. In all the counterexamples above whose zero set consists of hypersurfaces, including the real-analytic example of \cref{uni-thm:analytic-example}, the positivity set has three connected components. None of them contradicts \cref{conj:continuous-gaud-adjoint}, which concerns strictly positive densities $\mathrm{e}^F$ with $F$ continuous.

For a fixed nonzero density $f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, the exponential equation does select a solution. By \cref{thm:vanishing-exponential-selection}, a weakly compact set of norming probabilities determines the limit: as the exponent tends to zero, the suitably shifted exponential solutions converge to the greatest subsolution of the critical equation that satisfies the integral normalizations given by these probabilities. In a Hermitian background the convergence is uniform; in an arbitrary smooth big background it holds in $L^1(X)$ and locally uniformly off the pole set of the strict barrier. The selected limit inherits the symmetries of the data (\cref{prop:symmetric-equation-clusters}), and in the projective example \cref{thm:numa-selected-limit} identifies it with the explicit symmetric $C^{1,1}$ solution of \cref{prop:numa-radial-continuum}. Selection thus coexists with nonuniqueness of the normalized critical equation. For the merely integrable radial densities on zero-lower-mass products, \cref{thm:radial-integrable-selection} determines the whole supremum-normalized limit, even with joint variation of the density parameter and exponent. Scalar centering has a finite limit exactly below the moment boundary of \cref{prop:radial-scalar-centering}; at the boundary and above it diverges. For logarithmic product models with positive residual, \cref{thm:moving-logarithmic-selection} also gives selection under independent variation of the reference coefficients, background, density and exponent; centering follows each varying critical scalar. Selection for arbitrary integrable data and general varying singular reference types remains open.

Dual attainment gives a different criterion. In dimension at least two, suppose that $f\neq0$, that $b_\omega(f)>0$, and that $D_{f^{1/n}}$ has a smooth strictly positive maximizing Gauduchon current. Then the normalized threshold solution is unique, and the whole family of normalized solutions with positive exponent converges uniformly to it (\cref{cor:smooth-dual-exact-equation,cor:smooth-dual-exponential-limit}). The density of \cref{ex:nonclosed-dual-zero-density} satisfies this condition although it vanishes on an open set. The size of the zero set alone therefore does not decide uniqueness; the dual condition also depends on the background.

For prescribed radial nonlogarithmic types, \cref{thm:radial-residual-regularity} gives a different kind of regularity: a relative density error $O(r^\delta)$ controls the residual after the singularity is subtracted. The critical $C^1$ and $C^2$ thresholds are attained and are sharp by \cref{prop:radial-residual-sharpness}. Positive cumulative central mass allows smooth crossing of density zeros, and \cref{prop:radial-zero-annulus} gives an open zero annulus with a globally smooth residual. The product construction \cref{cor:radial-residual-nonclosed-product} transfers this regularity under a zero-lower-mass factor hypothesis. These are radial criteria; they do not imply regularity or uniqueness for general nonsymmetric data.

\section{Positive Gauduchon volume and bigness}
\label{sec:gaud-volume-bigness}
\index{Gauduchon variational volume!failure of bigness}

Fix a smooth strictly positive probability form $\mu$ on $X$. For a pseudoeffective smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$, the Gauduchon variational volume $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta)=\lambda_\mu(\theta)^n$ of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} is an infimum over Gauduchon metrics, whereas bigness asks for one potential $u$ making $\theta+\ddc u$ strictly positive. Bigness implies $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta)>0$ (\cref{cor:gaud-volume-big-positive}). The converse fails for nef nonclosed backgrounds: for every $n\geq3$, \cref{thm:radial-positive-volume-nonbig} constructs a smooth semipositive background $\theta$ on $\mathbb P^{n-1}\times\mathbb P^1$ with $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta)=n$ that is not big: no current $\theta+\ddc u$ is bounded below by a positive multiple of a Hermitian form. In this example a radial potential compensates the determinant, while a closed test current carried by a degenerate fiber detects the missing strict positivity.

Positivity of $\mathcal V_\mu$ does not depend on the choice of $\mu$ (\cref{prop:gaud-volume-density-comparison}), but the infimum over all such $\mu$ is a different invariant: by \cref{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema} it is the lower mass, and it vanishes for every Hermitian form on the threefold of \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}, although Hermitian forms are big. In dimension one, positive volume and bigness are equivalent, since the volume is $\int_X\theta$. For a closed nef class on a compact K\"ahler manifold, $\mathcal V_\mu$ is the top self-intersection, and the equivalence is the nef-and-big criterion recalled in \cite{SGPT}. On a compact K\"ahler manifold, \cref{thm:closed-core-subtraction} also gives bigness when a closed nef core of positive volume is supplemented by a semipositive form. Whether positive volume implies bigness for general nef nonclosed backgrounds on surfaces is open. In higher dimension, any such criterion requires geometric hypotheses that exclude the radial obstruction.

One way to rule out this collapse is to deform the test metrics. Solving a Gauduchon equation replaces the volume density of a test metric while keeping the Aeppli class of $\gamma^{n-1}$. When $\theta$ is closed, this leaves the numerator $A_\theta$ unchanged. For nonclosed $\theta$, integration by parts leaves derivatives of the background, so such a deformation must also control the change of the pairing; this is the additional estimate needed to use the smooth equation against collapse.

\section{Closed and twisted classes}

A volume attached to a closed Bott--Chern class must not depend on the representative, while a volume equation for a locally conformally K\"ahler (LCK) form must preserve its Lee class and its twisted differential. The examples below show where ordinary Hermitian perturbations fail to respect these requirements. We use the LCK notions of \cref{def:lck}: for a closed one-form $\lambda$, $D_\lambda$ is the twisted Hessian of \eqref{eq:lck-gauge-operators}, and $j_X\colon H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)\to H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ is the Lee map \eqref{eq:lck-lee-bc-map}; the associated Lee--Gauduchon cone is studied in \cref{sec:lee-gauduchon-cone}.

The non-nef and non-K\"ahler loci of \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci} separate closed currents with small negative errors from strictly positive closed currents. Any numerical restricted volume proposed for closed classes on general compact complex manifolds must be compatible with \cref{prop:bc-zero-class-infinite-error-volume}: if a Hermitian form $\omega$ fails the bounded mass property, as on the Hopf threefold, then for every $\varepsilon>0$ the supremum of $\int_X(T+\varepsilon\omega)^n$ over smooth closed representatives $T\geq-\varepsilon\omega$ of the zero class is already infinite. Since the failure occurs at each fixed error size, letting the error tend to zero afterwards cannot turn this expression into a class invariant.

For $n\geq2$, the subsolution threshold also constrains divisorial singularities. Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form, $\mathrm{d}V$ a smooth positive probability volume form and $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ a nonzero density with $b=b_\omega(f)>0$, and put $p=f^{1/n}$. If a subsolution at the threshold $b$ dominates the current of integration of an effective divisor $E$, then by \cref{prop:threshold-divisorial-constraint} the weak limits of every smooth maximizing Gauduchon sequence annihilate the class of every component of $E$. If one such sequence has uniformly integrable determinant-weighted densities, \cref{cor:semipositive-divisor-exclusion} excludes divisors whose classes have suitable smooth semipositive representatives, for instance the hyperplane divisor on the Hopf threefold, and \cref{cor:nef-divisor-threshold-volume} shows that a component with nef class has zero variational volume for every smooth density. Both conclusions hold under the root-tail condition $p\in\mathcal D_\omega^0$. Characterizing the components that can occur would require a realization theorem; note that the weak closure of smooth tests can be smaller than the set of all normalized Gauduchon currents (\cref{ex:gauduchon-current-smoothing-obstruction}).

For a prescribed logarithmic divisor with a positive Hermitian residual, \cref{thm:divisorial-type-thresholds} identifies the threshold on the entire one-sided class with that of the residual background. The ordinary class and relative envelope class have the same attained coefficient for smooth strictly positive data. A nonzero divisor then makes it strictly smaller than the global coefficient, and \cref{cor:critical-no-divisor} excludes divisorial components at the smooth global threshold. The projective product in \cref{ex:projective-divisor-threshold-loss} computes this loss explicitly. These positive-residual results do not give a converse from an annihilating face for general integrable data.

For closed classes on manifolds in Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$, \cref{sec:finite-restricted-invariants} defines a finite restricted invariant on each subvariety, independent of the K\"ahler resolution used to compute it. It is positive exactly when the class is big on the resolved subvariety, and for a nef class on $X$ the union of the subvarieties where it vanishes is the null locus. For a specified divisorial trace with bounded remainder, \cref{sec:resolved-trace-masses} computes the mass of the trace itself and turns positive residual mass into a strict current, more singular than the trace. The nonclosed ruled family of \cref{sec:nonclosed-ruled-trace-masses} goes beyond bounded residuals: \cref{thm:nonclosed-ruled-ordinary-volumes} computes both masses over the whole ordinary type, and \cref{prop:ruled-intrinsic-ideal-orders} identifies its ambient ideal orders, although the unbounded residual is invisible to those ideals. Positive lower volume gives a strict current in that ordinary type downstairs. It is preserved under pure pullback, while \cref{prop:ruled-blowup-strict-type-obstruction} excludes a strict current in the pulled-back ordinary type and still allows a one-sided strict representative. The same obstruction holds after every nonempty finite composition of point blowups, including centers at poles. More generally, \cref{thm:small-curvature-strict-type} gives an exact ordinary-type strictness criterion for a finite logarithmic divisor plus a residual with arbitrarily small negative curvature. Its ambient ideals and, in the normal crossing case, all intrinsic traces are given by \cref{prop:small-curvature-intrinsic-traces}, even when the naive residual restriction is identically $-\infty$. Thus a general restricted invariant must specify which singularity relation its strictness criterion uses. Its construction for arbitrary nonclosed backgrounds, and its relation to intrinsic multiplier-ideal trace data outside these families, remain open.

The LCK setting raises separate questions about equations. By \cref{cor:lck-no-closed-big-class}, a compact strictly LCK manifold has no big ordinary Bott--Chern class. Its LCK form belongs instead to a twisted Bott--Chern class, whose representatives are described by the twisted Hessian $D_\lambda$. Prescribing the volume form of a Gauduchon LCK metric in such a class leads to the coupled system \eqref{eq:lck-coupled-equation}, whose second equation is the additional conformal condition imposed by the Gauduchon condition. \Cref{q:lck-gauduchon-volume} asks whether this system has at most one solution when the Lee class and the twisted class are fixed; the nonuniqueness examples for ordinary potentials do not decide it.

On scalar Hopf manifolds the radial case is solved: for every smooth positive radial volume form and every negative Lee period, \cref{thm:radial-gauduchon-lck-volume} gives exactly one Gauduchon metric in the full positive radial family. By \cref{prop:radial-lck-positive-twisted-potential}, this metric has a positive potential in its own Lee gauge, so its twisted class is zero, and the coupled system determines it among radial candidates. For nonconstant radial densities these metrics are not Vaisman (\cref{prop:radial-lck-non-vaisman-volume}). Uniqueness among arbitrary LCK candidates is open.

Two geometric conjectures use the Lee form in different ways. \Cref{conj:lck-lee-psef} asserts that the class $j_X([\lambda])$, for the Lee form $\lambda$ of a strictly LCK metric, is pseudoeffective, that is, pairs nonnegatively with every Gauduchon metric; the LCK metric itself provides one positive pairing. \Cref{conj:lck-calabi-yau-vaisman} asks whether a compact complex manifold with vanishing first Bott--Chern class that admits a strictly LCK metric also admits a Vaisman metric. The rigidity theorem \cref{thm:lck-chern-ricci-rigidity} gives this when a single strictly LCK metric is both Chern--Ricci flat and Gauduchon; the remaining existence problem is to arrange these two conditions simultaneously.

There is a pointwise sufficient condition for the first conjecture. If the least Lee-curvature eigenvalue relative to the chosen strictly LCK metric is constant, \cref{lck:constant-minimum-lee} makes it zero and gives a smooth semipositive representative of the nonzero ordinary Bott--Chern class. In particular this class is nef, and the balanced obstruction applies; compact invariant LCK quotients satisfy the criterion. The volume polynomial \cref{lck:lee-volume-ray} supplies the argument. The two Hopf families show its scope: \cref{lck:variable-positive-lee-spectrum} has constant minimum but varying remaining spectrum, whereas \cref{lck:indefinite-hopf-lee} has indefinite Lee curvature and a smooth semipositive correction. In the latter family the first pointwise degeneracy retains positive total volume. A general LCK metric need not have constant minimum, so the unrestricted pseudoeffectivity conjecture stays open.

The Lee--Gauduchon cone of \cref{sec:lee-gauduchon-cone} makes part of this geometry invariant under bimeromorphic maps. Its dual is the cone of classes in $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)$ whose image under $j_X$ is pseudoeffective, and the strict inequalities defining it exclude $\ker j_X$; the duality and its behavior under modifications are proved in that section. A nonzero pseudoeffective class in the image of $j_X$ obstructs balanced metrics on every manifold bimeromorphic to $X$. On the Hopf threefold, the classes in $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)$ whose image under $j_X$ is pseudoeffective form a positive half-ray, although the Monge--Amp\`ere masses there are unbounded. This cohomological invariant thus captures a geometric obstruction, while the nonlinear estimates for the scalar equation remain a separate task. The converse of its consequence for balanced metrics fails: the threefold of \cref{ex:lee-calabi-eckmann} has zero Lee--Gauduchon cone but no strongly Gauduchon metric.

\section{Degeneration orders and finite-distance boundaries}

Approximating a nef background by positive ones yields two kinds of boundary data: the rate at which the variational volume vanishes, and the distance traveled in the Hessian metric of \cref{sec:gauduchon-metric-geometry}. These need not detect the same boundary points. Throughout this section $\mu$ is a smooth strictly positive probability form on $X$.

For a nef background $\theta$ and a Hermitian form $h$, the lower and upper degeneration orders $\underline a(\theta)\leq\overline a(\theta)$ of \cref{prop:nef-degeneration-orders} are the lower and upper limits of $\log\mathcal V_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h)/\log\epsilon$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. They do not depend on $\mu$ or on the perturbation direction $h$, but they need not coincide. On a fixed product of two elliptic curves, \cref{prop:nef-oscillatory-orders} realizes every pair $(1+\alpha,1+\beta)$ with $0<\alpha<\beta<1$, as well as irrational common orders, and \cref{prop:nef-diagonal-fractional-and-zero} gives zero boundary volume with decay slower than every positive power of $\epsilon$. Equality of the two orders and their integrality therefore require additional geometry, as in the closed K\"ahler case, where the order is governed by the numerical dimension.

Pulling back by a modification can only increase both orders (\cref{prop:nef-modification-volume}), and a single blowup can increase them strictly, or remove positive limiting volume while both orders remain zero. Subtracting a positive current gives the same one-sided comparison when the residual stays nef. The perturbative minimal multiplicities of a nef background vanish, whereas in \cref{cor:nef-boundary-minimal-removal} the boundary class contains a unique positive current, the current of integration along a divisor; it has minimal singularities, and removing it changes the order. The remaining questions, collected in \cref{qu:nef-degeneration-exponent}, concern equality of the orders and geometric restrictions on these behaviors.

The Hessian metric gives boundary information of a different kind. Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional space of smooth real $(1,1)$-forms containing no nonzero form $\ddc f$ with $f$ smooth, and let $\mathscr C$ be the open cone of elements of $V$ that admit a smooth positive representative. By \cref{prop:gaud-volume-metric-product}, the metric $-D^2\log\lambda_\mu$ on $\mathscr C$ splits isometrically as the product of the logarithmic volume direction with the unit-volume hypersurface $\mathscr S=\{\lambda_\mu=1\}$; consequently every path along which the volume tends to zero or to infinity has infinite length. Nevertheless, the blowup example of \cref{ex:gaud-metric-blowup} has boundary points at finite distance, represented by nef classes of positive volume. Completing the metric cone therefore cannot mean simply adjoining all nef classes, nor declaring every nef boundary point infinitely far away.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:gaud-metric-boundary}
Let $\mu$ be a smooth strictly positive probability form on $X$, and let $\mathscr C$ be a cone of backgrounds in a specified finite-dimensional space $V$ as in \cref{sec:gauduchon-metric-geometry}, with the Hessian metric $-D^2\log\lambda_\mu$. Describe the metric completion of its unit-volume hypersurface $\mathscr S=\{\theta\in\mathscr C:\lambda_\mu(\theta)=1\}$. Which boundary points admit a current representative, and which estimates for the minimizing Gauduchon metrics distinguish boundary points at finite distance from those at infinite distance?
\end{problem}

On a compact quotient of a complex Lie group, with $\mu$ the normalized invariant probability, \cref{thm:parallelizable-unit-volume-completion} answers the question for every fixed linear space of invariant backgrounds containing a positive form: the unit-volume hypersurface is complete, and its Cauchy sequences are exactly those whose coefficient matrices converge to a positive matrix. After normalization, nonzero singular limits of the coefficients lie at infinite distance. These assertions depend on the invariant probability and do not classify arbitrary cones of backgrounds or reference densities.

On the Iwasawa manifold, \cref{thm:iwasawa-fiber-invariant-density} extends the same coefficient metric and completion to every smooth reference density that is invariant along the central elliptic fibers; these are exactly the probabilities that maximize the variational volume of each invariant positive background. The minimizing forms need not be invariant, but their coefficient means still satisfy the spectral distance bounds. A reference density that varies along the fibers gives smaller volume, and the completion of its Hessian metric is a separate question.

For the warped product cones of \cref{sec:warped-gauduchon-completion}, on the product of a compact K\"ahler manifold with a curve, \cref{thm:warped-unit-volume-completion} shows that the completion of the unit-volume hypersurface is a closed strip, a closed half-plane or the whole plane. Which ends lie at finite distance is decided by two ratios of real codimension to vanishing order, attached to the minimum set and the maximum set of the warping coefficient. Every end at finite distance is represented by a smooth semipositive, nef, nonbig form of volume one. The length estimates of \cref{lem:warped-endpoint-length} are bounds for the variance of the traces of the balanced minimizers; they use the product probability and extreme sets consisting of finitely many disjoint strata with power-type vanishing. Other densities and other geometries of the extreme sets remain to be studied.

The cone must be specified in \cref{qu:gaud-metric-boundary}, since allowing additional directions can shorten distances along paths. The finite-dimensional calculations above do not define a distance on the whole infinite-dimensional quotient of backgrounds modulo $\ddc$ of smooth functions.

\section{Weak Gauduchon adjoints}

Let $\theta$ be a background with a model reference $\varphi$ and $\mathrm{d}V$ a smooth positive volume form, as in \cref{chap:normalization}. For a bounded logarithmic density $F$, the normalized equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)=c_\varphi(F)\mathrm{e}^F\,\mathrm{d}V$ in the ordinary type of $\varphi$ determines a unique scalar, and $B_\varphi(F)=\log c_\varphi(F)$ is a concave functional of $F$ (\cref{sec:normalization-scalar}). For smooth data, differentiating the smooth equation produces a Gauduchon adjoint probability. At a continuous logarithmic density $F$, the supporting probabilities of $B_\varphi$, which form the set $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ of \eqref{eq:adjoint-probability-set}, provide a weak replacement. In the regimes of background and reference considered in \cref{chap:normalization}, $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ is nonempty, convex and weakly compact, and its members are absolutely continuous (\cref{thm:weak-adjoint-probabilities}). If this set consisted of a single probability, the scalar functional would be differentiable at $F$ even where no classical linearized equation is available.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:continuous-gaud-adjoint}\index{Adjoint measure!continuous-density conjecture}
Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on $X$ and $\mathrm{d}V$ a smooth positive volume form, and take the nonsingular reference $\varphi=0$. For every real $F\in C(X)$, the set $\mathcal A_0(F)$ of supporting probabilities of the normalization functional $B_0$ at $F$ consists of a single probability measure.
\end{conjecture}

Equivalently, by \cref{prop:normalization-directional}, the concave functional $B_0$ is G\^ateaux differentiable at every continuous logarithmic density. The conjecture holds at smooth $F$, by the linearized equation, and in the closed K\"ahler setting, where the normalization is explicit. By \cref{prop:weak-adjoint-stability}, it would imply that for any smooth approximations of $F$ the Gauduchon adjoint probabilities have a unique weak limit.

A further sufficient condition is a bounded solution with continuous, everywhere positive complex Hessian. Then \cref{thm:continuous-hessian-adjoint-uniqueness} identifies a unique stationary supporting probability and the G\^ateaux derivative. \Cref{prop:continuous-product-adjoint} gives its formula on $\mathbb P^1$ times any compact Hermitian factor for every positive continuous density of the stated product form; \cref{ex:non-dini-hopf-product-adjoint} includes a non-Dini density on a non-K\"ahler threefold. The criterion does not prove that an arbitrary continuous density has a solution with such a Hessian.

For bounded potentials there is a direct result. Suppose that $\omega+\ddc v$ is Gauduchon for every smooth $v$ with $\omega+\ddc v>0$; this holds for every Gauduchon form on a compact surface. Then, by \cref{prop:bounded-adjoint-persistence}, the normalized Monge--Amp\`ere measures of bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions extend the smooth adjoint probabilities and are continuous under convergence in capacity of uniformly bounded potentials. This defines no weak linearized operator, and it does not settle approximation by unbounded potentials in a general background.

A Baire category argument gives a partial result. In every regime of background and reference considered in \cref{chap:normalization}, \cref{thm:generic-adjoint-uniqueness} shows that $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ is a single probability for $F$ in a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $C(X)$, and that at such $F$ all choices of supporting probabilities along uniform approximations of $F$ have the same weak limit. The assertion is generic for the uniform topology and does not exclude exceptional continuous densities.

For $n\geq2$, the compact formulation with currents gives a more precise target. By \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints}, the supporting probabilities at $F$ are exactly the normalized determinant-weighted measures of the currents $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$ maximizing the functional $\mathfrak D_F$ of \eqref{eq:weak-gaud-functional}. The conjecture thus asks whether all maximizers have the same weighted measure. \Cref{cor:gaud-adjoint-lq} bounds the densities of these measures in $L^q$, for every $1<q<n/(n-1)$, uniformly as $F$ ranges over a bounded subset of $C(X)$; the endpoint $q=n/(n-1)$ is included when $v_-(\omega)>0$. For $n\geq3$, \cref{thm:gaud-local-adjoint-endpoint} gives uniform $L^\infty$ bounds for both the adjoint density and the determinant density for smooth logarithmic densities in a small $C^{2,\alpha}$ ball around the logarithmic density of $\omega^n$ with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$, even when the lower mass vanishes, and by \cref{cor:gaud-local-density-family} this ball contains distinct data with distinct exact maximizing currents. The controlled-Hessian family of \cref{thm:gaud-hessian-adjoint-endpoint} gives a larger sufficient condition: if the smooth solution forms are uniformly comparable to the background, both densities have uniform two-sided bounds depending only on that comparison and fixed geometry. The divergence of the cofactor uses only the fixed torsion, so derivatives of the varying Hessian do not enter the constants. \Cref{ex:gaud-oscillatory-controlled-hessians} gives such a smooth family whose densities have unbounded $C^1$ norms and no common modulus of continuity. Arbitrary bounded families of continuous data remain outside these hypotheses. These estimates alone give compactness but not uniqueness at continuous data. For a fixed smooth density the maximizing current is unique, smooth and strictly positive; nevertheless, \cref{prop:gaud-determinant-concentration} shows that a sequence approaching the maximum can have unbounded determinant moments above the critical exponent. Another approach to the conjecture would be to show directly that the upper and lower directional derivatives of $B_0$ agree.

More is known about the functional itself. Relative inequalities between positive residual forms give uniform convergence of the whole scalar functional, by the explicit bound \eqref{eq:geometric-normalization-comparison} of \cref{thm:geometric-normalization-comparison}. Under $L^1$ convergence of tests with a common $L^\infty$ bound, \cref{cor:geometric-adjoint-stability} shows that the supporting probabilities have uniformly integrable densities whose weak $L^1$ cluster limits lie in the limiting supporting set. The adjoint probabilities at smooth tests, together with the value of the functional at zero, determine the whole functional by \eqref{eq:adjoint-functional-reconstruction}. None of these statements decides the conjectured uniqueness at continuous tests.

For a singular reference or a degenerate background, the same uniqueness question makes sense under the hypotheses of \cref{chap:normalization}. A geometric interpretation of the supporting probabilities, however, requires a well-defined weak linearization. For a smooth background $\theta$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, write $\theta_u=\theta+\ddc u$, and let $\rho$ be a measurable weight. The expression $\ddc(\rho\theta_u^{n-1})$ is not a definition until multiplication of the product $\theta_u^{n-1}$ by $\rho$ has been justified.

An explicit divisorial model shows what can go wrong. For the admissible reference of \cref{lem:divisorial-adjoint-model}, the supporting set is a single probability and the residual linearization is well defined (\cref{prop:divisorial-residual-adjoint}). Nevertheless, \cref{thm:divisorial-smoothing-adjoints} gives decreasing smooth approximations, with positive forms, whose classical adjoint probabilities have different weak limits, each carrying mass one half on the divisor. These potentials converge in capacity and their logarithmic densities converge in every finite $L^p$, but their exponential densities concentrate and their smooth mass exceeds the relative mass. Within this fixed divisorial type, \cref{cor:divisorial-residual-recovery} proves recovery, with an explicit estimate, under convergence in capacity and a uniform bound on the residual potentials. On the non-Gauduchon product surface of \cref{subsec:singular-product-adjoints}, \cref{thm:singular-product-full-adjoint} constructs a weak linearization for a logarithmic solution with continuous positive residual and identifies its full supporting set as a singleton. Uniform convergence of the positive residual forms, with the divisor fixed, gives convergence of both the weighted currents and their probabilities in \cref{prop:singular-product-adjoint-approximation}; pole-filling smoothings still retain unwanted divisor mass (\cref{ex:singular-product-adjoint-boundary}). For general singular references, adjoints that do not depend on the approximation still require control of the singularity type, of the mass and of the conformal multiplier.

\section{Topologies on singularity types}

A topology on singularity types must keep information that weak convergence of currents can lose. Deficits of upper volume achieve this when bounded mass and positive lower volume provide uniform control, and on curves polar curvature gives a complete description even at zero volume. The problem is to extend such control to degenerations in higher dimension and to backgrounds with unbounded mass. Throughout this section, for a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, $P_\theta[u]$ is the ordinary envelope of \cref{def:singularity-envelope} and $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ the multiplier-ideal envelope of \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope}. An ordinary model is a potential $u$ with $P_\theta[u]=u$, an ideal model is one with $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=u$, and an analytic model is an ordinary model with analytic singularities. The lower and upper singularity volumes $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ are the infimum and the supremum of the non-pluripolar masses over the ordinary singularity type of $u$ (\cref{def:singularity-volumes}).

\subsection{Weak convergence and singularity data}

Weak convergence alone keeps little singularity information. The complete Gauduchon trace metric of \cref{cor:gaud-trace-metric} induces the $L^1$ topology on normalized potentials, and the ideal metric of \cref{prop:ideal-lone-metric} is the $L^1$ distance between the canonical ideal representatives $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. The completion of the ideal metric can acquire points that are not ideal envelopes of their own classes: on a curve, \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} approximates every normalized potential by ideal envelopes with finitely many logarithmic poles and zero non-pluripolar mass. In particular, such envelopes can converge to a smooth potential with strictly positive curvature. This convergence does not hold in capacity, since damped continuity would then preserve their zero measures.

A positive lower bound on the mass does not repair this. In \cref{prop:positive-mass-ideal-discontinuity}, analytic ideal envelopes of one fixed positive mass converge in the ideal metric to the smooth model $0$, whose mass is larger. Nor can the normalization functional recover the lost data: on the closed model domain of \cref{thm:normalization-mass-quotient}, it records exactly the mass. The examples with positive residual in \cref{ex:normalization-distance-projective,ex:nonclosed-normalization-nonseparation} have identical functionals and supporting sets but different poles, different multiplier ideals and different limits of normalized potentials, also for nonclosed backgrounds.

An order bound gives more control than weak convergence. Let $h$ be a Hermitian form, let $\epsilon_j\geq0$ tend to zero, and let $a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta+\epsilon_jh)$ satisfy the one-sided bound $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$ in the ideal order of \cref{sec:psh-singularity-types}, that is, $\mathcal I(ku)\subseteq\mathcal I(ka_j)$ for all $k>0$. Then \cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence} identifies the limit of the ideal envelopes from the convergence of divisorial Lelong numbers, and it shows that along such ordered analytic approximations, including dyadic regularization, the ordinary envelopes converge to $P_\theta[u]$ exactly when $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Inclusions of multiplier ideals at fixed weights, as in \cref{cor:fixed-weight-envelope-convergence}, give the required convergence of divisorial numbers. Without the order bound, $L^1$ convergence together with convergence of every divisorial number is not enough, by \cref{ex:divisorial-convergence-envelope-failure}. These tests make sense even when the upper volume is infinite. In contrast, in a Hermitian background analytic classes are dense for the ideal metric in the whole space (\cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density}), so density for that metric cannot characterize equality of the envelopes.

The finite damped measures of \cref{chap:damped-measures} control nonlinear limits throughout the space of potentials, including potentials of infinite mass. Since their weak continuity already follows from convergence in capacity, requiring it in addition imposes no further condition on multiplier ideals.

For a Hermitian form $\omega$, the uniform topology $\tau_{\omega,0}$ of \cref{sec:ordinary-type-topology} on ordinary singularity types combines both envelopes with controlled inclusions of multiplier ideals and the total variation of divisorial orders on each fixed compact modification. By \cref{thm:ordinary-topology-analytic-closure}, the closure and the sequential closure of the analytic classes are both the set of ordinary classes with $P_\omega[u]=P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$. This characterizes the analytic closure in every Hermitian background, including classes represented by potentials of infinite mass. The topology is not $T_0$ on ordinary classes. Transport by a smooth gauge change of the background is exact when the obstacle changes with the background, but with the zero obstacle kept fixed it is discontinuous already on curves, even within a cutoff of positive mass (\cref{prop:ordinary-topology-gauge-failure}).

The uniformity of \cref{subsec:all-obstacle-topology}, generated by the envelopes $P_{\omega,F}[u]$ and $P_{\omega,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$ below all smooth obstacles $F$ (\cref{def:singularity-envelope}), is invariant under smooth changes of background, and its analytic closure consists of the classes for which the two envelopes agree below every smooth obstacle (\cref{thm:all-obstacle-analytic-closure}). On curves this is the same analytic locus, but by \cref{prop:curve-all-obstacle-incomplete} the topology is incomplete and strictly weaker than polar total variation. In higher dimension, membership of $u$ in the relative envelope class $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I})$ suffices for equality below every obstacle (\cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection}), and under bounded mass and positive lower volume of the ideal envelope, equality at the zero obstacle is already sufficient (\cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization}).

Several geometric cases are known without the volume condition. For every admissible smooth background on a curve, \cref{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion} shows that equality at the zero obstacle, equality at all smooth obstacles and membership in the relative envelope class of the ideal envelope are equivalent. On products with separated backgrounds, \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections} computes both envelopes coordinatewise below separated smooth obstacles, also for curve backgrounds of zero area, and \cref{prop:separated-product-all-obstacle-criterion} identifies equality at the zero obstacle with equality at every smooth obstacle, including obstacles that mix the coordinates and factors of zero area. This equivalence persists after adding a nonclosed auxiliary coefficient and a unit divisor (\cref{cor:auxiliary-product-all-obstacle-criterion}), and for arbitrary compact complex auxiliary factors with a semipositive vertical term, without any mass bound on the ambient manifold (\cref{cor:general-auxiliary-all-obstacle-criterion}). For divisorial fibrations over curves, \cref{cor:fibration-component-cover} gives the same equivalence at zero model volume, even with poles along whole fibers or with disconnected fibers, by reducing the obstacles to the cover formed by the fiber components. In \cref{ex:zero-volume-unbounded-mass-fibration} the ambient background fails bounded mass, while the base potential belongs to the relative envelope class required for equality at all obstacles. Whether equality at the zero obstacle implies equality at all obstacles at zero lower volume in general, including backgrounds with unbounded mass, is unresolved.

\subsection{Volume comparison and analytic approximation}

In this subsection $X$ has bounded mass and $\theta$ is a smooth big background. For $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the comparison $d_\theta(u,v)$ of \cref{def:model-volume-comparison} is the sum of the deficits of the upper volumes of $u$ and $v$ relative to the upper volume of $\max(u,v)$, and $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ denotes the set of ordinary models with lower volume at least $\delta>0$. A common positive lower bound for the volumes converts a small deficit into a stretching estimate. The resulting quantitative $L^1$ bound, together with a modulus for three points that replaces the triangle inequality, gives separation and Cauchy completeness on $\mathcal M_\delta(\theta)$ (\cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness}); the cutoff must be closed, as \cref{ex:strict-volume-cutoff-incomplete} shows. The positive lower bound is also what prevents the comparison from identifying distinct ordinary models: \cref{ex:zero-volume-comparison-collapse} gives two distinct models at zero lower volume with comparison zero, in positive closed backgrounds in every dimension at least two and in positive nonclosed backgrounds in every dimension at least three. Rooftop continuity gives monotone lower bounds, which yield convergence in capacity (\cref{prop:volume-rooftop-continuity,thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}).

Analytic approximation detects exactly the ideal models. \Cref{thm:fixed-big-analytic-approximation} corrects analytic regularizations so that they stay in the fixed background; their ordinary models converge for $d_\theta$ to the ideal envelope, and their comparison with the original potential tends to the excess of the upper volume of its ideal envelope over its own upper volume. Conversely, at positive lower volume, \cref{thm:analytic-model-volume-criterion} shows that ideal models are closed under this convergence. Although $d_\theta$ fails the triangle inequality even on analytic models on a projective manifold, \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric} constructs a complete metric on the ordinary models of positive lower volume with the same convergent sequences; its Cauchy criterion also controls the reciprocals of the lower volumes, which excludes approach to zero volume. By \cref{cor:positive-ideal-metric-completion}, the ideal models form a closed complete subspace, which is the completion of the analytic models of positive volume.

Monotone limits show where positivity enters. For decreasing ordinary models in backgrounds that decrease uniformly to a smooth big form, \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} gives continuity of the lower volume even when the limiting volume is zero; if the limiting lower volume is positive, the upper volumes and the Lelong numbers on every fixed modification are continuous as well. For increasing sequences in a fixed background, \cref{thm:increasing-envelope-volume-continuity} gives continuity of the ordinary envelopes and of both volumes under the same positivity condition. For arbitrary decreasing potentials whose limit has positive lower volume, \cref{prop:decreasing-envelope-volume-criterion} shows that the ordinary envelopes converge exactly when the upper volumes do. Likewise, when the ideal envelope has positive lower volume, \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization} shows that the ordinary and ideal envelopes of a potential agree exactly when the potential and its ideal envelope have the same upper volume.

There is an explicit divisorial positivity test on $\mathbb P^2$. Let $\kappa$ be the normalized Fubini--Study form, prescribe distinct hyperplanes $H_j$ with summable coefficients $a_j>0$, and put $A=\sum_ja_j$. If $\ddc\theta=0$ and $\theta-A\kappa>0$, equality with the profile $\sum_ja_j\operatorname{ord}_E(\pi^*H_j)$ on every smooth compact modification specifies one ideal type. By \cref{countable-divisorial:positive-mass}, its ideal envelope is ordinarily equivalent to the logarithmic series, and every representative of this ordinary type has mass $\int_X(\theta-A\kappa)^2$. The nonclosed example of \cref{countable-divisorial:nonclosed-example} has infinitely many hyperplanes outside one pencil and positive lower volume. General positivity criteria from restricted ideals or divisorial orders, and the remaining zero-volume implications in \cref{qu:hermitian-i-characterization}, remain open.

The comparison is compatible with changes of background as long as these controls persist. A smooth gauge change, followed by ordinary projection, preserves it exactly (\cref{prop:model-comparison-gauge}). For a fixed potential and backgrounds decreasing to $\theta$, \cref{cor:ordinary-envelope-background-limit} gives continuity of the lower volume and, at positive lower volume, of the ordinary envelope. The uniform estimate \eqref{eq:vanishing-background-comparison} allows the curvature errors of analytic regularization to tend to zero, as in \cref{thm:analytic-approximation-curvature-error}. For perturbations of the background in both directions, a convex correction produces admissible transported models, with a uniform comparison whose limiting notion of convergence does not depend on the chosen strict reference; this gives the analytic criterion of \cref{thm:two-sided-model-transport,thm:two-sided-analytic-criterion}. None of these arguments covers loss of bigness or the boundary at zero volume in higher dimension.

\subsection{Polar curvature}

Let $Y$ be a compact connected Riemann surface with a smooth positive form $\theta$. For $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$, the polar curvature $\mu_u=\mathbf1_{\{u=-\infty\}}(\theta+\ddc u)$ determines the ordinary model of $u$, and its atomic part determines the ideal model. By \cref{thm:curve-model-volume-distance}, $d_\theta(u,v)$ is the total variation of $\mu_u-\mu_v$, also at zero non-pluripolar mass. By \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}, all normalized ordinary models form a complete geodesic space, and the ideal models form the closed subspace obtained by completing the analytic models of positive mass. On ideal models of positive mass this agrees with the topology of the source metric in \cref{thm:curve-atomic-source-metric,cor:curve-ideal-source-completeness}, for which this space is not totally bounded. Its completeness therefore has a different content from the compact completion obtained from weak convergence of traces.

Keeping only the atomic part of the polar curvature is a $1$-Lipschitz retraction onto the ideal models and selects the unique nearest ideal model; by \cref{prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect}, the distance to the analytic closure is exactly the mass of the diffuse part of the polar curvature. The model of zero mass in \cref{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-model} has trivial multiplier ideals but positive distance from that closure. Convergence in total variation implies convergence in $L^1$ and in capacity, even at zero mass (\cref{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence}), whereas the moving pole of \cref{ex:moving-pole-capacity-not-variation} shows that convergence in capacity does not imply convergence in total variation.

Degeneration of the background is also explicit on curves. Between smooth backgrounds of positive area, \cref{thm:curve-background-homothety} gives correspondences of the model spaces that rescale by the ratio of the areas and are compatible with ideal projection, also at models of zero mass. Under uniform convergence of the backgrounds, the corresponding normalized representatives vary uniformly in $L^1$ and in capacity (\cref{cor:curve-varying-background-continuity}). When the limiting area is zero, \cref{thm:curve-zero-area-collapse} shows that all normalized potentials converge to the unique limiting potential, uniformly over the admissible families, and the unscaled diameters of the model spaces tend to zero.

The joint space of \cref{thm:joint-curve-model-completeness} treats the smooth background as a further coordinate. It is complete for the combination of smooth convergence of backgrounds and total variation of polar curvature, its analytic closure is exactly the ideal locus, even at zero area, and on each fixed background it restricts to the curve topology described above. In higher dimension, the realization of compatible singularity data and a replacement for the comparison of finite upper volumes are unresolved.

Finite curve maps give another compatibility within the curve theory. For $r:C'\to C$ of degree $d$, a smooth base form $\beta$ of nonnegative area and $\sigma=r^*\beta$, \cref{finite-curve:envelope-pullback} commutes both envelopes with pullback below every pulled-back smooth obstacle. The polar metric scales by $d$, and \cref{finite-curve:model-retraction} gives a $1$-Lipschitz retraction with polar measure $d^{-1}r^*r_*\mu$, preserving polar mass and commuting with ideal projection. By \cref{finite-curve:original-base-subspace}, this describes the closed complete subspace of models that descend to the original base of a compact curve map, with its original-base normalization. The cubic example shows that this averaging need not select a nearest model. These conclusions do not supply a complete topology for general higher-rank backgrounds.

In dimension at least two the polar part of the first curvature current loses information. For $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, write $\operatorname{Pol}_1(u)=\mathbf1_{\{u=-\infty\}}(\omega+\ddc u)$, and $\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(u)$ for the corresponding current of $u\circ\pi$ on a modification $\pi$ of $X$. By \cref{prop:isolated-pole-polar-current-blindness}, distinct analytic ideal models with isolated logarithmic poles, whose multiplier ideals differ at suitable weights, all have $\operatorname{Pol}_1=0$. The blowup at the pole detects the coefficient on the exceptional divisor (\cref{cor:exceptional-polar-current-detects-pole}), but every fixed countable family of modifications misses some such models (\cref{cor:countable-modification-polar-blindness}).

Tracing polar curvature against Gauduchon forms gives a uniform normalization over modifications. Fix a family $\mathscr R$ of smooth modifications $\pi\colon Y_\pi\to X$ and Gauduchon forms $\eta_\pi$ on $Y_\pi$. The comparison $\delta^{\mathrm{pol}}_\omega$ of \eqref{eq:gauduchon-polar-comparison} is the supremum over $\pi\in\mathscr R$ of the total variation of the polar curvature of $u\circ\pi$ traced against $\eta_\pi^{n-1}$, normalized by the trace of $\pi^*\omega$. By \cref{prop:gauduchon-polar-comparison}, it separates analytic models and, on curves, recovers the complete curve topology up to a constant factor. Bounded equivalence preserves polar currents (\cref{prop:bounded-type-polar-invariance}), so transport by a smooth gauge change, followed by ordinary projection, preserves the comparison (\cref{cor:polar-model-gauge-transport}). Since polar curvature is closed for every pole set (\cref{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}), equality of the scalar traces recovers the full polar currents on each modification (\cref{cor:closed-pole-trace-recovery}). Arbitrary independent choices of the Gauduchon forms can nevertheless fail to induce the positive-volume topology, even for analytic ideal models on $\mathbb P^2$ (\cref{prop:gauduchon-choice-topology-obstruction}). The space of polar trace coordinates is complete (\cref{prop:polar-trace-coordinate-completeness}), and for sequences that are Cauchy for the comparison, \cref{prop:closed-pole-compatible-limits} gives compatible weak limits of the polar currents, all carried by the pole set of a single $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function. Whether these limiting currents are realized by an ordinary model is a further problem.

Even all modifications together do not make the first polar comparison separate models or give the right analytic closure. On every compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension at least two, \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness} constructs a nonzero ordinary model whose polar currents vanish on every modification, whose non-pluripolar volume is positive but strictly smaller than that of the zero model, and whose multiplier ideals are all trivial. More precisely, \cref{cor:diffuse-product-exact-volume} gives a complete ordered interval of such models with volumes $A-Mt^n$ and mutual distances $M|s^n-t^n|$, where $A=\int_X\omega^n$ and $M>0$ is a constant of the construction; for every $0<\delta<A$, the interval can be chosen inside $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$. Every first polar coordinate vanishes on this interval, and only its zero endpoint is ideal. Consequently every choice of Gauduchon forms identifies distinct models, and the closure of the analytic models for the polar comparison contains nonideal models.

The same diffuse cutoff can be placed in a Hermitian background. \Cref{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial} separates the loss $Mt^n$ in the mass of the potential from lower-degree terms coming from the background. If $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, the mass is the same for all bounded-equivalent representatives, and \cref{cor:diffuse-mass-invariant-models} extends the whole interval of models to this Hermitian background; in particular the obstruction occurs on every compact complex surface with a Gauduchon form. For an arbitrary Hermitian background, \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass} computes the mass of each representative from finitely many torsion coefficients and the same loss $Mt^n$. On a surface, \cref{cor:diffuse-surface-mass-invariance} shows that the mass is constant over the bounded-equivalence class exactly when $\omega$ is Gauduchon; otherwise even admissible smooth bounded perturbations change it, and the exact values would require optimizing these terms, which depend on the representative.

Under bounded mass and positive lower mass, \cref{prop:diffuse-two-mass-model-obstruction} still gives positive lower volume and a strict gap in upper volume for the diffuse ordinary models, while their first polar currents vanish on every modification. By the same proposition, this obstruction holds for every Hermitian metric on every compact complex surface. The theorem on preservation of residuals, \cref{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual}, distinguishes the diffuse parameters after ordinary projection. Consequently, in every background with both mass properties, \cref{cor:diffuse-hermitian-model-interval} gives a complete ordered interval of models throughout each range $0\leq t\leq t_0<1/C$, with $C$ the constant of \cref{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial}. Its upper-volume coordinate is strictly decreasing and Lipschitz, although the torsion terms prevent the explicit power formula from being inferred, and every first polar coordinate still vanishes. The same theorem preserves the entire residual measure, so its location matters: in \cref{prop:disjoint-diffuse-scalar-obstruction}, two models carry residual mass $r$ in disjoint regions, have zero first polar data, and are at distance at least $2r$; when $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, their two singularity volumes also coincide. A complete extension must therefore distinguish this spatial residual information.

Even the full residual measure on the base is not enough. When $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, \cref{prop:equal-residual-anisotropic-models} uses anisotropic maxima on one diffuse pole set to build a compact metric family of ordinary models indexed by positive weight vectors. Fixing the product of the weights gives complete subfamilies of real dimension $n-1$ whose members have the same residual measure, the same two singularity volumes, trivial multiplier ideals and zero first polar currents on every modification. The comparison distance nevertheless separates them, through the symmetric difference of the associated coordinate rectangles. A complete extension must therefore also detect singularity information lost by the full base residual. The proposition does not assert that the full residual products agree on every modification.

Return to a curve $Y$ with a smooth positive form $\theta$. On ideal models, the ordinary-type topology $\tau_{\theta,0}$ is the topology of total variation of the atomic data, and its canonical metric includes the boundary of zero mass (\cref{thm:curve-ordinary-type-topology}). On general ordinary models it is weaker than polar total variation: diffuse polar curvature can concentrate into an atom in the limit of the envelopes, which produces the Cauchy sequence without an ordinary-type limit in \cref{prop:curve-ordinary-topology-incomplete}. Characterizing the analytic closure on the whole ordinary space and extending the model topology to a complete one are therefore separate requirements.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\enlargethispage{2\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:singularity-topology-program}\index{Singularity type!topology problem}
For a smooth big background $\theta$ on a manifold with bounded mass, \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric} gives a complete metrizable topology on the ordinary models of positive lower volume, in which the closure of the analytic models is exactly the set of ideal models. Extend this topology to ordinary models beyond bounded mass and positive lower volume, keeping the closure of the analytic models exactly the set of ideal models. The extension should keep the compatibility with smooth gauge changes and with two-sided perturbations of the background established in \cref{sec:model-background-comparison,sec:two-sided-model-backgrounds}, and it should treat limits of backgrounds that lose bigness or a uniform positive lower bound for the volume. On curves it should recover the complete topology of polar total variation of \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness} on all models. Determine whether the extension in higher dimension is completely metrizable.
\end{problem}

Any such extension must keep the singularity information that weak limits lose. Potentials of infinite mass are already covered by $\tau_{\omega,0}$: for instance, the potential of \cref{prop:goodness-infinite-mass} has infinite mass, while its canonical envelopes, the ordinary and the ideal one, both equal zero. On curves, zero volume does not prevent completeness, but in higher dimension the stretching argument still needs a positive lower bound for the volume. There is nevertheless a restricted locus where a complete metric is known at zero volume. On the divisorial product locus of \cref{prop:divisorial-product-polar-metric}, polar curvature on the curve factor gives a complete metric whose analytic closure is exactly the set of ideal models; the top-degree volume comparison vanishes identically on this locus and therefore cannot produce this metric. The same restricted metric and analytic closure exist in a positive nonclosed background on a projective manifold (\cref{cor:nonclosed-product-polar-metric}), and the locus, the metric and the ideal projection persist when the second curve coefficient vanishes and the limiting background is not big (\cref{cor:degenerate-product-polar-metric}). \Cref{prop:separated-product-polar-metric} gives a complete sum metric, with the same properties of ideal projection and analytic closure, on the locus induced by several independent curve models, and \cref{cor:nonclosed-separated-product-metric} preserves this locus and metric through a nonclosed auxiliary coefficient and its nonbig limit at zero. When the curve coefficients vary, \cref{prop:weighted-product-polar-metric} gives compatible weighted metrics, discarding vanishing coordinates, with uniform $L^1$ collapse when all curve areas vanish, and \cref{prop:weighted-product-metric-comparison} compares these metrics quantitatively across different sets of active coordinates, with the exact distance for ordered coefficients. \Cref{prop:arbitrary-curve-product-metric,cor:arbitrary-product-zero-area-collapse} extend the construction to arbitrary smooth admissible curve backgrounds and identify the limiting singleton, and \cref{prop:general-auxiliary-product-metric} preserves the restricted complete metric through arbitrary compact complex auxiliary factors with semipositive vertical terms, independently of bounded mass on the ambient manifold.

\section{Trace realization and recovery}

Prescribing divisorial lower bounds and taking an envelope may produce larger orders than those prescribed. Let $u$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ and $Y\subset X$ a connected compact submanifold of positive dimension along which $u$ has zero generic Lelong number, $\nu(u,Y)=0$. As in \cref{chap:trace-data}, the trace data $\tau_{Y,u}(E)$ of \eqref{eq:trace-profile} are the asymptotic vanishing orders of the restrictions to $Y$ of the multiplier ideals $\mathcal I(tu)$, along prime divisors $E$ on smooth modifications $\pi\colon Z\to Y$. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\Omega$ on $Y$, the trace envelope $\mathcal T_\Omega(u;Y)$ of \eqref{eq:trace-profile-envelope} is the upper envelope of the nonpositive $\Omega$-plurisubharmonic functions $a$ with $\nu(a\circ\pi,E)\geq\tau_{Y,u}(E)$ for all such $E$. The realization problem asks when these inequalities are equalities. \Cref{thm:restricted-analytic-envelope-limit} constructs the envelope in the restricted background itself and identifies it as the $L^1$ limit of the envelopes of the restricted analytic approximants, and \cref{thm:trace-envelope-modification} shows that it pulls back exactly to every compact modification and is there the limit of the envelopes for vanishing Hermitian perturbations. When $Y$ has bounded mass and carries a Hermitian form of positive lower mass, \cref{cor:mass-bound-exact-trace} proves equality in all divisorial inequalities for sufficiently large backgrounds $\Omega$; when $Y$ is K\"ahler, equality also follows directly from the closed theory (\cref{thm:kahler-target-exact-trace}). Ambient potentials with analytic singularities have exact ordinary restrictions. In higher dimension, \cref{thm:curve-fibration-exact-trace} realizes all orders when the data reduce to a problem on an ambient curve, with an ordinary type independent of a sufficiently large background. For general data, \cref{thm:trace-profile-model-envelope} shows that in a larger Hermitian background the envelope has positive finite mass.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:exact-trace-program}
Let $u$ and $Y$ be as above, and suppose that $Y$ fails at least one of the two mass properties used in \cref{cor:mass-bound-exact-trace}: bounded mass, and the existence of a Hermitian form of positive lower mass. Can the Hermitian background $\Omega$ on $Y$ in \cref{thm:trace-profile-model-envelope} be chosen so that
\[
 \nu\bigl(\mathcal T_\Omega(u;Y)\circ\pi,E\bigr)
       =\tau_{Y,u}(E)
\]
for every prime divisor $E$ on every smooth modification $\pi$ of $Y$? If so, determine an ordinary or ideal singularity class of this realization that is compatible with changes of background, and prove its addition and approximation properties.
\end{problem}

The equality is required on all modifications, which is stronger than realizing the divisorial part of the data on one model. For that part, \cref{cor:divisorial-current-ordinary-type} gives a canonical ordinary type by combining a Gauduchon bound for the mass of the divisorial current with a smooth decomposition of currents; it does not determine the trace data of higher codimension, which appear on further modifications. The exceptional divisor of \cref{thm:trace-exceptional-difference} describes the discrepancy between the canonical types on two models, together with a cocycle relation and approximation in linear mass. By \cref{ex:exceptional-trace-with-exact-potential}, a nonzero exceptional term is compatible with an exact trace potential: the term records information absent from the divisorial part on the original model. Enlarging the background provides a competitor with controlled curvature, but it does not automatically exclude additional divisorial orders. In the proof for curve fibrations, every order on a further model reduces to a multiplicity in one variable. Under the two mass properties, \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} controls Lelong numbers through the deficit of upper volume and a positive lower volume. The missing step is to extend such control beyond these mass conditions.

Even exact realization does not control the mass of all representatives of the ideal class. In \cref{prop:exact-trace-infinite-representatives}, on a product with the Hopf threefold, the realizing potential has finite extended mass, while another potential with the same multiplier ideals and the same trace numbers has infinite mass. A proposed characterization by volumes or by continuity must specify which representative, or which class of masses, it uses.

\Cref{thm:hopf-projective-exact-trace} answers the realization question for the Hopf pair $(H_{n+1},H_n)$, $n\geq3$, when the ambient potential is pulled back from $\mathbb P^n$ and has zero generic Lelong number along the projective hyperplane. Every Hermitian metric on $H_n$ fails bounded mass, yet the same pulled-back potential realizes the trace data on all compact models. Within the invariant backgrounds $a\alpha+b\beta$, \cref{cor:hopf-trace-ideal-class,prop:hopf-trace-analytic-approximation} give an ideal class compatible with changes of background, addition, positive scaling and decreasing analytic approximation. Exactness persists in every larger Hermitian background. This settles the projective pullback case; general ambient data and background compatibility outside this family remain part of the problem.

\subsection{Recovery of compatible divisor systems}

Prescribing finitely many divisorial poles leaves a smooth residual background. When the residual has a smooth positive representative, \cref{thm:divisorial-residual-equation} solves the residual equation for a smooth strictly positive probability measure, and every subsolution at the same scalar whose Lelong numbers along the prescribed divisors are at least the prescribed coefficients differs from this solution by a constant. Consequently a solution in the prescribed ordinary type has the same scalar and a smooth remainder. Passing to compatible data on all modifications requires keeping this positivity and this exact singularity information uniformly.

Passing to all modifications raises two further issues. First, a smooth positive probability measure pulls back to a density that vanishes on the exceptional divisor, and the smooth dual formula cannot be applied to such a density until a degenerate extension has been proved. Second, the residual classes on two models are related explicitly: \cref{cor:affine-trace-exceptional-difference} subtracts the Bott--Chern class of the effective exceptional divisor while keeping the prescribed exterior derivative. The approximating coefficients converge in linear trace mass, but this formula gives no transformation rule for the nonlinear volume. Exact pullback preserves singularity volumes by \cref{prop:singularity-volumes-modification}; subtracting an exceptional divisorial class changes the background and is a different operation. Assigning an intersection number to a nonclosed form resolves neither the degenerate equation nor the question of volume.

The envelope systems of \cref{chap:hermitian-envelope-systems} are compatible families, over the positive Hermitian perturbations of a background, that record representatives of one multiplier-ideal class (\cref{def:hermitian-envelope-system}). Their construction gives an order adjunction and an exact criterion for recovery: the limit as the perturbation vanishes must keep the prescribed ideal type. Under bounded mass, for a big background, \cref{thm:positive-volume-system-recovery} gives a sufficient condition involving only the initial slices: their lower singularity volumes must have a positive limit as the perturbation vanishes. Concavity then gives positive lower volume at every finite parameter, and stretching preserves all local divisorial orders. The ruled-surface example of that chapter has zero residual volume and shows why recovery fails without such a condition.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:positive-system-recovery}
Let $X$ fail the bounded mass property, and let $\theta$ be a smooth big background on $X$. Find a condition on a compatible Hermitian envelope system over $\theta$ (\cref{def:hermitian-envelope-system}) that forces recovery of its ideal type at every finite parameter, and that reduces to the criterion of \cref{thm:positive-volume-system-recovery}, a positive limiting lower volume of the initial slices, when bounded mass holds. Specify the invariant and the uniform estimates that replace the finite deficit of upper volume, and describe their behavior under modifications.
\end{problem}

On the scalar Hopf threefold, \cref{thm:hopf-transverse-recovery} gives a sufficient condition despite unbounded ambient upper mass. If every positive scalar slice is invariant under the elliptic fiber action and the initial transverse limiting model has positive lower volume on $\mathbb P^2$, the system is recovered at every finite parameter and every positive Hermitian perturbation. The symmetry constructs the full transverse system rather than assuming survival of the limiting ideals. \Cref{ex:hopf-nonanalytic-recovery} supplies nonanalytic ideal data with exact positive transverse volumes. The recovery identity and transverse volumes survive modifications in the exact pulled backgrounds, and smooth gauge transport preserves recovery. A criterion for general nonsymmetric systems and arbitrary new Hermitian backgrounds on modifications remains unresolved.

For a fixed finite divisorial support, \cref{prop:finite-divisorial-equation-limits} settles the case where the residual stays in the positive cone: if the coefficients converge and the limiting residual background still has a smooth positive representative, the normalized smooth remainders converge smoothly, the singular solutions converge in capacity, their measures converge in total variation, and their logarithmic coefficients remain exact on every modification. Multiplier ideals can nevertheless jump at an integer coefficient. For divisors of sections moving in fixed finite-dimensional spaces, positive limiting weights and residuals give convergence of the full potentials (\cref{thm:moving-divisorial-potential-stability}), in each of the two regimes stated there. Recovery beyond the regime of bounded mass and positive volume, degeneration of residual positivity, and estimates uniform over infinitely many models all require further arguments.

\section{Sections, kernels and the dimension of growth}

A Hermitian form is the curvature of a holomorphic line bundle only if it is closed, so the algebraic part of the theory must keep the closed curvature data separate from the Hermitian background. The weighted spaces of sections of \cref{chap:sections}, their multiplicative variant and their intrinsic flag bodies do this, and their dimension of growth can be strictly smaller than the complex dimension of the manifold.

For the partial Bergman kernels of \cref{chap:partial-bergman}, the upper bound by the envelope is proved in general; a matching lower bound is known in the explicit regimes below, and the general problem is open. Let $L\to X$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth Hermitian metric of curvature $\theta$. If $X$ admits a modification from a compact K\"ahler manifold and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has positive non-pluripolar mass, \cref{thm:fujiki-partial-kernel-limit} proves convergence of the kernel measures normalized in full dimension. The proof keeps track of the relative canonical bundle, whose Jacobian factor corrects both the integrability condition of the ideals and the Hilbert norm. This geometric assumption implies the bounded mass property, although the birational transformation identities themselves do not require it. On a holomorphic submersion with descended data, the equilibrium theorem on the base gives a different exact limit on the total space. On the Hopf $n$-fold, the dimension of the selected space of sections of the $m$th tensor power grows like $m^{n-1}$, so normalizing by $m^n$ would erase the limit; adding the vertical Hermitian form recovers a nonzero measure, with the factor computed in \eqref{eq:hopf-partial-equilibrium}.

Isolated point poles that do not descend are treated in \cref{thm:isolated-pole-kernel-limit}: their projected jet conditions differ from the multiplier ideals on the base by only finitely many orders. For a single point pole on the Hopf manifold, \cref{thm:hopf-point-potential-limit} proves the lower asymptotics of the potentials as well as convergence of the kernel measures in total variation. Moving the pole along its fiber leaves every selected space unchanged, so these limits recover the projected equilibrium, while the multiplier-ideal type of the original Hermitian weight can be different.

\Cref{thm:horizontal-projective-kernels} gives a further regime: a logarithmic pole along a section over a hyperplane, with arbitrary compact connected fibers of positive dimension. The growth degree is the dimension of the base. The partial potentials attain the domination envelope of the original singularity on the total space, and the kernel measures converge in total variation for every smooth positive volume used in the norm. The limit on the base is determined by the metric of the line bundle, and its lift keeps exactly the conditional probabilities on the fibers.

For an interior rational Newton polytope with integral affine offset, \cref{thm:newton-potential-lower-bound,thm:newton-moment-shell-limit} prove a lower bound by the envelope and a positive limit of the measures in the relative growth dimension. Here the smooth weight and the sampling probability are invariant under the torus, the logarithmic weight is strictly convex, and the target is the specified envelope of Newton growth. In lower dimension the limit lies on a real moment shell and differs from every mixed measure formed from the curvature of the envelope and a smooth Hermitian form on the ambient manifold. These results do not identify an arbitrary multiplier-ideal envelope.

The multiplier-ideal family of \cref{thm:ideal-rounding-kernel-limits} has positive growth exponent and potentials converging to its ideal envelope, yet its kernel measures have two different limits: rounding at two critical divisors makes the leading term of the section count depend on the parity of the degree. Convergence of the normalized dimensions is therefore an additional necessary condition for a finite limit of the measures. This selecting weight has zero top non-pluripolar mass, so it lies outside the positive-mass regime above.

For the two isolated base points of a pencil, \cref{thm:pencil-ideal-envelope,thm:pencil-ideal-kernel-limits} identify the full ideal envelope and prove a global lower bound for the potentials and a positive limit of the measures. The selected spaces consist of three neighboring bands of monomials and have dimension exactly $3m+1$, although their class of competitors at all weights equals the upper ordinary type class. The specified compact logarithmic box and Gaussian norm give intrinsic mass three. Locally uniform convergence away from the poles, convergence in every finite $L^p$ and uniform convergence after exponentiation coexist with an unbounded additive error at each fixed degree. These spaces are not multiplicative, and this selecting weight also has zero top non-pluripolar mass.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:partial-kernel-lower-program}
Let $L\to X$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth Hermitian metric, and consider a series of selected spaces of sections of $L^m$ with weighted $L^2$ norms, as in \cref{sec:partial-selected-space}, together with its intrinsic growth dimension. Identify hypotheses on the series under which the partial Fubini--Study potentials attain the lower bound given by the envelope and the kernel measures, normalized in the growth dimension, converge. Relate the limiting measure to the Hermitian background while keeping track of the closed curvature and of the growth dimension of the series.
\end{problem}

Beyond the regimes treated above (isolated points, horizontal projective poles, descended data, interior Newton polytopes and the pencil example), the problem involves two tasks: producing enough sections with the required ideal constraints, and controlling the resulting measures. A Bergman upper bound gives neither. On a manifold that is not Moishezon, normalization by the ambient degree may already force every kernel measure to vanish, for reasons independent of singularity theory.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Locality of H\"older regularity, and regularity of weighted extremal functions under a uniform density condition in capacity, give special cases of the regularity problem; see \cref{sec:history-regularity}. These hypotheses do not cover every singular envelope, and they do not settle the problem of lower asymptotics above.

On a fibration, the comparison of spaces of finite codimension in \cref{lem:kernel-codimension-comparison} suggests a more specific question, which asks for an analytic description of the constraints on sections after descent.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:fiber-selected-approximation}
Let $\pi:X\to B$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion of connected compact complex manifolds with connected fibers, where $B$ is K\"ahler of dimension $q\geq1$. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$ and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, and a holomorphic line bundle $A$ on $B$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h$. Set $\theta=c_1(A,h)$. For positive integers $m$, identify
\[
 V_m=H^0\bigl(X,\pi^*A^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\varphi)\bigr)
          \subseteq H^0(B,A^m)
\]
by holomorphic descent. Find geometric or analytic conditions that produce $\psi\in\PSH(B,\theta)$ of positive non-pluripolar mass such that, for all sufficiently large $m$,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 W_m=H^0\bigl(B,A^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\psi)\bigr)\subseteq V_m,\\
 \dim V_m-\dim W_m=o(m^q).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Determine which features of the singularities of $\varphi$ along the fibers suffice for this approximation.
\end{problem}

For descended continuous weights and probability norms, such an approximation, combined with \cref{lem:kernel-codimension-comparison} and the equilibrium theorem on the base, would give the limits of the kernel measures and of the determinant functionals, although not by itself convergence of the potentials. For finitely many isolated poles, \cref{thm:isolated-pole-kernel-limit} gives the stronger error $O(m^{q-1})$ under its hypothesis on the projected poles. \Cref{thm:horizontal-center-descent} gives the same error for a logarithmic singularity along a section over a smooth divisor of the base, under the curvature margin stated there; its base reference is explicitly divisorial, and \cref{ex:horizontal-center-sharp-error} shows that the order of the error is sharp. The problem does not presuppose that every selecting weight in $\PSH(X,\omega)$ admits such a base reference.

The band criterion of \cref{thm:fiber-integrability-band}, which places $V_m$ between two spaces of sections vanishing along a divisor, requires a global logarithmic lower bound for $\varphi$, a positive residual on the base after a smooth gauge change, and a single chart over a smooth connected divisor in which $\varphi$ has an anisotropic upper bound. It constructs a base reference of positive mass and gives the error $O(m^{q-1})$ for every tensor power. \Cref{cor:exact-anisotropic-fiber-series} computes the selected series exactly, including pole sets of positive dimension inside the fibers, and \cref{ex:anisotropic-fiber-sharp-error} attains the order of the error. These are sufficient conditions; general selecting weights and necessary conditions remain to be understood.

For finitely many divisors with simple normal crossings, \cref{thm:crossing-fiber-integrability-band} gives the same error from a global lower bound by a sum of logarithms, one anisotropic witness for each divisor and a positive residual after subtracting all the divisorial terms. \Cref{ex:crossing-fiber-sharp-error} computes the exact series for simultaneous poles in the fibers and attains the order of the error. The normal-crossing hypothesis provides a separated integrability test on the base; general ideal constraints on the base remain outside these results.

\Cref{thm:modified-base-fiber-band} allows the divisorial data to live on a specified smooth modification of the base, provided the residual there is positive and the pulled-back selecting weight has the required lower bound and witnesses; the transformation rule for adjoint multiplier ideals keeps the Jacobian in the coefficient of the sections. \Cref{ex:modified-point-fiber-series} computes the resulting orders of point ideals on projective space and gives a sharp error for a base pole of positive mass in higher codimension. Which general selecting weights admit such modification data is open.

In all three band criteria, \cref{cor:semipositive-fiber-band} allows a smooth semipositive residual with positive top integral. \Cref{ex:exceptional-semipositive-fiber-series} lies outside the regime of strictly positive residuals: contracted exceptional lines obstruct every strictly positive smooth gauge, although the base reference has positive mass. There the exceptional fixed divisor makes the selected and reference spaces identical for every tensor power, which separates the local gap between orders from the global defect of sections; the positive volume of the reference remains part of the sufficient hypotheses.

\Cref{prop:exceptional-coefficient-series} computes this mechanism on the same family of blowups for every positive selecting coefficient, every integral fixed multiplicity of the exceptional divisor and any two anisotropic powers. A coefficient at most the fixed multiplicity leaves the whole space of sections. Above it, the selected spaces impose point jets of linearly growing order. As long as the coefficient stays below the fixed multiplicity plus the projective degree, the explicit reference has positive mass and the defect of sections is $O(m^{q-1})$. At that threshold the reference has zero mass and the sections grow with degree $q-1$; beyond it the series eventually vanishes. These exact model computations leave the general geometric criterion open.

\Cref{ex:two-point-semipositive-fiber-series} shows that a nonzero sharp defect also occurs outside the regime of strictly positive residuals. On the blowup of projective space at two points, the residual is the sum of the pullbacks of the Fubini--Study forms under the two resolved linear projections; it has degree zero on the line through the two centers but positive top mass $2^q-2$. Simultaneous interpolation of point jets gives the defect $2\binom{m+q-2}{q-1}$ for every tensor power. Thus a semipositive reference of positive volume does not force the local gap between orders to be absorbed by fixed divisors. Whether such conditions are necessary in general is open.

\subsection{Growing vanishing orders on curves}

Fix an integer $r\geq1$ and, for each positive integer $m$, a nonnegative integer $q_m$. The model of \cref{sec:degenerate-selected-kernels} on $\mathbb C$ has the smooth weight $|z|^{2r}$ and the selecting ideals $(z^{q_m})$. When $q_m\to\infty$, the kernel vanishes in the natural rescaled limit, but it has a Gaussian transition at the larger radius $(q_m/(rm))^{1/(2r)}$. For $q_m=\lfloor m\lambda\rfloor$ with a real $\lambda>0$, the limiting kernel measure misses the atom at the pole of the limiting envelope current. On a compact curve with the curvature and local expansion of \cref{thm:compact-growing-jets}, that theorem transfers the Gaussian transition to global sections when $q_m=o(m^{1/(2r+1)})$, and the construction with curvature boundaries in \cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets} extends the corrected transition to every sublinear order. We record the original question; it is answered by \cref{thm:optimal-growing-jet-range} together with the corrections from the weight in \cref{sec:growing-jet-corrections}.

\Needspace{27\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{prob:growing-jets-localization}
Let $C$ be a connected compact Riemann surface, let $x\in C$, and let $(L,h)$ be a smooth Hermitian holomorphic line bundle whose curvature is semipositive and strictly positive on $C\setminus\{x\}$. Fix an integer $r\geq1$. Suppose that in a local coordinate $z$ centered at $x$ and a holomorphic frame $e$ one has
\[
 |e|_h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\phi},\qquad
 \phi(z)=|z|^{2r}+O(|z|^{2r+1}).
\]
Let $\nu$ be a smooth positive volume form with local expression $a(z)\,\mathrm{d}A$ and $a(0)=1$, where $\mathrm{d}A$ is Euclidean area. For positive integers $m$, choose integers $q_m\to\infty$ with $q_m=o(m)$. Give
\[
 V_m=H^0(C,L^m\otimes\mathfrak m_x^{q_m})
\]
the norm $\|s\|^2=\int_C|s|_{h^m}^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu$, where $\mathfrak m_x$ is the ideal sheaf of $x$. Write $B_m=\sum_j|s_{m,j}|_{h^m}^2$ for an orthonormal basis $(s_{m,j})$ of $V_m$, with value zero when $V_m=0$. Determine the growth ranges of $q_m$ for which, for each fixed real $\tau$, every sequence of points $x_m$ in the coordinate neighborhood satisfying
\[
 m|z(x_m)|^{2r}=q_m/r+\tau\sqrt{q_m/r}
\]
obeys
\[
 \frac{\pi B_m(x_m)}{mr^2|z(x_m)|^{2r-2}}
 \longrightarrow
 \Phi(\tau):=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}
                         \mathrm{e}^{-s^2/2}\,\mathrm{d}s.
\]
When this transition law fails, determine how further terms of the local weight change its center and scale.
\end{problem}

For the data of this problem, \cref{thm:optimal-growing-jet-range} shows that the law holds in the optimal universal range $q_m=o(m^{1/(r+1)})$, uniformly in the direction of observation; outside that range, a compact example obtained by a holomorphic change of coordinate violates the uncorrected law along a subsequence. For a fixed weight, the exact criterion is \eqref{eq:fixed-weight-jet-criterion}.

In the whole range $q_m\to\infty$, $q_m=o(m)$, \cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets} centers the transition on the boundary of the curvature quadrature domain and uses the normal length scale $1/(2\sqrt{m\rho})$, where $\rho$ is the density of the curvature. The first effective Taylor coefficient and the ranges of the corrections of finite order are computed in \cref{sec:growing-jet-corrections}; a flat perturbation there shows that no fixed finite Taylor jet of the weight determines the center to within the width of the transition for every sublinear sequence. This resolves the problem on compact curves as stated.

In higher dimension, for ordinary point jets of growing total order on the explicit anisotropic product of \cref{sec:anisotropic-growing-jets}, \cref{thm:anisotropic-growing-jets} proves a Gaussian transition throughout the sublinear range of orders. The correct radial center includes the nonzero remainder terms of the weight, whereas \cref{prop:anisotropic-homogeneous-center} gives the sharp, smaller range in which the homogeneous center can be used in this class. \Cref{thm:anisotropic-fiber-selector} transfers these laws through a submersion with compact connected fibers, with a pulled-back metric and a matched smooth volume; there the isolated selecting weight varies with the tensor power. Mixed or angular remainders, observation near the coordinate faces, other bases and general transfers through fiber selectors remain open directions.

\section{Equations on singular spaces}

On a resolution of a singular space, a pulled-back Hermitian form degenerates, and the pulled-back density can vanish or become singular. Existence on the resolution must therefore be combined with an estimate that allows the solution to descend. \Cref{app:hermitian-spaces} develops potentials, envelopes, ideal equivalence and extended Monge--Amp\`ere measures on reduced irreducible compact unibranch spaces, keeping the original nonclosed background. On projective cones, \cref{thm:cone-logarithmic-equation,thm:cone-bounded-equation} solve explicit equations, for prescribed angular and radial data, in full ordinary logarithmic classes and in the bounded class of the closed background. The logarithmic construction includes nonclosed deformations that preserve the determinant, and its full solution descends even when the smooth residual is nonconstant on the exceptional fiber. The continuity results discussed in \cref{sec:history-regularity} use additional geometric and integrability hypotheses, notably log terminal singularities.

On the regular locus of a compact reduced irreducible Hermitian space of dimension at least two there is a conformal Gauduchon metric uniformly comparable to the original metric; see \cref{sec:history-regularity}. This existence theorem does not by itself give the nonlinear equations asked for below.

In dimension one, \cref{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation} gives a different sufficient condition: the normalization is a torus, the prescribed ordinary type has finitely many logarithmic poles of total weight smaller than the mass of the background, and the measure has a nonnegative $L^p$ density, $p>1$, with respect to the flat area on the normalization. Convolution with a Green function compensates for the degeneracy of the pulled-back background. The atoms at the poles determine the mass of every potential in the prescribed type and give uniqueness even among solutions that are a priori rough, and the residual descends continuously. The projective cusp of \cref{ex:unibranch-elliptic-cusp} has an unbounded density on the curve itself although the data on the normalization are smooth. Every background on a curve is closed, so this construction gives no equation with a nonclosed background in higher dimension.

The family of logarithmic densities in \cref{thm:unibranch-curve-density-endpoint} makes the integrability issue precise. Densities on the normalization with local form $C_0|z|^{-2}(\log(1/|z|))^{-\gamma}$ are integrable for $\gamma>1$, but they give a solution in the prescribed ordinary type exactly when $\gamma>2$. For $1<\gamma\leq2$, the constructed solution keeps all multiplier ideals and the same non-pluripolar mass, but loses the lower bound defining the type. Multiplier-ideal equivalence alone therefore does not give a bounded residual. A criterion for general integrable data remains to be found.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:singular-space-equations-program}
Consider reduced irreducible compact unibranch complex spaces with a Hermitian form, as in \cref{app:hermitian-spaces}. Beyond the projective cones of \cref{sec:projective-cone-equations}, determine geometric conditions on such spaces and conditions on the measure that give existence and uniqueness of solutions in specified singularity classes. Identify estimates ensuring finite mass, descent from resolutions and continuity, and describe their behavior under common refinements of resolutions.
\end{problem}

In the cone examples, the pulled-back density vanishes on the exceptional divisor, and the logarithmic remainder solves an equation in a strictly positive background; the bounded equation for the closed background uses instead an energy that is coercive off that divisor. More general spaces of higher dimension require a replacement for these two controls. The curve construction uses a Green operator on the normalization; a linear construction by traces on a singular space needs further estimates across the singular locus.
