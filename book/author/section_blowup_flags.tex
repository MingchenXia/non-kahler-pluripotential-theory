\section{Flags on a point blowup}
\label{sec:auxiliary-blowup-flag}

We now remove the assumption that $X$ carries a complete flag. A curve always does, since any point completes the flag. In dimension at least two, the exceptional divisor of the blowup of a point is a projective space and carries a complete flag of linear subspaces, while Hartogs extension identifies the sections on the blowup with those on $X$. The growth formula on the blowup therefore counts the original section spaces.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:point-blowup-section-descent}
Every connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n\geq1$ admits a smooth compact modification $\pi\colon\widetilde X\to X$ such that $\widetilde X$ carries a complete flag and, for every holomorphic line bundle $L$ on $X$ and every $m\geq0$, pullback is an isomorphism
\[
 H^0(X,L^m)\simeq H^0(\widetilde X,\pi^*L^m).
\]
These isomorphisms are compatible with products.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $n=1$, take $\pi$ to be the identity and any point as the last member of the flag; the remaining assertions are then immediate. If $n\geq2$, blow up a point $p\in X$. The local description of the blowup of $\mathbb C^n$ at the origin shows that $\widetilde X$ is smooth and that its exceptional divisor is $E\simeq\mathbb P^{n-1}$. A nested sequence of linear subspaces of $E$, together with $\widetilde X$, gives a flag
\[
 \widetilde X\supset E\supset\mathbb P^{n-2}
       \supset\cdots\supset\mathbb P^0.
\]
Each member is smooth, compact and connected, and is a prime divisor in the preceding member. The modification is an isomorphism off $E$.

Let $s$ be a section of $\pi^*L^m$. Off $E$, it gives a section of $L^m$ on $X\setminus\{p\}$. In a trivialization of $L^m$ on a coordinate ball centered at $p$, its coefficient is holomorphic on the punctured ball, and by Hartogs' extension theorem, valid since $n\geq2$, it extends holomorphically across $p$. The extension glues with the section away from $p$ to a section of $L^m$ on $X$. Its pullback agrees with $s$ off $E$, hence everywhere by the identity theorem. This proves surjectivity; injectivity and compatibility with products follow because pullback is an isomorphism on the dense open set $\widetilde X\setminus E$.
\end{proof}

Given a graded subalgebra $W_\bullet$ of the section ring of $L$, choose a complete flag on the modification of \cref{lem:point-blowup-section-descent} and apply the constructions of \cref{sec:graded-flag-body} on $\widetilde X$ to the pulled-back algebra $\widetilde W_m=\pi^*W_m\subseteq H^0(\widetilde X,\pi^*L^m)$. The shape of the resulting body may depend on the point and on the flag, but its dimension and its normalized count are determined by $W_\bullet$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:all-compact-section-count}
Assume that some positive-degree piece of $W_\bullet$ is nonzero. There are an integer $0\leq d\leq n$, a degree period $e\geq1$, and a number $V(W_\bullet)>0$ such that
\[
 \lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\dim W_{eq}}{(eq)^d}=V(W_\bullet),
 \qquad W_m=0\quad\hbox{if }e\nmid m.
\]
Here $d$ is the transcendence degree of the section-ratio field $K(W_\bullet)$, $e$ is the greatest common divisor of the degrees $m$ with $W_m\neq0$, and $V(W_\bullet)$ is the intrinsic lattice volume of every body constructed on such a modification. In particular, these three quantities do not depend on the choice of the modification or the flag.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pullback preserves the graded dimensions and the degrees with nonzero sections. Applying \cref{thm:gauduchon-linear-flag-bound,thm:intrinsic-flag-count} on $\widetilde X$ gives the displayed limit and the positivity of the lattice volume. Pullback also identifies the section-ratio fields, since their elements are the corresponding ratios and pullback is injective on the dense set where $\pi$ is an isomorphism. Thus \cref{prop:flag-dimension-section-field} identifies $d$ with the transcendence degree of the original field. The unchanged degrees with nonzero sections determine $e$, and the limit of the unchanged dimensions determines $V(W_\bullet)$. None of the three depends on the auxiliary choices.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:non-moishezon-section-vanishing}
For every holomorphic line bundle $L$ on a connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n$,
\[
 h^0(X,L^m)=O(m^n).
\]
If $X$ is not Moishezon, then, for each $L$, either all positive-degree section spaces of $L$ vanish, or there is an integer $d<n$ for which
\[
 h^0(X,L^m)=O(m^d).
\]
In particular $m^{-n}h^0(X,L^m)\to0$. These conclusions apply when $X$ fails the bounded mass property.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If all positive-degree section spaces vanish, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, apply the theorem to the full section ring. Since $W_m=0$ for $e\nmid m$, the limit gives $h^0(X,L^m)=O(m^d)$ for all degrees, with $d\leq n$. If $d=n$, the section-ratio field contains $n$ algebraically independent meromorphic functions on $X$, so $X$ is Moishezon. Thus $d<n$ when $X$ is not Moishezon. Finally, by Moishezon's theorem a Moishezon manifold admits a modification from a projective, hence K\"ahler, manifold, so it has the bounded mass property by \cref{lem:kahler-modification-bounded-mass}. The contrapositive gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Since the multiplier-ideal section spaces $\mathcal H_m(u)$ of \cref{sec:weighted-section-spaces} are contained in $H^0(X,L^m)$, the same upper bounds hold for them. Identifying weighted section spaces on a modification, however, requires a correction by the Jacobian; \cref{lem:adjoint-ideal-transform} gives the corresponding formula involving the relative canonical bundle.
