\section{Bounds for the Gauduchon potential}
\label{sec:stw-czero}

Taking the trace of the positive form $\Gamma_u$ gives a lower bound for a fixed linear elliptic operator applied to the potential, and its Green kernel then gives an $L^1$ bound. Near a minimum, supporting affine functions select a contact set on which the gradient is small and the real Hessian is bounded below; the determinant equation forces this set to have uniformly positive volume. Combining the two estimates bounds the minimum. This is an Alexandrov--Bakelman--Pucci type argument.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:stw-czero-reconstructed}
Every smooth solution $(u,b)$ of the equation in \cref{thm:stw-volume} with $\sup_Xu=0$ satisfies
\[
 |b|+\|u\|_{L^\infty(X)}\leq C.
\]
The constant depends only on $X$, $\gamma_0$, $\gamma$ and an upper bound for $\|F\|_\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first bound the scalar. At a maximum of $u$, the first derivatives of $u$ vanish and $\ddc u\leq0$, so $\Gamma_u\leq\gamma_0^{n-1}$; at a minimum the reverse inequality holds. The determinant is monotone with respect to the order of positive matrices, and $(\eta_u^n/\gamma^n)^{n-1}$ is the determinant of the coefficient matrix of $\Gamma_u$ relative to $\gamma$ (see \eqref{eq:stw-cofactor-matrix-scaling} below). Evaluating the equation at these two points gives
\[
 \inf_X\left(\log\frac{\gamma_0^n}{\gamma^n}-F\right)
 \leq b\leq
 \sup_X\left(\log\frac{\gamma_0^n}{\gamma^n}-F\right).
\]
This proves the scalar bound.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Consider the fixed real linear elliptic operator
\[
 Lu=\frac{\gamma\wedge\mathcal D_\gamma u}{\gamma^n}.
\]
Its principal part is $\ddc u\wedge\gamma^{n-1}/\gamma^n$, its remaining term is of first order, and it has no zeroth-order term. Since $\Gamma_u$ is positive, $\gamma\wedge\Gamma_u\geq0$, and therefore $Lu\geq-C_0$ for a fixed constant $C_0$.

By the Fredholm and maximum-principle argument in the proof of \cref{thm:gauduchon}, the operator $L$ has an adjoint probability measure $\nu$ with smooth strictly positive density, that is, $\int_XLv\,\mathrm{d}\nu=0$ for every smooth $v$. As for the Laplacian, $Lv\leq0$ at a maximum of $v$, and the normalized Green kernel of $L$ gives the representation
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 v(x)-\int_Xv\,\mathrm{d}\nu
       =-\int_X K(x,y)Lv(y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y),\\
 K\geq0,\qquad \sup_x\int_XK(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)<+\infty.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here the kernel is smooth off the diagonal and locally integrable. To obtain this representation, construct the inverse of $L$ on functions of zero $\nu$-mean by the Fredholm alternative. With the minus sign above, the local fundamental solution has a positive leading singularity, so compactness gives a lower bound for the kernel. Adding a constant to $K$ makes it nonnegative and does not change the formula, since $\int_XLv\,\mathrm{d}\nu=0$. The local estimates for the Green kernel give the uniform integral bound.

Apply the formula to $u$ and use $Lu\geq-C_0$. At a maximum point, where $u=0$, we obtain
\[
 -\int_Xu\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq C_1.
\]
Because $u\leq0$ and $\nu$ has a fixed smooth positive density, this is a uniform $L^1$ bound for $u$ with respect to any fixed smooth volume form.

Now let $M=\min_Xu$, and choose holomorphic coordinates on a ball centered at a minimum point. By compactness, the coordinate radius can be fixed, with uniform bounds for the metric coefficients and their derivatives; rescale the ball to the unit ball $B\subset\R^{2n}$. Write $x$ for the real coordinates, $D$ and $D^2$ for the Euclidean gradient and Hessian, $\mathrm{d}x$ for Euclidean volume, and $I$ for an identity matrix of the appropriate size. Fix a small number $\varepsilon>0$, depending only on those bounds, and set
\[
 v(x)=u(x)+\varepsilon|x|^2.
\]
For every real vector $p$ with $|p|<\varepsilon/2$, the function $v(x)-p\cdot x$ attains its minimum at an interior point, because its boundary values are larger than $M+\varepsilon/2$, whereas its value at the center is $M$. At such a point,
\[
 Dv=p,\qquad D^2v\geq0,\qquad
 v\leq M+\varepsilon/2.
\]
\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Let $A\subset B$ be the set of all points satisfying
$|Dv|\leq\varepsilon/2$, $D^2v\geq0$, and $v\leq M+\varepsilon/2$.
Since $u\geq M$, this set lies in $\{|x|\leq1/\sqrt2\}$. It is closed there, hence compact in $B$, and by the preceding paragraph its image under the gradient map $Dv$ contains the ball $B_{\varepsilon/2}$ of radius $\varepsilon/2$. The area formula for the smooth gradient map therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stw-contact-area}
 |B_{\varepsilon/2}|
       \leq\int_A\det D^2v\,\mathrm{d}x.
\end{equation}
The Hessian is nonnegative on $A$, so no absolute value is needed in the determinant.

We now bound this determinant pointwise on $A$. There,
\[
 |Du|\leq\frac52\varepsilon,
 \qquad D^2u\geq-2\varepsilon I.
\]
The complex Hessian of $u$ has the corresponding lower bound. Wedging with the fixed positive form $\gamma^{n-2}$ and bounding the first-order term of $\Gamma_u$ by the gradient estimate, we find that $\Gamma_u\geq\gamma_0^{n-1}-C\varepsilon\gamma^{n-1}$. Let $Q_u$ be the coefficient matrix of $\Gamma_u$ in a fixed local frame of $(n-1,0)$-forms. Since $\gamma_0^{n-1}$ is strictly positive, choosing $\varepsilon$ small gives
\[
 Q_u\geq cI
\]
with a uniform $c>0$. The equation and the bounds for $F+b$ already proved bound $\det Q_u$ from above, because the determinant of this matrix is, up to a fixed positive factor depending on the frame, the $(n-1)$st power of the density of $\eta_u^n$. All eigenvalues of $Q_u$ are therefore bounded above as well: their product is bounded, and each of them is at least $c$.

In particular, $\gamma\wedge\Gamma_u/\gamma^n$ is uniformly bounded on $A$. Subtracting its fixed background term and its bounded first-order term, we obtain an upper bound for
\[
 \frac{\ddc u\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}{\gamma^n}.
\]
This is a fixed positive multiple of the $\gamma$-trace of the complex Hessian of $u$, which differs from that of $v$ by a bounded term. The complex Hessian of $v$ is nonnegative on $A$, since the real Hessian is nonnegative there, so comparison of metrics bounds its Euclidean trace from above. The real trace of $D^2v$ is four times this complex trace. It follows that
\[
 0\leq\det D^2v
 \leq\left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}_{\R}D^2v}{2n}\right)^{2n}
 \leq C_2\quad\hbox{on }A.
\]
Inequality \eqref{eq:stw-contact-area} now gives $|A|\geq c_2>0$, where $c_2$ is independent of $u$. On $A$ we also have $u\leq v\leq M+\varepsilon/2$. Consequently the uniform $L^1$ bound gives
\[
 C_3\geq\int_A(-u)\,\mathrm{d}x
       \geq c_2(-M-\varepsilon/2).
\]
This bounds $-M$ and completes the proof.
\end{proof}
