\subsection{Growth ranges and compact examples}
\label{sec:optimal-growing-jet-ranges}

In the coordinate $z$, the quadrature boundary $\Gamma_t$ differs from the model circle $|z|=R$ by $O(R^2)$. Comparing this displacement with the transition width $R/\sqrt q$ gives the range of orders in which the uncorrected law holds for every metric. A holomorphic change of coordinate produces a compact example in which the displacement has exactly this size.

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:optimal-growing-jet-range}\index{Bergman kernel!optimal growing-jet range}
Keep the setting of \cref{sec:curvature-quadrature-jets}, fix the integer $r\geq1$ and a real $\tau$, and consider observation points with coordinates $z_m$ and the law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimal-growing-jet-law}
 \frac{\pi B_{m,q}(z_m)}{mr^2|z_m|^{2r-2}}
 \longrightarrow\Phi(\tau),\qquad
 m|z_m|^{2r}=q/r+\tau\sqrt{q/r}.
\end{equation}
where the second relation prescribes the radii of the observation points. If
$q=o(m^{1/(r+1)})$, this law holds for every choice of the geometric data and every sequence of arguments of $z_m$. This range is optimal: for every sequence $q\to\infty$ with $q=o(m)$ that is not $o(m^{1/(r+1)})$, there is a compact example satisfying the same hypotheses in which the law fails along a subsequence.

For fixed geometric data, write $\Gamma_t$ as $\{|z|=R c_R(\theta)\}$. Then \eqref{eq:optimal-growing-jet-law} holds for every fixed $\tau$ and every sequence of arguments if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-weight-jet-criterion}
 \sqrt q\,\|c_R-1\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:curvature-quadrature-domain}, $c_R-1=O(R)$ in every fixed norm required here. The observation points in \eqref{eq:optimal-growing-jet-law} satisfy
\[
 |z_m|/R=1+\tau/(2\sqrt{rq})+O(q^{-1}).
\]
Since the slope of the boundary graph tends to zero, the argument of $\Phi$ in \cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets} is $\tau+o(1)$ whenever $\sqrt q R\to0$, and this condition is equivalent to
$q=o(m^{1/(r+1)})$. Moreover, $a(z)=1+o(1)$ and
$\rho(z)=r^2|z|^{2r-2}(1+o(1))$ at these points, so the two normalizations agree. Thus \cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets} proves the law for all data in this range.

The same reasoning shows that \eqref{eq:fixed-weight-jet-criterion} is sufficient. For necessity, suppose that \eqref{eq:fixed-weight-jet-criterion} fails. On a subsequence, choose an angle realizing the maximal boundary displacement and observe at $|z|=R$ with $\tau=0$. A nonzero finite subsequential limit of the scaled displacement gives a nonzero Gaussian argument, and an infinite one gives saturation to zero or one. Radial and normal distances have relative error tending to zero, because the graph has vanishing slope. In either case the limit differs from $1/2$.

It remains to construct the compact examples. On $\mathbb P^1$ choose a radial metric on $\mathcal O(d)$ whose local potential $f(u)$, $u=|w|^2$, equals $u^r$ near zero, such that
$g(u)=uf'(u)$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\infty)$ and tends to $d$, and such that
$g(u)=d-\epsilon/u$ for large $u$, with a constant $\epsilon>0$.
Such a smooth interpolation exists for a sufficiently large integer
$d$: prescribe a positive function $g'$, equal to $r^2u^{r-1}$ near zero and to
$\epsilon/u^2$ near infinity, with total integral $d$.
Then $f=d\log u+\mathrm{constant}+\epsilon/u$ near infinity, so the potential in the frame at infinity is smooth with strictly positive curvature. Choose a smooth radial volume form equal to $\mathrm{d}A_w$ near zero.

For sufficiently small $t$, the hole $D_t$ lies in the region where $f(u)=u^r$ and equals the disk $|w|<R$: rotational invariance and uniqueness make it a disk, and its curvature mass is $rR^{2r}=t$. Hence the transition and saturation statements of \cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets} apply to this compact example.

Now use instead the coordinate $z$ defined by $w=z+cz^2$, with a constant $c>0$. In this coordinate the same compact metric satisfies
$\phi(z)=|z|^{2r}+O(|z|^{2r+1})$, the volume form still has $a(0)=1$, and vanishing orders are unchanged. At the uncorrected observation point $z=R$ with $\tau=0$,
\[
 2\sqrt{mr^2R^{2r-2}}\,(|w|-R)
 =2c\sqrt r\,\sqrt q R,
\]
and the normalizing factors computed in the two coordinates have ratio tending to one. If $q$ is not $o(m^{1/(r+1)})$, there is a subsequence on which $\sqrt q R$ tends to a positive finite number or to infinity. \Cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets}, together with its saturation statement when the argument diverges, then gives a limit strictly larger than $1/2$. This proves that the range is sharp.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Corrections from the smooth weight}
\label{sec:growing-jet-corrections}
\index{Bergman kernel!effective Taylor correction}

The boundary $\Gamma_t$ can be expanded in powers of $R$, and the leading terms of this expansion give explicit corrections to the uncorrected law. Write
\[
 \phi(z)=|z|^{2r}+P_{2r+1}(z,\bar z)+O(|z|^{2r+2}),\qquad
 P(\theta)=P_{2r+1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta},\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}),
\]
where $P_{2r+1}$ is a real homogeneous polynomial of degree $2r+1$ in $(z,\bar z)$. Let $\Lambda$ be the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator of the unit circle,
$\Lambda \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} n\theta}=|n|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} n\theta}$. The first boundary coefficient is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-boundary-coefficient}
 c_R=1+Rb_1+O(R^2),\qquad
 b_1=-\frac{((2r+1)-\Lambda)P}{4r^2}.
\end{equation}
To derive this, write the interior obstacle, to first order in $R$ and in polar coordinates $(s,\theta)$ for $\zeta$, as
$1+2r\log s+R H$ with $H$ harmonic. Matching values on the boundary
$s=1+Rb_1$ gives $H|_{s=1}=P$; the terms involving $b_1$ cancel there, since the radial model already matches first derivatives. Matching radial derivatives then gives $4r^2b_1+(2r+1)P-\Lambda P=0$.
If $P_{2r+1}$ is harmonic, then $\Lambda P=(2r+1)P$ and $b_1=0$, as it must be, since such a term can be removed by a change of frame.

For $q=O(m^{1/(r+1)})$, the argument of $\Phi$ at the uncorrected observation point of \eqref{eq:optimal-growing-jet-law} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-growing-jet-shift}
 \tau+\frac{\sqrt q R}{2r^{3/2}}((2r+1)-\Lambda)P(\theta)+o(1),
\end{equation}
uniformly in $\theta$, and the normalized kernel differs from $\Phi$ of this argument by $o(1)$. The remainder is small because $\sqrt q R^2\to0$ in this range. For $w=z+cz^2$ we have $P=2r\operatorname{Re}(c\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta})$, and the shift is
$2\sqrt{rq}R\operatorname{Re}(c\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta})$, in agreement with the compact example above.

For every fixed integer $J\geq1$, the parameter construction of \cref{lem:curvature-quadrature-domain} gives
\[
 c_R=1+\sum_{j=1}^J R^j b_j+O(R^{J+1})
\]
in every fixed norm needed. The coefficients satisfy an explicit recursion, obtained by expanding the interior harmonic obstacle in powers of $R$. At order $j$, matching values determines the boundary data of the new harmonic term from the Taylor term of degree $2r+j$ of $\phi$ and all preceding coefficients, and harmonic extension determines the term itself. Matching derivatives then gives an equation $4r^2b_j+\text{(known terms)}=0$; the new coefficient $b_j$ does not enter the value equation of order $j$. For $j\geq2$, nonlinear contributions of lower-order terms also appear.

Replacing $\Gamma_t$ by the curve given by the truncated expansion moves the center of the transition by $O(R^{J+2})$. This curve can therefore be used in place of $\Gamma_t$ whenever
\[
 \sqrt q R^{J+1}\to0,
 \quad\hbox{equivalently}\quad
 q=o(m^{(J+1)/(r+J+1)}).
\]
If $b_1=\cdots=b_{j-1}=0$ but $b_j\not\equiv0$, the criterion
\eqref{eq:fixed-weight-jet-criterion} shows that, for this fixed weight, the uncorrected law holds exactly in the range
$q=o(m^{j/(r+j)})$, and this range is sharp when all directions of observation are allowed. Indeed, the higher terms are relatively $O(R)$ at an angle where $b_j\ne0$.

In the radial case $\phi=f(u)$ with $u=|z|^2$, the hole is a disk by uniqueness and rotational invariance. By \eqref{eq:curvature-quadrature-identity}, its boundary circle $\{u=u_*\}$ is determined by
\[
 m u_*f'(u_*)=q.
\]
With $\rho(u)=f'(u)+uf''(u)$, the asymptotic variance of the dominant monomial degree is $m u\rho(u)$, and the corresponding Gaussian argument is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-growing-jet-argument}
 \frac{m u f'(u)-q}{\sqrt{m u\rho(u)}}.
\end{equation}
For bounded arguments, Taylor expansion at $u_*$ identifies it with
$2\sqrt{m\rho(u_*)}(\sqrt u-\sqrt{u_*})+o(1)$. Hence
\cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets} proves this radial formula for all sublinear orders. For $f=u^r+c u^{r+1}+\cdots$, the first shift at the uncorrected observation point is
$c(r+1)r^{-3/2}\sqrt q R^2$, so the critical growth is $m^{2/(r+2)}$.
This larger radial threshold does not apply in arbitrary coordinates.

No fixed finite Taylor jet of the weight determines the correction for every
$q=o(m)$. For example, near zero take
\[
 f(u)=u^r+c u^r \mathrm{e}^{-1/\sqrt u},\qquad c>0,
\]
with the second term extended by zero at $u=0$, and interpolated globally as above.
It has the same Taylor jet as $u^r$ at zero to every finite order, but at
$u=R^2$ its relative contribution to $uf'(u)$ is
$c \mathrm{e}^{-1/R}(1+1/(2rR))$. For $q=\lfloor m/\log m\rfloor$, we have
$\sqrt q \mathrm{e}^{-1/R}/R\to\infty$. Hence the uncorrected observation point with $\tau=0$ lies outside the true boundary circle by many transition widths, and the normalized kernel tends to one. The quadrature-domain correction includes this flat contribution.
