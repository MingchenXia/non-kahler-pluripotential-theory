\subsubsection{Diffuse product poles}
\label{subsec:diffuse-product-polar-obstruction}

Even polar currents on all modifications, with any choice of Gauduchon forms, miss some ordinary models. A maximum of diffuse polar potentials on the factors of a polydisk has a joint pole set of Hausdorff dimension zero, so all its first polar currents vanish. Nevertheless, its bounded truncations keep a fixed positive amount of top-degree mass near that set. The non-pluripolar product loses this mass, so the volume of the model drops, although the polar comparison does not see any pole.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}
Let $\Delta\subset\C$ be the unit disk. There are a compact set $K\Subset\Delta$ of Hausdorff dimension zero and a negative subharmonic function $g$ on $\Delta$, smooth and harmonic off $K$, with
\[
 \{g=-\infty\}=K,\qquad \nu(g,a)=0\quad(a\in\Delta),
 \qquad g|_{\partial\Delta}=0.
\]
Put $D=\Delta^n$, $S=K^n$ and
\[
 h(z)=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}g(z_j),\qquad
 h_L=\max\{h,-L\}\quad(L>0).
\]
The function $h$ is locally Lipschitz off $S$, and its bounded local Monge--Amp\`ere measure $(\ddc h)^n$ vanishes there. The measures $(\ddc h_L)^n$ all have the same finite mass $M$. Their supports are compact and converge to $S$ as $L\to\infty$, in the sense that they lie in any given neighborhood of $S$ for all large $L$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the binary Cantor set and the nonatomic probability measure $\sigma$ of \cref{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-model}, placed in a small real interval in $\Delta$. Its intervals of level $k$ have length $r_k=\exp(-4^{k+1})$ and mass $2^{-k}$. For every $s>0$,
\[
 2^kr_k^s\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 2^{nk}(\sqrt n r_k)^s\longrightarrow0.
\]
Covering $K$ by its $2^k$ intervals of level $k$, and $S$ by the $2^{nk}$ products of such intervals, which have diameter at most $\sqrt nr_k$, shows that $K$ and $S$ have Hausdorff dimension zero. Define the Green potential of the disk
\[
 g(z)=\int_\Delta
       \log\left|\frac{z-y}{1-\bar yz}\right|^2\,\mathrm d\sigma(y).
\]
Then $\ddc g=\sigma$, with the normalization of $\ddc$ fixed in the conventions. The Green kernel is nonpositive, vanishes on the boundary and is smooth away from its pole. On a fixed smaller disk it is at most $\log|z-y|^2+C$. For $z\in K$, integration over the interval of level $k$ containing $z$, together with nonpositivity of the kernel elsewhere, gives
\[
 g(z)\leq2^{-k}(2\log r_k+C)\longrightarrow-\infty.
\]
Outside $K$ the potential is finite, smooth and harmonic. Its Lelong number at $a$ is $\sigma(\{a\})=0$. Since $K$ stays away from the boundary, $g$ is smooth near the boundary and tends uniformly to zero there.

Near a point $z^0\notin S$, some coordinate $z^0_j$ lies outside $K$. Since $g$ is upper semicontinuous and equal to $-\infty$ on $K$, every branch $g(z_i)$ with $z^0_i\in K$ lies below the finite branch $g(z_j)$ near $z^0$. Hence $h$ is locally the maximum of the at most $n$ pluriharmonic branches $g(z_i)$ with $z^0_i\notin K$, and in particular it is locally Lipschitz. For $m\leq n$ pluriharmonic functions $a_j$, use the smooth approximations
\[
 \varepsilon\log\sum_{j=1}^m e^{a_j/\varepsilon},
\]
which decrease to $\max_ja_j$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Their complex Hessian is a positive multiple of the weighted covariance of the forms $\partial a_j$, with probability weights proportional to $e^{a_j/\varepsilon}$. The centered forms satisfy one weighted linear relation, so this Hessian has rank at most $m-1<n$, and its $n$th power vanishes. Bounded decreasing convergence in \cref{thm:bt-input} gives the same conclusion for the maximum.

The bounded truncation $h_L$ is continuous, since near a pole of any curve potential the corresponding branch lies below the constant $-L$. The same argument, applied to the $n+1$ branches $g(z_1),\ldots,g(z_n),-L$, shows that $(\ddc h_L)^n$ is carried by
\[
 Q_L=\{z:g(z_j)=-L\text{ for every }j\}.
\]
Indeed, off $Q_L$ one branch lies strictly below the maximum. Discarding it, together with any branches near poles, leaves at most $n$ pluriharmonic branches locally, so the measure vanishes there. Since $Q_L$ is compact, the Chern--Levine--Nirenberg estimate \eqref{eq:local-cln-range} gives finite mass.

For $L,L'>0$, the functions $h_L$ and $h_{L'}$ agree near $\partial D$, since $h$ tends uniformly to zero there. Integration by parts for bounded potentials gives
\[
 (\ddc h_L)^n-(\ddc h_{L'})^n
       =\ddc\left((h_L-h_{L'})
         \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\ddc h_L)^j
                         \wedge(\ddc h_{L'})^{n-1-j}\right).
\]
The current on the right has compact support, so its integral is zero.
Finally, $g$ is bounded on compact subsets of $\overline\Delta\setminus K$. Its sublevel sets $\{g\leq-L\}$ therefore converge to $K$ as $L\to\infty$, and $Q_L$ converges to $S$.
\end{proof}

For a separable maximum, the full top-degree product of \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products} can be computed exactly. Its restriction to the pole set, the residual measure of \cref{subsec:small-pole-local-products}, depends only on the product of the weights of the branches, so different positive weights can give the same residual measure and different singularity types.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:anisotropic-diffuse-product}\index{Monge--Ampere measure@Monge--Amp\`ere measure!separable maximum}
Use the Green potential $g$, the probability measure $\sigma$ and the sets $D=\Delta^n$, $S=K^n$ of \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}. For $s,L>0$ and $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in(0,+\infty)^n$, put
\[
 \mu_s=\ddc\max(g,-s),\qquad h_a(z)=\max_j a_jg(z_j),
\]
and $h_{a,L}=\max(h_a,-L)$. Each $\mu_s$ is a nonatomic probability measure supported on $\{g=-s\}$, and $\mu_s\to\sigma$ weakly as $s\to\infty$. The bounded products satisfy
\[
 (\ddc h_{a,L})^n
   =\left(\prod_{j=1}^n a_j\right)
           \mu_{L/a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_{L/a_n}.
\]
The function $h_a$ has pole set $S$, is locally Lipschitz off $S$, and has zero bounded top-degree product there. Its full product in the sense of \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products} is
\[
 (\ddc h_a)^n_{\mathrm{full}}
       =\left(\prod_{j=1}^n a_j\right)\sigma^{\otimes n}.
\]
In particular, the common mass $M$ in \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} is $1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The truncation $\max(g,-s)$ agrees with $g$ near the boundary of $\Delta$. Their difference is a compactly supported integrable function, so its complex Hessian has integral zero, and $\mu_s(\Delta)=\sigma(\Delta)=1$. The truncation is constant near $K$ and harmonic off $K$ wherever $g\ne-s$, which proves the support assertion. Since the truncation is bounded, $\mu_s$ has no point masses, by \cref{thm:bt-input}. Decreasing local $L^1$ convergence to $g$ gives $\mu_s\to\ddc g=\sigma$; for large $s$, the supports lie in one fixed compact subset of $\Delta$.

The rank argument of \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} applies with the weights $a_j$. Away from $S$, the finite branches dominate the branches near poles, so $h_a$ is locally a Lipschitz maximum of at most $n$ pluriharmonic functions, with zero top-degree product. For the bounded maximum with the constant branch $-L$, the same argument shows that its top-degree product is supported on
\[
 Q_{a,L}=\{z:a_jg(z_j)=-L\text{ for every }j\}.
\]
Indeed, off this set one branch lies strictly below the maximum, leaving at most $n$ active pluriharmonic branches; this also applies near branches with poles, which lie below the constant branch.

Near a point of $Q_{a,L}$ at which all the differentials $\mathrm dg(z_j)$ are nonzero, set $x_j=a_jg(z_j)+L$. Each $x_j$ is the real part of a local holomorphic coordinate in its own factor. For $\varepsilon>0$, smooth the maximum locally by
\[
 f_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon\log\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^n e^{x_j/\varepsilon}\right),
\]
with weights
\[
 w_0=\frac1{1+\sum_j e^{x_j/\varepsilon}},\qquad
 w_j=e^{x_j/\varepsilon}w_0.
\]
Then $-L+f_\varepsilon$ decreases to $h_{a,L}$. The Hessian matrix of $f_\varepsilon$ in the variables $x_j$ is $\varepsilon^{-1}(\operatorname{diag}(w_j)-(w_jw_k)_{j,k})$, whose determinant is $\varepsilon^{-n}w_0\prod_jw_j$. Hence
\[
 (\ddc f_\varepsilon)^n
   =n!\varepsilon^{-n}w_0\prod_{j=1}^n w_j
           \bigwedge_{j=1}^n(\mathrm dx_j\wedge\dc x_j).
\]
The scalar kernel on $\mathbb R^n$ is an approximate identity of mass $1$. To check the constant, write $y=x/\varepsilon$. The map
\[
 y\longmapsto\left(\frac{e^{y_j}}{1+\sum_k e^{y_k}}\right)_{j=1}^n
\]
is a diffeomorphism onto the open simplex $\{w_j>0,\ \sum_jw_j<1\}$, with Jacobian $w_0\prod_jw_j$. The simplex has volume $1/n!$, so the kernel above has integral $1$. Integrability of the fixed kernel in $y$ gives concentration at $x=0$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

Bounded decreasing convergence of products therefore identifies the local limit with the tensor product of the one-variable level measures. In the $j$th factor, the chain rule for $\max(x_j,0)$ gives
\[
 \delta_0(x_j)\,\mathrm dx_j\wedge\dc x_j
      =a_j\ddc\max(g,-L/a_j),
\]
where the Dirac notation denotes the level measure in the real normal coordinate $x_j$. The critical points of the nonconstant harmonic function $g$ off $K$ are isolated. In each coordinate neighborhood, their inverse images are analytic hypersurfaces, hence pluripolar. The bounded top-degree product gives them zero mass by \cref{thm:bt-input}, and so does the candidate tensor measure, because each $\mu_{L/a_j}$ has no point masses. Thus the formula extends across these points and, together with the support assertion, holds on all of $D$.

Tensor products of the curve probabilities converge to $\sigma^{\otimes n}$: this follows first for products of continuous functions of one variable, and then for all continuous test functions by uniform approximation on a common compact product. Finally, $h_{a,L}\downarrow h_a$, whose unbounded locus is the closed set $S$ of Hausdorff dimension zero. By \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products}, the weak limit is the full top-degree product of $h_a$. Taking $a_j=1$ proves $M=1$.
\end{proof}

From here on, $n\geq2$ and $\omega$ is a Hermitian form on $X$. Place the polydisk $D$ of \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} in a holomorphic coordinate chart of $X$, choose a smooth function $0\leq\chi\leq1$ with compact support in $D$ and equal to one on a neighborhood of $S$, and extend $H=\chi h$ by zero. Thus $H\leq0$, $\sup_XH=0$, $\{H=-\infty\}=S$, and $H$ is locally bounded off $S$. Neither the first polar currents nor the multiplier ideals of a potential bounded below by a multiple of $H$ detect its poles.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}
There is $C>0$ with $\ddc H\geq-C\omega$; thus $\omega+\ddc(tH)\geq(1-Ct)\omega$ for $0<t<1/C$. If $p\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ satisfies $p\geq sH-c$ for constants $s>0$ and $c$, then $\nu(p,x)=0$ for every $x\in X$, $\mathcal I(\lambda p)=\mathcal O_X$ for every $\lambda>0$, and $\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(p)=0$ for every smooth modification $\pi\colon Y\to X$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}, $h$ is locally Lipschitz off $S$. In the distributional product rule
\[
 \ddc(\chi h)=\chi\ddc h
       +h\ddc\chi+\mathrm d\chi\wedge\dc h
       +\mathrm dh\wedge\dc\chi,
\]
the first term is positive, and the other terms have bounded coefficients, since they are supported on the compact set $\operatorname{supp}\mathrm d\chi$, which does not meet $S$. This gives $C$.

Let $p\geq sH-c$. Off $S$, the function $p$ is locally bounded below, so its Lelong numbers vanish there. Near a point $a\in S$, where $\chi=1$, we have $p\geq s\,g(z_1)-c$, and the function $g(z_1)$ has Lelong number $\nu(g,a_1)=0$ at $a$; hence $\nu(p,a)=0$. Skoda's local integrability theorem \cite[Lemma~5.6]{DemAM11} then makes $\mathrm e^{-\lambda p}$ locally integrable for every $\lambda>0$.

Fix $\pi$, let $Z\subset X$ be a proper analytic subset over whose complement $\pi$ is an isomorphism, and let $E_1,\ldots,E_N$ be the irreducible components of dimension $n-1$ of $\pi^{-1}(Z)$. The current $R=\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(p)$ is closed and positive by \cref{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}. Off $\pi^{-1}(Z)$, it is carried by the inverse image of $S$, which has Hausdorff dimension zero in each relatively compact chart; by \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}, $R$ vanishes there. The support theorem for closed positive currents \cite[Chapter III, Corollaries 2.11 and 2.14, pp.~141--143]{DemCADG} therefore gives
\[
 R=\sum_i b_i[E_i],\qquad b_i\geq0,
\]
since analytic sets of dimension less than $n-1$ carry no closed $(1,1)$-current of order zero. Since $R\leq\pi^*\omega+\ddc(p\circ\pi)$, restriction to $E_i$ and the proof of \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} give $b_i\leq\nu(p\circ\pi,E_i)$.

If $\pi(E_i)\not\subset S$, then $p\circ\pi$ is bounded below near some point of $E_i$, so $\nu(p\circ\pi,E_i)=0$. Otherwise $\pi(E_i)$ is an irreducible analytic set contained in the set $S$ of Hausdorff dimension zero, hence a point $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$. At a general point of $E_i$, with a local equation $\zeta$ of $E_i$, the first coordinate satisfies
\[
 z_1\circ\pi-a_1=\zeta^m f,\qquad m\geq1,\quad f\ne0.
\]
A local holomorphic $m$th root of the unit $f$ turns this expression into $\zeta'^m$ for another local equation $\zeta'$ of $E_i$. Taking circular means gives
\[
 \nu(g(a_1+\zeta'^m),E_i)=m\nu(g,a_1)=0.
\]
Since $p\circ\pi\geq s\,g(z_1\circ\pi)-c$ there, again $\nu(p\circ\pi,E_i)=0$. Thus $R=0$.
\end{proof}

In a nonclosed background, the mass of a potential also involves the terms of the mass expansion \eqref{eq:mass-expansion} that contain derivatives of the background, and these terms can vary within an ordinary type. Bounded truncations separate them from the loss of top-degree mass on $S$, which depends only on the ordinary type.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:diffuse-representative-mass}\label{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!bounded-equivalent diffuse representatives}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!diffuse cutoff polynomial}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be locally bounded off $S$, with $\{u=-\infty\}=S$, and put $A=\int_X\omega^n$. For $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $v\sim u$ and $k>0$, put $v_k=\max(v,-k)$. As $k\to\infty$, the measures $(\omega+\ddc v_k)^n$ converge weakly to a finite positive measure $\Phi_v$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diffuse-residual-local}
 \mathbf1_S\Phi_v=\mathbf1_S(\ddc(u+\psi))^n_{\mathrm{full}}
\end{equation}
on every coordinate neighborhood on which $u+\psi$ is plurisubharmonic for a smooth function $\psi$. The limits
\[
 c_j(v)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_X
     v_k(\ddc v_k)^{j-1}\wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-j}),
       \qquad 1\leq j<n,
\]
exist and are finite, and
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)
       =A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_j(v)-\Phi_v(S).
\]
In particular, $v_-(\omega)-\Phi_v(S)\leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)\leq v_+(\omega)-\Phi_v(S)$, with the lower and upper masses of \cref{def:bmp}; if $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, then $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)=A-\Phi_v(S)$; and if $n=2$, then $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)=A+2\int_Xv\ddc\omega-\Phi_v(S)$.

For $0<t<1/C$ and $u=tH$, one has $\Phi_v(S)=Mt^n$ and $c_j(tH)=c_jt^j$, where $H_L=\max(H,-L)$ and
\[
 c_j=\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_X
       H_L(\ddc H_L)^{j-1}\wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-j}).
\]
Thus $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(tH)=A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_jt^j-Mt^n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $v\sim u$, the function $v$ is locally bounded off $S$ and $\{v=-\infty\}=S$. On a relatively compact coordinate neighborhood, choose a smooth function $\psi_1$ with $\ddc\psi_1\geq\omega$. Then $v+\psi_1$ and $v_k+\psi_1=\max(v+\psi_1,\psi_1-k)$ are plurisubharmonic and locally bounded off $S$, and $v_k+\psi_1$ decreases to $v+\psi_1$ as $k$ increases. By \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products}, for $1\leq j\leq n$ the currents $(\ddc(v_k+\psi_1))^j$ converge weakly to the closed positive currents $(\ddc(v+\psi_1))^j_{\mathrm{full}}$ of order zero. Writing $\omega+\ddc v_k=(\omega-\ddc\psi_1)+\ddc(v_k+\psi_1)$ and expanding, we obtain weak convergence of $(\ddc v_k)^j$, $1\leq j\leq n$, and of $(\omega+\ddc v_k)^n$; the local limits patch together because the approximating bounded currents are global. On the chosen neighborhood,
\[
 \Phi_v=\sum_{j=0}^n\binom nj(\omega-\ddc\psi_1)^{n-j}
            \wedge(\ddc(v+\psi_1))^j_{\mathrm{full}}.
\]
For $j<n$, the coefficient measures of $(\ddc(v+\psi_1))^j_{\mathrm{full}}$ vanish on $S$ by \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}, and smooth forms give $S$ zero mass. Hence $\mathbf1_S\Phi_v=\mathbf1_S(\ddc(v+\psi_1))^n_{\mathrm{full}}$. If $u+\psi$ is plurisubharmonic on this neighborhood, then $(u+\psi)-(v+\psi_1)$ is bounded, and \cref{lem:small-pole-residual-invariance} gives \eqref{eq:diffuse-residual-local}. Off $S$, the truncations eventually agree with $v$ on every compact set, so $\Phi_v$ equals $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)$ there, while $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)$ does not charge the pluripolar set $S$. Therefore $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)=\Phi_v(X)-\Phi_v(S)$.

For $1\leq j<n$, integration by parts for bounded potentials gives
\[
 \int_X(\ddc v_k)^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}
       =\int_Xv_k(\ddc v_k)^{j-1}
                           \wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-j});
\]
this follows by uniformly bounded decreasing regularization, as in \cref{thm:mass-expansion,thm:bounded-exact-regularization}. The left side converges, so $c_j(v)$ exists and is finite. By Stokes' theorem for bounded potentials, $(\ddc v_k)^n$ has zero integral. The binomial expansion of $(\omega+\ddc v_k)^n$ therefore gives $\Phi_v(X)=A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom njc_j(v)$, which proves the mass formula.

Each measure $(\omega+\ddc v_k)^n$ has total mass between $v_-(\omega)$ and $v_+(\omega)$; passing to the limit gives the two bounds, the upper one in the extended real sense. If $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, every $c_j(v)$ vanishes. For $n=2$, the convergence $v_k\to v$ in $L^1$ gives $c_1(v)=\int_Xv\ddc\omega$.

Finally, let $u=tH$, which belongs to $\PSH(X,\omega)$ by \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}. Near $S$, the function $u=th$ is plurisubharmonic, so \eqref{eq:diffuse-residual-local} with $\psi=0$ and \cref{lem:anisotropic-diffuse-product}, with all weights equal to one, give $\mathbf1_S\Phi_v=t^n\sigma^{\otimes n}$, a measure of mass $t^n=Mt^n$. Since $\max(tH,-k)=tH_{k/t}$, the limit defining $c_j(tH)$ is $t^j$ times the limit of the integrals defining $c_j$, taken along $L=k/t\to\infty$. Hence $c_j$ exists and $c_j(tH)=c_jt^j$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:diffuse-surface-mass-invariance}\index{Gauduchon metric!diffuse bounded-equivalence mass}
Let $n=2$, $0<t<1/C$ and $u=tH$. The non-pluripolar mass is constant on the ordinary type of $u$ if and only if $\omega$ is Gauduchon. More precisely, for every smooth real $f$ and every sufficiently small real $\varepsilon$,
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u+\varepsilon f)
       -\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u)
       =2\varepsilon\int_Xf\ddc\omega.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $c_f>0$ with $-c_f\omega\leq\ddc f\leq c_f\omega$. By \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}, $u+\varepsilon f$ is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic for $|\varepsilon|<(1-Ct)/c_f$. These potentials differ boundedly from $u$, so the surface formula of \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass} gives the stated difference.

If $\ddc\omega=0$, the same formula gives mass $A-Mt^2$ for every potential ordinarily equivalent to $u$. Conversely, write $\ddc\omega=\rho\omega^2$ with $\rho$ smooth and real. If $\rho\not\equiv0$, choose $f=\rho$. Then $\int_Xf\ddc\omega=\int_X\rho^2\omega^2>0$, so the masses of the smooth perturbations above vary. Thus constancy of the mass forces $\ddc\omega=0$, which is the Gauduchon condition on a surface.
\end{proof}

Under the bounded mass property and positive lower mass, the bounds of \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass} give a definite volume gap between the ordinary models of the potentials $tH$ and zero, which the polar comparison does not see.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness}\label{prop:diffuse-two-mass-model-obstruction}\index{Polar curvature!diffuse product poles}\index{Model potential!all modifications and separation}\index{Polar curvature!Hermitian mass bounds}\index{Model potential!diffuse poles under mass bounds}
Assume
\[
 0<v_-(\omega)\leq v_+(\omega)<+\infty;
\]
this holds for every K\"ahler form, with $v_\pm(\omega)=\int_X\omega^n$, and for every Hermitian form if $n=2$. For $0<t<1/C$ with $Mt^n<v_-(\omega)$, the potential $p_t=P_\omega[tH]$ is an ordinary model with
\[
 \begin{split}
 0<v_-(\omega)-Mt^n
 &\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t)\\
 &\leq v_+(\omega)-Mt^n.
 \end{split}
\]
Its first polar currents vanish on every smooth modification, all its multiplier ideals are trivial, and
\[
 d_\omega(p_t,0)\geq Mt^n>0,\qquad
 \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_t,0)=0
\]
for every family of modifications and every choice of Gauduchon forms as in \cref{subsec:gauduchon-polar-comparison}. Thus the polar comparison does not separate ordinary models of positive lower volume; $p_t$ is not an ideal model, and it belongs to the closure of the analytic models for the polar comparison. For any $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$, all sufficiently small positive parameters give $p_t,0\in\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass}, every $v\sim tH$ has mass between $v_-(\omega)-Mt^n$ and $v_+(\omega)-Mt^n$, which gives these bounds for the singularity volumes of $tH$. The lower volume is positive, so by \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} the envelope $p_t$ is an ordinary model with the same volumes. The type of zero consists of the bounded potentials, so its upper volume is $v_+(\omega)$. Since $p_t\leq0$, \eqref{eq:volume-comparison-basic} gives
$d_\omega(p_t,0)=v_+(\omega)-\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t)\geq Mt^n$.
In particular $p_t\ne0$.

Since $p_t\geq tH$, \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness} shows that all first polar currents of $p_t$ vanish and all its multiplier ideals are trivial. Hence the ideal envelope of $p_t$ is the zero model, while $p_t\ne0$, so $p_t$ is not an ideal model. The zero model has analytic singularities and vanishing first polar currents, so $\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_t,0)=0$, and $p_t$ lies in the closure of the analytic models for the polar comparison. Given $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$, choose $t_0<1/C$ with $Mt_0^n<v_-(\omega)-\delta$. The lower bound for the volume then puts zero and every $p_t$ with $0<t\leq t_0$ in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.

For a K\"ahler form, Stokes' theorem for bounded potentials gives $v_\pm(\omega)=\int_X\omega^n$. On a compact complex surface, a Gauduchon form has finite upper mass by \cref{thm:gauduchon,thm:mass-invariance-characterization}. By the metric independence in \cref{prop:bmp-metric}, every Hermitian form then has finite upper mass, and \cref{cor:lower-mass-dimension} gives positive lower mass.
\end{proof}

The residual measure distinguishes the parameters of the family $p_t$ even after ordinary projection, and volume addition controls the volumes over the whole range $0<t<1/C$. The ordered family is therefore a complete interval under the two mass bounds, although the terms $c_j(v)$ of \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass} need not vanish.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:diffuse-hermitian-model-interval}\index{Model potential!Hermitian diffuse interval}\index{Residual mass!diffuse model parameters}
Assume $0<v_-(\omega)\leq v_+(\omega)<+\infty$, and fix $0<t_0<1/C$. Set $p_t=P_\omega[tH]$ for $0<t\leq t_0$, define $p_0=0$ separately, and write
\[
 L(t)=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t),\qquad
 U(t)=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t).
\]
For $0\leq t\leq t_0$,
\[
 (1-Ct)^n v_-(\omega)\leq L(t)\leq U(t)
       \leq v_+(\omega)-Mt^n.
\]
The models are pairwise distinct, and $p_s\geq p_t$ for $s\leq t$. For either $V=L$ or $V=U$, and $0\leq s<t\leq t_0$,
\[
 \left(\frac{1-Ct}{1-Cs}\right)^n V(s)
       \leq V(t)\leq V(s).
\]
Consequently
\[
 d_\omega(p_s,p_t)=|U(s)-U(t)|
       \leq\frac{nC v_+(\omega)}{1-Ct_0}|s-t|,
 \qquad
 \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_s,p_t)=0.
\]
With the restriction of $d_\omega$, the family is a complete ordered interval, isometric to $[0,v_+(\omega)-U(t_0)]$. Every model with positive parameter has trivial multiplier ideals and is not an ideal model. For any $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$, $t_0$ can be chosen so that the whole interval lies in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $u_t=tH$ for $t>0$ and $u_0=0$. For $0<t<1/C$, the potential $u_t$ is $Ct\omega$-plurisubharmonic by \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}. Adding it to the zero potential in the background $(1-Ct)\omega$, \cref{prop:volume-addition} and homogeneity show that either singularity volume of $u_t$ is at least $(1-Ct)^n$ times the corresponding volume of zero. In particular, the lower volume of $u_t$ is positive on the whole range $0<t<1/C$, and ordinary projection preserves both volumes by \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}. The upper bound follows from \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass}, and the zero endpoint has $L(0)=v_-(\omega)$ and $U(0)=v_+(\omega)$.

By \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass,thm:small-pole-envelope-residual}, $\Phi_{p_t}(S)=\Phi_{u_t}(S)=Mt^n$ for $t>0$, whereas $\Phi_0=\omega^n$ gives $S$ zero mass. Thus two models of the family coincide only if their parameters are equal.

For $s<t$, set $c=(1-Ct)/(1-Cs)$. Then $0<c<1$ and
\[
 u_t=cu_s+(t-cs)H,\qquad
 C(t-cs)=1-c.
\]
The second summand is $(1-c)\omega$-plurisubharmonic. Addition and homogeneity give $V(t)\geq c^nV(s)$, whereas monotonicity gives $V(t)\leq V(s)$. The same computation holds for $s=0$, with the separately defined zero potential. Monotonicity of the envelope gives $p_s\geq p_t$, so \eqref{eq:volume-comparison-basic} gives $d_\omega(p_s,p_t)=U(s)-U(t)$. Since $U(s)\leq v_+(\omega)$, the Lipschitz bound follows from
\[
 1-c^n\leq n(1-c)
       =\frac{nC(t-s)}{1-Cs}
       \leq\frac{nC(t-s)}{1-Ct_0}.
\]

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
By this bound, $U$ is continuous. Distinct ordinary models of positive lower volume are separated by \cref{prop:model-volume-separation}, so $U$ is strictly decreasing. Thus $t\mapsto v_+(\omega)-U(t)$ identifies the family isometrically with the closed interval in the statement, which proves completeness.

Since $p_t\geq u_t$, \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness} shows that all first polar currents of $p_t$ vanish and all its multiplier ideals are trivial. Hence the polar comparison vanishes on the family, and the ideal envelope of every $p_t$ is zero. The deficit $Mt^n$ of the upper volume separates every positive parameter from zero, so the models with positive parameter are not ideal models. Finally, choose $t_0<1/C$ with $(1-Ct_0)^nv_-(\omega)>\delta$ to place the whole interval in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.
\end{proof}

If all bounded potentials have the same mass, the volumes of the family are explicit.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:diffuse-mass-invariant-models}\label{cor:diffuse-product-exact-volume}\index{Model potential!diffuse poles in invariant mass backgrounds}\index{Singularity volume!diffuse product family}\index{Model potential!ordered diffuse family}
Assume
\[
 \ddc(\omega^q)=0,\qquad 1\leq q<n;
\]
for instance, $\omega$ is K\"ahler, or $n=2$ and $\omega$ is Gauduchon. Put $A=\int_X\omega^n$ and fix $0<t_0<1/C$. Every $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $v\sim tH$, $0<t<1/C$, satisfies $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)=A-Mt^n$. Set $p_t=P_\omega[tH]$ for $0<t\leq t_0$ and $p_0=0$. Both singularity volumes of $p_t$ equal $A-Mt^n$ for $0\leq t\leq t_0$, and for all $s,t\in[0,t_0]$,
\[
 d_\omega(p_s,p_t)=M|s^n-t^n|,
 \qquad \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_s,p_t)=0.
\]
Thus this family, with the restriction of $d_\omega$, is a complete ordered interval, isometric to $[0,Mt_0^n]$. For $t>0$, the model $p_t$ has trivial multiplier ideals and vanishing first polar currents on every smooth modification, and it is not an ideal model. For any prescribed $0<\delta<A$, one can choose $t_0>0$ so that the whole interval lies in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}, every bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function has mass $A$, so $v_-(\omega)=v_+(\omega)=A$. The mass formula is the invariant-mass case of \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass}, and it shows that both singularity volumes of $tH$ equal $A-Mt^n$. This number is positive by the lower bound in \cref{cor:diffuse-hermitian-model-interval}, so \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} shows that both volumes of $p_t$ equal $A-Mt^n$, also for $t=0$. The same corollary gives $d_\omega(p_s,p_t)=|U(s)-U(t)|=M|s^n-t^n|$; thus $p_t\mapsto Mt^n$ is an isometry onto $[0,Mt_0^n]$. The remaining assertions are those of \cref{cor:diffuse-hermitian-model-interval,lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}, with $v_-(\omega)=A$. Finally, a K\"ahler form satisfies $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for every $q$, and on a surface the only condition is $\ddc\omega=0$, the Gauduchon condition; Gauduchon forms exist by \cref{thm:gauduchon}.
\end{proof}

The residual measure distinguishes the parameters of one ordered family, but its total mass alone cannot distinguish poles placed in different regions. Cutoffs with disjoint supports give a pair whose maximum is exactly zero, so that the volume comparison remains explicit.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:disjoint-diffuse-scalar-obstruction}\index{Residual mass!failure of scalar separation}\index{Model potential!disjoint diffuse poles}
Assume $0<v_-(\omega)\leq v_+(\omega)<+\infty$, and fix $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$. For every sufficiently small $r>0$, there are distinct ordinary models $p_1,p_2\in\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$ and disjoint compact sets $S_1,S_2$ of Hausdorff dimension zero such that the full Monge--Amp\`ere measures $\Phi_{p_i}$ of \cref{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual} satisfy
\[
 R_i:=\mathbf1_{S_1\cup S_2}\Phi_{p_i},\qquad
 R_i(X)=r,\qquad \operatorname{supp}R_i\subset S_i.
\]
Neither model is an ideal model, all their multiplier ideals are trivial, and their first polar currents vanish on every smooth modification. Moreover,
\[
 \max(p_1,p_2)=0,\qquad
 d_\omega(p_1,p_2)\geq2r,
 \qquad\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_1,p_2)=0.
\]
If $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, both singularity volumes of both models equal $A-r$, where $A=\int_X\omega^n$, and their distance is exactly $2r$. Thus the first polar currents together with the total residual mass do not separate ordinary models, and when $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, they still do not separate them after both singularity volumes are added.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Place the polydisk of \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} in two disjoint coordinate charts, and let $H_1,H_2$ be the corresponding cutoffs, with pole sets $S_1,S_2$ and constants $C_1,C_2$ as in \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}. For small $r>0$ put
\[
 t=(r/M)^{1/n},\qquad
 u_i=tH_i,\qquad p_i=P_\omega[u_i].
\]
Choose $r$ so small that $C_it<1$ and $(1-C_it)^nv_-(\omega)>\delta$ for both $i$. By \cref{cor:diffuse-hermitian-model-interval}, the $p_i$ are ordinary models in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$; they are not ideal models, their multiplier ideals are trivial and their first polar currents vanish. Their upper volumes $U_i$ satisfy $U_i\leq v_+(\omega)-r$.

By \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass,thm:small-pole-envelope-residual}, $\Phi_{p_i}(S_i)=\Phi_{u_i}(S_i)=Mt^n=r$. Outside the compact support of $H_i$, the bounds $u_i\leq p_i\leq0$ force $p_i=0$. In particular $p_i$ vanishes near $S_j$ for $j\ne i$, so $\Phi_{p_i}=\omega^n$ there, which gives $S_j$ zero mass. Hence $R_i=\mathbf1_{S_i}\Phi_{p_i}$. These measures have the same total mass $r>0$ and disjoint supports, so they differ, and so do $p_1$ and $p_2$.

Since the cutoffs have disjoint supports, at every point at least one $p_i$ vanishes, and both are nonpositive; this proves $\max(p_1,p_2)=0$. By \cref{def:model-volume-comparison},
\[
 d_\omega(p_1,p_2)=2v_+(\omega)-U_1-U_2\geq2r.
\]
All first polar coordinates of both models are zero, so their polar comparison vanishes. When $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, \cref{cor:diffuse-mass-invariant-models} gives both volumes equal to $A-Mt^n=A-r$, and $v_+(\omega)=A$. Substituting these values gives the exact distance $2r$ and the final assertion.
\end{proof}

Knowing the location and the size of the whole residual measure still does not determine the model. For anisotropic maxima on the same pole set, the residual measure depends only on the product of the weights, whereas the volume of the maximum of two such potentials detects the individual weights. On a compact box of positive weights, this comparison has an explicit metric interpretation.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:equal-residual-anisotropic-models}\index{Residual mass!failure of measure separation}\index{Model potential!anisotropic diffuse poles}
Assume $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$. Put $A=\int_X\omega^n$, fix $0<\delta<A$ and $0<\alpha<\beta$, and write $B=[\alpha,\beta]^n$. There are a compact set $S\subset X$ of Hausdorff dimension zero, a nonatomic probability measure $\rho$ supported on $S$, and $t_0>0$ with the following properties. For each fixed $0<t\leq t_0$, ordinary models $p_a\in\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$ are defined for all $a\in B$ and satisfy
\[
 \mathbf1_S\Phi_{p_a}=t^n\left(\prod_j a_j\right)\rho,
 \qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_a)
 =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_a)
 =A-t^n\prod_j a_j.
\]
For $a,b\in B$, set $c_j=\min(a_j,b_j)$. Then
\[
 d_\omega(p_a,p_b)
 =t^n\left(\prod_j a_j+\prod_j b_j-2\prod_j c_j\right).
\]
The restriction of $d_\omega$ to this family is a metric, and
\[
 t^n\alpha^{n-1}\max_j|a_j-b_j|
 \leq d_\omega(p_a,p_b)
 \leq t^n\beta^{n-1}\sum_j|a_j-b_j|.
\]
Thus $a\mapsto p_a$ identifies $B$ homeomorphically with a compact complete metric space. No model in the family is an ideal model. Each model has trivial multiplier ideals, and its first polar currents vanish on every smooth modification. For $\alpha^n<P<\beta^n$, the subfamily defined by $\prod_j a_j=P$ is complete and homeomorphic to a convex polytope of real dimension $n-1$. Throughout this subfamily, the residual measure on $S$ is $Pt^n\rho$ and both singularity volumes are $A-Pt^n$. Hence these data, even together with all multiplier ideals and the first polar currents on all modifications, do not separate ordinary models.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the chart and the cutoff $\chi$ fixed before \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}. For $a\in B$, extend $H_a=\chi h_a$ by zero and put $u_a=tH_a$, with $h_a$ as in \cref{lem:anisotropic-diffuse-product}. We first prove a curvature bound that is uniform in $a$.

Choose a compact neighborhood $\Sigma$ of the supports of the derivatives of $\chi$, disjoint from $S$. The isotropic maximum $\max_j g(z_j)$ is bounded below on $\Sigma$ by $-B_0$, for some $B_0>0$. Since $g\leq0$ and $a_j\leq\beta$, it follows that $-\beta B_0\leq h_a\leq0$ there. If the $j$th branch $a_jg(z_j)$ attains the maximum, then $a_jg(z_j)\geq-\beta B_0$, so $g(z_j)\geq-\beta B_0/\alpha$. Within the fixed compact range of the coordinates, this superlevel set stays away from $K$, and $g$ and its gradient are bounded on a neighborhood of it. Thus all maximizing branches have uniformly bounded gradients, and the maxima $h_a$ satisfy one local Lipschitz bound on $\Sigma$, independent of $a\in B$.

In the distributional product rule for $\ddc(\chi h_a)$, the term $\chi\ddc h_a$ is positive. The remaining terms contain derivatives of $\chi$ together with $h_a$ or its first derivatives, so the preceding bounds control their coefficients uniformly. As in the proof of \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}, this gives one $C'>0$ with $\ddc H_a\geq-C'\omega$ for every $a\in B$. Choose $t_0<1/C'$ with $t_0^n\beta^n<A-\delta$. Then all $u_a$, $0<t\leq t_0$, are normalized and satisfy $\omega+\ddc u_a\geq(1-C't)\omega$.

Near $S$, the function $u_a=th_a$ is plurisubharmonic. Hence \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass}, with \eqref{eq:diffuse-residual-local} for $\psi=0$, and \cref{lem:anisotropic-diffuse-product} show that every $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $v\sim u_a$ satisfies
\[
 \mathbf1_S\Phi_v=t^n\left(\prod_j a_j\right)\rho,\qquad \rho=\sigma^{\otimes n},
\]
with $\rho$ extended by zero from the chart, and
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)=A-t^n\prod_j a_j>\delta.
\]
Hence, by \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, ordinary projection preserves both singularity volumes, and by \cref{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual} it preserves the residual measure. Setting $p_a=P_\omega[u_a]$ proves the formulas for the residual measure and the volumes simultaneously for all $a\in B$.

For $a,b\in B$ and $c_j=\min(a_j,b_j)$, positivity of the weights and $g\leq0$ give, pointwise and also at the poles,
\[
 \max(h_a,h_b)=h_c,\qquad \max(u_a,u_b)=u_c.
\]
The invariance of the comparison under ordinary projection in \cref{prop:model-volume-separation} and the definition of the comparison give
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\omega(p_a,p_b)&=d_\omega(u_a,u_b)\\
 &=t^n\left(\prod_j a_j+\prod_j b_j-2\prod_j c_j\right).
 \end{split}
\]
For the boxes $R_a=\prod_j[0,a_j]\subset\mathbb R^n$ and Lebesgue measure $\lambda$, the last expression is $t^n\lambda(R_a\mathbin{\triangle}R_b)$, since $R_a\cap R_b=R_c$; equivalently, it is $t^n\|\mathbf1_{R_a}-\mathbf1_{R_b}\|_{L^1(\lambda)}$. This proves the triangle inequality within the family. A slab in the coordinate where $|a_j-b_j|$ is largest, with every other coordinate in $[0,\alpha]$, lies in the symmetric difference up to its boundary, and its volume gives the lower bound. The symmetric difference is covered by the $n$ coordinate slabs between $a_j$ and $b_j$, with every other coordinate in $[0,\beta]$, and their total volume gives the upper bound. In particular, the distance separates the parameters, and the two estimates show that it induces the Euclidean topology of $B$. A sequence that is Cauchy for the distance has Cauchy weights in the Euclidean sense, whose limit lies in $B$, and the upper bound gives convergence for the distance. Compactness follows from the same bounds.

Since $g\leq0$, we have $h_a\geq(\max_ja_j)h$, hence $p_a\geq u_a\geq t(\max_ja_j)H$. By \cref{lem:diffuse-cutoff-polar-blindness}, every first polar current of $p_a$ vanishes and all its multiplier ideals are trivial. The strict volume deficit $t^n\prod_j a_j$ shows that $p_a\ne0$, whereas the ideal envelope of $p_a$ is zero, so $p_a$ is not an ideal model.

Finally, the constraint $\prod_ja_j=P$ defines a closed subset of $B$, so the subfamily is complete. Taking logarithms coordinatewise identifies it with
\[
 [\log\alpha,\log\beta]^n
       \cap\left\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_jx_j=\log P\right\}.
\]
For $\alpha^n<P<\beta^n$, this convex polytope meets the interior of the cube at the point with $x_j=(\log P)/n$ for all $j$, and it has dimension $n-1$. The formulas for the volume and the residual measure are constant on it, while the distance separates its members. For example, taking $\alpha=1/2$, $\beta=2$, $P=1$, $a=(2,1/2,1,\ldots,1)$ and $b=(1,1,1,\ldots,1)$ gives two models with common residual measure $t^n\rho$, both volumes equal to $A-t^n$, and distance $t^n$.
\end{proof}
