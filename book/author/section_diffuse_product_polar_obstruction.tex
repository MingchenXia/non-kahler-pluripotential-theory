\subsubsection{Diffuse product poles}
\label{subsec:diffuse-product-polar-obstruction}

Polar curvature on every modification still misses some ordinary models. A maximum of diffuse curve potentials has a very small joint pole set. Its first polar currents vanish, while the bounded truncations retain positive top-degree mass near that set.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}
Let $\Delta\subset\C$ be the unit disc. There are a compact set $K\Subset\Delta$ of Hausdorff dimension zero and a negative subharmonic function $g$ on $\Delta$, smooth and harmonic off $K$, with
\[
 \{g=-\infty\}=K,\qquad \nu(g,a)=0\quad(a\in\Delta),
 \qquad g|_{\partial\Delta}=0.
\]
For $n\geq2$, put $D=\Delta^n$, $S=K^n$ and
\[
 h(z)=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}g(z_j),\qquad
 h_L=\max\{h,-L\}\quad(L>0).
\]
The function $h$ is locally Lipschitz off $S$ and its bounded local Monge--Amp\`ere measure there is zero. The measures $(\ddc h_L)^n$ have one common finite mass $M>0$. Their supports are compact and approach $S$ as $L\to\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the binary Cantor set and nonatomic probability $\sigma$ of \cref{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-model}, placed in a small real interval in $\Delta$. Its level-$k$ intervals have length $r_k=\exp(-4^{k+1})$ and mass $2^{-k}$. For every $s>0$,
\[
 2^kr_k^s\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 2^{nk}(\sqrt n r_k)^s\longrightarrow0.
\]
These covers show that both $K$ and $S$ have Hausdorff dimension zero. Define the disc Green potential
\[
 g(z)=\int_\Delta
       \log\left|\frac{z-y}{1-\bar yz}\right|^2\,\mathrm d\sigma(y).
\]
Then $\ddc g=\sigma$, with the normalization of this book. The Green kernel is nonpositive, zero on the boundary, and smooth away from its pole. On a fixed smaller disc it is at most $\log|z-y|^2+C$. Integration over the level-$k$ interval containing $z\in K$ gives
\[
 g(z)\leq2^{-k}(2\log r_k+C)\longrightarrow-\infty.
\]
Outside $K$ the potential is finite, smooth and harmonic. Its Lelong number at $a$ is $\sigma(\{a\})=0$. Since $K$ stays away from the boundary, $g$ is smooth near the boundary and tends uniformly to zero there.

Off $S$, a finite curve potential suppresses all pole branches locally. Hence $h$ is a locally Lipschitz maximum of at most $n$ pluriharmonic functions. For $m\leq n$ such functions $a_j$, use the smooth approximations decreasing as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$,
\[
 \varepsilon\log\sum_{j=1}^m e^{a_j/\varepsilon}
\]
whose Hessian is a positive multiple of the weighted covariance of $\partial a_j$, with probability weights proportional to $e^{a_j/\varepsilon}$. The centered covectors satisfy one weighted relation, giving rank at most $m-1<n$. Thus the $n$-th product vanishes; bounded decreasing convergence in \cref{thm:bt-input} gives the assertion for their maximum.

The bounded truncation $h_L$ is continuous: near a pole of any curve potential its branch is suppressed by $-L$. The same argument for the $n+1$ branches $g(z_j),-L$ shows that $(\ddc h_L)^n$ is carried by
\[
 Q_L=\{z:g(z_j)=-L\text{ for every }j\}.
\]
Off $Q_L$, one branch is strictly below the maximum. Discarding it and any pole branches leaves at most $n$ pluriharmonic branches locally, so the measure vanishes. The compact set $Q_L$ and \eqref{eq:local-cln-range} give finite mass.

For $L,L'>0$, the functions $h_L,h_{L'}$ agree near $\partial D$, since $h$ tends uniformly to zero there. Bounded integration by parts gives
\[
 (\ddc h_L)^n-(\ddc h_{L'})^n
       =\ddc\left((h_L-h_{L'})
         \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\ddc h_L)^j
                         \wedge(\ddc h_{L'})^{n-1-j}\right).
\]
The compact support on the right makes its integral zero. The common mass $M$ is positive: for fixed $L$, choose $\varepsilon>0$ small enough that
\[
 U=\{h_L<\varepsilon(|z|^2-n-1)\}
\]
is nonempty. It is relatively compact in $D$, because $h_L=0$ on the boundary and the quadratic is strictly negative there. The bounded comparison \eqref{eq:interior-power-comparison} gives
\[
 0<\int_U(\varepsilon\ddc|z|^2)^n
       \leq\int_U(\ddc h_L)^n\leq M.
\]
Finally, $g$ is bounded on compact subsets of $\overline\Delta\setminus K$. Its deep level sets therefore approach $K$, and $Q_L$ approaches $S$.
\end{proof}

The separable maximum also permits an exact calculation. Different positive weights can preserve its entire residual measure while changing its singularity type.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:anisotropic-diffuse-product}\index{Monge--Ampere measure@Monge--Amp\`ere measure!separable maximum}
Use the Green potential $g$, probability $\sigma$ and sets $D=\Delta^n$, $S=K^n$ from \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}. For $s,L>0$ and $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in(0,+\infty)^n$, put
\[
 \mu_s=\ddc\max(g,-s),\qquad h_a(z)=\max_j a_jg(z_j),
\]
and $h_{a,L}=\max(h_a,-L)$. Each $\mu_s$ is a nonatomic probability supported on $\{g=-s\}$, and $\mu_s\to\sigma$ weakly as $s\to\infty$. The bounded products satisfy
\[
 (\ddc h_{a,L})^n
   =\left(\prod_{j=1}^n a_j\right)
           \mu_{L/a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_{L/a_n}.
\]
The function $h_a$ has pole set $S$, is locally Lipschitz off $S$, and has zero bounded top product there. Its full product is
\[
 (\ddc h_a)^n_{\mathrm{full}}
       =\left(\prod_{j=1}^n a_j\right)\sigma^{\otimes n}.
\]
In particular, the common mass $M$ in \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} is $1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The curve truncation agrees with $g$ near the boundary of $\Delta$. Their difference is a compactly supported integrable function, so its complex Hessian has integral zero. Thus $\mu_s(\Delta)=\sigma(\Delta)=1$. The truncation is constant near $K$ and harmonic wherever $g\ne-s$ off $K$, proving the support assertion. Its boundedness gives no point masses, by \cref{thm:bt-input}. Decreasing local $L^1$ convergence to $g$ gives $\mu_s\to\ddc g=\sigma$; for large $s$ the supports lie in one fixed compact subset of $\Delta$.

The rank argument of \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} applies with the weights $a_j$. Away from $S$, finite branches suppress the pole branches, giving a locally Lipschitz maximum of at most $n$ pluriharmonic functions and zero top product. For the bounded maximum with the constant branch $-L$, that argument shows that its top product is supported on
\[
 Q_{a,L}=\{z:a_jg(z_j)=-L\text{ for every }j\}.
\]
Indeed, off this set one branch is strictly below the maximum, leaving at most $n$ active pluriharmonic branches. This also applies near pole branches, which the constant branch suppresses.

Near a point of $Q_{a,L}$ at which all $\mathrm dg(z_j)\ne0$, set $x_j=a_jg(z_j)+L$. Each $x_j$ is the real part of a local holomorphic coordinate in its own factor. For $\varepsilon>0$, smooth the maximum locally by
\[
 f_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon\log\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^n e^{x_j/\varepsilon}\right),
\]
with weights
\[
 w_0=\frac1{1+\sum_j e^{x_j/\varepsilon}},\qquad
 w_j=e^{x_j/\varepsilon}w_0.
\]
Then $-L+f_\varepsilon$ decreases to $h_{a,L}$. Its Hessian matrix is $\varepsilon^{-1}(\operatorname{diag}(w_j)-w_jw_k)$, whose determinant is $\varepsilon^{-n}w_0\prod_jw_j$. Hence
\[
 (\ddc f_\varepsilon)^n
   =n!\varepsilon^{-n}w_0\prod_{j=1}^n w_j
           \bigwedge_{j=1}^n(\mathrm dx_j\wedge\dc x_j).
\]
The scalar kernel on $\mathbb R^n$ is an approximate identity of mass $1$. To check the constant, write $y=x/\varepsilon$. The map
\[
 y\longmapsto\left(\frac{e^{y_j}}{1+\sum_k e^{y_k}}\right)_{j=1}^n
\]
is a diffeomorphism onto the open simplex $\{w_j>0,\ \sum_jw_j<1\}$, with Jacobian $w_0\prod_jw_j$. Its volume is $1/n!$, so the displayed kernel has integral $1$. Integrability of the fixed kernel in $y$ gives concentration at $x=0$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

Bounded decreasing product convergence therefore identifies the local limit with the tensor product of the one-variable level measures. In the $j$-th factor, the chain rule for $\max(x_j,0)$ gives
\[
 \delta_0(x_j)\,\mathrm dx_j\wedge\dc x_j
      =a_j\ddc\max(g,-L/a_j).
\]
Here the Dirac notation denotes the level measure in the real normal coordinate $x_j$. The critical points of the nonconstant harmonic function $g$ off $K$ are isolated. In each such coordinate neighborhood their inverse images are analytic hypersurfaces, hence pluripolar. The bounded top product gives them zero mass by \cref{thm:bt-input}; the candidate tensor measure does so because each $\mu_{L/a_j}$ has no point masses. Thus the formula extends across these points and, together with the support assertion, holds throughout $D$.

Tensor products of the curve probabilities converge to $\sigma^{\otimes n}$. This follows first for products of continuous one-variable test functions, and then for all continuous tests by uniform approximation on a common compact product. Finally $h_{a,L}\downarrow h_a$, whose unbounded locus is the closed Hausdorff-zero-dimensional set $S$. By \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products}, the weak limit is its full top product. Taking $a_j=1$ proves $M=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness}\index{Polar curvature!diffuse product poles}\index{Model potential!all modifications and separation}
Let $X$ be a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, with K\"ahler form $\omega$. There are a normalized ordinary model $p\ne0$ and $\delta>0$ such that $p,0\in\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$, all multiplier ideals of $p$ are trivial, and
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{p\circ\pi=-\infty\}}
       \bigl(\pi^*\omega+\ddc(p\circ\pi)\bigr)=0
\]
for every smooth modification $\pi:Y\to X$. Consequently, for every allowed family and every choice of Gauduchon forms,
\[
 \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,0)=0,\qquad d_\omega(p,0)>0.
\]
The polar comparison fails to separate ordinary models of positive lower volume. Its analytic closure contains the nonideal model $p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Place the polydisc of the lemma in a holomorphic coordinate chart of $X$. Choose a smooth cutoff $0\leq\chi\leq1$, compactly supported in that polydisc and equal to one on a neighborhood of $S$. Extend $H=\chi h$ by zero. The function is genuine and quasi-plurisubharmonic: outside $S$, the gradients of $h$ are locally bounded, so the terms containing derivatives of $\chi$ in
\[
 \ddc(\chi h)=\chi\ddc h
       +h\ddc\chi+\mathrm d\chi\wedge\dc h
       +\mathrm dh\wedge\dc\chi
\]
are bounded coefficient functions on their compact support. The first term is positive. Hence $\ddc H\geq-C\omega$ for some $C>0$. Take $0<t<1/C$ and put $u=tH$. Then $u\leq0$, $\sup_Xu=0$, its pole set is $S$, and
\[
 \omega+\ddc u\geq(1-tC)\omega.
\]

Set $u_k=\max(u,-k)$. The bounded currents $\omega+\ddc u_k$ have total Monge--Amp\`ere mass $A=\int_X\omega^n$ by closed-background Stokes. Near $S$, where $\chi=1$,
\[
 (\omega+\ddc u_k)^n
       =(\omega+t\ddc h_{k/t})^n
       \geq t^n(\ddc h_{k/t})^n.
\]
For large $k$, the entire support of the measure on the right lies in this neighborhood. Those measures have mass $t^nM$ and approach $S$. Take a common weakly convergent subsequence of these local measures and the global bounded-product measures. The global limit has mass at least $t^nM$ on $S$. Off $S$, the function $u$ is locally bounded, and $u_k=u$ eventually on every compact set. Thus the global limit there is precisely the non-pluripolar measure of $u$. It follows that
\[
 (1-tC)^nA
       \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u)
       \leq A-t^nM<A.
\]
The lower bound follows from the strict current inequality and bounded local products off the Lebesgue-null set $S$.

In a closed K\"ahler background, ordinary bounded equivalence preserves non-pluripolar mass. Therefore both singularity volumes of $u$ equal this positive number. By \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, $p=P_\omega[u]$ is a normalized ordinary model with the same volumes. In particular $p\ne0$, and $d_\omega(p,0)=A-\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p)>0$. Taking half its positive lower volume for $\delta$ puts both models in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$. Also $u\leq p\leq0$, so the pole set of $p$ is contained in $S$.

Fix a smooth modification $\pi$, and let $E_i$ be the irreducible divisorial components of its exceptional locus. Away from that analytic locus, $\pi$ is an isomorphism. The polar current $R$ of $p\circ\pi$ is closed and positive by \cref{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}. On the isomorphism locus it is carried by the inverse image of $S$, which has Hausdorff dimension zero in each relatively compact chart. By \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}, $R$ vanishes there. The analytic support theorem gives
\[
 R=\sum_i b_i[E_i],\qquad b_i\geq0.
\]
See \cite[Chapter III, Corollaries 2.11 and 2.14, pp.~141--143]{DemCADG}; components of codimension at least two contribute no closed order-zero $(1,1)$-current. The sum is finite by compactness. Since $R\leq\pi^*\omega+\ddc(p\circ\pi)$, its coefficients satisfy $b_i\leq\nu(p\circ\pi,E_i)$.

If $\pi(E_i)$ is not contained in $S$, some regular point of $E_i$ maps outside $S$. The lower bound by $u\circ\pi$ makes $p\circ\pi$ bounded near that point, forcing $b_i=0$. Every remaining image is an analytic set contained in the Hausdorff-zero-dimensional set $S$, hence a point $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$. Near that point, $u=t\max_jg(z_j)$. At a general point of $E_i$, the first coordinate satisfies
\[
 z_1\circ\pi-a_1=\zeta^m f,\qquad m\geq1,\quad f\ne0.
\]
A local holomorphic root of the unit $f$ makes this expression $\zeta'^m$. Circular means give
\[
 \nu(g(a_1+\zeta'^m),E_i)=m\nu(g,a_1)=0.
\]
Since $p\circ\pi\geq u\circ\pi\geq t\,g(z_1\circ\pi)$ there, again $b_i=0$. Thus $R=0$ for every modification.

The same local comparison gives zero Lelong numbers for $u$, and hence for $p\geq u$, at every point. Skoda's local integrability theorem, recalled in \cite{SGPT}, makes $\mathrm e^{-sp}$ locally integrable for every $s>0$. Thus all multiplier ideals of $p$ are trivial. Its ideal envelope is therefore the zero model. Since $p\ne0$, it is nonideal. The zero model is analytic, and $\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,0)=0$ puts $p$ in its polar-comparison closure. This proves the final assertions.
\end{proof}

The lost top-degree mass also measures the distance along the scaled family. Lower-degree closed positive currents cannot charge its zero-dimensional pole set, so they contribute no additional volume loss.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:diffuse-product-exact-volume}\index{Singularity volume!diffuse product family}\index{Model potential!ordered diffuse family}
Fix $H=\chi h$, $S=K^n$, $C>0$ and $M>0$ from the construction in \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness}, and put $A=\int_X\omega^n$. Choose $0<t_0<1/C$. For $0<t\leq t_0$ set
\[
 u_t=tH,\qquad p_t=P_\omega[u_t],
\]
and define $u_0=p_0=0$ separately. Both singularity volumes of $p_t$ equal
\[
 V(p_t)=A-Mt^n,\qquad 0\leq t\leq t_0.
\]
For every $s,t\in[0,t_0]$,
\[
 d_\omega(p_s,p_t)=M|s^n-t^n|,
 \qquad \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_s,p_t)=0.
\]
Thus this family, with its restricted $d_\omega$ metric, is a complete ordered interval, isometric to $[0,Mt_0^n]$. Every $p_t$ with $t>0$ is nonideal and has trivial multiplier ideals. For any prescribed $0<\delta<A$, one can choose $t_0>0$ so that the entire interval lies in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix $t>0$. Let $u_{t,k}=\max(tH,-k)$ and $\Theta_k=\omega+\ddc u_{t,k}$. These bounded closed positive products satisfy, for $1\leq j\leq n$,
\[
 \int_X\Theta_k^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}=A.
\]
Indeed, subtract $\omega^j$, telescope the mixed products, and integrate
$\ddc\bigl(u_{t,k}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\Theta_k^i\wedge\omega^{j-1-i}\bigr)\wedge\omega^{n-j}$
by bounded integration by parts.

Choose an open neighborhood $U$ of $S$ on which $\chi=1$. There
$\Theta_k=\omega+t\ddc h_{k/t}$, so positivity gives
\[
 t^j(\ddc h_{k/t})^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}
       \leq\Theta_k^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}.
\]
For the finitely many $1\leq j<n$, take a common subsequence along which the local closed positive currents $(\ddc h_{k/t})^j$ converge weakly on $U$, with limits $R_j$. The displayed bounds give the required local trace bounds. Also take weak limits of the global measures $\Theta_k^n$ and of the local top-degree measures. By \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}, the latter have mass $M$ and supports eventually in a fixed compact subset of $U$, tending to $S$. Their zero extensions as measures therefore converge, along a further subsequence, to a measure $\rho$ supported on $S$ with $\rho(S)=M$.

The set $S$ has Hausdorff dimension zero. By \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}, every $R_j$, $j<n$, has coefficient measures vanishing on $S$. The smooth measure $\omega^n$ also gives $S$ zero mass. Expand on $U$:
\[
 \Theta_k^n=\sum_{j=0}^n\binom nj
       t^j(\ddc h_{k/t})^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}.
\]
If $\eta$ is the chosen global weak limit, this expansion gives
$\eta(S)=t^n\rho(S)=Mt^n$, while $\eta(X)=A$. Off $S$, the function $u_t$ is locally bounded and its truncations eventually agree with it on each compact set. Hence $\eta$ there agrees with $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u_t)$. The non-pluripolar measure gives $S$ zero mass, proving
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u_t)=A-Mt^n.
\]
This argument requires only a weak subsequential top-degree limit; it does not define an unbounded Bedford--Taylor product on $S$.

The strict current bound gives
$A-Mt^n\geq(1-Ct_0)^nA>0$.
Closed-background bounded equivalence and \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} now give the formula for both singularity volumes of the normalized ordinary model $p_t$. Since $H\leq0$, monotonicity of the ordinary envelope gives $p_s\geq p_t$ for $s\leq t$; the zero endpoint satisfies the same order. Thus $\max(p_s,p_t)=p_s$, and the ordered upper-volume comparison gives
$d_\omega(p_s,p_t)=M(t^n-s^n)$. The parameter $Mt^n$ proves the claimed isometry and completeness of this interval.

The proof of \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness} applies to every $0<t\leq t_0$: all first polar currents vanish on all modifications, and all multiplier ideals are trivial. The polar currents also vanish for $p_0=0$, giving the zero polar comparison. For $t>0$, the strict volume deficit makes $p_t\ne0$, so its trivial ideals make it nonideal. Finally choose $t_0<1/C$ so small that $(1-Ct_0)^nA>\delta$. The uniform lower-volume bound puts every $p_t$ in the prescribed sector.
\end{proof}

For a nonclosed background, the derivatives in \eqref{eq:mass-expansion} also contribute. Bounded cutoff approximations isolate those lower-degree terms from the top-degree loss on $S$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!diffuse cutoff polynomial}
Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, with Hermitian form $\omega$. Place the polydisc of \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} in a coordinate chart. Choose $0\leq\chi\leq1$ smooth, compactly supported there and equal to one near $S=K^n$. Extend $H=\chi h$ and $H_L=\chi h_L$ by zero. There is $C>0$ such that $\ddc H,\ddc H_L\geq-C\omega$ for all sufficiently large $L$. Put $A=\int_X\omega^n$. The finite real limits
\[
 c_j=\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_X
       H_L(\ddc H_L)^{j-1}\wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-j}),
       \qquad 1\leq j<n,
\]
exist, and, for $0<t<1/C$,
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(tH)
       =A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_jt^j-Mt^n.
\]
If $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, this mass is $A-Mt^n$. On a surface the formula is
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(tH)
       =A+2t\int_X H\ddc\omega-Mt^2.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Outside a fixed neighborhood of $S$, the truncations $h_L$ eventually agree with $h$ on every compact set. In particular they agree on the supports of the derivatives of $\chi$. The cutoff calculation in \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness} gives one uniform lower bound for $H_L$ and $H$.

Let $\kappa=\ddc|z|^2$ on the coordinate polydisc $D$, and choose $B>0$ so that $T_L=B\kappa+\ddc H_L$ is positive and closed on $D$. The potentials of $T_L$ are locally bounded. Since $H_L$ has compact support, bounded integration by parts gives, for $1\leq j\leq n$,
\[
 \int_D T_L^j\wedge\kappa^{n-j}
       =B^j\int_D\kappa^n.
\]
Indeed, every nonconstant term in the binomial expansion is the complex Hessian of a compactly supported bounded-product current wedged with a closed power of $\kappa$. Thus all degrees have uniform trace bounds, independently of $L$.

For $j<n$, every subsequential weak limit of $T_L^j$ has coefficient measures vanishing on $S$, by \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}. Off $S$, local boundedness and eventual equality identify it with $(B\kappa+\ddc H)^j$. These two properties identify the limit uniquely. Expanding $(T_L-B\kappa)^j$ therefore proves full weak convergence of $(\ddc H_L)^j$ to a finite signed current $W_j$, with no coefficient mass on $S$. It vanishes near $\partial D$ and extends by zero to $X$. Bounded integration by parts then gives
\[
 \int_X(\ddc H_L)^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}
       =\int_X H_L(\ddc H_L)^{j-1}
                         \wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-j}).
\]
The left side converges to $\int_XW_j\wedge\omega^{n-j}$, proving existence and finiteness of $c_j$.

Fix $0<t<1/C$. The positive measures $(\omega+t\ddc H_L)^n$ have uniformly bounded mass, by their local expansion in the powers of $T_L$ and smooth forms. Their total masses also satisfy
\[
 \int_X(\omega+t\ddc H_L)^n
       =A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj t^j
                    \int_X(\ddc H_L)^j\wedge\omega^{n-j};
\]
the pure top Hessian integrates to zero by bounded Stokes. Take a weak subsequential limit $\eta$. Its total mass is the polynomial $A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_jt^j$.

Near $S$, where $\chi=1$, expand $(\omega+t\ddc h_L)^n$. All lower-degree limits give $S$ zero mass. The top-degree measures have mass $M$ and supports tending to $S$, by \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}. Hence $\eta(S)=Mt^n$. Off $S$, eventual equality with the locally bounded $tH$ identifies $\eta$ with its non-pluripolar measure. Subtracting the mass on $S$ proves the formula.

If all displayed background derivatives vanish, every $c_j$ is zero. For $n=2$, dominated $L^1$ convergence $H_L\to H$ gives $c_1=\int_XH\ddc\omega$, proving the surface identity. These are the masses of the displayed potentials; passing to singularity volumes in a nonclosed background requires control of their entire bounded-equivalence classes.
\end{proof}

The mass-invariance identities provide that control for this pole set. The full local product preserves its residual mass under bounded equivalence, while bounded global truncations all have the same total mass.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:diffuse-mass-invariant-models}\index{Model potential!diffuse poles in invariant mass backgrounds}
Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on a connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n\geq2$, and assume
\[
 \ddc(\omega^q)=0,\qquad 1\leq q<n.
\]
For the cutoff $H$ of \cref{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial}, every $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ ordinarily bounded-equivalent to $tH$, $0<t<1/C$, has
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)=A-Mt^n,\qquad A=\int_X\omega^n.
\]
Consequently all conclusions of \cref{cor:diffuse-product-exact-volume} hold in this background: the ordinary models have both volumes $A-Mt^n$, form a complete ordered interval with distances $M|s^n-t^n|$, and have zero first polar comparison on all modifications. The interval can lie in any prescribed cutoff $0<\delta<A$. Its positive-parameter models are nonideal with trivial multiplier ideals.

In particular these conclusions hold on every connected compact complex surface equipped with a Gauduchon form.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The mass-invariance theorem \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization} gives mass $A$ to every bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic potential. Thus $v_+(\omega)=A$, so $X$ has bounded mass by \cref{prop:bmp-metric}.

Fix $v\sim tH$. Its pole set is $S$ and it is locally bounded off $S$. In a coordinate neighborhood of $S$, choose a smooth Euclidean correction $q$ so that $a=v+q$ is plurisubharmonic. Since $\chi=1$ there, $a-th$ is bounded near $S$. By \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products}, the full product of $h$ is the weak limit of $(\ddc h_L)^n$. The support and mass assertions of \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} show that this limit is supported on $S$ with total mass $M$. The residual invariance lemma \cref{lem:small-pole-residual-invariance} therefore gives
\[
 (\ddc a)^n_{\mathrm{full}}(S)=Mt^n.
\]

Set $v_k=\max(v,-k)$. After each fixed local smooth correction, these functions decrease to a plurisubharmonic function whose unbounded locus lies in $S$. The full local product theorem and the expansion \eqref{eq:bounded-expansion} identify the weak limit of $(\omega+\ddc v_k)^n$ with a global positive full measure $T_v$ of total mass $A$. The local expressions agree on overlaps because they are limits of the same bounded global measures. Every closed positive power $(\ddc a)^j_{\mathrm{full}}$, $j<n$, has coefficient measures vanishing on $S$, by \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}. Smooth forms also give $S$ zero mass. Hence only the top closed power contributes there, and $T_v(S)=Mt^n$. Off $S$, the truncations eventually agree with $v$ on each compact set, so $T_v$ equals the non-pluripolar measure there. Removing its mass on $S$ proves the displayed formula for every bounded-equivalent representative.

Both singularity volumes of $tH$ thus equal $A-Mt^n$. The strict current bound gives positive volume, uniformly for $0<t\leq t_0<1/C$. By \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, ordinary projection preserves both volumes and gives the normalized models. Their order and distance follow as in \cref{cor:diffuse-product-exact-volume}, with the zero endpoint defined separately.

The support and Lelong-number argument in \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness} uses smoothness of the background and closedness of its polar restriction, not closedness of the background. It therefore gives zero first polar currents on all modifications and trivial all-weight ideals here as well. The strict volume deficit makes every positive-parameter model nonideal. Choosing $t_0$ small enough gives the prescribed cutoff. Finally, on a surface the Gauduchon condition is $\ddc\omega=0$; the remaining power identities are automatic by degree. Such forms exist by \cref{thm:gauduchon}.
\end{proof}

In an arbitrary Hermitian background, bounded equivalence still preserves the top-degree pole loss. The total mass of the full truncation limit can vary with the representative, through the lower-degree background derivatives.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:diffuse-representative-mass}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!bounded-equivalent diffuse representatives}
Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on a connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n\geq2$. Fix the cutoff $H$, constant $C$ and mass $M$ of \cref{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial}, and $0<t<1/C$. For every $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ ordinarily bounded-equivalent to $tH$, put $v_k=\max(v,-k)$. The limits
\[
 c_j(v)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_X
     v_k(\ddc v_k)^{j-1}\wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-j}),
       \qquad 1\leq j<n,
\]
are finite real numbers, and
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)
       =A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_j(v)-Mt^n,
       \qquad A=\int_X\omega^n.
\]
In particular
\[
 v_-(\omega)-Mt^n
       \leq\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(v)
       \leq v_+(\omega)-Mt^n.
\]
On a surface this mass is $A+2\int_Xv\ddc\omega-Mt^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The pole set of $v$ is $S$, and $v$ is locally bounded off $S$. On each relatively compact coordinate neighborhood choose a fixed smooth Euclidean correction $q$ making $v+q$ and every $v_k+q$ plurisubharmonic. By \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products}, all closed powers of $\ddc(v_k+q)$ converge weakly to finite order-zero currents. Expanding $\ddc v_k=\ddc(v_k+q)-\ddc q$ gives full weak convergence in every degree. The limits patch because the bounded currents $(\ddc v_k)^j$ are global. Write their limits as $W_j$.

Bounded integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_X(\ddc v_k)^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}
       =\int_Xv_k(\ddc v_k)^{j-1}
                           \wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-j}).
\]
This follows by uniformly bounded decreasing regularization as in \cref{thm:mass-expansion,thm:bounded-exact-regularization}. Pairing $W_j$ with the smooth background power proves existence and finiteness of $c_j(v)$. The pure top Hessian has zero integral for every $k$, by bounded Stokes. Expanding the positive bounded measures $(\omega+\ddc v_k)^n$ therefore gives a global positive weak limit $T_v$, of finite total mass
$A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_j(v)$.

Near $S$, compare $a=v+q$ with $th$. Their difference is bounded there. The full top mass of $h$ is $M$, supported on $S$, by \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products,lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}. Hence \cref{lem:small-pole-residual-invariance} gives $(\ddc a)^n_{\mathrm{full}}(S)=Mt^n$. Every lower closed power has zero coefficient mass on $S$ by \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}. In the expansion with the smooth form $\omega-\ddc q$, only the top power contributes to $T_v(S)$, giving $T_v(S)=Mt^n$. Off $S$, eventual truncation equality identifies $T_v$ with the non-pluripolar measure of $v$. Subtraction proves the formula.

Every bounded global measure has total mass between $v_-(\omega)$ and $v_+(\omega)$. Passing to its global weak limit and subtracting $Mt^n$ gives the two bounds, with the upper one interpreted in the extended real sense. In dimension two, $L^1$ convergence of $v_k$ to $v$ gives $c_1(v)=\int_Xv\ddc\omega$, proving the surface formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:diffuse-surface-mass-invariance}\index{Gauduchon metric!diffuse bounded-equivalence mass}
On a connected compact complex surface with Hermitian form $\omega$, fix the preceding cutoff and $0<t<1/C$. The non-pluripolar mass is constant over the ordinary bounded-equivalence class of $u=tH$ if and only if $\omega$ is Gauduchon. More precisely, for every smooth real $f$ and every sufficiently small real $\varepsilon$,
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u+\varepsilon f)
       -\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u)
       =2\varepsilon\int_Xf\ddc\omega.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The strict bound $\omega+\ddc u\geq(1-Ct)\omega$ makes $u+\varepsilon f$ admissible for both signs of sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Indeed choose $D>0$ with $-D\omega\leq\ddc f\leq D\omega$ and take $|\varepsilon|<(1-Ct)/D$. These potentials differ boundedly from $u$, so \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass} gives the displayed difference.

If $\ddc\omega=0$, the same proposition gives mass $A-Mt^2$ for every bounded-equivalent representative. Conversely write $\ddc\omega=\rho\omega^2$ with $\rho$ smooth and real. If this form is nonzero, choose $f=\rho$. Then $\int_Xf\ddc\omega=\int_X\rho^2\omega^2>0$, so the masses of the admissible smooth perturbations vary. Thus constancy forces $\ddc\omega=0$, which is the Gauduchon condition on a surface.
\end{proof}

The representative mass bounds still give a strict model-volume gap when the background has both mass properties. This extends the first-polar obstruction beyond the invariant-mass regime.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:diffuse-two-mass-model-obstruction}\index{Polar curvature!Hermitian mass bounds}\index{Model potential!diffuse poles under mass bounds}
Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, with Hermitian form $\omega$ satisfying
\[
 0<v_-(\omega)\leq v_+(\omega)<+\infty.
\]
Fix $H,C,M$ from \cref{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial}. For $0<t<1/C$ with $Mt^n<v_-(\omega)$, the normalized ordinary model $p_t=P_\omega[tH]$ satisfies
\[
 \begin{split}
 0<v_-(\omega)-Mt^n
 &\leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t)
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t)\\
 &\leq v_+(\omega)-Mt^n.
 \end{split}
\]
Its first polar currents vanish on every smooth modification, all its multiplier ideals are trivial, and
\[
 d_\omega(p_t,0)\geq Mt^n>0,\qquad
 \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_t,0)=0
\]
for every allowed family and choice of Gauduchon forms. Thus $p_t$ is nonideal and belongs to the polar-comparison closure of the analytic models. For any $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$, all sufficiently small positive parameters give $p_t,0\in\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.

In particular the conclusion applies to every Hermitian form on every connected compact complex surface.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taking the infimum and supremum in \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass} gives the bounds for the singularity volumes of $tH$. Its lower volume is positive, so \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes} preserves both volumes in the ordinary-model projection. Since $tH\leq p_t\leq0$ and $\sup_XtH=0$, the model is normalized.

The type of zero consists of the bounded potentials, so its upper volume is $v_+(\omega)$. Since $p_t\leq0$, \eqref{eq:volume-comparison-basic} gives
$d_\omega(p_t,0)=v_+(\omega)-\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t)\geq Mt^n$.
In particular $p_t\ne0$.

The support and Lelong-number portion of the proof of \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness} applies to this model: its pole set lies in $S$, the polar restriction is closed and positive, and each exceptional divisor whose image lies in $S$ maps to a point. The zero curve Lelong numbers force every divisorial coefficient to vanish. The lower bound $p_t\geq tH$ and Skoda integrability give trivial multiplier ideals at all weights. Hence its ideal envelope is the zero model, while $p_t\ne0$. All first polar comparisons with zero vanish, proving nonideality and the analytic-closure assertion.

Given $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$, choose $t_0<1/C$ with $Mt_0^n<v_-(\omega)-\delta$. The lower-volume bound puts every $p_t$, $0<t\leq t_0$, and zero in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.

On a compact complex surface, a Gauduchon form has finite bounded mass by \cref{thm:gauduchon,thm:mass-invariance-characterization}. Metric independence in \cref{prop:bmp-metric} gives bounded mass for every Hermitian form, and \cref{cor:lower-mass-dimension} gives positive lower mass.
\end{proof}

The residual mass distinguishes every parameter after ordinary projection. Volume addition also controls the entire strict parameter range, so the ordered family remains a complete interval even when the torsion coefficients vary.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:diffuse-hermitian-model-interval}\index{Model potential!Hermitian diffuse interval}\index{Residual mass!diffuse model parameters}
Under the two mass assumptions of \cref{prop:diffuse-two-mass-model-obstruction}, fix $0<t_0<1/C$. Set $p_t=P_\omega[tH]$ for $0<t\leq t_0$ and define $p_0=0$ separately. Write
\[
 L(t)=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t),\qquad
 U(t)=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_t).
\]
For $0\leq t\leq t_0$,
\[
 (1-Ct)^n v_-(\omega)\leq L(t)\leq U(t)
       \leq v_+(\omega)-Mt^n.
\]
The models are pairwise distinct and ordered by $p_s\geq p_t$ for $s\leq t$. For either $V=L$ or $V=U$, and $0\leq s<t\leq t_0$,
\[
 \left(\frac{1-Ct}{1-Cs}\right)^n V(s)
       \leq V(t)\leq V(s).
\]
Consequently
\[
 d_\omega(p_s,p_t)=|U(s)-U(t)|
       \leq\frac{nC v_+(\omega)}{1-Ct_0}|s-t|,
 \qquad
 \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_s,p_t)=0.
\]
The family with this restricted metric is a complete ordered interval, isometric to $[0,v_+(\omega)-U(t_0)]$. Every positive-parameter model is nonideal with trivial multiplier ideals. For any $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$, $t_0$ can be chosen so that the whole interval lies in $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $u_t=tH$ for $t>0$ and $u_0=0$. For $0<t<1/C$, the potential $u_t$ is $Ct\omega$-plurisubharmonic. Add it to zero in the background $(1-Ct)\omega$. By \cref{prop:volume-addition} and homogeneity, either singularity volume of $u_t$ is at least $(1-Ct)^n$ times the corresponding volume of zero. In particular its lower volume is positive throughout this strict range. Ordinary projection therefore preserves both volumes by \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}. The upper bound follows from \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass}, and the zero endpoint has $L(0)=v_-(\omega)$ and $U(0)=v_+(\omega)$.

Near $S$, $u_t=th$. After a fixed smooth Euclidean correction, bounded residual invariance and \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass} give top residual $Mt^n$. By \cref{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual}, $p_t$ has that same residual. Zero has residual zero, since its corrected top measure is smooth. Thus equality of two models forces equality of their parameters.

For $s<t$, set $c=(1-Ct)/(1-Cs)$. Then $0<c<1$ and
\[
 u_t=cu_s+(t-cs)H,\qquad
 C(t-cs)=1-c.
\]
The second summand is $(1-c)\omega$-plurisubharmonic. Addition and homogeneity give $V(t)\geq c^nV(s)$, whereas monotonicity gives $V(t)\leq V(s)$. The same computation holds at $s=0$, using the separately defined zero potential. Envelope monotonicity gives $p_s\geq p_t$. The ordered comparison \eqref{eq:volume-comparison-basic} therefore gives $d_\omega(p_s,p_t)=U(s)-U(t)$. Since $U(s)\leq v_+(\omega)$, the Lipschitz bound follows from
\[
 1-c^n\leq n(1-c)
       =\frac{nC(t-s)}{1-Cs}
       \leq\frac{nC(t-s)}{1-Ct_0}.
\]

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
The function $U$ is continuous by this bound. Distinct positive-lower-volume models are separated by \cref{prop:model-volume-separation}, so $U$ is strictly decreasing. Thus $t\mapsto v_+(\omega)-U(t)$ identifies the family isometrically with the claimed closed real interval, proving completeness.

The support and Lelong-number argument in \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness} applies throughout the strict range: all first polar currents vanish and all multiplier ideals are trivial. The upper-volume deficit $Mt^n$ separates every positive parameter from zero, proving nonideality and the zero polar comparison. Finally choose $t_0<1/C$ with $(1-Ct_0)^nv_-(\omega)>\delta$ to obtain the common cutoff.
\end{proof}

The residual distinguishes the parameters of one ordered family. Its total mass alone cannot distinguish poles placed in different regions. Disjoint cutoff supports give a pair whose maximum is exactly zero, so the volume comparison remains explicit.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:disjoint-diffuse-scalar-obstruction}\index{Residual mass!failure of scalar separation}\index{Model potential!disjoint diffuse poles}
Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on a connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n\geq2$, with $0<v_-(\omega)\leq v_+(\omega)<+\infty$. Fix $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$. For every sufficiently small $r>0$, there are distinct normalized ordinary models $p_1,p_2\in\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$ and disjoint compact Hausdorff-zero-dimensional sets $S_1,S_2$ such that the full measures of \cref{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual} satisfy
\[
 R_i:=\mathbf1_{S_1\cup S_2}\Phi_{p_i},\qquad
 R_i(X)=r,\qquad \operatorname{supp}R_i\subset S_i.
\]
Both models are nonideal, all their multiplier ideals are trivial, and their first polar currents vanish on every smooth modification. Moreover,
\[
 \max(p_1,p_2)=0,\qquad
 d_\omega(p_1,p_2)\geq2r,
 \qquad\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_1,p_2)=0.
\]
If $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, both singularity volumes of both models equal $A-r$, where $A=\int_X\omega^n$, and their distance is exactly $2r$. Thus first polar data together with one residual total fail to separate ordinary models; in this invariant-mass regime, adjoining both scalar singularity volumes still fails.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose two disjoint coordinate polydiscs and place a cutoff $H_i$, pole set $S_i$, curvature constant $C_i>0$ and truncation mass $M_i>0$ from \cref{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial} in each. For small $r>0$ put
\[
 t_i=(r/M_i)^{1/n},\qquad
 u_i=t_iH_i,\qquad p_i=P_\omega[u_i].
\]
Choose $r$ so small that $C_it_i<1$ and $(1-C_it_i)^nv_-(\omega)>\delta$ for both $i$. By \cref{cor:diffuse-hermitian-model-interval}, these are normalized ordinary models in the prescribed sector, nonideal with trivial ideals and zero first polar currents. Their upper volumes $U_i$ satisfy $U_i\leq v_+(\omega)-r$.

The full top product of $h$ in the $i$-th chart is a positive measure $\rho_i$ supported on $S_i$, of mass $M_i$. Near $S_i$, $u_i=t_ih$, so bounded residual invariance and \cref{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual} give
$\mathbf1_{S_i}\Phi_{p_i}=t_i^n\rho_i$, with zero extension outside that chart. Outside the compact support of $H_i$, the bounds $u_i\leq p_i\leq0$ force $p_i=0$. In particular it is smooth near $S_j$ for $j\ne i$; its full measure there is $\omega^n$, which gives $S_j$ zero mass. Hence $R_i=t_i^n\rho_i$. The measures have the same total $r>0$ and disjoint supports, so they differ.

The cutoff supports are disjoint. At every point at least one $p_i$ is zero, and both are nonpositive, proving $\max(p_1,p_2)=0$. By \cref{def:model-volume-comparison},
\[
 d_\omega(p_1,p_2)=2v_+(\omega)-U_1-U_2\geq2r.
\]
Every first polar coordinate of both models is zero, giving their zero polar comparison. In the invariant-mass regime, \cref{cor:diffuse-mass-invariant-models} gives both volumes $A-M_it_i^n=A-r$ and $v_+(\omega)=A$. Substitution yields the exact distance $2r$ and the final nonseparation assertion.
\end{proof}

Knowing the location and size of the whole residual measure still does not determine the model. Anisotropic maxima on the same pole set have residual measure determined by the product of their weights; the volume of their maximum also detects the individual weights.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:equal-residual-anisotropic-models}\index{Residual mass!failure of measure separation}\index{Model potential!anisotropic diffuse poles}
Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form on a connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n\geq2$, with $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$. Put $A=\int_X\omega^n$, and fix $0<\delta<A$. There are a compact Hausdorff-zero-dimensional set $S\subset X$, a nonatomic probability $\rho$ supported there, and $t_0>0$ such that, for every $0<t\leq t_0$, two normalized ordinary models $p_a,p_b\in\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$ satisfy
\[
 \mathbf1_S\Phi_{p_a}=\mathbf1_S\Phi_{p_b}=t^n\rho.
\]
Their lower and upper singularity volumes satisfy
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_e)
 =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\omega(p_e)=A-t^n,
 \qquad e=a,b,
\]
but
\[
 d_\omega(p_a,p_b)=t^n>0.
\]
Both models are nonideal, all their multiplier ideals are trivial, and their first polar currents vanish on every smooth modification. Thus even the complete base residual measure, both scalar singularity volumes, all multiplier ideals and all-modification first polar currents together fail to separate ordinary models.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Place $D$ from \cref{lem:anisotropic-diffuse-product} in a coordinate chart and use one cutoff $\chi$ as before, equal to one near $S=K^n$. Set
\[
 a=(2,1/2,1,\ldots,1),\qquad
 b=(1,1,1,\ldots,1),\qquad
 c=(1,1/2,1,\ldots,1).
\]
For $e=a,b,c$, extend $H_e=\chi h_e$ by zero and put $u_e=tH_e$. The cutoff proof of \cref{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial} gives one $C>0$ with $\ddc H_e\geq-C\omega$ for all three functions. Take $t_0<1/C$ so small that $(1-Ct_0)^nA>\delta$. Then every $u_e$, $0<t\leq t_0$, is normalized and has current at least $(1-Ct)\omega$.

Bounded mass invariance gives $v_-(\omega)=v_+(\omega)=A$. For any $v\sim u_e$, the full global truncation measure has total mass $A$, as in the proof of \cref{cor:diffuse-mass-invariant-models}. Near $S$, the difference of a smooth corrected $v$ and $th_e$ is bounded. By \cref{lem:small-pole-residual-invariance,lem:anisotropic-diffuse-product}, its top residual measure is
\[
 t^n\left(\prod_j e_j\right)\rho,\qquad \rho=\sigma^{\otimes n},
\]
extended by zero from the chart. Every lower corrected closed power gives $S$ zero coefficient mass. Thus this is also the residual of the full global truncation measure. Off $S$ that full measure equals the non-pluripolar measure, proving that every such representative has mass
\[
 V(u_e)=A-t^n\prod_j e_j.
\]
The strict current bound for $u_e$ gives $V(u_e)\geq(1-Ct)^nA>\delta$. Hence ordinary projection preserves both singularity volumes by \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}, and preserves the residual measure by \cref{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual}. Set $p_e=P_\omega[u_e]$. Since $\prod_ja_j=\prod_jb_j=1$, this proves the common residual and volume assertions for $p_a,p_b$.

The weights are positive and $g\leq0$, so pointwise, including the poles,
\[
 \max(h_a,h_b)=h_c,\qquad \max(u_a,u_b)=u_c.
\]
Projection invariance in \cref{prop:model-volume-separation} and the definition of the comparison now give
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_\omega(p_a,p_b)&=d_\omega(u_a,u_b)\\
 &=2\left(A-\tfrac12t^n\right)
       -(A-t^n)-(A-t^n)=t^n.
 \end{split}
\]
Finally the support and Lelong-number proof of \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness} applies with these positive weights. Every exceptional divisor mapping into $S$ maps to a point, and a comparison with $te_1g(z_1\circ\pi)$ gives zero divisorial Lelong number. Thus every first polar current is zero. The same comparison gives zero point Lelong numbers and trivial multiplier ideals at all weights. The strict volume deficit $t^n$ makes $p_a,p_b$ nonzero, whereas their ideal envelope is zero, so both are nonideal. Their distance is positive despite all the asserted common data.
\end{proof}
