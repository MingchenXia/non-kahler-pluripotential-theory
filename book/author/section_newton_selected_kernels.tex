\section{Newton selection and moment-shell limits}
\label{sec:newton-selected-kernels}
\index{Bergman kernel!Newton selection}

An explicit monomial selection gives a lower envelope bound without a
global approximation theorem. Its measure limit depends on the relative
dimension of the Newton polytope. In lower dimension it is carried by
real tori and differs from a mixed curvature measure of the envelope.

Let $X=(\mathbb P^1)^n$, $n\geq1$, and let
$L=\mathcal O(k_1,\ldots,k_n)$, where each $k_j$ is a positive integer.
Equip $L$ with its product Fubini--Study metric $h$. On
$T=(\mathbb C^*)^n$, in the standard frame $e$, write
\[
 r_j=\log|z_j|^2,\qquad
 \varphi_0(r)=\sum_jk_j\log(1+\mathrm e^{r_j}),\qquad
 \theta=c_1(L,h)=\ddc\varphi_0.
\]
Put $P=\prod_j[0,k_j]$. Fix a nonempty rational polytope
$Q\Subset\operatorname{int}P$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{aff}Q=b+W,\qquad b\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad W\text{ is a rational vector space}.
\]
Set $d=\dim Q$ and $\Lambda=W\cap\mathbb Z^n$. The relative volume
$\mathrm dq_\Lambda$ assigns volume one to a fundamental lattice cell;
when $d=0$ it assigns mass one to the singleton. Select precisely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-selected-spaces}
 V_m=\operatorname{span}\{z^\alpha e^m:
                         \alpha\in mQ\cap\mathbb Z^n\}.
\end{equation}
Every displayed section extends globally, since
$0\leq\alpha_j\leq mk_j$. These spaces form a graded series.

Choose a real smooth torus-invariant weight $w$ on $X$ such that
$F=\varphi_0+w$ has $D^2F(r)>0$ at every finite $r$.
Choose a smooth strictly positive torus-invariant probability $\nu$ on
the whole of $X$. Thus on $T$,
$\mathrm d\nu=a(r)\,\mathrm dr\,\mathrm d\tau$, where $a>0$,
$\int a\,\mathrm dr=1$, and $\mathrm d\tau$ is angular Haar probability.
Use the Hilbert norm $\int_X|s|_{h^m}^2\mathrm e^{-mw}\,\mathrm d\nu$.
For an orthonormal basis put
\[
 \mathcal B_m=\sum_j|s_{m,j}|_{h^m}^2,\qquad
 B_m=\mathrm e^{-mw}\mathcal B_m,\qquad
 U_m=m^{-1}\log\mathcal B_m.
\]
Here $B_m$ includes the continuous weight, as in
\cref{sec:partial-selected-space}. The closed Chern curvature $\theta$
and an auxiliary Hermitian form $\omega$ remain distinct.

\subsection{The growth constraint and its envelope}

Put $H_Q(r)=\max_{q\in Q}q\cdot r$ and define
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal C_Q&=\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):
     v+\varphi_0\leq H_Q+C_v\text{ on }T,\ C_v<\infty\},\\
 E_Q&=\left(\sup\{v\in\mathcal C_Q:
                  v\leq w\text{ quasi-everywhere on }X\}\right)^*.
\end{align*}
All competitors are genuine and not identically $-\infty$.
This growth constraint specifies the target of the series; an
identification with a multiplier-ideal envelope requires a separate proof.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:newton-growth-envelope}
The gradient $\nabla F:\mathbb R^n\to\operatorname{int}P$ is a smooth
diffeomorphism. With $r(q)=(\nabla F)^{-1}(q)$ and
$F^*(q)=q\cdot r(q)-F(r(q))$, set
\[
 G_Q(r)=\max_{q\in Q}\{q\cdot r-F^*(q)\}.
\]
Then $E_Q=G_Q-\varphi_0$ on $T$, with canonical value $-\infty$
on $X\setminus T$. Moreover, a global section $s$ of $L^m$ belongs
to $V_m$ exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-entire-growth}
 |s/e^m|^2\leq C_s\mathrm e^{mH_Q(r)}\quad\text{on }T
 \quad\text{for some finite }C_s.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Inverted product charts show that every global section is a polynomial
with degree at most $mk_j$ in $z_j$. The forward growth bound is the
triangle inequality. Conversely, angular averaging of that bound gives
$\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha|^2\mathrm e^{\alpha\cdot r}
\leq C_s\mathrm e^{mH_Q(r)}$. If a nonzero coefficient has
$\alpha\notin mQ$, separation and a ray $r=tu$ contradict this bound.
Thus the condition controls the whole section, including cancellation
between its monomials.

For $p\in\operatorname{int}P$, put
$\delta_p=\min_j(p_j,k_j-p_j)>0$. Boundedness of $w$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-coercivity}
 F(r)-p\cdot r\geq\delta_p\sum_j|r_j|-\|w\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
Strict convexity gives a unique minimizer, whose gradient is $p$.
Conversely, supporting planes and the asymptotic slopes of $\varphi_0$
put every gradient in $P$. The inverse function theorem makes the image
open, hence contained in its interior. This proves the diffeomorphism.
The inverse gradient and both first and second derivatives of $F^*$
are bounded on a neighborhood of $Q$; its Hessian is positive definite.

In a product chart with some coordinates inverted, the local potential
of $G_Q-\varphi_0$ is
\[
 \max_{q\in Q}\left\{\sum_j c_j(q)\log|\zeta_j|^2-F^*(q)\right\},
 \qquad c_j(q)=q_j\text{ or }k_j-q_j.
\]
All $c_j$ are bounded below by a fixed positive number. The expression
is locally bounded above, tends uniformly to $-\infty$ at coordinate
hyperplanes with the other coordinates bounded, and is locally
integrable by comparison with one fixed summand. A maximizing summand
gives the psh submean inequality at finite centers; at a $-\infty$
center it is automatic. The expressions glue. Boundedness of $F^*$
gives $G_Q-H_Q=O(1)$, and Fenchel's inequality gives $G_Q\leq F$.
Thus the candidate is an admissible competitor.

For any competitor $v$, the obstacle holds everywhere: its exceptional
pluripolar set is Lebesgue-null, and shrinking-ball submeans pass the
inequality with the continuous obstacle to every point. Take the circular
maximum
$M(r)=\max_\tau\{v(\mathrm e^{r/2}\tau)+\varphi_0(r)\}$.
Compactness gives upper semicontinuity. Rotating a maximizing center
gives the psh submean inequality for its invariant lift. This lift
extends across the affine coordinate hyperplanes by the local upper
bound. If $M(r)=-\infty$, successive one-variable maximum principles
force this psh function to be $-\infty$ on the corresponding polydisc,
contradicting nontriviality. Thus $M$ is finite. In logarithmic complex
coordinates its invariant lift depends only on real parts, so its
positive complex Hessian gives convexity of $M$.

We have $M\leq F$ and $M\leq H_Q+C_v$. Separation along rays puts
every supporting slope of $M$ in $Q$. At any $r$ choose such a slope
$p$. Convex duality then gives
$M(r)=p\cdot r-M^*(p)\leq p\cdot r-F^*(p)\leq G_Q(r)$.
This dominates $v$ on $T$. Shrinking-ball submeans and the uniform
boundary decay give the same domination at the boundary, with value
$-\infty$. The candidate is already canonical, proving the envelope
identity after upper regularization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Norms, lattice growth and the lower bound}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:newton-potential-lower-bound}
The norms are nondegenerate and satisfy weighted Bernstein--Markov.
Writing $N_m=\dim V_m$, we have
\[
 N_m=m^d\operatorname{vol}_\Lambda(Q)+O(m^{d-1})\quad(d\geq1),
 \qquad N_m=1\quad(d=0).
\]
For every sufficiently large degree,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-potential-error}
 |U_m-E_Q|\leq\frac{C}{m}
       \left(1+\sum_j|r_j|+\log m\right)\quad\text{on }T.
\end{equation}
Consequently $U_m\to E_Q$ locally uniformly on $T$, in every finite
$L^p$ against smooth volume on $X$, and weakly for the closed curvature
currents. Both canonical potentials are $-\infty$ on $X\setminus T$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The smooth positive volume makes the norm positive on every nonzero
section. Angular Fourier orthogonality diagonalizes it:
\begin{align*}
 A_m(\alpha)&=\|z^\alpha e^m\|_m^2
   =\int_{\mathbb R^n}\mathrm e^{\alpha\cdot r-mF(r)}a(r)\,\mathrm dr,\\
 \mathcal B_m(r)&=\sum_{\alpha\in mQ\cap\mathbb Z^n}
       \frac{\mathrm e^{\alpha\cdot r-m\varphi_0(r)}}{A_m(\alpha)}.
\end{align*}
For $q=\alpha/m$, the unique minimum of $F(r)-q\cdot r$ is
$-F^*(q)$ at $r(q)$. Taylor's formula on a ball of radius $m^{-1/2}$,
using uniform bounds on a compact neighborhood of $r(Q)$, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-laplace-bounds}
 c m^{-n/2}\mathrm e^{mF^*(q)}\leq A_m(\alpha)
                         \leq\mathrm e^{mF^*(q)}.
\end{equation}
The upper bound uses only the minimum and $\int a=1$.

The integral offset gives
$(m\operatorname{aff}Q)\cap\mathbb Z^n=mb+\Lambda$.
Fundamental cells in $m(Q-b)$ give the dimension estimate; cells
meeting its facets lie in a fixed-width boundary layer with count
$O(m^{d-1})$. They also give the lattice Riemann sums on $Q$.
Every $q\in Q$ is within $O(m^{-1})$ of
$Q_m=Q\cap(b+m^{-1}\Lambda)$: move it toward a fixed relative
interior point by $O(m^{-1})$ to fit a ball larger than the lattice
covering radius, then round inside that ball. This works in every
sufficiently large degree; for a singleton it is exact.

Put $G_{Q,m}=\max_{q\in Q_m}(q\cdot r-F^*(q))$. The grid estimate
gives $0\leq G_Q-G_{Q,m}\leq C(1+\sum_j|r_j|)/m$.
The exact kernel and \eqref{eq:newton-laplace-bounds} give
\[
 G_{Q,m}-\varphi_0\leq U_m\leq G_{Q,m}-\varphi_0+
     \frac{(n/2)\log m+\log N_m-\log c}{m}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:newton-potential-error}. The monomials vanish
at every toric boundary component. Logarithmic poles have finite
$L^p$ norm for each finite $p$ against smooth volume, giving the global
convergence; distributional differentiation gives current convergence.
No uniform two-sided error across the poles is asserted.

Fenchel's inequality also gives
$\sup_X B_m\leq C N_m m^{n/2}$. Cauchy--Schwarz in the orthonormal
basis proves
$\sup_X|s|_{h^m}\mathrm e^{-mw/2}
\leq C m^{d/2+n/4}\|s\|_m$.
Absorb the finitely many initial degrees in $C$; a polynomial bound
implies Bernstein--Markov with any positive exponential loss.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The measure and the ambient metric}
\index{Equilibrium measure!moment shell}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:newton-moment-shell-limit}
Against every continuous test function on $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-moment-shell}
 \frac{d!}{m^d}B_m\nu\rightharpoonup
 \mu_Q=\Psi_*(d!\,\mathrm dq_\Lambda\,\mathrm d\tau),
 \qquad \Psi(q,\tau)=\mathrm e^{r(q)/2}\tau.
\end{equation}
Its mass is $d!\operatorname{vol}_\Lambda(Q)>0$. In dimension zero it
is Haar probability on one real $n$-torus. If $d<n$, it is singular
against smooth ambient volume and
\[
 \left\langle(\theta+\ddc E_Q)^{d+1}\right\rangle=0.
\]
If $d=n$, it equals
$\langle(\theta+\ddc E_Q)^n\rangle$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each monomial gives a probability
$\mathrm dP_{m,q}=A_m(\alpha)^{-1}
\mathrm e^{m(q\cdot r-F(r))}a(r)\,\mathrm dr\,\mathrm d\tau$.
For every $\eta>0$ there is $c_\eta>0$ such that
$F(r)-q\cdot r+F^*(q)\geq c_\eta$ whenever
$|r-r(q)|\geq\eta$, uniformly for $q\in Q$.
On a large compact set use uniqueness of the minima; outside it use
\eqref{eq:newton-coercivity} uniformly on $Q$. Hence
$P_{m,q}\{|r-r(q)|\geq\eta\}\leq C m^{n/2}\mathrm e^{-mc_\eta}$.
Continuous test integrals converge uniformly to their angular average
at $r(q)$. The identity
$d!m^{-d}B_m\nu=d!m^{-d}\sum_\alpha P_{m,\alpha/m}$ and the
lattice Riemann sums prove \eqref{eq:newton-moment-shell}.

Uniform strong convexity of $F^*$ on $Q$ makes the maximizing slope
$q(r)$ unique. Its variational inequalities give
$(r-s)\cdot(q(r)-q(s))\geq c|q(r)-q(s)|^2$.
Thus $q$ is Lipschitz; comparison of maximizing values gives
$\nabla G_Q=q$. The function $G_Q$ is $C^{1,1}$, and its Hessian
has rank at most $d$ almost everywhere since $q(r)\in b+W$.
On logarithmic charts, convex mollifications converge in $C^1$,
their Hessians stay bounded and converge in every finite local $L^p$,
and their minors converge in $L^1$. Induction in the bounded-psh
product definition, with integration by parts, identifies the wedge
with these minors. The nonpluripolar extension agrees on $T$ and
puts no mass on its pluripolar complement. The rank bound proves the
vanishing assertion.

When $d=n$, the interior maximizer satisfies $r=\nabla F^*(q)$, so
$G_Q=F$ where $\nabla F(r)\in\operatorname{int}Q$.
Elsewhere the slope belongs to one of the finitely many facets. On
each facet level set its scalar normal component has derivative zero
almost everywhere: at a differentiability and density point, a nonzero
derivative would exclude a cone of positive density from that level set.
Consequently
$\det D^2G_Q=\boldsymbol1_{\{\nabla F(r)\in Q\}}\det D^2F$
almost everywhere. In our normalization
$(\ddc F)^n=n!\det D^2F(r)\,\mathrm dr\,\mathrm d\tau$.
Changing variables $q=\nabla F(r)$ gives the claimed top product.
\end{proof}

For any smooth positive Hermitian form $\omega$, the map $\Psi$
embeds $S_Q=\Psi(\operatorname{relint}Q\times(S^1)^n)$ as a real
$(n+d)$-dimensional submanifold. For $d=0$ use the single torus.
With $g_\omega(u,v)=\omega(u,Jv)$, define $J_\omega>0$ by
$\Psi^*\mathrm d\operatorname{vol}_{\omega,S_Q}
=J_\omega\,\mathrm dq_\Lambda\,\mathrm d\tau$.
The relative boundary has zero measure, and therefore
\[
 \mathrm d\mu_Q=\frac{d!}{J_\omega\circ\Psi^{-1}}
                    \,\mathrm d\operatorname{vol}_{\omega,S_Q}.
\]
Closedness of $\omega$ is unnecessary for this density formula.
If $d<n$, the mixed measure
$\langle(\theta+\ddc E_Q)^d\rangle\wedge\omega^{n-d}$ has locally
bounded densities on $T$ and gives $S_Q$ zero mass. It differs from
$\mu_Q$; for $d=0$ interpret the zeroth power as $1$.
The positive limit is independent of the invariant sampling probability,
although the finite-degree norms depend on it.

\subsection{A rank-one selection with nonclosed sampling}

\begin{example}\label{ex:newton-rational-map-kernels}
On $(\mathbb P^1)^2$ take $L=\mathcal O(4,4)$, $w=0$, and
$Q=\{(2+t,2-t):-1/2\leq t\leq1/2\}$.
Then $\Lambda=\mathbb Z(1,-1)$, $\mathrm dq_\Lambda=\mathrm dt$, and
\[
 V_m=\operatorname{span}\{z_1^{2m+j}z_2^{2m-j}e^m:
                          j\in\mathbb Z,\ |j|\leq m/2\},
 \qquad N_m=2\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1.
\]
The mass-one limit averages angular Haar probabilities on
$|z_1|^2=(2+t)/(2-t)$ and $|z_2|^2=(2-t)/(2+t)$ for this interval.
Indeed, these are the inverse gradient equations. The carrier is a
compact three-dimensional portion of $|z_1z_2|=1$.
Writing $c(p)=p\log(p/4)+(4-p)\log((4-p)/4)$ gives
$F^*(2+t,2-t)=2c(2+t)$, and hence an explicit envelope.

Adjacent section ratios are $z_1/z_2$. In homogeneous coordinates
$z_j=v_j/u_j$, the map $[v_1u_2:u_1v_2]$ is indeterminate at
$(0,0)$ and $(\infty,\infty)$. The limit is obtained by radial
concentration rather than by a globally defined submersion.

Let $\omega_0$ be the sum of the two Fubini--Study forms and set
$f=c|z_1|^2/(1+|z_1|^2)$ with $c\ne0$.
Then $\omega=\mathrm e^f\omega_0$ is smooth positive and invariant,
and $\mathrm d\omega=\mathrm e^f\mathrm df\wedge\omega_0\ne0$
where $\mathrm df\ne0$. Sampling by
$\nu=\omega^2/\int_X\omega^2$ gives the same mass-one limit.
The curvature remains $\theta=4\omega_0$.
\end{example}

The integral affine offset prevents a degree obstruction: a
half-integral singleton has no lattice point in every odd dilation.
The theorem guarantees nonzero spaces in every sufficiently large
degree. Polytopes touching the degree-box boundary, noninvariant
weights or sampling, and arbitrary multiplier-ideal series require
additional arguments. In particular the Newton-growth constraint
above alone does not identify the ideal envelope of
\cref{sec:hermitian-partial-envelopes}.
