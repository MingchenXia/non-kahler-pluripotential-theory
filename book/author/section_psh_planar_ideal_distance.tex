\subsection{The optimal constant in dimension one}
\label{subsec:psh-planar-ideal-distance}

In this subsection, $\Omega\subset\mathbb C$ is a pseudoconvex domain with Euclidean area $\mathrm{d}V$, and we use the weighted norms and spaces of global sections of \cref{subsec:psh-quantitative-ideals}. In one variable, every multiplier ideal is determined by vanishing orders at points. This allows us to combine the weights into a single one before applying the one-weight estimate, and leads to a better constant than the product bound.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal}\index{Multiplier ideal!on a plane domain}
For a subharmonic function $u$ on $\Omega$ and $x\in\Omega$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-multiplier-ideal}
 \mathcal I(u)_x=(z-x)^{\lfloor\nu(u,x)\rfloor}\mathcal O_{\Omega,x}.
\end{equation}
These ideals form a locally principal ideal sheaf.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With the normalization of $\ddc$ fixed in the conventions, $\mu=\ddc u$ is a positive Radon measure and $a=\mu(\{x\})=\nu(u,x)$. After removal of this atom, the distribution $u-a\log|z-x|^2$ has positive complex Hessian, so it has a subharmonic representative $v$, and $\ddc v$ has no atom at $x$. Since subharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere,
\[
 u=a\log|z-x|^2+v.
\]

We claim that, for each $t>0$, the function $\mathrm e^{-tv}$ is integrable on some neighborhood of $x$. Indeed, since $\ddc v$ has no atom at $x$, there is a disk $D=D(x,R)\Subset\Omega$ with $M=(\ddc v)(D)<1/t$. On $D$ write
\[
 v(z)=\int_D\log|z-y|^2\,\mathrm{d}(\ddc v)(y)+h(z),
\]
where $h$ is harmonic: the integral is locally integrable by Fubini's theorem and has complex Hessian $\ddc v$ on $D$, so it differs from $v$ by a harmonic distribution. If $M=0$, the claim follows from harmonicity. For $M>0$, Jensen's inequality for the probability measure $(\ddc v)/M$ gives
\[
 \exp\left(-t\int_D\log|z-y|^2\,\mathrm{d}(\ddc v)(y)\right)
 \leq\frac1M\int_D |z-y|^{-2tM}\,\mathrm{d}(\ddc v)(y).
\]
Since $tM<1$, the integral of $|z-y|^{-2tM}$ over $z\in D(x,R/2)$ is bounded uniformly in $y\in D$. The harmonic function $h$ is bounded below on this smaller disk, so Tonelli's theorem proves the claim. (Truncating the logarithmic kernel justifies Jensen's inequality at its poles.)

A nonzero holomorphic germ of order $k$ at $x$ is a unit times $(z-x)^k$. Since $v$ is locally bounded above, weighted integrability requires $k+1>a$; equality is excluded by the logarithmic divergence of the radial integral. Conversely, if $k+1>a$, choose $b>1$ such that $b(a-k)<1$. Then $|z-x|^{2k-2a}$ belongs locally to $L^b$, and $\mathrm e^{-v}$ belongs locally to $L^{b/(b-1)}$ by the claim, so H\"older's inequality proves integrability. Thus the germ belongs to $\mathcal I(u)_x$ exactly when $k\geq\lfloor a\rfloor$.

The points $y$ with $\nu(u,y)\geq1$ form a locally finite set, since each of them carries an atom of mass at least one of the locally finite measure $\ddc u$. Near any point, therefore, the ideal sheaf is generated by a single power of a coordinate, which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The distance condition below involves a sum of spaces of global sections. In one variable, this sum coincides with the space of global sections of the sum of the ideal sheaves.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:planar-global-ideal-sum}
If $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ are subharmonic functions on $\Omega$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-global-ideal-sum}
 \Gamma\left(\Omega,\sum_{i=1}^r\mathcal I(u_i)\right)
       =\sum_{i=1}^r\Gamma(\Omega,\mathcal I(u_i)).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $r=1$ there is nothing to prove, so let $r\geq2$. Put $J_i=\mathcal I(u_i)$ and $J=\sum_iJ_i$. By \cref{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal}, these sheaves are locally free of rank one. At each point, the order of $J$ is the smallest of the orders of the $J_i$, and a local generator of an ideal $J_i$ attaining this order also generates $J$. Consequently the sum map $\bigoplus_iJ_i\to J$ is locally split, and its kernel $E$ is a holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r-1$.

Fix $f\in\Gamma(\Omega,J)$. Gluing local holomorphic lifts of $f$ with a smooth partition of unity gives a smooth section $s$ of $\bigoplus_iJ_i$ whose image is $f$. Since $f$ is holomorphic, $\beta=\bar\partial s$ is an $E$-valued $(0,1)$-form. It suffices to solve $\bar\partial v=\beta$ for a section $v$ of $E$, for then $s-v$ is a holomorphic lift of $f$.

We solve this equation using a bundle metric of positive curvature. Choose a smooth strictly subharmonic exhaustion function $\rho\geq1$ of $\Omega$. The K\"ahler metric
\[
 \omega_*=\mathrm i\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z
                   +\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\rho^2
\]
is complete: it dominates $2\mathrm i\partial\rho\wedge\bar\partial\rho$, so $\rho$ has bounded gradient with respect to it, and therefore every curve leaving all compact subsets has infinite length.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Equip $E$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h$. Choose a smooth increasing convex function $\chi$ on $[1,+\infty)$ so that the metric $h_\chi=h\mathrm e^{-\chi(\rho)}$ has
\[
 \mathrm i\Theta_{E,h_\chi}\geq\omega_*\operatorname{Id}_E,
 \qquad \int_\Omega |\beta|_{h_\chi,\omega_*}^2\,\mathrm{d}V_{\omega_*}<+\infty.
\]
These two requirements can be imposed successively on the compact sublevel sets of $\rho$: increase $\chi'$ so that $\chi'(\rho)$ times the positive Hessian of $\rho$ dominates the negative part of the curvature, and increase $\chi$ so that the integral over each shell $\{m\leq\rho<m+1\}$ is at most $2^{-m}$. Increasing the slopes preserves the inequalities already achieved.

Use the global frame $\mathrm{d}z$ of the canonical bundle $K_\Omega$ to identify $E$-valued $(0,q)$-forms with $(E\otimes K_\Omega^{-1})$-valued $(1,q)$-forms, and give $K_\Omega^{-1}$ its flat coordinate metric. Its curvature is zero, and $\omega_*$ dominates the Euclidean metric, so the identified form $\beta$ remains $L^2$. The Bochner--Kodaira inequality for compactly supported forms $w$ of type $(1,1)$ gives
\[
 \|\bar\partial^*w\|^2\geq
       \langle[\mathrm i\Theta_{E,h_\chi},\Lambda_{\omega_*}]w,w\rangle
       \geq\|w\|^2.
\]
Here we used that there are no $(1,2)$-forms. By completeness, cutoffs and smoothing extend this inequality to the domain of the adjoint. Since $\beta$ is $L^2$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality bounds the functional $w\mapsto\langle w,\beta\rangle$ by $\|\beta\|\|\bar\partial^*w\|$, and the Riesz representation theorem gives a distributional solution $v$ of $\bar\partial v=\beta$. Thus $s-v$ is distributionally holomorphic, hence holomorphic in every bundle chart, and it is the required global lift. No global weighted norm condition is imposed on its components.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:planar-optimal-ideal-distance}\index{Multiplier ideal!optimal planar distance}
Let $\psi$ and $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r$ be subharmonic functions on $\Omega$, with $r\geq1$ and $\phi_i<0$, and choose $p_i>1$. For holomorphic $f$ with $I=\|f\|_{\psi+\sum_i\phi_i}^2<+\infty$, define
\[
 c=\inf\left\{\|g\|_\psi^2:g\in\mathcal O(\Omega),\quad
 f-g\in\sum_{i=1}^r\Gamma(\Omega,\mathcal I(p_i\phi_i+\psi))\right\},
 \qquad s=\sum_{i=1}^r\frac1{p_i}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-optimal-ideal-distance}
 c\leq\max\{1-s,0\}\,I.
\end{equation}
For every fixed choice of the exponents, this constant is optimal over the stated class of data. If $s\geq1$, the function $g=0$ is admissible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $J_i=\mathcal I(p_i\phi_i+\psi)$ and $J=\sum_iJ_i$. At a point $x$, write $A=\nu(\psi,x)$ and $B_i=\nu(\phi_i,x)$. By \cref{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal}, the order of $J_x$ is $\min_i\lfloor A+p_iB_i\rfloor$.

Suppose first that $s<1$, and put $P=1/s>1$. The numbers $P/p_i$ sum to one, so
\[
 \min_i p_iB_i\leq\sum_i\frac{P}{p_i}p_iB_i=P\sum_iB_i.
\]
Taking integer parts and using \cref{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal} gives $\mathcal I(P\sum_i\phi_i+\psi)\subseteq J$. By \cref{lem:planar-global-ideal-sum}, every global section of this smaller ideal sheaf lies in $\sum_i\Gamma(\Omega,J_i)$, so every remainder admissible for the one-weight problem is admissible for $c$. \Cref{prop:one-weight-ideal-distance} with $\phi=\sum_i\phi_i$ and $p=P$ therefore gives $c\leq(1-1/P)I=(1-s)I$.

Now let $s\geq1$. Since $I<+\infty$, we have $f\in\Gamma(\Omega,\mathcal I(\psi+\sum_i\phi_i))$. If $f\equiv0$ there is nothing to prove; otherwise let $k$ be its order at $x$. Then $k+1>A+\sum_iB_i$. If its germ did not belong to $J_x$, we would have $k+1\leq A+p_iB_i$ for every $i$. With $L=k+1-A>0$, this would give $\sum_iB_i\geq sL\geq L$, a contradiction. Hence $f\in\Gamma(\Omega,J)$, and \cref{lem:planar-global-ideal-sum} shows that $g=0$ is admissible.

For sharpness when $s<1$, take the unit disk, $\psi=0$, $f=1$, and $\phi_i=p_i^{-1}\log|z|^2$. Then $J_i=(z)$ for all $i$, so an admissible $g$ has $g(0)=1$. Orthogonality of the monomials on the disk gives $c=\pi$, attained by $g=1$, whereas $I=\pi/(1-s)$. If $s\geq1$, keep these weights and take $f=z^q$ with an integer $q\geq0$ satisfying $q+1>s$. Then $I=\pi/(q+1-s)>0$ and $c=0$, so the constant $0$ cannot be replaced by a negative number.
\end{proof}

Consequently, for $r\geq2$ the product constant of \cref{thm:product-ideal-distance} is not optimal on plane domains. The argument above relies on all local ideals being principal; it does not determine the optimal constant when $n\geq2$ and $r>n$.
