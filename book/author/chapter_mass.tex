\chapter{Torsion and the variation of mass}
\label{chap:mass}

Throughout this chapter, $\omega$ is a Hermitian form on $X$. If $\omega$ were closed, Stokes' theorem would make the total Monge--Amp\`ere mass $\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^n$ independent of the potential $u$. For a nonclosed Hermitian form this invariance can fail. Integration by parts expresses the mass of a smooth potential as a polynomial whose coefficients pair the potential with the complex Hessians of the powers of $\omega$, and polarization shows that the vanishing of these Hessians is also necessary for invariance. When they do not vanish, even smooth potentials of the same singularity type can have different masses, and the usual global comparison principle fails. The masses may nevertheless remain bounded, and whether they do depends only on the manifold, not on the chosen Hermitian form.

\section{Integration by parts with a nonclosed form}

Integration by parts moves the complex Hessian of a function onto the form against which it is integrated. When that form is a power of a nonclosed $(1,1)$-form, its complex Hessian contains, besides the complex Hessian of the form itself, a quadratic term in its exterior derivative.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ibp}\index{Integration by parts}\index{Torsion!integration by parts}
For a smooth real function $f$ and a smooth form $T$ of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$,
\[
 \int_X\ddc f\wedge T=\int_X f\ddc T.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\dc f$ is a one-form, $\mathrm{d}(\dc f\wedge T)=\ddc f\wedge T-\dc f\wedge\mathrm{d}T$, and Stokes' theorem gives the first equality in
\[
 \int_X\ddc f\wedge T
 =\int_X\dc f\wedge \mathrm{d}T
 =\int_X f\ddc T.
\]
For the second, recall that the integral of $\dc\sigma$ vanishes for every form $\sigma$ of total degree $2n-1$, by Stokes' theorem for $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$. We apply this to $\sigma=f\,\mathrm{d}T$ and use $\dc\mathrm{d}=-\mathrm{d}\dc$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:power-torsion}
For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ and an integer $q\geq1$,
\[
 \ddc(\theta^q)
 =q\,\theta^{q-1}\wedge\ddc\theta
 +q(q-1)\,\theta^{q-2}\wedge \mathrm{d}\theta\wedge\dc\theta,
\]
where the second term is omitted when $q=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $q=1$ there is nothing to prove. For $q\geq2$, since $\theta$ has even degree, $\dc(\theta^q)=q\,\theta^{q-1}\wedge\dc\theta$. Applying $\mathrm{d}$ and using again that $\theta^{q-1}$ has even degree, we obtain
\[
 \mathrm{d}\dc(\theta^q)
 =q(q-1)\,\theta^{q-2}\wedge \mathrm{d}\theta\wedge\dc\theta
 +q\,\theta^{q-1}\wedge \mathrm{d}\dc\theta.
\]
\end{proof}

Thus the quadratic torsion term $\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\dc\omega$ can make the higher powers of $\omega$ fail to be $\ddc$-closed even when $\ddc\omega=0$. The mass expansion below involves the complex Hessians of all these powers separately.

\section{The mass polynomial}

For a smooth real function $u$ on $X$, recall the notation $\omega_u=\omega+\ddc u$ of \cref{sec:psh-bounded-products}, and set
\[
 \HH_\omega=\{u\in C^\infty(X,\R):\omega+\ddc u>0\},
 \qquad \Mass_\omega(u)=\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^n.
\]
The set $\HH_\omega$ of smooth strictly $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions is convex and open in the $C^2$ topology, while the mass $\Mass_\omega(u)$ is defined for every smooth real $u$. Expanding it along a smooth direction gives a polynomial whose nonconstant coefficients detect the complex Hessians of the powers of the background.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mass-expansion}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!variation polynomial}
For every smooth real function $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass-expansion}
 \Mass_\omega(u)=\int_X\omega^n+
 \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom nk
 \int_X u(\ddc u)^{k-1}\wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-k}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expand $(\omega+\ddc u)^n$ by the binomial theorem. The term with $k=n$ integrates to zero, since it is exact:
\[
 (\ddc u)^n=\mathrm{d}\bigl(\dc u\wedge(\ddc u)^{n-1}\bigr).
\]
For $1\leq k\leq n-1$, apply \cref{lem:ibp} with $f=u$ and
$T=(\ddc u)^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}$. Since both $\mathrm{d}$ and $\dc$ annihilate $\ddc u$,
\[
 \ddc T=(\ddc u)^{k-1}\wedge\ddc(\omega^{n-k}),
\]
which gives the $k$th term of \eqref{eq:mass-expansion}. When $n=1$, the sum is empty and the mass is constant.
\end{proof}

If every $\ddc(\omega^j)$ with $1\leq j\leq n-1$ vanishes, \eqref{eq:mass-expansion} shows that the mass is constant. Conversely, varying the potential in several independent directions recovers each of these forms by polarization.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Chiose]\label{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!invariance}
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^n$ is independent of $u\in\HH_\omega$;
\item $\ddc(\omega^j)=0$ for $1\leq j\leq n-1$;
\item $\ddc\omega=0$ and $\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\dc\omega=0$.
\end{enumerate}
They are also equivalent to the condition that $\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^n$ be the same for all bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions $u$, where $(\omega+\ddc u)^n$ is the Bedford--Taylor product of \cref{sec:psh-bounded-products}. Under these conditions the common mass is $\int_X\omega^n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the first condition implies the second.} Let $u$ be a smooth real function. For $|t|$ small, $tu$ belongs to $\HH_\omega$ for both signs of $t$, so the polynomial $t\mapsto\int_X(\omega+t\ddc u)^n$ is constant on an interval and its nonconstant coefficients vanish. For $1\leq j\leq n-1$ this gives
\[
 \int_X\omega^j\wedge(\ddc u)^{n-j}=0.
\]
Replace $u$ by a linear combination of $n-j$ smooth real functions $u_1,\ldots,u_{n-j}$ and compare the coefficients of the monomial in which every parameter appears once. We obtain
\[
 \int_X\omega^j\wedge\ddc u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{n-j}=0.
\]
Integrating by parts in the first factor and using that $u_1$ is arbitrary, we obtain the pointwise identity
\[
 \ddc(\omega^j)\wedge\ddc u_2\wedge\cdots\wedge\ddc u_{n-j}=0
\]
for every choice of the remaining functions. At a given point, the complex Hessians of these functions can be prescribed arbitrarily: multiply a quadratic polynomial in a coordinate chart by a cutoff function equal to one near that point. Since the identity is multilinear, we may complexify it and use the quadratic monomials $z_a\bar z_b$. Wedge products of their complex Hessians give all coordinate forms of the complementary bidegree, and pairing with these forms shows that every coefficient of $\ddc(\omega^j)$ vanishes. When $j=n-1$ there are no remaining factors, and the same conclusion follows directly from the arbitrariness of $u_1$. This proves the second condition.

\emph{Step 2: the remaining equivalences for smooth potentials.} The second condition implies the first by \cref{thm:mass-expansion}. For $n\geq3$, the identities for $j=1,2$ and \cref{lem:power-torsion} give $\ddc\omega=0$ and
\[
 0=\ddc(\omega^2)=2\omega\wedge\ddc\omega
                         +2\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\dc\omega,
\]
so the second condition implies the third. For $n=2$, the second condition is $\ddc\omega=0$, while $\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\dc\omega$ vanishes for degree reasons; for $n=1$, both forms in the third condition vanish for degree reasons. In both cases the implication is immediate. Conversely, \cref{lem:power-torsion} derives all the identities of the second condition from the third.

\emph{Step 3: bounded potentials.} Assume the first condition and let $u$ be a bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function. By \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization}, there are uniformly bounded functions $u_j\in\HH_\omega$ decreasing to $u$. Their masses all equal $\int_X\omega^n$, and the continuity of the bounded products of \cref{sec:psh-bounded-products} along such sequences passes this common value to the limit. Conversely, every element of $\HH_\omega$ is a bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic function, so constancy for bounded potentials implies the first condition. The zero potential shows that the common mass is $\int_X\omega^n$.
\end{proof}

In particular, a closed background has invariant mass. Under the equivalent conditions above, \eqref{eq:mass-expansion} even gives $\Mass_\omega(u)=\int_X\omega^n$ for every smooth real $u$, not only for $u\in\HH_\omega$.

Mass invariance can also hold in a nonclosed background. In \cref{thm:fixed-determinant-nonclosed-degeneration}, a pluriclosed metric with nonzero exterior derivative satisfies the second condition of \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}, so all bounded potentials have the same mass in this background, although the perturbed forms of a family of smooth potentials have least eigenvalue, relative to a flat metric, tending to zero.

The first variation of the mass involves only the complex Hessian of the power $\omega_u^{n-1}$ of the perturbed form.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mass-variation}
For smooth real functions $u,v$ on $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass-first-variation}
 \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\Mass_\omega(u+tv)
 =n\int_X v\ddc(\omega_u^{n-1}).
\end{equation}
If $\Mass_\omega$ is constant on $\HH_\omega$, then $\ddc(\omega_u^{n-1})=0$ for every $u\in\HH_\omega$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\Mass_\omega(u+tv)$ is a polynomial in $t$, its derivative at $t=0$ is
\[
 n\int_X\ddc v\wedge\omega_u^{n-1},
\]
and \cref{lem:ibp} turns this into the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:mass-first-variation}. For the second assertion, fix $u\in\HH_\omega$. For every smooth real direction $v$, the segment $u+tv$ lies in $\HH_\omega$ when $|t|$ is sufficiently small, so the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:mass-first-variation} vanishes for all smooth real $v$. Hence the smooth top-degree form $\ddc(\omega_u^{n-1})$ vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:surface-mass}
If $n=2$, then for every smooth real function $u$ on $X$,
\[
 \Mass_\omega(u)=\int_X\omega^2+2\int_Xu\ddc\omega.
\]
Consequently, $\Mass_\omega$ is constant on $\HH_\omega$ if and only if $\ddc\omega=0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The formula is the case $n=2$ of \eqref{eq:mass-expansion}, and the equivalence is the case $n=2$ of \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}.
\end{proof}

The identity $\ddc(\omega^{n-1})=0$ makes the first variation vanish at $u=0$; Hermitian forms satisfying it are the Gauduchon metrics of \cref{chap:gauduchon}. On a surface this identity already gives mass invariance. In dimension three, however, \eqref{eq:mass-expansion} reads
\[
 \Mass_\omega(u)=\int_X\omega^3
 +3\int_Xu\ddc(\omega^2)
 +3\int_Xu\ddc u\wedge\ddc\omega.
\]
Vanishing of $\ddc(\omega^2)$ removes the linear term, while $\ddc\omega$ still governs the quadratic term. Thus stationarity of the mass at one potential must be distinguished from its invariance on $\HH_\omega$; an explicit example is given in \cref{ex:gauduchon-persistence-torus}.

\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
\section{Mass variation on a complex torus}

By \cref{cor:surface-mass}, the mass on a surface varies precisely through the pairing $2\int_Xu\ddc\omega$ of the potential with the complex Hessian of the background. A product of elliptic curves gives an explicit background and a family of smooth potentials for which this pairing is nonzero.

\begin{example}\label{ex:torus-mass}\index{Torus!varying mass}
Let $X=(\C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z))^2$. For $j=1,2$, let $z_j=x_j+\mathrm i y_j$ be the standard coordinates on the universal cover $\C^2$, with $x_j,y_j$ real, and put
\[
 \beta_j=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{d}z_j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z_j.
\]
The forms $\beta_j$ descend to $X$. For a real parameter $t$, define
\[
 b(x_1)=2+\cos(2\pi x_1),\qquad
 \omega=\beta_1+b(x_1)\beta_2,\qquad
 u_t=t\cos(2\pi x_1).
\]
Since $1\leq b\leq3$, the form $\omega$ is Hermitian. It is not closed, because $\mathrm{d}\omega=\mathrm{d}b\wedge\beta_2$ is not identically zero.

For a function $f$ of $x_1$ alone, one has $\ddc f=(f''/4)\beta_1$, where the prime denotes differentiation in $x_1$. Hence
\[
 \omega_{u_t}
 =\bigl(1-\pi^2t\cos(2\pi x_1)\bigr)\beta_1+b\beta_2>0
 \quad\text{if}\quad |t|<\pi^{-2}.
\]
Writing $B=\int_X\beta_1\wedge\beta_2>0$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:torus-mass}
 \Mass_\omega(u_t)=(4-\pi^2t)B.
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
 \omega_{u_t}^2
 =2\bigl(1-\pi^2t\cos(2\pi x_1)\bigr)
       \bigl(2+\cos(2\pi x_1)\bigr)\beta_1\wedge\beta_2,
\]
and over a period the average of $\cos(2\pi x_1)$ is zero while that of its square is $1/2$.
\end{example}

Although the complex torus is K\"ahler, the background $\omega$ chosen above is not closed, and this already allows the mass to vary. On non-K\"ahler manifolds even an upper bound for the mass can fail; the Hopf manifolds of \cref{chap:hopf} give examples.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:mass-not-singularity-invariant}\index{Singularity type!variation of mass}
In general, the total Monge--Amp\`ere mass is not determined by the ordinary singularity type or by the multiplier ideals, even among smooth strictly $\omega$-plurisubharmonic potentials.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $|t|<\pi^{-2}$, the potentials $u_t$ of \cref{ex:torus-mass} belong to $\HH_\omega$. They are smooth on the compact manifold $X$, so any two of them differ by a bounded function and have the same singularity type in the sense of \cref{sec:psh-singularity-types}. For the same reason, their multiplier ideals are the structure sheaf for every positive multiple of the weight. By \eqref{eq:torus-mass}, their masses differ.
\end{proof}

\section{Comparison and mass invariance}

The global comparison principle of K\"ahler geometry compares the Monge--Amp\`ere measures of two potentials on the set where one lies below the other. Adding a constant to a potential leaves its Monge--Amp\`ere measure unchanged, and after a large constant shift this set becomes the whole manifold. Such an inequality would therefore compare total masses, and the varying mass of \cref{ex:torus-mass} rules it out.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:comparison-forces-mass}\index{Comparison principle!mass obstruction}
Suppose that every pair $u,v\in\HH_\omega$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:naive-comparison}
 \int_{\{u<v\}}\omega_v^n
 \leq\int_{\{u<v\}}\omega_u^n.
\end{equation}
Then $\Mass_\omega$ is constant on $\HH_\omega$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $u,v\in\HH_\omega$. By compactness there is a constant $C$ such that $u<v+C$ everywhere. Since $\omega_{v+C}=\omega_v$, applying \eqref{eq:naive-comparison} to $u$ and $v+C$ gives $\Mass_\omega(v)\leq\Mass_\omega(u)$. Exchanging the roles of $u$ and $v$ gives the opposite inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:comparison-fails}
There is a Hermitian form $\omega$ on a compact complex manifold for which \eqref{eq:naive-comparison} fails for some pair $u,v\in\HH_\omega$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the form $\omega$ of \cref{ex:torus-mass}, $\Mass_\omega$ is not constant on $\HH_\omega$ by \eqref{eq:torus-mass}; apply \cref{prop:comparison-forces-mass}.
\end{proof}

The modified comparison principle of \cref{thm:modified-comparison} compensates for the derivatives of the background. Conversely, under the equivalent conditions of \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}, the ordinary comparison principle holds for bounded potentials, by \cref{uni-thm:GL-equivalence}.

\section{Uniform mass bounds}

Although the mass varies, it may remain bounded. Set
\[
 \Vsm(\omega)=\sup_{u\in\HH_\omega}\Mass_\omega(u)\in(0,+\infty].
\]
Comparing two Hermitian forms and rescaling the potentials shows that the finiteness of this supremum depends only on $X$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:smooth-bmp-metric}
If $\eta$ is another Hermitian form and $\omega\leq C\eta$ for a constant $C>0$, then
\[
 \Vsm(\omega)\leq C^n\Vsm(\eta).
\]
In particular, finiteness of $\Vsm$ is independent of the Hermitian form.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $u\in\HH_\omega$. Since $C\eta+\ddc u\geq\omega+\ddc u>0$, the function $u/C$ belongs to $\HH_\eta$. The top exterior power is monotone on positive $(1,1)$-forms: for positive Hermitian matrices $A\leq B$, all eigenvalues of $A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}$ are at least one, so $\det A\leq\det B$. Applying this pointwise gives
\[
 \int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^n
 \leq C^n\int_X\bigl(\eta+\ddc(u/C)\bigr)^n
 \leq C^n\Vsm(\eta).
\]
Taking the supremum over $u$ proves the inequality. Since any two Hermitian forms on the compact manifold $X$ are comparable in both directions, the last assertion follows.
\end{proof}

The smooth supremum considered here is also the upper mass over all bounded $\omega$-plurisubharmonic potentials: \cref{prop:bmp-metric} proves this using approximation by smooth strict potentials and continuity of bounded products. \Cref{chap:products} then passes to truncations of unbounded potentials. By \cref{cor:bmp-characterization}, finiteness of this common supremum is equivalent to finiteness of the extended non-pluripolar mass of every $\omega$-plurisubharmonic potential.
