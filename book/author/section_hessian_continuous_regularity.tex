\section{Continuous equations and regularity}
\label{sec:hessian-continuous-regularity}
\index{Hessian equation!regularity of continuous solutions}

The proper equation of \cref{hessian:smooth-proper-existence} is stable
in the uniform norm, so its smooth solutions extend to continuous data.
This also identifies the limit of the auxiliary equations with a
prescribed continuous weak solution.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{hessian:proper-uniform-stability}
Let $a>0$ and let smooth $H_j$ converge uniformly to a continuous
$H$. The smooth solutions of $H_m(w_j)=e^{aw_j+H_j}\omega^n$
converge uniformly to the unique continuous admissible solution of
$H_m(w)=e^{aw+H}\omega^n$. For two continuous data $H,\widetilde H$,
the corresponding solutions $w,\widetilde w$ satisfy
\[
 \|w-\widetilde w\|_\infty\leq a^{-1}\|H-\widetilde H\|_\infty.
\]
They also satisfy
$-\max_XH/a\leq w\leq-\min_XH/a$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Smooth solutions exist by \cref{hessian:smooth-proper-existence}.
For smooth data, at a maximum of $w-\widetilde w$, monotonicity of the
smooth Hessian operator gives
$e^{aw+H}\leq e^{a\widetilde w+\widetilde H}$. Hence
$a(w-\widetilde w)\leq\widetilde H-H$ there. Exchanging the functions
gives the stated norm estimate for smooth data. Thus a uniformly
convergent sequence of smooth data gives a uniformly Cauchy
sequence of solutions. Its limit is admissible because the linear
inequalities \eqref{eq:hessian-continuous-admissibility} pass to
distributional limits, and \cref{hessian:continuous-products}
passes the equation to the limit. The bounds follow from those
for the smooth solutions. Taking limits in the smooth norm estimate
proves it for continuous data. Finally,
\cref{hessian:hessian-exponential-order} in both directions gives
uniqueness among continuous admissible solutions.
\end{proof}

For smooth positive densities depending on the potential, the estimate
of \cref{sec:hessian-trace} gives complex-Hessian bounds independent of
derivatives of the prescribed approximation. The elliptic extension then
upgrades the continuous solution to a classical one.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{hessian:continuous-hessian-regularity}
If $\varphi$ is continuous and admissible on $X$, $f(x,t)$ is smooth
on a neighborhood of the compact set $X\times\varphi(X)$, and
\[
 H_m(\varphi)=e^{f(x,\varphi)}\omega^n,
\]
then $\varphi$ is smooth.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose smooth admissible $v_j\to\varphi$ uniformly, by \cref{hessian:continuous-smooth-approximation}. After discarding finitely many terms, their values lie in a fixed compact interval $I$ such that $f$ is
smooth on a neighborhood of $X\times I$. Put $h(x,t)=-t+f(x,t)$ and solve
\[
 H_m(u_j)=e^{u_j+h(x,v_j)}\omega^n.
\]
\Cref{hessian:proper-uniform-stability}, with $a=1$, shows that $u_j$ converges uniformly
to a continuous admissible $u$ satisfying
\[
 e^{-u}H_m(u)=e^{-\varphi+f(x,\varphi)}\omega^n
                              =e^{-\varphi}H_m(\varphi).
\]
\Cref{hessian:hessian-exponential-order}, applied in both directions, gives $u=\varphi$.

The zero-order bounds of \cref{hessian:proper-uniform-stability} for $u_j$ are uniform in $j$. Fix $M\geq1$ bounding them
and choose $B>\|\varphi\|_\infty+1$. Modify $h$ outside a neighborhood of $X\times\varphi(X)$ so that it is smooth on a neighborhood of $X\times[-B-1,B+1]$; this does not change the equations for large $j$. The tolerance $\varepsilon$ in
\cref{hessian:prescribed-potential-trace} depends on $M$, $B$ and
$h$, but not on derivatives of the smooth comparison function chosen next.
Choose a fixed smooth $\widetilde\varphi$ with
$\|\widetilde\varphi-\varphi\|_\infty<\varepsilon/2$ and
$\|\widetilde\varphi\|_\infty\leq B$.
For large $j$, $\|v_j-\widetilde\varphi\|_\infty<\varepsilon$.
\Cref{hessian:prescribed-potential-complex-hessian} then gives uniform gradient
and complex-Hessian bounds for $u_j$, independent of derivatives of
$v_j$. \Cref{hessian:complex-hessian-compactness} yields
$\varphi\in W^{2,p}$ for every finite $p$, and
$\varphi\in C^{1,\alpha}$ for every $\alpha<1$.

\Cref{hessian:proper-auxiliary-limit} makes $\varphi$ a continuous viscosity
solution of the concave uniformly elliptic extension, with right-hand
side $e^{f(x,\varphi)/m}$. This right-hand side is H\"older continuous, since $\varphi\in C^{1,\alpha}$. The analytic estimate in \cref{sec:hessian-complex-limits}, which applies directly to this viscosity limit, therefore gives
$\varphi\in C^{2,\beta}$, for some $\beta>0$.
In the proof of \cref{hessian:proper-auxiliary-limit}, the matrix $A$
of $\omega+\ddc\varphi$ lies almost everywhere in the compact set $K$
of \cref{hessian:uniform-elliptic-extension}; it is now continuous, so
it lies in $K$ everywhere. The extension agrees with the Hessian root
$F$ on $K$. The equation is therefore classical, depends smoothly on
$x$, $\varphi$ and the complex Hessian of $\varphi$, and is uniformly
elliptic near $K$.
Difference quotients and interior Schauder estimates give $C^{3,\beta}$;
iteration proves smoothness.
\end{proof}

The proper equation can now be solved when its exponent depends
smoothly on the unknown potential, provided that constant barriers
bound the desired range and the dependence is strictly increasing.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{hessian:proper-variable-existence}\index{Hessian equation!strictly increasing exponent}
Let $A<B$ and let $f$ be smooth on a neighborhood of
$X\times[A,B]$, with $\partial_tf>0$ on $X\times[A,B]$ and, for every
$x\in X$,
\[
 f(x,A)\leq0\leq f(x,B).
\]
There is a unique continuous admissible solution with values in
$[A,B]$ of
\[
 H_m(w)=e^{f(x,w)}\omega^n.
\]
This solution is smooth and strictly admissible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By compactness there are $0<\kappa\leq L$ with
$\kappa\leq\partial_tf\leq L$ on $X\times[A,B]$. Choose $a\geq L$ and let
$\mathcal C=\{v\in C(X):A\leq v\leq B\}$, a complete subset of
$C(X)$ in the uniform norm. For $v\in\mathcal C$ put
$H_v(x)=f(x,v(x))-av(x)$ and let $T(v)$ be the continuous proper
solution with exponent $aT(v)+H_v$, furnished by
\cref{hessian:proper-uniform-stability}. The function $f(x,t)-at$
is nonincreasing in $t$, so
\[
 -aB\leq f(x,B)-aB\leq H_v(x)
                   \leq f(x,A)-aA\leq-aA.
\]
The zero-order bounds in that proposition give $A\leq T(v)\leq B$.
For $v,z\in\mathcal C$, the mean-value theorem and the stability
estimate give
\[
 \|H_v-H_z\|_\infty\leq(a-\kappa)\|v-z\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 \|T(v)-T(z)\|_\infty
          \leq(1-\kappa/a)\|v-z\|_\infty.
\]
Since $0\leq1-\kappa/a<1$, the contraction mapping theorem gives a unique
fixed point $w\in\mathcal C$. It is admissible because it equals
$T(w)$, and its equation is exactly the stated equation.
Conversely, a continuous admissible solution $\widetilde w$ with values
in $[A,B]$ solves the continuous proper equation with data
$H_{\widetilde w}$. Uniqueness for that equation gives
$T(\widetilde w)=\widetilde w$, so $\widetilde w=w$.
This proves uniqueness in the stated class.
\Cref{hessian:continuous-hessian-regularity} makes $w$ smooth.
The form $\omega+\ddc w$ lies in $\overline{\Gamma_m(\omega)}$ and has positive
$m$th symmetric polynomial; Maclaurin's inequalities then put it in
$\Gamma_m(\omega)$. Thus $w$ is strictly admissible.
\end{proof}
