\section{Continuous equations and regularity}
\label{sec:hessian-continuous-regularity}
\index{Hessian equation!regularity of continuous solutions}

The proper equation is stable in the uniform norm, so its smooth solutions
extend to continuous data. This also identifies the limit of the
auxiliary equations with a prescribed continuous weak solution.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{hessian:proper-uniform-stability}
Let $a>0$ and let smooth $H_j$ converge uniformly to a continuous
$H$. The smooth solutions of $H_m(w_j)=e^{aw_j+H_j}\omega^n$
converge uniformly to the unique continuous admissible solution of
$H_m(w)=e^{aw+H}\omega^n$. For two continuous data $H_1,H_2$,
the corresponding solutions satisfy
\[
 \|w_1-w_2\|_\infty\leq a^{-1}\|H_1-H_2\|_\infty.
\]
They also satisfy
$-\max_XH/a\leq w\leq-\min_XH/a$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Smooth solutions exist by \cref{hessian:smooth-proper-existence}.
At a maximum of $w_1-w_2$, monotonicity of the smooth Hessian
operator gives $e^{aw_1+H_1}\leq e^{aw_2+H_2}$. Hence
$a(w_1-w_2)\leq H_2-H_1$ there. Exchanging the functions gives
the stated norm estimate for smooth data. Thus a uniformly
convergent sequence of smooth data gives a uniformly Cauchy
sequence of solutions. Its limit is admissible by the linear
distributional inequalities, and \cref{hessian:continuous-products}
passes the equation to the limit. The bounds follow from those
for the smooth solutions. Taking limits in the smooth norm estimate
proves it for continuous data. Finally,
\cref{hessian:hessian-exponential-order} in both directions gives
uniqueness among continuous admissible solutions.
\end{proof}

For smooth positive densities depending on the potential, the estimate
of \cref{sec:hessian-trace} gives complex-Hessian bounds independent of
derivatives of the prescribed approximation. The elliptic extension then
upgrades the continuous solution to a classical one.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{hessian:continuous-hessian-regularity}
If $\varphi$ is continuous and admissible on $X$, $f(x,t)$ is smooth
on a neighborhood of its bounded range, and
\[
 H_m(\varphi)=e^{f(x,\varphi)}\omega^n,
\]
then $\varphi$ is smooth.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose smooth admissible $v_j\to\varphi$ uniformly, by \cref{hessian:continuous-smooth-approximation}. Their values lie in a fixed compact interval on which $f$ is
smooth. Put $h(x,t)=-t+f(x,t)$ and solve
\[
 H_m(u_j)=e^{u_j+h(x,v_j)}\omega^n.
\]
The preceding stability proposition shows that $u_j$ converges uniformly
to a continuous admissible $u$ satisfying
\[
 e^{-u}H_m(u)=e^{-\varphi+f(x,\varphi)}\omega^n
                              =e^{-\varphi}H_m(\varphi).
\]
\Cref{hessian:hessian-exponential-order}, applied in both directions, gives $u=\varphi$.

The zero-order bounds for $u_j$ are uniform. Fix $M$ bounding them
and choose $B>\|\varphi\|_\infty+1$. Extend $h$ smoothly away from a neighborhood of the range of $\varphi$ to $X\times[-B-1,B+1]$; this does not change the equations for large $j$. The tolerance $\varepsilon$ in
\cref{hessian:prescribed-potential-trace} depends on these bounds and on
$h$, without derivatives of the smooth comparison function.
Choose a fixed smooth $\widetilde\varphi$ with
$\|\widetilde\varphi-\varphi\|_\infty<\varepsilon/2$ and
$\|\widetilde\varphi\|_\infty\leq B$.
For large $j$, $\|v_j-\widetilde\varphi\|_\infty<\varepsilon$.
\Cref{hessian:prescribed-potential-complex-hessian} then gives uniform gradient
and complex-Hessian bounds for $u_j$, independent of derivatives of
$v_j$. \Cref{hessian:complex-hessian-compactness} yields
$\varphi\in W^{2,p}$ for every finite $p$, and
$\varphi\in C^{1,\alpha}$ for every $\alpha<1$.

\Cref{hessian:proper-auxiliary-limit} makes $\varphi$ a continuous viscosity
solution of the concave uniformly elliptic extension, with right-hand
side $e^{f(x,\varphi)/m}$. This right-hand side is H\"older. The analytic estimate in \cref{sec:hessian-complex-limits} therefore gives
$\varphi\in C^{2,\beta}$, for some $\beta>0$.
The operator agrees with the original Hessian root on the attained
compact cone set; the now classical equation thus has smooth dependence
on $x,\varphi$ and its complex Hessian, and is uniformly elliptic there.
Difference quotients and interior Schauder estimates give $C^{3,\beta}$;
iteration proves smoothness. The estimate applies directly to the viscosity limit.
\end{proof}

The proper equation can now be solved when its exponent depends
smoothly on the unknown potential, provided that constant barriers
bound the desired range and the dependence is strictly increasing.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{hessian:proper-variable-existence}\index{Hessian equation!strictly increasing exponent}
Let $A<B$ and let $f$ be smooth on a neighborhood of
$X\times[A,B]$. Suppose that, for every $x\in X$,
\[
 f(x,A)\leq0\leq f(x,B),\qquad
 0<\kappa\leq\partial_t f(x,t)\leq L\quad(A\leq t\leq B).
\]
There is a unique continuous admissible solution with values in
$[A,B]$ of
\[
 H_m(w)=e^{f(x,w)}\omega^n.
\]
This solution is smooth and strictly admissible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $a\geq L$ and let
$\mathcal C=\{v\in C(X):A\leq v\leq B\}$, a complete subset of
$C(X)$ in the uniform norm. For $v\in\mathcal C$ put
$H_v(x)=f(x,v(x))-av(x)$ and let $T(v)$ be the continuous proper
solution with exponent $aT(v)+H_v$, furnished by
\cref{hessian:proper-uniform-stability}. The function $f(x,t)-at$
is nonincreasing in $t$, so
\[
 -aB\leq f(x,B)-aB\leq H_v(x)
                   \leq f(x,A)-aA\leq-aA.
\]
The zero-order bounds in that proposition give $A\leq T(v)\leq B$.
The mean-value theorem also gives
\[
 \|H_v-H_z\|_\infty\leq(a-\kappa)\|v-z\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 \|T(v)-T(z)\|_\infty
          \leq(1-\kappa/a)\|v-z\|_\infty.
\]
Since $0\leq1-\kappa/a<1$, the contraction theorem gives a unique
fixed point $w\in\mathcal C$. It is admissible because it equals
$T(w)$, and its equation is exactly the stated equation. Every
continuous admissible solution in this interval is a fixed point,
by uniqueness of the continuous proper equation with its prescribed
$H_w$. This proves uniqueness in the stated class.
\Cref{hessian:continuous-hessian-regularity} makes $w$ smooth.
Its form lies in the closed $m$-cone and has positive $m$th
symmetric polynomial; Maclaurin's inequalities then put it in the
open cone. Thus it is strictly admissible.
\end{proof}
