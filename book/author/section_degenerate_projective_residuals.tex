\subsection{Positive mass with a degenerate smooth residual}
\label{sec:degenerate-projective-residuals}
\index{Reference stability!degenerate residual}

A second family has a logarithmic pole whose resolution leaves a smooth residual form degenerating along a different exceptional divisor. Let $n\geq2$ and $o=[1:0:\cdots:0]$ as in \cref{sec:curved-nonanalytic-references}, and fix $q\ne o$ in $\mathbb P^n$. On $X=\operatorname{Bl}_q\mathbb P^n$, keep $\Theta=\pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$. For $0<t<1$, put
\[
 \psi_t=t\log\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n|Z_i|^2}{\sum_{i=0}^n|Z_i|^2},
 \qquad K_t=\{p\in\Sigma:\textstyle\sum_i p_i\geq t\}.
\]
On $T$, the function $\psi_t+L$, a potential of $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}+\ddc\psi_t$, equals $(1-t)L+t\log\sum_i\mathrm{e}^{x_i}$. It differs by a bounded function from
\[
 (1-t)h_\Sigma+t\max_i x_i=h_{K_t},\qquad
 K_t=(1-t)\Sigma+t\operatorname{conv}(e_1,\ldots,e_n).
\]
Thus $\psi_t\sim\psi_{K_t}\sim\Phi_{K_t}$, and $n!\operatorname{Vol}_n(K_t)=1-t^n$. We call $\psi_t$ the prototype of this ordinary type.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:degenerate-projective-residual}
Let $\tau\colon Z\to X$ be the blowup of the point of $X$ above $o$, with exceptional divisor $E_o$, and denote the inverse image of $E_q$ in $Z$ again by $E_q$. There is a smooth semipositive closed form $\alpha_t$ on $Z$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degenerate-projective-residual}
 (\pi\circ\tau)^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}
       +\ddc(\psi_t\circ\pi\circ\tau)=\alpha_t+t[E_o].
\end{equation}
It is positive away from $E_q$, has rank exactly one at every point of $E_q$, and satisfies $\int_Z\alpha_t^n=1-t^n>0$. Moreover, for every $u\in\PSH(X,\Theta)$ with the ordinary type of $\pi^*\Phi_{K_t}$, in particular for every solution of the equation in this type, the function $u\circ\tau-\psi_t\circ\pi\circ\tau$ extends from $Z\setminus E_o$ to a bounded $\alpha_t$-plurisubharmonic function on $Z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On $Y=\operatorname{Bl}_o\mathbb P^n$, let $\pi_o$ be the blowdown map and $Q_o\colon Y\to\mathbb P^{n-1}$ the map induced by the linear projection from $o$. As in the proof of \cref{lem:convex-body-mass}, the incidence graph embedding shows that
\[
 \beta_t=(1-t)\pi_o^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}
             +tQ_o^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS},n-1}
\]
is a K\"ahler form, where $\omega_{\mathrm{FS},n-1}$ is the Fubini--Study form of $\mathbb P^{n-1}$. Since the two centers are distinct, $Z$ is also the blowup $\sigma\colon Z\to Y$ of the point above $q$. Put $\alpha_t=\sigma^*\beta_t$. The Poincar\'e--Lelong formula gives \eqref{eq:degenerate-projective-residual}; there is no term along $E_q$, since $\psi_t$ is locally bounded near $q$.

In a blowup chart, $\sigma$ is $(s,w_2,\ldots,w_n)\mapsto(s,sw_2,\ldots,sw_n)$. At $s=0$ its differential has rank one and kills the $n-1$ tangent directions of $E_q$. Positivity of $\beta_t$ therefore gives the assertion about the rank; off $E_q$ the map is biholomorphic.

Write $H=\pi_o^*[\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}]$ and $E=[E_o]$ for the cohomology classes on $Y$. The current identity gives $[\beta_t]=H-tE$. Since $H|_{E_o}=0$, all mixed top intersections vanish. The normal bundle of $E_o\cong\mathbb P^{n-1}$ is $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^{n-1}}(-1)$, so $\int_YE^n=(-1)^{n-1}$. Therefore
\[
 \int_Z\alpha_t^n=\int_Y(H-tE)^n=1-t^n.
\]
The first equality holds because $\sigma$ has degree one. Finally, if $u\sim\pi^*\Phi_{K_t}\sim\pi^*\psi_t$, then $u\circ\tau$ differs from the pulled-back prototype $\psi_t\circ\pi\circ\tau$ by a bounded function off $E_o$. By \eqref{eq:degenerate-projective-residual}, after adding a local potential of $\alpha_t$ this bounded difference becomes plurisubharmonic off $E_o$, and it extends across $E_o$ by \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:degenerate-residual-reference-stability}
Let $0<t_{\max}<1$, let $t_j,t\in(0,t_{\max}]$ with $t_j\to t$, and let $r_j>0$ tend to $r>0$. The references $r_j\pi^*\Phi_{K_{t_j}}$ satisfy the hypotheses of \cref{thm:convex-body-reference-stability}, so in particular the equation is solvable in the limiting ordinary type, and
\[
 \sup_G|B_j(G)-B(G)|
 \leq n|\log(r_j/r)|+
       \frac{nt_{\max}^{n-1}}{1-t_{\max}^n}|t_j-t|.
\]
At continuous tests, all supporting sets consist of the normalized exponential probabilities of that theorem. At the endpoint $t=1$, where the mass vanishes, no equation with a positive bounded density is solvable in the ordinary type of the prototype $\pi^*\psi_1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $p_{*,i}=t_{\max}/n$ and $\delta=(1-t_{\max})/2$; then $p_*+\delta\Sigma$ lies in every $K_t$ with $t\leq t_{\max}$. For $s\geq t$, moving a point of $K_t$ toward a vertex $e_i$ until its coordinate sum reaches $s$ gives
\[
 d_H(K_s,K_t)\leq\frac{\sqrt2}{1-t_{\max}}|s-t|.
\]
Hence the stability theorem applies. Instead of its general bound, the bound $n t_{\max}^{n-1}/(1-t_{\max}^n)$ for the derivative of $-\log(1-t^n)$ on $(0,t_{\max}]$ and the mass formula give the sharper estimate for the functionals.

At $t=1$, the resolved residual form $\alpha_1$ is pulled back from $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, so $\int_Z\alpha_1^n=0$. The argument of \cref{prop:degenerate-projective-residual} shows that every potential of the ordinary type of $\pi^*\psi_1$ has a bounded $\alpha_1$-plurisubharmonic remainder. Stokes' theorem for bounded products in a closed background gives this remainder mass zero, and positivity forces its Monge--Amp\`ere measure to vanish. Thus no such potential can solve the equation with density $c\mathrm{e}^F\mathrm{d}V$ for bounded $F$ and $c>0$. In contrast, the endpoint $a=1$ in \cref{cor:curved-reference-stability} has positive mass and remains solvable.
\end{proof}

Scaling by $r>0$ multiplies both the resolved residual form and the divisor coefficient by $r$. Solvability is obtained on $X$ through the criterion of \cref{thm:normalization-mass-quotient}, without requiring the resolved residual form to be strictly positive. These criteria use closed projective backgrounds and fixed modifications; we do not know analogous convergence criteria for general nonclosed Hermitian backgrounds, for moving modifications, or for unrestricted degenerations of the singularities. Moreover, the supporting probabilities here only identify the limiting functional: we make no claim about convergence of the solutions or of weighted adjoint products.
