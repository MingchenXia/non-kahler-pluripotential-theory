\section{Smooth nonuniqueness for a degenerate density}
\label{sec:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness}

The first example places the freedom in the potential inside an open region where the density vanishes. Three independent semipositive forms of rank one reduce equality of the Monge--Amp\`ere measures to equality of products of three scalar coefficients. We construct two semipositive forms with the same cube, and then add the same exact correction to both to obtain a strictly positive background.

In this section and the following ones, $X=\mathbb P(\mathbb C^2\oplus\mathbb C^2)=\mathbb P^3$, with block homogeneous coordinates $[A:B]$, where $A,B\in\mathbb C^2$, and $\omega_X$ is its Fubini--Study form. Here $A$ and $B$ denote coordinates, not the forms of \eqref{uni-eq:linearization}; the two solution forms will be written $A_0$ and $B_0$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness}\index{Uniqueness!failure for degenerate densities}
On $X=\mathbb P^3$ there exist a smooth Hermitian form $\omega$ with $\mathrm{d}\omega\ne0$, a smooth nonnegative nonzero function $f$, and distinct smooth functions $u,v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ such that
\[
 \sup_Xu=\sup_Xv=0,\qquad
 (\omega+\ddc u)^3=f\omega_X^3=(\omega+\ddc v)^3.
\]
The density vanishes on a nonempty open set, and both potentials have minimal singularities.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Three rank-one directions}
\label{subsec:numa-rank-directions}

On $U=\{A\ne0,B\ne0\}$ put
\[
 r=\log\frac{|A|^2}{|B|^2},\qquad
 P=\ddc\log|A|^2,\quad Q=\ddc\log|B|^2,\quad
 R=\mathrm{d}r\wedge \dc r.
\]
The logarithms are computed in a local homogeneous lift, but their Hessians do not depend on the lift. The forms $P,Q$ are pullbacks of Fubini--Study forms from the two projective lines $\mathbb P(\mathbb C^2)$. All three forms are semipositive of rank one, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-rank-frame}
 P^2=Q^2=R^2=0,\qquad P\wedge Q\wedge R>0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, in local coordinates $A=(1,a)$ and $B=\tau(1,b)$, $P$ and $Q$ are positive multiples of $\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}a\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar a$ and $\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}b\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar b$, while
\[
 \partial r=\frac{\bar a\,\mathrm{d}a}{1+|a|^2}
             -\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}
             -\frac{\bar b\,\mathrm{d}b}{1+|b|^2}.
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Its $\mathrm{d}\tau$ component is nonzero, so the three rank-one forms correspond to independent directions. In particular, for nonnegative $x,y,z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-cube}
 (xP+yQ+zR)^3=6xyz\,P\wedge Q\wedge R,
\end{equation}
and the form is positive exactly when all three coefficients are positive.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Since $\ddc r=P-Q$, the chain rule gives, for a smooth function $G:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-chain}
 \ddc G(r)=G'(r)(P-Q)+G''(r)R.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Let $\sigma(r)=\mathrm{e}^r/(1+\mathrm{e}^r)$. Since
$\log(|A|^2+|B|^2)=\log|B|^2+\log(1+\mathrm{e}^r)$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-fs}
 \omega_X=\sigma(r)P+\sigma(-r)Q+\sigma'(r)R.
\end{equation}
A function of $r$ that is constant near each end of $\mathbb R$ extends smoothly across $X\setminus U$. A form $xP+yQ+zR$ whose coefficients equal those of \eqref{eq:numa-fs} for $|r|\geq2$ extends smoothly by $\omega_X$ across $X\setminus U$.

\subsection{The scalar coefficients}
\label{subsec:numa-scalar-coefficients}

On the central interval $-1\leq r\leq1$, the coefficients will make a different rank-one factor vanish in each of the two solution forms. Outside a larger interval they coincide with the Fubini--Study coefficients, so the resulting forms extend across the omitted projective lines. Primes denote derivatives with respect to $r$.

Choose an even smooth function $\beta$ supported in $[-1,1]$, strictly positive on $(-1,1)$, with $\beta'>0$ on $(-1,0)$, and normalized by $\int_{-1}^1\beta=2$; for instance, a positive multiple of $\exp(-1/(1-r^2))$ on $(-1,1)$, extended by zero. Define
\[
 F(r)=-1+\int_{-1}^r\beta(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\quad(-1<r<1),
 \qquad F=-1\ (r\leq-1),\quad F=1\ (r\geq1),
\]
and put $p=\beta/2$, $q=\beta'/2$. Then $F$ is smooth and odd, $p$ is even, and $q$ is odd, positive on $(-1,0)$ and negative on $(0,1)$. Both $p$ and $q$ vanish to infinite order at the endpoints.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Take smooth $a\geq0$ with $a=1$ on $(-\infty,-2/3]$ and $a=0$ on $[-1/3,\infty)$, and put $b(r)=a(-r)$. Choose an even nonnegative $\chi\in C_c^\infty((-1/3,1/3))$ with $\chi(0)>0$. On $[-1,1]$ set
\[
 z_0=(q^2+\chi^2)^{1/2}.
\]
It is smooth: the expression under the square root is positive in the interior, and near each endpoint $z_0=|q|$, where $q$ has a fixed sign. It is positive in the interior and vanishes to infinite order at the endpoints. Also,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-coefficient-cancellation}
 z_0\geq|q|,\qquad a(z_0-q)=0,\qquad b(z_0+q)=0.
\end{equation}
The first cancellation holds because $\chi=0$ on the support of $a$ and $q\geq0$ there; the second follows by reflection.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
On $(-\infty,-1]$ choose smooth functions $\ell,s$ such that $0\leq\ell\leq1$, $\ell=0$ on $(-\infty,-2]$, $\ell=1$ on $[-3/2,-1]$, while $s>0$ for $r<-1$, $s=1$ on $(-\infty,-3/2]$, and $s$ vanishes to infinite order at $-1$. Such functions are easily obtained from smooth cutoff functions and a function like $\exp(-1/(r+1)^2)$. Define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 x&=(1-\ell)\sigma+\ell,&
 y&=(1-\ell)\sigma(-r)+\ell s,&
 z&=(1-\ell)\sigma'+\ell s &&(r\leq-1),\\
 x&=p+a,& y&=p+b,& z&=z_0 &&(-1\leq r\leq1),
 \end{aligned}
\]
and for $r\geq1$ use $x(r)=y(-r)$, $y(r)=x(-r)$, $z(r)=z(-r)$.

The definitions join smoothly: at $-1$ both definitions give the values $(1,0,0)$, and their differences vanish to infinite order there, since $a=1$ and $b=0$ near $-1$. The central definitions are symmetric under reflection, so the same holds at $1$. We have
\[
 x,y,z>0\quad\hbox{for }r\ne\pm1,\qquad
 (x,y,z)(-1)=(1,0,0),\quad (x,y,z)(1)=(0,1,0).
\]
For $|r|\geq2$ their values are $(\sigma(r),\sigma(-r),\sigma'(r))$.

\subsection{Two forms with the same cube}
\label{subsec:numa-equal-cubes}

The cancellation identities now turn the scalar coefficients into two semipositive forms with the same cube. Their average is strictly positive except at the two ends of the central interval.

By the extensions described above, the form and the function
\[
 \omega_0=x(r)P+y(r)Q+z(r)R,\qquad w=F(r)
\]
are smooth on $X$. The form is semipositive, and positive except on the hypersurfaces $M_- =\{r=-1\}$ and $M_+=\{r=1\}$. Put
\[
 H=\tfrac12\ddc w=p(P-Q)+qR,\qquad A_0=\omega_0-H,\quad B_0=\omega_0+H.
\]
For $|r|\geq1$ we have $H=0$. On $|r|<1$,
\[
 A_0=aP+(2p+b)Q+(z_0-q)R,\qquad
 B_0=(2p+a)P+bQ+(z_0+q)R.
\]
All coefficients are nonnegative, and by \eqref{eq:numa-cube} and \eqref{eq:numa-coefficient-cancellation}, $A_0^3=B_0^3=0$ on this band. Outside it both cubes equal $\omega_0^3$. Thus $A_0,B_0$ are smooth semipositive forms on $X$ with equal cubes. This common top-degree form vanishes on the closed band $|r|\leq1$ and is positive on its complement, including near $X\setminus U$.

\subsection{Restoring strict positivity of the background}
\label{subsec:numa-positive-background}

The forms $A_0,B_0$ already have the required common measure. We obtain a Hermitian background by adding an exact correction $\ddc\rho$ to their average $\omega_0$ and subtracting $\rho$ from both potentials. The correction must restore positivity at $M_\pm$ while preserving it in the transition regions.

Take $\kappa\in C_c^\infty((-2,2))$, equal to one on $[-3/2,3/2]$. For a parameter $\varepsilon>0$ to be chosen, set
\[
 \rho(r)=\varepsilon(r^2-1)\kappa(r),\qquad
 \omega=\omega_0+\ddc\rho.
\]
By \eqref{eq:numa-chain}, its coefficients on $U$ are $x+\rho'$, $y-\rho'$ and $z+\rho''$. On $[-3/2,3/2]$ one has $\rho'=2\varepsilon r$ and $\rho''=2\varepsilon$, so the last coefficient is positive there. For $r\geq0$, $x+2\varepsilon r$ is positive, including at $r=0$ since $x(0)=p(0)>0$. On $[-3/2,0]$, the function $x$ has a positive minimum $m$, so $x+2\varepsilon r\geq m-3\varepsilon>0$ for small $\varepsilon$. Reflection gives the corresponding estimate for $y-2\varepsilon r$. On the compact region $3/2\leq|r|\leq2$, all three original coefficients have a positive minimum and the perturbations are $O(\varepsilon)$. For $|r|\geq2$ and near $X\setminus U$, the form is exactly $\omega_X$. Hence $\omega$ is strictly positive on $X$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$.

The form $\omega$ is not closed. Indeed, since $\mathrm{d}R=-\mathrm{d}r\wedge(P-Q)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-differential}
 \mathrm{d}\omega=\mathrm{d}\omega_0
   =\mathrm{d}r\wedge\bigl((x'-z)P+(y'+z)Q\bigr).
\end{equation}
At $r=0$, $x'=y'=0$ and $z=\chi(0)>0$. Hence $\mathrm{d}\omega=z\,\mathrm{d}r\wedge(Q-P)\ne0$ there. The nonzero $\mathrm{d}\tau$ component used in \eqref{eq:numa-rank-frame} also shows that $\mathrm{d}r\wedge P$ and $\mathrm{d}r\wedge Q$ are linearly independent.

\begin{proof}[of \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness}]
Set $\widetilde u=-w/2-\rho$ and $\widetilde v=w/2-\rho$. Since $\ddc w=2H$, the construction gives
\[
 \omega+\ddc\widetilde u=A_0\geq0,\qquad
 \omega+\ddc\widetilde v=B_0\geq0.
\]
Subtracting their respective suprema gives $u,v$. Their Hessians are unchanged, and $v-u=w+\text{constant}$ is nonconstant. The function $f=A_0^3/\omega_X^3=B_0^3/\omega_X^3$ is smooth, nonnegative and nonzero, and it vanishes on the nonempty open band $|r|<1$. Both potentials are smooth, hence bounded, so they have minimal singularities: for a Hermitian background, the envelope $V_\omega$ of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} is zero.
\end{proof}

Every Hermitian background on $\mathbb P^3$ has the bounded mass property of \cref{def:bmp}: since the Fubini--Study form is closed, Stokes' theorem for bounded potentials shows that all its bounded potentials have the same mass, and \cref{prop:bmp-metric} shows that the property does not depend on the metric. This applies to all the backgrounds on $\mathbb P^3$ constructed below.

The linearized equation also explains why the maximum principle argument fails. The form
\[
 T=A_0^2+A_0\wedge B_0+B_0^2
\]
satisfies $\ddc w\wedge T=B_0^3-A_0^3=0$, but $T=0$ on $M_\pm$, where $A_0=B_0=\omega_0$ has rank one. Thus the linear equation for $w$ is not elliptic everywhere, even though both potentials and their common density are smooth.
