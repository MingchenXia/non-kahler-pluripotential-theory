\subsection{Fiber integrability on a modified base}
\label{sec:modified-base-fiber-integrability}

Keep $\pi\colon X\to B$, $(A,h)$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ and $V_m$ as in \cref{sec:fiber-integrability-band}. A singularity of higher codimension on the base can become divisorial after a modification of the base. By \cref{lem:adjoint-ideal-transform}, the integrability test upstairs then applies to the pulled-back section multiplied by the Jacobian. This fixed factor shifts the divisorial orders, while the restriction estimate still bounds the number of lost sections.

Let $\rho\colon Y\to B$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds, and form the fiber product and its projections
\[
 X'=X\times_B Y,\qquad \pi'\colon X'\to Y,\qquad \mu\colon X'\to X.
\]
In local submersion coordinates one sees that $X'$ is smooth, that $\pi'$ is a submersion with the same compact connected fibers as $\pi$, and that $\mu$ is a modification. Let $R_\rho$ and $J_\rho$ be the relative canonical bundle and the Jacobian section of $\rho$, as in \cref{sec:psh-birational-ideals}. The coordinate Jacobian matrix of $\mu$ is that of $\rho$, extended by the identity in the fiber variables, so
\[
 R_\mu=\pi'^*R_\rho,\qquad J_\mu=\pi'^*J_\rho.
\]
Put $\varphi'=\varphi\circ\mu$ and $\theta'=\rho^*\theta$. Choose distinct nonempty smooth connected reduced divisors $D_1,\ldots,D_N$ on $Y$ whose union has simple normal crossings, together with smooth metrics $H_i$ on $\mathcal O_Y(D_i)$, canonical sections $s_i$ and curvatures $\eta_i$.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:modified-base-fiber-band}\index{Holomorphic section!modified base band}
Suppose that $c_i>0$ and $u\in C^\infty(Y,\mathbb R)$ satisfy
\[
 \beta=\theta'+\ddc u-\sum_i c_i\eta_i>0,
 \qquad \varphi'\geq\sum_i c_i\log\|\pi'^*s_i\|_{H_i}^2-C.
\]
Suppose that for each $i$ the function $\varphi'$ satisfies an anisotropic upper bound, with respect to $\pi'$, on a submersion polydisk over an open subset of $D_i$ away from the other divisors, as in \cref{thm:crossing-fiber-integrability-band}, with fixed $p_i,b_{ij}$. Define $a_i$, $L_{m,i}$ and $K_{m,i}$ by the formulas of that theorem, and put
\[
 \psi'=u+\sum_i c_i\log\|s_i\|_{H_i}^2,
 \qquad M_m=\rho^*A^m\otimes R_\rho.
\]
Then there is a unique $\psi\in\PSH(B,\theta)$ with $\psi\circ\rho=\psi'$, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(\psi)=\rho_*(\beta^q),\qquad
 \int_B\mathrm{MA}_\theta(\psi)=\int_Y\beta^q\in(0,\infty).
\]
The map $T_m(s)=J_\rho\rho^*s$ identifies $H^0(B,A^m)$ with $H^0(Y,M_m)$, and for $W_m=H^0(B,A^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\psi))$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 T_m(W_m)&=H^0\left(Y,M_m\left(-\sum_iL_{m,i}D_i\right)\right),\\
 T_m(W_m)&\subseteq T_m(V_m)
       \subseteq H^0\left(Y,M_m\left(-\sum_iK_{m,i}D_i\right)\right).
 \end{split}
\]
Consequently $W_m\subseteq V_m$ and $\dim V_m-\dim W_m=O(m^{q-1})$. If $q=1$, the difference is at most $\sum_i\lceil a_i\rceil$ for every $m$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula, $\theta'+\ddc\psi'=\beta+\sum_i c_i[D_i]$. Thus $\psi'\in\PSH(Y,\rho^*\theta)$, and \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification} gives the unique potential $\psi$ on $B$ with $\psi\circ\rho=\psi'$. On $Y$, locality off the union of the divisors and the fact that the non-pluripolar measure does not charge this union give $\mathrm{MA}_{\theta'}(\psi')=\beta^q$. The measure identity in \cref{prop:birational-partial-envelopes} then gives the pushforward formula and the mass.

\Cref{lem:adjoint-ideal-transform}, applied first with weight zero and then with weight $m\psi$, gives the identification of the full section spaces and the formula for $T_m(W_m)$; here $\mathcal I(m\psi')=\mathcal O_Y(-\sum_iL_{m,i}D_i)$ by the integrability computation for simple normal crossings in the proof of \cref{thm:crossing-fiber-integrability-band}. Since $u$ is bounded, the global lower bound gives $\varphi'\geq\psi'\circ\pi'-C'$. As $\rho\circ\pi'=\pi\circ\mu$ and $\mu$ is surjective, this descends to $\varphi\geq\psi\circ\pi-C'$, and integrability in bounded submersion charts proves $W_m\subseteq V_m$.

For $s\in V_m$, the transformation of multiplier ideals under $\mu$ gives
\[
 J_\mu\mu^*s=\pi'^*T_m(s)
 \in H^0\bigl(X',\pi'^*M_m\otimes\mathcal I(m\varphi')\bigr).
\]
Hence, in each polydisk where the upper bound holds, the descended coefficient of $T_m(s)$ vanishes to order at least $K_{m,i}$ along $D_i$, by \cref{lem:anisotropic-fiber-integrability} and the argument with a unit coefficient in the proof of \cref{thm:fiber-integrability-band}. This is the vanishing order of $T_m(s)$, including the Jacobian factor, and it proves the upper inclusion.

The chain of restrictions along the divisors on $Y$ has at most $\sum_i\lceil a_i\rceil$ steps. On $D_i$, each target is $(\rho^*A|_{D_i})^m$ twisted by powers of the bundles $\mathcal O_Y(-D_j)|_{D_i}$ in a linear range and by the fixed bundle $R_\rho|_{D_i}$. Including $R_\rho|_{D_i}$ in \cref{lem:finite-family-section-bound} with exponent one, we see that every target has dimension $O(m^{q-1})$, uniformly. Summing gives the estimate. For $q=1$ each divisor is a point and every target has dimension one.
\end{proof}

The theorem requires the modification and the divisorial data to be given, with a positive residual; it does not say which selecting potentials admit such data. In the following model with a point pole on the base, both the residual and the Jacobian correction are explicit.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:modified-point-fiber-series}\index{Holomorphic section!point pole Jacobian}
Let $q\geq2$, $B=\mathbb P^q$, $A=\mathcal O_B(1)$, $p=[1:0:\cdots:0]$ and $X=B\times\mathbb P^1$. Use the normalized Fubini--Study forms $\omega_B,\omega_F$ and the corresponding metrics, and for a point $o\in\mathbb P^1$ put
\[
 R=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^q|Z_j|^2}{\sum_{j=0}^q|Z_j|^2},\quad
 T=\|s_o\|^2,\quad \varphi=\tfrac14\log(R^2+T^3),\quad
 \psi=\tfrac12\log R.
\]
Then $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\omega_B+\omega_F)$, and $\psi\in\PSH(B,\omega_B)$ has mass $1-2^{-q}$. Let $\mathfrak m_p$ be the ideal sheaf of $p$. The selected and reference spaces are exactly
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 V_m=H^0\bigl(B,A^m\otimes\mathfrak m_p^{k_m}\bigr),\qquad
 W_m=H^0\bigl(B,A^m\otimes\mathfrak m_p^{\ell_m}\bigr),\\
 k_m=\max\{\lfloor m/2-2/3\rfloor-q+1,0\},\qquad
 \ell_m=\max\{\lfloor m/2\rfloor-q+1,0\}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Their defect vanishes for $m<2q$, and for $m\geq2q$ it is
\[
 \dim V_m-\dim W_m=\binom{\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1}{q-1}
       \sim\frac{2^{1-q}}{(q-1)!}m^{q-1}.
\]
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Over a common denominator, $R^2+T^3$ is a sum of squared moduli of holomorphic coefficients of sections of $\mathcal O_X(2,3)$, divided by the corresponding metric factor. Hence $\ddc\varphi\geq-\tfrac12\omega_B-\tfrac34\omega_F$, also across the common zero set by the extension theorem.

Let $\rho\colon Y\to B$ be the blowup of $p$, with exceptional divisor $E$, and let $\tau\colon Y\to\mathbb P^{q-1}$ be the extension to $Y$ of the projection $[Z_0:\cdots:Z_q]\mapsto[Z_1:\cdots:Z_q]$. Since $(\rho,\tau)$ is an embedding, the forms
\[
 \alpha=\rho^*\omega_B,\quad\gamma=\tau^*\omega_{\mathbb P^{q-1}},\qquad
 \beta=\tfrac12(\alpha+\gamma)
\]
satisfy $\beta>0$. The divisor bundle is $\mathcal O_Y(E)=\rho^*\mathcal O_B(1)\otimes\tau^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^{q-1}}(-1)$; its quotient metric has curvature $\alpha-\gamma$ and satisfies $R\circ\rho=\|s_E\|^2$. Thus the theorem applies with the single divisor $E$, $u=0$, $c=1/2$, $a=2/3$ and residual $\beta$. Along $E\times\{o\}$ the pulled-back selecting potential has the exact local model $\tfrac14\log(|z|^4+|w|^6)+O(1)$, and it is locally bounded elsewhere. If $T_m(s)$ is divisible by $z^{K_m}$, where $K_m=\max\{\lfloor m/2-2/3\rfloor,0\}$, then \cref{lem:anisotropic-fiber-integrability} gives integrability of $\pi'^*T_m(s)=J_\mu\mu^*s$ in every chart of this model. By \cref{lem:adjoint-ideal-transform} for $\mu$, this gives the inclusion opposite to the upper inclusion of the theorem.

In the blowup coordinates $Z_1/Z_0=z$ and $Z_j/Z_0=zt_j$ for $j\geq2$, the Jacobian of $\rho$ is $z^{q-1}$. Hence, if a section $s$ of $A^m$ vanishes to order $d$ at $p$, then $T_m(s)$ vanishes to order $d+q-1$ generically along $E$. The two divisorial thresholds $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ and $K_m$ therefore give exactly the orders $\ell_m$ and $k_m$ above. Homogeneous polynomials of degree $m$ realize every jet of total degree at most $m$ at $p$. For $m\geq2q$ we have $k_m=\ell_m-1$, and the additional jets of degree $\ell_m-1$ are spanned by $\binom{\ell_m+q-2}{q-1}$ monomials, which gives the defect. For $m<2q$ both orders vanish.

Finally, in cohomology $[\beta]=\rho^*c_1(A)-\tfrac12[E]$. The intersection numbers containing both $\rho^*c_1(A)$ and $[E]$ vanish, because $\rho^*c_1(A)$ restricts to zero on $E$. The normal bundle of $E\simeq\mathbb P^{q-1}$ is $\mathcal O_E(-1)$, so $\int_Y[E]^q=(-1)^{q-1}$, while $\int_Y(\rho^*c_1(A))^q=1$. Expanding gives $\int_Y\beta^q=1-2^{-q}$, which is the mass of $\psi$ by the theorem.
\end{proof}

The selecting potential is finite at most points of the fiber over $p$ and equals $-\infty$ at the point $(p,o)$, so again it does not descend, even up to a bounded function. The reference potential $\psi$ has a pole of codimension $q$ at $p$, and the Jacobian factor is what converts the divisorial orders along $E$ into vanishing orders at $p$.
