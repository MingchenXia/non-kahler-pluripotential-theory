\section{Curvature conditions for mass extrema}
\label{sec:mass-extremum-geometry}

We now ask when the lower and upper masses are attained by smooth potentials. At a smooth local extremum of the mass, the first variation forces the metric $\Omega=\omega+\ddc v$ to be Gauduchon, and the second variation gives a sign for $\ddc(\Omega^{n-2})$. On a threefold these necessary conditions are also sufficient, because the mass is then a polynomial of degree two in the variation. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$, and recall from \cref{chap:mass} the set $\HH_\omega$ of smooth real functions $u$ with $\omega+\ddc u>0$ and the mass functional $\Mass_\omega(u)=\int_X(\omega+\ddc u)^n$.

For $n\geq2$, \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization,prop:gaud-volume-density-independence} give the differential conditions under which $\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)$ is independent of the smooth positive probability measure $\mu$. By \cref{prop:entropy-mass-extrema}, the same conditions characterize when the difference $H_0-\operatorname{Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}$ considered there is constant on probability measures of finite entropy, for a fixed smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$; it suffices to test smooth positive probability measures. In dimension one the mass is always $\int_X\omega$, so both conclusions hold automatically.

Recall that a smooth real $(n-1,n-1)$-form $T$ is nonnegative, written $T\geq0$, if $\mathrm i\alpha\wedge\bar\alpha\wedge T\geq0$ for every $(1,0)$-covector $\alpha$; in this bidegree this is the usual notion of positivity. The second variation of the mass produces integrals of the form $\int_X\mathrm df\wedge\dc f\wedge T$, and the following elementary lemma shows that their signs detect the sign of $T$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gradient-test-positive-form}
Suppose a smooth real $(n-1,n-1)$-form $T$ satisfies
\[
 \int_X \mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc f\wedge T\geq0
 \quad\hbox{for every smooth real }f.
\]
Then $T\geq0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose instead that $\mathrm i\alpha\wedge\bar\alpha\wedge T<0$ at a point $x_0$ for some $(1,0)$-covector $\alpha$. Every $(1,0)$-covector is the $(1,0)$-part of a real covector, so we can choose a real linear function $\ell$ in local real coordinates around $x_0$ whose differential has $(1,0)$-part $\alpha$ at $x_0$. Then $\mathrm{d}\ell\wedge\dc\ell$ is a positive multiple of $\mathrm i\alpha\wedge\bar\alpha$ at $x_0$, and after shrinking the coordinate ball, $\mathrm{d}\ell\wedge\dc\ell\wedge T$ is strictly negative on it. Choose a nonzero smooth cutoff function $\chi$ supported in this ball and, for positive integers $k$, set
\[
 f_k=k^{-1}\chi\sin(k\ell),
\]
extended by zero to $X$. Since $\mathrm{d}\ell$ is a constant nonzero covector in these coordinates,
\[
 \mathrm{d}f_k\wedge\dc f_k
 =\chi^2\cos^2(k\ell)\,\mathrm{d}\ell\wedge\dc\ell+O(k^{-1}),
\]
where $k$ times the remainder has uniformly bounded coefficients. In $\cos^2(k\ell)=(1+\cos(2k\ell))/2$, the oscillatory part contributes integrals tending to zero, as one sees by integrating by parts in a coordinate direction in which $\ell$ has nonzero derivative. Hence
\[
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\int_X\mathrm{d}f_k\wedge\dc f_k\wedge T
 =\frac12\int_X\chi^2\mathrm{d}\ell\wedge\dc\ell\wedge T<0,
\]
which contradicts the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:local-mass-extremum-necessary}
Let $n\geq3$, and let $v\in\HH_\omega$ be a local minimum of $\Mass_\omega$ for the $C^2$ topology. Then $\Omega=\omega+\ddc v$ satisfies
\[
 \ddc(\Omega^{n-1})=0,
 \qquad \ddc(\Omega^{n-2})\leq0.
\]
At a local maximum the first condition still holds and the second inequality is reversed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every smooth real $f$, the potentials $v+tf$ lie in $\HH_\omega$ for all sufficiently small $|t|$. The vanishing of the first variation therefore gives the Gauduchon condition. The second variation is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2}\right|_{t=0}\Mass_\omega(v+tf)
 &=n(n-1)\int_X(\ddc f)^2\wedge\Omega^{n-2}\\
 &=-n(n-1)\int_X\mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc f\wedge\ddc(\Omega^{n-2}),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality follows by integrating by parts twice, using that $\ddc(\Omega^{n-2})$ is both $\mathrm{d}$-closed and $\dc$-closed. At a local minimum this second variation is nonnegative for every $f$, and \cref{lem:gradient-test-positive-form}, applied to $-\ddc(\Omega^{n-2})$, gives $\ddc(\Omega^{n-2})\leq0$. The case of a maximum is identical, with the opposite sign.
\end{proof}

In dimension three these necessary conditions are also sufficient, and every local extremum is global.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:threefold-mass-minimizer}
Assume $n=3$ and $v\in\HH_\omega$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $v$ is a local minimum of $\Mass_\omega$;
\item $v$ is a global minimum of $\Mass_\omega$ on $\HH_\omega$;
\item $\Omega=\omega+\ddc v$ is Gauduchon and $\ddc\omega\leq0$.
\end{enumerate}
In this case $v_-(\omega)=\int_X\Omega^3$, and the infimum in \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula} is attained. The corresponding statements for maxima hold with $\ddc\omega\geq0$, and then $v_+(\omega)=\int_X\Omega^3<+\infty$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $\ddc\Omega=\ddc\omega$, the preceding proposition shows that the first condition implies the third. Conversely, if $\Omega$ is Gauduchon, expanding the cube and integrating by parts gives, for every smooth real $f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threefold-mass-dirichlet}
 \int_X(\Omega+\ddc f)^3-\int_X\Omega^3
       =-3\int_X\mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc f\wedge\ddc\omega.
\end{equation}
The form $\mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc f$ is nonnegative, so the right side is nonnegative if $\ddc\omega\leq0$; then $v$ is a global minimum, and in particular a local one. By \cref{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema}, regularization identifies the infimum of the masses of smooth strictly $\omega$-plurisubharmonic potentials with $v_-(\omega)$, and \cref{prop:lower-mass-smooth-attainment} proves attainment in \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula}. Reversing the sign in the same identity proves the statements for maxima, including the equality of $\int_X\Omega^3$ with the supremum over bounded potentials.
\end{proof}

These conditions characterize smooth minimizers when they exist; neither argument produces a Gauduchon metric of the form $\omega+\ddc v$ in an arbitrary background.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For the Hopf metric $\omega$ and the pulled-back Fubini--Study form $\alpha$ of \cref{chap:hopf}, one has $\ddc\omega=-\alpha^2\leq0$, and $\omega$ is Gauduchon. Thus the minimum of the mass is attained by the constant potential and equals $6$, while the upper mass is infinite. On a surface, by contrast, the mass $\int_X\omega^2+2\int_Xv\,\ddc\omega$ is affine in the potential, so a local extremum exists precisely when $\omega$ is Gauduchon, and then all masses are equal. In dimension $n\geq4$ the sign condition of \cref{prop:local-mass-extremum-necessary} does not control the terms of order three and higher in the mass polynomial, and the argument above does not show that the conditions are sufficient.
