\section{Fredholm theory for a continuous positive form}
\label{sec:continuous-hermitian-fredholm}

A continuous strictly positive $(1,1)$-form still defines a uniformly
elliptic trace operator. Its range is described in Sobolev spaces:
continuity of the coefficients suffices for the $W^{2,p}$ estimates,
and the maximum principle identifies the kernel. The lemma is used for
continuous positive complex Hessians in
\cref{thm:continuous-hessian-adjoint-uniqueness}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:continuous-hermitian-fredholm}\index{Elliptic operator!continuous coefficients}
Let $\sigma$ be a continuous strictly positive real $(1,1)$-form on $X$,
and let $\mathrm dV$ be a smooth positive volume form.
For $p>2n$, the real operator
\[
 L_\sigma v=\operatorname{tr}_\sigma\ddc v,
 \qquad L_\sigma\colon W^{2,p}(X)\longrightarrow L^p(\mathrm dV),
\]
is Fredholm of index zero. Its kernel consists of the constants, and
its range has a one-dimensional annihilator in $L^{p'}(\mathrm dV)$,
where $p'=p/(p-1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a smooth positive Hermitian form $\eta$ and put
$L_s=(1-s)L_\eta+sL_\sigma$, $0\leq s\leq1$. In local holomorphic
coordinates, these are real second-order operators without lower-order
terms, whose principal matrices are continuous, uniformly positive and
have a common modulus of continuity.

\emph{Estimates.} The interior $L^p$ estimates for continuous
coefficients \cite[Theorem~9.11]{GT01}, applied on a finite cover by
coordinate balls, give, uniformly in $s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuous-hermitian-wtwo}
 \|v\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq C\bigl(\|L_sv\|_p+\|v\|_p\bigr)
 \qquad(v\in W^{2,p}(X)).
\end{equation}

\emph{Kernels.} Since $p>2n$, every $v\in W^{2,p}(X)$ is continuous
and belongs to $W^{2,2n}$ on coordinate balls, so the strong maximum
principle for strong solutions \cite[Theorem~9.6]{GT01} applies in
coordinate charts. If $L_sv=0$, then $v$ attains its maximum, the set
of maximum points is open by that principle and closed, and $v$ is
constant because $X$ is connected. Thus $\ker L_s=\mathbb R$. If
$(L_s-1)v=0$ and $v$ has a positive maximum, the same principle for the
operator $L_s-1$, whose zeroth-order coefficient is negative, shows in
the same way that $v$ is a positive constant, which contradicts
$L_sv=v$. Applying this to $-v$ gives $\ker(L_s-1)=0$.

\emph{Index and range.} Compactness of $W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow L^p$
and \eqref{eq:continuous-hermitian-wtwo} now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuous-hermitian-inverse}
 \|v\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq C\|(L_s-1)v\|_p
\end{equation}
uniformly in $s$. If this failed, we could normalize a contradicting sequence in
$W^{2,p}$, pass to $s_j\to s$ and a strongly $L^p$ convergent subsequence,
and apply \eqref{eq:continuous-hermitian-wtwo} to differences. Operator
norm continuity in $s$ gives strong $W^{2,p}$ convergence to a nonzero
member of $\ker(L_s-1)$, which is impossible.
The smooth operator $L_\eta-1$ has index zero and trivial kernel, hence
is an isomorphism, by smooth elliptic theory. The method of continuity
\cite[Theorem~5.2]{GT01} and \eqref{eq:continuous-hermitian-inverse}
show that every $L_s-1$ is an isomorphism. Finally, $L_\sigma$ differs
from $L_\sigma-1$ by the compact inclusion $W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow L^p$,
so it is Fredholm of index zero. Its one-dimensional kernel makes its
range have codimension one. $L^p$ duality gives the annihilator
assertion.
\end{proof}
