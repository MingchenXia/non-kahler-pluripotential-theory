\chapter{Holomorphic sections and Hermitian mass}
\label{chap:sections}

On a projective manifold, holomorphic sections of line bundles connect singularities with geometry: a singular weight selects the sections whose vanishing compensates for its poles, and the growth of the selected section spaces is governed by Monge--Amp\`ere masses. On a compact Hermitian manifold the curvature of a Hermitian line bundle is still closed, whereas the background form of the Monge--Amp\`ere operator need not be. This part of the book asks what section spaces, and the geometric models attached to sections and singularities, can still detect. Gauduchon degrees often take the place of intersection numbers: they bound the vanishing orders of sections, the valuations along a flag and the divisorial parts of positive currents on modifications.

Two kinds of geometric models appear. A complete flag turns vanishing orders into lattice points and section growth into the volume of a convex body, and the elliptic fibration of a Hopf manifold over projective space reduces its sections and its descended potentials to the base. Compact modifications carry the divisorial part of a singularity: on each model, the divisorial Lelong numbers or trace numbers sum to a closed positive current, and subtracting it from the pulled-back background gives residual classes that form b-divisor classes. Finally, positive Hermitian perturbations of a background organize multiplier-ideal envelopes into compatible systems; the limit of such a system in the original background need not recover the system.

In this chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, $L$ is a holomorphic line bundle on $X$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h$, $u\in\QPSH(X)$ is a fixed weight, and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive volume form. In a local holomorphic frame $e$ of $L$, write $|e|_h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\chi}$. The form $\beta=\ddc\chi$ does not depend on the frame; it is the smooth closed curvature form of $h$.

The weight selects the global sections of $L^m$ that are square integrable against $h^m\mathrm{e}^{-mu}$, and the Bergman kernels of these finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces produce closed positive currents in the curvature class of $L$. Pairing the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula with a Gauduchon metric bounds the vanishing that a nonzero section can carry; on a submanifold, this gives a necessary condition for realizing the trace data of \cref{chap:trace-data} by restricted sections. When $X$ is a curve, $\beta+\ddc u\geq0$ and $u$ has finitely many logarithmic poles, Riemann--Roch identifies the leading growth of the section spaces with the extended Monge--Amp\`ere mass of $u$. In higher dimensions this identification depends on the background. Homogeneous sections on a Hopf manifold grow only like the sections on its projective base, and a potential of infinite Hermitian mass with trivial multiplier ideals leaves every section space unchanged. Section counts therefore cannot recover a mass computed in a Hermitian background unrelated to the curvature of $L$.

\section{Weighted sections and Bergman currents}\label{sec:weighted-section-spaces}\index{Holomorphic section!weighted space}\label{sec:weighted-bergman-kernel}\index{Bergman kernel!weighted}

The multiplier ideals of $mu$ determine which sections of $L^m$ have finite weighted $L^2$ norm. Evaluation at points of the resulting Hilbert space defines a Bergman kernel, and its extremal characterization turns comparisons of norms into comparisons of the associated potentials.

For every positive integer $m$, set
\[
 \mathcal H_m(u)=H^0(X,L^m\otimes\mathcal I(mu)),\qquad
 \|s\|_{m,u}^2=\int_X|s|_{h^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mu}\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
The integrand is defined off the pole set $\{u=-\infty\}$, which has volume zero. A section $s\in H^0(X,L^m)$ belongs to $\mathcal H_m(u)$ if and only if $\|s\|_{m,u}<\infty$. Indeed, if the local coefficients of $s$ lie in $\mathcal I(mu)$, the integrand is locally integrable, and a finite covering of the compact manifold $X$ gives a finite integral; conversely, a finite integral gives local integrability on every coordinate ball. Thus $\mathcal H_m(u)$, a subspace of the finite-dimensional space $H^0(X,L^m)$, is a Hilbert space for this norm. Write $N_m=\dim\mathcal H_m(u)$.

Let $v\in\QPSH(X)$. If $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}v$, that is, $\mathcal I(ku)\subseteq\mathcal I(kv)$ for every real $k>0$ (\cref{sec:psh-singularity-types}), then $\mathcal H_m(u)\subseteq\mathcal H_m(v)$ for every $m$. In particular, weights of the same ordinary singularity type (\cref{def:singularity-order}) have the same section spaces, by \cref{prop:ideal-order}. When the two weights differ by a bounded function, their Hilbert norms are also comparable. Below, $\|s\|_{m,v,\mathrm{d}V'}$ denotes the norm defined with $v$ and $\mathrm{d}V'$ in place of $u$ and $\mathrm{d}V$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:section-norm-comparison}
Let $v\in\QPSH(X)$, let $D,E\geq0$, and let $F\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$. Suppose that $|u-v|\leq D$ outside the union of the pole sets of $u$ and $v$, and that $\mathrm{d}V'=\mathrm{e}^F \mathrm{d}V$ with $|F|\leq E$. Then $u$ and $v$ have the same section spaces, and for every positive integer $m$ and every $s$ in the common space $\mathcal H_m(u)=\mathcal H_m(v)$,
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-mD-E}\|s\|_{m,u}^2
 \leq\|s\|_{m,v,\mathrm{d}V'}^2
 \leq \mathrm{e}^{mD+E}\|s\|_{m,u}^2.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Off the pole sets, the integrand of $\|s\|_{m,v,\mathrm{d}V'}^2$ is that of $\|s\|_{m,u}^2$ multiplied by $\mathrm{e}^{-m(v-u)+F}$, a function with values between $\mathrm{e}^{-mD-E}$ and $\mathrm{e}^{mD+E}$. Hence one norm is finite if and only if the other is, so the section spaces coincide, and integration gives both estimates.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
Fix $m\geq1$ and choose an orthonormal basis $s_1,\ldots,s_{N_m}$ of $\mathcal H_m(u)$. Define $K_{m,u}$ on $X$ and $B_{m,u}$ off the pole set by
\[
 K_{m,u}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_m}|s_j(x)|_{h^m}^2,
 \qquad B_{m,u}(x)=K_{m,u}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-mu(x)},
\]
and set $B_{m,u}=0$ on the pole set. When $N_m=0$, both functions are zero. Since two orthonormal bases differ by a unitary matrix, $K_{m,u}$ does not depend on the choice of basis. We call $\Phi_m=m^{-1}\log K_{m,u}$ the \emph{Bergman potential}; when $N_m>0$, $\beta+\ddc\Phi_m$ is its \emph{Bergman current}. For $N_m=0$, we have $\Phi_m\equiv-\infty$.\index{Bergman kernel!potential}\index{Bergman kernel!current}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite-bergman-identities}
If $N_m>0$, then
\[
 K_{m,u}(x)=\sup_{0\neq s\in\mathcal H_m(u)}
                   \frac{|s(x)|_{h^m}^2}{\|s\|_{m,u}^2},
 \qquad \int_X B_{m,u}\,\mathrm{d}V=N_m.
\]
Moreover, the Bergman potential $\Phi_m=m^{-1}\log K_{m,u}$ is not identically $-\infty$, has analytic singularities, and satisfies
\[
 \beta+\ddc\Phi_m\geq0.
\]
In particular, the Bergman current $\beta+\ddc\Phi_m$ is a closed positive current.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write a section as $s=\sum_ja_js_j$ and trivialize the fiber of $L^m$ at $x$ by a unit vector. By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, $|s(x)|_{h^m}^2\leq\|s\|_{m,u}^2K_{m,u}(x)$, with equality when the coefficient vector $(a_j)$ is proportional to the complex conjugate of $(s_j(x))$. If $K_{m,u}(x)=0$, both sides of the extremal formula vanish. This proves the extremal formula. Integrating the finite sum defining $B_{m,u}$ gives $\sum_j\|s_j\|_{m,u}^2=N_m$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Locally $s_j=f_j e^{\otimes m}$ with holomorphic functions $f_j$, so
\[
 \Phi_m=\frac1m\log\sum_j|f_j|^2-\chi,
 \qquad
 \beta+\ddc\Phi_m=\frac1m\ddc\log\sum_j|f_j|^2\geq0.
\]
Since $X$ is connected, a nonzero section cannot vanish identically on an open set; hence the sum $\sum_j|f_j|^2$ does not vanish identically. The local expression therefore shows that $\Phi_m$ is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities, not identically $-\infty$. It also shows directly that $\beta+\ddc\Phi_m$ is closed.
\end{proof}

Write $\Phi_m(u,\mathrm{d}V)$ when the dependence on the weight and the volume form matters. Under the hypotheses of \cref{prop:section-norm-comparison}, and provided that the common section space is nonzero, the extremal formula gives
\[
 |\Phi_m(u,\mathrm{d}V)-\Phi_m(v,\mathrm{d}V')|\leq D+E/m
\]
outside the common base locus. Both potentials equal $-\infty$ on that locus. In particular, the two potentials have the same ordinary singularity type, and for a fixed weight a change of the smooth volume form changes the Bergman potential by $O(m^{-1})$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
A simultaneous change of metric and weight leaves the weighted norm unchanged. Let $a\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$, replace the metric by $h'=h\mathrm{e}^{-a}$, so that $|\cdot|_{h'}^2=|\cdot|_h^2\mathrm{e}^{-a}$, and replace the weight by $u'=u-a$. Then $h'^m\mathrm{e}^{-mu'}=h^m\mathrm{e}^{-mu}$, so the Hilbert space, its norm and the density $B_{m,u}\,\mathrm{d}V$ are unchanged, while
\[
 \beta'=\beta+\ddc a,\qquad \Phi'_m=\Phi_m-a.
\]
Consequently the Bergman current $\beta+\ddc\Phi_m$ does not change.

\section{Gauduchon degree and vanishing}\label{sec:gauduchon-section-degree}

The zero divisor of a holomorphic section is effective, and the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula expresses its integration current as the curvature form of the line bundle plus $\ddc$ of a global function. Pairing with a Gauduchon metric removes the $\ddc$ term, so the size of the divisor is bounded by a degree of the line bundle. Recall that a Hermitian form $\gamma$ is Gauduchon if $\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0$ (\cref{def:gauduchon-metric}); every Hermitian form on a curve is Gauduchon. Write $h^0(X,L^m)=\dim H^0(X,L^m)$. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$ on $X$, the degree
\[
 \deg_\gamma L=\int_X\beta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
\]
is the degree $\deg_{\gamma^{n-1}}L$ of \cref{sec:convex-bundle-norm-obstruction}, with $\gamma^0=1$ when $n=1$; as there, Stokes' theorem shows that it does not depend on the smooth metric on $L$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gauduchon-section-obstruction}\index{Gauduchon degree}\index{Holomorphic section!vanishing obstruction}
If $\deg_\gamma L<0$, then $H^0(X,L^m)=0$ for every $m\geq1$. If $\deg_\gamma L=0$ and $H^0(X,L^m)\neq0$, then $L^m$ is holomorphically trivial and $h^0(X,L^m)=1$. At degree zero, each nonzero weighted subspace $\mathcal H_m(u)$ is also one-dimensional.
\end{theorem}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Let $s$ be a nonzero section of $L^m$ and let $D$ be its zero divisor. The function $\log|s|_{h^m}^2$ is locally integrable, and the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula gives
\[
 \ddc\log|s|_{h^m}^2=[D]-m\beta.
\]
Since $\gamma$ is Gauduchon, integration by parts against $\gamma^{n-1}$ annihilates the left-hand side, and therefore
\[
 m\deg_\gamma L=\int_D\gamma^{n-1}\geq0,
\]
where the components of $D$ are counted with multiplicities. If $D\neq0$, the right-hand side is strictly positive, because $\gamma$ is positive on the regular locus of each component. Hence negative degree is impossible. At degree zero we obtain $D=0$, so $s$ has no zeros and trivializes $L^m$. Every other section of $L^m$ is then a holomorphic function times $s$, and holomorphic functions on the connected compact manifold $X$ are constant. Thus $h^0(X,L^m)=1$, and every nonzero subspace $\mathcal H_m(u)\subseteq H^0(X,L^m)$ is one-dimensional.
\end{proof}

The degree constrains positive curvature currents in the same way. If $T=\beta+\ddc u\geq0$, the trace identity of \cref{prop:gauduchon-trace} gives
\[
 \deg_\gamma L=\int_X T\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq0.
\]
If the degree is zero, the positivity of $T$ and the strict positivity of $\gamma$ imply $T=0$.

\section{Bounded weighted sections}\label{sec:bounded-section-algebra}

The spaces $\mathcal H_m(u)$ need not be closed under multiplication: the product of two sections with integrable weighted squared norms need not have an integrable weighted squared norm, as the following logarithmic example shows. Requiring the weighted pointwise norm to be bounded rather than integrable gives spaces that do multiply. They form the graded algebra used in \cref{chap:flag-bodies}.

\begin{example}\label{ex:multiplier-sections-not-graded}\index{Holomorphic section!failure of graded multiplication}
Let $X=\mathbb P^1$ with affine coordinate $z$, and let $\delta_0$ be the Dirac mass at the origin. Take $L=\mathcal O(1)$ with its Fubini--Study metric, whose curvature form is $\beta$, and let
\[
 u(z)=\frac35\log\frac{|z|^2}{1+|z|^2}.
\]
Then $\beta+\ddc u=\frac25\beta+\frac35\delta_0\geq0$,
$\mathcal I(u)=\mathcal O$ and $\mathcal I(2u)=\mathcal O(-[0])$. A section of $L$ that does not vanish at $0$ belongs to $\mathcal H_1(u)$, but its square does not belong to $\mathcal H_2(u)$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The curvature formula follows from the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula $\ddc\log|z|^2=\delta_0$ and from $\beta=\ddc\log(1+|z|^2)$ on $\mathbb C$. The weight is bounded near infinity and equals $\frac35\log|z|^2+O(1)$ near zero, its only singularity. The one-variable calculation of \cref{ex:ideal-disc} therefore gives the two stated ideals: $\mathcal I(u)$ is trivial, while $\mathcal I(2u)$ consists of the functions vanishing at the origin. A section of $L$ with $s(0)\neq0$ thus lies in $\mathcal H_1(u)$, whereas $s^2$ does not vanish at the origin and so does not lie in $\mathcal H_2(u)$.
\end{proof}

We return to the general data $(X,L,h,u)$. Define $\mathcal W_0=\mathbb C$ and, for $m\geq1$,
\[
 \mathcal W_m(u)=\{s\in H^0(X,L^m):
             |s|_{h^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mu}\text{ is bounded off }\{u=-\infty\}\}.
\]
These are vector spaces, since $|s+t|^2\leq2|s|^2+2|t|^2$; they are finite-dimensional and do not depend on the smooth metric, because the ratio of two smooth metrics is bounded above and below on $X$. Since $X$ has finite volume, they are contained in $\mathcal H_m(u)$. For positive integers $m,k$, $s\in\mathcal W_m(u)$ and $t\in\mathcal W_k(u)$, the identity
\[
 |st|_{h^{m+k}}^2\mathrm{e}^{-(m+k)u}
 =\bigl(|s|_{h^m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mu}\bigr)
  \bigl(|t|_{h^k}^2\mathrm{e}^{-ku}\bigr)
\]
proves $\mathcal W_m(u)\mathcal W_k(u)\subseteq\mathcal W_{m+k}(u)$. Thus $\bigoplus_m\mathcal W_m(u)$ is a graded subalgebra of the section ring $\bigoplus_{m\geq0}H^0(X,L^m)$ of $L$.

On a smooth projective variety, for big line bundles with metrics of positive curvature current and positive multiplier-ideal volume, the almost-semigroup construction of \cite[Section~10.3]{SGPT} works with the integrable section spaces themselves.

\section{Section growth on a curve}

On a curve, a weight with logarithmic poles imposes vanishing orders at the poles. Integrability rounds the required order down and boundedness rounds it up, so the two resulting divisors differ by a bounded amount, and Riemann--Roch gives the same leading growth for both section spaces.

Throughout this section, $X$ is a compact Riemann surface and $\beta+\ddc u\geq0$. Let $p_1,\ldots,p_r$ be distinct points such that $u$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus\{p_1,\ldots,p_r\}$ and, in coordinates $z_i$ centered at $p_i$,
\[
 u=c_i\log|z_i|^2+O(1),\qquad c_i>0.
\]
Set $\ell=\deg L=\int_X\beta$ and $d=\sum_i c_i$. Recall that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta(u)$ denotes the extended non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-weighted-section-growth}\index{Holomorphic section!growth on a curve}
In this situation $0\leq d\leq\ell$, and
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal H_m(u)&=H^0\left(X,L^m\Bigl(-\sum_i\lfloor mc_i\rfloor p_i\Bigr)\right),\\
 \mathcal W_m(u)&=H^0\left(X,L^m\Bigl(-\sum_i\lceil mc_i\rceil p_i\Bigr)\right).
 \end{split}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim\mathcal H_m(u)}m
 =\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim\mathcal W_m(u)}m
 =\ell-d
 =\int_X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta(u).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the section spaces.} A germ of a section of $L^m$ vanishing to order $k$ at $p_i$ has weighted squared norm comparable to $|z_i|^{2(k-mc_i)}$. As in \cref{ex:ideal-disc}, it is locally integrable if and only if $k-mc_i>-1$, that is, $k\geq\lfloor mc_i\rfloor$; it is bounded if and only if $k\geq mc_i$, that is, $k\geq\lceil mc_i\rceil$. Away from the points $p_i$ the weight is locally bounded and imposes no condition. Since $X$ is compact, this gives the displayed global identifications.

\emph{Step 2: the mass.} The positive measure $\beta+\ddc u$ has total mass $\ell$, and it has an atom of mass $c_i$ at $p_i$. Subtracting these atoms leaves a positive measure, which proves $d\leq\ell$. This remaining measure does not charge polar sets: after subtraction of the logarithmic singularity, its local potential has a bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic representative, and by \cref{prop:bounded-products} its curvature gives polar sets zero mass. Thus the remaining measure is exactly the extended measure, and its mass is $\ell-d$.

\emph{Step 3: growth.} If $\ell>d$, the degrees of both line bundles in the display equal $m(\ell-d)+O(1)$ and tend to infinity. Let $g$ be the genus of $X$. By Riemann--Roch and Serre duality, $h^0=\deg+1-g$ once the degree exceeds $2g-2$. Dividing by $m$ proves both limits.

If $\ell=d$, both degrees remain bounded. A line bundle of negative degree has no nonzero section, and one of nonnegative degree $k$ has at most $k+1$ independent sections: the sections vanishing at $k+1$ given distinct points are sections of a line bundle of negative degree, hence zero. Therefore both dimensions are uniformly bounded, and both limits vanish. These arguments include the case $r=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Section counts and infinite Hermitian mass}

We return to a connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n$. For a weight $u$ with trivial multiplier ideals, every weighted section space $\mathcal H_m(u)$ is the full space $H^0(X,L^m)$. When $X$ fails the bounded mass property of \cref{def:bmp}, such a weight can have infinite extended mass, so section growth in a closed curvature class does not determine the mass in an unrelated Hermitian background.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sections-infinite-mass-obstruction}
Let $\omega$ be a Hermitian metric on $X$ with $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$. There is a potential $U\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ such that, with $L=\mathcal O_X$ and its constant metric,
\[
 \dim\mathcal H_m(U)=1\quad\hbox{for every }m\geq1,
 \qquad \int_X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(U)=+\infty.
\]
All Bergman currents of $U$ are identically zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $U$ from \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness}; its extended mass is infinite, and $\mathcal I(tU)=\mathcal O_X$ for every $t>0$. Global holomorphic functions on $X$ are constant, and the constant function $1$ lies in $\mathcal H_m(U)$ because $\mathcal I(mU)=\mathcal O_X$ and $X$ is compact. Hence every weighted section space is one-dimensional, and its orthonormal basis consists of a nonzero constant. Each Bergman potential $\Phi_m$ is therefore constant. Since the trivial metric has curvature zero, every Bergman current is zero.
\end{proof}
