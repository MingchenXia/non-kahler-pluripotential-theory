A positive lower bound for the top product gives a current lower bound when the Hessian coefficients are bounded.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:c11-ellipticity}\index{Regularity!C11 positivity@strict positivity from $C^{1,1}$}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, let $h>0$ be Hermitian, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap C^{1,1}(X)$. If
\[
 (\theta+\ddc u)^n\geq a h^n
\]
for a constant $a>0$, then $\theta+\ddc u\geq\delta h$ as a current for some $\delta>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $T=\theta+\ddc u$. Its measurable coefficients are bounded on compact $X$, so there is $K>0$ such that its eigenvalues relative to $h$ belong to $[0,K]$ almost everywhere. By \cref{prop:c11-products}, their product is the density of $T^n$ relative to $h^n$, and is at least $a$. Each eigenvalue is therefore at least $a/K^{n-1}$. The almost-everywhere matrix inequality gives the asserted distributional inequality with $\delta=a/K^{n-1}$.
\end{proof}
