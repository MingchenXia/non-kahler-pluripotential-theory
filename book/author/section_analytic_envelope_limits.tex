\section{Limits of analytic envelopes}
\label{sec:analytic-envelope-limits}

An ideal envelope can acquire additional singularities in an $L^1$ limit. A common lower envelope prevents this loss of integrability, while convergence of divisorial Lelong numbers prevents the limit from becoming less singular. These two bounds identify the limit without requiring a monotone sequence.

Fix $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence}
Let $\epsilon_j\geq0$ tend to zero, and let $a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta+\epsilon_jh)$ satisfy
\[
 u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j,\qquad
 \nu_E(a_j)\longrightarrow\nu_E(u)
 \quad\text{for every local divisor }E\text{ over }X.
\]
Then
\[
 P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}
       \longrightarrow P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}
       \quad\text{in }L^1.
\]
If the $a_j$ have analytic singularities, their ordinary envelopes converge to $P_\theta[u]$ in $L^1$ if and only if $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $p=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}$. Projection and ideal order give
\[
 p\leq P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[p]_{\mathcal I}\leq q_j\leq0.
\]
The first inequality uses $p$ as a competitor in the larger background. All $q_j$ have supremum zero and belong to $\PSH(X,\theta+Ch)$ for a fixed $C\geq\sup_j\epsilon_j$. Normalized compactness therefore gives an $L^1$-convergent further subsequence of every subsequence. Let $q$ be such a limit. Passing the current inequalities to the limit gives $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} gives $p\leq q\leq0$.

Fix a local modification $\pi:Z\to U$ and a prime divisor $E\subset Z$. By \cref{lem:modification-lone-pullback}, the chosen subsequence converges after pullback in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The pointwise semicontinuity in \cref{lem:lelong-lone-semicontinuity} yields
\[
 \nu_E(q)\geq\limsup_j\nu_E(q_j)
       =\lim_j\nu_E(a_j)=\nu_E(u).
\]
Indeed, at every point of $E$ each pullback has Lelong number at least its generic number on $E$; apply the pointwise inequality and then take the generic value of the limit. On the other hand, $p\leq q$ gives $\nu_E(q)\leq\nu_E(p)=\nu_E(u)$. Thus $q$ and $u$ have the same Lelong numbers on all local divisors. Blowing up a point on any local modification recovers its pointwise Lelong number as a generic divisorial number; on a curve the point is already a divisor. By \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}, $q\sim_{\mathcal I}u$.

Consequently $q$ is a competitor for $p$, so $q\leq p$. Every cluster limit is therefore $p$, which proves convergence of the entire sequence. For analytic $a_j$, \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} identifies the ordinary and ideal envelopes. The last assertion follows from uniqueness of the $L^1$ limit and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.
\end{proof}

The divisorial hypothesis can be checked using multiplier ideals at fixed weights. The small dilation of the weight allows for the discrepancy term in the local Bergman estimate.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fixed-weight-envelope-convergence}
Let $\epsilon_j\geq0$ tend to zero, and let $a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta+\epsilon_jh)$ satisfy $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. Suppose that, for every integer $m\geq1$ and every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \mathcal I((1+\delta)m a_j)\subseteq\mathcal I(mu)
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }j.
\]
Then the conclusions of \cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence} hold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix a local divisor $E$ over a coordinate neighborhood $U$. Write $A=A_U(E)$ for its log discrepancy. By \cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error}, the asserted inclusion implies
\[
 (1+\delta)m\nu_E(a_j)
 \geq\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I((1+\delta)m a_j)
 \geq\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(mu)
 \geq m\nu_E(u)-A.
\]
Take the lower limit in $j$ first, then let $m\to\infty$ and $\delta\downarrow0$. It follows that $\liminf_j\nu_E(a_j)\geq\nu_E(u)$. The opposite inequality holds term by term because $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. The preceding proposition applies.
\end{proof}

The common ideal bound is essential: convergence of the potentials and of all divisorial Lelong numbers does not by itself control their envelopes.

\begin{example}\label{ex:divisorial-convergence-envelope-failure}
On a compact connected curve $Y$ with a positive form $\theta$ of total mass one, there are normalized analytic $a_j\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ and a smooth $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ such that
\[
 a_j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1,\qquad
 \nu_E(a_j)\longrightarrow\nu_E(u)\text{ for every local divisor }E,
\]
but $P_\theta[a_j]=P_\theta[a_j]_{\mathcal I}\to u\ne P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ in $L^1$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Choose $u$ smooth, nonconstant and normalized, with $\theta+\ddc u>0$. Apply the construction in \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} to the smooth probability measure $\nu=\theta+\ddc u$. In partitions of vanishing diameter, the largest cell mass tends to zero because $\nu$ has no atoms. Choose the rational approximations of the weights so that their total absolute error tends to zero. The resulting atomic probability measures $\nu_j$ converge weakly to $\nu$, and their largest atoms tend to zero.

Their normalized Green potentials $a_j$ are analytic, satisfy $P_\theta[a_j]=P_\theta[a_j]_{\mathcal I}=a_j$, and converge to $u$ in $L^1$, by the same theorem. At every point $y\in Y$,
\[
 0\leq\nu(a_j,y)=\nu_j(\{y\})
       \leq\max_{z\in Y}\nu_j(\{z\})\longrightarrow0=\nu(u,y).
\]
Local modifications of a smooth curve are isomorphisms, so this gives all the required divisorial limits. Since $u$ is bounded and $\theta>0$, both its envelopes are zero. Its nonconstancy proves the failure of envelope convergence. Each $a_j$ has a logarithmic pole, so $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$ fails.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dyadic regularization}

Demailly regularization supplies the ideal bound and the divisorial limits in \cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence}. Along dyadic indices, subadditivity also orders the analytic singularity types, so the envelopes decrease pointwise. The ideal-theoretic mechanism is the one used in \cite[Theorem~7.1.1, proof of $(3)\Rightarrow(1)$, Step~1]{SGPT}.

Choose the regularization from \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input} along $m_j=2^j$, starting at a sufficiently large index. Denote it by $a_j$ and choose numbers $\epsilon_j>0$ decreasing to zero such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}
 a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta+\epsilon_jh),\qquad
 a_j\geq u,\qquad
 a_j\text{ has type }(1/m_j,\mathcal I(m_ju)).
\end{equation}
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
The chosen regularization satisfies $a_j\to u$ in $L^1$. Its values need not decrease. Its ordinary singularity types do: subadditivity in \eqref{eq:trace-local-ideal-inputs} gives
\[
 \mathcal I(2m_ju)\subseteq\mathcal I(m_ju)^2,
 \qquad a_{j+1}\preceq a_j.
\]
Taking logarithms of generators proves the ordinary comparison, with a constant allowed to depend on $j$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:dyadic-analytic-envelope-limit}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and every choice in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}, put
\[
 q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j].
\]
Then $q_j$ has analytic singularities, and
\[
 q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}
       \searrow P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}
\]
pointwise and in $L^1$. The limit is independent of the regularizing representatives, the admissible error sequence and the metric $h$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, $q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}\sim a_j$. Since the backgrounds decrease and $a_{j+1}\preceq a_j$, the function $q_{j+1}$ is a competitor for $P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]$. Hence $q_{j+1}\leq q_j$.

The inequality $a_j\geq u$ gives $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. For every local divisor $E$, \cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error} gives
\[
 \nu_E(a_j)=\frac1{m_j}\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(m_ju)
       \longrightarrow\nu_E(u).
\]
By \cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence}, $q_j\to p:=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ in $L^1$ and $p\leq q_j\leq0$. The decreasing limit is a genuine $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function and agrees almost everywhere with $p$, hence everywhere. This identifies the limit solely from $\theta$ and the ideal class of $u$, proving every asserted independence.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dyadic-goodness-criterion}
For the sequence above, the following are equivalent:
\[
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 q_j\longrightarrow P_\theta[u]\text{ in }L^1,
 \qquad q_j\searrow P_\theta[u]\text{ pointwise}.
\]
They hold for one admissible choice of approximation if and only if they hold for every such choice. For the fixed volume $\mathrm{d}V$,
\[
 \lim_j\int_X(q_j-P_\theta[u])\,\mathrm{d}V
   =\int_X(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}-P_\theta[u])\,\mathrm{d}V\geq0,
\]
and equality to zero is another equivalent condition.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The theorem identifies the pointwise and $L^1$ limit. Equality almost everywhere is equality everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. Finally $0\leq q_j-P_\theta[u]\leq-(u-\sup_Xu)$ almost everywhere, so dominated convergence gives the integral formula. A nonnegative difference has zero integral precisely when the two canonical representatives agree.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Absorbing the curvature error}

For a Hermitian background $\omega$, take $\theta=h=\omega$ in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}. A rational rescaling absorbs the curvature error and preserves the rational coefficients of analytic singularities. It gives an approximation entirely within $\PSH(X,\omega)$.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density}
Let $\omega$ be Hermitian and let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. There are analytic $b_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $b_j\to u$ in $L^1$, with decreasing ordinary singularity types, such that
\[
 P_\omega[b_j]=P_\omega[b_j]_{\mathcal I}\searrow P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
Consequently analytic singularity classes are dense in the entire ideal-type metric space of \cref{prop:ideal-lone-metric}. The equality $P_\omega[u]=P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$ is equivalent to convergence of these ordinary envelopes to $P_\omega[u]$ in $L^1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use $h=\omega$ in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}. Enlarge the error bounds to positive rational numbers $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$, and set
\[
 c_j=\frac1{1+\epsilon_j},\qquad b_j=c_ja_j.
\]
Then $c_j\uparrow1$ is rational and
\[
 \omega+\ddc b_j
 =c_j\bigl((1+\epsilon_j)\omega+\ddc a_j\bigr)\geq0.
\]
The analytic coefficient is $c_j/m_j$. Also $b_j\to u$ in $L^1$, since $a_j\to u$ there and the sequence has bounded $L^1$ norm. Because $a_{j+1}\preceq a_j$, $c_{j+1}\geq c_j$, and each $a_j$ is bounded above, one has $b_{j+1}\preceq b_j$.

Put $q_j=P_{(1+\epsilon_j)\omega}[a_j]=P_{(1+\epsilon_j)\omega}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}$. The preceding theorem gives $q_j\searrow P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$ pointwise and in $L^1$. Since $b_j$ is analytic, \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} and the homogeneity in \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations} give
\[
 r_j:=P_\omega[b_j]=P_\omega[b_j]_{\mathcal I}=c_jq_j.
\]
Here $q_j\leq0$ decreases and $c_j\uparrow1$, so $r_j$ also decreases, with pointwise limit $P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Moreover
\[
 P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}\leq q_j\leq r_j\leq0.
\]
The genuine lower bound proves $L^1$ convergence by dominated convergence.

The metric distance from $[b_j]$ to $[u]$ is exactly the $L^1$ distance from $r_j$ to $P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$, so it tends to zero. The last assertion follows from the identified limit.
\end{proof}
