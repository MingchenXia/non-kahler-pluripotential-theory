\section{Limits of analytic envelopes}
\label{sec:analytic-envelope-limits}

By the curve construction of \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion}, convergence of potentials alone does not determine the limit of their ideal envelopes. Two further conditions do: a common lower bound in the multiplier-ideal order, and convergence of all divisorial Lelong numbers. Together they force every cluster value of the envelopes to have the multiplier ideals of the limiting potential, and maximality of the envelope then identifies the cluster value. The dyadic analytic approximants satisfy both conditions.

We fix $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, which will absorb the curvature errors of the approximants, and we use the local divisors $E$ over $X$ and their generic Lelong numbers $\nu_E$ of \cref{sec:psh-divisorial-estimates}.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence}
Let $\epsilon_j\geq0$ tend to zero, and let $a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta+\epsilon_jh)$ satisfy
\[
 u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j,\qquad
 \nu_E(a_j)\longrightarrow\nu_E(u)
 \quad\text{for every local divisor }E\text{ over }X.
\]
Then
\[
 P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}
       \longrightarrow P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}
       \quad\text{in }L^1.
\]
If the $a_j$ have analytic singularities, their ordinary envelopes converge to $P_\theta[u]$ in $L^1$ if and only if $P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $p=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}$. Projection and the ideal order give
\[
 p\leq P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[p]_{\mathcal I}\leq q_j\leq0.
\]
The first inequality holds because $p$ is a competitor in the larger background, and the second follows from \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations}, since $p\sim_{\mathcal I}u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. All $q_j$ have supremum zero and belong to $\PSH(X,\theta+Ch)$ for a fixed $C\geq\sup_j\epsilon_j$. By normalized compactness, every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence converging in $L^1$. Let $q$ be such a limit. Passing to the limit in the current inequalities gives $q\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} gives $p\leq q\leq0$.

Fix a local modification $\pi\colon Z\to U$ and a prime divisor $E\subset Z$. By \cref{lem:modification-lone-pullback}, the chosen subsequence still converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ after pullback. Since $q_j\sim_{\mathcal I}a_j$, the two functions have the same divisorial Lelong numbers. At every point of $E$, the pullback of each $q_j$ has Lelong number at least $\nu_E(q_j)$; applying the pointwise semicontinuity of \cref{lem:lelong-lone-semicontinuity} at a point of $E$ where the pullback of $q$ has Lelong number $\nu_E(q)$ yields
\[
 \nu_E(q)\geq\limsup_j\nu_E(q_j)
       =\lim_j\nu_E(a_j)=\nu_E(u).
\] On the other hand, $p\leq q$ gives $\nu_E(q)\leq\nu_E(p)=\nu_E(u)$. Thus $q$ and $u$ have the same Lelong numbers along all local divisors. Blowing up a point of any local modification recovers the pointwise Lelong number there as a generic divisorial number; on a curve the point is already a divisor. By \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}, $q\sim_{\mathcal I}u$.

Consequently $q$ is a competitor for $p$, so $q\leq p$. Every cluster value is therefore $p$, which proves convergence of the whole sequence. For analytic $a_j$, \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} identifies the ordinary and ideal envelopes, and the last assertion follows from uniqueness of $L^1$ limits and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}.
\end{proof}

The divisorial convergence follows from inclusions of multiplier ideals at fixed weights. The local Bergman estimate bounds the error in terms of the log discrepancy, and this error tends to zero after division by the weight.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fixed-weight-envelope-convergence}
Let $\epsilon_j\geq0$ tend to zero, and let $a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta+\epsilon_jh)$ satisfy $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. Suppose that, for every $\delta>0$ and every sufficiently large integer $m$,
\[
 \mathcal I((1+\delta)m a_j)\subseteq\mathcal I(mu)
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }j.
\]
Then the conclusions of \cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence} hold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix a local divisor $E$ over a coordinate neighborhood $U$, and write $A=A_U(E)$ for its log discrepancy. By \cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error}, the assumed inclusion implies
\[
 (1+\delta)m\nu_E(a_j)
 \geq\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I((1+\delta)m a_j)
 \geq\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(mu)
 \geq m\nu_E(u)-A
\]
for all large $j$. Taking the lower limit in $j$ first, and then letting $m\to\infty$ and $\delta\downarrow0$, we obtain $\liminf_j\nu_E(a_j)\geq\nu_E(u)$. The opposite inequality $\nu_E(a_j)\leq\nu_E(u)$ holds for every $j$ because $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. Hence \cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence} applies.
\end{proof}

The common ideal bound cannot be omitted, even when all divisorial Lelong numbers converge. In the atomic approximation of \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion}, every individual atom can tend to zero while the limiting potential is not an ideal model.

\begin{example}\label{ex:divisorial-convergence-envelope-failure}
On a compact connected curve $Y$ with a positive form $\theta$ of total mass one, there are normalized $a_j\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ with analytic singularities and a smooth $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ such that
\[
 a_j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1,\qquad
 \nu_E(a_j)\longrightarrow\nu_E(u)\text{ for every local divisor }E,
\]
but $P_\theta[a_j]=P_\theta[a_j]_{\mathcal I}\to u\ne P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ in $L^1$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Choose $u$ smooth, nonconstant and normalized, with $\theta+\ddc u>0$, and apply the construction in \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} to the smooth probability measure $\nu=\theta+\ddc u$. Since $\nu$ has no atoms, the largest mass of a cell tends to zero along partitions of vanishing diameter. Choose the rational approximations of the weights so that their total absolute error tends to zero. The resulting atomic probability measures $\nu_j$ converge weakly to $\nu$, and their largest atoms tend to zero.

By the same theorem, their normalized Green potentials $a_j$ have analytic singularities, satisfy $P_\theta[a_j]=P_\theta[a_j]_{\mathcal I}=a_j$, and converge to $u$ in $L^1$. At every point $y\in Y$,
\[
 0\leq\nu(a_j,y)=\nu_j(\{y\})
       \leq\max_{z\in Y}\nu_j(\{z\})\longrightarrow0=\nu(u,y).
\]
Since local modifications of a smooth curve are isomorphisms, this gives all the required divisorial limits. As $u$ is bounded and $\theta>0$, both envelopes of $u$ are zero. Since $u$ is normalized and not constant, $u\ne0$, so the limit $u$ of the envelopes of the $a_j$ differs from the envelopes of $u$. Each $a_j$ has a logarithmic pole, so $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$ fails.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dyadic regularization}

Demailly regularization provides the common ideal bound, since its approximants lie above $u$, and the Bergman estimate gives convergence of each divisorial Lelong number. Along dyadic indices, subadditivity of multiplier ideals also orders the ordinary singularity types of the approximants, which makes their envelopes decrease pointwise. This is the mechanism used in \cite[Theorem~7.1.1, proof of $(3)\Rightarrow(1)$, Step~1]{SGPT}.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Apply \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input} to $u$, with $\gamma=-\theta$, along the indices $m_j=2^j$, starting at a sufficiently large index. Denote the resulting potentials by $a_j$, and choose numbers $\epsilon_j>0$ decreasing to zero such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}
 a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta+\epsilon_jh),\qquad
 a_j\geq u,\qquad
 a_j\text{ has type }(1/m_j,\mathcal I(m_ju)).
\end{equation}
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
These approximants satisfy $a_j\to u$ in $L^1$. Their values need not decrease, but their ordinary singularity types do: the subadditivity property in \eqref{eq:trace-local-ideal-inputs} gives
\[
 \mathcal I(2m_ju)\subseteq\mathcal I(m_ju)^2,
 \qquad a_{j+1}\preceq a_j,
\]
where the ordinary comparison follows by taking logarithms of local generators, with a constant that may depend on $j$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:dyadic-analytic-envelope-limit}\index{Envelope!analytic approximation}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and every choice in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}, put
\[
 q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j].
\]
Then $q_j$ has analytic singularities, and
\[
 q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}
       \searrow P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}
\]
pointwise and in $L^1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, $q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}\sim a_j$. Since the backgrounds decrease and $a_{j+1}\preceq a_j$, the function $q_{j+1}$ is a competitor for $P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]$. Hence $q_{j+1}\leq q_j$.

The inequality $a_j\geq u$ gives $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. For every local divisor $E$, \cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error} gives
\[
 \nu_E(a_j)=\frac1{m_j}\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(m_ju)
       \longrightarrow\nu_E(u).
\]
By \cref{prop:ordered-ideal-envelope-convergence}, $q_j\to p:=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ in $L^1$, and its proof gives $p\leq q_j\leq0$. The decreasing limit of the $q_j$ is therefore bounded below by $p$; it is a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function that agrees with $p$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dyadic-goodness-criterion}
For the sequence above, the following are equivalent:
\[
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 q_j\longrightarrow P_\theta[u]\text{ in }L^1,
 \qquad q_j\searrow P_\theta[u]\text{ pointwise}.
\]
For the fixed volume form $\mathrm{d}V$,
\[
 \lim_j\int_X(q_j-P_\theta[u])\,\mathrm{d}V
   =\int_X(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}-P_\theta[u])\,\mathrm{d}V\geq0,
\]
and the vanishing of this limit is a further equivalent condition.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The theorem identifies both limits of the $q_j$ as $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, and its $L^1$ convergence gives the integral formula. By \cref{prop:p-below-i}, the limiting difference is nonnegative. Its integral therefore vanishes exactly when the two envelopes agree almost everywhere, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. This proves all the equivalences.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Absorbing the curvature error}

When $\theta=\omega$ is Hermitian, we can take $h=\omega$, and dividing the approximants by $1+\epsilon_j$ absorbs the curvature error. If the error bounds are rational, the analytic coefficients remain rational, so the whole approximation takes place in the fixed class $\PSH(X,\omega)$.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density}\index{Analytic singularity!density in the ideal metric}
Let $\omega$ be Hermitian and let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. There are $b_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with analytic singularities and decreasing ordinary singularity types, such that $b_j\to u$ in $L^1$ and
\[
 P_\omega[b_j]=P_\omega[b_j]_{\mathcal I}\searrow P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
Consequently the classes of potentials with analytic singularities are dense in the whole metric space of ideal classes of \cref{prop:ideal-lone-metric}. The equality $P_\omega[u]=P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$ is equivalent to convergence of these ordinary envelopes to $P_\omega[u]$ in $L^1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use $h=\omega$ in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}. Enlarge the error bounds to positive rational numbers $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$, and set
\[
 c_j=\frac1{1+\epsilon_j},\qquad b_j=c_ja_j.
\]
Then $c_j\uparrow1$ is rational and
\[
 \omega+\ddc b_j
 =c_j\bigl((1+\epsilon_j)\omega+\ddc a_j\bigr)\geq0.
\]
The analytic coefficient of $b_j$ is $c_j/m_j$. Moreover $b_j\to u$ in $L^1$, since $a_j\to u$ there and $c_j\to1$. Because $a_{j+1}\preceq a_j$, $c_{j+1}\geq c_j$, and each $a_j$ is bounded above, one has $b_{j+1}\preceq b_j$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Put $q_j=P_{(1+\epsilon_j)\omega}[a_j]=P_{(1+\epsilon_j)\omega}[a_j]_{\mathcal I}$. \Cref{thm:dyadic-analytic-envelope-limit} gives $q_j\searrow P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$ pointwise and in $L^1$. Since $b_j$ has analytic singularities, \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} and the homogeneity in \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations} give
\[
 r_j:=P_\omega[b_j]=P_\omega[b_j]_{\mathcal I}=c_jq_j.
\]
Here $q_j\leq0$ decreases and $c_j\uparrow1$, so $r_j$ also decreases, with pointwise limit $P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Moreover
\[
 P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}\leq q_j\leq r_j\leq0.
\]
Since the lower bound is integrable, dominated convergence gives convergence in $L^1$.

The distance in \cref{prop:ideal-lone-metric} from $[b_j]_{\mathcal I}$ to $[u]_{\mathcal I}$ is exactly the $L^1$ distance from $r_j$ to $P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$, so it tends to zero. The last assertion follows because the limit of the $r_j$ has been identified.
\end{proof}
