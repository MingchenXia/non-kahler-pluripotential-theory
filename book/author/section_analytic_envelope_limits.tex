\section{Analytic singularities and monotone envelope limits}
\label{sec:analytic-envelope-limits}

The elementary topology of ideal envelopes can be compared directly with analytic regularization. We first prove that an analytic singularity and its two envelopes have the same ordinary type. We then show that the envelopes of a dyadic Demailly approximation decrease to the ideal envelope. The argument uses only local ideal theory, divisorial removal and compactness. Neither bigness nor a bound on Monge--Amp\`ere mass is required.

The closed-background analogue of the first assertion appears in \cite[Proposition~3.2.10]{SGPT}. The limiting argument is also the ideal-theoretic part of the proof of its $\mathcal I$-goodness characterization, \cite[Theorem~7.1.1, proof of $(3)\Rightarrow(1)$, Step~1]{SGPT}. We give the details for a nonclosed background; no mass or $d_S$ conclusion is imported.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance}
Let $v$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on a compact complex manifold, and let $a$ have analytic singularities. If $v\preceq_{\mathcal I}a$, then $v\preceq a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Resolve the coherent ideal of an analytic presentation of $a$. Locally on the resulting smooth modification, write
\[
 a\circ\pi=\sum_{i=1}^r b_i\log|z_i|^2+B,
 \qquad b_i\geq0,\qquad B\in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}.
\]
By \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}, the generic Lelong number of $v\circ\pi$ along $\{z_i=0\}$ is at least $b_i$. Add a smooth local function to $v\circ\pi$ to make it plurisubharmonic. Divisorial removal for this closed positive complex Hessian shows that
\[
 v\circ\pi-\sum_i b_i\log|z_i|^2
\]
has a quasi-plurisubharmonic representative. Here the difference is first interpreted off the divisor, or as an integrable distribution. Such a representative is locally bounded above. Since $B$ is locally bounded, $v\circ\pi\leq a\circ\pi+C$ on each relatively compact resolution chart away from the divisor. A finite cover above a smaller coordinate neighborhood makes $C$ uniform there. Push the inequality down off the analytic exceptional set; the canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representatives extend it everywhere. Finitely many neighborhoods on the compact manifold give $v\leq a+C$ globally.

The only resolution input is the standard principalization of an analytic ideal. Divisorial removal is applied to a locally closed positive Hessian after smooth correction, not to a purported closed background on the original manifold.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:analytic-envelope-type}
Let $\theta$ be any smooth real background, and let $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have analytic singularities. Then
\[
 a\simeq P_\theta[a]=Q_\theta[a].
\]
In particular the common envelope has analytic singularities with the same coefficient and ideal as $a$, up to a bounded remainder.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $q=Q_\theta[a]$. Projection gives $q\sim_{\mathcal I}a$, so the lemma gives $q\preceq a$. Since $q\leq0$, it is a competitor for $P_\theta[a]$. Combine this with \cref{prop:p-below-i} to obtain
\[
 a-\sup_Xa\leq P_\theta[a]\leq q\leq P_\theta[a].
\]
The two envelopes are equal and ordinarily equivalent to $a$. Their bounded difference from $a$ preserves the analytic presentation. No smoothness of the remainder is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:analytic-reference-normalization}
If $\theta$ is also semipositive and big, this gives an explicit family to which \cref{prop:reference-normalization-comparison,cor:small-reference-adjoint-limits} apply. Normalize an analytic $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ by $a\leq0$, and set
\[
 \varphi_t=P_\theta[ta],\qquad t\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1).
\]
The proposition gives $\varphi_t\simeq ta$, so invariance under bounded changes gives $P_\theta[\varphi_t]=\varphi_t$. Moreover $ta\in\PSH(X,t\theta)$ and $ta\leq\varphi_t$, hence $P_{t\theta}(\varphi_t)$ is genuine. Consequently
\[
 \sup_{G\in C(X)}|B_{\varphi_t}(G)-B_0(G)|
       \leq-n\log(1-t)\longrightarrow0
       \qquad(t\downarrow0).
\]
When $\theta$ is Hermitian and $G$ is fixed and smooth, every choice in $\mathcal A_{\varphi_t}(G)$ therefore converges weakly in $L^1$ to the smooth Gauduchon adjoint probability, by \cref{thm:normalization-gauduchon-recovery}.
\end{example}

We need the approximation estimate on every fixed local modification, not merely the Lelong estimate at points of $X$. A \emph{local divisor over $X$} means a prime divisor $E$ on a smooth modification $\pi:Z\to U$ of a coordinate neighborhood. Write
\[
 \nu_E(u)=\nu(u\circ\pi,E),\qquad
 A_U(E)=1+\operatorname{ord}_E(\operatorname{Jac}\pi).
\]
The second number is the log discrepancy. These local modifications are not required to extend over the compact manifold.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bergman-divisorial-error}
For a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$, every fixed local divisor $E$, and every real $m>0$, one has
\[
 \max\!\left\{\nu_E(u)-\frac{A_U(E)}m,0\right\}
 \leq \frac1m\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(mu)
 \leq \nu_E(u).
\]
Consequently, an analytic weight of type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$ has divisorial Lelong number tending to $\nu_E(u)$ as $m\to\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The upper estimate follows from the local Bergman lower bound in \cref{prop:bergman-local-estimates}. The logarithm of local generators of $\mathcal I(mu)$, divided by $m$, is at least $u$ up to a locally bounded term. Pulling back and taking a generic Lelong number reverses this inequality.

For the lower estimate, take a local generator $f$ of the ideal and set $k=\operatorname{ord}_E(f)$. At a generic smooth point of $E$, choose a coordinate $z_1$ defining it. Divisorial removal, after a smooth correction, gives
\[
 u\circ\pi\leq\nu_E(u)\log|z_1|^2+C.
\]
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
Shrink around a point where the residual factors of $f\circ\pi$ and of the Jacobian are nonvanishing. The change-of-variables formula and local integrability of $|f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-mu}$ then require integrability in $z_1$ of
\[
 |z_1|^{2(k+A_U(E)-1-m\nu_E(u))}.
\]
This implies $k+A_U(E)>m\nu_E(u)$. In particular the weaker non-strict lower bound in the statement holds. Take the minimum over a finite set of local generators and use nonnegativity of the order. The last assertion now follows by letting $m\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

Choose the regularization from \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input} along $m_j=2^j$, starting at a sufficiently large index. Denote it by $a_j$ and choose numbers $\epsilon_j>0$ decreasing to zero such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}
 a_j\in\PSH(X,\theta+\epsilon_jh),\qquad
 a_j\geq u,\qquad
 a_j\text{ has type }(1/m_j,\mathcal I(m_ju)),
\end{equation}
where $h$ is a fixed Hermitian metric. The values $a_j$ need not decrease. Their ordinary singularity types do: the local subadditivity theorem gives
\[
 \mathcal I(2m_ju)\subseteq\mathcal I(m_ju)^2,
 \qquad a_{j+1}\preceq a_j.
\]
The inclusion follows from the standard local diagonal-restriction argument recalled in \eqref{eq:trace-local-ideal-inputs}; smooth corrections do not change the ideals. Taking logarithms of generators proves the ordinary comparison, with a constant allowed to depend on $j$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:dyadic-analytic-envelope-limit}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and every choice in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}, put
\[
 q_j=P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j].
\]
Then $q_j$ has analytic singularities, and
\[
 q_j=Q_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]
       \searrow Q_\theta[u]
\]
pointwise and in $L^1$. The limit is independent of the regularizing representatives, the admissible error sequence and the metric $h$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, $q_j=Q_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]\simeq a_j$. Since the backgrounds decrease and $a_{j+1}\preceq a_j$, the function $q_{j+1}$ is a competitor for $P_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[a_j]$. Hence $q_{j+1}\leq q_j$.

Put $p=Q_\theta[u]$. The inequality $a_j\geq u$ implies $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}a_j$. Regard $p$ in the larger background and use ideal projection and order preservation there. This gives
\[
 u-\sup_Xu\leq p\leq
 Q_{\theta+\epsilon_jh}[p]\leq q_j\leq0.
\]
Thus the decreasing limit $q$ is genuine, has supremum zero and belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$: the current inequalities pass to the limit, and a nested intersection of the compact maximum sets preserves the supremum. The displayed lower bound also proves $L^1$ convergence by dominated convergence.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Fix a local divisor $E$. The inequalities $u-\sup_Xu\leq q\leq q_j$ imply
\[
 \nu_E(u)\geq\nu_E(q)\geq\nu_E(q_j)
       =\frac1{m_j}\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(m_ju).
\]
By \cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error}, the last term tends to $\nu_E(u)$. Therefore $q$ and $u$ have the same Lelong number on every local divisor over $X$. This gives the pointwise data on every local modification as well: blow up the point and use that its Lelong number is the generic number on the exceptional divisor; on a curve the point already is a divisor. The local ideal criterion \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} now gives $q\sim_{\mathcal I}u$.

Since $q\leq0$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, it is a competitor for $p=Q_\theta[u]$. The earlier inequality $p\leq q$ proves equality. This identifies the limit solely from $\theta$ and the ideal class of $u$, proving every asserted independence.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dyadic-goodness-criterion}
For the sequence above, the following are equivalent:
\[
 P_\theta[u]=Q_\theta[u],\qquad
 q_j\longrightarrow P_\theta[u]\text{ in }L^1,
 \qquad q_j\searrow P_\theta[u]\text{ pointwise}.
\]
They hold for one admissible choice of approximation if and only if they hold for every such choice. For any smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$,
\[
 \lim_j\int_X(q_j-P_\theta[u])\,\mathrm{d}V
   =\int_X(Q_\theta[u]-P_\theta[u])\,\mathrm{d}V\geq0,
\]
and equality to zero is another equivalent condition.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The theorem identifies the pointwise and $L^1$ limit. Canonical quasi-pluri\-subharmonic functions agreeing almost everywhere agree everywhere. Finally $0\leq q_j-P_\theta[u]\leq-(u-\sup_Xu)$ almost everywhere, so dominated convergence gives the integral formula. A nonnegative difference has zero integral precisely when the two canonical representatives agree.
\end{proof}

This is a criterion using the envelopes of a specified analytic regularization. It does not claim that arbitrary $L^1$ approximation by analytic potentials characterizes goodness: that approximation is available for every $u$. It also supplies no mass identity and no triangle inequality for a distance on ordinary singularity types.

\subsection{Approximation in one Hermitian background}

When the background is itself Hermitian, scaling absorbs the curvature error. Rational error bounds preserve the rational-coefficient convention for analytic singularities.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density}
Let $\omega$ be Hermitian and let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. There are analytic $b_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $b_j\to u$ in $L^1$, with decreasing ordinary singularity types, such that
\[
 P_\omega[b_j]=Q_\omega[b_j]\searrow Q_\omega[u].
\]
Consequently analytic singularity classes are dense in the entire ideal-type metric space of \cref{prop:ideal-lone-metric}, without BMP. The equality $P_\omega[u]=Q_\omega[u]$ is equivalent to convergence of these ordinary envelopes to $P_\omega[u]$ in $L^1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use $h=\omega$ in \eqref{eq:dyadic-analytic-backgrounds}. Enlarge the error bounds to positive rational numbers $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$, and set
\[
 c_j=\frac1{1+\epsilon_j},\qquad b_j=c_ja_j.
\]
Then $c_j\uparrow1$ is rational and
\[
 \omega+\ddc b_j
 =c_j\bigl((1+\epsilon_j)\omega+\ddc a_j\bigr)\geq0.
\]
The analytic coefficient is $c_j/m_j$. Also $b_j\to u$ in $L^1$, since $a_j\to u$ there and the sequence has bounded $L^1$ norm. Because $a_{j+1}\preceq a_j$, $c_{j+1}\geq c_j$, and each $a_j$ is bounded above, one has $b_{j+1}\preceq b_j$.

Put $q_j=P_{(1+\epsilon_j)\omega}[a_j]=Q_{(1+\epsilon_j)\omega}[a_j]$. The preceding theorem gives $q_j\searrow Q_\omega[u]$ pointwise and in $L^1$. Since $b_j$ is analytic, \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} and the homogeneity in \cref{prop:i-envelope-operations} give
\[
 r_j:=P_\omega[b_j]=Q_\omega[b_j]=c_jq_j.
\]
Here $q_j\leq0$ decreases and $c_j\uparrow1$, so $r_j$ also decreases, with pointwise limit $Q_\omega[u]$. Moreover
\[
 Q_\omega[u]\leq q_j\leq r_j\leq0.
\]
The genuine lower bound proves $L^1$ convergence by dominated convergence.

The metric distance from $[b_j]$ to $[u]$ is exactly the $L^1$ distance from $r_j$ to $Q_\omega[u]$, so it tends to zero. The last assertion follows from the identified limit.
\end{proof}

In particular, density of analytic classes for the elementary ideal metric imposes no restriction at all on the ideal class. That metric alone cannot reproduce the $d_S$-closure characterization of ordinary singularities. The new criterion instead compares ordinary envelopes, retaining the possible difference between $P_\theta[u]$ and $Q_\theta[u]$. Its invariance under a change of the auxiliary regularization is proved above; a background-independent singularity topology and a compatible volume characterization remain separate questions.
