\begin{proposition}\label{prop:singularity-volumes-modification}\index{Singularity volume!pullback by a modification}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Then
\[
 \pi_*\mathrm{MA}_{\pi^*\theta}(u\circ\pi)
       =\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u).
\]
Both singularity volumes satisfy
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\pi^*\theta}(u\circ\pi)
       =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\pi^*\theta}(u\circ\pi)
       =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),
\]
including infinite values.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a proper analytic set $S\subset X$ such that $\pi$ restricts to a biholomorphism from $Y\setminus D$ onto $X\setminus S$, where $D=\pi^{-1}(S)$. On these complements, the truncations satisfy $\max(u\circ\pi,-t)=\max(u,-t)\circ\pi$, and Bedford--Taylor products of bounded potentials are preserved by the biholomorphic change of coordinates. Passing to the increasing limits in \eqref{eq:extended-ma} gives the measure identity there. By \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, the two measures give no mass to the pluripolar sets $S$ and $D$, which proves the global identity and the equality of total masses.

Pullback is a bijection between the potentials in the two backgrounds by \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}. Since $\pi$ is surjective, an inequality $v\leq u+C$ holds on $X$ if and only if $v\circ\pi\leq u\circ\pi+C$ holds on $Y$. Hence this bijection maps the singularity type of $u$ onto that of $u\circ\pi$, which identifies the competitors in the definitions of the two pairs of volumes. Their masses agree by the first assertion. Taking their infima and suprema proves the volume identities.
\end{proof}

The background on $Y$ is the pullback $\pi^*\theta$ itself, which may be degenerate even when $\theta>0$. These identities do not compare volumes for an independently chosen Hermitian form on $Y$.
