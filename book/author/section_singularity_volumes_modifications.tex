\begin{proposition}\label{prop:singularity-volumes-modification}\index{Singularity volume!pullback by a modification}
Let $\mu:Y\to X$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Then
\[
 \mu_*\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\mu^*\theta}(u\circ\mu)
       =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u).
\]
Both singularity volumes satisfy
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\mu^*\theta}(u\circ\mu)
       =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),\qquad
 \overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\mu^*\theta}(u\circ\mu)
       =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u),
\]
including infinite values.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a proper analytic set $S\subset X$ such that $\mu$ is an isomorphism outside $D=\mu^{-1}(S)$. On these complements, bounded truncations commute with composition by $\mu$, and their products commute with the biholomorphic change of coordinates. Passing to the increasing limits gives the measure identity there. By \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, the two measures give no mass to $S$ and $D$, which proves the global identity and equality of total masses.

Pullback is a bijection between the potentials in the two backgrounds by \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}. Surjectivity of $\mu$ preserves and reflects bounded differences, so this bijection identifies the ordinary-type competitor families. Their masses agree by the first assertion. Taking their infima and suprema proves the volume identities.
\end{proof}

The background upstairs is the exact pullback $\mu^*\theta$, which may be degenerate even when $\theta>0$. These identities do not compare volumes for an independently chosen Hermitian form on $Y$.
