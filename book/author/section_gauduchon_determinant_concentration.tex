\section{Geometric bounds and determinant concentration}
\label{sec:gaud-determinant-concentration}

The mass-invariance criterion gives geometric conditions for the critical determinant bound. That bound cannot in general be improved to a higher exponent, even inside one K\"ahler class. A concentrating family also separates the smooth maximizing current for one density from sequences approaching its value.

Assume $n\geq2$ and use the determinant densities $k_T$ of \cref{sec:gaud-determinant-integrability}, relative to a smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$. Put $q=n/(n-1)$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:geometric-determinant-bound}
Suppose $g$ is a Hermitian form with $\ddc g=\ddc(g^2)=0$, and put $V_g=\int_Xg^n$. For every Hermitian form $h\geq cg$, $c>0$,
\[
 v_-(h)\geq c^nV_g,\qquad
 \sup_{T\in\mathscr G_h}\|k_T\|_{L^q(\mathrm{d}V)}
       \leq c^{-1}V_g^{-1/n}.
\]
Equality holds in the determinant bound when $h=g$ and $c=1$. In particular, the critical norm is uniformly bounded for every Hermitian background on a compact K\"ahler manifold or a compact complex surface.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The identity $\ddc(g^2)=2g\wedge\ddc g+2\mathrm{d}g\wedge\dc g$ and \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization} give $v_-(g)=V_g$. The lower-mass quotient \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula} is monotone and homogeneous in its numerator, so $h\geq cg$ implies $v_-(h)^{1/n}\geq cV_g^{1/n}$. Apply \cref{thm:gaud-determinant-endpoint}.

A K\"ahler form satisfies both differential identities. On a surface, \cref{thm:gauduchon} supplies a metric $g$ with $\ddc g=0$, and the second identity holds by degree. Compactness gives $h\geq cg$ for each fixed $h$.
\end{proof}

For a fixed smooth $F$, \cref{thm:weak-gaud-compact-duality} already gives a unique smooth strictly positive maximizing current for $\mathfrak D_F$. Its determinant density is bounded. The following construction shows that approaching this maximum imposes no uniform higher determinant moments.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-determinant-concentration}
Let $X=E^n$, where $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$ has its area-one form, and write $\omega_i$ for its pullbacks. Set
\[
 h=\sum_{i=1}^n\omega_i,\qquad
 \mathrm{d}V=\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n,\qquad V=n!,\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{n-1}{n}.
\]
For $F=0$, the unique maximizing current is
$T_*=h^{n-1}/V$, with $k_{T_*}=V^{-1/n}$ and
$\mathfrak D_0(T_*)=V^{-1/n}$.
There are smooth strictly positive closed currents $S_\varepsilon\in\mathscr G_h$, $0<\varepsilon<\min\{1/4,\mathrm e^{-1}\}$, such that
\[
 \|k_{S_\varepsilon}\|_{L^q}=V^{-1/n},\qquad
 \mathfrak D_0(S_\varepsilon)\longrightarrow V^{-1/n},
\]
while for every fixed finite $p>q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-concentration-moments}
 \int_X k_{S_\varepsilon}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq C_{n,p}[\log(1/\varepsilon)]^{-\alpha p}
                    \varepsilon^{2-2\alpha p}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\end{equation}
Their $L^\infty$ norms also diverge. Each current is an $(n-1)$st power of a K\"ahler form in the fixed class $V^{-1/(n-1)}[h]$, and has the Bott--Chern, de Rham and Aeppli classes of $T_*$. No $S_\varepsilon$ attains the fixed-density maximum.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $H_i=\bigwedge_{j\ne i}\omega_j$. Wedge expansion gives
$h^{n-1}=(n-1)!\sum_iH_i$ and $h\wedge H_i=\mathrm{d}V$.
Thus $T_*=(1/n)\sum_iH_i$ is normalized, and the determinant formula gives $k_{T_*}=V^{-1/n}$. The smooth equation for $F=0$ is solved by $h^n=V\,\mathrm{d}V$; the compact duality theorem proves the value and uniqueness assertion.

Choose a nonnegative smooth bump $\beta$ on the unit disk in $\mathbb C$, of integral one, with $\beta\geq\beta_0>0$ on a disk of radius $r_0\in(0,1)$. On an embedded disk in the first elliptic factor put
$b_\varepsilon(z)=\varepsilon^{-2}\beta(z/\varepsilon)$ and extend by zero. It has integral one. For $0<t<1$, set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 f_{\varepsilon,t}=1-t+tb_\varepsilon,\qquad a=V^{-1/(n-1)},\\
 \gamma_{\varepsilon,t}=a(f_{\varepsilon,t}\omega_1+\omega_2+\cdots+\omega_n),
 \qquad T_{\varepsilon,t}=\gamma_{\varepsilon,t}^{n-1}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The form $\gamma_{\varepsilon,t}$ is strictly positive and closed: $f_{\varepsilon,t}$ depends only on the first, two-real-dimensional factor. Direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-concentration-density}
 T_{\varepsilon,t}=\frac1n\left(H_1+f_{\varepsilon,t}\sum_{i=2}^nH_i\right),
 \qquad k_{T_{\varepsilon,t}}=V^{-1/n}f_{\varepsilon,t}^{\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Since $\int_Xf_{\varepsilon,t}\,\mathrm{d}V=1$, the trace integral is one and
$\|k_{T_{\varepsilon,t}}\|_{L^q}=V^{-1/n}$.

The mean-zero function $f_{\varepsilon,t}-1$ has a smooth real Poisson potential $\psi$ on $E$ with
$\ddc\psi=(f_{\varepsilon,t}-1)\omega_1$.
For example its mean-zero Fourier series solves
$\Delta\psi=4\pi(f_{\varepsilon,t}-1)$ in the area-one coordinates.
Consequently $\gamma_{\varepsilon,t}-ah=\ddc(a\psi)$. The closed form
$P=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\gamma_{\varepsilon,t}^{n-2-j}\wedge(ah)^j$ satisfies
\[
 T_{\varepsilon,t}-T_*=\ddc(a\psi P).
\]
This proves all the class assertions, including $n=2$, when $P=1$.

Jensen's inequality and $f_{\varepsilon,t}\geq1-t$ give
\[
 V^{-1/n}(1-t)^\alpha
 \leq\mathfrak D_0(T_{\varepsilon,t})
 =V^{-1/n}\int_Xf_{\varepsilon,t}^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}V
 <V^{-1/n}.
\]
The strict inequality follows because $f_{\varepsilon,t}$ is nonconstant and $0<\alpha<1$.
On the disk of radius $r_0\varepsilon$ times the other factors,
$f_{\varepsilon,t}\geq t\beta_0\varepsilon^{-2}$; its volume is $\pi r_0^2\varepsilon^2$. Hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_Xk_{T_{\varepsilon,t}}^p\,\mathrm{d}V
   &\geq V^{-p/n}\pi r_0^2\beta_0^{\alpha p}t^{\alpha p}
                    \varepsilon^{2-2\alpha p},\\
 \|k_{T_{\varepsilon,t}}\|_\infty
   &\geq V^{-1/n}\beta_0^\alpha t^\alpha\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}.
 \end{split}
\]
Take $t=1/\log(1/\varepsilon)$ and $S_\varepsilon=T_{\varepsilon,t}$.
The objective converges to its maximum, while both lower bounds diverge for $p>q$ because $\alpha p>1$. All constants are independent of $\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

Keeping $t\in(0,1)$ fixed in the same construction gives
$\int_Xk_{T_{\varepsilon,t}}^p\,\mathrm{d}V\geq C_{n,p,t}\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha p}$ and
$\|k_{T_{\varepsilon,t}}\|_\infty\geq C_{n,t}\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}$.
Thus failure of higher bounds occurs both on the full normalized cone and along a maximizing sequence for one smooth density. The critical norm is attained by every member of these families; the fixed-density objective is attained only by $T_*$.
