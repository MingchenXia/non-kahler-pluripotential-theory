\section{Subtraction on warped products}
\label{sec:warped-endpoint-subtraction}

The closed core in \cref{sec:nef-boundary-subtraction} keeps the endpoint big. On a warped product, the subtraction cost can instead be computed all the way to an endpoint that is not big. Its rate is determined by two inverse moments of the fiber coefficient, and the total cost remains finite even when the endpoint volume vanishes.

Let $(B,\alpha)$ be a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $m\geq2$, put $W=\int_B\alpha^m$, and let $C$ be a connected compact complex curve. Fix smooth strictly positive probability forms $\sigma$ on $B$ and $w$ on $C$. On $X=B\times C$, of dimension $n=m+1$, use $\mu=\sigma\wedge w$ and omit pullbacks. For a smooth function $a\geq0$ on $B$ and a real number $t>0$, set
\[
 \theta_s=\alpha+(a+t-s)w\qquad(s\leq t).
\]
The endpoint $\theta_t=\alpha+aw$ is semipositive, hence nef. For $\epsilon>0$, write
\[
 H(\epsilon)=\int_B\frac{\sigma}{a+\epsilon},\qquad
 J(\epsilon)=\int_B\frac{\sigma}{(a+\epsilon)^2},\qquad
 H(0)=\int_B\frac{\sigma}{a},
\]
allowing $H(0)=+\infty$, with $1/a=+\infty$ at a zero of $a$.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:warped-endpoint-subtraction}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!warped subtraction cost}
For $s<t$, the infimum defining $\lambda_\mu(\theta_s)$ is attained by a balanced metric, and
\[
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta_s)=\frac{nW}{H(t-s)},\qquad
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta_t)=\frac{nW}{H(0)},
\]
where the last value is zero if $H(0)=+\infty$. Every minimizing Gauduchon metric $g_s$ for $\theta_s$ gives the same cost rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:warped-subtraction-cost}
 \frac{\int_Xw\wedge g_s^{n-1}}{D_\mu(g_s)}
 =\mathcal C(s)
 :=n^{-(n-1)/n}W^{1/n}
        H(t-s)^{-(n+1)/n}J(t-s).
\end{equation}
For every selection of these metrics, the improper integral satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:warped-improper-subtraction}
 \int_0^t\mathcal C(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
 =\lambda_\mu(\theta_0)-\lambda_\mu(\theta_t)<\infty.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $\epsilon=t-s$ and $L=a+\epsilon$. The second paragraph of the proof of \cref{thm:warped-gauduchon-volume} uses neither the normalization $\int_B\kappa^m=1$ nor the conditions on $a$ imposed there. Applied with $\alpha$ in place of $b\kappa$, so that $b^m$ becomes $W$, and with the fiber coefficient $L$, it gives a smooth function $\phi_\epsilon$ on $B$ with
\[
 A_\epsilon=\alpha+\ddc\phi_\epsilon>0,\qquad
 A_\epsilon^m=\frac{W}{H(\epsilon)}\frac{\sigma}{L},\qquad
 \Omega_\epsilon=A_\epsilon+Lw=\theta_s+\ddc\phi_\epsilon,
\]
and shows that $\Omega_\epsilon^n=(nW/H(\epsilon))\mu$ and that the balanced metric $\gamma_s=L^{-1/m}\Omega_\epsilon$ minimizes the quotient, with numerator $nW$ and denominator $D_\mu(\gamma_s)=(nW/H(\epsilon))^{m/n}H(\epsilon)$. This proves the volume formula for $s<t$.

For a fixed Gauduchon metric, the quotient at $\theta_t+\epsilon w$ is affine in $\epsilon$ with nonnegative slope, so it decreases to the quotient at $\theta_t$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. The two infima, over $\epsilon>0$ and over the metrics, commute, as in \cref{thm:gaud-volume-nef-limit}. Hence $\lambda_\mu(\theta_t)$ is the limit of $\lambda_\mu(\theta_s)$ as $s\uparrow t$. Monotone convergence of $H(\epsilon)$ gives the asserted value, including when the integral is infinite.

For the balanced metric $\gamma_s$, the formula $\gamma_s^m=L^{-1}A_\epsilon^m+mA_\epsilon^{m-1}\wedge w$ of \eqref{eq:warped-balanced-cofactor} shows that the numerator of the cost rate is $\int_Xw\wedge\gamma_s^m=WJ(\epsilon)/H(\epsilon)$. Dividing by $D_\mu(\gamma_s)$ gives \eqref{eq:warped-subtraction-cost}. Also $\mathrm{d}H(t-s)/\mathrm{d}s=J(t-s)$, so differentiation of the volume formula gives $-\mathrm{d}\lambda_\mu(\theta_s)/\mathrm{d}s=\mathcal C(s)$. By \cref{prop:gaud-volume-first-variation}, applied in the direction $-w$, this is the cost rate of every minimizing metric, regardless of the regularity of the selection. On each interval $[0,r]$ with $r<t$, integrate this identity. Since $\mathcal C\geq0$ and the root volume has the finite endpoint limit just proved, letting $r\uparrow t$ gives \eqref{eq:warped-improper-subtraction}.
\end{proof}

The identity \eqref{eq:warped-improper-subtraction} applies to every smooth nonnegative coefficient, including coefficients with flat zeros. Finite-order zeros give more precise rates. Fix a Riemannian metric on $B$ for the following distance comparisons.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:warped-subtraction-profiles}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!endpoint cost profiles}
Suppose that $Z=\{a=0\}$ is a nonempty smooth compact real submanifold of positive codimension $d$, that $a\asymp\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,Z)^k$ in a fixed tube for a real number $k>0$, and that $a$ is bounded below by a positive constant outside that tube. Put $r=d/k$. Then, as $\epsilon\downarrow0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:warped-subtraction-profiles}
 \mathcal C(t-\epsilon)\asymp
 \begin{cases}
  \epsilon^{(1-r)/n-1},&r<1,\\
  \epsilon^{-1}(\log(1/\epsilon))^{-(n+1)/n},&r=1,\\
  \epsilon^{r-2},&1<r<2,\\
  \log(1/\epsilon),&r=2,\\
  1,&r>2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Each rate is integrable, and the endpoint volume is positive exactly when $r>1$, that is, when $d>k$. For $r>2$, the cost rate tends to
\[
 n^{-(n-1)/n}W^{1/n}H(0)^{-(n+1)/n}J(0)>0,
 \qquad J(0)=\int_B\frac{\sigma}{a^2}.
\]
For finitely many disjoint zero components satisfying these hypotheses with codimensions $d_i$ and orders $k_i$, the same assertions hold with $r=\min_i d_i/k_i$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The positive density and the tubular Jacobians are bounded above and below by positive constants. For $p=1,2$, radial integration therefore gives
\[
 \int_B\frac{\sigma}{(a+\epsilon)^p}
 \asymp 1+\int_0^R\frac{u^{d-1}}{(u^k+\epsilon)^p}\,\mathrm{d}u
 =1+\frac{\epsilon^{r-p}}k
       \int_0^{R^k/\epsilon}\frac{v^{r-1}}{(1+v)^p}\,\mathrm{d}v,
\]
with the equality applying to the radial expression. For $r<p$ the last integral tends to a finite positive constant; for $r=p$ it grows logarithmically; for $r>p$ the unregularized moment is finite, and monotone convergence gives its positive limit. Applying this to $H$ and $J$ in \eqref{eq:warped-subtraction-cost} proves the five rates and the criterion $H(0)<\infty$ exactly when $r>1$.

The power $(1-r)/n-1$ exceeds $-1$ for $r<1$, as does $r-2$ for $1<r<2$. At $r=1$, substitution of $\log(1/\epsilon)$ gives an integrable tail because $(n+1)/n>1$. The logarithmic and bounded rates are also integrable. When $r>2$, both inverse moments have finite positive limits, which gives the displayed limit. With finitely many disjoint components, the moments are finite sums of positive tube contributions and a bounded complement term; their smallest ratio determines the divergence, without cancellation.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:warped-endpoint-closed-core}\index{Big background!warped endpoints}
The endpoint $\theta_t$ is big exactly when $a$ is positive everywhere. If $a$ has a zero, there is no decomposition
\[
 \theta_t+\ddc f=\chi+\beta
\]
with $f$ smooth and real, $\chi$ a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form with nef class and $\int_X\chi^n>0$, and $\beta\geq0$ a smooth $(1,1)$-form.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every Hermitian form on $X$ is comparable to $\alpha+w$. Suppose therefore that $\theta_t+\ddc u\geq\delta(\alpha+w)$ for a quasi-plurisubharmonic $u$ and $\delta>0$. Pull back an arbitrary smooth nonnegative top-degree form $\zeta$ on $B$. It is $\ddc$-closed by dimension, so Stokes' theorem for distributions gives
\[
 0\leq\int_X(\theta_t+\ddc u-\delta(\alpha+w))\wedge\zeta
       =\int_B(a-\delta)\zeta.
\]
If $a$ has a zero, a nonzero $\zeta$ supported where $a<\delta$ contradicts this inequality, so $\theta_t$ is not big. Conversely, if $a>0$ everywhere, compactness makes $\theta_t$ itself Hermitian, so it is big.

Suppose that $a$ has a zero and that such a decomposition exists. The manifold $X$ is compact K\"ahler, and the closed nef class of $\chi$ has positive top self-intersection, so it is big by the criterion of Demailly and P{\u a}un used in the proof of \cref{thm:closed-core-subtraction}. Hence there are a quasi-plurisubharmonic $v$ and $\delta>0$ with $\chi+\ddc v\geq\delta(\alpha+w)$. Then
\[
 \theta_t+\ddc(f+v)=\chi+\ddc v+\beta
                         \geq\delta(\alpha+w),
\]
and $f+v$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, contradicting the first part.
\end{proof}

In the closed case $a\equiv a_0\geq0$, put $P=\alpha+(a_0+t)w$ and $Q=tw$. These closed nef forms satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:warped-closed-morse}
 \int_XP^n-n\int_XP^{n-1}\wedge Q
 =nWa_0=\mathcal V_\mu(\theta_t).
\end{equation}
This is the lower bound in the transcendental Morse inequality for the difference of the nef classes of $P$ and $Q$, discussed in \cref{sec:history-geometry}; the equality follows from $w^2=0$, $\alpha^{m+1}=0$ and $\int_Cw=1$. For nonconstant $a$, the exterior derivative $\mathrm{d}\theta_s=\mathrm{d}a\wedge w$ is not identically zero, and the endpoint value depends on the probability $\sigma$ through $H(0)$. The comparison in \eqref{eq:warped-closed-morse} therefore concerns the constant closed case.

For example, take $B=\mathbb P^m$, normalized Fubini--Study $\alpha$, $\sigma=\alpha^m$, and $a([Z])=\sum_{j=1}^m|Z_j|^2/\sum_{j=0}^m|Z_j|^2$. The push-forward of $\sigma$ under $a$ is $m x^{m-1}\,\mathrm{d}x$ on $[0,1]$, by the polar integration formula \eqref{eq:radial-volume-integration}. Hence $W=1$, $H(0)=m/(m-1)$ and $\mathcal V_\mu(\theta_t)=(m+1)(m-1)/m>0$, while the endpoint is not big and admits no closed nef core of positive top intersection. Its sole zero has real codimension $2m$ and order two, so $r=m$. The cost rate is therefore logarithmic when $m=2$ and tends to a positive finite value when $m>2$, by \cref{prop:warped-subtraction-profiles}. Replacing $a$ by $ma/(m-1)$ gives $H(0)=1$ and recovers the value $\mathcal V_\mu=n$ of \cref{thm:radial-positive-volume-nonbig}.
