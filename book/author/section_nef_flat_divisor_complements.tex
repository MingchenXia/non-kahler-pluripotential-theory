\subsection{Flat divisors and Hartogs extension}
\label{subsec:flat-divisor-complements}

The curvature in \cref{prop:flat-divisor-rank-one-curvature} vanishes on a tube around the divisor. On that tube the defining logarithm is pluriharmonic, and its regular levels are Levi flat. Their complex leaves depend on the unitary holonomy of the normal line: finite holonomy gives compact fibers of a local holomorphic function, whereas infinite holonomy forces every holomorphic function on the punctured tube to be constant.

Let $S$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, and let $D\subset S$ be a smooth compact connected divisor. Suppose $L=\mathcal O_S(D)$ has a unitary flat metric $h$ on a neighborhood of $D$. Write $s_D$ for its defining section and $N=L|_D$ for the normal line with the induced metric and flat connection, and let $\chi:\pi_1(D)\to\mathrm U(1)$ be its holonomy character. In parallel unit frames, $s_D$ is represented by holomorphic functions $W_j$ satisfying $W_j=t_{jk}W_k$ for locally constant $t_{jk}\in\mathrm U(1)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:flat-divisor-foliated-tube}
There are $R>0$, a neighborhood $U$ of $D$ and a smooth diffeomorphism
\[
 \Upsilon:\{v\in N:|v|<R\}\longrightarrow U
\]
that is the identity on the zero section and preserves the defining scalar: in each parallel normal frame, $W_j(\Upsilon(p,w))=w$. In particular, $|s_D|_h\circ\Upsilon=|w|$. The flat horizontal leaves on each circle bundle $\{|w|=r\}$ correspond to the complex leaves of the compact connected Levi-flat hypersurface
\[
 M_r=\{x\in U:|s_D(x)|_h=r\},\qquad0<r<R.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a smooth normal exponential map $\Upsilon_0$ with complex-linear first normal derivative. The derivative of $s_D$ identifies the normal line with $L|_D$; use this identification to choose that first derivative. Parallel transport along the normal rays identifies $\Upsilon_0^*L$ with the pullback of $N$. In compatible parallel frames one then has
\[
 W_j(\Upsilon_0(p,a))=a+O(|a|^2).
\]
For each $p$, this is a smooth map between real two-dimensional disks whose derivative at zero is the identity. The parameter-dependent inverse function theorem, uniformly over the compact base $D$, gives a unique small $a=a(p,w)$ with $W_j(\Upsilon_0(p,a))=w$. It depends smoothly on $(p,w)$ and has first derivative the identity. On an overlap both $a$ and $w$ change by the same constant unitary factor, so uniqueness makes these corrections agree. After decreasing $R$, the map $(p,w)\mapsto(p,a(p,w))$ is a diffeomorphism of a tube onto its image. Its composition with $\Upsilon_0$ gives $\Upsilon$.

Off $D$, the function $F=\log|s_D|_h^2$ is pluriharmonic. Its radial derivative in the tube is nonzero, so every $M_r$ is a smooth compact connected hypersurface. Locally its complex tangent space is $\ker\mathrm dW_j$, and its leaves are the complex hypersurfaces $W_j=\text{constant}$. The identity $W_j\circ\Upsilon=w$ pulls this distribution back to $\mathrm dw=0$ in flat normal frames. Thus it is precisely the flat horizontal foliation of the circle bundle. This construction is a smooth tubular identification; its base projection is not asserted to be holomorphic.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:flat-divisor-finite-holonomy}
In this setting, $N$ is torsion as a holomorphic line bundle exactly when its unitary holonomy has finite image. If its exact order is $m$, a sufficiently small tube admits a proper surjective holomorphic map
\[
 p:U\longrightarrow\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<R^m\}
\]
with connected fibers and $\operatorname{div}(p)=mD$. Its nonzero fibers are smooth compact complex hypersurfaces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\chi^m=1$, a parallel nowhere-zero section trivializes $N^m$ holomorphically. Conversely, if a holomorphic section $e$ trivializes $N^m$, then $\log|e|^2$ is pluriharmonic on the compact connected manifold $D$. The maximum principle makes it constant. In parallel holomorphic frames the coefficient of $e$ is a holomorphic function of constant modulus, hence constant. Therefore $e$ is parallel and $\chi^m=1$. The least such $m$ is both the holomorphic torsion order and the order of the finite holonomy image.

The tube retracts onto $D$ by \cref{lem:flat-divisor-foliated-tube}. Consequently the flat bundle $L^m$ on $U$ has trivial holonomy and a global parallel unit section $e_U$. Define $p=s_D^m/e_U$. Then $p$ is holomorphic, $\operatorname{div}(p)=mD$ and $|p|=|s_D|_h^m$. The inverse image of a compact subset of the target lies in a closed smaller normal disk bundle and is closed there, proving properness. All target values occur by the tubular model.

Choose local parallel unit frames $e_j$ of $L$ with $e_j^m=e_U$, so $p=W_j^m$. For a nonzero value, the model fiber consists over each base point of the $m$ roots of that value. Parallel transport acts on these roots through $\chi$, whose image has exact order $m$ and acts transitively. Thus this fiber is a connected smooth covering of $D$ as a real manifold. Since $\mathrm dp=mW_j^{m-1}\mathrm dW_j$, it is a smooth complex hypersurface. Compactness follows from properness. The zero fiber has connected support $D$, completing the connected-fiber assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:flat-divisor-holomorphic-rigidity}
\index{Hartogs extension!non-torsion flat divisor}
\index{Levi-flat hypersurface!flat divisor tube}
Suppose $N$ is non-torsion. Every complex leaf of $M_r$ is dense in $M_r$, and
\[
 H^0(U\setminus D,\mathcal O_S)=\mathbb C.
\]
If $S$ is connected, put $M=S\setminus D$. For every compact $K\subset M$ such that $M\setminus K$ is connected, every holomorphic function on $M\setminus K$ is constant and extends holomorphically to $M$. Moreover, for every connected open neighborhood $V$ of $D$, every holomorphic function on $V\setminus D$ is constant and extends to $M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding proposition makes $\chi(\pi_1(D))$ an infinite subgroup of $\mathrm U(1)$. Such a subgroup is dense: a proper closed subgroup of the circle is finite, as follows by lifting it to a closed subgroup of $\mathbb R$ containing $2\pi\mathbb Z$. The circle bundle with its flat horizontal foliation is the suspension of $\chi$. A leaf projects onto $D$, and its intersection with each fiber contains the holonomy orbit of any one of its points. This orbit is dense in the fiber. In a local trivialization, horizontal transport identifies neighboring orbits by a fixed rotation, so the leaf is dense in the whole circle bundle. The tubular lemma gives the corresponding density on $M_r$.

Let $f$ be holomorphic on $U\setminus D$. Its modulus attains a maximum on the compact $M_r$. On the complex leaf through a maximum point, $f$ is bounded by that same maximum and attains it. The maximum modulus principle makes $f$ constant on this connected leaf, and continuity makes it equal to the same constant $c$ on $M_r$. Near any point of $M_r$, choose holomorphic coordinates $(z,W_j)$, with $z\in\mathbb C^{n-1}$. For each fixed $z$, the holomorphic function $W_j\mapsto f(z,W_j)-c$ vanishes on an arc of $|W_j|=r$ lying in its domain. The one-variable identity theorem makes it vanish in that coordinate neighborhood. Analytic continuation on the connected punctured tube proves $f\equiv c$.

A compact $K\subset M$ is disjoint from a sufficiently small tube around the compact $D$. The restriction of a holomorphic function on $M\setminus K$ to this punctured tube is therefore constant. The identity theorem on the connected $M\setminus K$ makes the function constant everywhere, so its constant extension gives the claim. Similarly, every open neighborhood $V$ of $D$ contains a smaller full tube. The complement $V\setminus D$ is connected: paths in the connected manifold $V$ can be perturbed off the smooth real-codimension-two submanifold $D$. The same identity theorem gives the neighborhood-extension assertion. This path argument also shows that $M$ is connected.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:flat-divisor-nonstein}
\index{Stein manifold!flat divisor obstruction}
For any holonomy of $N$, the punctured tube admits no smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function, and $S\setminus D$ is not Stein. In the non-torsion nine-point blowup of \cref{prop:nine-point-anticanonical-curvature}, every holomorphic function on the divisor complement is constant and the compact-hole Hartogs conclusion of \cref{thm:flat-divisor-holomorphic-rigidity} holds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The restriction of a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function to a complex leaf of $M_r$ remains strictly plurisubharmonic. At a maximum on the compact $M_r$ it has a local maximum on that leaf, contradicting the strict submean inequality; the leaf has positive dimension because $n\geq2$.

A Stein manifold is holomorphically separable. In the finite-holonomy case, each nonzero fiber of \cref{prop:flat-divisor-finite-holonomy} is a connected compact positive-dimensional complex hypersurface. Every global holomorphic function is constant on it by the maximum modulus principle, so two of its distinct points cannot be separated. In the infinite-holonomy case, \cref{thm:flat-divisor-holomorphic-rigidity} makes every holomorphic function constant on the punctured tube, again contradicting separation. Finally, \cref{thm:flat-anticanonical-neighborhood} supplies the flat neighborhood for the nine-point example, whose normal line is non-torsion by hypothesis. Apply the rigidity theorem, also with $K=\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Punctured line bundles and elliptic quotients}
\label{subsec:elliptic-affine-quotients}

The obstruction in \cref{cor:flat-divisor-nonstein} uses flat circle levels. For a line bundle of nonzero degree over a compact curve, the coefficients of fiberwise Laurent expansions provide another obstruction. It persists inside a closed holomorphic fiber of an affine quotient, even when the universal cover is Stein.

For a compact subset $K$ of a complex manifold $Y$, its holomorphic hull is
\[
 \widehat K_Y=\{y\in Y:|f(y)|\leq\max_K|f|\text{ for every }f\in\mathcal O(Y)\}.
\]
Holomorphic convexity requires every such hull to be compact. A Stein manifold is holomorphically convex and holomorphically separable.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive-line-puncture-hull}
\index{Holomorphic hull!punctured line bundle}
\index{Line bundle!punctured positive degree}
Let $C$ be a compact connected Riemann surface, and let $A\to C$ be a holomorphic line bundle of positive degree. Write $A^\times$ for its total space with the zero section removed. Then $A^\times$ is not holomorphically convex. More precisely, put $B=A^*$ and choose a smooth Hermitian norm on $B$. Every holomorphic function on $B^\times$ extends holomorphically to $B$, and the holomorphic hull in $B^\times$ of
\[
 K_r=\{v\in B:|v|=r\},\qquad r>0,
\]
contains $\{v:0<|v|\leq r\}$ and is not compact.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a local holomorphic frame of $B$, a function $f\in\mathcal O(B^\times)$ has a fiberwise Laurent expansion
\[
 f(z,t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}a_k(z)t^k.
\]
The Cauchy integral formula makes each coefficient holomorphic in $z$, and the change of frame identifies the coefficients with global sections of $B^{-k}$. For $k<0$, this line bundle has negative degree. It has no nonzero section, since the zero divisor of a nonzero holomorphic section has nonnegative degree. Thus all negative coefficients vanish. On a smaller base chart, Cauchy estimates on a fixed fiber circle give locally uniform convergence of the remaining power series at $t=0$. These extensions agree on overlaps and define a holomorphic function on all of $B$.

The maximum modulus principle on the disk in each fiber gives
\[
 |f(v)|\leq\max_{K_r}|f|\qquad(0<|v|\leq r).
\]
The circle bundle $K_r$ is compact because $C$ is compact. A sequence in one fiber whose positive norms tend to zero lies in its hull and has no convergent subsequence in $B^\times$. Hence the hull is not compact. Fiberwise inversion identifies $A^\times$ biholomorphically with $B^\times$: a nonzero $v\in A$ corresponds to the unique $\ell\in A^*$ with $\ell(v)=1$. This proves the assertion for $A^\times$.
\end{proof}

The following quotient isolates the holomorphic obstruction in an affine-bundle model. Its universal cover is Stein, although the quotient is not.

Let $\Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau\subset\mathbb C$, with $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$, and put $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$. Let $b_\lambda(z)$ be nowhere-zero holomorphic functions satisfying
\[
 b_{\lambda+\gamma}(z)=b_\lambda(z+\gamma)b_\gamma(z).
\]
Suppose the line bundle
\[
 A=(\mathbb C_z\times\mathbb C_t)/\Lambda,
 \qquad\lambda\cdot(z,t)=(z+\lambda,b_\lambda(z)t),
\]
has positive degree.

\begin{example}\label{ex:positive-elliptic-automorphy}
\index{Factor of automorphy!degree-one elliptic bundle}
The factors
\[
 b_{m\tau+n}(z)=\exp\bigl(-\pi\mathrm{i}(m^2\tau+2mz)\bigr)
\]
define a degree-one line bundle on $E$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
For $\gamma=r\tau+s$, the cocycle identity follows from
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b_{m\tau+n}(z+\gamma)b_{r\tau+s}(z)
       &=b_{(m+r)\tau+n+s}(z)\exp(-2\pi\mathrm{i}ms)\\
       &=b_{(m+r)\tau+n+s}(z).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Put $v=\operatorname{Im}\tau$. The squared norm
\[
 |t|^2\exp\bigl(-2\pi(\operatorname{Im}z)^2/v\bigr)
\]
is invariant under the action, because $\operatorname{Im}(z+m\tau+n)=\operatorname{Im}z+mv$ and
\[
 |b_{m\tau+n}(z)|^2=\exp\bigl(2\pi(m^2v+2m\operatorname{Im}z)\bigr).
\]
Its first Chern form is
\[
 \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial
              \left(\frac{2\pi(\operatorname{Im}z)^2}{v}\right)
       =\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2v}\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z.
\]
This positive form integrates to one on a fundamental parallelogram of area $v$. Thus the degree is one.
\end{proof}

On
\[
 Q=\{([x_0:x_1],[w_0:w_1])\in\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1:
                    D=x_0w_1-x_1w_0\ne0\}
\]
define the holomorphic function $\sigma=x_1w_0/D$.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-affine-quotient-nonstein}
\index{Stein manifold!elliptic affine quotient}
Fix $\kappa\in\mathbb C^*$ and $K\in\mathbb C$. For $\lambda=m\tau+n\in\Lambda$, the formula
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lambda\cdot(z,u,[x],[w])={}&
 \bigl(z+\lambda,u+m(K+\kappa\sigma),\\
       &[x_0:b_\lambda(z)x_1],[w_0:b_\lambda(z)w_1]\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
defines a free properly discontinuous holomorphic action on $\mathbb C^2\times Q$. The quotient $Z$ is not Stein. If $K\notin\{0,\kappa\}$, the descended function $\sigma:Z\to\mathbb C$ has the closed smooth fiber
\[
 \sigma^{-1}(-K/\kappa)\simeq\mathbb C\times A^\times,
\]
which is not holomorphically convex. If $K=0$ or $K=\kappa$, the quotient contains a compact elliptic curve. In every case its universal cover is the Stein manifold $\mathbb C^2\times Q$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Simultaneously multiplying $x_1,w_1$ by $b_\lambda(z)$ multiplies $D$ and $x_1w_0$ by the same factor. Thus $\sigma$ is invariant. Its invariance and the cocycle identity for $b$ prove the group law, including addition of the integer $m$. Translation in the $z$ coordinate makes the action free and properly discontinuous: only finitely many lattice translates of the projection of a compact set can meet another fixed compact set. The quotient is therefore a complex manifold, and $\sigma$ descends holomorphically.

Set $a=-K/\kappa$. If $a\notin\{0,-1\}$, every point of $\{\sigma=a\}\subset Q$ has $x_0x_1\ne0$. In the chart
\[
 (t,y)\longmapsto([1:t],[y:ty+1]),
\]
the equation is $ty=a$. Hence this fiber is $\mathbb C^*$, with parameter $t$, and the group action on its preimage is
\[
 \lambda\cdot(z,u,t)=(z+\lambda,u,b_\lambda(z)t).
\]
This gives the asserted biholomorphism with $\mathbb C\times A^\times$. Its submanifold $u=0$ is closed in the fiber and therefore closed in $Z$. Under fiberwise inversion, let $K_r$ be the compact circle bundle in this submanifold from \cref{lem:positive-line-puncture-hull}. Every holomorphic function on $Z$ restricts to a function on $B^\times$. Consequently its hull in $Z$ contains the same sequence with fiber norms tending to zero. That sequence has no convergent subsequence in the closed submanifold, and hence none in $Z$. Thus $Z$ is not holomorphically convex.

For $K=0$, take $q_+=([1:0],[0:1])$, where $\sigma=0$. The invariant submanifold $\{u=0,q=q_+\}$ descends to a copy of $E$. For $K=\kappa$, use $q_-=([0:1],[1:0])$, where $\sigma=-1$. The same conclusion holds. Every holomorphic function on $Z$ is constant on this compact curve by the maximum modulus principle, so holomorphic separation fails.

Finally, the map
\[
 Q\longrightarrow\{(s,c,d)\in\mathbb C^3:cd=s(s-1)\},
 \qquad ([x],[w])\longmapsto
 \left(\frac{x_0w_1}{D},\frac{x_0w_0}{D},\frac{x_1w_1}{D}\right)
\]
is an isomorphism. Its inverse recovers $[x],[w]$ from the column and row lines of the rank-one matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}c&s\\s-1&d\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix never vanishes, and the hypersurface is smooth: simultaneous vanishing of its three defining derivatives would give $c=d=0$, $s=1/2$, contradicting its equation. Thus $Q$ is affine and Stein. Projection to $[x]\in\mathbb P^1$ makes it a locally trivial affine-line bundle. The chart above and the chart over $x_1\ne0$ have transition
\[
 v=1/t,\qquad y_\infty=-t^2y-t.
\]
Its contractible fibers give $\pi_1(Q)=\pi_1(\mathbb P^1)=0$. Therefore $\mathbb C^2\times Q$ is simply connected and Stein, and is the universal cover of $Z$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cotangent torsors on split elliptic ruled surfaces}
\label{subsec:elliptic-cotangent-torsor-classes}

The affine term in \cref{prop:elliptic-affine-quotient-nonstein} is the coordinate form of a cotangent extension on a ruled surface. Its class has one component on the elliptic base and another on the ruling fibers. Their normalization identifies the quotient for every positive line degree.

Let $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$, where $\Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau$ and $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$. Write $\theta$ for the holomorphic one-form on $E$ whose pullback is $\mathrm{d}z$. On a complex manifold $Y$, a holomorphic vector bundle extension
\[
 0\longrightarrow\Omega_Y^1\longrightarrow W
       \xrightarrow{\epsilon}\mathcal O_Y\longrightarrow0,
\]
defines the affine bundle $T_W=\epsilon^{-1}(1)$ modeled on $\Omega_Y^1$. Its class is represented by the differences $s_j-s_i$ of local holomorphic lifts of $1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:elliptic-additive-class}
\index{Cech cocycle!elliptic additive class}
Choose local lifts $z_i:U_i\to\mathbb C$ of $E$, and on each component of an overlap write
\[
 z_i-z_j=m_{ij}\tau+n_{ij},\qquad m_{ij},n_{ij}\in\mathbb Z.
\]
The cocycle $\{m_{ij}\theta\}$ defines a nonzero class $\eta\in H^1(E,\Omega_E^1)$, and this class spans that space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider the vector bundle obtained from $\mathbb C_z\times\mathbb C_a\times\mathbb C_b$ by the action
\[
 (m\tau+n)\cdot(z,a,b)=(z+m\tau+n,a+mb,b).
\]
Its quotient map to $b$ gives an extension of $\mathcal O_E$ by $\Omega_E^1$, identifying the kernel coordinate $a$ with $a\theta$. The local lifts $s_i=(z_i,0,1)$ satisfy $s_j-s_i=m_{ij}\theta$, by expressing $s_i$ in the frame over $z_j$. Thus the extension has class $\eta$.

If it split, its pulled-back holomorphic lift of $1$ would have the form $(z,h(z),1)$ with $h$ entire and
\[
 h(z+1)=h(z),\qquad h(z+\tau)=h(z)+1.
\]
The derivative $h'$ would be lattice periodic, hence constant by the maximum principle on the compact torus. The first identity then forces that constant to be zero, contradicting the second identity. Hence $\eta\ne0$. Serre duality on the compact connected curve gives $H^1(E,\Omega_E^1)\simeq H^0(E,\mathcal O_E)^*\simeq\mathbb C$, proving the spanning assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:elliptic-degree-automorphy-normalization}
\index{Factor of automorphy!positive-degree normalization}
Every holomorphic line bundle $A$ of positive degree $d$ on $E$ admits factors
\[
 b_{m\tau+n}(z)=\chi_{m\tau+n}
       \exp\bigl(-\pi\mathrm{i}d(m^2\tau+2mz)\bigr),
\]
where $\chi:\Lambda\to\mathrm U(1)$ is a character. In particular,
\[
 \frac{b_{m\tau+n}'(z)}{b_{m\tau+n}(z)}=-2\pi\mathrm{i}dm.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $A_d$ be the $d$th tensor power of the degree-one bundle in \cref{ex:positive-elliptic-automorphy}. Its factors are the stated exponential factors and its degree is $d$. The bundle $F=A\otimes A_d^*$ has degree zero. Start with a smooth Hermitian metric on $F$. Its Chern curvature has integral zero. Solvability of the scalar Poisson equation on the flat compact torus supplies a smooth real function whose addition to the metric weight makes that curvature zero. The resulting Chern connection is flat and unitary.

On the simply connected cover $\mathbb C$, parallel transport gives a global parallel unit frame of the pullback of $F$. It is holomorphic because the $(0,1)$ part of the Chern connection is the holomorphic structure. Each lattice element changes this frame by a constant unitary factor. These factors form a character. Tensoring with the given frame of $A_d$ supplies the factors for $A$. Differentiation gives the last formula.
\end{proof}

Put $M=\mathbb P_{\mathrm{lines}}(\mathcal O_E\oplus A)$ and let $f:M\to E$ be its projection. Let $C_\infty=\mathbb P_{\mathrm{lines}}(A)$. Use affine coordinates $t_i$ away from $C_\infty$ and $v_i=1/t_i$ away from the other section. Over $U_i\cap U_j$,
\[
 t_i=b_{ij}t_j,\quad v_i=b_{ij}^{-1}v_j,
 \qquad b_{ij}=b_{m_{ij}\tau+n_{ij}}(z_j).
\]
Define $\xi\in H^1(M,\Omega_M^1)$ by the logarithmic cocycle of $\mathcal O_M(-C_\infty)$. More precisely, use its local defining functions $q_{i,0}=1$ and $q_{i,\infty}=v_i$ and the cocycle $\mathrm{d}\log(q_{j,b}/q_{i,a})$. This convention contains no factor $1/(2\pi\mathrm{i})$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:split-elliptic-cotangent-cohomology}
\index{Ruled surface!cotangent cohomology}
There is a direct sum
\[
 H^1(M,\Omega_M^1)=\mathbb C\xi\oplus\mathbb C f^*\eta.
\]
The restriction of $c=\rho\xi+Kf^*\eta$ to any ruling fiber is nonzero exactly when $\rho\ne0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The relative cotangent sequence is
\[
 0\longrightarrow f^*\Omega_E^1\longrightarrow\Omega_M^1
      \longrightarrow\Omega_{M/E}^1\longrightarrow0.
\]
The two affine charts of a projective line compute
\[
 f_*\mathcal O_M=\mathcal O_E,\quad R^1f_*\mathcal O_M=0,
 \qquad f_*\Omega_{M/E}^1=0,\quad
 R^1f_*\Omega_{M/E}^1=\mathcal O_E.
\]
For the last identity, a Laurent cocycle $g(z,t)\,\mathrm{d}t$ is reduced, by forms holomorphic on the two charts, to its coefficient of $\mathrm{d}t/t$. Its residue is unchanged by $t_i=b_{ij}t_j$, with differentials taken relative to the base. Thus the local generators glue to the trivial line. For functions the two Laurent halves exhaust the cocycle, giving the vanishing of $R^1f_*\mathcal O_M$. The degree-zero direct images follow from the absence of nonconstant functions and holomorphic one-forms on $\mathbb P^1$.

The Leray exact sequences and the one-dimensional base now give
\[
 H^1(M,f^*\Omega_E^1)\simeq H^1(E,\Omega_E^1),\quad
 H^2(M,f^*\Omega_E^1)=0,\quad
 H^1(M,\Omega_{M/E}^1)\simeq\mathbb C.
\]
The cotangent sequence consequently gives an exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathbb C f^*\eta
 \longrightarrow H^1(M,\Omega_M^1)\longrightarrow\mathbb C\longrightarrow0.
\]
On a fiber, the class $\xi$ is represented on the two affine charts by $\mathrm{d}\log v=-\mathrm{d}t/t$, whose residue is nonzero. It splits the sequence. The pulled-back base class restricts to zero, proving both assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:split-elliptic-cotangent-torsor}
\index{Canonical extension!split elliptic ruled surface}
\index{Affine bundle!cotangent torsor}
Let $W$ be a holomorphic extension of $\mathcal O_M$ by $\Omega_M^1$ with class $c=\rho\xi+Kf^*\eta$, where $\rho\ne0$. Then $T_W$ is biholomorphic to the quotient in \cref{prop:elliptic-affine-quotient-nonstein}, with the factors of \cref{lem:elliptic-degree-automorphy-normalization} and
\[
 \kappa=2\pi\mathrm{i}d\rho.
\]
In particular, $T_W$ is not Stein and its universal cover is Stein. This applies to the canonical extension associated with any K\"ahler class on $M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Over $\mathbb C_z\times\mathbb P^1$, glue two affine cotangent charts by
\[
 v=1/t,\qquad u_\infty=u,\qquad
 y_\infty=-t^2y-\rho t.
\]
The linear part is the change of coefficients of $u\,\mathrm{d}z+y\,\mathrm{d}t$. If $s_0,s_\infty$ are the zero lifts in these charts, the affine term says
\[
 s_\infty-s_0=\rho\,\mathrm{d}v/v.
\]
For $\lambda=m\tau+n$, give the first chart the lattice action
\[
 (z,t,u,y)\longmapsto
 \bigl(z+\lambda,b_\lambda(z)t,
 u+Km-(b_\lambda'/b_\lambda)ty,y/b_\lambda(z)\bigr).
\]
Its linear fiber part is the transport of cotangent vectors under $(z,t)\mapsto(z+\lambda,b_\lambda(z)t)$. The factor identity and invariance of $ty$ give the group law. In the other chart it becomes
\[
 (z,v,u_\infty,y_\infty)\longmapsto
 \bigl(z+\lambda,b_\lambda^{-1}v,
 u_\infty+Km+(b_\lambda'/b_\lambda)(v y_\infty+\rho),
 b_\lambda y_\infty\bigr),
\]
which is holomorphic also at $v=0$. The two formulas agree on their overlap. Lattice translation in $z$ gives a free properly discontinuous action.

We compute the descended torsor class using the local lifts $z_i$. On the two types of base overlaps its zero lifts satisfy
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 s_{j,0}-s_{i,0}&=Km_{ij}f^*\theta,\\
 s_{j,\infty}-s_{i,\infty}
   &=Km_{ij}f^*\theta+\rho\,\mathrm{d}\log(v_j/v_i).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For a mixed overlap the same formulas and $s_{j,\infty}-s_{j,0}=\rho\,\mathrm{d}v_j/v_j$ give
\[
 s_{j,\infty}-s_{i,0}=Km_{ij}f^*\theta+\rho\,\mathrm{d}v_j/v_j.
\]
These are precisely the cocycles defining $Kf^*\eta+\rho\xi$. Two extensions with the same class have local splitting cocycles differing by a coboundary. Changing their local lifts by that coboundary identifies their inverse images of $1$. Thus this constructed torsor is $T_W$.

Since $\rho\ne0$, set $Y=y/\rho$ and $Y_\infty=y_\infty/\rho$. Their transition is $Y_\infty=-t^2Y-t$. The maps
\[
 (t,Y)\longmapsto([1:t],[Y:tY+1]),\qquad
 (v,Y_\infty)\longmapsto([v:1],[vY_\infty+1:Y_\infty])
\]
agree on overlaps and identify these charts with $Q$. In the first chart, $\sigma=tY=ty/\rho$. Substituting $b_\lambda'/b_\lambda=-2\pi\mathrm{i}dm$ gives the quotient action with $\kappa=2\pi\mathrm{i}d\rho$. The non-Stein assertion and the Stein universal cover follow from \cref{prop:elliptic-affine-quotient-nonstein}.

Finally, a K\"ahler class has nonzero restriction to a ruling fiber, because its integral on that fiber is positive. Its cotangent extension therefore has $\rho\ne0$. For an extension $0\to\mathcal O_M\to V\to T_M\to0$ associated with this class, the dual extension has the negative of that class, so still has nonzero fiber restriction. The complement $\mathbb P(V)\setminus\mathbb P(T_M)$, using the quotient convention for projectivization, identifies with $\epsilon^{-1}(1)\subset V^*$: normalize a nonzero quotient functional to take value one on the distinguished copy of $\mathcal O_M$. This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Symmetric tensors on split elliptic ruled surfaces}
\label{subsec:split-elliptic-symmetric-tensors}

Let $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$, where $\Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau$ and $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$. Let $A$ have positive degree $d$, and put $M=\mathbb P_{\mathrm{lines}}(\mathcal O_E\oplus A)$. Use the normalized factors of \cref{lem:elliptic-degree-automorphy-normalization}, so that
\[
 b_\lambda(z)=\chi_\lambda
       \exp\bigl(-\pi\mathrm{i}d(m^2\tau+2mz)\bigr),
 \qquad q_\lambda=-b_\lambda'/b_\lambda=2\pi\mathrm{i}dm,
 \qquad \lambda=m\tau+n.
\]
A symmetric tangent tensor is a polynomial function on the cotangent bundle, homogeneous in its cotangent fiber variables. Write $u\,\mathrm{d}z+y\,\mathrm{d}t$ for a cotangent vector in the finite ruling chart. The Euler field $t\partial/\partial t$ gives the global linear function $h=ty$. The ruling action decomposes these polynomials into weights. Their dimensions measure the abundance of symmetric tensors, while their products detect the obstruction to finite generation.

Let $U$ be the rank-two bundle on $E$ whose sections on the universal cover are linear polynomials in formal variables $h,u$ satisfying
\[
 F(z+\lambda,h,u)=F(z,h,u-q_\lambda h).
\]
Thus the invariant polynomial $h$ and the coefficient of $u$ give an exact sequence $0\to\mathcal O_Eh\to U\to\mathcal O_E\to0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:split-elliptic-tensor-weights}
\index{Weight decomposition!cotangent polynomials}
\index{Symmetric tensor!elliptic ruled surface}
For every integer $r\geq0$, the space $H^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M)$ has a finite weight decomposition with weights $-r\leq\ell\leq r$. The weight-zero space is $H^0(E,\operatorname{Sym}^rU)$. For $1\leq k\leq r$, the weight $-k$ space is
\[
 H^0(E,A^k\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{r-k}U),
\]
and the weight $k$ space is $H^0(E,A^{-k}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{r-k}U)$. Multiplication adds the tensor degrees and the weights.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the cover $\mathbb C\times\mathbb P^1$, ordinary cotangent coordinates change by
\[
 v=1/t,\qquad u_\infty=u,\qquad y_\infty=-t^2y.
\]
The lattice acts by
\[
 (z,t,u,y)\longmapsto
 (z+\lambda,b_\lambda t,u+q_\lambda ty,y/b_\lambda).
\]
These formulas follow by preserving the one-form $u\,\mathrm{d}z+y\,\mathrm{d}t$. In the other ruling chart the action is
\[
 (z,v,u_\infty,y_\infty)\longmapsto
 (z+\lambda,b_\lambda^{-1}v,
 u_\infty-q_\lambda v y_\infty,b_\lambda y_\infty).
\]
It is holomorphic at $v=0$ and agrees with the finite-chart formula. Both formulas preserve $h=ty=-v y_\infty$.

A degree-$r$ homogeneous function is a sum of terms $g_b(z,t)y^bu^{r-b}$ for $0\leq b\leq r$, with $g_b$ holomorphic on $\mathbb C^2$. Holomorphy at $v=0$ says that $v^{2b}g_b(z,1/v)$ is holomorphic there. Its Taylor or Laurent expansion consequently gives
\[
 g_b(z,t)=\sum_{a=0}^{2b}c_{a,b}(z)t^a,
\]
with entire coefficients. No cancellation between different $b$ is possible, since the monomials in $u_\infty,y_\infty$ are independent.

Give $t$ weight $1$, $y$ weight $-1$ and $u$ weight $0$. A term $t^ay^bu^{r-b}$ has weight $\ell=a-b$. Both chart changes and the lattice preserve this grading; equivalently, it is the cotangent lift of the global action $t\mapsto at$ for $a\in\mathbb C^*$. The bounds on $a$ imply $-r\leq\ell\leq r$.

For $\ell=-k<0$, a weight block is uniquely $y^kF(z,h,u)$, with $F$ homogeneous of degree $r-k$. Its invariance is equivalent to
\[
 F(z+\lambda,h,u)
       =b_\lambda(z)^kF(z,h,u-q_\lambda h).
\]
This is the factor for $A^k\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{r-k}U$. Conversely, each such polynomial defines a function holomorphic in both ruling charts: the exponents satisfy $0\leq a\leq2b$. For $\ell=k>0$, a weight block is uniquely $t^kh^kF(z,h,u)$ of the same remaining degree. Its invariance has the factor $b_\lambda^{-k}$, giving $A^{-k}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{r-k}U$. Weight zero consists precisely of homogeneous polynomials $F(z,h,u)$ with the factor defining $\operatorname{Sym}^rU$. Products of polynomial functions add degrees and weights, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:elliptic-unipotent-symmetric-cohomology}
\index{Unipotent bundle!elliptic symmetric powers}
The extension $U$ is nonsplit. For every $s\geq0$, one has
\[
 H^0(E,\operatorname{Sym}^sU)=\mathbb C h^s.
\]
For $k\geq1$ and $s\geq0$, one has
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^0(E,A^{-k}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^sU)&=0,\\
 H^1(E,A^k\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^sU)&=0,\\
 h^0(E,A^k\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^sU)&=kd(s+1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For $s\geq1$, the map taking the coefficient of $u^s$ gives a surjection
\[
 H^0(E,A\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^sU)\longrightarrow H^0(E,A),
\]
whose kernel is $hH^0(E,A\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{s-1}U)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A splitting of $U$ would give a section $u+a(z)h$ lifting $1$, with $a$ entire and
\[
 a(z+1)=a(z),\qquad a(z+\tau)=a(z)-2\pi\mathrm{i}d.
\]
Differentiating shows that $a'$ is lattice periodic, hence constant on the compact torus. The first identity forces that constant to vanish, contradicting the second. Thus the extension is nonsplit.

More generally, write an invariant polynomial of degree $s$ as
\[
 F(z,h,u)=\sum_{j=0}^s a_j(z)h^{s-j}u^j.
\]
If its largest nonzero $u$-degree is $j>0$, then $a_j$ is lattice periodic and therefore a nonzero constant. Comparing the next coefficient gives
\[
 a_{j-1}(z+1)=a_{j-1}(z),\qquad
 a_{j-1}(z+\tau)=a_{j-1}(z)-j(2\pi\mathrm{i}d)a_j.
\]
The same derivative argument gives a contradiction. Hence $F=a_0h^s$, with $a_0$ constant. This also treats $s=0$.

The powers of the invariant line $\mathcal O_Eh$ give a filtration of $\operatorname{Sym}^sU$ with $s+1$ trivial line quotients. A negative-degree line bundle on a compact curve has no nonzero section, since the zero divisor of a nonzero section has nonnegative degree. The filtration twisted by $A^{-k}$ thus has no global section. Serre duality and $\Omega_E^1\simeq\mathcal O_E$ give $H^1(E,A^k)=0$. The positive twist of the filtration and its cohomology exact sequences then give $H^1(E,A^k\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^sU)=0$. Riemann--Roch on the genus-one curve, with rank $s+1$ and degree $kd(s+1)$, gives the dimension formula.

Finally, the leading coefficient gives the bundle exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow A\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{s-1}U
 \xrightarrow{\ h\ }A\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^sU
 \longrightarrow A\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed, translation of $u$ by a multiple of $h$ leaves its leading coefficient unchanged, and a polynomial with zero leading coefficient is divisible by $h$. The vanishing just proved gives the asserted surjectivity and kernel on global sections.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:split-elliptic-symmetric-dimensions}
\index{Vector bundle!big tangent bundle}
\index{Volume!tautological line bundle}
For every integer $r\geq0$,
\[
 h^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M)
       =1+\frac{d\,r(r+1)(r+2)}6.
\]
In the quotient convention for $\pi:\mathbb P(T_M)\to M$, the tautological line bundle $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(T_M)}(1)$ is big and has volume $d$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The positive weights vanish, the zero weight contributes one dimension and the weight $-k$ contributes $kd(r-k+1)$. Thus
\[
 h^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M)
 =1+d\sum_{k=1}^r k(r-k+1)
 =1+\frac{d\,r(r+1)(r+2)}6.
\]
The elliptic curve and its holomorphic line bundle are algebraic, so $M$ and $\mathbb P(T_M)$ are projective. The projective-bundle identity $\pi_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(T_M)}(r)=\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M$ identifies their section spaces. Since $\mathbb P(T_M)$ has dimension three, the definition of volume gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}\bigl(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(T_M)}(1)\bigr)
 =\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{6h^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M)}{r^3}=d>0.
\]
Positive volume is the section-growth criterion for bigness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:split-elliptic-tangent-ring-nonfinite}
\index{Section ring!nonfinite generation}
The complex algebra
\[
 R=\bigoplus_{r\geq0}H^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M)
\]
is not finitely generated.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $R_{r,k}$ for tensor degree $r$ and weight $-k$. The surviving weights have $k\geq0$, and multiplication adds these indices. The zero-weight subalgebra is $\mathbb C[h]$. Let $I$ be the ideal consisting of all components of positive $k$, and form
\[
 S=R/(hR+I^2).
\]
Its zero-weight component is $\mathbb C$. Its positive-weight ideal $J$ satisfies $J^2=0$, so $S=\mathbb C\oplus J$ as a vector space.

For every $r\geq1$, products in $I^2$ have weight index at least two. Consequently, the degree-$r$, weight-index-one component of $S$ is
\[
 R_{r,1}/hR_{r-1,1}\simeq H^0(E,A).
\]
For $r=1$, this is the identity and $R_{0,1}=0$. For $r\geq2$, it follows from the last assertion of \cref{lem:elliptic-unipotent-symmetric-cohomology}, with $s=r-1$. Each of these components has dimension $d>0$. Since their degrees are distinct, $J$ is infinite dimensional.

If $R$ were finitely generated, its quotient $S$ would be finitely generated. Write a finite set of algebra generators of $S$ as $c_i+j_i$, with $c_i\in\mathbb C$ and $j_i\in J$. Because $J^2=0$, every polynomial in these generators lies in the finite-dimensional span of $1,j_1,\ldots,j_N$. This contradicts the infinite dimension of $J$, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Section algebras of cotangent extensions}
\label{subsec:elliptic-cotangent-extension-algebra}

Continue with $M=\mathbb P_{\mathrm{lines}}(\mathcal O_E\oplus A)$, where $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$, $\Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau$, $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$, and $\deg A=d>0$. Use the class generators of \cref{lem:split-elliptic-cotangent-cohomology}. Let $W$ be an extension of $\mathcal O_M$ by $\Omega_M^1$ with class $\rho\xi+Kf^*\eta$, where $\rho\ne0$, and put $V=W^*$. The homogeneous version of the charts in \cref{thm:split-elliptic-cotangent-torsor} adds the quotient coordinate $w$. On the elliptic cover they change by
\[
 v=1/t,\qquad u_\infty=u,\qquad
 y_\infty=-t^2y-\rho t w,\qquad w_\infty=w.
\]
For $\lambda=m\tau+n$, the lattice acts by
\[
 (z,t,u,y,w)\longmapsto
 (z+\lambda,b_\lambda t,u+m(Kw+q_0ty),y/b_\lambda,w),
 \qquad q_0=2\pi\mathrm{i}d.
\]
Here $b_\lambda$ is the normalized factor in \cref{lem:elliptic-degree-automorphy-normalization}. Fiber-homogeneous functions on $W$ represent symmetric tensors of $V$. Their ruling-coordinate algebra retains the quadratic relation of the affine torsor, while the elliptic monodromy controls which weights descend.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:elliptic-extension-fiber-ring}
\index{Quadric!cotangent-extension functions}
\index{Weight decomposition!cotangent extension}
On $\mathbb C\times\mathbb P^1$, the graded algebra of fiber-homogeneous polynomial functions on the pulled-back $W$ is
\[
 \mathcal O(\mathbb C)
 [c_-,c_+,h,w,u]/\bigl(c_-c_+-h(h+\rho w)\bigr),
\]
with every displayed variable of degree one. In the finite ruling chart,
\[
 c_-=y,\qquad c_+=t(ty+\rho w),\qquad h=ty.
\]
The lattice fixes $h,w$, multiplies $c_-$ by $b_\lambda^{-1}$ and $c_+$ by $b_\lambda$, and sends $u$ to $u+m(q_0h+Kw)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $s_1=y$ and $s_2=ty+\rho w$ in the finite chart. Since $\rho\ne0$, these are invertible linear coordinates in place of $y,w$, with $w=(s_2-ts_1)/\rho$. In the infinite chart put
\[
 (s_{1,\infty},s_{2,\infty})
       =(-v y_\infty-\rho w,-y_\infty).
\]
The transition is $(s_{1,\infty},s_{2,\infty})=t(s_1,s_2)$. Thus the two coordinates are those of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)^{\oplus2}$, while $u$ is the coordinate of a trivial line.

A polynomial homogeneous of degree $j$ in $s_1,s_2$ has ruling coefficients of degree at most $j$ in $t$, by holomorphy in the infinite chart. Every admissible monomial is a product of $s_1,s_2,ts_1,ts_2$: distribute its $t$-exponent among its $j$ fiber factors. These four generators satisfy the determinant relation
\[
 s_1(ts_2)-(ts_1)s_2=0.
\]
There are no further relations. Indeed, the dimension in degree $j$ of the function space is $(j+1)^2$, by counting the $j+1$ fiber monomials and $j+1$ ruling coefficients. The polynomial algebra in four variables modulo this nonzero quadratic relation has the same dimension
\[
 \binom{j+3}{3}-\binom{j+1}{3}=(j+1)^2,
\]
where the second term is zero for $j<2$. The surjective graded map is consequently an isomorphism in every degree. Including $u$ adjoins a polynomial variable. The argument is uniform in $z$ and all coefficient functions are entire.

Identify the four generators as $c_-=s_1$, $h=ts_1$, $h+\rho w=s_2$, and $c_+=ts_2$. Their determinant relation becomes the stated quadratic relation. They are polynomial also in the infinite chart, where
\[
 c_-=-v^2y_\infty-\rho v w,\qquad
 c_+=-y_\infty,\qquad h=-v y_\infty-\rho w.
\]
The lattice formulas follow by substitution in the finite chart, proving the assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:elliptic-extension-polynomial-ring}
\index{Section ring!canonical extension}
\index{Canonical extension!section algebra}
There is a degree-preserving isomorphism of complex algebras
\[
 \bigoplus_{r\geq0}H^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rV)
 \simeq
 \left(\bigoplus_{r\geq0}H^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M)\right)[w],
\]
where the variable $w$ on the right has degree one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Give $c_-$ weight $-1$, $c_+$ weight $1$, and $h,w,u$ weight zero. These weights come from the ruling action and are preserved by the quadratic relation and lattice. Reducing products $c_-c_+$ shows that a weight $-k$ polynomial is uniquely $c_-^kF(z,h,w,u)$, and a weight $k$ polynomial is uniquely $c_+^kF(z,h,w,u)$, for $k\geq1$. Here $F$ has remaining degree $r-k$ in tensor degree $r$. Weight zero consists of $F(z,h,w,u)$.

For uniqueness, embed the relation algebra into
\[
 \mathcal O(\mathbb C)[h,w,u,c_-,c_-^{-1}],
 \qquad c_+\longmapsto h(h+\rho w)/c_-.
\]
The reduced expressions have distinct powers of $c_-$; their coefficients can vanish only if the original coefficients vanish, since $h(h+\rho w)$ is a nonzero polynomial. This proves both the weight decomposition and its directness.

Let $U_3$ be the coefficient bundle of linear polynomials in $h,w,u$, with lattice rule
\[
 F(z+\lambda,h,w,u)
       =F(z,h,w,u-m(q_0h+Kw)).
\]
The negative weight $-k$ block is $H^0(E,A^k\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{r-k}U_3)$, and the positive weight $k$ block has $A^{-k}$ in place of $A^k$. This follows from the factors of $c_-$ and $c_+$ in the preceding lemma, using the inverse translation of $u$ when expressing invariance. In particular, the signs of the line twists agree with \cref{lem:split-elliptic-tensor-weights}.

Since $q_0\ne0$, put $H=h+(K/q_0)w$. Then the translation of $u$ is $u\mapsto u-mq_0H$. The coefficient bundle is consequently
\[
 U_3\simeq U\oplus\mathcal O_Ew,
\]
where the variable $h$ of the previously defined $U$ is replaced by $H$. Its symmetric powers are the direct sums of $w^j\operatorname{Sym}^{s-j}U$ for $0\leq j\leq s$. The negative-twist vanishing in \cref{lem:elliptic-unipotent-symmetric-cohomology} eliminates every positive weight. The same lemma identifies the zero-weight section algebra with $\mathbb C[H,w]$.

In each negative weight, expansion in powers of $w$ identifies the remaining spaces with the corresponding tangent blocks in \cref{lem:split-elliptic-tensor-weights}, with a polynomial variable $w$ adjoined. Send their formal Euler variable $h$ to $H$ and their leading factor $y^k$ to $c_-^k$. This gives bijections in every tensor degree and weight. Products between surviving blocks multiply their coefficients and add their powers of $c_-$; the eliminated positive weights contribute no products. The bijections therefore preserve multiplication and give the asserted graded algebra isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:elliptic-extension-symmetric-dimensions}
\index{Volume!canonical-extension tautological line}
For every integer $r\geq0$,
\[
 h^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rV)
       =r+1+d\binom{r+3}{4}.
\]
The tautological line on the quotient projective bundle $\mathbb P(V)$ is big of volume $d$, and the section algebra of $V$ is not finitely generated. These conclusions apply to every K\"ahler canonical extension on $M$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The polynomial-algebra isomorphism and \cref{thm:split-elliptic-symmetric-dimensions} give
\[
 h^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rV)
 =\sum_{j=0}^r h^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^jT_M)
 =r+1+d\sum_{j=0}^r\binom{j+2}{3}
 =r+1+d\binom{r+3}{4}.
\]
Here $\binom{2}{3}=0$. The surface $M$ is projective, and coherent GAGA makes its holomorphic bundle $V$ algebraic. Thus $\mathbb P(V)$ is a projective fourfold, and its tautological volume is the limit of $24h^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rV)/r^4$, equal to $d>0$. Thus the line is big. A finitely generated section algebra of $V$ would have a finitely generated quotient by $w$, contradicting \cref{thm:split-elliptic-tangent-ring-nonfinite}.

For a K\"ahler canonical extension $0\to\mathcal O_M\to V\to T_M\to0$, the dual cotangent extension has nonzero ruling-fiber component by \cref{thm:split-elliptic-cotangent-torsor}. Its class therefore has $\rho\ne0$ in the conventions used here. The same argument applies, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cotangent extensions with zero ruling component}
\label{subsec:elliptic-zero-ruling-extension}

Let $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$, with $\Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau$ and $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$. Let $A$ have degree $d>0$, and put $M=\mathbb P_{\mathrm{lines}}(\mathcal O_E\oplus A)$, with projection $f:M\to E$. Let $\eta,\theta$ be the class and form of \cref{lem:elliptic-additive-class}. Use the normalized factors of \cref{lem:elliptic-degree-automorphy-normalization}. Let $W$ be an extension of $\mathcal O_M$ by $\Omega_M^1$ with class $Kf^*\eta$, and put $V=W^*$. The homogeneous charts of \cref{thm:split-elliptic-cotangent-torsor}, now with zero ruling component, have transitions
\[
 v=1/t,\qquad u_\infty=u,\qquad
 y_\infty=-t^2y,\qquad w_\infty=w.
\]
For $\lambda=m\tau+n$, the lattice action is
\[
 (z,t,u,y,w)\longmapsto
 (z+\lambda,b_\lambda t,u+m(q_0ty+Kw),y/b_\lambda,w),
 \qquad q_0=2\pi\mathrm{i}d.
\]
For the local lift differences $z_i-z_j=m_{ij}\tau+n_{ij}$, the zero lifts in these charts differ by $Km_{ij}f^*\theta$ on base overlaps and agree on the two ruling charts, so they give the class $Kf^*\eta$. The linear parts are the cotangent transitions, and the inverse ruling chart gives the holomorphic lattice formula $u_\infty\mapsto u_\infty+m(Kw-q_0v y_\infty)$. Thus the same cocycle computation constructs $W$ also when its ruling component is zero. The fiberwise splitting used for a nonzero ruling component is unavailable, but ordinary cotangent polynomials give the homogeneous algebra directly.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:elliptic-zero-ruling-quadric}
\index{Quadric!zero-ruling cotangent extension}
The graded algebra of fiber-homogeneous polynomial functions on $W$ over $\mathbb C\times\mathbb P^1$ is
\[
 \mathcal O(\mathbb C)[c_-,c_+,h,u,w]/(c_-c_+-h^2),
 \qquad c_-=y,\quad c_+=t^2y,\quad h=ty,
\]
where all five variables have degree one. The lattice fixes $h,w$, sends $c_-$ to $b_\lambda^{-1}c_-$ and $c_+$ to $b_\lambda c_+$, and translates $u$ by $m(q_0h+Kw)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Holomorphy in the infinite ruling chart bounds the degree in $t$ of the coefficient of $y^j$ by $2j$, as in \cref{lem:split-elliptic-tensor-weights}; the extra variable $w$ is unchanged in that chart. Every monomial $t^ay^j$ with $0\leq a\leq2j$ is a product of $y,ty,t^2y$, by distributing the exponent $a$ into $j$ integers in $\{0,1,2\}$. These generators satisfy $y(t^2y)=(ty)^2$.

In degree $j$ their function space has dimension $2j+1$. The polynomial algebra in three variables modulo the displayed nonzero quadratic relation has the same dimension
\[
 \binom{j+2}{2}-\binom{j}{2}=2j+1,
\]
where the second term is zero for $j<2$. The surjective graded map is consequently an isomorphism. Adjoining the two trivial fiber coordinates $u,w$ gives the stated algebra, with entire coefficients in $z$. In the other chart,
\[
 c_-=-v^2y_\infty,\qquad c_+=-y_\infty,
 \qquad h=-v y_\infty,
\]
so all generators are holomorphic. Substitution gives their lattice transformations.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-zero-ruling-section-ring}
\index{Section ring!zero-ruling cotangent extension}
There is a degree-preserving algebra isomorphism
\[
 \bigoplus_{r\geq0}H^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rV)
 \simeq
 \left(\bigoplus_{r\geq0}H^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M)\right)[w],
 \qquad \deg w=1.
\]
Consequently the dimension, volume and nonfinite-generation conclusions of \cref{cor:elliptic-extension-symmetric-dimensions} hold also for this extension.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Give $c_-,c_+$ weights $-1,1$, and $h,u,w$ weight zero. Reducing $c_-c_+$ by $h^2$ yields the negative blocks $c_-^kF(z,h,u,w)$, the positive blocks $c_+^kF(z,h,u,w)$ and the zero block $F$. They are unique: substituting $c_+=h^2/c_-$ embeds the relation algebra into $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)[h,u,w,c_-,c_-^{-1}]$, and distinct reduced weights have distinct Laurent powers with nonzero polynomial coefficients. In tensor degree $r$, the remaining polynomial degree is $r-k$.

The coefficient bundle $U_3$ has inverse translation $u\mapsto u-m(q_0h+Kw)$. Thus the weight $-k$ block is $H^0(E,A^k\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{r-k}U_3)$, the weight $k$ block has $A^{-k}$ instead, and the zero block is $H^0(E,\operatorname{Sym}^rU_3)$. Put $H=h+(K/q_0)w$. Since $q_0\ne0$, this identifies $U_3$ with the previously defined $U$, using $H$ in place of its Euler variable, plus the trivial line generated by $w$.

By \cref{lem:elliptic-unipotent-symmetric-cohomology}, all positive weights vanish and the zero-weight algebra is $\mathbb C[H,w]$. Expanding the negative blocks in powers of $w$ identifies them with the tangent blocks of \cref{lem:split-elliptic-tensor-weights}. The substitutions $h\mapsto H$ and $y^k\mapsto c_-^k$ give bijections in every degree and surviving weight. Their products multiply the coefficient polynomials and add the powers of $c_-$, so these bijections preserve multiplication. This proves the algebra isomorphism. Summing the tangent dimensions and taking the quotient by $w$ gives the remaining assertions exactly as in \cref{cor:elliptic-extension-symmetric-dimensions}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-zero-ruling-torsor-cover}
\index{Stein manifold!zero-ruling torsor obstruction}
\index{Universal cover!zero-ruling cotangent torsor}
The cotangent torsor $T_W$ is not Stein. Its universal cover is
\[
 \mathbb C^2\times\operatorname{Tot}
                      (\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)),
\]
which is also not Stein.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $w=1$. The subset $y=0$ in the finite ruling chart agrees with $y_\infty=0$ in the other chart. It descends to a submanifold fibered over
\[
 B_K=\mathbb C^2_{z,u}/\Lambda,\qquad
 (m\tau+n)\cdot(z,u)=(z+m\tau+n,u+Km),
\]
with fibers $\mathbb P^1$. The quotient is a complex manifold because translation in $z$ gives a free properly discontinuous action. Every ruling fiber is a compact connected curve in $T_W$, and holomorphic functions are constant on it. Thus $T_W$ is not holomorphically separable and is not Stein.

Before the lattice quotient, the affine charts have no affine ruling term and glue to $\mathbb C^2_{z,u}\times\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2))$. This space is simply connected: contraction of each vector-bundle fiber retracts its last factor onto $\mathbb P^1$, while $\mathbb C^2$ is contractible. The lattice action is free and properly discontinuous, so this is the universal cover. The zero section contains a compact projective line, again obstructing holomorphic separation and hence Steinness.
\end{proof}

Thus the zero and nonzero ruling classes have isomorphic graded section algebras, although \cref{thm:split-elliptic-cotangent-torsor} gives a Stein universal cover only for the nonzero ruling classes.
