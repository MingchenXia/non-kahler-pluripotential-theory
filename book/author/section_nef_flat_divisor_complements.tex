\subsection{Flat divisors and Hartogs extension}
\label{subsec:flat-divisor-complements}

The curvature in \cref{prop:flat-divisor-rank-one-curvature} vanishes on a tube around the divisor. On that tube the defining logarithm is pluriharmonic, and its regular levels are Levi flat. Their complex leaves depend on the unitary holonomy of the normal line: finite holonomy gives compact fibers of a local holomorphic function, whereas infinite holonomy forces every holomorphic function on the punctured tube to be constant.

Let $S$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, and let $D\subset S$ be a smooth compact connected divisor. Suppose $L=\mathcal O_S(D)$ has a unitary flat metric $h$ on a neighborhood of $D$. Write $s_D$ for its defining section and $N=L|_D$ for the normal line with the induced metric and flat connection, and let $\chi:\pi_1(D)\to\mathrm U(1)$ be its holonomy character. In parallel unit frames, $s_D$ is represented by holomorphic functions $W_j$ satisfying $W_j=t_{jk}W_k$ for locally constant $t_{jk}\in\mathrm U(1)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:flat-divisor-foliated-tube}
There are $R>0$, a neighborhood $U$ of $D$ and a smooth diffeomorphism
\[
 \Upsilon:\{v\in N:|v|<R\}\longrightarrow U
\]
that is the identity on the zero section and preserves the defining scalar: in each parallel normal frame, $W_j(\Upsilon(p,w))=w$. In particular, $|s_D|_h\circ\Upsilon=|w|$. The flat horizontal leaves on each circle bundle $\{|w|=r\}$ correspond to the complex leaves of the compact connected Levi-flat hypersurface
\[
 M_r=\{x\in U:|s_D(x)|_h=r\},\qquad0<r<R.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a smooth normal exponential map $\Upsilon_0$ with complex-linear first normal derivative. The derivative of $s_D$ identifies the normal line with $L|_D$; use this identification to choose that first derivative. Parallel transport along the normal rays identifies $\Upsilon_0^*L$ with the pullback of $N$. In compatible parallel frames one then has
\[
 W_j(\Upsilon_0(p,a))=a+O(|a|^2).
\]
For each $p$, this is a smooth map between real two-dimensional disks whose derivative at zero is the identity. The parameter-dependent inverse function theorem, uniformly over the compact base $D$, gives a unique small $a=a(p,w)$ with $W_j(\Upsilon_0(p,a))=w$. It depends smoothly on $(p,w)$ and has first derivative the identity. On an overlap both $a$ and $w$ change by the same constant unitary factor, so uniqueness makes these corrections agree. After decreasing $R$, the map $(p,w)\mapsto(p,a(p,w))$ is a diffeomorphism of a tube onto its image. Its composition with $\Upsilon_0$ gives $\Upsilon$.

Off $D$, the function $F=\log|s_D|_h^2$ is pluriharmonic. Its radial derivative in the tube is nonzero, so every $M_r$ is a smooth compact connected hypersurface. Locally its complex tangent space is $\ker\mathrm dW_j$, and its leaves are the complex hypersurfaces $W_j=\text{constant}$. The identity $W_j\circ\Upsilon=w$ pulls this distribution back to $\mathrm dw=0$ in flat normal frames. Thus it is precisely the flat horizontal foliation of the circle bundle. This construction is a smooth tubular identification; its base projection is not asserted to be holomorphic.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:flat-divisor-finite-holonomy}
In this setting, $N$ is torsion as a holomorphic line bundle exactly when its unitary holonomy has finite image. If its exact order is $m$, a sufficiently small tube admits a proper surjective holomorphic map
\[
 p:U\longrightarrow\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<R^m\}
\]
with connected fibers and $\operatorname{div}(p)=mD$. Its nonzero fibers are smooth compact complex hypersurfaces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\chi^m=1$, a parallel nowhere-zero section trivializes $N^m$ holomorphically. Conversely, if a holomorphic section $e$ trivializes $N^m$, then $\log|e|^2$ is pluriharmonic on the compact connected manifold $D$. The maximum principle makes it constant. In parallel holomorphic frames the coefficient of $e$ is a holomorphic function of constant modulus, hence constant. Therefore $e$ is parallel and $\chi^m=1$. The least such $m$ is both the holomorphic torsion order and the order of the finite holonomy image.

The tube retracts onto $D$ by \cref{lem:flat-divisor-foliated-tube}. Consequently the flat bundle $L^m$ on $U$ has trivial holonomy and a global parallel unit section $e_U$. Define $p=s_D^m/e_U$. Then $p$ is holomorphic, $\operatorname{div}(p)=mD$ and $|p|=|s_D|_h^m$. The inverse image of a compact subset of the target lies in a closed smaller normal disk bundle and is closed there, proving properness. All target values occur by the tubular model.

Choose local parallel unit frames $e_j$ of $L$ with $e_j^m=e_U$, so $p=W_j^m$. For a nonzero value, the model fiber consists over each base point of the $m$ roots of that value. Parallel transport acts on these roots through $\chi$, whose image has exact order $m$ and acts transitively. Thus this fiber is a connected smooth covering of $D$ as a real manifold. Since $\mathrm dp=mW_j^{m-1}\mathrm dW_j$, it is a smooth complex hypersurface. Compactness follows from properness. The zero fiber has connected support $D$, completing the connected-fiber assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:flat-divisor-holomorphic-rigidity}
\index{Hartogs extension!non-torsion flat divisor}
\index{Levi-flat hypersurface!flat divisor tube}
Suppose $N$ is non-torsion. Every complex leaf of $M_r$ is dense in $M_r$, and
\[
 H^0(U\setminus D,\mathcal O_S)=\mathbb C.
\]
If $S$ is connected, put $M=S\setminus D$. For every compact $K\subset M$ such that $M\setminus K$ is connected, every holomorphic function on $M\setminus K$ is constant and extends holomorphically to $M$. Moreover, for every connected open neighborhood $V$ of $D$, every holomorphic function on $V\setminus D$ is constant and extends to $M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding proposition makes $\chi(\pi_1(D))$ an infinite subgroup of $\mathrm U(1)$. Such a subgroup is dense: a proper closed subgroup of the circle is finite, as follows by lifting it to a closed subgroup of $\mathbb R$ containing $2\pi\mathbb Z$. The circle bundle with its flat horizontal foliation is the suspension of $\chi$. A leaf projects onto $D$, and its intersection with each fiber contains the holonomy orbit of any one of its points. This orbit is dense in the fiber. In a local trivialization, horizontal transport identifies neighboring orbits by a fixed rotation, so the leaf is dense in the whole circle bundle. The tubular lemma gives the corresponding density on $M_r$.

Let $f$ be holomorphic on $U\setminus D$. Its modulus attains a maximum on the compact $M_r$. On the complex leaf through a maximum point, $f$ is bounded by that same maximum and attains it. The maximum modulus principle makes $f$ constant on this connected leaf, and continuity makes it equal to the same constant $c$ on $M_r$. Near any point of $M_r$, choose holomorphic coordinates $(z,W_j)$, with $z\in\mathbb C^{n-1}$. For each fixed $z$, the holomorphic function $W_j\mapsto f(z,W_j)-c$ vanishes on an arc of $|W_j|=r$ lying in its domain. The one-variable identity theorem makes it vanish in that coordinate neighborhood. Analytic continuation on the connected punctured tube proves $f\equiv c$.

A compact $K\subset M$ is disjoint from a sufficiently small tube around the compact $D$. The restriction of a holomorphic function on $M\setminus K$ to this punctured tube is therefore constant. The identity theorem on the connected $M\setminus K$ makes the function constant everywhere, so its constant extension gives the claim. Similarly, every open neighborhood $V$ of $D$ contains a smaller full tube. The complement $V\setminus D$ is connected: paths in the connected manifold $V$ can be perturbed off the smooth real-codimension-two submanifold $D$. The same identity theorem gives the neighborhood-extension assertion. This path argument also shows that $M$ is connected.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:flat-divisor-nonstein}
\index{Stein manifold!flat divisor obstruction}
For any holonomy of $N$, the punctured tube admits no smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function, and $S\setminus D$ is not Stein. In the non-torsion nine-point blowup of \cref{prop:nine-point-anticanonical-curvature}, every holomorphic function on the divisor complement is constant and the compact-hole Hartogs conclusion of \cref{thm:flat-divisor-holomorphic-rigidity} holds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The restriction of a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function to a complex leaf of $M_r$ remains strictly plurisubharmonic. At a maximum on the compact $M_r$ it has a local maximum on that leaf, contradicting the strict submean inequality; the leaf has positive dimension because $n\geq2$.

A Stein manifold is holomorphically separable. In the finite-holonomy case, each nonzero fiber of \cref{prop:flat-divisor-finite-holonomy} is a connected compact positive-dimensional complex hypersurface. Every global holomorphic function is constant on it by the maximum modulus principle, so two of its distinct points cannot be separated. In the infinite-holonomy case, \cref{thm:flat-divisor-holomorphic-rigidity} makes every holomorphic function constant on the punctured tube, again contradicting separation. Finally, \cref{thm:flat-anticanonical-neighborhood} supplies the flat neighborhood for the nine-point example, whose normal line is non-torsion by hypothesis. Apply the rigidity theorem, also with $K=\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Punctured line bundles and elliptic quotients}
\label{subsec:elliptic-affine-quotients}

The obstruction in \cref{cor:flat-divisor-nonstein} uses flat circle levels. For a line bundle of nonzero degree over a compact curve, the coefficients of fiberwise Laurent expansions provide another obstruction. It persists inside a closed holomorphic fiber of an affine quotient, even when the universal cover is Stein.

For a compact subset $K$ of a complex manifold $Y$, its holomorphic hull is
\[
 \widehat K_Y=\{y\in Y:|f(y)|\leq\max_K|f|\text{ for every }f\in\mathcal O(Y)\}.
\]
Holomorphic convexity requires every such hull to be compact. A Stein manifold is holomorphically convex and holomorphically separable.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive-line-puncture-hull}
\index{Holomorphic hull!punctured line bundle}
\index{Line bundle!punctured positive degree}
Let $C$ be a compact connected Riemann surface, and let $A\to C$ be a holomorphic line bundle of positive degree. Write $A^\times$ for its total space with the zero section removed. Then $A^\times$ is not holomorphically convex. More precisely, put $B=A^*$ and choose a smooth Hermitian norm on $B$. Every holomorphic function on $B^\times$ extends holomorphically to $B$, and the holomorphic hull in $B^\times$ of
\[
 K_r=\{v\in B:|v|=r\},\qquad r>0,
\]
contains $\{v:0<|v|\leq r\}$ and is not compact.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a local holomorphic frame of $B$, a function $f\in\mathcal O(B^\times)$ has a fiberwise Laurent expansion
\[
 f(z,t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}a_k(z)t^k.
\]
The Cauchy integral formula makes each coefficient holomorphic in $z$, and the change of frame identifies the coefficients with global sections of $B^{-k}$. For $k<0$, this line bundle has negative degree. It has no nonzero section, since the zero divisor of a nonzero holomorphic section has nonnegative degree. Thus all negative coefficients vanish. On a smaller base chart, Cauchy estimates on a fixed fiber circle give locally uniform convergence of the remaining power series at $t=0$. These extensions agree on overlaps and define a holomorphic function on all of $B$.

The maximum modulus principle on the disk in each fiber gives
\[
 |f(v)|\leq\max_{K_r}|f|\qquad(0<|v|\leq r).
\]
The circle bundle $K_r$ is compact because $C$ is compact. A sequence in one fiber whose positive norms tend to zero lies in its hull and has no convergent subsequence in $B^\times$. Hence the hull is not compact. Fiberwise inversion identifies $A^\times$ biholomorphically with $B^\times$: a nonzero $v\in A$ corresponds to the unique $\ell\in A^*$ with $\ell(v)=1$. This proves the assertion for $A^\times$.
\end{proof}

The following quotient isolates the holomorphic obstruction in an affine-bundle model. Its universal cover is Stein, although the quotient is not.

Let $\Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau\subset\mathbb C$, with $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$, and put $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$. Let $b_\lambda(z)$ be nowhere-zero holomorphic functions satisfying
\[
 b_{\lambda+\gamma}(z)=b_\lambda(z+\gamma)b_\gamma(z).
\]
Suppose the line bundle
\[
 A=(\mathbb C_z\times\mathbb C_t)/\Lambda,
 \qquad\lambda\cdot(z,t)=(z+\lambda,b_\lambda(z)t),
\]
has positive degree.

\begin{example}\label{ex:positive-elliptic-automorphy}
\index{Factor of automorphy!degree-one elliptic bundle}
The factors
\[
 b_{m\tau+n}(z)=\exp\bigl(-\pi\mathrm{i}(m^2\tau+2mz)\bigr)
\]
define a degree-one line bundle on $E$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
For $\gamma=r\tau+s$, the cocycle identity follows from
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b_{m\tau+n}(z+\gamma)b_{r\tau+s}(z)
       &=b_{(m+r)\tau+n+s}(z)\exp(-2\pi\mathrm{i}ms)\\
       &=b_{(m+r)\tau+n+s}(z).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Put $v=\operatorname{Im}\tau$. The squared norm
\[
 |t|^2\exp\bigl(-2\pi(\operatorname{Im}z)^2/v\bigr)
\]
is invariant under the action, because $\operatorname{Im}(z+m\tau+n)=\operatorname{Im}z+mv$ and
\[
 |b_{m\tau+n}(z)|^2=\exp\bigl(2\pi(m^2v+2m\operatorname{Im}z)\bigr).
\]
Its first Chern form is
\[
 \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial
              \left(\frac{2\pi(\operatorname{Im}z)^2}{v}\right)
       =\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2v}\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z.
\]
This positive form integrates to one on a fundamental parallelogram of area $v$. Thus the degree is one.
\end{proof}

On
\[
 Q=\{([x_0:x_1],[w_0:w_1])\in\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1:
                    D=x_0w_1-x_1w_0\ne0\}
\]
define the holomorphic function $\sigma=x_1w_0/D$.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-affine-quotient-nonstein}
\index{Stein manifold!elliptic affine quotient}
Fix $\kappa\in\mathbb C^*$ and $K\in\mathbb C$. For $\lambda=m\tau+n\in\Lambda$, the formula
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lambda\cdot(z,u,[x],[w])={}&
 \bigl(z+\lambda,u+m(K+\kappa\sigma),\\
       &[x_0:b_\lambda(z)x_1],[w_0:b_\lambda(z)w_1]\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
defines a free properly discontinuous holomorphic action on $\mathbb C^2\times Q$. The quotient $Z$ is not Stein. If $K\notin\{0,\kappa\}$, the descended function $\sigma:Z\to\mathbb C$ has the closed smooth fiber
\[
 \sigma^{-1}(-K/\kappa)\simeq\mathbb C\times A^\times,
\]
which is not holomorphically convex. If $K=0$ or $K=\kappa$, the quotient contains a compact elliptic curve. In every case its universal cover is the Stein manifold $\mathbb C^2\times Q$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Simultaneously multiplying $x_1,w_1$ by $b_\lambda(z)$ multiplies $D$ and $x_1w_0$ by the same factor. Thus $\sigma$ is invariant. Its invariance and the cocycle identity for $b$ prove the group law, including addition of the integer $m$. Translation in the $z$ coordinate makes the action free and properly discontinuous: only finitely many lattice translates of the projection of a compact set can meet another fixed compact set. The quotient is therefore a complex manifold, and $\sigma$ descends holomorphically.

Set $a=-K/\kappa$. If $a\notin\{0,-1\}$, every point of $\{\sigma=a\}\subset Q$ has $x_0x_1\ne0$. In the chart
\[
 (t,y)\longmapsto([1:t],[y:ty+1]),
\]
the equation is $ty=a$. Hence this fiber is $\mathbb C^*$, with parameter $t$, and the group action on its preimage is
\[
 \lambda\cdot(z,u,t)=(z+\lambda,u,b_\lambda(z)t).
\]
This gives the asserted biholomorphism with $\mathbb C\times A^\times$. Its submanifold $u=0$ is closed in the fiber and therefore closed in $Z$. Under fiberwise inversion, let $K_r$ be the compact circle bundle in this submanifold from \cref{lem:positive-line-puncture-hull}. Every holomorphic function on $Z$ restricts to a function on $B^\times$. Consequently its hull in $Z$ contains the same sequence with fiber norms tending to zero. That sequence has no convergent subsequence in the closed submanifold, and hence none in $Z$. Thus $Z$ is not holomorphically convex.

For $K=0$, take $q_+=([1:0],[0:1])$, where $\sigma=0$. The invariant submanifold $\{u=0,q=q_+\}$ descends to a copy of $E$. For $K=\kappa$, use $q_-=([0:1],[1:0])$, where $\sigma=-1$. The same conclusion holds. Every holomorphic function on $Z$ is constant on this compact curve by the maximum modulus principle, so holomorphic separation fails.

Finally, the map
\[
 Q\longrightarrow\{(s,c,d)\in\mathbb C^3:cd=s(s-1)\},
 \qquad ([x],[w])\longmapsto
 \left(\frac{x_0w_1}{D},\frac{x_0w_0}{D},\frac{x_1w_1}{D}\right)
\]
is an isomorphism. Its inverse recovers $[x],[w]$ from the column and row lines of the rank-one matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}c&s\\s-1&d\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix never vanishes, and the hypersurface is smooth: simultaneous vanishing of its three defining derivatives would give $c=d=0$, $s=1/2$, contradicting its equation. Thus $Q$ is affine and Stein. Projection to $[x]\in\mathbb P^1$ makes it a locally trivial affine-line bundle. The chart above and the chart over $x_1\ne0$ have transition
\[
 v=1/t,\qquad y_\infty=-t^2y-t.
\]
Its contractible fibers give $\pi_1(Q)=\pi_1(\mathbb P^1)=0$. Therefore $\mathbb C^2\times Q$ is simply connected and Stein, and is the universal cover of $Z$.
\end{proof}
