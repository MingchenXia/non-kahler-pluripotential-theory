\section{The Hopf flag and its lower-dimensional body}

Return to $X_n$ and $L_k$ from \cref{prop:hopf-bergman-exact}, with $n\geq2$ and $k\geq0$ an integer. There is a complete compact flag
\[
 Y_i=\{z_1=\cdots=z_i=0\}/\langle \mathrm{e}^{-1}\rangle
 \quad(1\leq i\leq n-1),\qquad Y_n=\{p\}\subset Y_{n-1}.
\]
Here $Y_{n-1}=\C^*/\langle \mathrm{e}^{-1}\rangle$ is an elliptic curve, and $p$ is any of its points. Every inclusion has the smoothness and divisor properties required above. Take the complete section algebra $W_m=H^0(X_n,L_k^m)$, which is also $\mathcal W_m(0)$.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hopf-flag-simplex}
For this flag,
\[
 \{\nu(s):0\neq s\in W_m\}
 =\{(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1},0):a_i\in\mathbb N,
                                  \ \textstyle\sum_i a_i\leq km\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \Delta(W_\bullet)=
 \{(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1},0):x_i\geq0,
                                  \ \textstyle\sum_i x_i\leq k\}.
\]
Its $n$-dimensional volume is zero. When $k>0$, its volume in the coordinate hyperplane $x_n=0$, with the Euclidean measure for which the coordinate lattice has covolume one, is
\[
 \frac{k^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}
 =\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim W_m}{m^{n-1}}.
\]
For $k=0$ the body is a point and $\dim W_m=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The sections are precisely homogeneous polynomials of degree $km$. Successively taking the least power of $z_1$, dividing and restricting, and then doing the same with $z_2,\ldots,z_{n-1}$, leaves a nonzero multiple of
$z_n^{km-a_1-\cdots-a_{n-1}}$. It is nowhere zero on $Y_{n-1}$. Hence the last valuation coordinate, the vanishing order at $p$, is zero. The preceding coordinates are nonnegative and their sum is at most $km$.

Conversely every such tuple is realized by the monomial
\[
 z_1^{a_1}\cdots z_{n-1}^{a_{n-1}}
 z_n^{km-a_1-\cdots-a_{n-1}}.
\]
This proves the equality of valuation sets. Their normalized vectors lie in the displayed simplex. Its vertices $0,ke_1,\ldots,ke_{n-1}$ already occur in degree one, so their closed convex hull is the whole simplex. Its intrinsic volume is the elementary simplex volume. Finally the exact binomial count of \cref{prop:hopf-bergman-exact} has leading coefficient $k^{n-1}/(n-1)!$, giving the limit. The constant-polynomial case gives the statements for $k=0$.
\end{proof}
