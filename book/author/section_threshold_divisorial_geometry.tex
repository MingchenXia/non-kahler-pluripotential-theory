The difference between $\mathscr C_\omega$ and $\mathscr G_\omega$ comes from positive currents that pair negatively with a class which is nonnegative on all smooth tests. Integration currents along negative curves give such examples.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:gauduchon-current-smoothing-obstruction}
The weak closure $\mathscr C_\omega$ of the smooth strictly positive normalized Gauduchon forms can be smaller than $\mathscr G_\omega$, even on a K\"ahler surface. Let $X$ be the blowup of $\mathbb P^2$ at one point and $C$ its exceptional curve. Fix a K\"ahler form $\omega$ and a smooth closed representative $\beta_C$ of the class of $[C]$. Then
\[
 T_C=\frac{[C]}{\int_C\omega}\in\mathscr G_\omega,
 \qquad
 \int_X\beta_C\wedge T_C=\frac{-1}{\int_C\omega}<0,
\]
whereas every smooth strictly positive $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$ satisfies $\int_X\beta_C\wedge T=\int_C T>0$. In particular $T_C\notin\mathscr C_\omega$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The integration current $[C]$ is positive and closed, so its normalization belongs to $\mathscr G_\omega$. Its pairing with the smooth form $\beta_C$ is the self-intersection number $C^2=-1$ of \cref{ex:gaud-metric-blowup}. For smooth $T$, the Poincar\'e--Lelong formula and $\ddc T=0$ give the second identity. Since the pairing with the fixed smooth form $\beta_C$ is continuous under weak convergence of currents, it is nonnegative on the weak closure of the smooth tests, and this closure does not contain $T_C$.
\end{proof}

Let $m_\omega(\beta)$ be the minimum of $\int_X\beta\wedge T$ over all $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$, as in \cref{sec:smooth-cone-current-duality}, whereas by \eqref{eq:gauduchon-linear-minimum} the threshold $\kappa_\omega(\beta)$ is the minimum over $\mathscr C_\omega$. Since $T_C\in\mathscr G_\omega$ and $[C]$ is a positive representative of the class of $\beta_C$, the two thresholds satisfy
\[
 m_\omega(\beta_C)\leq-\frac1{\int_C\omega}<0
 \leq\kappa_\omega(\beta_C).
\]
Thus extending the minimum in \eqref{eq:gauduchon-linear-minimum} to all normalized Gauduchon currents changes its value in this example.

We return to the general setting $(X,\omega,f)$ of this section. The next two corollaries assume uniform integrability along one smooth maximizing sequence. The condition $p\in\mathcal D_\omega^0$ suffices, since it gives $b>0$ and uniform integrability over the whole of $\mathscr G_\omega$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:semipositive-divisor-exclusion}
Assume $b>0$ and that $D_p$ has a smooth maximizing sequence $(T_j)$ with uniformly integrable densities $p k_{T_j}$. Let $E$ be an effective real divisor whose class has a smooth semipositive representative $\beta_E$. If
\[
 \int_{\{\beta_E=0\}}f\,\mathrm{d}V=0,
\]
then no threshold subsolution $u$ for $f$ satisfies $\omega+\ddc u\geq[E]$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Suppose such a subsolution exists. By \cref{prop:threshold-divisorial-constraint}, there is a maximizing current $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$ with $\int_X\beta_E\wedge T=0$. We use the matrix-density notation of \cref{sec:weak-gauduchon-duality}: $\sigma_T=\omega\wedge T$ is the trace measure of $T$, $P_T$ is its matrix density and $B$ is the coefficient matrix of $\beta_E$. Since both matrices are positive semidefinite, the vanishing pairing gives $\operatorname{tr}(BP_T)=0$ at $\sigma_T$-almost every point. Wherever $B\ne0$, this forces $P_T$ to be singular: a positive definite $P_T$ would have strictly positive trace against the nonzero positive semidefinite matrix $B$. The determinant measure $\Gamma_0(T)$ vanishes where $P_T$ is singular, so it is concentrated on $\{\beta_E=0\}$. By \cref{thm:gaud-determinant-subcritical}, $\Gamma_0(T)=k_T\,\mathrm{d}V$, and the hypothesis on $f$ gives $p k_T=0$ almost everywhere. This contradicts $D_p(T)=\rho_\omega(p)>0$.
\end{proof}
