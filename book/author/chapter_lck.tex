\chapter{Locally conformally K\"ahler geometry}
\label{chap:lck}

\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$. A locally conformally K\"ahler (LCK) metric is a Hermitian metric that becomes K\"ahler after local conformal changes, and its Lee form measures the obstruction to making this change globally. On a covering on which the Lee form is exact, the K\"ahler metric and its admissible potentials change by the same character under deck transformations. Descending this potential calculus to $X$ gives a twisted complex Hessian with a zeroth-order term, so that even adding a constant to a potential changes the metric. The standard Vaisman metric on the Hopf manifold illustrates the difference between the two potential theories: although it is locally conformally K\"ahler, its ordinary potentials have unbounded Monge--Amp\`ere masses.

Prescribing the volume while keeping the Lee class and the Gauduchon gauge couples the determinant equation with an equation for the conformal factor. For a volume invariant under the Lee and anti-Lee flows of a Vaisman metric, uniqueness for the ordinary smooth equation gives a potential that is constant along these flows. Stokes' theorem in the transverse directions fixes the normalizing constant, and changing the Lee form reconstructs the required Vaisman metric. On scalar Hopf manifolds, radial symmetry reduces the coupled equations to a periodic ordinary differential equation. Its positive solutions are ordered by their Lee periods, so the metric is determined by its Lee period and its volume; for a nonconstant radial volume, this metric is not Vaisman. A different compatibility condition gives rigidity: when an LCK metric is both Gauduchon and Chern--Ricci flat, the Bianchi and Bochner identities force its harmonic Lee form to be parallel.

The Lee form also produces a closed curvature class that can be nonzero in Bott--Chern cohomology while vanishing in de Rham cohomology. Pairing these classes with all Gauduchon metrics defines the Lee--Gauduchon cone, which detects their pseudoeffectivity and is invariant under bimeromorphic maps. Strongly Gauduchon metrics exclude all nonzero positive de Rham-exact currents and bound the linear mass of closed positive currents by their de Rham classes. The Calabi--Eckmann example separates this condition from the presence of zero in the Lee--Gauduchon cone, and a balanced elliptic fibration shows that it still allows zero lower Monge--Amp\`ere mass.

\section{The Lee form and the K\"ahler covering}\label{sec:lck-lee-cover}

A Hermitian form is locally conformally K\"ahler when its exterior derivative is controlled by a closed one-form.

\begin{definition}\label{def:lck}\index{LCK|see{Locally conformally K\"ahler metric}}\index{Locally conformally Kahler metric@Locally conformally K\"ahler metric}\index{Lee form}\index{Vaisman metric}
A Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$ is \emph{locally conformally K\"ahler}, or LCK, if there is a smooth real one-form $\lambda$ on $X$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{d}\omega=\lambda\wedge\omega,
 \qquad \mathrm{d}\lambda=0.
\]
The form $\lambda$ is called its \emph{Lee form}. The metric is \emph{strictly LCK} if $\lambda$ is not exact. A strictly LCK metric is \emph{Vaisman} if its Lee form is parallel for the Levi--Civita connection of the associated Riemannian metric.
\end{definition}

Fix an LCK metric $\omega$ with Lee form $\lambda$. On a compact manifold a nonzero parallel one-form cannot be exact: the differential of a function vanishes at a maximum, whereas a parallel form has constant length. Thus a nonzero parallel Lee form is automatically nowhere zero and not exact.

The Lee form is determined by $\omega$, because the map $a\mapsto a\wedge\omega$ is injective on one-forms: after wedging with $\omega^{n-2}$, this follows from the bijectivity of the Lefschetz map $a\mapsto a\wedge\omega^{n-1}$ on one-forms.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Locally write $\lambda=\mathrm{d}f$. Then
\[
 \Omega=\mathrm{e}^{-f}\omega>0,\qquad \mathrm{d}\Omega=0,
\]
so $\Omega$ is a K\"ahler form. Choose a connected regular covering $\pi:\widetilde X\to X$ on which $\pi^*\lambda=\mathrm{d}F$, for example the universal covering. The quotient of $\widetilde X$ by its deck group is $X$. The same construction gives a K\"ahler form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-cover}
 \widetilde\omega=\mathrm{e}^{-F}\pi^*\omega.
\end{equation}
For a deck transformation $\sigma$, the difference $\sigma^*F-F=c_\sigma$ is constant, and
\[
 \sigma^*\widetilde\omega=\chi(\sigma)\widetilde\omega,
 \qquad \chi(\sigma)=\mathrm{e}^{-c_\sigma}.
\]
Thus $\chi$ is a homomorphism from the deck group to the multiplicative group of positive real numbers, which records how the K\"ahler metric is rescaled. Forms on $\widetilde X$ transforming by $\chi$ may equivalently be viewed as forms on $X$ with values in the flat real line bundle defined by $\chi$, the \emph{weight bundle}. In this sense an LCK form is closed as a form with values in the weight bundle \cite[Section 3.4]{OV24LCK}.

Let $g$ be the Riemannian metric of $\omega$, let $J$ be the complex structure, and let $\lambda^\sharp$ be the vector field dual to $\lambda$ with respect to $g$. On a chart where $\lambda=\mathrm df$, the metric $\widetilde g=\mathrm{e}^{-f}g$ is K\"ahler. For real vector fields $U,V$, the Levi--Civita connections $\nabla$ of $g$ and $\widetilde\nabla$ of $\widetilde g$ are related by
\[
 \widetilde\nabla_UV=\nabla_UV-\tfrac12\bigl(\lambda(U)V+\lambda(V)U-g(U,V)\lambda^\sharp\bigr).
\]
Substituting $JV$ for $V$ and using $\widetilde\nabla J=0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-complex-connection}
 (\nabla_UJ)V=\tfrac12\bigl(\lambda(JV)U-\lambda(V)JU+g(U,V)J\lambda^\sharp+g(JU,V)\lambda^\sharp\bigr).
\end{equation}
Both sides are global tensors, so this identity holds on all of $X$.

For $a\in C^\infty(X,\R)$, the conformal change $\omega'=\mathrm{e}^a\omega$ replaces the Lee form by $\lambda+\mathrm{d}a$. In particular, the de Rham class $[\lambda]$ depends only on the conformal class of $\omega$. If $\lambda$ is exact, the construction above gives a K\"ahler form on $X$ itself.

\section{Gauduchon representatives and the Lee class}
\label{sec:lck-lee-class}

In the conformal class of an LCK metric, the Gauduchon representative is the one whose Lee form is harmonic. A closed $(1,1)$-form built from the Lee form pairs positively with this Gauduchon metric when the structure is strictly LCK, even though the form is de Rham exact. It therefore defines a nonzero Bott--Chern class, which obstructs K\"ahler modifications.

Set
\[
 \lambda^{\mathrm c}
 =\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}(\lambda^{0,1}-\lambda^{1,0}),
\]
so that $\lambda^{\mathrm c}=\dc f$ wherever $\lambda=\mathrm{d}f$. The closed real $(1,1)$-form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-lee-curvature}
 \kappa_\lambda:=\dc\lambda=-\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\mathrm c}
\end{equation}
connects the Lee class with Bott--Chern cohomology. It is de Rham exact, but it need not be $\ddc$-exact.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lck-gauduchon}
An LCK metric is Gauduchon if and only if its Lee form is co-closed for that metric. Consequently, the Gauduchon representative in its conformal class has harmonic Lee form. Moreover, for every LCK metric,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-lee-degree}
 \int_X\kappa_\lambda\wedge\omega^{n-1}
 =(n-1)\int_X\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
If $\lambda\not\equiv0$, the right side is strictly positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On a chart where $\omega$ is conformally K\"ahler,
\[
 \dc(\omega^{n-1})
 =(n-1)\lambda^{\mathrm c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}.
\]
Let $*$ be the real Hodge star of the Riemannian metric associated with $\omega$, and let $\mathrm d^*$ be the formal adjoint of the exterior derivative. By the pointwise Hodge-star identities for Hermitian metrics, $\lambda^{\mathrm c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}$ is a fixed nonzero constant multiple of $*\lambda$. Consequently, the Gauduchon condition $\ddc(\omega^{n-1})=0$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{d}*\lambda=0$, that is, to $\mathrm{d}^*\lambda=0$. Since $\mathrm{d}\lambda=0$, co-closedness is equivalent to harmonicity. The Gauduchon representative exists by \cref{thm:gauduchon}, and conformal changes preserve the LCK condition.

Stokes' theorem and $\mathrm{d}(\omega^{n-1})=(n-1)\lambda\wedge\omega^{n-1}$ give
\[
 \begin{split}
 -\int_X\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\mathrm c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}
 &=-\int_X\lambda^{\mathrm c}\wedge\mathrm{d}(\omega^{n-1})\\
 &=(n-1)\int_X\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}.
 \end{split}
\]
For a real one-form,
$\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}
=\mathrm i\lambda^{1,0}\wedge\lambda^{0,1}/(2\pi)$ is semipositive, and its trace with respect to $\omega$ is positive wherever $\lambda\ne0$. This proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The same construction applies to every closed real one-form and defines a linear map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-lee-bc-map}
 j_X:H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)\longrightarrow H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\R),
 \qquad [\lambda]\longmapsto\{\kappa_\lambda\},
\end{equation}
which we call the \emph{Lee map}. It is well defined, since replacing $\lambda$ by $\lambda+\mathrm{d}a$ replaces $\kappa_\lambda$ by $\kappa_\lambda-\ddc a$. Consequently, the pairing of $\{\kappa_\lambda\}$ with any Gauduchon form $\gamma^{n-1}$ is well defined. For a strictly LCK structure, the Gauduchon representative in its conformal class gives a strictly positive pairing by \eqref{eq:lck-lee-degree}. In particular $\{\kappa_\lambda\}\ne0$, although its image in de Rham cohomology is zero.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lck-no-closed-big-class}
If $X$ carries a strictly LCK metric, then no class in $H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\R)$ is big. In particular, $X$ does not belong to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If there were a big class, the modification criterion recalled in \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci} would give a modification $\pi:Y\to X$ with $Y$ compact K\"ahler. The form $\pi^*\kappa_\lambda$ is closed and de Rham exact, so the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma on $Y$ gives a smooth real function $h$ on $Y$ with $\pi^*\kappa_\lambda=\ddc h$. Since $\pi$ has degree one, pushing forward gives the current identity $\kappa_\lambda=\ddc(\pi_*h)$. By Stokes' theorem, the pairing of this current with a Gauduchon form is zero, which contradicts \eqref{eq:lck-lee-degree} for the Gauduchon representative of the LCK structure.
\end{proof}

For the Hopf manifold considered below, $\kappa_\lambda=\alpha$ is a smooth semipositive form, so its class is nef. Its non-nef locus is empty, whereas its non-K\"ahler locus is all of $X$, by the convention for classes that are not big in \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci}. These statements concern the closed class $\{\alpha\}$; the Monge--Amp\`ere equations on the Hopf manifold use instead the strictly positive, nonclosed form $\omega$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lck-balanced-obstruction}
Suppose that $X$ carries a strictly LCK metric with Lee form $\lambda$ and that $\{\kappa_\lambda\}$ is pseudoeffective. Then $X$ admits no balanced metric.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $T=\kappa_\lambda+\ddc u\geq0$ be a closed positive current representing the class. It is nonzero, since the class is nonzero, and it is de Rham exact. If a Hermitian metric $b$ were balanced, then $\mathrm{d}(b^{n-1})=0$, and Stokes' theorem for currents would give
\[
 \int_XT\wedge b^{n-1}=0.
\]
This is impossible, since the mass of a nonzero positive $(1,1)$-current against a smooth strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form is strictly positive.
\end{proof}

In view of \cref{prop:lck-balanced-obstruction}, we propose the following conjecture.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:lck-lee-psef}
If $X$ carries a strictly LCK metric with Lee form $\lambda$, then the Bott--Chern class $\{\kappa_\lambda\}$ is pseudoeffective.
\end{conjecture}

The positivity in \eqref{eq:lck-lee-degree} tests the class only against the Gauduchon representative of the LCK metric. By \cref{thm:lamari}, pseudoeffectivity requires a nonnegative pairing with \emph{every} Gauduchon metric.

\section{Ordinary and twisted potentials}

The K\"ahler covering provides a potential calculus compatible with the Lee equation. Conjugating the ordinary differential operators by the local conformal factor produces a twisted complex Hessian on $X$.

For the fixed closed one-form $\lambda$, define
\[
 \mathrm{d}_\lambda=\mathrm{d}-\lambda\wedge,
 \qquad \dc_\lambda=\dc-\lambda^{\mathrm c}\wedge,
 \qquad D_\lambda=\mathrm{d}_\lambda\dc_\lambda.
\]
On a chart where $\lambda=\mathrm{d}f$, these operators satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-gauge-operators}
 \mathrm{d}_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^f\mathrm{d}\mathrm{e}^{-f},
 \qquad \dc_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^f\dc \mathrm{e}^{-f},
 \qquad D_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^f\ddc\mathrm{e}^{-f}.
\end{equation}
These identities show that $D_\lambda u$ is a real $(1,1)$-form for every smooth real function $u$, and that $\mathrm{d}_\lambda D_\lambda u=0$.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lck-two-potentials}\index{Twisted potential}
Let $u$ be a smooth real function. Then
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\omega+\ddc u)-\lambda\wedge(\omega+\ddc u)
 =-\lambda\wedge\ddc u,
\]
whereas
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\omega+D_\lambda u)
 =\lambda\wedge(\omega+D_\lambda u).
\]
Consequently, an ordinary potential preserves the LCK equation with the same Lee form precisely when $\lambda\wedge\ddc u=0$. A twisted potential always preserves that equation, and it gives an LCK metric whenever $\omega+D_\lambda u>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first identity follows from $\mathrm{d}\ddc u=0$ and $\mathrm{d}\omega=\lambda\wedge\omega$. The second follows from $\mathrm{d}_\lambda\omega=0$ and \eqref{eq:lck-gauge-operators}.
\end{proof}

On the K\"ahler covering, a twisted potential becomes the automorphic function $\widetilde u=\mathrm{e}^{-F}\pi^*u$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-lift-potential}
 \mathrm{e}^{-F}\pi^*(\omega+D_\lambda u)
 =\widetilde\omega+\ddc\widetilde u.
\end{equation}
The top power $(\widetilde\omega+\ddc\widetilde u)^n$ transforms by $\chi^n$ under deck transformations, so its ratio to $\widetilde\omega^n$ is invariant and descends to $X$. A measure on $X$ is obtained from this ratio only after a metric in the conformal class has been fixed; the automorphic top-degree form cannot simply be integrated over the noncompact cover.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
The two kinds of potentials also differ in their normalization. Expanding the twisted Hessian gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-twisted-expansion}
 D_\lambda u
 =\ddc u-\mathrm{d}u\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}
       -\lambda\wedge\dc u
       +u\bigl(\kappa_\lambda+\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular,
$D_\lambda1=\kappa_\lambda+\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}$ need not vanish, so adding a constant to $u$ can change the metric $\omega+D_\lambda u$. The usual normalization $\sup_Xu=0$ is therefore not harmless for twisted equations.

For a chosen Lee form $\lambda$ in its de Rham class, the real twisted Bott--Chern group in bidegree $(1,1)$ is
\[
 H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC},\lambda}(X,\R)
 =\frac{\{\eta\in C^\infty(X,\Lambda^{1,1}_{\R}):
                       \mathrm{d}_\lambda\eta=0\}}
        {D_\lambda C^\infty(X,\R)}.
\]
This is Bott--Chern cohomology with values in the weight bundle \cite[Section 18.5]{OV24LCK}. It differs both from ordinary Bott--Chern cohomology and from the quotient of all backgrounds by ordinary $\ddc$-terms considered in \cref{chap:nonclosed-bclasses}.

\subsection{The Vaisman metric on the Hopf manifold}

Return to the Hopf threefold and the forms $s$, $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\omega=\alpha+\beta$ of \cref{chap:hopf}; recall that $s=\log|z|^2$ on the covering $\C^3\setminus\{0\}$. The radial description makes the Lee form explicit and shows that this Gauduchon metric is also Vaisman.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lck-hopf-vaisman}
The Gauduchon form $\omega=\alpha+\beta$ of \cref{chap:hopf} is Vaisman, with Lee form $\lambda=-\mathrm{d}s$, and
\[
 \kappa_\lambda=\alpha,
 \qquad D_\lambda1=\omega.
\]
In particular, the bounded mass property of \cref{def:bmp} fails for the ordinary potential theory of a background that is simultaneously Gauduchon and Vaisman.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Using $\alpha=\ddc s$ and $\beta=\mathrm{d}s\wedge\dc s$, direct differentiation gives
\[
 \mathrm{d}\omega=-\mathrm{d}s\wedge\alpha
                   =-\mathrm{d}s\wedge\omega,
 \qquad \mathrm{e}^s\omega=\ddc(\mathrm{e}^s)=\ddc|z|^2.
\]
Although $s$ is not a function on the quotient, $\mathrm{d}s$ descends to it. Its integral along the loop induced by $z\mapsto \mathrm{e}^{-1}z$ is $-2$, so the Lee form is not exact.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Let $g_{S^5}$ be the round metric on the unit sphere. Up to a constant factor, the Riemannian metric associated with $\omega$ on the cover is
\[
 \frac{|\mathrm{d}z|^2}{|z|^2}
 =\mathrm{d}t^2+g_{S^5},\qquad t=\log|z|.
\]
The one-form $\mathrm{d}t$ is parallel for this product metric, which proves the Vaisman condition. Finally,
$\kappa_\lambda=\ddc s=\alpha$ and
$\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\mathrm c}=\beta$, so $D_\lambda1=\omega$ by \eqref{eq:lck-twisted-expansion}.
The unbounded masses are given by \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}.
\end{proof}

The unbounded masses arise in the ordinary family $\omega+\ddc u$, whose members need not satisfy the Lee equation. The volume problem within the class of Vaisman metrics imposes this additional geometric constraint.

\section{Volume equations in Gauduchon gauge}
\label{sec:lck-volume-equations}\label{prop:lck-coupled-equation}

Let $X$ again be a compact LCK manifold of dimension $n\geq2$. Prescribing the volume of an LCK metric in Gauduchon gauge couples two equations: one for the determinant and one for the conformal factor. To compare metrics with a fixed Lee class, we first identify the twisted groups for cohomologous Lee forms. For a closed real one-form $\lambda$, smooth real functions $a,u$ and a smooth differential form $\eta$, multiplication by $\mathrm{e}^{-a}$ gives
\[
 \mathrm{d}_\lambda(\mathrm{e}^{-a}\eta)
 =\mathrm{e}^{-a}\mathrm{d}_{\lambda+\mathrm{d}a}\eta,
 \qquad
 D_\lambda(\mathrm{e}^{-a}u)=\mathrm{e}^{-a}D_{\lambda+\mathrm{d}a}u.
\]
We use this identification when comparing classes; the additive constant in $a$ corresponds to a constant rescaling of the weight bundle.

Let $\omega_1$ be an LCK Gauduchon metric with Lee form $\lambda$, and let $\omega_2$ be an LCK metric with Lee form $\lambda+\mathrm{d}a$. If their twisted classes agree under the chosen identification, then
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-a}\omega_2=\omega_1+D_\lambda u=:A>0
\]
for a smooth real function $u$. Substituting $\omega_2=\mathrm{e}^aA$ shows that $\omega_2$ has the same volume form as $\omega_1$ and is Gauduchon exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-coupled-equation}
 \begin{cases}
  A^n=\mathrm{e}^{-na}\omega_1^n,\\
  \ddc\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{(n-1)a}A^{n-1}\bigr)=0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The scalar Hermitian equation of \cref{chap:smooth-equations} does not impose the second equation, and it uses $\ddc u$ in place of $D_\lambda u$. The prescribed-volume Gauduchon theorem, \cref{thm:stw-volume}, does not assert that its solution is LCK or lies in the given twisted class.

\begin{problem}\label{q:lck-gauduchon-volume}
Does a compact LCK manifold have at most one Gauduchon LCK metric with a given Lee class, a given twisted Bott--Chern class and a given smooth positive volume form?
\end{problem}

This is the uniqueness problem posed in \cite[Question 47.56(3)]{OV24LCK}, with the identification of classes made explicit. The question concerns metrics: uniqueness of a scalar potential $u$ would additionally require control of the kernel of $D_\lambda$. The counterexamples with degenerate densities in \cref{chap:degenerate-uniqueness} concern ordinary potentials and do not decide this question.
