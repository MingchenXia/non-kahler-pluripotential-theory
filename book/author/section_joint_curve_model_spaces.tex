\section{A complete space over varying curve backgrounds}
\label{sec:joint-curve-model-spaces}

The polar description also compares models in different backgrounds. Using the background as an additional coordinate gives a complete space that includes the zero-area boundary. The singularity coordinate still records the diffuse polar curvature, and taking its atomic part still gives the nearest ideal model.

We keep the compact connected curve $Y$ and the smooth positive form $\eta$ of area one. Let
\[
 \mathcal B(Y)=\{\sigma\in C^\infty(Y,\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb R}):
                         A_\sigma=\textstyle\int_Y\sigma\geq0\}
\]
be the set of admissible backgrounds. Choose $C^k$ norms on smooth forms, defined by a fixed smooth metric on $Y$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-background-smooth-metric}
 b(\sigma,\tau)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty2^{-k-1}
              \min\{1,\|\sigma-\tau\|_{C^k}\}.
\end{equation}
This is a complete metric for the $C^\infty$ topology on $\mathcal B(Y)$. Indeed, a $b$-Cauchy sequence is Cauchy in every $C^k$ norm; the limits agree and define a smooth form, whose area is nonnegative by $C^0$ convergence. Conversely, convergence in every $C^k$ norm gives convergence for $b$: a finite part of the sum becomes small, and the tail is uniformly small.

Let $\mathcal M(\sigma)$ be the space of ordinary models of \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}, and define
\[
 \mathscr M(Y)=\{(\sigma,p):\sigma\in\mathcal B(Y),
                                  p\in\mathcal M(\sigma)\}.
\]
For such a pair write $\mu_p^\sigma=\mathbf1_{\{p=-\infty\}}(\sigma+\ddc p)$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-curve-model-metric}
 D((\sigma,p),(\tau,q))
       =b(\sigma,\tau)+\|\mu_p^\sigma-\mu_q^\tau\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathscr I(Y)\subset\mathscr M(Y)$ consist of the pairs $(\sigma,p)$ with $P_\sigma[p]_{\mathcal I}=p$, and let $\mathscr A_+(Y)$ consist of the pairs $(\sigma,p)$ for which $p$ has analytic singularities and positive non-pluripolar mass in background $\sigma$. For a finite measure $\mu$ on $Y$, write $\mu=\mu^{\mathrm{at}}+\mu^{\mathrm{na}}$ for its decomposition into an atomic and a nonatomic part.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:joint-curve-model-completeness}\index{Model potential!varying curve backgrounds}\index{Metric completeness!joint curve model space}
The metric space $(\mathscr M(Y),D)$ is complete. Its restriction to each fixed-background fiber is the polar metric $d_\sigma$. The map
\[
 R(\sigma,p)=(\sigma,P_\sigma[p]_{\mathcal I})
\]
is a $1$-Lipschitz retraction onto the closed subspace $\mathscr I(Y)$, and $R(\sigma,p)$ is the unique ideal pair nearest to $(\sigma,p)$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-curve-analytic-closure}
 \overline{\mathscr A_+(Y)}^{\,D}=\mathscr I(Y),\qquad
 \operatorname{dist}_D((\sigma,p),\mathscr I(Y))
       =(\mu_p^\sigma)^{\mathrm{na}}(Y).
\end{equation}
If $(\sigma_j,p_j)\to(\sigma,p)$ for $D$, then $p_j\to p$ in $L^1(Y,\eta)$ and in capacity for $\eta$, including when $A_\sigma=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}, polar curvature identifies $\mathscr M(Y)$ with the set of pairs $(\sigma,\mu)$ in which $\sigma\in\mathcal B(Y)$ and $\mu$ is a polar measure of mass at most $A_\sigma$. In these coordinates, \eqref{eq:joint-curve-model-metric} is the restriction of the product metric $b+\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$, so it separates pairs and restricts to $d_\sigma$ on every fiber.

Suppose that $(\sigma_j,\mu_j)$ is a Cauchy sequence. Completeness of $b$ gives $\sigma_j\to\sigma$ in the $C^\infty$ topology, and completeness of the space of finite signed measures gives $\mu_j\to\mu$ in total variation. The limit $\mu$ is positive, satisfies $\mu(Y)\leq A_\sigma$, and is a polar measure, as in the proof of \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}. Thus it defines a model in background $\sigma$, which proves completeness.

Ideal projection takes the atomic part, by \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}. Taking the atomic part does not increase total variation, so $R$ is $1$-Lipschitz. The positive atomic measures are closed in total variation, as in the proof of \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}, so $\mathscr I(Y)$ is closed. Let $\mu$ be the polar measure of $p$. For every ideal pair $(\tau,q)$, whose polar measure $\nu$ is atomic, mutual singularity gives
\[
 D((\sigma,p),(\tau,q))
 =b(\sigma,\tau)+\mu^{\mathrm{na}}(Y)
                         +\|\mu^{\mathrm{at}}-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Its unique minimum is attained at $\tau=\sigma$ and $\nu=\mu^{\mathrm{at}}$, that is, at $R(\sigma,p)$. This proves the retraction and distance assertions.

For the analytic closure, let $(\sigma,q)$ be an ideal pair. If $A_\sigma>0$, finite atomic measures with rational coefficients and total mass strictly less than $A_\sigma$ approximate every positive atomic measure of mass at most $A_\sigma$ in total variation. Their models in background $\sigma$ are analytic, by the classification on curves and the gauge transport of \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}, and they have positive non-pluripolar mass. If $A_\sigma=0$, the polar measure of $q$ is zero. The pairs with backgrounds $\sigma+j^{-1}\eta$ and zero polar measure converge to $(\sigma,q)$ for $D$. Their models are bounded, so they have analytic singularities with trivial ideal, and their non-pluripolar masses are $j^{-1}>0$. Hence every ideal pair lies in the closure of $\mathscr A_+(Y)$. Conversely, an analytic model is an ideal model by \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, and $\mathscr I(Y)$ is closed. This proves \eqref{eq:joint-curve-analytic-closure}.

Finally, let $(\sigma_j,p_j)\to(\sigma,p)$, and write $\mu_j=\mu_{p_j}^{\sigma_j}$ and $\mu=\mu_p^\sigma$. If $A_\sigma>0$, put $\lambda_j=A_{\sigma_j}/A_\sigma$. For all sufficiently large $j$ these numbers are positive and tend to one, and
\[
 \|\lambda_j^{-1}\mu_j-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\lambda_j^{-1}\|\mu_j-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
                  +|\lambda_j^{-1}-1|\,\mu(Y)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Now apply \cref{cor:curve-varying-background-continuity}. If $A_\sigma=0$, the backgrounds converge uniformly and all areas are nonnegative, and the uniform collapse of \cref{thm:curve-zero-area-collapse} gives $L^1$ and capacity convergence to the unique normalized potential $u_\sigma=p$ in background $\sigma$.
\end{proof}

The $C^\infty$ topology on backgrounds is needed for completeness: a sequence of smooth forms that is Cauchy for the uniform norm can have a limit that is not smooth. On a fixed background of positive area, $D$ induces on models of positive mass the topology of \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric}, but it allows sequences to converge to models of zero mass. In particular, the Cauchy sequences for $D$ need not satisfy the condition on reciprocal volumes in that theorem.

Smooth gauge changes and positive scaling act compatibly on all fibers of the joint space.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:joint-curve-model-gauge}\index{Model potential!joint gauge isometry}
For $f\in C^\infty(Y,\mathbb R)$, the map
\[
 \mathcal G_f(\sigma,p)
   =(\sigma+\ddc f,P_{\sigma+\ddc f}[p-f])
\]
is an isometry of $\mathscr M(Y)$, with inverse $\mathcal G_{-f}$ and $\mathcal G_g\mathcal G_f=\mathcal G_{f+g}$. It preserves analytic models and commutes with $R$.

For $\lambda>0$, the bijection $\mathcal H_\lambda(\sigma,p)=(\lambda\sigma,\lambda p)$ commutes with $R$ and satisfies
\[
 \min\{1,\lambda\}D(x,y)
 \leq D(\mathcal H_\lambda x,\mathcal H_\lambda y)
 \leq\max\{1,\lambda\}D(x,y).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The gauge transport in \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models} preserves the polar measure and, after subtraction of $f$, the ordinary type. In the coordinates $(\sigma,\mu)$ of the proof of \cref{thm:joint-curve-model-completeness}, $\mathcal G_f$ is therefore the map $(\sigma,\mu)\mapsto(\sigma+\ddc f,\mu)$. It does not change the area, and $b(\sigma+\ddc f,\tau+\ddc f)=b(\sigma,\tau)$. This proves that $\mathcal G_f$ is an isometry and the laws for inverses and compositions. Taking the atomic part commutes with this map, and subtraction of a smooth function and bounded changes preserve analytic singularities.

Scaling the competitors of the ordinary envelope shows that $\lambda p$ is an ordinary model for $\lambda\sigma$. In the coordinates $(\sigma,\mu)$, $\mathcal H_\lambda$ is the map $(\sigma,\mu)\mapsto(\lambda\sigma,\lambda\mu)$, with inverse $\mathcal H_{\lambda^{-1}}$, and taking the atomic part commutes with scaling. For $r\geq0$,
\[
 \min\{1,\lambda\}\min\{1,r\}
 \leq\min\{1,\lambda r\}
 \leq\max\{1,\lambda\}\min\{1,r\}.
\]
Apply this to every $C^k$ norm in \eqref{eq:curve-background-smooth-metric}; the total-variation term is multiplied by $\lambda$. The two bounds follow.
\end{proof}
