\section{Exact equations on products with zero lower mass}
\label{sec:zero-lower-mass-product-equations}

Positive lower mass guarantees solvability for all measures, but an individual density can have a positive threshold and an exact equation even when the lower mass vanishes. We construct explicit examples on products. Let $(X_0,h_0)$ be a connected compact Hermitian manifold of dimension $m\geq1$ with $v_-(h_0)=0$. On its product with a projective space, a singular potential pulled back from the projective factor solves an explicit equation whose density has a sublogarithmic singularity. The coefficient is computed from the normalization scalars of bounded truncations, the equation itself follows from the product structure of the Monge--Amp\`ere measure, and the product background again has zero lower mass.

Throughout this section, $Y=\mathbb P^k$ with $k\geq1$, $\kappa$ is the Fubini--Study form normalized by $\int_Y\kappa^k=1$, and $X=X_0\times Y$, a compact complex manifold of dimension $N=m+k$ with the Hermitian background $H=h_0+\kappa$. Forms and functions on the factors are identified with their pullbacks to $X$. Put
\[
 V_0=\int_{X_0}h_0^m,\qquad C=\binom Nm,\qquad
 \mu=V_0^{-1}h_0^m\wedge\kappa^k,\qquad R=\frac{\mu}{\mathrm{d}V},
\]
where $\mathrm{d}V$ is any smooth positive probability volume form on $X$. Then $\mu$ is a smooth positive probability volume form, and $R$ is smooth and bounded above and below by positive constants. The normalization scalar $c_H$ and the threshold $b_H$ are those of \cref{sec:lower-mass-solvability,sec:individual-density-subsolutions}, now on the $N$-dimensional manifold $X$ with background $H$.

For $0<\alpha<1$ define, on $Y$,
\[
 \psi([z_0:\cdots:z_k])=\log\frac{\sum_{i=1}^k|z_i|^2}{\sum_{i=0}^k|z_i|^2},
 \qquad u_\alpha=1-(1-\psi)^\alpha.
\]
The function $\psi$ is smooth on $Y\setminus\{o\}$, where $o=[1:0:\cdots:0]$, and tends to $-\infty$ at $o$; moreover $\psi\leq0$, with equality exactly on the hyperplane $\{z_0=0\}$. Thus $u_\alpha\leq0$, and $u_\alpha$ has a single pole at $o$, where $u_\alpha(o)=-\infty$.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projective-sublogarithmic-density}\index{Monge--Ampere density@Monge--Amp\`ere density!sublogarithmic pole}
The function $u_\alpha$ is $\kappa$-plurisubharmonic with supremum zero, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\kappa(u_\alpha)=q_\alpha\kappa^k,
 \qquad \int_Yq_\alpha\kappa^k=1.
\]
The density $q_\alpha$ is smooth and strictly positive on $Y\setminus\{o\}$, belongs to $L^1(\kappa^k)$, and belongs to no $L^p(\kappa^k)$ with $p>1$. If we set $q_\alpha(o)=+\infty$, then every truncation $\min(q_\alpha,s)$, $s>0$, is strictly positive and continuous on $Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In homogeneous coordinates, $\kappa+\ddc\psi=\ddc\log\sum_{i=1}^k|z_i|^2\geq0$ on $Y\setminus\{o\}$. The function $\chi(t)=1-(1-t)^\alpha$, $t\leq0$, is increasing and convex, with $0<\chi'\leq\alpha$. Hence, off $o$,
\[
 \kappa+\ddc u_\alpha\geq(1-\chi'(\psi))\kappa+\chi'(\psi)(\kappa+\ddc\psi)
 \geq(1-\alpha)\kappa.
\]
Since $u_\alpha$ is bounded above, it extends across $o$ as a $\kappa$-plurisubharmonic function, with value $-\infty$ at $o$. On the hyperplane $\{z_0=0\}$ we have $\psi=0$, hence $u_\alpha=0$, so $\sup_Yu_\alpha=0$.

To compute the measure, work in the affine chart $z_0=1$ and put $r=\sum_{i=1}^k|z_i|^2$. On this chart $\kappa=\ddc\log(1+r)$ and $\kappa+\ddc u_\alpha=\ddc F$, where $s(r)=1-\log(r/(1+r))$ and
\[
 F(r)=\log(1+r)+1-s(r)^\alpha,\qquad
 Q(r)=rF'(r)=\frac{r+\alpha s(r)^{\alpha-1}}{1+r}.
\]
For a radial function, the Levi form has the tangential eigenvalue $F'(r)=Q(r)/r$, with multiplicity $k-1$, and the radial eigenvalue
\[
 Q'(r)=\frac{1-\alpha s(r)^{\alpha-1}+\alpha(1-\alpha)r^{-1}s(r)^{\alpha-2}}{(1+r)^2}>0.
\]
Integrating in the radial variable gives $\int_{\{a<r<b\}}(\ddc F)^k=Q(b)^k-Q(a)^k$; the constant of proportionality is fixed by the case $F=\log(1+r)$, for which $(\ddc F)^k=\kappa^k$ has mass one on the chart and $Q(r)=r/(1+r)$. Since again $Q(0)=0$ and $Q(\infty)=1$, the punctured chart has mass one. The complement of the chart is a hyperplane, which has zero volume, and the non-pluripolar measure does not charge $o$. Hence $\int_Yq_\alpha\kappa^k=1$.

Relative to $\kappa^k$, whose tangential and radial eigenvalues are $1/(1+r)$ and $1/(1+r)^2$, the density on this chart is
\[
 q_\alpha(r)=(1+r)^{k+1}(Q(r)/r)^{k-1}Q'(r)
 \asymp r^{-k}(1-\log r)^{-1-k(1-\alpha)}\quad(r\downarrow0).
\]
Near $r=0$ the smooth volume is comparable to $r^{k-1}\mathrm{d}r$ in the radial variable, so $q_\alpha$ is integrable. For $p>1$, the substitution $L=1-\log r$ turns the integral of $q_\alpha^p$ into the integral of $\mathrm{e}^{k(p-1)L}$ times a power of $L$, which diverges. The same asymptotics show that $q_\alpha(r)\to+\infty$ uniformly as $r\downarrow0$, so every truncation $\min(q_\alpha,s)$ is continuous at $o$. Finally, $q_\alpha\geq(1-\alpha)^k>0$ by the lower bound $\kappa+\ddc u_\alpha\geq(1-\alpha)\kappa$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:split-product-normalization}\index{Normalization scalar!split products}
Let $g$ be a strictly positive continuous function on $Y$, and put $M_g=\int_Yg\kappa^k$. Then
\[
 c_H(Rg)=\frac{CV_0}{M_g}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $g$ is smooth. Since $\kappa$ is K\"ahler, \cref{thm:tw-smooth} on $Y$ gives a smooth $\phi_g$ with $A_g=\kappa+\ddc\phi_g>0$ and $A_g^k=(g/M_g)\kappa^k$; the scalar is determined by integration, since both sides have mass one. On $X$, since $h_0^{m+1}=0$ and $A_g^{k+1}=0$ for dimensional reasons,
\[
 (H+\ddc\phi_g)^N=C h_0^m\wedge A_g^k
                    =(CV_0/M_g)Rg\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Uniqueness of the scalar gives $c_H(Rg)=CV_0/M_g$. For continuous $g>0$, choose smooth $g_l>0$ converging uniformly to $g$. Since $g$ has a positive minimum, $(1-\varepsilon_l)g\leq g_l\leq(1+\varepsilon_l)g$ for some $\varepsilon_l\in(0,1)$ tending to zero. The order and homogeneity of the scalar in \cref{lem:bounded-normalizer-subsolution} give
\[
 (1-\varepsilon_l)c_H(Rg_l)\leq c_H(Rg)
                         \leq(1+\varepsilon_l)c_H(Rg_l).
\]
Since $M_{g_l}\to M_g$, the smooth case proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:zero-lower-mass-product-equation}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!zero lower mass products}
The Hermitian form $H$ is not closed and satisfies $v_-(H)=0$. For every $0<\alpha<1$, the function $f=Rq_\alpha$ is a probability density in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, belonging to no $L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ with $p>1$, and
\[
 b_H(f)=CV_0>0,\qquad
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u_\alpha)=b_H(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
The potential $u_\alpha$, pulled back to $X$, has supremum zero and finite mass $CV_0$. It belongs to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,H,0)$, and $P_{\delta H}(u_\alpha)\not\equiv-\infty$ for every $0<\delta<1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the threshold.} Write $a=\min_XR>0$, $A=\max_XR$, and $M_s=\int_Y\min(q_\alpha,s)\kappa^k$ for $s>0$. By \cref{lem:projective-sublogarithmic-density}, the truncations $\min(q_\alpha,s)$ are continuous and strictly positive, and $M_s\uparrow1$ as $s\to\infty$. The truncations of $f$ satisfy
\[
 R\min(q_\alpha,j/A)\leq\min(Rq_\alpha,j)
                         \leq R\min(q_\alpha,j/a).
\]
Hence \cref{lem:split-product-normalization} and the monotonicity of the scalar give
\[
 \frac{CV_0}{M_{j/a}}\leq c_H(\min(f,j))
                         \leq\frac{CV_0}{M_{j/A}}.
\]
Both bounds tend to $CV_0$ as $j\to\infty$, so $b_H(f)=CV_0$, whatever the reference volume $\mathrm{d}V$.

\emph{Step 2: the equation.} For $u_{\alpha,j}=\max(u_\alpha,-j)$, the binomial expansion of bounded local products, with factor forms of degrees $m$ and $k$, gives
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_H(u_{\alpha,j})
       =C h_0^m\wedge\mathrm{MA}_\kappa(u_{\alpha,j}).
\]
Multiplying by $\mathbf1_{\{u_\alpha>-j\}}$ and letting $j\to\infty$, as in the definition \eqref{eq:extended-ma} of the extended measure, we obtain
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u_\alpha)=Ch_0^m\wedge q_\alpha\kappa^k
 =CV_0\,q_\alpha\mu=CV_0f\,\mathrm{d}V,
\]
a measure of mass $CV_0$. The bounds $a\leq R\leq A$ transfer the integrability properties of $q_\alpha$ to $f$.

\emph{Step 3: lower mass and nonclosedness.} Pull back bounded $h_0$-plurisubharmonic functions whose masses on $X_0$ tend to zero. The same expansion shows that their $H$-masses are $C$ times their masses on $X_0$, so $v_-(H)=0$. If $h_0$ were closed, Stokes' theorem would give every bounded $h_0$-plurisubharmonic function the mass $V_0>0$, contradicting $v_-(h_0)=0$. Hence $h_0$, and therefore $H$, is not closed.

\emph{Step 4: the envelopes.} Choose $\alpha<\beta<1$. For $b\geq1$ and $\varepsilon>0$ with $\varepsilon\beta\leq1$, the number
\[
 K_b=\sup_{s\geq1}\{b s^\alpha-\varepsilon s^\beta\}-b
\]
is finite, since $\beta>\alpha$. The function $g_b=-\varepsilon(1-\psi)^\beta-K_b$ on $Y$ satisfies $\kappa+\ddc g_b\geq(1-\varepsilon\beta)\kappa\geq0$, by the chain-rule computation in the proof of \cref{lem:projective-sublogarithmic-density}, applied to the increasing convex function $t\mapsto-\varepsilon(1-t)^\beta$, whose derivative is at most $\varepsilon\beta$ for $t\leq0$. With $L=1-\psi\geq1$, the inequality $g_b\leq bu_\alpha$ reads $bL^\alpha-\varepsilon L^\beta-b\leq K_b$, so it holds on $Y\setminus\{o\}$, and also at $o$, where both sides are $-\infty$. Thus the pullback of $g_b$ belongs to $\PSH(X,H)$ and lies below $bu_\alpha$. Since $b\geq1$ is arbitrary, $u_\alpha\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,H,0)$ by \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}. For $0<\delta<1$, take $b=1$ and $\varepsilon\beta\leq\delta$. Then $g_1$ is $\delta H$-plurisubharmonic and lies below $u_\alpha$, so $P_{\delta H}(u_\alpha)\geq g_1$ is not identically $-\infty$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:elliptic-fivefold-exact-threshold}\index{Elliptic fibration!exact product equation}
Let $X_0$ be the elliptic threefold of \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}, with the balanced metric $\omega$ of \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric}. On $X_0\times\mathbb P^2$, with background $H=\omega+\kappa$, the densities $f=Rq_\alpha$ of the preceding theorem satisfy
\[
 v_-(H)=0,\qquad b_H(f)=240\pi,
 \qquad\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u_\alpha)=240\pi f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass} gives $v_-(\omega)=0$, and \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric} gives $V_0=24\pi$. The product theorem applies with $m=3$, $k=2$ and $C=\binom53=10$.
\end{proof}

These examples show that an individual density can have a positive threshold and an exact equation even though the background has zero lower mass. The exactness comes from the product structure; the argument does not identify the limits of the normalized exponential solutions for these densities. \Cref{qu:individual-equation-threshold} asks for an exact equation for every integrable density with positive threshold.
