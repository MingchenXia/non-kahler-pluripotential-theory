\subsection{A logarithmic endpoint for integrable data}
\label{subsec:unibranch-curve-density-endpoint}
\index{Unibranch space!integrable density endpoint}

The hypothesis $p>1$ in \cref{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation} guarantees that the Green correction is bounded and continuous. For merely integrable densities, the solution can leave the ordinary type. The following family of densities locates the boundary exactly, and it also gives continuous residuals for some densities outside every $L^p$, $p>1$.

We keep the notation $X,E,h,\beta,\beta_0,H,\Psi,\psi,A,M$ of \cref{sec:unibranch-curve-equations}, with $A<H$. Fix $q\in E$ and a flat coordinate $z$ centered at $q$, and put $r=|z|$ and $t=\log(1/r)$, so that $t>1$ on a small punctured disk around $q$. For $C_0>0$ and $\gamma>1$, choose a positive density $f_\gamma$, smooth away from $q$, which equals
\[
 f_\gamma=C_0r^{-2}t^{-\gamma}
\]
on a sufficiently small punctured disk around $q$, and extend it smoothly and positively to the complement. Set
\[
 F_\gamma=\int_Ef_\gamma\beta_0,\qquad
 c_\gamma=M/F_\gamma,\qquad
 \nu_\gamma=\pi_*(f_\gamma\beta_0).
\]
By the local computation in the proof of \cref{thm:curve-products}, the local integral of $f_\gamma$ is finite exactly for $\gamma>1$, and $f_\gamma$ lies outside $L^p(E,\beta_0)$ for every $p>1$.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:unibranch-curve-density-endpoint}
For this family, the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,h}(u)=c\nu_\gamma$ with some constant $c$ has a solution in the ordinary type of $\psi$ if and only if $\gamma>2$. In that case $c=c_\gamma$, the normalized solution is unique in the whole type, and its residual is continuous but, on the normalization, not H\"older continuous for any positive exponent.

For $1<\gamma\leq2$, there is a solution $u_\gamma\in\PSH(X,h)$ of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,h}(u_\gamma)
          =c_\gamma\nu_\gamma
\]
whose pullback to $E$ has the same multiplier ideals as $\Psi$ at every positive weight, but which violates the lower inequality $\psi\leq u_\gamma+C$ of the ordinary type for every constant $C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define on the punctured coordinate disk
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unibranch-density-primitive}
 P_\gamma(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{4\pi C_0}{V(\gamma-1)(\gamma-2)}
                         t^{2-\gamma},&\gamma\ne2,\\[4pt]
 \displaystyle-\frac{4\pi C_0}{V}\log t,&\gamma=2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Since $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\mathrm dz\wedge\mathrm d\bar z=dA/\pi$ and $\beta_0=dA/V$, this is the function $P_b$ of \eqref{eq:curve-log-primitive} with $b=\gamma$ and with $C_0$ replaced by $\pi C_0/V$. The local computation in the proof of \cref{thm:curve-products} therefore gives $\ddc P_\gamma=f_\gamma\beta_0$ off $q$; the flux of $P_\gamma$ through small circles tends to zero, so the distributional extension across $q$ adds no atom; and $|P_\gamma(t)|/t\to0$, so the Lelong number of $P_\gamma$ at $q$ is zero.

Take a smooth cutoff $\chi$ supported in the coordinate disk and equal to one near $q$, and extend $L=c_\gamma\chi P_\gamma$ by zero. The signed measure
\[
 \rho\beta_0=(c_\gamma f_\gamma-M)\beta_0-\ddc L
\]
has a smooth density $\rho$ on all of $E$: near $q$ the singular densities cancel, leaving $-M\beta_0$. Its integral is zero by Stokes' theorem and $c_\gamma F_\gamma=M$. Hence \cref{lem:torus-logarithmic-green} gives a smooth correction $R=G*(\rho\beta_0)$. Set $v_\gamma=L+R$ and $U_\gamma=\Psi+v_\gamma$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unibranch-density-current}
 \beta+\ddc U_\gamma
       =c_\gamma f_\gamma\beta_0+\sum_j a_j\delta_{q_j}.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
As in the proof of \cref{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation}, the positive local Laplacians give $U_\gamma\in\PSH(E,\beta)$, and \cref{thm:hermitian-space-descent} descends $U_\gamma-\sup_EU_\gamma$ to a potential $u_\gamma\in\PSH(X,h)$. It is smooth away from the original poles and from $q$. Its non-pluripolar product removes the original atoms, and a possible new value $-\infty$ at $q$ carries no atom. The mass is therefore $M$, and \eqref{eq:space-extended-ma} gives the equation on $X$.

For $\gamma>2$, $P_\gamma\to0$, so $v_\gamma$ extends continuously and boundedly, and the solution belongs to the prescribed ordinary type. Near $q$, the difference of $v_\gamma$ from its value at $q$ is $c_\gamma P_\gamma$, a nonzero multiple of $t^{2-\gamma}$, plus a smooth remainder of order $O(r)$. This decays more slowly than every power $r^\alpha$, $\alpha>0$, so the residual is not H\"older continuous for any positive exponent. Uniqueness follows from the mass of the whole type, the restoration of the fixed pole atoms and the harmonic-difference argument used in the proof of \cref{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation}; none of these steps requires $p>1$ once the bounded correction exists.

For $1<\gamma\leq2$, $P_\gamma\to-\infty$. The correction is bounded above but unbounded below near $q$, so the inequality $\Psi\leq U_\gamma+C$ fails off the pole set. Since its Lelong number is zero, the point atoms of $\beta+\ddc U_\gamma$ are those of $\beta+\ddc\Psi$. Applying \cref{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal} to each positive multiple, after a smooth local correction, shows that all multiplier ideals of $U_\gamma$ and $\Psi$ on $E$ coincide, also when $q$ is not one of the original poles. On $X$, this is resolution-ideal equivalence in the sense defined before \cref{prop:space-resolution-i-envelope}.

If another solution belonged to the ordinary type, its mass would be $M$, which forces the constant $c_\gamma$. Restoring its prescribed atoms in the extended equation would give exactly \eqref{eq:unibranch-density-current}. Its difference from $U_\gamma$ would then be harmonic on the torus, hence constant, with equality of the values everywhere. This contradicts the unboundedness of the correction near $q$, so no constant admits a solution in the ordinary type.

\end{proof}

The theorem is an exact criterion for this logarithmic family only; it does not characterize general $L^1$ data, and it gives no mass or uniqueness statement for the whole resolution-ideal class. On the projective cusp, all these constructions apply with the same, possibly degenerate, pulled-back background. The failure at $\gamma=2$ occurs already in the bounded ordinary type, that is, when there are no original poles.
