\subsection{A logarithmic endpoint for integrable data}
\label{subsec:unibranch-curve-density-endpoint}
\index{Unibranch space!integrable density endpoint}

The $p>1$ hypothesis of \cref{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation} is sufficient for a bounded continuous Green correction. Integrable data alone need not preserve the ordinary type. The following family gives an exact boundary and also gives continuous residuals for some data outside every $L^p$, $p>1$.

Retain $X,E,h,\beta,\beta_0,H,\Psi,A,M$ from \cref{sec:unibranch-curve-equations}, with $A<H$. Fix $q\in E$ and a flat coordinate $z$ centered there, with $r=|z|$ and $t=\log(1/r)>1$. For $C_0>0$ and $\gamma>1$, choose a positive density $f_\gamma$, smooth away from $q$, which equals
\[
 f_\gamma=C_0r^{-2}t^{-\gamma}
\]
on a sufficiently small punctured disk. Extend it smoothly and positively on the complement, and set
\[
 F_\gamma=\int_Ef_\gamma\beta_0,\qquad
 c_\gamma=M/F_\gamma,\qquad
 \nu_\gamma=\pi_*(f_\gamma\beta_0).
\]
The local integral is finite exactly for $\gamma>1$. For every such $\gamma$, this density lies outside every normalization-side $L^p$, $p>1$. Absolute continuity upstairs makes $\nu_\gamma$ nonpluripolar by the pullback argument in \cref{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation}.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:unibranch-curve-density-endpoint}
For this family, an equation with a positive proportionality constant has a solution in the prescribed ordinary type of $\psi$ if and only if $\gamma>2$. In that case the constant is $c_\gamma$, the normalized solution is unique in the entire genuine type, and its residual is continuous but has no positive H\"older exponent on the normalization.

For $1<\gamma\leq2$, there is a genuine descended solution of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,h}(u_\gamma)
          =c_\gamma\nu_\gamma
\]
with nonpluripolar mass $M$, whose normalization pullback is all-weight multiplier-ideal equivalent to $\Psi$. It fails the lower ordinary-type inequality. No solution in the prescribed ordinary type exists, even with an initially unspecified positive proportionality constant.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient differs from \eqref{eq:curve-log-primitive} because here $\beta_0=dA/V$. Define on the punctured coordinate disk
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unibranch-density-primitive}
 P_\gamma(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{4\pi C_0}{V(\gamma-1)(\gamma-2)}
                         t^{2-\gamma},&\gamma\ne2,\\[4pt]
 \displaystyle-\frac{4\pi C_0}{V}\log t,&\gamma=2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Direct differentiation gives
\[
 P_\gamma''(t)=\frac{4\pi C_0}{V}t^{-\gamma},\qquad
 \ddc P_\gamma=f_\gamma\beta_0
\]
off $q$. The radial mass in the disk is
\[
 -\tfrac12P_\gamma'(t)
       =\frac{2\pi C_0}{V(\gamma-1)}t^{1-\gamma}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the distributional extension adds no atom. Also $P_\gamma$ is locally integrable and $|P_\gamma(t)|/t\to0$, so its Lelong number is zero. These are the same flux and growth mechanisms as in \cref{thm:curve-products}, with the present area normalization.

Take a smooth cutoff $\chi$ supported in the coordinate disk and equal to one near $q$, and extend $L=c_\gamma\chi P_\gamma$ by zero. The signed measure
\[
 \rho\beta_0=(c_\gamma f_\gamma-M)\beta_0-\ddc L
\]
has a smooth density on all $E$: the singular densities cancel near $q$, leaving $-M\beta_0$ there. Its mean is zero by distributional Stokes and $c_\gamma F_\gamma=M$. Hence \cref{lem:torus-logarithmic-green} gives a smooth correction $R=G*(\rho\beta_0)$. Set $v_\gamma=L+R$ and $U_\gamma=\Psi+v_\gamma$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unibranch-density-current}
 \beta+\ddc U_\gamma
       =c_\gamma f_\gamma\beta_0+\sum_j a_j\delta_{q_j}.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Positive local Laplacians give the genuine canonical representatives. The direct disk-lift argument in \cref{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation} descends $U_\gamma-\sup_EU_\gamma$. It is smooth away from the original poles and $q$. Its nonpluripolar product removes the original atoms, and any new negative-infinite value at $q$ carries no atom. The mass is therefore $M$, and \eqref{eq:space-extended-ma} gives the equation downstairs.

For $\gamma>2$, $P_\gamma\to0$ and $v_\gamma$ extends continuously and boundedly. It belongs to the prescribed ordinary type. Its difference from its value at $q$ has a nonzero leading term proportional to $t^{2-\gamma}$, with a smooth remainder of order $O(r)$. This decays more slowly than every $r^\alpha$, $\alpha>0$, so the residual has no positive H\"older exponent. Uniqueness follows from the same full-type mass, restoration of the fixed pole atoms and harmonic-difference argument used in the preceding theorem; none of these steps requires $p>1$ once this bounded correction exists.

For $1<\gamma\leq2$, $P_\gamma\to-\infty$. The correction is bounded above but unbounded below near $q$, so the inequality $\Psi\leq U_\gamma+C$ fails off the pole set. Its zero Lelong contribution leaves the point atoms of $\beta+\ddc U_\gamma$ equal to those of $\beta+\ddc\Psi$. Applying \cref{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal} to each positive multiple after smooth local correction gives equality of all multiplier ideals on $E$, including when $q$ was not an original pole. This is resolution-ideal equivalence downstairs in the sense of \cref{prop:space-resolution-i-envelope}.

If another solution belonged to the ordinary type, its mass would be $M$ and would force the same constant $c_\gamma$. Restoring its prescribed atoms in the extended equation would give exactly \eqref{eq:unibranch-density-current}. Its $L^1$ difference from $U_\gamma$ would be harmonic on the torus and therefore constant, with equality of canonical values. This contradicts the unbounded correction on the punctured disk. It excludes every positive proportionality constant in the ordinary type.

Finally the density integral is a constant times $\int^\infty t^{-\gamma}dt$, whereas its $p$th power for $p>1$ has integral comparable to
\[
 \int^\infty e^{2(p-1)t}t^{-p\gamma}dt=+\infty.
\]
This proves both integrability assertions and completes the boundary calculation.
\end{proof}

The conclusion is an exact criterion for this logarithmic density family. It is not a characterization of arbitrary $L^1$ data or a mass and uniqueness theorem for the whole resolution-ideal class. On the projective cusp, all these constructions retain the same possibly degenerate pulled-back background. The failure at $\gamma=2$ already occurs in the bounded ordinary type, when the original pole list is empty.
