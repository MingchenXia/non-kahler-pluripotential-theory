\chapter{Potentials and equations on unibranch spaces}
\label{app:hermitian-spaces}
\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

To solve an equation on a singular complex space, we pass to a resolution and then descend the solution. The pullback background can degenerate along exceptional fibers, and removing a logarithmic pole can leave a residual that does not descend. The examples in this appendix address these two difficulties directly.

Except in the local toric calculations of \cref{subsec:quotient-boundary-ideals,subsec:affine-toric-adapted-ideals}, $X$ denotes a reduced irreducible compact complex space of pure dimension $n\geq1$ that is \emph{unibranch}: its germ at every point is irreducible, or equivalently, its normalization is a homeomorphism. We write $X_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and $X_{\mathrm{sing}}$ for the regular and singular loci. Every point of $X$ has a neighborhood that embeds as a closed analytic subset of an open set in some $\mathbb C^N$. Smooth functions and forms on $X$ are defined through such local embeddings: they are locally restrictions of smooth functions and forms on the ambient open sets. A Hermitian form on $X$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is locally the restriction of a smooth strictly positive ambient $(1,1)$-form. Fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ on $X$.

A \emph{resolution} of $X$ is a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map $\pi\colon Y\to X$ from a compact complex manifold $Y$; on $Y$ the pullback $\pi^*\theta$ is an ordinary smooth form, to which the theory of the main text applies. In the last three sections, $X$ is a specific space of this kind.

The basic tool is descent through a resolution. The pullback $\pi^*\theta$ has no component along the fibers of $\pi$, so the maximum principle on the connected compact fibers shows that every $\pi^*\theta$-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$ is constant on fibers and descends to $X$. This correspondence preserves envelopes and convergence. Exceptional sets carry no non-pluripolar mass, so the extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure defined through a resolution does not depend on the resolution.

On a projective cone, a logarithmic pole at the vertex becomes a divisor on the incidence resolution, and removing it leaves a strictly positive residual background. This gives uniqueness in the prescribed ordinary class, including for certain nonclosed backgrounds. The bounded equation on the cone uses closedness and an energy identity. The full logarithmic potential descends even when its smooth residual varies along the contracted divisor.

On a curve whose normalization is a torus, a periodic Green function gives a separate construction. It allows the pulled-back background to vanish at singular points, and it treats every potential in a fixed ordinary type with finitely many logarithmic poles. A projective cusp shows that the residual can be continuous even though the density on the curve is unbounded.

On products of a cuspidal curve with elliptic curves, a smooth potential repairs the degenerate background of the normalization, and two torsion identities fix the masses of all bounded potentials. For data close to the reference density, a positive solution gives uniqueness in the whole bounded class by a $\ddc$ energy comparison, even though the background is nonclosed.

We use three facts about unibranch spaces. Resolutions and common smooth refinements of two resolutions exist, and a proper bimeromorphic map to a unibranch space has connected fibers \cite[Theorems B.1.1--B.1.3]{SGPT}. The Fornaess--Narasimhan criterion identifies plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ as the upper semicontinuous functions that are subharmonic on holomorphic disks \cite[Theorem B.2.1]{SGPT}. The ramified disk lift used below is the argument in \cite[proof of Theorem B.2.2]{SGPT}.

\section{Potentials and disk tests}

\label{sec:space-background-setting}
Since $X$ may be singular, $\theta$-plurisubharmonicity is defined by testing along holomorphic disks. Let $\Delta\subset\mathbb C$ be the unit disk. For a holomorphic map $f\colon \Delta\to X$, the pullback $f^*\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $\Delta$ and therefore has smooth local potentials. Disk tests thus express the background inequality even when $\theta$ has no local potential on $X$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:space-theta-psh}\index{Unibranch space!plurisubharmonic potentials}
An upper semicontinuous function $u\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty)$, not identically $-\infty$ on any nonempty open subset, is \emph{$\theta$-plurisubharmonic} if it satisfies the following disk condition. For every holomorphic $f\colon \Delta\to X$, the function $u\circ f+a$ is subharmonic or identically $-\infty$ wherever $a$ is a smooth local potential for $f^*\theta$. The set of these functions is denoted by $\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\end{definition}

Two choices of $a$ differ by a harmonic function, so the condition is independent of that choice. On every disk where $u\circ f\not\equiv-\infty$, it gives $\ddc(u\circ f)+f^*\theta\geq0$ in the sense of distributions.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:space-local-correction}
Every member of $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is locally the sum of a plurisubharmonic function on $X$ and a smooth function. If $X$ is smooth, this definition agrees with \cref{def:theta-psh}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a local embedding, choose an ambient smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function $r$ with $\ddc r\geq\theta$ on a smaller neighborhood. For a disk $f$ and a local potential $a$ of $f^*\theta$, the function $r\circ f-a$ is subharmonic. Hence $(u+r)\circ f$ is subharmonic or identically $-\infty$. The Fornaess--Narasimhan criterion, together with the assumption that $u$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any open set, shows that $u+r$ is plurisubharmonic. The smooth case now follows from \cref{lem:smooth-disk-criterion}.
\end{proof}

\section{Descent through a resolution}

Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a resolution. Near a fiber of $\pi$, the background $\pi^*\theta$ is dominated by the pullback of a local $\ddc$-exact form, so a local correction makes a $\pi^*\theta$-plurisubharmonic function plurisubharmonic near the fiber. It is then constant on each compact irreducible component of the fiber, and connectedness of the fiber identifies these constants.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-space-descent}\index{Unibranch space!resolution descent}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a resolution. Pullback $u\mapsto u\circ\pi$ is an order-preserving bijection
\[
 \pi^*:\PSH(X,\theta)\longrightarrow\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta).
\]
It preserves suprema of individual functions, boundedness, and ordinary singularity comparisons.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pullback preserves the disk inequalities. Every nonempty open subset of $Y$ meets the locus where $\pi$ is biholomorphic, so a pullback is not identically $-\infty$ on any nonempty open set. Injectivity follows from surjectivity of $\pi$.

For the converse, take $v\in\PSH(Y,\pi^*\theta)$. Near a fiber, choose the local correction $r$ on $X$ used above. The function $v+r\circ\pi$ is plurisubharmonic on the inverse image of that neighborhood, since
\[
 \ddc(v+r\circ\pi)\geq\pi^*(\ddc r-\theta)\geq0.
\]
Its restriction to each compact irreducible component of the fiber is constant, possibly $-\infty$, by the maximum principle on a resolution of that component. Connectedness of the fiber makes these constants equal: intersecting components have the same value, and the finite intersection graph of the components is connected. Since $r\circ\pi$ is constant on the fiber, so is $v$.

Thus $v=u\circ\pi$ for a unique function $u$ on $X$. This function is upper semicontinuous: for each real $c$, the closed set $\{v\geq c\}$ has closed image under the proper map $\pi$, and that image is $\{u\geq c\}$. If $u$ were identically $-\infty$ on a nonempty open set, the same would hold for $v$ on its inverse image. Hence $u$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any nonempty open set.

It remains to verify the disk condition for $u$ with the background $\theta$ itself. Let $f\colon \Delta\to X$ be a holomorphic disk with $u\circ f\not\equiv-\infty$. Near a given point of $\Delta$, take a curve in the fiber product $\Delta\times_XY$ dominating the disk. Its normalization gives, after shrinking, a finite cover $\rho(t)=t^k$ and a holomorphic lift $f':\Delta\to Y$ with $\pi\circ f'=f\circ\rho$. Choose a smooth local function $a$ on the original disk with $\ddc a=f^*\theta$. The function
\[
 (u\circ f+a)\circ\rho=v\circ f'+a\circ\rho
\]
is subharmonic by \cref{lem:smooth-disk-criterion}. Subharmonicity descends through $\rho$ by the finite-disk argument following that lemma. Thus $u\circ f+a$ is subharmonic near every point, which proves that pullback is a bijection.

Since $\pi$ is surjective, pullback preserves suprema, uniform bounds and inequalities up to additive constants; this proves the remaining assertions.
\end{proof}

\section{Envelopes and normalized compactness}

Fix a resolution $\pi\colon Y\to X$. The envelopes of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} and \cref{def:singularity-envelope} are defined on $X$ by the same formulas: for an obstacle $f\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty]$, $P_\theta(f)$ is the upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of all $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\leq f$ pointwise, and $P_\theta[u]$ is defined as in \cref{def:singularity-envelope}. In the next proposition we assume that the family of these competitors is nonempty and locally uniformly bounded above. The descent bijection identifies the competitors on $X$ and on $Y$; the remaining issue is whether upper semicontinuous regularization commutes with pullback.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:space-envelope-pullback}\index{Unibranch space!envelopes}
For such an obstacle $f\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty]$, the envelope $P_\theta(f)$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$ and
\[
 P_{\pi^*\theta}(f\circ\pi)=P_\theta(f)\circ\pi.
\]
The same identity holds for the ordinary envelope $P_\theta[u]$, for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The preceding theorem identifies the competitor families. Let $s$ be their pointwise supremum on $X$; its pullback is the pointwise supremum upstairs. The regularization of $s\circ\pi$ on $Y$ is $\pi^*\theta$-plurisubharmonic and not identically $-\infty$, since there is a competitor below it. By the theorem, it descends to a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function $q$. Since $q\geq s$ and $q$ is upper semicontinuous, $q\geq s^*$.

For the reverse inequality, fix $x\in X$ and $y\in\pi^{-1}(x)$. For each neighborhood $U$ of $x$, the unregularized supremum upstairs is bounded by $\sup_U s$ on the open neighborhood $\pi^{-1}(U)$ of $y$. Therefore $q(x)\leq\sup_U s$, and taking the infimum over $U$ gives $q(x)\leq s^*(x)$. This proves the identity and shows that $P_\theta(f)=s^*$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. The proof for $P_\theta[u]$ is identical, because pullback preserves the ordinary singularity order together with its constants.
\end{proof}

We give the normalized space $\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):\sup_Xu=0\}$ the $L^1$ topology of the pullbacks to $Y$. By \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} and descent, it is compact. On a fixed resolution, different smooth positive volume forms give equivalent norms. Under a further modification, pullback is continuous by \cref{lem:modification-lone-pullback} and bijective by descent, hence a homeomorphism between the normalized compact spaces. Since any two resolutions are dominated by a common one, the topology does not depend on the resolution; the norms on different models are not compared.

\section{Ideal envelopes defined on resolutions}

Multiplier ideals on smooth models give a singularity relation on $X$. Call two potentials $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ \emph{resolution-ideal equivalent} if $u\circ\pi\sim_{\mathcal I}v\circ\pi$ on one resolution $\pi\colon Y\to X$ (the ideal type of \cref{sec:psh-singularity-types}), that is, if $\mathcal I(k\,u\circ\pi)=\mathcal I(k\,v\circ\pi)$ for every real $k>0$. By \cref{lem:ideal-equivalence-modification}, applied on a common refinement, this does not depend on the resolution. The ideal projection of \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection} then defines an envelope in this class.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:space-resolution-i-envelope}\index{Unibranch space!ideal envelope}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, there is a unique potential $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}^{\mathrm{res}}$ on $X$ satisfying
\[
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}^{\mathrm{res}}\circ\pi
       =P_{\pi^*\theta}[u\circ\pi]_{\mathcal I}
\]
on every resolution $\pi\colon Y\to X$, where the right-hand side is the multiplier-ideal envelope of \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope} on $Y$. It preserves the resolution-ideal class, is idempotent, has supremum zero, and is independent of the resolution.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed resolution, the right-hand side exists by \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection} and descends by \cref{thm:hermitian-space-descent}. Under a further smooth modification, the competitor families of the ideal envelopes correspond by the descent theorem and \cref{lem:ideal-equivalence-modification}, and the regularized suprema commute by the argument of \cref{prop:space-envelope-pullback}. Thus two resolutions give the same pullback on a common refinement, and hence the same function on $X$. Idempotence, preservation of the class and the normalization follow from the corresponding assertions on $Y$ and surjectivity of $\pi$.
\end{proof}

An intrinsic restricted ideal trace on a subspace contained in the singular locus would require a regularization compatible with restriction to that subspace; the resolution-ideal envelope provides neither the regularization nor the trace.

\section{Extended measures and finite damping}

Choose a resolution $\pi\colon Y\to X$. Two resolutions are isomorphic over the complement of a proper analytic subset of $X$, and their extended measures agree there. Since these measures do not charge pluripolar sets, the omitted analytic sets do not affect the comparison, even when the total mass is infinite.

For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:space-extended-ma}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,\theta}(u)
   =\pi_*\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{Y,\pi^*\theta}(u\circ\pi),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{Y,\pi^*\theta}$ is the extended measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma} on $Y$.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:space-extended-measures}\index{Unibranch space!resolution-independent measure}
The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:space-extended-ma} is the same for every resolution, including resolutions that modify regular points. It is a positive $\sigma$-finite Borel measure, gives zero mass to $X_{\mathrm{sing}}$ and to locally pluripolar sets, and may have infinite total mass; for bounded $u$ it is finite. Put $r_n=\max(n-2,0)$. There are finite constants $C$ and, for each $q>r_n$, $C_q$, depending only on the fixed background data and, for $C_q$, on $q$, such that for every nonpositive $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and every $p>0$,
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{e}^{pu}\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,\theta}(u)
       \leq C(1+p^{-1})^{r_n},\qquad
 \int_X(1-u)^{-q}\,\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,\theta}(u)
       \leq C_q.
\]
The constants may be obtained on any one fixed resolution.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Given two resolutions, choose a proper analytic subset $S\subset X$ containing $X_{\mathrm{sing}}$ and the images of both exceptional sets. Each map is an isomorphism over $X\setminus S$, and there the two measures are given by the same bounded local products, followed by truncation and exhaustion. On each resolution, the inverse image of $S$ is a proper analytic subset and has zero extended mass by \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}. Hence both pushforwards give $S$ zero mass. This proves independence, including for resolutions whose exceptional sets lie over regular points.

Positivity follows from positivity on $Y$. On $X\setminus S$ either resolution is a Borel isomorphism, so a countable exhaustion of $Y$ by sets of finite measure pushes forward to one on $X$; adjoining the null set $S$ proves $\sigma$-finiteness. A locally pluripolar subset of $X$ pulls back to a pluripolar set off the exceptional locus, and the exceptional locus itself has zero mass. This gives the asserted vanishing.

If $u$ is bounded, its pullback is bounded on the compact manifold $Y$, and local bounded-product estimates give finite total mass. For general nonpositive $u$, apply \cref{thm:general-background-damping} on $Y$ to the smooth background $\pi^*\theta$. Since the weights on $Y$ are the pullbacks of the weights on $X$, pushforward identifies the weighted integrals exactly, and the constants come from $\pi^*\theta$ and a Hermitian metric on $Y$.
\end{proof}

We define convergence in capacity on $X$\label{sec:space-capacity-convergence}\index{Unibranch space!capacity convergence} as convergence in capacity of the pullbacks to a resolution, using a smooth Hermitian metric there. The local capacity comparison of \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity} makes the metric immaterial, and \cref{lem:modification-capacity-pullback}, applied on a common resolution, makes the choice of resolution immaterial.

By \cref{thm:general-background-damping} and proper pushforward, both damped measures in the theorem depend weakly continuously on nonpositive potentials for this convergence in capacity. For nonpositive potentials $u_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ converging in capacity, the undamped measures $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,\theta}(u_j)$ converge weakly if, in addition, their total masses are uniformly bounded and their masses on $\{u_j<-T\}$ tend to zero uniformly in $j$ as $T\to\infty$. Pullback preserves these mass and sublevel conditions.

General equations on singular spaces require estimates for the degeneracy of the pulled-back background and of the density; see \cref{qu:singular-space-equations-program}. For the cone geometry below, a radial construction gives solutions in a prescribed logarithmic class and in the bounded class of a closed background.
