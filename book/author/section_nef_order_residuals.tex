\subsection{Positive-current subtraction and divisorial residuals}\label{sec:nef-divisorial-residuals}

A positive current difference orders every Gauduchon pairing. If the residual remains nef, the perturbed volumes stay positive and their logarithmic orders can be compared.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nef-positive-current-subtraction}
Let $\theta,\beta$ be smooth nef real $(1,1)$-forms on a compact complex $n$-fold, and suppose
\[
 S=(\theta-\beta)+\ddc\varphi\geq0
\]
is a positive $(1,1)$-current. Then for every $\epsilon>0$ and every fixed positive $h,\mu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-residual-volume-comparison}
 \mathcal V_\mu(\beta+\epsilon h)\leq \mathcal V_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\underline a(\beta)\geq\underline a(\theta)$ and $\overline a(\beta)\geq\overline a(\theta)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every admissible Gauduchon test $\gamma$, distributional integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_X(\theta-\beta)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 =\langle S,\gamma^{n-1}\rangle\geq0.
\]
The quotient for $\theta+\epsilon h$ is therefore at least that for $\beta+\epsilon h$ for the same test. Taking infima over the identical full test family proves the corresponding inequality for $\lambda_\mu$. Both sides are positive by \cref{prop:nef-degeneration-orders}, so raising to the $n$th power gives \eqref{eq:nef-residual-volume-comparison}. Division by the negative number $\log\epsilon$ reverses the logarithmic inequality and proves both order comparisons.
\end{proof}

For an effective real divisor $D$, Poincar\'e--Lelong gives $[D]=\eta_D+\ddc\varphi$, where $\eta_D$ is a smooth curvature representative. Thus \cref{prop:nef-positive-current-subtraction} applies to $\beta=\theta-\eta_D$ whenever $\beta$ is smooth nef. The same reasoning applies to a convergent positive divisorial sum only when its curvature representative is smooth and a global potential realizes the current difference. If $T=\theta+\ddc u$ is a chosen positive current and $S$ is the part removed, then $T-S=\beta+\ddc(u-\varphi)$. This identifies the residual to which the comparison applies.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nef-specified-residual-examples}
Within the smooth-nef domain, specified effective divisorial removal can preserve an order, increase it by an arbitrary integer up to the dimension or by a fraction, and destroy equality of lower and upper orders. Removing an effective divisor can also leave a non-nef residual.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On $E^n$, let $\omega_i$ be the flat area forms with integral one, set $h=\sum_i\omega_i$ and $\mu=\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n$. A coordinate divisor $F_i$ has smooth curvature representative $\omega_i$: on the corresponding elliptic curve, solve a zero-mean Poisson equation to adjust any curvature representative to $\omega_i$, then pull it back. For $\theta=h$ and $D=\sum_{i=1}^kF_i$, the residual $\beta=\sum_{i=k+1}^n\omega_i$ is smooth nef. Its perturbations have constant density, and the positive closed perturbation itself attains the all-test equality in \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-integer-residual-jump}
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h)=n!(1+\epsilon)^n,\qquad
 \mathcal V_\mu(\beta+\epsilon h)=n!\epsilon^k(1+\epsilon)^{n-k}.
\end{equation}
For $n=1$ the same formula follows from $\lambda_\mu(\xi)=\int_X\xi$. These examples realize each integer jump $1,\ldots,n$. A nonzero divisor with jump zero is given by $\theta=2h$ and $D=F_1$, since the residual remains strictly positive.

For a fractional jump, use the diagonal background $\beta=b\omega_1+q\omega_2$ of \cref{prop:nef-diagonal-fractional-and-zero} with $q=|\zeta|^{2m}$ near its unique zero, $m>1$. Choose $c>0$ and put $\theta=\beta+c\omega_2>0$ and $D=c(E\times\{w_0\})$, whose smooth curvature representative is $c\omega_2$. The original background has both orders zero; the residual has common order $1-1/m$.

To lose a common order, let $\beta_{\rm res}$ be the oscillatory rank-one background of \cref{prop:nef-oscillatory-orders} and put
\[
 \theta=\beta_{\rm res}+ch>0,\qquad
 D=c(\{z_0\}\times E)+c(E\times\{w_0\}),\qquad
 \eta_D=ch.
\]
The original $\theta$ has both orders zero: $\theta\geq ch$ yields a uniform positive lower bound for every test quotient, while one fixed test gives a finite upper bound. Its smooth nef residual is exactly $\beta_{\rm res}$ and has unequal orders. In each of these examples, the smooth residual plus $[D]$ is a positive current in the smooth $\ddc$-class of $\theta$, with divisorial part exactly $[D]$ because the other summand is smooth. Its minimality is not needed for the comparison; the minimal-current case is treated below.

Finally blow up $\mathbb P^2$ at two distinct points. Let $H$ be the pullback of the normalized Fubini--Study class, $E_1,E_2$ the exceptional curves, and $L$ the strict transform of the line through the points. The smooth semipositive pullback $\theta$ is nef. Subtract curvature representatives of $D=E_1+E_2$. The residual $\beta$ has class $H-E_1-E_2$ and contains the positive current $[L]$, by Poincar\'e--Lelong for the total transform of the line. The intersection numbers $H^2=1$, $H\cdot E_i=0$, $E_i^2=-1$, and $E_1\cdot E_2=0$ give
\[
 \int_L\beta=(H-E_1-E_2)^2=-1.
\]
If $\beta$ were nef, smooth representatives $\beta+\epsilon h+\ddc u_\epsilon>0$ would imply $-1\geq-\epsilon\int_Lh$ for all $\epsilon>0$, which is impossible.
\end{proof}

For a nef background, perturbative minimal divisorial multiplicities vanish. Indeed, every $\theta+\delta h$ has a smooth positive representative, so the infimum of its generic divisorial Lelong numbers is zero. Passing to $\delta\downarrow0$ still gives zero. Thus removing the divisor defined by these multiplicities leaves the background unchanged. Removing the divisorial part of an actual boundary current is different, even when that current has minimal singularities.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nef-boundary-minimal-removal}
On the ruled surface of \cref{lem:ruled-explicit-nef}, retain its nef curvature form $\theta$, infinity section $D$ and K\"ahler form $\kappa=\theta+\pi^*\sigma_0$. For every smooth positive probability form $\mu$,
\[
 \mathcal V_\mu(\theta+\epsilon\kappa)=2\epsilon(1+\epsilon),
 \qquad \mathcal V_\mu(\epsilon\kappa)=2\epsilon^2.
\]
The unique positive current in the boundary class is $[D]$. It has minimal singularities, and removing its full divisor leaves the zero background and changes the common order from one to two.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The earlier lemma proves uniqueness of $[D]$ and nefness. The calculation in \cref{prop:nonrecovery-zero-residual-volume} gives $\int_X\theta^2=0$. Put $B=\pi^*\sigma_0$. We have $B^2=0$ and
\[
 \int_X\theta\wedge B=\int_D B=1,
\]
by Poincar\'e--Lelong, Stokes and the fact that the section maps isomorphically to the elliptic base. The closed nef formula following \cref{thm:gaud-volume-nef-limit} now computes both volumes by expanding their squares. It holds for every $\mu$. The unique boundary current has minimal singularities because any two of its global potentials differ by a pluriharmonic function, hence by a constant. Choosing $\theta$ as the curvature of its entire divisor leaves zero. The two volume polynomials give the claimed orders.
\end{proof}
