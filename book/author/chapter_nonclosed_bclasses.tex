\chapter{Classes with a non-closed background}
\label{chap:nonclosed-bclasses}

Removing a closed divisorial current from a nonclosed background preserves its exterior derivative. The residual class therefore belongs to an affine space over Bott--Chern cohomology. The trace envelope supplies positive representatives on every model, while additional exceptional vanishing translates the class on a higher model. Analytic regularization gives nef Cartier realizations in backgrounds approaching the original one, and their positive residual currents converge on each fixed model. This linear approximation does not control the Monge--Amp\`ere mass: when bounded mass fails, even the family realized by a smooth positive form has an infinite-mass representative.

Throughout the chapter, $Y$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, $\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $Y$, and $\pi:Z\to Y$ denotes a smooth compact modification.

\section{Affine classes with prescribed derivative}\label{sec:affine-model-space}\index{Affine class!prescribed derivative}

Fixing the exterior derivative leaves closed currents as the differences between representatives. After quotienting by global potential terms, these differences are Bott--Chern classes. Write $\mathcal D'(Z,\mathbb R)$ for real distributions and set
\[
 \mathscr A_Z(\theta)=
 \frac{\{\pi^*\theta+C:C\text{ is a closed real }(1,1)\text{-current on }Z\}}
      {\ddc\mathcal D'(Z,\mathbb R)}.
\]
Write $\{T\}$ for the class of $T$. By \cref{lem:bc-one-one-current-description}, every class has a smooth representative, and translation by Bott--Chern classes gives
\[
 \mathscr A_Z(\theta)=\{\pi^*\theta\}+H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R).
\]
The smooth forms representing elements of $\mathscr A_Z(\theta)$ are exactly those satisfying $\mathrm{d}\eta=\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fixed-background-class-maps}
If $g:Z'\to Z$ is a compact modification over $Y$, pushforward and pullback induce maps
\[
 g_*:\mathscr A_{Z'}(\theta)\to\mathscr A_Z(\theta),
 \qquad g^*:\mathscr A_Z(\theta)\to\mathscr A_{Z'}(\theta),
 \qquad g_*g^*=\mathrm{id}.
\]
Pushforward preserves classes with positive current representatives.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $\pi'=\pi\circ g$. Translation by the pulled-back background identifies the required maps with the Bott--Chern maps of \cref{sec:bc-closed-currents}. Indeed, for a closed current $C'$ on $Z'$ and a smooth closed form $\beta$ on $Z$,
\[
 g_*(\pi'^*\theta+C')=\pi^*\theta+g_*C',\qquad
 g^*(\pi^*\theta+\beta)=\pi'^*\theta+g^*\beta.
\]
The first equality uses degree-one change of variables on the smooth background. The Bott--Chern maps are well-defined on the closed terms, so these identities define the affine maps independently of representatives. Their composition is the identity because $g_*g^*$ is the identity on the background and on Bott--Chern classes. Proper pushforward preserves positive currents, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

The prescribed derivative is essential to this pushforward construction: $g_*\pi'^*\mathrm{d}\theta=\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$ is smooth, whereas pushing forward an arbitrary smooth exterior derivative can produce a singular current.

A family with entries in $\mathscr A_Z(\theta)$ compatible with pushforward is a \emph{b-divisor class over the background $\theta$}. Pseudoeffectivity and Cartier realization have the meanings of \cref{def:bott-chern-bdivisor-class}, with affine classes in place of Bott--Chern classes. Nefness uses the almost positive smooth-representative condition of \cref{sec:nef-cartier-realizations}, now within $\mathscr A_Z(\theta)$.

\section{Residual trace classes}\label{sec:affine-trace-class}

The trace envelope dominates the prescribed divisorial currents on all models. Subtracting those currents from its curvature gives positive residual representatives with the derivative prescribed by the background. The classes are defined using the prescribed trace currents, even when the envelope has larger Lelong numbers.

Suppose $Y$ is a smooth submanifold of a connected compact complex manifold $X$. Let $\Theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ with $\Theta|_Y=\theta$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\Theta)$ satisfy $\nu(u,Y)=0$. Use the trace currents $S_Z(u;Y)$ of \cref{sec:divisorial-model-bound}.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nonclosed-trace-bclass}\index{Affine class!residual trace}
For the trace data just fixed, the family defined by
\[
 \mathbb B_Z(u;Y,\theta)
 =\{\pi^*\theta-S_Z(u;Y)\}\in\mathscr A_Z(\theta)
\]
is a pseudoeffective b-divisor class over $\theta$. It is unchanged by ambient multiplier-ideal equivalence and by a smooth $\ddc$ change of the restricted background, after identifying the resulting affine spaces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The currents $S_Z$ are closed, so the displayed classes belong to the indicated affine spaces. Their compatibility follows from \cref{thm:trace-divisorial-model-compatibility} and \cref{prop:fixed-background-class-maps}. Take the canonical $v=\mathcal T_\theta(u;Y)$ of \eqref{eq:trace-profile-envelope}. By \cref{prop:original-background-trace-majorant},
\[
 \pi^*\theta+\ddc(v\circ\pi)-S_Z(u;Y)\geq0
\]
on every model, proving pseudoeffectivity in the original restricted background. The currents $S_Z$ depend only on the ambient multiplier ideals. For a smooth real function $f$ on $Y$, a change $\theta\mapsto\theta+\ddc f$ adds $\ddc(f\circ\pi)$ to every representative, which is zero in the quotient. This proves both invariances.
\end{proof}

When $\theta$ is closed, this family is the Bott--Chern family of \cref{prop:trace-bott-chern-bclass}.

When the ambient manifold is $Y$ itself and $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$, the self-trace identity \cref{lem:self-trace-is-siu} gives
\[
 N_Z(u)=\sum_E\nu(u\circ\pi,E)[E]=S_Z(u;Y),
 \qquad \mathbb D_\theta(u)_Z=\{\pi^*\theta-N_Z(u)\}.
\]
Here $E$ ranges over prime divisors on $Z$. The theorem makes $\mathbb D_\theta(u)$ pseudoeffective. Divisorial removal gives the representative $\pi^*\theta+\ddc(u\circ\pi)-N_Z(u)\geq0$ directly, by \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} and passage to increasing finite sums.

\section{Nef realizations and residual-current limits}\label{sec:nonclosed-nef-approximation}

Resolving analytic singularities leaves a bounded residual potential. Its smooth approximants prove nefness, and its pullbacks acquire no new divisorial part. For a general potential, analytic approximation controls both the divisorial part and the remaining positive current on each fixed model. Absorbing the curvature error makes the approximants positive but changes their exterior derivative.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonclosed-analytic-nef-cartier}\index{Nef class!analytic realization}
If $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ has analytic singularities, then $\mathbb D_\theta(u)$ has a nef Cartier realization on a smooth compact modification.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Resolve the analytic singularity ideal as in the proof of \cref{thm:analytic-current-nef-cartier}. On one model $\pi:Z\to Y$ we have
\[
 \pi^*\theta+\ddc(u\circ\pi)=[D]+R,
 \qquad R=\beta+\ddc v\geq0,
\]
where $D$ is a finite effective rational divisor, $\beta$ is smooth, $v$ is globally bounded after choosing divisor metrics, and $\mathrm{d}\beta=\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$. Positivity follows from divisorial removal.

Fix a Hermitian form $\gamma$ on $Z$ and apply \cref{prop:bounded-almost-regularization} to $v$ in the smooth background $\beta$. It gives smooth $v_j$ with $\beta+\ddc v_j\geq-\epsilon_j\gamma$, $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$. These forms represent the same affine class and have exterior derivative $\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$, proving nefness.

On every further modification $g:Z'\to Z$, the current $g^*\beta+\ddc(v\circ g)$ is positive by \cref{lem:smooth-disk-criterion}. Its bounded potential has zero Lelong numbers, so the divisorial part of the full pullback is precisely $g^*D$. The residual affine class is consequently $g^*\{R\}$, proving the Cartier realization.
\end{proof}

For general $u$, add twice the curvature error of analytic regularization to the background. This gives positive analytic currents to which the proposition applies, while the ideal orders identify the limiting residual family.

\Needspace{21\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:varying-background-nef-approximation}\index{Nef class!varying derivative}\index{Residual current!weak approximation}
Let $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ and fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $Y$. There are analytic potentials $u_m\to u$ in $L^1(Y)$ and positive numbers $\epsilon_m\downarrow0$, indexed by sufficiently large positive integers $m$, such that
\[
 \theta_m=\theta+2\epsilon_m\omega,
 \qquad \theta_m+\ddc u_m\geq\epsilon_m\omega.
\]
For each $m$, the family $\mathbb D_{\theta_m}(u_m)$ has a nef Cartier realization. On every fixed compact model $\pi:Z\to Y$, its class has the current representative
\[
 C_{m,Z}=\pi^*\theta+2\epsilon_m\pi^*\omega-D_{m,Z},
 \qquad D_{m,Z}=\frac1m\sum_E\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(mu)[E].
\]
Here $E$ ranges over prime divisors on $Z$, and the order is computed after pulling the ideal back to $Z$. These representatives satisfy
\[
 C_{m,Z}\longrightarrow\pi^*\theta-N_Z(u)
 \quad\hbox{weakly as currents}.
\]
The positive residual representatives satisfy
\[
 R_{m,Z}:=C_{m,Z}+\ddc(u_m\circ\pi)
 \longrightarrow \pi^*\theta+\ddc(u\circ\pi)-N_Z(u)\geq0
 \quad\hbox{weakly as currents}.
\]
The exterior derivatives of both $C_{m,Z}$ and $R_{m,Z}$ converge smoothly to $\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $u_m$ from \cref{cor:nonclosed-analytic-approximation}, retaining its $L^1$ convergence. If $e_m\to0$ are its curvature errors, put $\epsilon_m=\sup_{\ell\geq m}e_\ell+1/m$. This gives positive decreasing errors while keeping the original integer indices. Adding $2\epsilon_m\omega$ changes the lower bound $-\epsilon_m\omega$ into $\epsilon_m\omega$. Each $u_m$ has analytic type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$, so \cref{prop:nonclosed-analytic-nef-cartier} gives a nef Cartier realization in background $\theta_m$.

On a fixed model the generic divisorial Lelong numbers of $u_m\circ\pi$ are exactly $m^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(mu)$. Dropping the $\ddc$ of that potential from the residual-current representative leaves precisely $C_{m,Z}$ in its affine class. The self-trace identity and \cref{thm:trace-divisorial-current-budget} give $D_{m,Z}\to N_Z(u)$ in trace mass, hence weakly as currents. The smooth error $2\epsilon_m\pi^*\omega$ tends to zero in every derivative, proving the convergence of $C_{m,Z}$.

Divisorial removal gives $R_{m,Z}\geq0$. All the $u_m$ and $u$ have one smooth lower curvature bound: choose $A>0$ with $\theta+\epsilon_m\omega\leq A\omega$ for every retained index. By \cref{lem:modification-lone-pullback}, their $L^1(Y)$ convergence implies $u_m\circ\pi\to u\circ\pi$ in $L^1(Z)$. Applying $\ddc$ and adding the already convergent $C_{m,Z}$ proves the residual-current limit. Positivity persists under weak convergence. Since each $D_{m,Z}$ is closed,
\[
 \mathrm{d}R_{m,Z}=\mathrm{d}C_{m,Z}
 =\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta+2\epsilon_m\pi^*\mathrm{d}\omega,
\]
which proves the stated smooth convergence.
\end{proof}

The classes lie in $\mathscr A_Z(\theta_m)$. Translation by $-2\epsilon_m\pi^*\omega$ identifies this space with $\mathscr A_Z(\theta)$, but subtracting that form need not preserve nefness. Thus the current convergence above does not give nef Cartier approximation with exactly fixed exterior derivative.

\section{Divisorial classes and infinite mass}\label{sec:affine-infinite-mass}

Recording the residual classes on all models does not determine the extended mass of a representative: the infinite-mass potential of \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} has the same multiplier ideals, and hence the same divisorial data, as a smooth potential.

Take the background to be a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $Y$, with upper mass $v_+(\omega)$ and extended measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega$ as in \cref{chap:products}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:affine-bclass-infinite-mass}\index{Affine class!infinite-mass representative}
If $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, there is $U\in\PSH(Y,\omega)$ such that
\[
 \mathbb D_\omega(U)=\mathbb D_\omega(0),\qquad
 \int_Y\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(U)=+\infty,
 \qquad \int_Y\omega^n<+\infty.
\]
The common family has the nef Cartier realization $(Y,\{\omega\})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $U$ from \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness}. Every positive-weight multiplier ideal is the unit ideal. Hence every divisorial order in $D_{m,Z}$ is zero on every model. The self-trace identity gives $N_Z(U)=0$, which is also the divisorial part of the constant potential. Both families are therefore the pullback classes of $\omega$. The form $\omega$ is smooth and positive, so its affine class is nef and realizes the family on the base. The infinite extended mass is part of the construction of $U$, whereas the smooth measure $\omega^n$ has finite mass by compactness.
\end{proof}
