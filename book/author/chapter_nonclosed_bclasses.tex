\chapter{Classes with a nonclosed background}
\label{chap:nonclosed-bclasses}

When the background $\theta$ is not closed, subtracting a closed divisorial current from its pullback leaves the exterior derivative unchanged, so the residual currents of \cref{chap:divisorial-models} are not closed and define no Bott--Chern class. They do define classes in an affine space over Bott--Chern cohomology, since two representatives with the same exterior derivative differ by a closed current. The trace envelope still gives positive residual representatives, and the exceptional divisors of \cref{sec:trace-exceptional-divisors} describe how the classes change between models.

Analytic singularities give nef Cartier realizations in these affine spaces. For a general potential, analytic regularization gives such realizations in slightly larger backgrounds, and on each fixed model the positive residual currents converge weakly. The error in the background tends to zero smoothly, but removing it can destroy nefness. Nor does this linear convergence control Monge--Amp\`ere mass: when the bounded mass property fails, a potential of infinite mass can have the same residual classes as the zero potential, whose classes are realized by a smooth positive form.

Throughout the chapter, $Y$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, $\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $Y$, and $\pi\colon Z\to Y$ denotes a smooth compact modification, called a model of $Y$ as in \cref{chap:divisorial-models}.

\section{Affine classes with prescribed derivative}\label{sec:affine-model-space}\index{Affine class!prescribed derivative}

Two real $(1,1)$-currents on $Z$ with exterior derivative $\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$ differ by a closed current, and modulo $\ddc$ of global distributions such differences are Bott--Chern classes. Let $\mathcal D'(Z,\mathbb R)$ denote the space of real distributions on $Z$, and set
\[
 \mathscr A_Z(\theta)=
 \frac{\{\pi^*\theta+C:C\text{ is a closed real }(1,1)\text{-current on }Z\}}
      {\ddc\mathcal D'(Z,\mathbb R)}.
\]
Write $\{T\}$ for the class of $T$ in this space. By \cref{lem:bc-one-one-current-description}, every class has a smooth representative, and translation by Bott--Chern classes gives
\[
 \mathscr A_Z(\theta)=\{\pi^*\theta\}+H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R).
\]
A smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$ represents an element of $\mathscr A_Z(\theta)$ exactly when $\mathrm{d}\eta=\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$. As for Bott--Chern classes, pullback of a class is defined on a smooth representative and pushforward on current representatives.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fixed-background-class-maps}
If $g\colon Z'\to Z$ is a smooth compact modification over $Y$, pushforward and pullback induce maps
\[
 g_*\colon\mathscr A_{Z'}(\theta)\to\mathscr A_Z(\theta),
 \qquad g^*\colon\mathscr A_Z(\theta)\to\mathscr A_{Z'}(\theta),
 \qquad g_*g^*=\mathrm{id}.
\]
Pushforward preserves classes with positive current representatives.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $\pi'=\pi\circ g$. Translation by the pulled-back background identifies the required maps with the Bott--Chern maps of \cref{sec:bc-closed-currents}. Indeed, for a closed current $C'$ on $Z'$ and a smooth closed form $\beta$ on $Z$,
\[
 g_*(\pi'^*\theta+C')=\pi^*\theta+g_*C',\qquad
 g^*(\pi^*\theta+\beta)=\pi'^*\theta+g^*\beta,
\]
where the first equality uses the change of variables formula for the degree-one map $g$ on the smooth background. Since the Bott--Chern maps are well defined on the closed terms, these identities define the affine maps independently of the representatives. Their composition is the identity because $g_*g^*$ is the identity both on the background and on Bott--Chern classes. Finally, pushforward under a proper map preserves positive currents.
\end{proof}

The prescribed derivative is essential for this pushforward: $g_*\pi'^*\mathrm{d}\theta=\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$ is smooth, whereas the pushforward of an arbitrary smooth exact form can be a singular current.

We call a family $(\mathbb A_Z)$, with $\mathbb A_Z\in\mathscr A_Z(\theta)$ for every model $Z$ and compatible with pushforward, a \emph{b-divisor class over the background $\theta$}. Pseudoeffectivity and Cartier realizations are defined as in \cref{def:bott-chern-bdivisor-class}, with affine classes in place of Bott--Chern classes. A class in $\mathscr A_Z(\theta)$ is \emph{nef} if, for a Hermitian form $\gamma$ on $Z$, it has for every $\epsilon>0$ a smooth representative bounded below by $-\epsilon\gamma$, as in \cref{sec:nef-cartier-realizations}.

\section{Residual trace classes}\label{sec:affine-trace-class}

Since the trace envelope dominates the trace currents on every model, subtracting them from its curvature current gives positive residual representatives, with exterior derivative $\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$. The classes are determined by the prescribed trace currents, even when the envelope has larger Lelong numbers.

In this section, $Y$ is a complex submanifold of a connected compact complex manifold $X$. Let $\Theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ with $\Theta|_Y=\theta$, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\Theta)$ have generic Lelong number $\nu(u,Y)=0$. We use the trace currents $S_Z(u;Y)$ of \cref{sec:divisorial-model-bound}, now with $\Theta$ in the role of the ambient background.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nonclosed-trace-bclass}\index{Affine class!residual trace}
The family defined by
\[
 \mathbb B_Z(u;Y,\theta)
 =\{\pi^*\theta-S_Z(u;Y)\}\in\mathscr A_Z(\theta)
\]
is a pseudoeffective b-divisor class over $\theta$. It is unchanged by ambient multiplier-ideal equivalence and, after the resulting affine spaces are identified, by a smooth $\ddc$ change of the restricted background.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The currents $S_Z$ are closed, so the classes belong to the indicated affine spaces, and their compatibility follows from \cref{thm:trace-divisorial-model-compatibility,prop:fixed-background-class-maps}. Take $v=\mathcal T_\theta(u;Y)$, the trace envelope of \eqref{eq:trace-profile-envelope}. By \cref{prop:original-background-trace-majorant},
\[
 \pi^*\theta+\ddc(v\circ\pi)-S_Z(u;Y)\geq0
\]
on every model, which proves pseudoeffectivity in the original restricted background. The currents $S_Z$ depend only on the ambient multiplier ideals. For a smooth real function $f$ on $Y$, the change $\theta\mapsto\theta+\ddc f$ adds $\ddc(f\circ\pi)$ to every representative, which is zero in the quotient. This proves both invariances.
\end{proof}

When $\theta$ is closed, this family is the Bott--Chern family of \cref{prop:trace-bott-chern-bclass}.

For the rest of the chapter, the ambient manifold is $Y$ itself, and $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$. For a smooth compact modification $\pi\colon Z\to Y$, we write $N_Z(u)$ for the divisorial current of $u\circ\pi$ and $\mathbb D_\theta(u)$ for the resulting residual family. By the self-trace identity of \cref{lem:self-trace-is-siu},
\[
 N_Z(u)=\sum_E\nu(u\circ\pi,E)[E]=S_Z(u;Y),
 \qquad \mathbb D_\theta(u)_Z=\{\pi^*\theta-N_Z(u)\},
\]
where $E$ ranges over the prime divisors on $Z$. By \cref{thm:nonclosed-trace-bclass}, $\mathbb D_\theta(u)$ is pseudoeffective. In this case divisorial removal gives the positive representative $\pi^*\theta+\ddc(u\circ\pi)-N_Z(u)\geq0$ directly, by \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} and passage to the limit of increasing finite sums.

\section{Nef realizations and residual-current limits}\label{sec:nonclosed-nef-approximation}

On a resolution of an analytic singularity, the bounded residual potential has smooth approximants with arbitrarily small curvature error, and these prove nefness within the same affine class. Boundedness also prevents new divisorial terms on further modifications, which gives the Cartier realization.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonclosed-analytic-nef-cartier}\index{Nef class!analytic realization}
If $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ has analytic singularities, then $\mathbb D_\theta(u)$ has a nef Cartier realization on a smooth compact modification.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Resolve the analytic singularity ideal as in the proof of \cref{thm:analytic-current-nef-cartier}. On a suitable model $\pi\colon Z\to Y$ we obtain
\[
 \pi^*\theta+\ddc(u\circ\pi)=[D]+R,
 \qquad R=\beta+\ddc v\geq0,
\]
where $D$ is a finite effective rational divisor, $\beta$ is smooth with $\mathrm{d}\beta=\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$, and $v$ is globally bounded after the choice of metrics on the line bundles of the components of $D$. Positivity of $R$ follows from divisorial removal.

Fix a Hermitian form $\gamma$ on $Z$ and apply \cref{prop:bounded-almost-regularization} to $v$ in the smooth background $\beta$. It gives smooth functions $v_j$ with $\beta+\ddc v_j\geq-\epsilon_j\gamma$ and $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$. These forms represent the same affine class and have exterior derivative $\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$, which proves nefness.

On every further modification $g\colon Z'\to Z$, the current $g^*\beta+\ddc(v\circ g)$ is positive by \cref{lem:smooth-disk-criterion}. Its bounded potential has zero Lelong numbers, so the divisorial part of the full pullback is exactly $g^*D$. The residual affine class on $Z'$ is therefore $g^*\{R\}$, which proves the Cartier realization.
\end{proof}

For general $u$, add twice the curvature error of analytic regularization to the background. This gives positive currents with analytic singularities, to which the proposition applies, while the ideal orders identify the limiting residual family.

\Needspace{21\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:varying-background-nef-approximation}\index{Nef class!varying derivative}\index{Residual current!weak approximation}
Let $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ and fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $Y$. There are potentials $u_m$ with analytic singularities, converging to $u$ in $L^1(Y)$, and positive numbers $\epsilon_m\downarrow0$, indexed by sufficiently large positive integers $m$, such that
\[
 \theta_m=\theta+2\epsilon_m\omega,
 \qquad \theta_m+\ddc u_m\geq\epsilon_m\omega.
\]
For each $m$, the family $\mathbb D_{\theta_m}(u_m)$ has a nef Cartier realization. On every fixed compact model $\pi\colon Z\to Y$, its class has the current representative
\[
 C_{m,Z}=\pi^*\theta+2\epsilon_m\pi^*\omega-D_{m,Z},
 \qquad D_{m,Z}=\frac1m\sum_E\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(mu)[E],
\]
where $E$ ranges over the prime divisors on $Z$ and the order is computed after pulling the ideal back to $Z$. These representatives satisfy
\[
 C_{m,Z}\longrightarrow\pi^*\theta-N_Z(u)
 \quad\hbox{weakly as currents},
\]
and the positive residual representatives satisfy
\[
 R_{m,Z}:=C_{m,Z}+\ddc(u_m\circ\pi)
 \longrightarrow \pi^*\theta+\ddc(u\circ\pi)-N_Z(u)\geq0
 \quad\hbox{weakly as currents}.
\]
The exterior derivatives of both $C_{m,Z}$ and $R_{m,Z}$ converge smoothly to $\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $u_m$ from \cref{cor:nonclosed-analytic-approximation}; they converge to $u$ in $L^1$. If $e_m\to0$ are their curvature errors, put $\epsilon_m=\sup_{\ell\geq m}e_\ell+1/m$. These numbers are positive and decrease to zero, and the indices $m$ are unchanged. Adding $2\epsilon_m\omega$ to the background turns the lower bound $-\epsilon_m\omega$ into $\epsilon_m\omega$. Since each $u_m$ has analytic type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$, \cref{prop:nonclosed-analytic-nef-cartier} gives a nef Cartier realization in the background $\theta_m$.

On a fixed model, the generic divisorial Lelong numbers of $u_m\circ\pi$ are exactly $m^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(mu)$, so $N_Z(u_m)=D_{m,Z}$ and $C_{m,Z}=\pi^*\theta_m-N_Z(u_m)$ represents the class of $\mathbb D_{\theta_m}(u_m)$ on $Z$. The self-trace identity and \cref{thm:trace-divisorial-current-budget} give $D_{m,Z}\to N_Z(u)$ in the trace mass of \cref{sec:divisorial-model-bound}, hence weakly as currents. Since the smooth error $2\epsilon_m\pi^*\omega$ tends to zero with all its derivatives, $C_{m,Z}$ converges as stated.

Divisorial removal gives $R_{m,Z}\geq0$. All the $u_m$ and $u$ have a common smooth lower curvature bound: choose $A>0$ with $\theta+\epsilon_m\omega\leq A\omega$ for every index $m$ considered. By \cref{lem:modification-lone-pullback}, the convergence in $L^1(Y)$ implies $u_m\circ\pi\to u\circ\pi$ in $L^1(Z)$. Applying $\ddc$ and adding the convergent currents $C_{m,Z}$ proves the convergence of the residual currents, and positivity persists in the weak limit. Finally, since each $D_{m,Z}$ is closed,
\[
 \mathrm{d}R_{m,Z}=\mathrm{d}C_{m,Z}
 =\pi^*\mathrm{d}\theta+2\epsilon_m\pi^*\mathrm{d}\omega,
\]
which proves the smooth convergence of the exterior derivatives.
\end{proof}

The classes $\mathbb D_{\theta_m}(u_m)_Z$ lie in $\mathscr A_Z(\theta_m)$. Translation by $-2\epsilon_m\pi^*\omega$ identifies this space with $\mathscr A_Z(\theta)$, but subtracting this form need not preserve nefness. Thus the convergence of currents above does not give an approximation by nef Cartier classes with exactly the prescribed exterior derivative.

\section{Divisorial classes and infinite mass}\label{sec:affine-infinite-mass}

Now let the background be a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $Y$, with upper mass $v_+(\omega)$ (\cref{def:bmp}) and extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega$ (\cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}). When $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, the potential of \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} has the same multiplier ideals as a smooth potential. The two potentials therefore have the same residual classes on every model, while their extended masses differ.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:affine-bclass-infinite-mass}\index{Affine class!infinite-mass representative}
If $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, there is $U\in\PSH(Y,\omega)$ such that
\[
 \mathbb D_\omega(U)=\mathbb D_\omega(0),\qquad
 \int_Y\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(U)=+\infty,
 \qquad \int_Y\omega^n<+\infty.
\]
The common family has the nef Cartier realization $(Y,\{\omega\})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $U$ from \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness}. All its multiplier ideals at positive weights are trivial, so every divisorial order in $D_{m,Z}$ vanishes on every model. The self-trace identity gives $N_Z(U)=0$, which is also the divisorial part of the zero potential. Both families therefore consist of the pullbacks of the class of $\omega$. Since $\omega$ is smooth and positive, its affine class is nef and realizes the family on $Y$ itself. The extended mass of $U$ is infinite by construction, whereas the smooth measure $\omega^n$ has finite mass because $Y$ is compact.
\end{proof}
