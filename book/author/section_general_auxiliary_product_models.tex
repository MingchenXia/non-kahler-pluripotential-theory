\subsection{Auxiliary compact complex factors}
\label{subsec:general-auxiliary-product-models}

The auxiliary factor can have any positive dimension and need not be K\"ahler. The projection argument uses a semipositive term that vanishes on the curve-product slices. The potentials and their polar metric depend only on the curve coordinates and the fixed unit divisor.

Keep $Y$, $\sigma_i$, $B_i$ and $\gamma_\sigma$, and the unit-divisor data $(Z,\kappa,D,\psi)$ above. Let $T$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $\ell\geq1$, and put $B=Y\times T$, with projections $\pi$ to $Y$ and $\operatorname{pr}_T$ to $T$. Choose a smooth semipositive real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha$ on $B$ whose restriction to every $Y\times\{t\}$ is zero. Semipositivity makes the tangent spaces of these slices part of its kernel, so $\alpha$ has only vertical coefficients in product coordinates. On $M=B\times Z$, let $\varpi,r$ be the projections and put $p=\pi\circ\varpi$ and
\[
 \Gamma_\alpha=\pi^*\gamma_\sigma+\alpha,\qquad
 \Theta_\alpha=\varpi^*\Gamma_\alpha+r^*\kappa,
 \qquad \Psi=\psi\circ r.
\]
Neither closedness of $\alpha$ nor bounded mass of $M$ is assumed.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:general-auxiliary-product-projections}\index{Envelope!compact complex auxiliary factor}
For $u_i\in\PSH(C_i,\sigma_i)$ put
\[
 u=\sum_i u_i\circ\pi_i,\qquad U=\Psi+u\circ p,
 \qquad q_i=P_{\sigma_i}[u_i],\quad
 r_i=P_{\sigma_i}[u_i]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{\Theta_\alpha}[U]&=\Psi+\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i\circ p,\\
 P_{\Theta_\alpha}[U]_{\mathcal I}
     &=\Psi+\sum_ir_i\circ\pi_i\circ p.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Every ordinary representative of either displayed envelope has zero extended mass. Its upper and lower singularity volumes are therefore zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $B$, sums of ordinary curve competitors give the lower bound
$P_{\Gamma_\alpha}[u\circ\pi]\geq\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i\circ\pi$.
For the upper bound, replace a competitor by its maximum with a sufficiently low translate of $u\circ\pi$. Every slice over $T$ is genuine. Since $\alpha$ restricts to zero there, the slice is a $\gamma_\sigma$-plurisubharmonic ordinary competitor for $u$. The zero-obstacle case of \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections} bounds it by $\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i$. This proves the ordinary base projection.

Use the atomic Green representatives $g_i$ from the proof of \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections}. Their sum pulled back to $B$ is $g\in\PSH(B,\Gamma_\alpha)$, ideally equivalent to $u\circ\pi$ and ordinarily equivalent to $\sum_ir_i\circ\pi_i\circ\pi$. Its Hessian is a finite-mass countable coordinate-divisor current minus a smooth form; finite mass follows using a fixed product Hermitian metric and the finite atomic curve masses. Thus every ideal competitor is ordinarily dominated by $g$, by \cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination}. If the divisor current is zero, $g$ is smooth and the domination follows directly from compactness. The proved ordinary projection, ordinary-type invariance and $P_{\sigma_i}[r_i]=r_i$ bound every such competitor by $\sum_ir_i\circ\pi_i\circ\pi$. The latter sum is itself an ideal competitor. This proves the ideal base identity.

Apply \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} to $M\to B$ with the unit divisor $B\times D$. Its induced zero obstacle is zero because $\sup_Z\psi=0$. This gives both upstairs identities. Applying its mass assertion to each displayed potential gives zero mass for every representative of its ordinary type, hence both zero singularity volumes.
\end{proof}

For normalized ordinary curve models $q_i$, write
\[
 \Phi_\alpha(q)=\Psi+\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i\circ p
\]
and let $\mathcal S_\alpha$ be their locus. Give it the metric
\[
 \rho_\alpha(\Phi_\alpha(q),\Phi_\alpha(\widetilde q))
       =\sum_i\|\mu_i-\widetilde\mu_i\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\]
where $\mu_i,\widetilde\mu_i$ are the corresponding polar curvature measures.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:general-auxiliary-product-metric}\index{Metric completeness!compact complex auxiliary factor}\index{Model potential!auxiliary factor independence}
The map $\Phi_\alpha(q)\mapsto\Phi_\sigma(q)$ is an isometry from $\mathcal S_\alpha$ onto the locus of \cref{prop:arbitrary-curve-product-metric}. It carries ideal subspaces and analytic models to each other. Consequently $\mathcal S_\alpha$ is complete and geodesic, with diameter $2\sum_iB_i$. Its ideal subspace is closed, geodesically convex and equal to the metric closure of the analytic models. Ideal projection is the unique nearest-point $1$-Lipschitz retraction, with distance equal to the sum of the nonatomic polar masses.

Changing $T$ or $\alpha$ leaves this abstract metric space unchanged. All area comparisons and ideal-commuting contractions of \cref{prop:arbitrary-curve-product-metric} hold even for different auxiliary data. The top-volume comparison expression vanishes on the locus. Metric convergence implies $L^1(M)$ convergence for every smooth positive volume form. For fixed $T,Z$ and curve backgrounds converging uniformly to zero-area backgrounds, the uniform model-tuple limit is the sum of $\Psi$ and their normalized smooth curve potentials, as in \cref{cor:arbitrary-product-zero-area-collapse}, for any choices of the semipositive terms.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lemma applied to model tuples proves that every $\Phi_\alpha(q)$ is a normalized ordinary model and computes its ideal projection coordinatewise. Genuine curve sections determine the tuple up to constants; the coordinate normalizations determine those constants. Thus the displayed map is well defined and bijective, and the polar formulas make it an isometry preserving ideal projection.

Each potential is pulled back from $Y\times Z$. Analytic presentations pull back under the auxiliary projection. Conversely a section fixing its auxiliary coordinate restricts an analytic presentation genuinely to $Y\times Z$, by \cref{prop:analytic-type-calculus}. Thus a potential is analytic on either product exactly when its common potential on $Y\times Z$ is analytic. The isometry therefore carries the analytic loci to each other. The complete metric, ideal closure, geodesics, nearest-point and area-comparison statements now follow from \cref{prop:arbitrary-curve-product-metric}.

A pairwise maximum is $\Psi$ plus the pullback of a genuine $\Gamma_\alpha$-plurisubharmonic base maximum. Unit-divisor reduction gives zero upper volume for it and for both models, so the comparison expression is zero. For $L^1$ convergence, integration along the fixed compact fibers bounds each pulled-back curve norm by a constant times that norm. The same estimate and \cref{thm:curve-zero-area-collapse} give the asserted uniform zero-area limit. Neither estimate contains $\alpha$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:general-auxiliary-all-obstacle-criterion}\index{Envelope!all-obstacle compact auxiliary criterion}
For the arbitrary curve potentials in the lemma, put $R=\Psi+\sum_ir_i\circ\pi_i\circ p$. The following are equivalent: $P_{\Theta_\alpha}[U]=P_{\Theta_\alpha}[U]_{\mathcal I}$; each polar curvature measure of $u_i$ equals its atomic part; $U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta_\alpha,R)$; and equality of the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $U$ below every smooth real obstacle on $M$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The lemma and the coordinate normalizations identify zero-obstacle equality with $q_i=r_i$ for every $i$. By \cref{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion}, this is equivalent to equality of the polar and atomic measures. In that case choose the curve stretching competitors relative to $r_i$. Their pullback sum plus $\Psi$ has positive current: the curve contributions, $\varpi^*\alpha$ and the unit-divisor current are all positive. Adding their convex stretching inequalities, including at poles, supplies a competitor for every finite stretch relative to $R$; adding ordinary bounds supplies $U\preceq R$. \Cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality at every smooth obstacle. Taking the zero obstacle recovers the initial condition, proving the equivalence.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:general-auxiliary-background-boundaries}\index{Model potential!nonbig auxiliary product}
If $\alpha=0$ or some $B_i=0$, the background $\Theta_\alpha$ is not big. If all $B_i>0$ and $\alpha$ is positive on every nonzero vertical tangent vector, it is big. In particular, for a Hermitian form $\omega_T$ and a positive nonconstant smooth function $h$ on $Y$, the choice $\alpha=(h\circ\pi)\operatorname{pr}_T^*\omega_T$ gives a big genuinely nonclosed background whenever all $B_i>0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $\alpha=0$, the background is pulled back from $Y\times Z$. By \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion}, every potential in it also descends there. Its current has zero vertical component along $T$ and cannot dominate a positive Hermitian form on $M$. Thus it is not big.

If $B_i=0$, choose any Hermitian form $\omega_T$ and write $s:M\to T$ for the projection. The positive $(m+\ell+k-1,m+\ell+k-1)$-form
\[
 L_i=p^*\left(\bigwedge_{j\ne i}\pi_j^*\eta_j\right)
       \wedge s^*(\omega_T^\ell)\wedge r^*(\kappa^k)
\]
is closed: $\omega_T^\ell$ has top degree on $T$. Its pairing with $\Theta_\alpha$ is zero, since only the integral of $\sigma_i$ survives. Its pairing with a positive product Hermitian form is strictly positive. Stokes' theorem therefore rules out a strictly positive current in the background.

When all areas are positive, write $\sigma_i=B_i\eta_i/A_i+\ddc\chi_i$. Subtracting the smooth pulled-back sum of the $\chi_i$ gives current $p^*\sum_i(B_i/A_i)\pi_i^*\eta_i+\varpi^*\alpha+r^*\kappa$. Positivity in the vertical directions makes this form strictly positive, proving bigness. For the stated choice of $\alpha$, its exterior derivative has nonzero component with one $Y$ direction and two $T$ directions, namely $\mathrm dh\wedge\omega_T$. The term with three $T$ directions cannot cancel it. Hence $\mathrm d\Theta_\alpha\ne0$ for nonconstant $h$.
\end{proof}

The auxiliary manifold may have unbounded mass; the proofs use no ambient mass bound. The complete metric and all-obstacle criterion concern this specified product locus. Extending the metric to unrestricted ordinary models remains open.
