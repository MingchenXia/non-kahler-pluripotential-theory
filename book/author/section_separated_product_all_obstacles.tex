\subsection{Equality below all obstacles on separated products}
\label{subsec:separated-product-all-obstacles}

We return to the question raised in \cref{subsec:all-obstacle-topology}: does equality of the two envelopes below zero imply their equality below every smooth obstacle? For sums of curve potentials the answer is yes, even for obstacles that mix the coordinates. By \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection}, membership in the relative envelope class of the ideal model implies equality below every obstacle, and this membership can be tested coordinatewise, because stretching competitors can be added before any obstacle is imposed.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:product-relative-envelope-classes}\index{Relative envelope class!separated products}
Let $N\geq1$, let $X_j$, $1\leq j\leq N$, be connected compact complex manifolds with smooth real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta_j$, and let $u_j,\varphi_j\in\PSH(X_j,\theta_j)$. On $X=\prod_jX_j$, with projections $\operatorname{pr}_j$, put
\[
 \vartheta=\sum_j\operatorname{pr}_j^*\theta_j,\qquad
 u=\sum_j u_j\circ\operatorname{pr}_j,\qquad
 \varphi=\sum_j\varphi_j\circ\operatorname{pr}_j.
\]
Then
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\vartheta,\varphi)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 u_j\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X_j,\theta_j,\varphi_j)
 \quad\text{for every }j.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The ordinary order between the sums is equivalent to the ordinary order in each coordinate. Indeed, the latter follows from the former by fixing the other coordinates at points where their potentials are finite and restricting the inequality, and the converse follows by adding the coordinate inequalities.

Suppose that each $u_j$ belongs to its relative class. For $b>1$, choose stretching competitors $h_j$ as in \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}. The sum $h=\sum_jh_j\circ\operatorname{pr}_j$ is $\vartheta$-plurisubharmonic and satisfies
\[
 b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})\varphi\leq u.
\]
This convex inequality is meaningful at poles. For $b=1$, use $h=u$. Thus $u$ belongs to the relative class on the product.

Conversely, fix $b>1$ and a stretching competitor $h$ on the product. Choose a point $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$ at which $h$ and all the coordinate potentials are finite; such points have full smooth volume. For each $j$, let $h^{(j)}$ be the restriction of $h$ to the slice through $x$ in the $j$th factor. It is $\theta_j$-plurisubharmonic and not identically $-\infty$, since it is finite at $x_j$. Put
\[
 c_j=\sum_{\ell\ne j}u_\ell(x_\ell),\qquad
 d_j=\sum_{\ell\ne j}\varphi_\ell(x_\ell).
\]
Restricting the product inequality to the slice gives
\[
 b^{-1}\bigl(h^{(j)}-bc_j+(b-1)d_j\bigr)
       +(1-b^{-1})\varphi_j\leq u_j.
\]
This gives a stretching competitor in the $j$th coordinate for every $b>1$. Together with the ordinary order and the competitor $u_j$ for $b=1$, it proves the converse.
\end{proof}

Keep the curves $C_i$ and the smooth forms $\sigma_i$ with $\PSH(C_i,\sigma_i)\ne\varnothing$ from \cref{subsec:arbitrary-curve-product-metrics}, and choose arbitrary $u_i\in\PSH(C_i,\sigma_i)$. Put
\[
 u=\sum_i u_i\circ\pi_i,\qquad
 q_i=P_{\sigma_i}[u_i],\qquad
 r_i=P_{\sigma_i}[u_i]_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 Q=\sum_i r_i\circ\pi_i.
\]
Write $\mu_i$ and $E_i$ for the polar and the atomic parts of the positive curvature measure $\sigma_i+\ddc u_i$, respectively.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:separated-product-all-obstacle-criterion}\index{Envelope!all-obstacle separated product criterion}
For these data, $Q=P_{\gamma_\sigma}[u]_{\mathcal I}$. The following are equivalent:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_{\gamma_\sigma}[u]=P_{\gamma_\sigma}[u]_{\mathcal I},
 \qquad \mu_i=E_i\quad\text{for every }i,\\
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\gamma_\sigma,Q),\\
 P_{\gamma_\sigma,F}[u]=P_{\gamma_\sigma,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
       \quad\text{for every smooth real }F\text{ on }Y.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The obstacle in the last condition may mix the curve coordinates. Curve backgrounds of zero area are allowed, and the models may have zero volume.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the zero obstacle, \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections} identifies the two envelopes with $\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i$ and $Q$, respectively. Since the coordinates are independent and all these functions have supremum zero, the two sums are equal if and only if $q_i=r_i$ for every $i$. By \cref{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion}, this is equivalent to $\mu_i=E_i$, and also to $u_i\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(C_i,\sigma_i,r_i)$, for every $i$, including backgrounds of zero area. The preceding lemma identifies these memberships with membership in the relative class on the product. Finally, \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality below every smooth obstacle, and taking $F=0$ gives the reverse implication. This proves all four equivalences.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:auxiliary-product-all-obstacle-criterion}\index{Envelope!nonclosed separated product criterion}
On $M=Y\times C_0\times Z$ with the background $\Theta_{\sigma,f}$, for any smooth $f\geq0$, put $U=\Psi+u\circ p$. Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{\Theta_{\sigma,f}}[U]&=\Psi+\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i\circ p,\\
 P_{\Theta_{\sigma,f}}[U]_{\mathcal I}&=\Psi+Q\circ p.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The following are equivalent: equality of these two envelopes; $\mu_i=E_i$ for every $i$; $U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta_{\sigma,f},\Psi+Q\circ p)$; and equality of the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $U$ below every smooth real obstacle on $M$. These statements include the case $f=0$ and curve backgrounds of zero area.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On $B=Y\times C_0$, with projection $\pi$ to $Y$, use the background $\gamma_{\sigma,f}$ of the proof of \cref{lem:arbitrary-product-model-projections}. Sums of ordinary competitors in the separate coordinates give
$P_{\gamma_{\sigma,f}}[u\circ\pi]\geq\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i\circ\pi$.
For the reverse inequality, replace an ordinary competitor by its maximum with a sufficiently low translate of $u\circ\pi$. Its restriction to every section $Y\times\{z\}$ is then not identically $-\infty$, nonpositive and at least as singular as $u$, so the case of the zero obstacle in \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections} bounds it by $\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i$. The reduction of \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}, applied to the divisor $B\times D$ with weight one, proves the ordinary identity upstairs.

By \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}, each $q_i$ has the multiplier ideals of $u_i$, so pullback and addition give $U\sim_{\mathcal I}\Phi_\sigma(q)$. These two potentials therefore have the same ideal envelope, which \cref{lem:arbitrary-product-model-projections} identifies with $\Psi+Q\circ p$.

If $\mu_i=E_i$ for every $i$, then $u_i\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(C_i,\sigma_i,r_i)$ by \cref{cor:curve-arbitrary-background-criterion}, and we choose stretching competitors in these curve classes. The sum of their pullbacks plus $\Psi$ is $\Theta_{\sigma,f}$-plurisubharmonic: its current is the sum of the pulled-back positive curve currents, the form $(f\circ p)s^*\tau$ and the current of the divisor with weight one. Adding the stretching inequalities gives the one for $U$ relative to $\Psi+Q\circ p$, also at poles, and the ordinary order follows in the same way. Thus $U$ belongs to that relative class, and \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection} gives equality below all obstacles. Taking the zero obstacle and using the two identities above recovers $q_i=r_i$, hence $\mu_i=E_i$. This closes the chain of equivalences.
\end{proof}

These criteria concern sums of curve potentials and their extensions to the product $M$. They give neither coordinatewise formulas for the envelopes below mixed obstacles nor the implication for general potentials of zero volume.
