\subsection{Quasi-continuous obstacles}
\label{sec:big-general-contact}

We now extend the contact property to discontinuous obstacles. Continuous approximations of an obstacle need not preserve every competitor of its envelope pointwise. For lower semicontinuous obstacles, the approximation of \cref{lem:big-pointwise-competitor} repairs this, and quasi-continuity then allows us to pass to limits of weighted measures. An unbounded obstacle is truncated at finite levels; in an arbitrary smooth background this produces signed products, whose limits are controlled by \cref{lem:signed-truncation-quasicont} before we restrict to the region where the product is positive. The strict analytic barrier $\rho$ and its pole set $Z$ are those fixed above.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:big-bounded-lsc-contact}
If $f$ is bounded and lower semicontinuous, then $p=P_\theta(f)$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus Z$, and
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{p<f\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)=0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Using the infimum construction in the proof of \cref{prop:lsc-obstacle-support}, take continuous $f_j\uparrow f$ with common bounds $m\leq f_j\leq M$, and shift $\rho$ below $m$. The envelopes $p_j=P_\theta(f_j)$ increase and satisfy
\[
 \rho\leq p_j\leq M.
\]
Their regularized limit $q=(\lim_jp_j)^*$ is therefore $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. Outside a pluripolar set it equals the pointwise limit, which is at most $f$; hence $q\leq p=P_\theta(f)$.

We prove the reverse inequality without using a domination theorem. Let $u$ be any competitor for $P_\theta(f)$, and let $u_t\leq f$ be the potentials given by \cref{lem:big-pointwise-competitor}, which satisfy this inequality everywhere. For fixed $t,\eta>0$, the sets
\[
 \{u_t<f_j+\eta\},\qquad j\geq1,
\]
are open, increase with $j$, and cover $X$. By compactness, $u_t\leq f_j+\eta$ everywhere for all large $j$. Thus $u_t-\eta$ is a competitor for $p_j$, and $q\geq u_t-\eta$. Letting first $\eta\downarrow0$ and then $t\downarrow0$, we obtain $q\geq u$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. Taking the supremum over all competitors $u$ gives $q\geq p$, so $q=p$.

For fixed $k$, the set $U_k=\{p<f_k\}$ is open. If $j\geq k$, then $p_j\leq p<f_k\leq f_j$ on $U_k$, so \cref{thm:big-continuous-envelope-contact} gives $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p_j)=0$ there. On every relatively compact open subset of $X\setminus Z$ the functions $p_j$ have common bounds. After a common smooth correction and a finite expansion, the local Bedford--Taylor continuity along increasing sequences shows that their measures converge to $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)$ there. Since the measure of an open set is at most the lower limit of the measures of the approximating sequence, $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)$ vanishes on $U_k\setminus Z$. The union of these sets is $\{p<f\}\setminus Z$, and the extended measure does not charge the pluripolar set $Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:big-bounded-quasicont-contact}
The conclusion of \cref{prop:big-bounded-lsc-contact} holds for every bounded quasi-continuous obstacle $f$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:quasicont-lsc-approximation}, there are bounded lower semicontinuous $f_j$ with common bounds and $f_j\downarrow f$ quasi-everywhere. The envelopes $p_j=P_\theta(f_j)$ decrease to $p=P_\theta(f)$. Indeed, the common lower barrier shows that the limit is not identically $-\infty$; the limit lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere and is thus a competitor for $P_\theta(f)$, while monotonicity gives the other inequality. All these functions have common local bounds off $Z$.

For fixed $k$, put $H_k=\min\{1,(f-p_k)_+\}$ on $X\setminus Z$, a bounded quasi-continuous function there. If $j\geq k$, then on $\{H_k>0\}$ we have $p_j\leq p_k<f\leq f_j$ outside a pluripolar set, so \cref{prop:big-bounded-lsc-contact} gives $H_k\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p_j)=0$ off $Z$.

Let $g$ be continuous with compact support in $X\setminus Z$. The continuity of bounded products against quasi-continuous weights from \cref{sec:psh-local-capacity} applies to the fixed quasi-continuous coefficient $gH_k$. Consequently $H_k\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)=0$ off $Z$. As $k\to\infty$, the sets $\{H_k>0\}$ exhaust $\{p<f\}\setminus Z$, and $Z$ is not charged because it is pluripolar.
\end{proof}

For an unbounded obstacle, we truncate and use the convergence of signed products in \cref{lem:signed-truncation-quasicont}.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:big-general-envelope-contact}\index{Contact set!big background}
Let $\theta$ be smooth and big. Let $f$ be a quasi-continuous obstacle, finite almost everywhere, such that $p=P_\theta(f)\not\equiv-\infty$. Then $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere and
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{p<f\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

As before, the measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)$ may have infinite total mass.

\begin{proof}
As in the proof of \cref{thm:envelope-contact}, the local envelope theorem and a countable Choquet family give $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. Set $f_j=\max(f,-j)$ and $p_j=P_\theta(f_j)$. Then $p$ is a competitor for every $f_j$. Since $f_j\leq f_1$ and $f_1$ is finite almost everywhere, the finite-sublevel argument in \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} gives a common upper bound for all competitors. Thus the envelopes $p_j$ are $\theta$-plurisubharmonic and uniformly bounded above. Choose $M$ strictly larger than this bound.

The obstacle $g_j=\min(f_j,M)$ is bounded and quasi-continuous. Since $p_j<M$ and $p_j\leq f_j$ quasi-everywhere, $p_j$ is a competitor for $P_\theta(g_j)$; the reverse inequality follows from $g_j\leq f_j$. Thus $p_j=P_\theta(g_j)$, and \cref{prop:big-bounded-quasicont-contact} gives contact support for $p_j$ with respect to $g_j$. Because $p_j<M$, no contact occurs at the artificial upper cutoff, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-truncated-obstacle-contact}
 \mathbf1_{\{p_j<f_j\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p_j)=0.
\end{equation}
Each $p_j$ is locally bounded off $Z$, with a lower barrier of the form $\rho-C_j$. The sequence decreases to $p$: its limit is not identically $-\infty$ because it dominates $p$, and it lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere, so it is itself a competitor for $P_\theta(f)$.

Fix a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta$. For integers $C,k\geq1$, consider the bounded $\Omega$-plurisubharmonic functions
\[
 v_{j,C}=\max(p_j,-C),\qquad v_C=\max(p,-C),
\]
and set
\[
 H_{k,C}
  =\min\{1,(f-p_k)_+\}\min\{1,(p+C)_+\}
       \quad\hbox{on }X\setminus Z,
\]
extended by zero on $Z$. This function is bounded and quasi-continuous. Indeed, off $Z$ the function $p_k$ is a finite quasi-plurisubharmonic function, truncation by bounded continuous functions preserves quasi-continuity, and the pluripolar set $Z$ can be removed by an open set of arbitrarily small capacity. At a pole of $p$ the second factor vanishes.

For $j\geq k$, on $\{H_{k,C}>0\}$ we have, outside pluripolar sets,
\[
 p_j\geq p>-C,\qquad p_j\leq p_k<f\leq f_j.
\]
By plurifine locality, the signed measure $\nu_{j,C}=(\theta+\ddc v_{j,C})^n$ therefore agrees there with $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p_j)$, and \eqref{eq:big-truncated-obstacle-contact} gives
\[
 H_{k,C}\nu_{j,C}=0.
\]
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
For fixed $C$, the potentials $v_{j,C}$ have common global bounds and decrease to $v_C$, so \cref{lem:signed-truncation-quasicont} yields
\[
 \int_X H_{k,C}(\theta+\ddc v_C)^n=0.
\]
On the set where $H_{k,C}$ is positive we have $p>-C$, so the restricted product in \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma} identifies $H_{k,C}(\theta+\ddc v_C)^n$ with the positive measure $H_{k,C}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)$; on the complement the coefficient is zero. Hence $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)$ vanishes on $\{H_{k,C}>0\}$.

Finally, if $p(x)$ is finite and $p(x)<f(x)$, then $p_k(x)<f(x)$ for large $k$ and $p(x)>-C$ for large $C$. The countable union of the sets $\{H_{k,C}>0\}$ therefore covers $\{p<f\}$ outside $Z\cup\{p=-\infty\}$. Both excluded sets are pluripolar, and the theorem follows.
\end{proof}
