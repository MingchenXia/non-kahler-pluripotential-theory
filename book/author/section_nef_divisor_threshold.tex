\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nef-divisor-threshold-volume}
Assume $b>0$ and that $D_p$ has a smooth maximizing sequence $(T_j)$ with uniformly integrable densities $p k_{T_j}$. Let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be a threshold subsolution for $f$, and suppose that $\omega+\ddc u\geq[E]$ for an effective real divisor $E$ whose Bott--Chern class is nef. Let $\beta_E$ be any smooth closed representative of that class. Then for every smooth strictly positive probability measure $\mu$, the Gauduchon root volume of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} satisfies
\[
 \lambda_\mu(\beta_E)=0.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:threshold-divisorial-constraint}, there is a maximizing current $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$ with $\int_X\beta_E\wedge T=0$. If $\lambda_\mu(\beta_E)>0$, then \cref{prop:nef-annihilator-determinant} gives $\Gamma_0(T)=0$, hence $D_{f^{1/n}}(T)=0$, contradicting the positivity of its maximal value. Since the nef class of $\beta_E$ has $\lambda_\mu(\beta_E)\geq0$, the conclusion follows for each $\mu$.
\end{proof}

The semipositive criterion and the nef-volume criterion detect different obstructions. On the Hopf threefold, the class of a hyperplane divisor has zero variational volume, so \cref{cor:nef-divisor-threshold-volume} says nothing about it, but its nowhere vanishing semipositive representative excludes a divisorial component at the threshold.

To see this, let $X$ be the Hopf threefold with its Hopf metric $\omega=\alpha+\zeta$ (\cref{chap:hopf,sec:hopf-divisorial-volume}), where $\alpha$ is the pullback of the Fubini--Study form under the elliptic fibration $X\to\mathbb P^2$, and let $H$ be the inverse image of a projective hyperplane. The class of $H$ is represented by $\alpha$, which is semipositive of rank two everywhere. Its variational volume vanishes for every smooth positive volume form $\mu$: the metrics $\gamma_t=\alpha+t\zeta$, $t>0$, are Gauduchon by the proof of \cref{prop:hopf-divisorial-threshold}, and since $\alpha^3=\zeta^2=0$ and $\omega^3=3\alpha^2\wedge\zeta$, one has $\int_X\alpha\wedge\gamma_t^2=\tfrac{2t}{3}\int_X\omega^3$ and $\gamma_t^3=t\,\omega^3$. Hence the quotient in \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} is $O(t^{1/3})$ as $t\downarrow0$. By \cref{cor:hopf-lower-mass,prop:root-uniform-tails}, one has $\mathcal D_\omega^0=L^3$ on this example. Hence, for every nonzero nonnegative $L^1$ density, a threshold subsolution has zero generic Lelong coefficient along $H$. Otherwise divisorial removal, as in \cref{prop:divisorial-gaud-feasibility}, would give $\omega+\ddc u\geq a[H]$ for some $a>0$, contradicting \cref{cor:semipositive-divisor-exclusion} applied to $aH$. This exclusion uses no bounded mass property. It is also stronger here than nonbigness of the class of $H$: the residual formulas of \cref{prop:hopf-divisorial-threshold} allow a positive coefficient along $H$ when the equation constant is smaller than the threshold.
