\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nef-divisor-threshold-volume}
Assume $b>0$ and that $D_p$ has a smooth maximizing sequence $(T_j)$ with uniformly integrable densities $p k_{T_j}$. Let $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ be a subsolution at $b$. Suppose it satisfies $\omega+\ddc u\geq[E]$ for an effective real divisor $E$ whose Bott--Chern class is nef. Let $\beta_E$ be any smooth closed representative of that class. For every smooth strictly positive probability measure $\mu$, the root volume of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} satisfies
\[
 \lambda_\mu(\beta_E)=0.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:threshold-divisorial-constraint}, there is a maximizing current $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$ with $\int_X\beta_E\wedge T=0$. If $\lambda_\mu(\beta_E)>0$, \cref{prop:nef-annihilator-determinant} gives $\Gamma_0(T)=0$. It follows that $D_{f^{1/n}}(T)=0$, contradicting its positive maximal value. Since nefness gives $\lambda_\mu(\beta_E)\geq0$, the conclusion follows for each $\mu$.
\end{proof}

The semipositive criterion and the nef-volume criterion detect different obstructions. The Hopf hyperplane class has zero variational volume, but its nowhere vanishing semipositive representative excludes a component at the threshold.

For example, the hyperplane divisor $H$ on the Hopf threefold has a smooth semipositive representative of rank two everywhere. By \cref{cor:hopf-lower-mass,prop:root-uniform-tails}, one has $\mathcal D_\omega^0=L^3$ on this example. Hence, for every nonzero nonnegative $L^1$ density, a threshold subsolution has zero generic divisorial coefficient along $H$. Otherwise divisorial removal, as in \cref{prop:divisorial-gaud-feasibility}, would give $\omega+\ddc u\geq a[H]$ for some $a>0$, contradicting \cref{cor:semipositive-divisor-exclusion} applied to $aH$. This exclusion uses no bounded mass property. It is stronger here than nonbigness alone: the residual formulas of \cref{prop:hopf-divisorial-threshold} allow a positive coefficient along $H$ at smaller equation constants.
