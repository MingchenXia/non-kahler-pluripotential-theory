\section{Integrability of Gauduchon determinant measures}
\label{sec:gaud-determinant-integrability}

A lower bound for the constants of smooth equations in terms of an $L^p$ norm of the density gives, by duality, a uniform $L^q$ bound for the determinant measures of all normalized Gauduchon currents, for every exponent $q<n/(n-1)$. At the endpoint $q=n/(n-1)$, the optimal constant is exactly $v_-(\omega)^{-1/n}$, where $v_-(\omega)$ is the lower Monge--Amp\`ere mass of \cref{def:bmp}. Thus a uniform bound for the determinant densities of the dual tests at the critical exponent is equivalent to positive lower mass of the background.

Write $\Gamma_0(T)$ for the determinant measure $\Gamma_F(T)$ of \eqref{eq:weak-gaud-functional} with $F=0$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gaud-determinant-subcritical}
For every $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$, the measure $\Gamma_0(T)$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$; we write $\Gamma_0(T)=k_T\,\mathrm{d}V$ and call $k_T\geq0$ the \emph{determinant density} of $T$. For every $1<q<n/(n-1)$ there is $C_q>0$ such that
\[
 \|k_T\|_{L^q(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq C_q
 \qquad(T\in\mathscr G_\omega).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $p>1$ and a smooth strictly positive function $h$. By \cref{thm:tw-smooth} there are a smooth form $\Omega_h=\omega+\ddc u_h>0$ and a constant $c_h>0$ such that
\[
 \Omega_h^n=c_hh^n\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
The lower-density estimate of \cref{thm:lp-potential-estimate} gives a constant $c_p>0$, independent of $h$, such that
\[
 c_h\|h^n\|_{L^p(\mathrm{d}V)}\geq c_p.
\]
The pointwise matrix arithmetic--geometric mean inequality, applied to $\Omega_h$ and the coefficient density of $T$, gives the inequality of measures
\[
 c_h^{1/n}h\,\Gamma_0(T)\leq\Omega_h\wedge T.
\]
Integrating and using $\ddc T=0$ yields
\[
 \int_Xh\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_0(T)
 \leq c_h^{-1/n}\int_X\Omega_h\wedge T
 =c_h^{-1/n}
 \leq c_p^{-1/n}\|h\|_{L^{np}(\mathrm{d}V)}.
\]
Approximation by smooth strictly positive functions extends this bound to every continuous nonnegative $h$, and applied to $|h|$ it bounds the integral of every continuous function against $\Gamma_0(T)$ by its $L^{np}$ norm. Since continuous functions are dense in $L^{np}$, the duality between $L^{np}$ and $L^{np/(np-1)}$ shows that $\Gamma_0(T)$ has a density in $L^{np/(np-1)}$, with norm at most $c_p^{-1/n}$. As $p$ ranges over $(1,+\infty)$, the exponent $np/(np-1)$ ranges over $(1,n/(n-1))$. This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

In particular, the determinant of the matrix density vanishes on the singular part of the trace measure: if $\sigma_T=\sigma_{T,\mathrm{ac}}+\sigma_{T,\mathrm{s}}$ is its Lebesgue decomposition with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$, then
\[
 \det P_T=0\quad\sigma_{T,\mathrm{s}}\text{-almost everywhere}.
\]
Indeed, $f_0=(\mathrm{d}V/\omega^n)^{1/n}$ is everywhere positive and $\Gamma_0(T)=f_0(\det P_T)^{1/n}\sigma_T$ is absolutely continuous. This says nothing about a possible singular part of the current $T$ itself.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gaud-determinant-endpoint}\index{Gauduchon metric!determinant integrability}
With the extended value $+\infty$ allowed, one has
\[
 \sup_{T\in\mathscr G_\omega}
       \|k_T\|_{L^{n/(n-1)}(\mathrm{d}V)}=v_-(\omega)^{-1/n},
\]
where the right side is interpreted as $+\infty$ when $v_-(\omega)=0$. The supremum does not change if $T$ is restricted to the smooth strictly positive members of $\mathscr G_\omega$. Thus a uniform estimate at the endpoint exponent holds if and only if $v_-(\omega)>0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $v_-(\omega)>0$. For the smooth equation in the preceding proof, the definition of $v_-$ gives
\[
 c_h\int_Xh^n\,\mathrm{d}V=\int_X\Omega_h^n\geq v_-(\omega).
\]
The same pointwise inequality and Stokes' theorem therefore give
\[
 \int_Xh\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_0(T)
      \leq v_-(\omega)^{-1/n}\|h\|_{L^n(\mathrm{d}V)}.
\]
By duality, $\|k_T\|_{L^{n/(n-1)}}\leq v_-(\omega)^{-1/n}$ for every $T\in\mathscr G_\omega$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For the reverse bound, let $g$ be any smooth positive probability density relative to $\mathrm{d}V$, and solve
\[
 \Omega_g^n=c(g)g\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Let $T_g$ be the trace-normalized $(n-1)$st power of the Gauduchon metric conformal to $\Omega_g$. Equality in the pointwise matrix inequality gives
\[
 \int_Xg^{1/n}\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_0(T_g)=c(g)^{-1/n}.
\]
Since $\|g^{1/n}\|_{L^n}=1$, H\"older's inequality gives $\|k_{T_g}\|_{L^{n/(n-1)}}\geq c(g)^{-1/n}$. By \cref{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema,thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula},
\[
 \inf_{g>0\ \mathrm{smooth},\ \int_Xg\,\mathrm{d}V=1}c(g)=v_-(\omega).
\]
Taking the supremum over these smooth currents $T_g$ proves the matching lower bound; if $v_-(\omega)=0$, the same argument makes this supremum infinite. Since the currents $T_g$ are smooth, this also proves that allowing nonsmooth currents does not change the supremum.
\end{proof}

The formula shows that the optimal endpoint constant does not depend on the choice of $\mathrm{d}V$, whereas the subcritical constants $C_q$ may depend on it.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gaud-adjoint-lq}
For every $R>0$ and $1<q<n/(n-1)$ there is a constant $C_{R,q}$ with the following property. If $F\in C(X)$ and $\|F\|_\infty\leq R$, every supporting probability $\nu\in\mathcal A_0(F)$ of \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints} has a density $a$ satisfying
\[
 \nu=a\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad\|a\|_{L^q(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq C_{R,q}.
\]
If $v_-(\omega)>0$, this holds also for $q=n/(n-1)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Recall that $M(F)=\exp(-B_0(F)/n)$, and represent $\nu$ by a maximizing current $T\in\mathscr M(F)$, using \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints}. Since $\Gamma_F(T)=\mathrm{e}^{F/n}\Gamma_0(T)$ and $B_0$ is $1$-Lipschitz,
\[
 \nu=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_T}{M(F)}\,\mathrm{d}V,
 \qquad M(F)\geq \mathrm{e}^{-R/n}M(0)>0.
\]
\Cref{thm:gaud-determinant-subcritical} therefore gives the subcritical bound with constant $\mathrm{e}^{2R/n}M(0)^{-1}C_q$. When $v_-(\omega)>0$, the same argument with the endpoint constant $v_-(\omega)^{-1/n}$ of \cref{thm:gaud-determinant-endpoint} gives the case $q=n/(n-1)$.
\end{proof}

On the threefold of \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}, every Hermitian form has zero lower mass, so the endpoint estimate fails for the whole family $\mathscr G_\omega$, while all the subcritical estimates above remain valid. For a fixed smooth density, \cref{thm:weak-gaud-compact-duality} gives a unique smooth maximizing current, and its determinant density is bounded. Sequences of currents whose values approach the maximum need not, however, have uniformly bounded higher moments, as \cref{prop:gaud-determinant-concentration} shows.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
On the other hand, the endpoint estimate can hold on a manifold with infinite upper mass. On the Hopf threefold of \cref{chap:hopf}, take the Gauduchon metric $\omega$ of that chapter, for which $v_-(\omega)=6$ and $v_+(\omega)=+\infty$, and set $\mathrm{d}V=\omega^3/6$. The normalized Gauduchon form $T_0=\omega^2/6$ has $\sigma_{T_0}=\mathrm{d}V$ and $P_{T_0}=I$. Hence $k_{T_0}=6^{-1/3}$, and
\[
 \sup_{T\in\mathscr G_\omega}\|k_T\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathrm{d}V)}=6^{-1/3}.
\]
Thus an endpoint determinant estimate, with its optimal constant attained, can coexist with infinite upper Monge--Amp\`ere mass.
