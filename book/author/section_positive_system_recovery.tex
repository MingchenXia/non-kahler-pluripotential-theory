\subsection{Recovery of envelope systems}
\label{subsec:positive-system-recovery}

We return to the big background $\theta$ on $X$ and apply the monotone limits above to the envelope systems of \cref{chap:hermitian-envelope-systems}. Fix real numbers $a<c$. Recall that $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta)$ consists of the pointwise concave, decreasing families $\Gamma=(\Gamma_\tau)_{\tau<c}$ of ideal models in background $\theta$ that are constant for $\tau\leq a$ (\cref{def:bounded-left-i-curve}). An envelope system $\boldsymbol\Gamma\in\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)$ is a compatible family of such curves $\Gamma^\eta\in\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta+\eta)$, indexed by the Hermitian forms $\eta$ (\cref{def:hermitian-envelope-system}). The map $\mathsf E$ projects a curve in background $\theta$ to a system, and $\mathsf R$ takes a system to its limiting curve $(\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma)_\tau=\lim_j\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau$, for any Hermitian form $h$ (\cref{thm:hermitian-system-retraction}). The system is recovered from its limit if $\boldsymbol\Gamma=\mathsf E\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma$.

Compatibility fixes every local divisorial Lelong number of the slices before the perturbation tends to zero, and recovery means that these numbers do not increase in the limit. A positive limiting lower volume rules out such an increase. Concavity propagates positivity from the constant left part to every finite parameter, so a single condition on the initial slice suffices for the whole system.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:positive-volume-system-recovery}\index{Envelope system!positive-volume recovery}
Let $\boldsymbol\Gamma\in\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta)$, fix a Hermitian form $h$, and put $\Gamma=\mathsf R\boldsymbol\Gamma$. Then
\[
 \lim_{j\to\infty}\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta+h/j}
       (\Gamma^{h/j}_a)
       =\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\Gamma_a).
\]
In particular this limit is independent of $h$. If it is positive, then
$\boldsymbol\Gamma=\mathsf E(\Gamma)$, and for every $\tau<c$ both singularity volumes satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_{\theta+h/j}(\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau)
       \searrow\operatorname{vol}_\theta(\Gamma_\tau),
 \qquad \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\Gamma_\tau)>0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every ideal model is an ordinary model by \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}, and the retraction theorem gives
$\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau\searrow\Gamma_\tau$ in the decreasing backgrounds $\theta+h/j$. Thus \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} proves the identity for the initial lower volumes, including the case where the limit is zero.

Suppose that this limit $\ell$ is positive. For $\tau\leq a$, the slice is $\Gamma_a$. For $a<\tau<c$, choose $r\in(\tau,c)$ and put $s=(\tau-a)/(r-a)$, so that $\tau=(1-s)a+sr$. Concavity gives
\[
 (1-s)\Gamma_a+s\Gamma_r\leq\Gamma_\tau.
\]
Homogeneity, \cref{prop:volume-addition} and volume monotonicity imply
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\Gamma_\tau)
       \geq(1-s)^n\ell>0.
\]
\Cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} now gives both volume limits for every fixed $\tau<c$.

It remains to show that the slices keep their multiplier ideals in the limit. Fix $\tau<c$ and write
$q=\Gamma_\tau$, $q_j=\Gamma^{h/j}_\tau$ and $\theta_j=\theta+h/j$. By \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison}, the upper volumes of the fixed potential $q$ in the backgrounds $\theta_j$ converge to its upper volume in $\theta$, which is the limit of the upper volumes of the $q_j$. Consequently
\[
 D_j=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)
          -\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)
          \geq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)>0.
\]
After discarding finitely many indices, choose $b_j>1$ with $b_j\to+\infty$ and
$b_j^nD_j<\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{\theta_j}(q_j)$.
Since $q\leq q_j$, the stretching lemma \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} in background $\theta_j$ gives $v_j\in\PSH(X,\theta_j)$ such that
\[
 (1-b_j^{-1})q_j+b_j^{-1}v_j\leq q.
\]

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta+h$, so that all $v_j$ are $\Omega$-plurisubharmonic. By \cref{lem:uniform-exponential}, there is $\alpha>0$ such that $\mathcal I(\alpha v_j)=\mathcal O_X$ for all $j$; additive normalization does not change these ideals. Let $E$ be a local divisor over a coordinate neighborhood $U$, with generic Lelong number $\nu_E$ and log discrepancy $A_U(E)$ as in \cref{sec:psh-divisorial-estimates}. \Cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error}, with $m=\alpha$, gives $\nu_E(v_j)\leq A_U(E)/\alpha$. Taking Lelong numbers in the convex inequality therefore yields
\[
 0\leq\nu_E(q)-\nu_E(q_j)
       \leq\frac{A_U(E)}{\alpha b_j}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Compatibility gives all components the same multiplier ideals, so $\nu_E(q_j)$ is independent of $j$, and hence equal to $\nu_E(q)$. Blowing up a point on a local modification converts its pointwise Lelong number into such a divisorial number; on a curve the point itself is a divisor. Thus all Lelong numbers of $q$ and $q_j$ on all local modifications agree, and \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} gives $q\sim_{\mathcal I}q_j$. This holds at every parameter $\tau<c$, so \cref{prop:hermitian-system-realization-criterion} proves recovery.
\end{proof}

The lower bound $(1-s)^n\ell$ may tend to zero as $\tau\uparrow c$; the theorem gives no positive lower bound that is uniform up to the cutoff. The hypothesis is invariant under a smooth change of background $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$, $f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$, through the gauge bijection of \cref{prop:hermitian-system-gauge}: the estimate there makes the transported initial slice ordinarily equivalent to $\Gamma_a-f$, and the map $u\mapsto u-f$ preserves the curvature currents and hence both singularity volumes. The ruled-surface system of \cref{thm:positive-background-system-nonrecovery}, which is not recovered from its limit, has zero initial limiting lower volume, so the theorem does not apply to it.
