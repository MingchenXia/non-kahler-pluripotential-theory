\section{Currents on the elliptic threefold}
\label{sec:elliptic-closed-current-descent}\label{subsec:elliptic-current-descent}

On the elliptic threefold of \cref{sec:vanishing-lower-mass}, closed classes can be described completely. The product of the two horizontal forms is $\ddc$-exact, so every positive $\ddc$-closed $(1,1)$-current has zero vertical component, and the fiberwise descent of \cref{lem:horizontal-pluriharmonic-descent} identifies it with a current on the base. Closedness descends as well. The closed positive cones, the singularity envelopes and the spaces of sections selected by multiplier ideals are therefore all computed on the base surface.

Let $X$ be the compact threefold of \cref{sec:vanishing-lower-mass}, and let
\[
 \pi\colon X\longrightarrow B=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1
\]
be its elliptic fibration. Write $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ for the Fubini--Study forms of the two factors, each of integral one, and put $\alpha=\pi^*\sigma_1$ and $\beta=\pi^*\sigma_2$. The product coordinates of that construction show that $\pi$ is locally a holomorphic product with fiber the elliptic curve $E$. The logarithmic ratio $t$ of that construction is defined only on the covering space, but its differentials descend to $X$. We take for $\omega$ the balanced metric $\omega=\alpha+\beta+D$, where $D=\mathrm{d}t\wedge\dc t$. By \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric}, it satisfies
\[
 \alpha\wedge\beta=\tfrac12\ddc\omega.
\]
The arguments below use this identity, not the vanishing of the lower Monge--Amp\`ere mass.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:elliptic-positive-current-descent}
Every positive $\ddc$-closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$ has the form
\[
 T=\pi^*S
\]
for a unique positive $\ddc$-closed $(1,1)$-current $S$ on $B$. Moreover, $T$ is closed if and only if $S$ is closed. Here $\pi^*S$ is the pullback of currents under the holomorphic submersion $\pi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The measure $T\wedge\alpha\wedge\beta$ is positive. Since $\ddc T=0$, Stokes' theorem for currents gives
\[
 \int_XT\wedge\alpha\wedge\beta
   =\tfrac12\int_X\omega\wedge\ddc T=0,
\]
so this measure vanishes. All products in this identity pair a current with smooth forms. Now apply \cref{lem:horizontal-pluriharmonic-descent} to $\pi$, with the base Hermitian form $\eta=\sigma_1+\sigma_2$. Since $\eta^2=2\sigma_1\wedge\sigma_2$, we have $T\wedge\pi^*\eta^2=0$, and since the fiber is a compact connected elliptic curve, the lemma gives the unique positive $\ddc$-closed current $S$. The same lemma shows that $S$ is closed when $T$ is; conversely, pullback commutes with $\mathrm{d}$.
\end{proof}

This description concerns $(1,1)$-currents. It does not conflict with the Gauduchon forms of \cref{lem:lower-mass-unbounded-root}: those have bidegree $(2,2)$ and satisfy $\ddc\Gamma_j=0$, with bounded degree and divergent volume of their positive roots, and they belong to a different cone of currents.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-bott-chern-cones}\index{Elliptic fibration!closed positive cones}
Pullback
\[
 \pi^*\colon H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(B,\mathbb R)
           \longrightarrow H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)
\]
is injective. The pseudoeffective and nef cones on $X$ coincide and are
\[
 \operatorname{Psef}(X)=\operatorname{Nef}(X)
 =\{a\{\alpha\}_{\mathrm{BC}}+b\{\beta\}_{\mathrm{BC}}:
                                           a,b\geq0\}.
\]
For the trace functional of the Gauduchon metric $\omega$, the slice of this cone at trace one is the segment $a+b=(8\pi)^{-1}$. Every class in the cone has a smooth semipositive representative, and none is big. The real form $\beta-\alpha$ is de Rham exact but represents a nonzero Bott--Chern class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a smooth closed real form $\tau$ on $B$ satisfies $\pi^*\tau=\ddc u$ with $u$ smooth on $X$. The restriction of $u$ to each compact connected elliptic fiber is harmonic, hence constant, and local holomorphic sections show that $u=v\circ\pi$ for a smooth function $v$ on $B$. Thus $\pi^*(\tau-\ddc v)=0$, which implies $\tau=\ddc v$ because $\pi$ is a submersion. This proves injectivity.

The usual cohomology computation for $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, together with the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma on this K\"ahler surface, gives
\[
 H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(B,\mathbb R)
       =\mathbb R\{\sigma_1\}\oplus\mathbb R\{\sigma_2\},
 \qquad \int_B\sigma_1\wedge\sigma_2=1,
 \qquad \sigma_1^2=\sigma_2^2=0.
\]
If $S$ is a positive closed current on $B$, the two coefficients of its class are
\[
 a=\int_BS\wedge\sigma_2\geq0,
 \qquad b=\int_BS\wedge\sigma_1\geq0.
\]
By \cref{lem:bc-one-one-current-description} and \cref{lem:elliptic-positive-current-descent}, every pseudoeffective class on $X$ is the pullback of the class of such a current, and it therefore lies in the cone of the statement. Conversely, every class in that cone has the smooth semipositive representative $a\alpha+b\beta$, so it is nef and pseudoeffective. This proves the equality of the cones; the whole Bott--Chern group of $X$ is not needed.

Since $\omega^2=2\alpha\wedge\beta+2(\alpha+\beta)\wedge D$ and $\int_E D=4\pi$ on each fiber, integration along the fibers gives
\[
 \int_X(a\alpha+b\beta)\wedge\omega^2=8\pi(a+b).
\]
This proves the assertion about the slice. By \cref{prop:lower-mass-no-pluriclosed}, $X$ carries no closed K\"ahler current, so none of these classes is big. Finally, the one-form $\dc t$ is globally defined on $X$ and satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{d}(\dc t)=\beta-\alpha.
\]
The class of $\sigma_2-\sigma_1$ on $B$ is nonzero, and injectivity makes its pullback nonzero in Bott--Chern cohomology. Thus de Rham exactness does not imply $\ddc$-exactness in this example.
\end{proof}

Consequently, every pseudoeffective class on this threefold is nef and has empty non-nef locus, whereas its non-K\"ahler locus is all of $X$, since no class is big. All its closed positive representatives come from the two-dimensional base; in particular $(a\alpha+b\beta)^3=0$.

\subsection{Potentials and envelopes from the base}

Descent also applies to potentials and preserves both ordinary singularity types and multiplier ideals, so the singularity envelopes in these backgrounds are computed on the base. The three-dimensional Monge--Amp\`ere measures of these potentials nevertheless vanish, because their currents have only two horizontal directions.

We use the ordinary singularity order $\preceq$ and equivalence $\sim$ of \cref{def:singularity-order}, the ideal order $\preceq_{\mathcal I}$ and equivalence $\sim_{\mathcal I}$ of \cref{sec:psh-singularity-types}, and the ordinary and multiplier-ideal envelopes $P_\theta[u]$ and $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ of \cref{def:singularity-envelope,def:hermitian-i-envelope}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-potential-descent}
Let $a,b\geq0$, put $\theta_B=a\sigma_1+b\sigma_2$ and $\theta=\pi^*\theta_B$. Pullback is a bijection
\[
 \PSH(B,\theta_B)\longrightarrow\PSH(X,\theta),\qquad v\longmapsto v\circ\pi.
\]
It preserves the ordinary singularity order and equivalence, as well as the ideal order and equivalence. For every $v\in\PSH(B,\theta_B)$,
\[
 P_\theta[v\circ\pi]=P_{\theta_B}[v]\circ\pi,
 \qquad P_\theta[v\circ\pi]_{\mathcal I}=P_{\theta_B}[v]_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi.
\]
Nevertheless, every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfies
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)=0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The form $\theta_B$ is closed, and $\pi$ is a proper holomorphic submersion with connected fibers, so \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion} gives the asserted bijection. Since $\pi$ is surjective, an ordinary singularity inequality holds upstairs exactly when it holds downstairs, with the same constant.

By \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback}, multiplier ideals of potentials on $B$ pull back to those of the composed potentials on $X$, at every positive weight. Restriction to local holomorphic sections shows conversely that an inclusion of multiplier ideals on $X$ implies the corresponding inclusion on $B$. Hence the ideal orders and equivalences correspond as well.

The competitors defining both envelopes correspond under the bijection, including the bound by zero and the required ordinary or ideal condition, and their pointwise suprema commute with pullback. Upper semicontinuous regularization also commutes with pullback: in a product chart, the supremum of a pulled-back function over a sufficiently small product neighborhood equals its supremum over the corresponding base neighborhood. This proves the two envelope identities.

Finally, write $u=v\circ\pi$ and, for positive integers $j$, set $u_j=\max\{u,-j\}$, which descends to $v_j=\max\{v,-j\}$. Since $\theta_B$ is semipositive, these are bounded admissible potentials. By \cref{lem:bounded-products-submersion}, $(\theta+\ddc u_j)^3=0$ because the base has dimension two. The definition of the extended non-pluripolar measure by increasing truncations now proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Thus the ordinary and ideal envelopes coincide in these backgrounds exactly when they coincide on the base, while every three-dimensional Monge--Amp\`ere measure vanishes. Criteria from the closed theory that require positive volume do not apply to these classes, which are not big. The Hermitian background $\omega$ behaves differently: its Monge--Amp\`ere equations with smooth positive densities are solvable, even though the infimum of its masses is zero.

\subsection{Sections selected by multiplier ideals}

Descent also identifies the spaces of holomorphic sections selected by multiplier ideals, together with their weighted $L^2$ norms. In particular, sections of pulled-back line bundles grow at most quadratically, as on the two-dimensional base. For integers $a,b$, write $\mathcal O_B(a,b)$ for the tensor product of the pullbacks of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(a)$ and $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(b)$ from the two factors of $B$.

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-selected-section-descent}
Let $a,b$ be nonnegative integers, let $L_B=\mathcal O_B(a,b)$ and $L=\pi^*L_B$. For every quasi-plurisubharmonic $v$ on $B$ and positive integer $m$, pullback gives an isomorphism
\[
 H^0\bigl(B,L_B^m\otimes\mathcal I(mv)\bigr)
   \longrightarrow
 H^0\bigl(X,L^m\otimes\mathcal I(mv\circ\pi)\bigr).
\]
For $v=0$ these spaces have dimension $(am+1)(bm+1)$.

Equip $L_B$ with a smooth Hermitian metric and $L$ with its pullback, choose a continuous real function $\varphi$ on $B$, let $\mathrm{d}V_X$ be a smooth positive volume form on $X$, and put $\nu=\pi_*\mathrm{d}V_X$. Use the norms
\[
 \|s\|_B^2=\int_B|s|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}\nu,
 \qquad
 \|\pi^*s\|_X^2=\int_X|\pi^*s|^2
                \mathrm{e}^{-m\varphi\circ\pi}\,\mathrm{d}V_X.
\]
Then the isomorphism is isometric. Let $K_{m,B}$ and $K_{m,X}$ be the corresponding partial Bergman kernels, that is, the sums of the weighted pointwise squared norms $|s|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\varphi}$ and $|\pi^*s|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\varphi\circ\pi}$ over orthonormal bases of the two spaces. Then
\[
 K_{m,X}=K_{m,B}\circ\pi,
 \qquad \pi_*(K_{m,X}\,\mathrm{d}V_X)=K_{m,B}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The identification of the section spaces is \cref{prop:partial-kernel-submersion}, applied to $L_B$ and $v$ on the base. For $v=0$, the usual basis of bihomogeneous monomials on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ has $(am+1)(bm+1)$ elements.

Integration along the fibers of the proper submersion shows that $\nu$ is a smooth positive volume form, and the defining identity $\int_Bg\,\mathrm d\nu=\int_X(g\circ\pi)\,\mathrm dV_X$ proves equality of the two norms. Pulling back an orthonormal basis and comparing pointwise norms gives the first kernel identity, and the same integration identity gives the second. The assertions include the case of a zero selected space, for which both kernels vanish.
\end{proof}

Even without a singular selecting weight, the growth is therefore at most quadratic:
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}m^{-2}h^0(X,L^m)=ab,
 \qquad \lim_{m\to\infty}m^{-3}h^0(X,L^m)=0.
\]
The next calculation shows that the variational volume of a small Hermitian perturbation detects this missing dimension of growth.
