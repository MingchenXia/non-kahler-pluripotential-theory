\section{Relative envelopes in a general big background}
\label{sec:big-relative-foundations}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$ and let $\theta$ be any smooth big real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Fix a smooth positive volume form for $L^1(X)$ and a Hermitian form for capacity. The standing assumptions of this chapter replace the semipositivity and smaller-background hypotheses in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures}. We assume bounded mass and fix a model reference $\varphi=P_\theta[\varphi]$ with
\[
 \ell:=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0,
 \qquad M:=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)<+\infty.
\]
Write $\mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$, using \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}. For $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ put $\theta_a=\theta+\ddc a$ and $\mu_a=\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a)$. For $b\geq1$ and potentials $a,w$, put $Q_b(a,w)=P_\theta(ba-(b-1)w)$ whenever genuine; differences are interpreted off poles. The relations $a\preceq w$ and $a\simeq w$ compare ordinary singularity types. Every member satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-relative-mass-interval}
 \ell\leq\mu_u(X)\leq M.
\end{equation}
These bounds concern the singularity class. Metric positive lower volume follows from these hypotheses, as explained after \cref{thm:li-general-measure}. The arguments below reconstruct the relative envelope and domination results of \cite[Sections 3--4]{XL26} in the precise regime just stated. The general-measure construction in \cref{sec:big-model-general-measures} will use these same hypotheses.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:big-relative-stretch}
If $u,w\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $u\leq w$, then $Q_b(u,w)$ is genuine and belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$ for every $b\geq1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is \cref{lem:relative-stretch-closure}, applied to the envelope-defined class. The identification with the volume-defined class is \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:big-relative-finite-rooftops}
For $u_1,\ldots,u_p\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, the envelope $P_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_p)$ is genuine and belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{prop:relative-finite-rooftops} to the envelope-defined class. Its convex-combination proof works for any smooth background and does not use either mass bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:big-relative-domination}
Let $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $v\preceq\varphi$, and $0\leq c<1$. If
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mu_u\leq c\,\mathbf1_{\{u<v\}}\mu_v,
\]
then $u\geq v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Replace $v$ by $\max(u,v)$. Locality preserves the hypothesis and upward stability puts the new $v$ in $\mathcal E_\varphi$. A common translation gives $u\leq v\leq\varphi\leq0$. Set
\[
 q_b=Q_b(u,v),\qquad
 D_b=\{b^{-1}q_b+(1-b^{-1})v=u\}.
\]
The preceding stretching lemma gives $q_b\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $q_b\leq u$. Contact support and positivity of the mixed products give
\[
 b^{-n}\mu_{q_b}+(1-b^{-1})^n\mathbf1_{D_b}\mu_v
 \leq\mathbf1_{D_b}\mu_u.
\]
Here $\mu_{q_b}$ is supported on $D_b$. Choose $b$ so large that $(1-b^{-1})^n>c$. Subtracting finite measures on $D_b\cap\{u<v\}$ shows that $\mu_{q_b}$ vanishes there. Thus it is supported on $D_b\cap\{u=v\}\subset\{q_b=u\}$. Since $q_b\leq u$, ordered contact with coefficient one now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-domination-fixed-tail}
 \ell\leq\mu_{q_b}(X)\leq\mu_u(\{q_b=u\}).
\end{equation}

If $u<v$ anywhere, there is $\delta>0$ such that $\{u<v-\delta\}$ has positive smooth volume. Indeed, the contrary would give $u\geq v$ almost everywhere and then everywhere for canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representatives. On this set
$q_b\leq u-(b-1)\delta$. The family $q_b$ decreases in $b$ and is bounded above by $u$. A genuine limit could not be $-\infty$ on a set of positive smooth volume. Hence its limit is identically $-\infty$, and Hartogs' lemma gives $\sup_Xq_b\to-\infty$. The sets $\{q_b=u\}$ then have $\mu_u$-mass tending to zero by dominated convergence off the pole set of $u$. This contradicts \eqref{eq:big-domination-fixed-tail}.
\end{proof}

The exponential order and uniqueness of the positive scalar follow exactly by applying this theorem to $v-\delta$ and then letting $\delta\downarrow0$, or by applying it to all constant translates of $v$, respectively. Thus the third regime in \cref{thm:relative-domination} now has an internal proof. Potential uniqueness for the equation without an exponential does not follow.

\section{Capacity and measures in a general big background}
\label{sec:big-relative-continuity}

Retain the connected compact complex $n$-fold $X$, big smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$, model reference $\varphi$, bounded mass hypothesis and positive lower relative volume of \cref{sec:big-relative-foundations}. Use its notation $\mathcal E_\varphi$, $\mu_a$, $\ell$, $M$, $Q_b$ and $\theta_a$, and its fixed smooth-volume and capacity conventions. The weighted lower-semicontinuity and monotone capacity arguments proved in \cref{lem:weighted-extended-lsc} apply to this background. They use bounded products after enlarging the background to a Hermitian metric, so no positivity of the uncut constant-truncation product is required.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:big-relative-fixed-continuity}
Suppose $u_j\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, $u_j\to u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\mu_{u_j}\leq\nu$ for one finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$. There is a subsequence with
\[
 h=P_\theta\left(\inf_j u_j\right)\in\mathcal E_\varphi.
\]
For the original sequence,
\[
 u\in\mathcal E_\varphi,\qquad
 u_j\longrightarrow u\text{ in capacity},\qquad
 \mu_{u_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_u\leq\nu.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We apply the arguments of \cref{lem:dominated-relative-rooftop-extraction,thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity}, checking their ingredients in the present background. The envelope identity and Hartogs' lemma give $u_j\leq\varphi+C$. Finite rooftops and stretching preserve $\mathcal E_\varphi$ by \cref{prop:big-relative-finite-rooftops,lem:big-relative-stretch}; their masses are at least $\ell$. These replace the corresponding semipositive-background facts.

After a common translation, assume $u_j,u\leq\varphi\leq0$. Using \cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals}, extract once so that smooth-volume almost-everywhere convergence holds and
\[
 I_j:=\int_X|\mathrm{e}^{u_j}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mu_{u_j}\leq2^{-j}.
\]
For $p_{j,N}=P_\theta(u_j,\ldots,u_N)$, disjoint first-contact pieces give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-finite-roof-domination}
 \mu_{p_{j,N}}\leq\nu,\qquad
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mu_{p_{j,N}}\leq2^{1-j}.
\end{equation}
In the extraction lemma's proof, replace $\kappa$ by $\ell$. For each fixed $B\geq1$, the stretched roofs $r_{j,N}=Q_B(p_{j,N},\varphi)$ satisfy
\[
 \ell\leq\mu_{r_{j,N}}(X),\qquad
 \mu_{r_{j,N}}\leq B^n\nu.
\]
Its exponential test therefore bounds $\sup_Xr_{j,N}$ below, independently of $N$, once $j$ is sufficiently large. The same prefix-and-tail construction with $B=2b$ uses only convex combinations and genuine finite rooftops. It supplies every defining $b$-competitor for
\[
 h=P_\theta(\inf_j u_j)\in\mathcal E_\varphi.
\]
Since $h\leq u\leq\varphi$, upward stability gives $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$. No semipositivity has entered this extraction argument.

For continuity, put $p_j=P_\theta(\inf_{k\geq j}u_k)$ and $p=(\lim_jp_j)^*$. Both $p_{j,N}\downarrow p_j$ and $p_j\uparrow p$ have the genuine lower bound $h$, so converge in capacity. All the limits lie in $\mathcal E_\varphi$ and $p\leq u$. The general-background weighted lower-semicontinuity lemma, applied twice to \eqref{eq:big-finite-roof-domination}, gives
\[
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^p-\mathrm{e}^u|\,\mu_p=0.
\]
Now use \cref{thm:big-relative-domination} with coefficient zero to get $p=u$. The upper tail envelopes decrease to $u$ by Hartogs' lemma. Squeezing between them and $p_j$ proves capacity convergence.

Finally, the fixed finite dominator gives
\[
 \mu_{u_j}(\{u_j\leq-T\})\leq\nu(\{h\leq-T\})\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly in $j$. The general-background undamped tail theorem proves weak convergence to $\mu_u\leq\nu$. Starting with an arbitrary subsequence gives a further subsequence with both convergences and the same limit. Hence the conclusions hold for the original sequence.
\end{proof}

Finite rooftops and stretching are properties of the envelope-defined class in an arbitrary smooth background. Here the mass hypotheses identify that class with the volume-defined class and give the interval \eqref{eq:big-relative-mass-interval}. The positive lower bound prevents collapse of the stretched rooftops; the fixed measure controls their masses and, after construction of a common lower envelope, the tails. The finite-rooftop construction itself uses neither bound. This proves the fixed-measure continuity statement of \cite[Theorem 4.2]{XL26}; the corresponding construction of general-measure solutions is given in \cref{sec:big-model-general-measures}.
