\section{Analytic types and restriction}\label{sec:analytic-types}\index{Lelong number}\index{Analytic singularity}

Analytic singularities express the poles of a potential through finitely many holomorphic functions. Their logarithmic growth is measured by the Lelong number. For a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ and a point $x\in X$, choose coordinates centered at $x$ and a smooth correction $q$ such that $p=u+q$ is plurisubharmonic. With $B(x,r)$ the coordinate Euclidean ball, set
\[
 \nu(u,x)=\lim_{r\downarrow0}
       \frac{\sup_{B(x,r)}p}{\log r^2}.
\]
The limit exists and is independent of the coordinates and the smooth correction, by the local growth theorem recalled in \cite{SGPT}. Thus $\log|z|^2$ has Lelong number one at the origin.

We follow the convention of \cite{SGPT}: a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ has analytic singularities if locally there are holomorphic functions $f_1,\ldots,f_N$, not all identically zero, and a rational $c>0$ such that
\[
 u=c\log\bigl(|f_1|^2+\cdots+|f_N|^2\bigr)+O(1),
 \qquad c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\]
The remainder is bounded; smoothness of this remainder is an additional property. A coherent ideal $\mathfrak a$ and a common coefficient $c$ specify the \emph{analytic type} $(c,\mathfrak a)$. Changing local generators changes the logarithm by a bounded amount on smaller relatively compact open sets.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:analytic-type-calculus}
Analytic singularities are preserved by addition, finite maxima, and multiplication by a positive rational number. If $Y\subset X$ is a connected complex submanifold and the ideal of an analytic presentation does not vanish identically on $Y$, the restriction is genuine and has analytic type
\[
 (c,\mathfrak a\mathcal O_Y).
\]
Here $\mathfrak a\mathcal O_Y$ means the image ideal generated by the restrictions of local sections of $\mathfrak a$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a rational scaling, multiply the coefficient. For two presentations with coefficients $c_1,c_2$ and ideals $\mathfrak a_1,\mathfrak a_2$, choose a common positive rational coefficient $d$ with $c_1=k d,c_2=\ell d$, where $k,\ell$ are positive integers. Products of generators present the sum, since a product of sums of squares is a sum of squares of pairwise products.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
For the maximum use
\[
 \max\{A,B\}\leq A+B\leq2\max\{A,B\},\qquad A,B\geq0,
\]
applied to the appropriate sums of squares of generators of $\mathfrak a_1^k$ and $\mathfrak a_2^\ell$. Taking logarithms gives the required bounded error. These are local assertions; finite covers give the corresponding ordinary singularity comparisons on a compact manifold.

For restriction, restrict the displayed inequalities defining the bounded remainder. At least one generator remains nonzero on the connected manifold $Y$, so its common zero set is proper. The restricted function is quasi-plurisubharmonic, is not identically $-\infty$, and has the stated presentation. A smooth local correction gives the same conclusion directly from the usual restriction theorem for psh functions.
\end{proof}

Zero generic Lelong number does not make the restriction of an arbitrary quasi-plurisubharmonic function genuine. For example, on a small polydisc in $\mathbb C^n$ with coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$ and $|z_1|<\mathrm{e}^{-1}$,
\[
 u(z)=-\sqrt{-\log|z_1|^2}
\]
is plurisubharmonic: the function $t\mapsto-\sqrt{-t}$ is increasing and convex for $t<0$. Along $\{z_1=0\}$, its Lelong number is zero but its restriction is identically $-\infty$. The analytic hypothesis in \cref{prop:analytic-type-calculus} will exclude this behavior for the approximants below.

\subsection{Lelong level sets}
\label{subsec:psh-lelong-levels}\index{Siu analyticity theorem}

Siu's analyticity theorem says that, for every $c>0$, the set $\{x:\nu(u,x)\geq c\}$ is analytic. A smooth local correction reduces the quasi-plurisubharmonic statement to the theorem for closed positive currents. We use this classical input in the form recalled in \cite{SGPT}; its original source is recorded in \cref{sec:history-psh}.

Closedness also bounds trace mass on small balls. The resulting Hausdorff estimate excludes a closed positive current carried by a set smaller than its expected dimension.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null}\index{Closed positive current!Hausdorff support}
Let $T$ be a closed positive $(j,j)$-current on a complex manifold of dimension $n$, with $1\leq j<n$. Its coefficient measures vanish on every Borel set of zero Hausdorff $2(n-j)$-measure.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In a coordinate chart put $\kappa=\ddc|z|^2$ and $\mu=T\wedge\kappa^{n-j}$. The Lelong mass formula, in bidimension $(n-j,n-j)$, makes $r^{-2(n-j)}\mu(B(x,r))$ nondecreasing in $r$; see \cite[Chapter III, formulas (5.5)--(5.8), pp.~158--159]{DemCADG}. Fix an inner compact chart and a radius $r_0$ such that all its radius-$r_0$ balls stay in a larger compact chart. Their masses are bounded by the mass on the larger chart. Hence
\[
 \mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{2(n-j)},\qquad 0<r\leq r_0,
\]
uniformly for centers in the inner chart. If a Borel set there has zero Hausdorff $2(n-j)$-measure, ball covers with arbitrarily small sum of these powers give it zero $\mu$-mass. Positivity dominates the total variation of each coefficient measure by a constant times this trace measure. A countable chart cover proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{The local Bergman estimates}

Fix a bounded pseudoconvex domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ and a plurisubharmonic function $\psi$ on $\Omega$. Write $\mathrm{d}\lambda$ for Euclidean volume and $B(x,r)$ for the Euclidean ball of radius $r>0$ about $x$. For a positive integer $m$, define the weighted holomorphic Hilbert space
\[
 \mathcal H_m=\left\{f\in\mathcal O(\Omega):
          \|f\|_m^2:=\int_\Omega|f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\psi}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda<\infty\right\}.
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Choose an orthonormal basis $(g_{m,j})_j$ of $\mathcal H_m$ and define $\beta_m=m^{-1}\log\sum_j|g_{m,j}|^2$. The sum is equivalently the squared norm of point evaluation:
\[
 \sum_j|g_{m,j}(x)|^2
   =\sup_{\|f\|_m\leq1}|f(x)|^2.
\]
It is independent of the basis. The standard local finite-generation part of Bergman regularization says that $\beta_m$ has analytic type
$(1/m,\mathcal I(m\psi))$ on smaller open sets. This follows from the weighted holomorphic generation of the multiplier ideal and local $L^2$ division; see \cite[Proposition 5.7 and Section 14.A]{DemAM11}. We use that local analytic statement as a standard input, together with the point-extension estimate used in the proof below.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bergman-local-estimates}
There are constants $C_1,C_2$ independent of $m$ such that, for $B(x,r)\Subset\Omega$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bergman-local-estimates}
 \psi(x)-\frac{C_1}{m}
 \leq\beta_m(x)
 \leq\sup_{B(x,r)}\psi+\frac1m\log\frac{C_2}{r^{2n}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\beta_m\to\psi$ pointwise and in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bergman-lelong-bounds}
 \max\{\nu(\psi,x)-n/m,0\}
 \leq\nu(\beta_m,x)\leq\nu(\psi,x).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\|f\|_m\leq1$, the submean inequality gives
\[
 |f(x)|^2
 \leq C_2r^{-2n}\int_{B(x,r)}|f|^2\,\mathrm{d}\lambda
 \leq C_2r^{-2n}\mathrm{e}^{m\sup_{B(x,r)}\psi}.
\]
Taking the supremum over the unit ball and then the logarithm proves the upper bound.

The local Ohsawa--Takegoshi point-extension theorem gives a holomorphic $f$ with $f(x)=1$ and
\[
 \int_\Omega|f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\psi}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda
 \leq C \mathrm{e}^{-m\psi(x)}
\]
when $\psi(x)>-\infty$. The constant is independent of the psh weight and of $m$. Normalize $f$ in $\mathcal H_m$. Point evaluation is then at least $C^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{m\psi(x)}$, proving the lower bound. At a pole the lower bound has no content and remains valid.

Choosing $r=1/m$ proves pointwise convergence by upper semicontinuity of $\psi$. On a fixed relatively compact set, the same estimates bound $\beta_m$ above by a constant and below by $\psi-C_1/m$. Since $\psi$ is locally integrable, dominated convergence also gives convergence in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

For the Lelong estimate, take suprema over $B(x,r)$ in the two inequalities and bound the upper one using $B(x,2r)$. Divide by $\log r^2<0$ and let $r\downarrow0$. The term $-(2n/m)\log r$ contributes $-n/m$ after the division. The remaining constant terms disappear. This proves the two inequalities in \eqref{eq:bergman-lelong-bounds}; nonnegativity supplies the maximum with zero.
\end{proof}

In \cite{DemAM11}, the local weight is $\mathrm{e}^{-2m\phi}$ and Lelong numbers are normalized using $\log|z|$. Putting $\psi=2\phi$ gives exactly the formulas above. In particular the error is $n/m$ with the present weight $\mathrm{e}^{-m\psi}$ and normalization by $\log|z|^2$.

The same local construction and estimates hold for every real parameter $m>0$. Its local logarithmic presentation has coefficient $1/m$; this coefficient is rational exactly when $m$ is rational. We use the term analytic type only with the rational-coefficient convention above. The global sequence below is indexed by integers.

The generators also compare multiplier ideals at nearby weights. The loss in the weight compensates for the finite Bergman index.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bergman-controlled-ideals}\index{Multiplier ideal!controlled approximation}
Let $u$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic, let $m>0$, and let $a_m=m^{-1}\log\sum_k|g_k|^2+O(1)$ locally, where the $g_k$ generate $\mathcal I(mu)$. For $t>0$ and $s>1$,
\[
 \mathcal I(sta_m)\subseteq\mathcal I(tu)
 \qquad\text{whenever}\qquad m\geq\frac{st}{s-1}.
\]
Also $u\preceq a_m$ locally, and hence $\mathcal I(tu)\subseteq\mathcal I(ta_m)$ for every $t>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a sufficiently small coordinate neighborhood, choose generators $g_1,\ldots,g_N$ of $\mathcal I(mu)$ and put $B=(\sum_k|g_k|^2)\mathrm{e}^{-mu}$. Shrink so that $B$ is integrable and $a_m=m^{-1}\log\sum_k|g_k|^2+O(1)$ with a bounded remainder. If $f\in\mathcal I(sta_m)$, shrink once more so that $f$ is bounded and $A=|f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-sta_m}$ is integrable. Almost everywhere,
\[
 |f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-tu}
 \leq C|f|^{2-2/s}A^{1/s}B^{t/m}.
\]
H\"older's inequality with exponents $s$ and $s/(s-1)$ applies because $st/(m(s-1))\leq1$. It proves local integrability of the left side, including at equality in the index bound. The local Bergman lower estimate in \cref{prop:bergman-local-estimates} gives $u\preceq a_m$; bounded smooth corrections do not affect this comparison or any of the ideals.
\end{proof}

\section{The global curvature bound}

Return to the compact manifold $X$ and its fixed Hermitian form $\omega$. The global regularization theorem glues the local Bergman constructions while controlling the curvature error. We retain it as a classical input in the following precise form.

\begin{theorem}[Demailly regularization]\label{thm:demailly-hermitian-input}\index{Regularization!analytic singularities}
Let $u$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on $X$ and let $\gamma$ be a continuous real $(1,1)$-form with $\ddc u\geq\gamma$. There are quasi-plurisubharmonic $u_m$, for sufficiently large positive integers $m$, and numbers $\epsilon_m\downarrow0$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $u_m$ has analytic type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$;
\item $u_m\geq u$ and $u_m\to u$ pointwise and in $L^1(X)$;
\item $\ddc u_m\geq\gamma-\epsilon_m\omega$;
\item $\max\{\nu(u,x)-n/m,0\}\leq\nu(u_m,x)\leq\nu(u,x)$ for all $x$.
\end{enumerate}
No monotonicity of the values $u_m$ is asserted here.
\end{theorem}

This is the form of \cite[Theorem 14.12]{DemAM11} used in this book. Its local generators are the weighted Bergman generators of $\mathcal I(mu)$; smooth local corrections do not change that ideal. The overlapping sums have comparable norms, so the glued logarithm retains this ordinary analytic type. The global construction freezes the continuous lower bound on sufficiently small coordinate balls. Its curvature error combines the modulus of continuity of that bound with an overlap term tending to zero as the Bergman index increases. It is this global estimate, rather than merely the local convergence in \cref{prop:bergman-local-estimates}, which is the standard input in the theorem.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonclosed-analytic-approximation}
For any smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ on $X$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the approximants can be chosen so that
\[
 \theta+\ddc u_m\geq-\epsilon_m\omega.
\]
Their analytic type is $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the theorem to the closed, almost positive current $\ddc u$ with lower bound $\gamma=-\theta$. The theorem requires no closedness of $\gamma$. Adding $\theta$ gives the asserted inequality. In the convention of \cite{DemAM11}, this application is to the potential $u/2$, since $\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\partial\bar\partial(u/2)=\ddc u$.
\end{proof}

These approximants retain the curvature lower bound up to a vanishing error. Their multiplier ideals will later identify the limit of their ordinary envelopes in \cref{thm:dyadic-analytic-envelope-limit}.


\subsection{Regularization of closed currents}
\label{subsec:psh-closed-regularization}

We also use the closed-current form of regularization recalled in \cite{SGPT}. Let $T=\eta+\ddc v$ be an almost positive closed $(1,1)$-current, where $\eta$ is smooth and closed and $v$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic. Its Lelong number is $\nu(T,x)=\nu(v,x)$. If $T\geq\gamma$ for a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\gamma$, there are smooth representatives $T_j=\eta+\ddc v_j$ with
\[
 T_j\geq\gamma-C\lambda_j\omega,
\]
where $C>0$ is fixed and the continuous functions $\lambda_j$ decrease pointwise to $\nu(T,\cdot)$. When all Lelong numbers vanish, Dini's theorem makes this loss uniformly small. Analytic regularization gives representatives $S_j$ with
\[
 S_j\geq\gamma-\varepsilon_j\omega,\qquad
 \varepsilon_j\downarrow0,\qquad
 \nu(S_j,x)\leq\nu(T,x).
\]
Thus it preserves strict positivity after a small loss and introduces no positive Lelong number at a point where the original number is zero.

\subsection{Smooth remainders after resolution}

For arguments on a modification, the bounded remainder in an analytic presentation is sometimes insufficient. The following version of regularization supplies a smooth remainder after resolution.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Demailly regularization]\label{thm:resolvable-regularization-input}
Let $\alpha$ be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form and let $u\in\PSH(X,\alpha)$. There are $\delta_j\downarrow0$ and $\widetilde u_j\in\PSH(X,\alpha+\delta_j\omega)$ decreasing to $u$ such that each $\widetilde u_j$ is smooth off an analytic pole set and, after a modification $\pi_j:Y_j\to X$ given by a finite sequence of blowups with smooth centers, has local expressions as follows. Let $s_\ell$ be local holomorphic defining functions of divisors, let $a_\ell>0$ be real numbers, and let $b$ be a smooth real function. Then
\[
 \pi_j^*\widetilde u_j=\sum_\ell a_\ell\log|s_\ell|^2+b,
 \qquad a_\ell>0,\quad b\in C^\infty.
\]
The divisors may be taken to have simple normal crossings.
\end{theorem}

This classical input applies to the closed current $\ddc u$ with smooth lower bound $-\alpha$; its source is recorded in \cref{sec:history-psh}. Its lower bound is not required to be closed, and its ambient manifold is only Hermitian. The resolved expression supplies the smooth remainder after resolution; ordinary analytic type with merely a bounded remainder would not suffice here. This version need not be the particular multiplier-ideal sequence in \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input}. We do not assert that the additional conclusions of these two regularization statements hold for the same sequence. Applying this theorem with a sufficiently small curvature error preserves strict positivity.
