\subsection{A continuum of radial solutions}
\label{sec:numa-radial-family}

The open band where the density vanishes allows more solutions than the two smooth ones constructed above. Writing a further potential as $G(r)-\rho$, with $G$ a function of one real variable, reduces the equation on the band to choosing the slope $G'$ so that one of the three coefficients vanishes. Switching the vanishing coefficient at two suitable levels produces a continuum of $C^{1,1}$ solutions for the same background $\omega$ and density $f$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Recall that $q=p'$ and $z_0\geq|q|$. The strict inequality $z_0>|p'|$ near zero, where $\chi>0$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-half-excess}
 \int_0^1z_0(r)\,\mathrm{d}r>\int_0^1|p'(r)|\,\mathrm{d}r=p(0).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:numa-radial-continuum}\index{Uniqueness!radial families}
For the smooth background $\omega$ and the smooth density $f$ of \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness}, the equation
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\omega(u)=f\omega_X^3.
\]
has uncountably many distinct $C^{1,1}$ solutions normalized by $\sup_Xu=0$. If the cutoff $\kappa$ defining $\rho$ is chosen even, one of them, constructed explicitly, is invariant under the block exchange $\iota[A:B]=[B:A]$.
\end{proposition}
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Choose $-1<I<0<J<1$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-transition-balance}
 \int_I^J z_0(r)\,\mathrm{d}r=p(I)+p(J).
\end{equation}
We first show that such pairs exist, with $I$ ranging over an interval. Define
\[
 K(I,J)=\int_I^Jz_0(r)\,\mathrm{d}r-p(I)-p(J).
\]
We have $K(0,0)=-2p(0)<0$ and $K(0,1)>0$ by \eqref{eq:numa-half-excess}. By continuity, there is $\epsilon>0$ such that $K(I,0)<0<K(I,1)$ for every $I\in[-\epsilon,0]$. For $0<J<1$,
\[
 \partial_JK(I,J)=z_0(J)-p'(J)>0.
\]
The intermediate value theorem therefore gives a unique $J=J(I)\in(0,1)$ satisfying \eqref{eq:numa-transition-balance} for every $I\in[-\epsilon,0)$; we may assume $\epsilon<1$.

For any pair satisfying \eqref{eq:numa-transition-balance}, define the slope on $[-1,1]$ by
\[
 h_{I,J}(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 p(r),&-1\leq r\leq I,\\
 p(I)-\displaystyle\int_I^r z_0(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,&I\leq r\leq J,\\
 -p(r),&J\leq r\leq1,
 \end{cases}
\]
and extend it by zero outside that interval. The balance identity makes it continuous at $J$, and continuity at $I$ is immediate. Each piece has bounded derivative, so $h_{I,J}$ is Lipschitz on $\mathbb R$; it vanishes to infinite order at $\pm1$. Let $G_{I,J}$ be a primitive of $h_{I,J}$. It is $C^{1,1}$ and constant near each end of the real line. Consequently $G_{I,J}(r)$ extends to a $C^{1,1}$ function on $X$ that is smooth near $X\setminus U$.

On the middle interval $[I,J]$ the slope lies between $-p$ and $p$. Indeed,
\[
 (h_{I,J}-p)'=-z_0-p'\leq0,
 \qquad
 (h_{I,J}+p)'=-z_0+p'\leq0.
\]
The first difference vanishes at $I$, and the second vanishes at $J$. Since both are nonincreasing, $h_{I,J}\leq p$ and $h_{I,J}\geq-p$ on $[I,J]$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Put $\widetilde u_{I,J}=G_{I,J}(r)-\rho$. By \eqref{eq:numa-chain}, almost everywhere on $U$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numa-slope-form}
 \omega+\ddc\widetilde u_{I,J}
 =\omega_0+\ddc G_{I,J}(r)
 =(x+h_{I,J})P+(y-h_{I,J})Q+(z+h_{I,J}')R.
\end{equation}
On $I<r<J$, the first two coefficients are nonnegative because $x=p+a$, $y=p+b$ and $-p\leq h_{I,J}\leq p$, and the third is zero. On $-1<r<I$, we have $h_{I,J}=p$ and $b=0$, since $I<0$; so the second coefficient is zero, and the others are nonnegative because $z_0+p'\geq0$. On $J<r<1$, we have $h_{I,J}=-p$ and $a=0$; so the first coefficient is zero, and the others are nonnegative because $z_0-p'\geq0$. Outside the band, $h_{I,J}=h_{I,J}'=0$, so the form equals $\omega_0$.

Formula \eqref{eq:numa-slope-form} has no additional term supported on the hypersurfaces $r=I$ and $r=J$: the function $G_{I,J}$ is $C^{1,1}$, so its first derivative is continuous at both transition points, and its distributional Hessian is its bounded almost-everywhere Hessian. The preceding coefficient inequalities therefore prove $\omega+\ddc\widetilde u_{I,J}\geq0$ as a current.

By \cref{prop:c11-products}, its Monge--Amp\`ere measure is the pointwise cube of \eqref{eq:numa-slope-form}, computed almost everywhere. By the rank-one identities \eqref{eq:numa-rank-frame} and \eqref{eq:numa-cube}, this cube is zero almost everywhere on the band, since one coefficient vanishes there, and it equals $\omega_0^3$ off the band. These are exactly the values of $f\omega_X^3$ in the preceding construction. Thus $\widetilde u_{I,J}$ solves the equation, and subtracting its supremum gives a normalized solution $u_{I,J}$.

Different values of $I\in[-\epsilon,0)$ give different normalized solutions. If $I_1<I_2<0$ and $I_1<r<I_2$, then the slope belonging to $I_2$ is $p(r)$, whereas
\[
 p(r)-h_{I_1,J(I_1)}(r)
       =\int_{I_1}^r(p'(s)+z_0(s))\,\mathrm{d}s>0,
\]
because $p'>0$ on $(-1,0)$. The two primitives therefore do not differ by a constant. Since the interval $[-\epsilon,0)$ is uncountable, this proves the first assertion. None of these slopes equals $p$ or $-p$ on all of $(-1,1)$, so the constructed solutions are also distinct from the two smooth solutions of \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness}.

Finally, the function
\[
 L(t)=\int_0^t z_0(r)\,\mathrm{d}r-p(t),\qquad 0\leq t\leq1,
\]
is negative at zero, positive at one, and strictly increasing on $(0,1)$. Its unique zero $t_*$ gives a symmetric pair $I=-t_*$, $J=t_*$ satisfying \eqref{eq:numa-transition-balance}. Since $z_0$ and $p$ are even, $h_{I,J}$ is odd: on the middle interval it equals $-\int_0^r z_0(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$. Choose its primitive even. Since $\iota$ changes $r$ into $-r$, if $\rho$ is even, the potential $G_{I,J}(r)-\rho$ and its normalization are fixed by $\iota$. This proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For these symmetric data, the normalized solutions of the equations with a positive exponent converge, as the exponent tends to zero, to the symmetric solution just constructed; see \cref{thm:numa-selected-limit}.
