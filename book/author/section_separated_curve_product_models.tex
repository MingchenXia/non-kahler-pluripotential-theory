\subsection{Several curve factors}
\label{subsec:separated-curve-product-models}

A single curve factor produces a model space isometric to the space of models on one curve. Products of several curves give larger complete model spaces at zero volume. The envelopes of a sum of curve models are again computed coordinatewise: the supremum of a competitor over all factors but one is a potential on the remaining curve, and restriction to slices treats the other factors by induction. The sum of the polar distances of the factors is then a complete metric on the induced product models.

Let $C_1,\ldots,C_m$ be connected compact curves with smooth positive forms $\eta_i$, where $m\geq2$, and put $A_i=\int_{C_i}\eta_i$. Set
\[
 Y=\prod_{i=1}^m C_i,\qquad
 \beta=\sum_{i=1}^m\pi_i^*\eta_i,
\]
where $\pi_i:Y\to C_i$ are the projections. For normalized ordinary models $q_i$ on $(C_i,\eta_i)$, write
\[
 w=\sum_{i=1}^m q_i\circ\pi_i,\qquad
 q_{i,\mathcal I}=P_{\eta_i}[q_i]_{\mathcal I}.
\]

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:separated-curve-model-projections}\index{Envelope!separated curve models}
The function $w$ is a normalized ordinary model on $(Y,\beta)$, and
\[
 P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}
       =\sum_{i=1}^m q_{i,\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The function $w$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic and has supremum zero. We prove $P_\beta[w]=w$ by induction on the number of factors, the case of one factor being the identity $P_{\eta_1}[q_1]=q_1$. Let $v\leq0$ be an ordinary competitor, and replace it by $\max(v,w)$. This preserves $v\preceq w$ and ensures that the restrictions of $v$ to slices are not identically $-\infty$ wherever the corresponding curve potentials are finite.

Write $Y=Y'\times C_m$, and take the regularized partial supremum
\[
 a(t)=\left(\sup_{y'\in Y'}v(y',t)\right)^*.
\]
The restrictions of $v$ to the slices $\{y'\}\times C_m$ are $\eta_m$-plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty$. They are bounded above by zero, and at least one of them is not identically $-\infty$, since $v\geq w$. The local upper-envelope theorem from \cref{chap:framework} therefore makes $a$ an $\eta_m$-plurisubharmonic function. The global inequality $v\leq w+C$ gives $a\leq q_m+C$, because the other normalized potentials have supremum zero. Thus $a$ is a competitor for $P_{\eta_m}[q_m]$, and $a\leq P_{\eta_m}[q_m]=q_m$.

For $t$ with $q_m(t)$ finite, the function $v(\cdot,t)-q_m(t)$ is a potential in the product background on $Y'$, not identically $-\infty$; it is nonpositive and at least as singular as $\sum_{i<m}q_i\circ\pi_i$. The induction hypothesis bounds it by that sum. Hence $v\leq w$ off the poles of $q_m$. At those poles the original bound $v\leq w+C$ gives the same inequality. Taking the envelope proves the ordinary identity.

For the ideal identity, let $E_i$ be the atomic part of the polar curvature of $q_i$, and take the Green potentials $g_i$ of \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}. They satisfy
\[
 q_i\sim_{\mathcal I}g_i,\qquad
 q_{i,\mathcal I}\sim g_i,\qquad
 \ddc g_i=E_i-\frac{E_i(C_i)}{A_i}\eta_i.
\]
By \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback,lem:hermitian-i-calculus}, $g=\sum_i g_i\circ\pi_i$ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $w$. Its complex Hessian is a closed countable sum of the divisors $\pi_i^{-1}(x)$ minus the smooth form $\sum_i E_i(C_i)\pi_i^*\eta_i/A_i$. Each coordinate fiber has constant mass for the product metric, so the divisor part has finite mass. Moreover $g$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic since $E_i(C_i)\leq A_i$. Therefore \cref{cor:divisorial-envelope-equality} gives $P_\beta[g]_{\mathcal I}=P_\beta[g]$. Since $g$ is ordinarily equivalent to $\sum_iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i$, the ordinary identity, applied to the models $q_{i,\mathcal I}$, shows that this envelope is $\sum_iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i$. As $w\sim_{\mathcal I}g$, the ideal envelope of $w$ is the same, which proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

We now return to the positive product background $\beta$ and the normalized curve models $q_i$, and add a divisorial factor as in \cref{subsec:zero-volume-product-obstacles}. Let $Z$ be a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $k\geq1$, and let $D\subset Z$ be a nonempty smooth reduced divisor, with a K\"ahler form $\kappa$ and a normalized potential $\psi$ satisfying $\kappa+\ddc\psi=[D]$. On $M=Y\times Z$, write $p,r$ for the projections, set $h_i=\pi_i\circ p$, and put
\[
 \Theta=p^*\beta+r^*\kappa,\qquad
 \Psi=\psi\circ r,\qquad
 \Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)=\Psi+\sum_i q_i\circ h_i.
\]
Let $\mathcal S_\Psi^{(m)}$ be the set of these potentials. For the polar curvatures $\mu_i=\mathbf1_{\{q_i=-\infty\}}(\eta_i+\ddc q_i)$ and the analogous measures $\nu_i$ of models $b_i$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-product-polar-metric}
 \rho_\Psi^{(m)}(\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m),\Phi(b_1,\ldots,b_m))
       =\sum_i\|\mu_i-\nu_i\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation}

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:separated-product-polar-metric}\index{Metric completeness!several curve factors}\index{Model potential!separated product metric}
The set $\mathcal S_\Psi^{(m)}$ consists of normalized ordinary models and is a complete geodesic metric space for $\rho_\Psi^{(m)}$. Its ideal models form a closed geodesically convex subspace, equal to the metric closure of its models with analytic singularities. Ideal projection is the unique nearest-point retraction onto this subspace and is $1$-Lipschitz, and the distance to the subspace is the sum of the nonatomic polar masses. Metric convergence implies $L^1(M)$ convergence for every smooth positive volume form. The upper-volume comparison $d_\Theta$ vanishes on every pair of points of this locus.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The preceding lemma and \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} give
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_\Theta[\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)]=\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m),\\
 P_\Theta[\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)]_{\mathcal I}
       =\Phi(q_{1,\mathcal I},\ldots,q_{m,\mathcal I}),
 \end{gathered}
\]
since the induced zero obstacle is zero; independence of the factors gives normalization. Restricting to sections on which the other potentials are finite shows that if two product potentials are equal, then each coordinate difference is constant, and normalization makes every such constant zero. Thus $\Phi$ identifies this locus with the finite product of the spaces of curve models, equipped with the sum of their metrics.

Completeness follows coordinate by coordinate from \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}. Interpolating all polar measures linearly with the same parameter gives a constant-speed geodesic for the sum metric. Ideal projection takes the atomic part in each coordinate, so the assertions on closedness, convexity, unique nearest points and the Lipschitz bound follow coordinate by coordinate from \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness,prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect}, and the distances add. Finite atomic approximations with rational coefficients in each coordinate have local logarithmic coefficients whose denominators, together with the unit coefficient of $D$, can be cleared in a product monomial. They give analytic models that are dense in the ideal subspace. Conversely, every ordinary model with analytic singularities is its own ideal projection by \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, so the closure of these models is exactly the ideal subspace.

By \cref{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence}, metric convergence gives $L^1$ convergence on each curve. Off the pole sets, the difference of two product potentials is the sum of the pulled-back coordinate differences. Integrating each term along the compact fibers of $h_i$ bounds its $L^1$ norm on $M$ by a constant times its $L^1$ norm on the curve, as in \cref{prop:divisorial-product-polar-metric}, and the finite sum tends to zero.

Finally, since $\dim Y=m<\dim M$, \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} shows that every model in this locus has zero upper singularity volume. A pairwise maximum is still $\Psi$ plus the pullback of a $\beta$-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$, so the same proposition gives zero upper volume for it. All three terms of $d_\Theta$ therefore vanish.
\end{proof}

As in \cref{cor:nonclosed-product-polar-metric,cor:degenerate-product-polar-metric}, an auxiliary curve factor with a variable coefficient makes the background nonclosed, and lets it degenerate, without changing this metric locus.

Keep $Y,\beta,\pi_i$ and $Z,\kappa,D,\psi$ from above. Let $C_0$ be another connected compact curve with a smooth positive form $\tau$. On $M=Y\times C_0\times Z$, write $p,s,r$ for the projections. For a smooth real function $f\geq0$ on $Y$, put
\[
 \Theta_f=p^*\beta+(f\circ p)s^*\tau+r^*\kappa,
 \qquad \Psi=\psi\circ r,
\]
and set $\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)=\Psi+\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i\circ p$.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonclosed-separated-product-metric}\index{Metric completeness!nonclosed separated product}\index{Model potential!nonbig separated locus}
For every tuple of normalized ordinary curve models, one has
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_{\Theta_f}[\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)]=\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m),\\
 P_{\Theta_f}[\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)]_{\mathcal I}
       =\Phi(q_{1,\mathcal I},\ldots,q_{m,\mathcal I}).
 \end{gathered}
\]
On the locus of these potentials, use the sum of polar distances \eqref{eq:separated-product-polar-metric}. All the metric conclusions of \cref{prop:separated-product-polar-metric} hold, and the diameter is $2\sum_i A_i$. The locus, its metric, ideal projection and the analytic closure are independent of $f$, including at $f=0$. The background is positive and not closed when $f$ is positive and nonconstant. The background $\Theta_0$ obtained for $f=0$ is not big.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On $B=Y\times C_0$, let $\pi,s_0$ be the projections and put $\beta_f=\pi^*\beta+(f\circ\pi)s_0^*\tau$. For $w=\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i$, an ordinary competitor $v\leq0$ for $w\circ\pi$ may be replaced by its maximum with $w\circ\pi$. Its restriction to every section $Y\times\{z\}$ is then a $\beta$-plurisubharmonic competitor for $P_\beta[w]=w$, not identically $-\infty$, by \cref{lem:separated-curve-model-projections}. Thus
\[
 P_{\beta_f}[w\circ\pi]=w\circ\pi.
\]

Take the atomic Green potentials $g_i$ from the proof of the lemma and put $g=\sum_i g_i\circ\pi_i\circ\pi$. Its complex Hessian is a closed countable divisor current of finite mass minus a smooth form. It is $\beta_f$-plurisubharmonic for every $f\geq0$, and it is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $w\circ\pi$. Put $w_{\mathcal I}=\sum_iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i$. Since $g\sim w_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi$, every ideal competitor $v\leq0$ is bounded above by
\[
 P_{\beta_f}[g]
       =P_{\beta_f}[w_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi]
       =w_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi.
\]
Here \cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination} gives $v\preceq g$, and the ordinary identity applies to $w_{\mathcal I}$. Conversely, $w_{\mathcal I}\circ\pi$ is a normalized ideal competitor, because ideal projection preserves the ideal type on each curve and the ideal type is compatible with pullback and addition. This computes the ideal envelope on $B$ without requiring $\beta_f$ to be positive.

Apply \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} to $M\to B$ and the divisor $B\times D$. Its induced zero obstacle is zero, which gives the two identities upstairs and normalization. Restriction to a fixed section of the auxiliary curve identifies this locus isometrically with the finite product of the spaces of curve models. Completeness, geodesics and the nearest-point property of ideal projection therefore follow as in \cref{prop:separated-product-polar-metric}, and the same rational atomic monomials give analytic density. For $f=1$, the background is positive and \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} shows that every analytic model lies in the ideal subspace. Analytic singularities and the computed ideal projection are independent of $f$, so this analytic-closure assertion holds for every nonnegative coefficient. Fiber integration, now including the factor $C_0$, gives the same $L^1$ convergence upstairs. Each model and each pairwise maximum is $\Psi$ plus a pullback from $B$, whose dimension is smaller than that of $M$, so their upper volumes vanish by \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}; the upper-volume comparison of \cref{def:model-volume-comparison} therefore vanishes for every $f$.

Each polar measure $\mu_i$ has mass at most $A_i$, so the sum distance is at most $2\sum_iA_i$. By \cref{lem:curve-polar-model}, this bound is attained by placing mass $A_i$ at two distinct points of each $C_i$. Hence the diameter does not change, even at the limiting coefficient.

Finally, $\mathrm d\Theta_f=p^*(\mathrm df)\wedge s^*\tau$ is nonzero for nonconstant $f$, and $\Theta_f$ is strictly positive when $f>0$. To see that $\Theta_0$ is not big, put $n=m+1+k$ and use the smooth closed positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form $T$ and the Hermitian form $\Omega$ given by
\[
 T=p^*\left(\bigwedge_i\pi_i^*\eta_i\right)\wedge r^*(\kappa^k),
 \qquad \Omega=p^*\beta+s^*\tau+r^*\kappa.
\]
Then $\int_M\Theta_0\wedge T=0$, whereas $\int_M\Omega\wedge T>0$. By Stokes' theorem, a lower bound $\Theta_0+\ddc u\geq\varepsilon\Omega$ with $\varepsilon>0$ would contradict these identities. Thus the background $\Theta_0$ is not big.
\end{proof}
