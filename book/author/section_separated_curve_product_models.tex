\section{Several curve factors}
\label{subsec:separated-curve-product-models}

A single curve factor produces a model space isometric to the space of models on one curve. Products of several curves give larger complete model spaces at zero volume. The envelopes of a sum of curve potentials are computed coordinatewise: the supremum of a competitor over all factors but one is a potential on the remaining curve, and restriction to slices treats the other factors by induction. The sum of the polar distances of the factors is then a complete metric on the induced product models.

Let $C_1,\ldots,C_m$ be connected compact curves with smooth positive forms $\eta_i$, where $m\geq1$, and put $A_i=\int_{C_i}\eta_i$. Set
\[
 Y=\prod_{i=1}^m C_i,\qquad
 \beta=\sum_{i=1}^m\pi_i^*\eta_i,
\]
where $\pi_i\colon Y\to C_i$ are the projections.

For normalized ordinary models $q_i$ on $(C_i,\eta_i)$, write
\[
 w=\sum_{i=1}^m q_i\circ\pi_i,\qquad
 q_{i,\mathcal I}=P_{\eta_i}[q_i]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
By the case $\sigma_i=\eta_i$, $F=0$ of \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections}, $w$ is a normalized ordinary model on $(Y,\beta)$ and $P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}=\sum_iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i$.

We now add a divisorial factor $(Z,\kappa,D,\psi)$ as in \cref{subsec:zero-volume-product-obstacles}. On $M=Y\times Z$, write $p,r$ for the projections, set $h_i=\pi_i\circ p$, and put
\[
 \Theta=p^*\beta+r^*\kappa,\qquad
 \Psi=\psi\circ r,\qquad
 \Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)=\Psi+\sum_i q_i\circ h_i.
\]
Let $\mathcal S_\Psi^{(m)}$ be the set of these potentials. For the polar curvatures $\mu_i=\mathbf1_{\{q_i=-\infty\}}(\eta_i+\ddc q_i)$ and the analogous measures $\nu_i$ of models $b_i$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-product-polar-metric}
 \rho_\Psi^{(m)}(\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m),\Phi(b_1,\ldots,b_m))
       =\sum_i\|\mu_i-\nu_i\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation}

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:separated-product-polar-metric}\index{Metric completeness!several curve factors}\index{Model potential!separated product metric}
The set $\mathcal S_\Psi^{(m)}$ consists of normalized ordinary models and is a complete geodesic metric space for $\rho_\Psi^{(m)}$. Its ideal models form a closed geodesically convex subspace, equal to the metric closure of its models with analytic singularities. Ideal projection is the unique nearest-point retraction onto this subspace and is $1$-Lipschitz, and the distance to the subspace is the sum of the nonatomic polar masses. Metric convergence implies $L^1(M)$ convergence for every smooth positive volume form. The upper-volume comparison $d_\Theta$ vanishes on every pair of points of this locus.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $m=1$ this is \cref{prop:divisorial-product-polar-metric}, and its proof applies coordinatewise. By the identities for $w$ above and \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}, whose induced zero obstacle is zero,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_\Theta[\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)]=\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m),\\
 P_\Theta[\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)]_{\mathcal I}
       =\Phi(q_{1,\mathcal I},\ldots,q_{m,\mathcal I}),
 \end{gathered}
\]
and independence of the factors gives normalization. Restriction to sections on which the other potentials are finite determines each $q_i$ up to a constant, which the normalization fixes. Thus $\Phi$ identifies the locus with the product of the spaces of curve models, with the sum of their metrics, and ideal projection takes the atomic part in each coordinate. Completeness, the linear interpolation of all polar measures as geodesics, and the assertions on the ideal subspace, its nearest points and the Lipschitz bound follow coordinate by coordinate from \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness,prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect}; the distances add. Finite rational atomic approximations in each coordinate give analytic models, as in the case $m=1$, and every model with analytic singularities is ideal by \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}. The $L^1$ convergence follows from \cref{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence} by integration along the compact fibers of each $h_i$. Finally, since $\dim Y<\dim M$, \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} gives zero upper volume for every model and every pairwise maximum, which is $\Psi$ plus the pullback of a $\beta$-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$; hence $d_\Theta$ vanishes.
\end{proof}

An auxiliary curve factor with a variable coefficient makes the background nonclosed, and lets it degenerate, without changing this metric locus. Let $C_0$ be another connected compact curve with a smooth positive form $\tau$. On $M=Y\times C_0\times Z$, write $p,s,r$ for the projections. For a smooth real function $f\geq0$ on $Y$, put
\[
 \Theta_f=p^*\beta+(f\circ p)s^*\tau+r^*\kappa,
 \qquad \Psi=\psi\circ r,
\]
and set $\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)=\Psi+\sum_iq_i\circ\pi_i\circ p$.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonclosed-separated-product-metric}\label{cor:nonclosed-product-polar-metric}\label{cor:degenerate-product-polar-metric}\index{Metric completeness!nonclosed separated product}\index{Model potential!nonbig separated locus}\index{Metric completeness!nonclosed zero-volume product}\index{Model potential!nonclosed polar product metric}\index{Model potential!nonbig product background}\index{Metric completeness!degenerating product background}
For every tuple of normalized ordinary curve models, one has
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_{\Theta_f}[\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)]=\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m),\\
 P_{\Theta_f}[\Phi(q_1,\ldots,q_m)]_{\mathcal I}
       =\Phi(q_{1,\mathcal I},\ldots,q_{m,\mathcal I}).
 \end{gathered}
\]
On the locus of these potentials, use the sum of polar distances \eqref{eq:separated-product-polar-metric}. All the conclusions of \cref{prop:separated-product-polar-metric} hold, and the diameter is $2\sum_i A_i$. The locus, its metric, ideal projection and the analytic closure are independent of $f$, including at $f=0$. The background $\Theta_f$ is positive when $f>0$ and not closed when $f$ is nonconstant, and $\Theta_0$ is not big.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On $B=Y\times C_0$, let $\pi$ be the projection to $Y$ and put $\beta_f=\pi^*\beta+(f\circ\pi)s_0^*\tau$, where $s_0$ is the projection to $C_0$. By \cref{prop:separated-curve-obstacle-projections}, $P_\beta[w]=w$, and $P_\beta[w]_{\mathcal I}=\sum_iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i$ satisfies the divisorial hypothesis of \cref{lem:auxiliary-slice-envelopes}. That lemma, with $T=C_0$ and $\alpha=(f\circ\pi)s_0^*\tau$, gives
\[
 P_{\beta_f}[w\circ\pi]=w\circ\pi,\qquad
 P_{\beta_f}[w\circ\pi]_{\mathcal I}=\sum_iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ\pi_i\circ\pi.
\]
\Cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction}, applied to $M\to B$ and the divisor $B\times D$, whose induced zero obstacle is zero, gives the two identities upstairs, and normalization holds because the factors are independent.

The conclusions of \cref{prop:separated-product-polar-metric} now follow by the same coordinatewise arguments, since restriction to sections determines the tuple and the identities show that ideal projection takes the atomic part in each coordinate. The analytic models used there pull back to analytic models under the projection forgetting $C_0$, and every model with analytic singularities is ideal by \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}. Fiber integration, now including the factor $C_0$, gives $L^1$ convergence. Every model and every pairwise maximum is $\Psi$ plus a pullback from $B$, and $\dim B<\dim M$, so \cref{prop:fibration-obstacle-reduction} makes $d_{\Theta_f}$ vanish. None of these statements involves $f$. Each polar measure $\mu_i$ has mass at most $A_i$, so the diameter is at most $2\sum_iA_i$; by \cref{lem:curve-polar-model}, the bound is attained by placing mass $A_i$ at two distinct points of each $C_i$.

Finally, $\Theta_f$ is positive when $f>0$, and $\mathrm d\Theta_f=p^*(\mathrm df)\wedge s^*\tau$ is nonzero for nonconstant $f$. To see that $\Theta_0$ is not big, put $n=m+1+k$ and use the smooth closed positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form $T$ and the Hermitian form $\Omega$ given by
\[
 T=p^*\left(\bigwedge_i\pi_i^*\eta_i\right)\wedge r^*(\kappa^k),
 \qquad \Omega=p^*\beta+s^*\tau+r^*\kappa.
\]
Then $\int_M\Theta_0\wedge T=0$, whereas $\int_M\Omega\wedge T>0$. By Stokes' theorem, a lower bound $\Theta_0+\ddc u\geq\varepsilon\Omega$ with $u$ quasi-plurisubharmonic and $\varepsilon>0$ would contradict these identities.
\end{proof}
