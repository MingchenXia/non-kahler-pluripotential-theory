\section{Maxima and the maximum principle}

Maxima allow us to truncate potentials and to modify them on an open set without losing the lower curvature bound. For a smooth background, the local maximum and gluing principles for plurisubharmonic functions keep this bound: one freezes the coefficients of the background on small balls and then lets the resulting curvature error tend to zero.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:max-psh}\index{Plurisubharmonic function!maximum}
If $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\max(u,v)\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Consequently, if $\theta\geq0$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\max(u,-t)$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic for every real $t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adding a common local smooth correction to $u$ and $v$ shows that $\max(u,v)$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic. To obtain the sharp lower bound for its Hessian, fix a positive constant $(1,1)$-form $\kappa$ on a coordinate ball. For each $\varepsilon>0$, every point of the ball has a neighborhood on which $\theta$ is close to a constant form $\theta'$, in the sense that $-\varepsilon\kappa\leq\theta-\theta'\leq\varepsilon\kappa$. Write $\theta'+\varepsilon\kappa=\ddc r$ with a quadratic polynomial $r$. Then $\ddc(u+r)\geq\theta+\ddc u\geq0$, so $u+r$ and, likewise, $v+r$ are plurisubharmonic; hence so is their maximum. It follows that
\[
 \theta+2\varepsilon\kappa+\ddc\max(u,v)\geq0
\]
on each such neighborhood, and letting $\varepsilon\to0$ gives $\theta+\ddc\max(u,v)\geq0$. The last assertion follows because constants belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$ when $\theta\geq0$.
\end{proof}

The proof is local. Hence the first assertion of \cref{lem:max-psh} holds for $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions on any open subset of a complex manifold, with $\theta$ a smooth real $(1,1)$-form there.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-gluing}\index{Plurisubharmonic function!gluing}
Let $Y$ be a complex manifold, let $\beta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $Y$, and let $D\subset Y$ be open. Suppose that $u$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic on $Y$, that $v$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic on $D$, and that
\[
 \limsup_{D\ni y\to x}v(y)\leq u(x)\qquad(x\in\partial D).
\]
Then the function equal to $\max(u,v)$ on $D$ and to $u$ on $Y\setminus D$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Denote the glued function by $w$; it is upper semicontinuous by the boundary inequality. Freeze $\beta$ on a small coordinate ball as in the proof of \cref{lem:max-psh}, and choose a quadratic correction $r$ such that $u+r$ and $v+r$ are plurisubharmonic on their respective domains. Off $\partial D$, the function $w+r$ is plurisubharmonic, being locally either $u+r$ or the maximum of two plurisubharmonic functions. At a point of $\partial D$, it equals $u+r$ and dominates $u+r$ on the ball, so it satisfies the submean inequality on every complex line through that point. Thus $w+r$ is plurisubharmonic on the whole ball. Letting the error of the frozen coefficients tend to zero gives $\beta+\ddc w\geq0$.
\end{proof}

For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, an inequality that holds almost everywhere holds everywhere. This allows comparisons proved for Sobolev or Lebesgue classes to be applied to the potentials themselves.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}
Let $a,b$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on a complex manifold. If $a\leq b$ almost everywhere, then $a\leq b$ everywhere. In particular, equality almost everywhere implies equality everywhere.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a coordinate ball, add the same smooth function $q$ to both functions so that $a+q$ and $b+q$ are plurisubharmonic. By the submean inequality and upper semicontinuity, a plurisubharmonic function is, at each point, the limit of its averages over balls centered there as the radius tends to zero. The almost-everywhere inequality orders these averages, so passing to the limit gives $a+q\leq b+q$. Subtracting $q$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:strict-set-positive-volume}
Let $a,b$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on a complex manifold. If $a(x)<b(x)$ at some point $x$, then $\{a<b\}$ has positive volume with respect to any smooth positive volume form.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a number $c$ with $a(x)<c<b(x)$. By upper semicontinuity, $a<c$ on a neighborhood $V$ of $x$. If $\{b>c\}\cap V$ had zero volume, then $b\leq c$ almost everywhere on $V$, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} would give $b(x)\leq c$, a contradiction. Thus $\{b>c\}\cap V$, which is contained in $\{a<b\}$, has positive volume.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:psh-liouville}\index{Liouville theorem}
A plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb C^m$, $m\geq1$, that is bounded above is constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the Liouville theorem of \cite[Lemma~2.28]{GZ17}.
\end{proof}

For a function $f$, we write $f^*(x)=\limsup_{y\to x}f(y)$ for its upper semicontinuous regularization. A subset of a complex manifold is \emph{pluripolar} if each of its points has a neighborhood $U$ such that the part of the subset in $U$ is contained in $\{p=-\infty\}$ for some $p\in\PSH(U)$.\index{Pluripolar set} The local upper-envelope theorem recalled in \cite{SGPT} states that if a nonempty family of plurisubharmonic functions is locally uniformly bounded above, then the regularization of its supremum is plurisubharmonic and differs from the supremum only on a pluripolar set.

Periodicity also forces rigidity when an entire subharmonic function has only logarithmic upper growth.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:periodic-subharmonic-log-growth}
Let $h$ be subharmonic on $\C$, with $h(\zeta+1)=h(\zeta)$. If there are real constants $A\geq0$ and $B$ such that
\[
 h(\zeta)\leq A\log(1+|\zeta|)+B\qquad(\zeta\in\C),
\]
then $h$ is constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the growth bound, the real translates $h(\cdot+s)$, $s\in[0,1]$, form a family of subharmonic functions that is locally uniformly bounded above. The local upper-envelope theorem therefore shows that
\[
 H(\zeta)=\bigl(\sup_{s\in[0,1]}h(\zeta+s)\bigr)^*
\]
is subharmonic. By periodicity, $H$ is invariant under every real translation, so it depends only on $\operatorname{Im}\zeta$, and its subharmonicity means that it is a convex function of $\operatorname{Im}\zeta$. On the imaginary axis it satisfies
$H(\mathrm i y)\leq A\log(2+|y|)+B$. Since a nonconstant convex function on $\mathbb R$ grows at least linearly at one end, this bound shows that $H$ is constant. Thus $h\leq H$ is bounded above, and \cref{lem:psh-liouville} shows that $h$ is constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}\label{lem:qpsh-removable-pluripolar}\index{Removable singularity}
Let $U$ be a complex manifold, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $U$, and let $Z\subset U$ be closed and locally pluripolar. Let $u$ be $\theta$-plurisubharmonic on $U\setminus Z$ and locally bounded above near $Z$. Then its upper-limit extension, defined at $x\in Z$ by $u(x)=\limsup_{U\setminus Z\ni y\to x}u(y)$, is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic on $U$. In particular, this applies when $Z$ is a nowhere dense analytic subset of $U$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First suppose that $u$ is plurisubharmonic. Near a point of $Z$, choose a plurisubharmonic function $\psi\leq0$ with $\psi=-\infty$ on $Z$. For $t>0$, extend $u+t\psi$ by $-\infty$ on $Z$. The local upper bound for $u$ makes this extension upper semicontinuous. It is plurisubharmonic off $Z$, and its submean inequality on complex lines is automatic at points of $Z$, where its value is $-\infty$. It is therefore plurisubharmonic on the whole neighborhood. As $t\downarrow0$, these functions increase, with a common local upper bound. By the local upper-envelope theorem, the regularization of their limit is a plurisubharmonic function that agrees with $u$ almost everywhere off $Z$, hence everywhere off $Z$ by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. By upper semicontinuity and the submean inequality, its value at a point of $Z$ is the upper limit of $u$ off $Z$, so it is the upper-limit extension of $u$.

For a smooth background, fix a positive constant form $\kappa$ on a coordinate chart and let $\epsilon>0$. On a sufficiently small ball, freeze $\theta$ to a constant form $\theta'$ with $-\epsilon\kappa\leq\theta-\theta'\leq\epsilon\kappa$, and choose a quadratic potential $q$ for $\theta'+\epsilon\kappa$. Applying the preceding argument to the plurisubharmonic function $u+q$ shows that the upper-limit extension satisfies $\theta+\ddc u\geq-2\epsilon\kappa$ on the ball. Since the extension is defined by upper limits, it does not depend on the ball, and letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives $\theta+\ddc u\geq0$. Finally, a nowhere dense analytic subset is locally pluripolar: locally, it is contained in the $-\infty$ locus of the logarithm of the sum of the squared moduli of local defining functions, not all identically zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projective-puncture-upper-bound}\label{lem:isolated-puncture-extension}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $p\in X$. Every $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function on $X\setminus\{p\}$ is locally bounded above near $p$ and extends uniquely to a member of $\PSH(X,\theta)$. If $X$ is compact, it is bounded above on $X\setminus\{p\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose coordinates centered at $p$ and write $\Delta_R=\{z\in\C:|z|<R\}$. On the coordinate polydisk $\Delta_R^n$, add a smooth quadratic function to the given function to obtain a plurisubharmonic function $h$ on $\Delta_R^n\setminus\{0\}$. Write the coordinates as $(z_1,z_2,z')$, with $z'$ absent when $n=2$, and choose $0<r<R$. The compact set
\[
 K=\{|z_1|\leq r,\ |z_2|=r,\ |z'_j|\leq r\}
\]
avoids the puncture, so $h\leq M$ on $K$ for some finite $M$. Consider a point of the punctured closed polydisk of radius $r$. If $z_1\ne0$, the disk in the $z_2$-direction through the point avoids the puncture and has its boundary in $K$, so the maximum principle gives $h\leq M$ at the point. If $z_1=0$ and $(z_2,z')\ne0$, the disk in the $z_1$-direction through the point avoids the puncture, and its boundary consists of points with $z_1\neq0$, where the bound has already been proved; the maximum principle again gives $h\leq M$. Subtracting the smooth correction proves the local upper bound.

By \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}, applied with $Z=\{p\}$, the upper-limit extension belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$. It is unique by \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. If $X$ is compact, the extension is an upper semicontinuous function on a compact space and is therefore bounded above.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-upper-test}
Let $u$ be $\theta$-plurisubharmonic near a point $x$. If a $C^2$ function $a$ touches $u$ from above at $x$, that is, $a\geq u$ near $x$ and $a(x)=u(x)$, then $\theta(x)+\ddc a(x)\geq0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In coordinates centered at $x$, let $q$ be a quadratic polynomial with $\ddc q=\theta(x)$, and let $\varepsilon>0$. On a sufficiently small ball about $x$, we have $\theta\leq\theta(x)+\varepsilon\ddc|z|^2$, so $u+q+\varepsilon|z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic, and $a+q+\varepsilon|z|^2$ touches it from above at $x$. By the submean inequality on complex lines through $x$, a $C^2$ function touching a plurisubharmonic function from above at $x$ has nonnegative complex Hessian at $x$. Hence $\theta(x)+\ddc a(x)+\varepsilon\ddc|z|^2\geq0$, and letting $\varepsilon\to0$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
