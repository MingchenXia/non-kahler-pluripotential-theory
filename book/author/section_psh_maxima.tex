\section{Maxima and the maximum principle}

Maxima allow us to truncate potentials and to replace them on an open set while preserving a lower curvature bound. For a smooth background, the local psh maximum and gluing principles retain that bound by freezing the coefficients and then letting the curvature error tend to zero.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:max-psh}\index{Plurisubharmonic function!maximum}
If $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\max(u,v)\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Consequently, if $\theta\geq0$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\max(u,-t)$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic for every real $t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The maximum is quasi-plurisubharmonic by a common local smooth correction. To verify its precise lower Hessian bound, fix a positive constant $(1,1)$-form $\kappa$ in a coordinate ball. For each $\varepsilon>0$, freeze $\theta$ on smaller neighborhoods to a constant form $\theta'$ with $-\varepsilon\kappa\leq\theta-\theta'\leq\varepsilon\kappa$. Write $\theta'+\varepsilon\kappa=\ddc r$ locally. Then $u+r$ and $v+r$ are plurisubharmonic, hence so is their maximum. It follows that
\[
 \theta+2\varepsilon\kappa+\ddc\max(u,v)\geq0
\]
on each such neighborhood. Let $\varepsilon\to0$. For the last assertion, constants belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$ when $\theta\geq0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-gluing}\index{Plurisubharmonic function!gluing}
Let $Y$ be a complex manifold, let $\beta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $Y$, and let $D\subset Y$ be open. Suppose $u$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic on $Y$, $v$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic on $D$, and
\[
 \limsup_{D\ni y\to x}v(y)\leq u(x)\qquad(x\in\partial D).
\]
Then the function equal to $\max(u,v)$ on $D$ and to $u$ on $Y\setminus D$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Call the pasted function $w$. It is upper semicontinuous by the boundary inequality. Freeze $\beta$ on a small coordinate ball as in \cref{lem:max-psh}, and choose a quadratic correction $r$ so that $u+r$ and $v+r$ are plurisubharmonic on their respective domains. Away from $\partial D$, the function $w+r$ is plurisubharmonic by the maximum theorem. At a point of $\partial D$ it equals $u+r$ and dominates $u+r$ on the ball, so it satisfies the submean inequality on every complex line through that point. Thus $w+r$ is plurisubharmonic on the whole ball. Letting the coefficient-freezing error tend to zero gives $\beta+\ddc w\geq0$.
\end{proof}

Almost-everywhere order also determines pointwise order. This permits comparisons proved in a Sobolev class to be applied to the quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials themselves.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}
Let $a,b$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions. If $a\leq b$ almost everywhere, then $a\leq b$ everywhere. In particular, equality almost everywhere implies equality everywhere.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a coordinate ball add the same smooth function $q$ so that $a+q$ and $b+q$ are plurisubharmonic. At each point a plurisubharmonic function equals the limit of its ball averages as the radius tends to zero, by the submean inequality and upper semicontinuity. The almost-everywhere inequality orders these averages, so passage to the limit gives $a+q\leq b+q$. Subtract $q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:strict-set-positive-volume}
If $a,b$ are quasi-plurisubharmonic functions and $\{a<b\}$ is nonempty at a point where both are finite, then this set has positive smooth volume.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a point $x$ with $a(x)<b(x)$ and a number $c$ between them. Upper semicontinuity gives $a<c$ on a neighborhood $V$ of $x$. If $\{b>c\}\cap V$ had zero volume, then $b\leq c$ almost everywhere on $V$. By \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, this would imply $b(x)\leq c$, a contradiction. Thus $\{a<b\}$ has positive volume.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:psh-liouville}\index{Liouville theorem}
A plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb C^m$, $m\geq1$, that is bounded above is constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First let $f$ be smooth and subharmonic on $\mathbb C$, with $f\leq M$. Write $\sigma=(\Delta f)\,\mathrm{d}A$ for its nonnegative Laplacian measure, where $\Delta$ and $\mathrm{d}A$ are Euclidean. Green's formula for circular means gives, for $R>0$,
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\,\mathrm{d}t-f(0)
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^R\frac{\sigma(B(0,r))}{r}\,\mathrm{d}r.
\]
For fixed $r_0>0$ and $R>r_0$, the right side is at least $\sigma(B(0,r_0))\log(R/r_0)/(2\pi)$, while the left side is at most $M-f(0)$. Letting $R\to\infty$ gives $\sigma(B(0,r_0))=0$. Thus $f$ is harmonic, and the harmonic Liouville theorem applied to the positive harmonic function $M+1-f$ makes $f$ constant.

For the given plurisubharmonic function, convolution with a smooth nonnegative radial probability kernel gives smooth plurisubharmonic functions with the same upper bound. Their restrictions to every affine complex line are constant by the planar argument, so each convolution is constant on $\mathbb C^m$. Local $L^1$ convergence of the convolutions and the submean representative give the assertion.
\end{proof}

We write $f^*(x)=\limsup_{y\to x}f(y)$ for upper semicontinuous regularization. A subset is \emph{pluripolar} if locally it is contained in $\{p=-\infty\}$ for a plurisubharmonic function $p$.\index{Pluripolar set} The local upper-envelope theorem in \cite{SGPT} states that a nonempty, locally uniformly upper-bounded family of plurisubharmonic functions has a plurisubharmonic regularized supremum; regularization changes the supremum only on a pluripolar set.

Periodicity also forces rigidity when an entire subharmonic function has only logarithmic upper growth.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:periodic-subharmonic-log-growth}
Let $h$ be subharmonic on $\C$, with $h(\zeta+1)=h(\zeta)$. If there are real constants $A\geq0$ and $B$ such that
\[
 h(\zeta)\leq A\log(1+|\zeta|)+B\qquad(\zeta\in\C),
\]
then $h$ is constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The locally bounded-above family of real translates has the subharmonic upper envelope
\[
 H(\zeta)=\bigl(\sup_{s\in[0,1]}h(\zeta+s)\bigr)^*.
\]
Periodicity makes $H$ invariant under every real translation. Subharmonicity therefore says that $H$ is a convex function of $\operatorname{Im}\zeta$. On the imaginary axis it satisfies
$H(\mathrm i y)\leq A\log(2+|y|)+B$. A nonconstant convex function on $\mathbb R$ grows at least linearly at one end, so this bound makes $H$ constant. Since $h\leq H$, \cref{lem:psh-liouville} now makes $h$ constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic}\label{lem:qpsh-removable-pluripolar}\index{Removable singularity}
Let $U$ be a complex manifold, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $U$, and let $Z\subset U$ be closed and locally pluripolar. If $u$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic on $U\setminus Z$ and locally bounded above near $Z$, its upper-limit extension is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic on $U$. In particular this applies when $Z$ is a proper analytic subset.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First suppose $u$ is plurisubharmonic. Locally choose a genuine plurisubharmonic $\psi\leq0$ with $\psi=-\infty$ on $Z$. For $t>0$, extend $u+t\psi$ by $-\infty$ on $Z$. The local upper bound makes this extension upper semicontinuous. It is plurisubharmonic off $Z$, and its submean inequality on any complex line is automatic at points of $Z$. It is therefore plurisubharmonic on the whole neighborhood. Let $t\downarrow0$ and regularize the increasing limit. The local upper-envelope theorem gives a plurisubharmonic function agreeing with $u$ almost everywhere off $Z$, hence everywhere there. This is the asserted upper-limit extension.

For a smooth background, fix a constant positive form $\kappa$ in a coordinate chart. On a sufficiently small ball, freeze $\theta$ to a constant form $\theta'$ with $|\theta-\theta'|\leq\epsilon\kappa$, and choose a quadratic potential $q$ for $\theta'+\epsilon\kappa$. Apply the preceding argument to $u+q$. Its extension gives $\theta+\ddc u\geq-2\epsilon\kappa$. Let $\epsilon\downarrow0$. The extensions agree on overlaps by their upper-limit definition. Analytic subsets are locally pluripolar, as follows by taking the logarithm of the squared norm of local defining functions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projective-puncture-upper-bound}\label{lem:isolated-puncture-extension}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $p\in X$. Every $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function on $X\setminus\{p\}$ is locally bounded above near $p$ and extends uniquely to a member of $\PSH(X,\theta)$. If $X$ is compact, it is bounded above on $X\setminus\{p\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose coordinates centered at $p$ and write $\Delta_R=\{z\in\C:|z|<R\}$. On the coordinate polydisc $\Delta_R^n$, add a smooth quadratic function to obtain a plurisubharmonic function $h$ on $\Delta_R^n\setminus\{0\}$. Write the coordinates as $(z_1,z_2,z')$, with $z'$ absent when $n=2$, and choose $0<r<R$. The compact set
\[
 K=\{|z_1|\leq r,\ |z_2|=r,\ |z'_j|\leq r\}
\]
avoids the puncture, so $h\leq M$ on $K$ for some finite $M$. For $z_1\ne0$, the maximum principle in the $z_2$-disc gives the same bound throughout the smaller polydisc. If $z_1=0$ and $(z_2,z')\ne0$, the $z_1$-disc avoids the puncture, and its boundary is already covered by this bound. Applying the maximum principle once more gives $h\leq M$ there too. Subtracting the smooth correction proves the local upper bound.

\Cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic} supplies the extension with its original lower curvature bound. Uniqueness follows from \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}. On a compact $X$, upper semicontinuity then gives a global upper bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-upper-test}
Let $u$ be $\theta$-plurisubharmonic near a point $x$. If a $C^2$ function $a$ touches $u$ from above at $x$, then $\theta(x)+\ddc a(x)\geq0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Freeze $\theta(x)$ on a small coordinate ball and take a quadratic potential for this constant form. After an arbitrarily small positive quadratic correction, its sum with $u$ is plurisubharmonic. An upper test for a plurisubharmonic function has nonnegative complex Hessian, by the submean inequality on complex lines. Let the size of the correction tend to zero.
\end{proof}
