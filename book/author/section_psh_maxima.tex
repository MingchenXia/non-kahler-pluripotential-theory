\section{Maxima and the maximum principle}

Truncating a potential requires stability under maxima. For a smooth background, this follows from the local psh maximum theorem by freezing the coefficients and then letting the curvature error tend to zero. The submean inequality also determines pointwise order from almost-everywhere order and forces upper-bounded plurisubharmonic functions on Euclidean space to be constant.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:max-psh}
If $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\max(u,v)\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. Consequently, if $\theta\geq0$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, then $\max(u,-t)$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic for every real $t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The maximum is quasi-plurisubharmonic by a common local smooth correction. To verify its precise lower Hessian bound, fix a positive constant $(1,1)$-form $\kappa$ in a coordinate ball. For each $\varepsilon>0$, freeze $\theta$ on smaller neighborhoods to a constant form $\theta'$ with $-\varepsilon\kappa\leq\theta-\theta'\leq\varepsilon\kappa$. Write $\theta'+\varepsilon\kappa=\ddc r$ locally. Then $u+r$ and $v+r$ are plurisubharmonic, hence so is their maximum. It follows that
\[
 \theta+2\varepsilon\kappa+\ddc\max(u,v)\geq0
\]
on each such neighborhood. Let $\varepsilon\to0$. For the last assertion, constants belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$ when $\theta\geq0$.
\end{proof}


Almost-everywhere order also determines pointwise order. This permits comparisons proved in a Sobolev class to be applied to the quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials themselves.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}
Let $a,b$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions. If $a\leq b$ almost everywhere, then $a\leq b$ everywhere. In particular, equality almost everywhere implies equality everywhere.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a coordinate ball add the same smooth function $q$ so that $a+q$ and $b+q$ are plurisubharmonic. At each point a plurisubharmonic function equals the limit of its ball averages as the radius tends to zero, by the submean inequality and upper semicontinuity. The almost-everywhere inequality orders these averages, so passage to the limit gives $a+q\leq b+q$. Subtract $q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:strict-set-positive-volume}
If $a,b$ are quasi-plurisubharmonic functions and $\{a<b\}$ is nonempty at a point where both are finite, then this set has positive smooth volume.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a point $x$ with $a(x)<b(x)$ and a number $c$ between them. Upper semicontinuity gives $a<c$ on a neighborhood $V$ of $x$. If $\{b>c\}\cap V$ had zero volume, then $b\leq c$ almost everywhere on $V$. By \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order}, this would imply $b(x)\leq c$, a contradiction. Thus $\{a<b\}$ has positive volume.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:psh-liouville}
A plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb C^m$, $m\geq1$, that is bounded above is constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First let $f$ be smooth and subharmonic on $\mathbb C$, with $f\leq M$. Write $\sigma=(\Delta f)\,\mathrm{d}A$ for its nonnegative Laplacian measure, where $\Delta$ and $\mathrm{d}A$ are Euclidean. Green's formula for circular means gives, for $R>0$,
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\,\mathrm{d}t-f(0)
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^R\frac{\sigma(B(0,r))}{r}\,\mathrm{d}r.
\]
For fixed $r_0>0$ and $R>r_0$, the right side is at least $\sigma(B(0,r_0))\log(R/r_0)/(2\pi)$, while the left side is at most $M-f(0)$. Letting $R\to\infty$ gives $\sigma(B(0,r_0))=0$. Thus $f$ is harmonic, and the harmonic Liouville theorem applied to the positive harmonic function $M+1-f$ makes $f$ constant.

For the given plurisubharmonic function, convolution with a smooth nonnegative radial probability kernel gives smooth plurisubharmonic functions with the same upper bound. Their restrictions to every affine complex line are constant by the planar argument, so each convolution is constant on $\mathbb C^m$. Local $L^1$ convergence of the convolutions and the submean representative give the assertion.
\end{proof}
