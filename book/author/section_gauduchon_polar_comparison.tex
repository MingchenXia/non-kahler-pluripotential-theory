\subsection{Gauduchon polar trace comparisons}
\label{subsec:gauduchon-polar-comparison}

To use polar currents on all modifications at once, their sizes must be normalized compatibly. Pairing with Gauduchon forms does this: for a Gauduchon form $\eta$ on a modification, the total $\eta$-trace of the curvature current does not depend on the potential. Taking the supremum, over a family of modifications, of the normalized total variations of the polar traces gives a comparison that separates analytic models without any bound on top-degree mass. The space of all possible values of these coordinates is complete; whether its limit points are realized by ordinary models is a separate question.

Here $n\geq1$ is arbitrary, and $\omega$ is a Hermitian form on $X$. Fix a set $\mathscr R$ of smooth modifications $\pi\colon Y_\pi\to X$ that contains, for any two analytic singularity types, a common log resolution. On each $Y_\pi$, choose a Gauduchon form $\eta_\pi$, that is, a Hermitian form with $\ddc(\eta_\pi^{n-1})=0$ as in \cref{def:gauduchon-metric}. For $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)$, with the polar current $\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(u)$ of \cref{subsec:polar-first-current-obstruction}, set
\[
 \mu_\pi(u)=\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(u)\wedge\eta_\pi^{n-1},
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauduchon-polar-comparison}
 \begin{gathered}
 A_\pi=\int_{Y_\pi}\pi^*\omega\wedge\eta_\pi^{n-1},\\
 \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(u,v)
       =\sup_{\pi\in\mathscr R}
          \frac{\|\mu_\pi(u)-\mu_\pi(v)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{A_\pi}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The Gauduchon forms are part of the data. Multiplying one of them by a positive constant does not change the corresponding term in the supremum.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gauduchon-polar-comparison}\index{Model potential!Gauduchon polar trace}\index{Gauduchon metric!polar trace comparison}
Every $A_\pi$ is positive. The comparison \eqref{eq:gauduchon-polar-comparison} is a pseudometric on $\PSH(X,\omega)$, bounded by two, and its restriction to ordinary models with analytic singularities is a metric. If $X$ is a curve, then for all ordinary models $p,q$,
\[
 \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,q)
       =\frac{\|\operatorname{Pol}_\omega(p)-\operatorname{Pol}_\omega(q)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\int_X\omega},
\]
so $\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}$ induces the complete topology of the polar metric on curve models, in which the closure of the analytic models is exactly the set of ideal models.

Let $f$ be a smooth real function and $\omega'=\omega+\ddc f$. If $\delta_{\omega'}^{\mathrm{pol}}$ is defined by the same formula with the same Gauduchon forms, then its denominators are positive and
\[
 \delta_{\omega'}^{\mathrm{pol}}(u-f,v-f)
       =\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(u,v)
       \qquad(u,v\in\PSH(X,\omega)).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The form $\pi^*\omega$ is semipositive, and it is positive on the nonempty open set where $\pi$ is an isomorphism, so $A_\pi>0$. By \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}, $u\circ\pi\in\PSH(Y_\pi,\pi^*\omega)$. Since $\ddc(\eta_\pi^{n-1})=0$, Stokes' theorem for currents gives
\[
 \int_{Y_\pi}\bigl(\pi^*\omega+\ddc(u\circ\pi)\bigr)
                 \wedge\eta_\pi^{n-1}=A_\pi.
\]
Thus $\mu_\pi(u)$ is a positive measure of mass at most $A_\pi$, and the normalized total variation distance of two such measures is at most two. Symmetry and the triangle inequality persist under the supremum, which proves the pseudometric assertion.

Let $p,q$ be ordinary models with analytic singularities, choose a common log resolution $\pi$ in $\mathscr R$, and suppose that $\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,q)=0$. Then their measures in this coordinate agree. Let $E_i$ be the prime components of the resolved ideals on $Y_\pi$, and $a_i,b_i$ the coefficients of $p$ and $q$ along them. The divisorial restriction argument of \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} gives
\[
 \mu_\pi(p)=\sum_i a_i[E_i]\wedge\eta_\pi^{n-1},\qquad
 \mu_\pi(q)=\sum_i b_i[E_i]\wedge\eta_\pi^{n-1}.
\]
Distinct components do not share trace mass on their intersections, and each component has strictly positive trace mass. Equality of these measures therefore implies $a_i=b_i$ for every $i$. The criterion of \cref{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance}, which uses only the finitely many divisors $E_i$, applied in both directions gives $p\sim q$. An ordinary model is the ordinary envelope of every potential of its type, so ordinary models with the same type are equal. This proves separation.

On a curve, every smooth modification is an isomorphism and $\eta_\pi^{n-1}=1$. Hence $A_\pi=\int_X\omega$, and pullback preserves the total variation of polar curvature, so every $\pi\in\mathscr R$ gives the same term in the supremum. This proves the formula on curves, and the assertions following it are consequences of \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness,prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect}.

Finally, $u-f$ has, in background $\omega'$, the same pulled-back curvature current and the same pole set as $u$ in background $\omega$, and Stokes' theorem gives $A_{\pi,\omega'}=A_{\pi,\omega}$. The gauge formula follows. It holds for the potentials themselves, before any ordinary projection.
\end{proof}

The coordinates $\mu_\pi(u)$ take values in a complete space. For each $\pi$, let $\mathscr B_\pi$ be the set of positive finite Borel measures on $Y_\pi$ of mass at most $A_\pi$.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:polar-trace-coordinate-completeness}\index{Metric completeness!polar trace coordinates}
The space $\prod_{\pi\in\mathscr R}\mathscr B_\pi$ is complete for the metric
\[
 \widehat\delta((\lambda_\pi),(\nu_\pi))
       =\sup_{\pi\in\mathscr R}
          \frac{\|\lambda_\pi-\nu_\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{A_\pi}.
\]
The map $u\mapsto(\mu_\pi(u))_\pi$ takes values in this space and preserves the comparison $\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The metric is finite, bounded by two, and separates points. A Cauchy sequence is Cauchy in total variation in each coordinate, and completeness of the space of finite signed measures gives a limit there; positivity and the mass bound pass to the limit. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ such that every normalized coordinate distance is at most $\varepsilon$ for all $j,k\geq N$. Letting $k$ tend to infinity separately in each coordinate preserves this inequality, and taking the supremum proves convergence for $\widehat\delta$. The last assertion follows from the definition and the mass bound proved above.
\end{proof}

Since the coordinates $\mu_\pi$ are invariant under bounded changes of the potential, the gauge identity of \cref{prop:gauduchon-polar-comparison}, which concerns the potentials themselves, transfers to ordinary models.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:polar-model-gauge-transport}\index{Model potential!polar gauge transport}
The comparison $\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}$ descends to ordinary classes. For a smooth real function $f$ and $\omega'=\omega+\ddc f$, the map
\[
 G_f(p)=P_{\omega'}[p-f]
\]
is a bijection between the ordinary models in the two backgrounds, with inverse $q\mapsto P_\omega[q+f]$. If the same Gauduchon forms are used in both backgrounds, $G_f$ preserves $\delta^{\mathrm{pol}}$, analytic models and ideal models.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Boundedly equivalent potentials have boundedly equivalent pullbacks on every modification, with the same pole set. \Cref{prop:bounded-type-polar-invariance} therefore gives equality of their polar currents and of their measures $\mu_\pi$ in every coordinate, which proves descent to ordinary classes.

If $p=P_\omega[p]$, the version of \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge} for ordinary envelopes gives
\[
 p+\min_Xf\leq G_f(p)+f\leq p+\max_Xf.
\]
Thus $G_f(p)\sim p-f$. Since ordinary envelopes depend only on the ordinary type, $P_{\omega'}[G_f(p)]=P_{\omega'}[p-f]=G_f(p)$, and similarly $P_\omega[G_f(p)+f]=P_\omega[p]=p$. The same argument applied to models in background $\omega'$ proves surjectivity and the formula for the inverse. Invariance of the polar coordinates and the gauge identity of \cref{prop:gauduchon-polar-comparison} now give
\[
 \delta_{\omega'}^{\mathrm{pol}}(G_f(p),G_f(q))
       =\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,q).
\]

Subtraction of a smooth function and bounded equivalence preserve analytic singularities. If $p=P_\omega[p]_{\mathcal I}$, the gauge estimate for ideal envelopes gives $P_{\omega'}[p-f]_{\mathcal I}\sim p-f\sim G_f(p)$. By \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}, this ideal envelope is an ordinary model, and two ordinary models of the same ordinary type coincide. Hence it equals $G_f(p)$, which shows that $G_f$ maps ideal models to ideal models; the inverse map gives the converse.
\end{proof}

The traces $\mu_\pi$ determine the whole polar currents, because these currents are closed by the local cutoff argument of \cref{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}, which also applies when the pole set is not closed.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:closed-pole-trace-recovery}\index{Polar curvature!recovery from trace}
Let $u,v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. For every $\pi\in\mathscr R$, the currents $\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(u)$ and $\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(v)$ are closed, and they agree if and only if $\mu_\pi(u)=\mu_\pi(v)$. Consequently, $\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(u,v)=0$ exactly when all these polar currents agree.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}, the pullbacks belong to $\PSH(Y_\pi,\pi^*\omega)$, and \cref{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}, applied with background $\pi^*\omega$, shows that their polar currents are closed and positive. Both currents are carried by the union of the two Borel pole sets, which has Lebesgue measure zero. Equality of their $\eta_\pi$-traces therefore gives equality of the currents by \cref{lem:closed-null-current-trace}, and the converse is immediate. Since every $A_\pi$ is positive, the last assertion follows.
\end{proof}

For a sequence that is Cauchy for $\delta^{\mathrm{pol}}_\omega$, total-variation convergence of the traces keeps the limiting polar mass on the union of the pole sets of the sequence, and the recovery from traces then gives compatible limits of the full polar currents.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:closed-pole-compatible-limits}\index{Polar curvature!compatible current limits}
Let $(p_j)$ be normalized functions in $\PSH(X,\omega)$ forming a Cauchy sequence for $\delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}$. For every $\pi\in\mathscr R$, the currents $\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(p_j)$ converge weakly to a unique closed positive current $R_\pi$, with
\[
 R_\pi\wedge\eta_\pi^{n-1}
       =\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu_\pi(p_j)
       \quad\text{in total variation}.
\]
If $g\colon Y_{\pi'}\to Y_\pi$ is a modification with $\pi'=\pi\circ g$ and $\pi,\pi'\in\mathscr R$, then $g_*R_{\pi'}=R_\pi$. There is $h\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ such that every $R_\pi$ is carried coefficientwise by $\{h\circ\pi=-\infty\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\pi$. By \cref{prop:polar-trace-coordinate-completeness}, the measures $\mu_\pi(p_j)$ converge in total variation to a positive measure $\lambda_\pi$. Put
\[
 B_\pi=\bigcup_{j\geq1}\{p_j\circ\pi=-\infty\}.
\]
This is a Borel set of Lebesgue measure zero. Every measure $\mu_\pi(p_j)$ vanishes on its complement, so $\lambda_\pi(Y_\pi\setminus B_\pi)=0$. The polar currents are closed and positive by \cref{cor:closed-pole-trace-recovery}, and their $\eta_\pi$-trace masses are at most $A_\pi$. By compactness of positive currents of bounded mass, a subsequence converges weakly. Its limit $R$ is closed and positive, and $R\wedge\eta_\pi^{n-1}=\lambda_\pi$. Since positivity bounds all coefficient measures by the Hermitian trace, $R$ is carried by $B_\pi$. Any two subsequential limits have the same null carrier and the same trace, so they agree by \cref{lem:closed-null-current-trace}. As every subsequence has a further convergent subsequence, the whole sequence of currents converges weakly to this unique limit $R_\pi$.

Apply \cref{prop:polar-curvature-pushforward} to $g$, with background $\pi^*\omega$ and potential $p_j\circ\pi$. It gives $g_*\operatorname{Pol}_{\pi'}(p_j)=\operatorname{Pol}_\pi(p_j)$. Proper pushforward is weakly continuous, since a smooth test form downstairs pulls back to a smooth test form upstairs, and passing to the limit proves compatibility.

To obtain one pole carrier, put $h_m=\sum_{j=1}^m2^{-j}p_j$. These functions decrease, and they are $\omega$-plurisubharmonic because the coefficients have sum at most one and $\omega>0$. Normalized compactness, \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, bounds their $L^1$ norms uniformly, so \cref{cor:decreasing-limit} gives a limit $h\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. Since all summands are nonpositive, each pole set of a $p_j$ is contained in the pole set of $h$. Hence $B_\pi\subset\{h\circ\pi=-\infty\}$, which gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The common pole carrier does not identify $R_\pi$ with the polar current of $h$. We do not know whether all the limits $R_\pi$ are the polar currents of a single ordinary model.

The topology defined by $\delta^{\mathrm{pol}}_\omega$ also depends on the choice of the Gauduchon forms. On a surface, a Gauduchon form close to the pullback of a K\"ahler form assigns little trace mass to exceptional curves, so choosing the forms independently on the different modifications can make a sequence of moving point poles of fixed strength converge to zero. We compare $\delta^{\mathrm{pol}}_\omega$ with the upper-volume comparison $d_\omega$ and with the sets $\mathcal M_\delta(\omega)$ of ordinary models of lower volume at least $\delta$, both introduced in \cref{def:model-volume-comparison}.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gauduchon-choice-topology-obstruction}\index{Gauduchon metric!choice and model topology}\index{Model potential!moving point poles}
Let $X=\mathbb P^2$ and let $\omega$ be the Fubini--Study form normalized by $\int_X\omega^2=1$. For every set $\mathscr R$ as above and every rational $a\in(0,1)$, there are Gauduchon forms $\eta_\pi$ and ideal models $p_j$ with analytic singularities, whose pole sets are pairwise distinct single points with logarithmic coefficient $a$, such that
\[
 \delta_\omega^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_j,0)\leq\frac1j,
 \qquad d_\omega(p_j,0)=a^2.
\]
All $p_j$ and $0$ belong to $\mathcal M_{(1-a^2)/2}(\omega)$. Thus, for these choices of the Gauduchon forms, $\delta^{\mathrm{pol}}_\omega$ does not induce the topology of \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric} on models of positive volume.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $x=[1:0:0]$, put
\[
 u_x([Z_0:Z_1:Z_2])
   =a\log\frac{|Z_1|^2+|Z_2|^2}
                    {|Z_0|^2+|Z_1|^2+|Z_2|^2}.
\]
The homogeneous quotient defines a global nonpositive potential. Its curvature is positive, since $\log(|Z_1|^2+|Z_2|^2)$ is plurisubharmonic in a local trivialization and the remaining background is $(1-a)\omega$. Its only pole is $x$, where it is $a\log|z|^2+O(1)$. By \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, $p_x=P_\omega[u_x]$ is an ideal model with analytic singularities, ordinarily equivalent to $u_x$. Unitary projective automorphisms preserve $\omega$ and commute with its envelopes for the zero obstacle. Transporting this construction to pairwise distinct points $x_j$, we obtain models $p_j$.

Fix $\pi\colon Y_\pi\to X$ and some Gauduchon form $\gamma_\pi$ on $Y_\pi$. The closed polar current of $p_j\circ\pi$ is supported on the analytic fiber over $x_j$. The divisorial restriction in \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} and the support theorem, \cite[Chapter III, Corollaries 2.11 and 2.14, pp.~141--143]{DemCADG}, give
\[
 \operatorname{Pol}_\pi(p_j)
    =\sum_{E\mapsto x_j}b_{E,j}[E],
 \qquad b_{E,j}=a\operatorname{ord}_E
                         (\mathfrak m_{x_j}\mathcal O_{Y_\pi}),
\]
where the sum runs over the irreducible curves $E\subset Y_\pi$ with $\pi(E)=x_j$. Indeed, \cref{cor:closed-pole-trace-recovery} shows that the polar current is closed, and after the curve components are removed, the remaining closed current of order zero is supported at finitely many points and therefore vanishes. The sum is finite. Every such curve is contracted by $\pi$, so the restriction of $\pi^*\omega$ to it is zero. Put
\[
 C_{\pi,j}=\sum_{E\mapsto x_j}b_{E,j}\int_E\gamma_\pi,
 \qquad J_\pi=\{j:C_{\pi,j}>0\}.
\]
A modification of a surface has finitely many exceptional curves, so there are only finitely many points over which $\pi$ has exceptional curves. Since the points $x_j$ are distinct, $J_\pi$ is finite. Choose $\varepsilon_\pi>0$ with $\varepsilon_\pi C_{\pi,j}\leq1/j$ for every $j\in J_\pi$; if $J_\pi$ is empty, take $\varepsilon_\pi=1$. Now fix
\[
 \eta_\pi=\pi^*\omega+\varepsilon_\pi\gamma_\pi.
\]
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
This form is positive and Gauduchon, since on a surface the Gauduchon equation is $\ddc\eta_\pi=0$. Moreover
\[
 A_\pi=1+\varepsilon_\pi
                  \int_{Y_\pi}\pi^*\omega\wedge\gamma_\pi\geq1,
 \qquad \|\mu_\pi(p_j)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
                  =\varepsilon_\pi C_{\pi,j}\leq\frac1j.
\]
These forms are fixed for the whole sequence, and the estimate holds for every modification. Since $\mu_\pi(0)=0$, taking the supremum gives the asserted convergence.

To compute the singularity volumes, let $\rho\colon Y\to X$ be the blowup at $x$, with exceptional curve $E$, and let $F\colon Y\to\mathbb P^1$ be the morphism resolving the pencil $[Z_1:Z_2]$. Write $\omega_1$ for the normalized Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^1$. Then
\[
 \rho^*\omega+\ddc(u_x\circ\rho)=a[E]+H_a,
 \qquad H_a=(1-a)\rho^*\omega+aF^*\omega_1.
\]
For every $v\sim u_x$, divisorial removal gives $H_a+\ddc w\geq0$, where $w=v\circ\rho-u_x\circ\rho$ is bounded after extension across $E$. The bounded product of \cref{prop:bounded-products} assigns no mass to $E$, so change of variables off $E$ identifies the non-pluripolar mass of $v$ with $\int_Y(H_a+\ddc w)^2$. Since $H_a$ is closed, integration by parts for bounded potentials gives
\[
 \int_Y(H_a+\ddc w)^2=\int_YH_a^2=1-a^2.
\]
For the last equality, note that $H_a$ represents the class $H-aE$, where $H=\rho^*[\omega]$, and that $H^2=1$, $H\cdot E=0$ and $E^2=-1$. Hence both singularity volumes of $p_x$, and by unitary transport those of every $p_j$, are equal to $1-a^2$. The zero model has both volumes equal to one. Since $p_j\leq0$, \cref{def:model-volume-comparison} gives $d_\omega(p_j,0)=1-(1-a^2)=a^2$. This also proves the common positive lower bound for the volumes and the incompatibility of the topologies.
\end{proof}

Thus compatibility with the positive-volume topology, even on analytic models, requires a coordinated choice of the Gauduchon forms. The obstruction in the next subsection is of a different kind: it applies to every choice of the forms, on general ordinary models. The normalization by $A_\pi$ also excludes curve backgrounds of zero area, for which $A_\pi=0$.
