\subsection{Convergence for all obstacles}
\label{subsec:curve-all-obstacle-topology}

Return to the compact curve $Y$ and its positive form $\theta$ of area $A$. The topology $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta$ retains every smooth-obstacle envelope. On a curve these envelopes are still determined by the polar measure, whereas their ideal counterparts use only its atomic part. This gives a countable pseudometric description and lets us compare the strengthened topology with polar total variation.

Choose the countable dense family in \cref{prop:all-obstacle-uniformity} with $F_1=0$. Write $d_{O,F}$ and $d_{I,F}$ for the two envelope distances defining $\tau_{\theta,F}$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-all-obstacle-distance}
 \begin{split}
 d^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta([u],[v])
 &=\|D_u-D_v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
 &\quad+\sum_{k=1}^\infty2^{-k}\bigl(\min\{1,d_{O,F_k}([u],[v])\}\\
 &\qquad\qquad+\min\{1,d_{I,F_k}([u],[v])\}\bigr).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve-all-obstacle-topology}\index{Singularity type!all-obstacle curve pseudometric}
The pseudometric $d^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta$ induces the uniformity of $\tau^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta$. Its zero-distance classes are exactly the classes with the same polar curvature. The closure of analytic classes is $\{[u]:\mu_u=D_u\}=\mathcal G_\theta$. On each canonical ideal locus $\mathcal C_s$, $0\leq s\leq A$, it is a complete metric inducing the total-variation topology. On ordinary models, polar total variation implies convergence for $d^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any smooth $F$, the competitor translation gives
\[
 P_{\theta,F}[u]-F=P_{\theta+\ddc F}[u-F],
\]
and likewise for the ideal envelope. The background on the right is a smooth real form of area $A$, though it may not be positive. We identify these envelopes with the models of $\mu_u$ and $D_u$, respectively, in that background. Set $\mu=\mu_u$ and use its Green potential $w_\mu$. In the new background let $r=P_{\theta+\ddc F}[w_\mu-F]$. The obstacle bounds give $r\sim w_\mu-F$, so $r$ is an ordinary model. Moreover $u-F\preceq r$ and both have mass $A-\mu(Y)$. If this mass is positive, \cref{cor:full-mass-envelope-characterization} identifies their envelopes. If it is zero, their curvature measures both equal $\mu$, and harmonic uniqueness makes them differ by a constant. The ideal assertion follows from ideal projection and the atomic classification. It follows also that the envelope below $F$ is the upper envelope of all $v\leq F$ satisfying $\theta+\ddc v\geq\mu$.

Polar total variation gives $L^1$ convergence of these models for every fixed $F$, by \cref{prop:curve-arbitrary-background-models}. The same assertion for atomic measures supplies continuity of every ideal-envelope coordinate in the atomic total-variation norm. The controlled ideal relations are uniformly implied by atomic total variation: for fixed $t>0$, $a>1$, choosing $\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<(a-1)/(at)$ guarantees both inclusions in \eqref{eq:controlled-ideal-entourage} by the local floor formula. Indeed, if $tE(\{p\})\geq1$, this bound gives $aD(\{p\})>E(\{p\})$; otherwise the target ideal is the unit ideal. Interchange $D,E$ for the other inclusion. Each divisorial coordinate is atomic total variation. The two envelope terms in the summable series, together with the atomic term, therefore generate exactly the all-obstacle uniformity: finitely many coordinates control the sum up to its uniformly small tail, and the sum controls each fixed coordinate.

The zero-obstacle classification shows that zero distance forces equal polar measures; the classification at every obstacle proves the converse. The same classification identifies equality of the ordinary and ideal envelopes at every $F$ with $\mu_u=D_u$. Apply \cref{thm:all-obstacle-analytic-closure} to obtain the analytic closure.

For a $d^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta$-Cauchy sequence in $\mathcal C_s$, its atomic measures have a total-variation limit of mass at most $s$, which is again atomic. The model convergence above gives convergence of every obstacle coordinate; the summable tail then gives convergence for $d^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta$. The converse implication to atomic total variation follows from the first summand. This proves completeness and equality of topologies on $\mathcal C_s$. Polar total variation controls the atomic part and all the ordinary-envelope coordinates, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The relative envelope class has the same curve criterion, including the zero-mass ideal models.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:curve-relative-envelope-criterion}\index{Relative envelope class!polar criterion on a curve}\index{Relative envelope class!zero-mass curve model}
For $u\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ put $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. The following are equivalent:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\theta,q),\qquad \mu_u=D_u,\\
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\\
 P_{\theta,F}[u]=P_{\theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
       \quad\text{for every smooth real }F.
 \end{gathered}
\]
This includes $D_u(Y)=A$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The ordinary and ideal classifications in \cref{lem:curve-polar-model,lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model} identify zero-obstacle equality with $\mu_u=D_u$. \Cref{prop:curve-all-obstacle-topology} identifies this with equality at every smooth obstacle. Relative-envelope membership implies the latter by \cref{prop:ideal-obstacle-reprojection}.

Conversely, assume $\mu_u=D_u$. The atomic-model lemma gives $\mu_q=D_u$. Ordinary equivalence preserves polar curvature by \cref{cor:polar-order-maximum}, so Stokes gives
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)
 =\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(q)=A-D_u(Y).
\]
If $D_u(Y)<A$, zero-obstacle equality and \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization} put $u$ in the stated relative class.

If $D_u(Y)=A$, the positive curvature measures $T_u,T_q$ both dominate $D_u$ and both have total mass $A$. Hence $T_u=T_q=D_u$. The distribution $u-q$ is harmonic and therefore constant on the compact connected curve. Canonical representatives give $u=q+C$, so \cref{prop:relative-elementary} gives relative-envelope membership also in this case.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve-all-obstacle-incomplete}
For the probability Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^1$, $d^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta$ is incomplete on the whole ordinary-type space, even on ordinary models of one fixed positive mass with trivial ideals. Its topology on ordinary models is strictly weaker than polar total variation and strictly stronger than $\tau_{\theta,0}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the diffuse polar probabilities $\sigma_j$ concentrating at $p$ and the models $p_j=p_{c\sigma_j}$ from \cref{prop:curve-ordinary-topology-incomplete}, with $0<c<1$. Fix a smooth obstacle $F$ and put $q_{y,F}=P_{\theta,F}[q_{c\delta_y}]$. Rotations preserving $\theta$ and sending $p$ to $y$ can be chosen continuously near $p$. They transport the obstacle to its composition with the rotation. The uniform obstacle bound and continuity of rotations in $L^1$ consequently give $q_{y,F}\to q_{p,F}$ in $L^1$ as $y\to p$.

The uniform $L^1$ bound near $p$ makes the average $w_{j,F}=\int q_{y,F}\,\mathrm d\sigma_j(y)$ well-defined. It lies below $F$, its curvature dominates $c\sigma_j$, and it converges to $q_{p,F}$ in $L^1$. The obstacle characterization in the proof of \cref{prop:curve-all-obstacle-topology} gives $w_{j,F}\leq P_{\theta,F}[p_j]\leq F$. These bounds and normalized compactness give $L^1$ precompactness. Any cluster limit of the envelopes has curvature at least $c\delta_p$, so is at most $q_{p,F}$. The lower bound gives the reverse inequality. Hence $P_{\theta,F}[p_j]\to q_{p,F}$ for every $F$. Their ideal envelopes are all $P_\theta(F)$, and their atomic parts vanish. Thus $[p_j]$ is $d^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta$-Cauchy. A limit would also be a $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$ limit, which the cited proposition excludes.

For strict comparison with polar variation, use the rotated diffuse model $\widetilde p_j=p\circ R_j^{-1}$ from the end of that proof. For every smooth $F$,
\[
 P_{\theta,F}[\widetilde p_j]
   =P_{\theta,F\circ R_j}[p]\circ R_j^{-1}.
\]
The obstacle bound and $L^1$ continuity of rotations give convergence to $P_{\theta,F}[p]$. All atomic parts vanish, so $d^{\mathrm{all}}_\theta([\widetilde p_j],[p])\to0$, whereas their polar total-variation distance remains $2c$. \Cref{prop:curve-all-obstacle-topology} supplies continuity in the other direction.

Finally, the sequence in \cref{prop:ordinary-topology-gauge-failure} converges for the zero obstacle and fails to converge for a smooth obstacle. Replacing its members by their ordinary models preserves every obstacle envelope by the curve classification, so the strict comparison already holds on ordinary models.
\end{proof}
