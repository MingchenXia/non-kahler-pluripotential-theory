\chapter{Smooth Monge--Amp\`ere equations}
\label{chap:smooth-equations}

Throughout this chapter, $\omega$ is a Hermitian form on $X$ and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive volume form. Given $F\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$, we seek a smooth real potential $u$ and a scalar $b\in\mathbb R$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-ma}
 \omega_u^n=\mathrm{e}^{F+b}\mathrm{d}V,\qquad \omega_u>0.
\end{equation}
Here $\omega_u=\omega+\ddc u$, and adding a constant to $u$ does not change the equation. For a closed background, integration determines $b$ from the prescribed density. For a Hermitian background, the total mass can depend on $u$, so the potential and the scalar must be found together. The adjoint probability measure of \cref{prop:adjoint-gauduchon} gives the compatibility condition for the linearized problem. Weighted integration and a trace estimate give the compactness needed to deform solutions from the background density by the continuity method, and the same adjoint probability governs the variation of $b$ with $F$.

For $n\geq2$, prescribing the volume form of a Gauduchon metric requires a different variation of the metric: we deform its $(n-1)$st power within its Aeppli class. The resulting equation contains first-order torsion terms with a specific matrix structure. This structure makes the second-derivative estimate possible, and a rescaling argument reduces the gradient bound to a Liouville theorem on $\mathbb C^n$.

\section{Scalar bounds and uniqueness}

Evaluating the equation at the extrema of the potential bounds the normalization scalar in terms of the prescribed data. Applied to the difference of two solutions, the same argument shows that their scalars agree, and ellipticity then determines the potential up to a constant. The scalar bounds will also give uniform control along the continuity path.

\begin{theorem}[Tosatti--Weinkove]\label{thm:tw-smooth}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!smooth data}
For every smooth real $F$, \eqref{eq:smooth-ma} has a unique pair $(u,b)\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)\times\mathbb R$ with $\sup_Xu=0$.
\end{theorem}

In dimension one, integration determines
$\mathrm{e}^b=\int_X\omega/\int_X\mathrm{e}^F \mathrm{d}V$, and the remaining equation for $u$ is a Poisson equation, which is uniquely solvable up to a constant because its right-hand side has the correct integral; positivity of $\omega_u$ is automatic. Thus the theorem holds in that dimension, and we prove it for $n\geq2$ at the end of \cref{sec:smooth-continuity-method}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:b-extrema}\index{Normalization scalar!extremum bounds}
Every solution of \eqref{eq:smooth-ma} satisfies
\[
 \inf_X\!\left(\log\frac{\omega^n}{\mathrm{d}V}-F\right)
 \leq b\leq
 \sup_X\!\left(\log\frac{\omega^n}{\mathrm{d}V}-F\right).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a maximum point $x_+$ of $u$, the real $(1,1)$-form $\ddc u$ is nonpositive, so $0<\omega_u\leq\omega$ there. Hence $\omega_u^n(x_+)\leq\omega^n(x_+)$, and
$b\leq\log(\omega^n/\mathrm{d}V)(x_+)-F(x_+)$.
At a minimum point the reverse argument gives the lower bound.
\end{proof}

When $\mathrm{d}\omega=0$, integration gives the familiar explicit formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-b}
 b=\log\frac{\int_X\omega^n}{\int_X\mathrm{e}^F \mathrm{d}V}.
\end{equation}
The same formula holds whenever the mass-invariance criterion of \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization} is satisfied. In general, integrating the equation gives only
\[
 \mathrm{e}^b=\frac{\Mass_\omega(u)}{\int_X\mathrm{e}^F \mathrm{d}V},
\]
where $\Mass_\omega(u)=\int_X\omega_u^n$ is the mass of \cref{thm:mass-expansion}; the numerator depends on the unknown potential.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:smooth-uniqueness}\index{Uniqueness!smooth positive equation}
Suppose $(u,b)$ and $(v,c)$ are smooth solutions of \eqref{eq:smooth-ma} with the same $F$ and $\mathrm{d}V$. Then $b=c$ and $u-v$ is constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a maximum of $u-v$, one has $\omega_u\leq\omega_v$, so the equations give $\mathrm{e}^b\leq \mathrm{e}^c$. At a minimum one obtains $\mathrm{e}^b\geq \mathrm{e}^c$. Therefore $b=c$.

Put $h=u-v$ and $\eta_t=\omega_v+t\ddc h=(1-t)\omega_v+t\omega_u$ for $0\leq t\leq1$. These forms are strictly positive. Subtracting the logarithms of the two equations and writing the difference as the integral of its derivative along the segment, we obtain
\[
 0=\int_0^1 n\,\frac{\ddc h\wedge\eta_t^{n-1}}{\eta_t^n}\,\mathrm{d}t.
\]
The right-hand side is a linear second-order operator applied to $h$. Its coefficients are smooth, its principal part is uniformly elliptic on the compact manifold, and it has no zeroth-order term. The strong maximum principle therefore implies that $h$ is constant.
\end{proof}

\section{The potential bound from weighted integration}

We now show that an upper bound for the density controls the normalized potential, following the method of Cherrier. Integrating the equation against $\mathrm{e}^{-pu}$ produces a gradient term and mixed weighted masses; a finite recurrence absorbs the latter together with the torsion error. Moser iteration then gives a set of uniformly positive volume near the minimum, and normalized $L^1$ compactness bounds that minimum. Norms below are computed with respect to the fixed metric $\omega$.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pointwise-torsion-estimate}
There is a constant $C$ depending on $\omega$ such that, for every positive $(1,1)$-form $\eta$, every smooth real $f$, every $\delta>0$, and $0\leq k\leq n-2$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 |\dc f\wedge \mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\eta^k\wedge\omega^{n-k-2}|
 \leq{}&\frac C\delta\,\mathrm{d}f\wedge\dc f\wedge\eta^k\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}\\
 &+C\delta\,\eta^k\wedge\omega^{n-k}.
 \end{split}
\]
Here the absolute value of a top-degree form is the absolute value of its coefficient relative to the positive volume form $\omega^n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a given point, choose an $\omega$-unitary frame in which $\eta$ is diagonal, with positive eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$. Expand $\eta^k$ as a sum over $k$-element subsets $I$ of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, with coefficients that are positive dimensional multiples of $\lambda_I=\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_i$. After wedging with $\dc f\wedge \mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-k-2}$, each surviving term is a bounded coefficient of $\mathrm{d}\omega$ times $\lambda_I$ times one first derivative $f_a$ with $a\notin I$. Young's inequality bounds its absolute value by
\[
 C\lambda_I|f_a|\leq (C/\delta)\lambda_I|f_a|^2
                              +C\delta\lambda_I.
\]
The sum of the first terms is bounded by a fixed multiple of the first form on the right of the assertion, whose diagonal expansion consists of the terms $\lambda_I|f_a|^2$ with $a\notin I$. The second form on the right contains all the $\lambda_I$. Since there are finitely many indices and $\mathrm{d}\omega$ is bounded, the constant $C$ is uniform.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cherrier-gradient}
Suppose $u$ is smooth, $\eta=\omega+\ddc u>0$, and
$\eta^n\leq\Lambda\omega^n$ for a fixed $\Lambda>0$. There are constants $p_0\geq1$ and $C$ depending only on $\omega$ and $\Lambda$ such that
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{e}^{-pu/2})\wedge\dc(\mathrm{e}^{-pu/2})\wedge\omega^{n-1}
 \leq Cp\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pu}\omega^n\quad(p\geq p_0).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed real $p\geq1$, define $M_k$ for $0\leq k\leq n$ and $J_k$ for $0\leq k\leq n-1$ by
\[
 M_k=\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pu}\eta^k\wedge\omega^{n-k},\qquad
 J_k=\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pu}\mathrm{d}u\wedge\dc u\wedge\eta^k\wedge\omega^{n-k-1},
 \quad J=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}J_k.
\]
All these numbers are nonnegative. The proof first bounds $pJ$ in terms of the masses $M_k$, and then bounds these masses by a recurrence. Let
$T=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\eta^k\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}$, so that $\eta^n-\omega^n=\ddc u\wedge T$. Since $\mathrm{d}\eta=\mathrm{d}\omega$, differentiation gives
\[
 \mathrm{d}T=n\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\eta^k\wedge\omega^{n-k-2}\wedge \mathrm{d}\omega.
\]
The sum is empty when $n=1$. Stokes' theorem gives the exact identity
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pu}(\eta^n-\omega^n)
 &=\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pu}\ddc u\wedge T\\
 &=pJ+\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pu}\dc u\wedge \mathrm{d}T.
 \end{split}
\]
The density assumption bounds the left-hand side above by $(\Lambda+1)M_0$. Apply \cref{lem:pointwise-torsion-estimate} to the last integral, with $\delta=K/p$ for a sufficiently large fixed $K$. The contribution proportional to $J/\delta$ is then at most $pJ/2$, and after absorbing it we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-first-bound}
 pJ\leq CM_0+\frac Cp\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}M_k.
\end{equation}
This estimate alone does not suffice, because the mixed weighted masses on the right have not yet been bounded.

For $1\leq k\leq n-2$, a second integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-recurrence}
 M_k-M_{k-1}=pJ_{k-1}+R_k,
 \qquad
 R_k=\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pu}\dc u\wedge
                \mathrm{d}(\eta^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}).
\end{equation}
The derivative in $R_k$ has two terms, with coefficients $k-1$ and $n-k$. Applying the pointwise estimate with $\delta=1/p$ to each of them, and omitting terms with negative indices, we obtain
\[
 |R_k|\leq Cp(J_{k-2}+J_{k-1})
                   +\frac Cp(M_{k-2}+M_{k-1}),
 \qquad M_{-1}=J_{-1}=0.
\]
Consequently, for $p\geq1$,
\[
 M_k\leq C(M_{k-1}+M_{k-2}+pJ).
\]
Induction over the finitely many values of $k$ proves
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{n-2}M_k\leq C(M_0+pJ).
\]
For $n=2$ this is immediate, and for $n=1$ the sum is empty. Inserting this bound in \eqref{eq:weighted-first-bound} gives
$pJ\leq CM_0+C'J$. For $p\geq2C'$ the last term can be absorbed, and $pJ\leq CM_0$.

Finally,
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{e}^{-pu/2})\wedge\dc(\mathrm{e}^{-pu/2})\wedge\omega^{n-1}
 =\frac{p^2}{4}J_0\leq\frac{p^2}{4}J\leq CpM_0.
\]
This proves the claimed estimate for all $p\geq p_0$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:smooth-czero}\index{A priori estimate!potential bound}
For each $\Lambda>0$ there is $C_\Lambda$ such that every smooth $u$ with
\[
 \omega+\ddc u>0,\qquad \sup_Xu=0,\qquad
 (\omega+\ddc u)^n\leq\Lambda\omega^n
\]
satisfies $\|u\|_\infty\leq C_\Lambda$.
\end{theorem}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathrm{d}\mu=\omega^n/\int_X\omega^n$. The Sobolev inequality for the Riemannian metric of $\omega$ gives, for some $\beta>1$ and every smooth real function $w$,
\[
 \left(\int_X |w|^{2\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/\beta}
 \leq C\left(\int_X\mathrm{d}w\wedge\dc w\wedge\omega^{n-1}
                         +\int_X|w|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right).
\]
We may take $\beta=n/(n-1)$ if $n\geq2$, and for example $\beta=2$ if $n=1$. Choose $p_0$ as in \cref{lem:cherrier-gradient}. Applying the Sobolev inequality to $w=\mathrm{e}^{-pu/2}$ and using that lemma, we obtain, with $Z=\mathrm{e}^{-u}$,
\[
 \|Z\|_{L^{p\beta}(\mu)}\leq(Cp)^{1/p}\|Z\|_{L^p(\mu)}
 \quad(p\geq p_0).
\]
Iterating with $p=p_0\beta^j$, $j=0,1,2,\ldots$, we obtain a bound for $\sup_XZ$; the product of the constants is finite because
$\sum_j(1+\log(p_0\beta^j))/(p_0\beta^j)<\infty$. Hence, for a uniform $C_1\geq1$ and $m=\inf_Xu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-minimum-integral}
 \mathrm{e}^{-p_0m}\leq C_1\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-p_0u}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side still involves the unknown potential. Divide by $\mathrm{e}^{-p_0m}$ and set $A=p_0^{-1}\log(2C_1)$. The integrand $\mathrm{e}^{-p_0(u-m)}$ is at most one everywhere and at most $(2C_1)^{-1}$ outside $S=\{u\leq m+A\}$. Therefore \eqref{eq:near-minimum-integral} implies
\[
 \mu(S)\geq\frac1{2C_1}=:\delta>0.
\]
Since $\sup_Xu=0$, \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives $\int_X(-u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq C_0$. On $S$ we have $-u\geq-m-A$, so
\[
 C_0\geq\int_X(-u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\delta(-m-A)
\]
when $-m\geq A$; otherwise the desired bound is immediate. Thus $-m\leq A+C_0/\delta$, proving the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{The linearized equation and smooth dependence}

Recall from \eqref{eq:linearized-ma} that the linearization of $u\mapsto\log(\omega_u^n/\mathrm{d}V)$ at $u$ is $L_uv=n\,\ddc v\wedge\omega_u^{n-1}/\omega_u^n$. This operator annihilates constants, and by \cref{prop:adjoint-gauduchon} its range consists of the functions with zero mean under the adjoint probability measure $\mu_u$. Including the scalar $b$ as an unknown removes this obstruction. In dimension one, $\mu_u=\omega_u/\int_X\omega_u$, and the compatibility condition follows from Stokes' theorem.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
The normalization $\sup_Xu=0$ is convenient for compactness, but it need not depend differentiably on the data. To differentiate solutions, we use instead the linear normalization
\[
 \int_Xu\,\mathrm{d}V=0.
\]
Passing from one normalization to the other only adds a constant to $u$ and leaves $b$ unchanged.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:smooth-dependence}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!smooth dependence}
With this linear normalization, the solution $(u,b)$ of \eqref{eq:smooth-ma} depends smoothly on $F$ in H\"older spaces, and hence smoothly on the parameters of every smooth finite-dimensional family of smooth data.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix an integer $k\geq0$ and a real exponent $0<\alpha<1$. Consider the map
\[
 (u,b,F)\longmapsto
 \log\frac{(\omega+\ddc u)^n}{\mathrm{d}V}-F-b
\]
on the open set of potentials in $C^{k+2,\alpha}$ with $\omega_u>0$, restricted to $\int_Xu\,\mathrm{d}V=0$. Its derivative in $(u,b)$ is
\[
 (v,a)\longmapsto L_uv-a.
\]
Given $h\in C^{k,\alpha}$, integrating $L_uv-a=h$ against $\mu_u$ forces
$a=-\int_Xh\,\mathrm{d}\mu_u$. For this value, the equation for $v$ satisfies the compatibility condition of \cref{prop:adjoint-gauduchon}, and it has a unique solution with zero $\mathrm{d}V$-mean. Schauder estimates show that this inverse is bounded. The implicit function theorem in Banach spaces therefore gives a smooth local solution map around any given solution; this step does not use the global existence theorem. Uniqueness identifies the local maps wherever they overlap, and elliptic regularity allows arbitrary $k$ for smooth data.
\end{proof}

\section{The continuity method}
\label{sec:smooth-continuity-method}

We join the background density to the prescribed one by a path of equations. The local inverse of \cref{prop:smooth-dependence} shows that the set of solvable parameters is open. The extremum bound for the scalar, the potential estimate and the derivative estimates give compactness along the path, and uniform ellipticity preserves positivity at a limiting parameter.

\begin{proof}[of \cref{thm:tw-smooth}]
The case $n=1$ was treated after the statement of the theorem. For $n\geq2$ put
$G=F+\log(\mathrm{d}V/\omega^n)$ and consider
\[
 (\omega+\ddc u_t)^n=\mathrm{e}^{tG+b_t}\omega^n,
 \qquad\sup_Xu_t=0,\qquad0\leq t\leq1.
\]
At $t=0$, take $u_0=0$ and $b_0=0$. The local implicit-function argument in \cref{prop:smooth-dependence}, with the additive constant temporarily fixed by a linear mean, shows that the set of solvable parameters is open.

At every solvable parameter, \cref{prop:b-extrema} gives
\[
 -\sup_X(tG)\leq b_t\leq-\inf_X(tG),
 \qquad \mathrm{e}^{tG+b_t}\leq \mathrm{e}^{\operatorname{osc}_XG}.
\]
The constants $b_t$ are therefore uniformly bounded, and \cref{thm:smooth-czero} bounds $u_t$ in $C^0$. By \cref{cor:classical-higher-estimate}, the solutions are bounded in every $C^k$ norm. Along any sequence of solvable parameters converging in $[0,1]$, a subsequence of $(u_t,b_t)$ therefore converges smoothly to a solution; uniform equivalence of the metrics preserves strict positivity, and the supremum normalization passes to the limit. Thus the solvable set is closed. Since it is nonempty, open and closed, connectedness of $[0,1]$ gives a solution at $t=1$, and \cref{prop:smooth-uniqueness} gives uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\section{The smooth exponential equation}

When the logarithmic density contains a positive multiple of the potential, the extrema of the potential are bounded directly, and the linearization acquires a negative zeroth-order term. The continuity method then needs neither an additive normalization nor an extra scalar unknown.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:smooth-proper-comparison}\label{prop:exp-smooth-unique}
Let $\lambda>0$, let $F$ be smooth, and let $v,w$ be smooth strictly $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions. If
\[
 \omega_v^n\geq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda v+F}\omega^n,
 \qquad \omega_w^n\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda w+F}\omega^n,
\]
then $v\leq w$. In particular, two smooth solutions coincide.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that $v-w$ has a positive maximum at a point $x$. Then $\omega_v(x)\leq\omega_w(x)$, and taking determinants and applying the two assumptions gives
$\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v(x)+F(x)}\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda w(x)+F(x)}$, which contradicts $v(x)>w(x)$. For two solutions, apply the comparison in both orders.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:smooth-proper-existence}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!smooth exponential equation}
For every $\lambda>0$ and every smooth real $F$, there is a unique smooth $u$ with $\omega_u>0$ such that
\[
 \omega_u^n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u+F}\omega^n.
\]
The solution satisfies
\[
 -\lambda^{-1}\sup_XF\leq u\leq-\lambda^{-1}\inf_XF.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a minimum of $u$, the equation gives $\lambda u+F\geq0$; at a maximum it gives $\lambda u+F\leq0$. These inequalities prove the stated bounds.

Consider the equations
\[
 \omega_{u_t}^n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_t+tF}\omega^n,\qquad0\leq t\leq1.
\]
At $t=0$, $u_0=0$ is a solution. The linearization in $u$ is $L_u-\lambda$, whose kernel is zero by the maximum principle. Its Fredholm index is zero: deform its principal coefficients to those of a fixed Laplacian while keeping the negative zeroth-order term; the latter operator is invertible, and the index is constant along this deformation of elliptic operators. Thus $L_u-\lambda$ is an isomorphism between the usual H\"older spaces, with bounded inverse by Schauder theory, and the implicit function theorem proves that the set of solvable parameters is open.

The extremum bounds give $\|u_t\|_\infty\leq\lambda^{-1}\|F\|_\infty$. The remark following \cref{cor:classical-higher-estimate} then gives uniform ellipticity and bounds in every $C^k$ norm. Along any convergent sequence of solvable parameters, a subsequence of the solutions therefore converges smoothly to a strictly positive solution at the limit parameter. This proves closedness, and hence existence at $t=1$. Uniqueness is \cref{prop:exp-smooth-unique}.
\end{proof}

For a fixed smooth real obstacle $f$ on $X$ and a parameter $\beta>0$, the choice $\lambda=\beta$ and $F=-\beta f$ gives
\[
 \omega_{u_\beta}^n=\mathrm{e}^{\beta(u_\beta-f)}\omega^n.
\]
These equations penalize values above the obstacle. In \cref{prop:smooth-obstacle-support} we use them to construct the envelope of $f$ and to show that its Monge--Amp\`ere measure is supported on the contact set. The convergence argument there uses only bounded regularization and the comparison just proved.

\section{Variation of the normalization scalar}

Integration against the adjoint probability measure eliminates the derivatives of the potential from the first variation of $b$. In the second variation, the second derivative of the logarithmic determinant contributes a nonnegative square with a minus sign. This gives concavity of $b$ and identifies the equality case.

Write $b(F)$ for the scalar in \eqref{eq:smooth-ma}, and use the linear normalization $\int_Xu\,\mathrm{d}V=0$. For a smooth family $F_t$, with $t$ in an open interval $I\subset\mathbb R$, write $(u_t,b_t)$ for the corresponding solutions and put $\eta_t=\omega_{u_t}$. By \cref{prop:smooth-dependence} these depend smoothly on $t$; a dot denotes differentiation in $t$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:b-derivatives}\index{Normalization scalar!smooth variation}
Let $A_t=\eta_t^{-1}\ddc\dot u_t$ be the endomorphism of the tangent bundle that represents the real $(1,1)$-form $\ddc\dot u_t$ with respect to $\eta_t$; it is Hermitian with respect to $\eta_t$. The normalization constant satisfies
\begin{align}
 \dot b_t&=-\int_X\dot F_t\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{u_t},\label{eq:b-first}\\
 \ddot b_t&=-\int_X\ddot F_t\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{u_t}
 -\int_X\operatorname{tr}(A_t^2)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{u_t}.\label{eq:b-second}
\end{align}
In particular, for smooth real functions $F_0,G$ on $X$ and the affine family $F_t=F_0+tG$, the function $b_t$ is concave. Its second derivative vanishes at a parameter value if and only if $G$ is constant on $X$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating the logarithm of \eqref{eq:smooth-ma} gives
\[
 L_{u_t}\dot u_t=\dot F_t+\dot b_t.
\]
Integration against $\mu_{u_t}$ proves \eqref{eq:b-first}.

For a smooth family of positive Hermitian matrices $H_t$, differentiation of the inverse matrix gives
\[
 \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2}\log\det H_t
 =\operatorname{tr}(H_t^{-1}\ddot H_t)
  -\operatorname{tr}\bigl((H_t^{-1}\dot H_t)^2\bigr).
\]
Apply this pointwise to the matrices of $\eta_t$, using $\dot\eta_t=\ddc\dot u_t$ and $\ddot\eta_t=\ddc\ddot u_t$. The second derivative of the logarithmic equation is
\[
 L_{u_t}\ddot u_t-\operatorname{tr}(A_t^2)
 =\ddot F_t+\ddot b_t.
\]
Integrating against $\mu_{u_t}$ at this fixed parameter proves \eqref{eq:b-second}; there is no need to differentiate the measure.

The eigenvalues of the Hermitian endomorphism $A_t$ are real, so $\operatorname{tr}(A_t^2)\geq0$. For affine $F_t$ we have $\ddot F_t=0$, so the second derivative of $b_t$ is nonpositive. If it vanishes, strict positivity of the smooth density of $\mu_{u_t}$ implies $A_t=0$ everywhere. Thus $\ddc\dot u_t=0$, and the maximum principle shows that $\dot u_t$ is constant. The first differentiated equation then gives $G=-\dot b_t$, a constant. Conversely, if $G$ is constant, the solution metric is independent of $t$ and $b_t=b_0-tG$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:b-lipschitz}
For two smooth functions $F,G$, the normalization constants satisfy
\[
 -\sup_X(G-F)\leq b(G)-b(F)\leq-\inf_X(G-F).
\]
In particular, $|b(G)-b(F)|\leq\|G-F\|_\infty$. If $G-F$ is nonconstant, both displayed inequalities are strict.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:b-first} along $F_t=F+t(G-F)$ and integrate over $0\leq t\leq1$. Each $\mu_{u_t}$ is a probability measure with strictly positive smooth density, so the integral of a nonconstant continuous function against it lies strictly between the minimum and the maximum of that function.
\end{proof}

Thus $b$ is a decreasing, concave, Lipschitz functional of the logarithmic density, with $b(F+a)=b(F)-a$ for every real constant $a$. By uniform approximation, it extends uniquely to a Lipschitz functional of continuous $F$. This extension concerns the scalar functional only; the existence and regularity of corresponding potentials require further estimates.

\section{Normalization on the Hopf threefold}
\label{sec:hopf-normalization}

On the Hopf threefold of \cref{chap:hopf}, the deviation of $b$ from the closed-background formula \eqref{eq:closed-b} can be computed: it is the energy of the solution along the elliptic fibers. In this section, $X$ is that threefold and $\omega$ its Gauduchon metric; we use the projection $\pi:X\to\mathbb P^2$, the probability measure $\nu=\omega^3/6$ and the averaging operator $A$ of \cref{prop:hopf-fiber-average}, and we write $\beta=\omega-\pi^*\omega_{\FS}$ for the rank-one form of that construction. As in \cref{chap:hopf}, the fiber derivative $u_w$ does not depend on the local trivialization of the elliptic bundle.

For $F\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$, let $(u_F,b(F))$ be the solution of
\[
 (\omega+\ddc u_F)^3=\mathrm{e}^{F+b(F)}\omega^3,
 \qquad \omega+\ddc u_F>0,\qquad \int_Xu_F\,\mathrm{d}\nu=0.
\]
Define
\[
 D(F)=b(F)+\log\int_X\mathrm{e}^F\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\]
For a closed background, the corresponding quantity vanishes by \eqref{eq:closed-b}.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hopf-normalization-defect}\index{Hopf manifold!normalization defect}
For every smooth real $F$ on this threefold,
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{e}^{D(F)}&=1+\int_X|(u_F)_w|^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu,
 \label{eq:hopf-normalization-energy}\\
 \|u_F-Au_F\|_{L^2(X,\nu)}^2
 &\leq4\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{D(F)}-1\bigr).
 \label{eq:hopf-normalization-average}
\end{align}
Thus $D(F)\geq0$, with equality if and only if $F=\pi^*H$ for some $H\in C^\infty(\mathbb P^2,\mathbb R)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integration of the equation and \eqref{eq:hopf-mass} give
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{D(F)}=\frac16\int_X(\omega+\ddc u_F)^3
                  =1+\int_X|(u_F)_w|^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\]
Substituting the first equality into \eqref{eq:hopf-average-mass} proves \eqref{eq:hopf-normalization-average}.

If $D(F)=0$, then $(u_F)_w=0$ everywhere, because $u_F$ is smooth. Since $u_F$ is real, it is constant on each connected fiber, so it descends to a smooth real function $q$ on $\mathbb P^2$, and $\omega_{\FS}+\ddc q>0$ as in the proof of \cref{prop:hopf-fiber-average}. Since $\beta^2=0$ and $\omega=\pi^*\omega_{\FS}+\beta$, we have
\[
 (\omega+\ddc\pi^*q)^3
       =3\pi^*(\omega_{\FS}+\ddc q)^2\wedge\beta.
\]
Dividing by $\omega^3=3\pi^*\omega_{\FS}^2\wedge\beta$ and taking logarithms proves that $F$ is a smooth base pullback.

Conversely, suppose $F=\pi^*H$. Apply \cref{thm:tw-smooth} on $(\mathbb P^2,\omega_{\FS})$ to obtain a smooth real $q$ and a scalar $c$ with
\[
 \omega_{\FS}+\ddc q>0,\qquad
 (\omega_{\FS}+\ddc q)^2=\mathrm{e}^{H+c}\omega_{\FS}^2.
\]
Since the base form is closed, Stokes' theorem gives $\mathrm{e}^c\int_{\mathbb P^2}\mathrm{e}^H\omega_{\FS}^2=1$. The preceding cube identity shows that $\pi^*q$, after subtraction of its $\nu$-mean, solves the equation on $X$ with scalar $c$, and uniqueness gives $b(F)=c$. Since the base measure of $\nu$ is $\omega_{\FS}^2$, it follows that $D(F)=0$.
\end{proof}

In particular, if a smooth positive density $f$ satisfies $\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}\nu=1$, the equation $(\omega+\ddc u)^3=\mathrm{e}^b f\omega^3$ has $b\geq0$, with equality precisely when $f$ comes from the base. Fixing the total integral of the density therefore does not determine the scalar on this non-closed background.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hopf-normalization-hessian}
Let $G\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$, let $L_0v=3\,\ddc v\wedge\omega^2/\omega^3$ be the linearized operator at $u=0$, and let $v$ be the unique smooth real function satisfying
\[
 L_0v=G-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu,
 \qquad \int_Xv\,\mathrm{d}\nu=0.
\]
Then
\[
 \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}D(tG)\right|_{t=0}=0,
 \qquad
 \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2}D(tG)\right|_{t=0}
       =2\int_X|v_w|^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\]
The second derivative vanishes if and only if $G$ is a smooth base pullback.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For real $t$ near zero put $u_t=u_{tG}$ and $b_t=b(tG)$. By \cref{prop:smooth-dependence}, this is a smooth family, with $u_0=b_0=0$. Differentiating the logarithmic equation at $t=0$ gives $L_0\dot u_0=G+\dot b_0$. Since $\omega$ is Gauduchon, $\nu$ is the adjoint probability measure of $L_0$, and integration gives $\dot b_0=-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu$. Hence $\dot u_0=v$; this also proves the existence and uniqueness of $v$.

Smooth dependence gives $(u_t)_w=t v_w+O(t^2)$ uniformly on $X$, so \eqref{eq:hopf-normalization-energy} gives
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{D(tG)}=1+t^2\int_X|v_w|^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu+O(t^3).
\]
Taking logarithms proves the derivative formulas.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
If the second derivative vanishes, then $v_w=0$, so $v=\pi^*q$ for a smooth real function $q$ on the base. Expanding $\omega^2=\pi^*\omega_{\FS}^2+2\pi^*\omega_{\FS}\wedge\beta$ gives
\[
 L_0(\pi^*q)=\pi^*\!\left(
       \frac{2\ddc q\wedge\omega_{\FS}}{\omega_{\FS}^2}\right).
\]
Hence $G=L_0v+\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu$ is a base pullback. Conversely, when $G$ is a base pullback, \cref{prop:hopf-normalization-defect} gives $D(tG)=0$ for every real $t$.
\end{proof}

This is a second-variation formula at the background density only. It does not determine whether $D$ is convex globally, because the second derivatives of $b$ and of the logarithmic integral have opposite signs.

\section{Prescribing a Gauduchon volume form}

We now return to a general connected compact complex manifold $X$, of dimension $n\geq2$. To stay in a fixed Aeppli class of Gauduchon metrics, we vary the $(n-1)$st power of the metric by a term of the form $\partial\xi+\bar\partial\bar\xi$. The resulting scalar ansatz contains first derivatives of the potential as well as its complex Hessian.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Sz\'ekelyhidi--Tosatti--Weinkove]
\label{thm:stw-volume}\index{Gauduchon metric!prescribed volume}
Fix a Hermitian form $\gamma_0$, a Gauduchon form $\gamma$, and a smooth real function $F$. There is a unique pair $(u,b)\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)\times\mathbb R$ with $\sup_Xu=0$ such that the $(n-1,n-1)$-form
\[
 \Gamma_u=\gamma_0^{n-1}+\ddc u\wedge\gamma^{n-2}
 +\operatorname{Re}\left(
     \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial u\wedge\bar\partial(\gamma^{n-2})
   \right)
\]
is positive, and the Hermitian form $\eta_u$ with $\eta_u^{n-1}=\Gamma_u$ satisfies
\[
 \eta_u^n=\mathrm{e}^{F+b}\gamma^n.
\]
\end{theorem}

Here we use the linear-algebra fact that a real $(n-1,n-1)$-form is strictly positive if and only if it is the $(n-1)$st power of a Hermitian form, and that this Hermitian form is unique; the matrix form of this correspondence is recalled in \cref{sec:stw-matrix-structure}. Keep $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma$ fixed for the remainder of the chapter, and write $\mathcal D_\gamma u=\Gamma_u-\gamma_0^{n-1}$ for the part of the ansatz that is linear in $u$.\index{Gauduchon metric!Aeppli ansatz}

To see which geometry is preserved, set
$\xi_u=\mathrm i\,\bar\partial u\wedge\gamma^{n-2}/(4\pi)$.
The Leibniz rule gives
$\Gamma_u-\gamma_0^{n-1}=\partial\xi_u+\bar\partial\overline{\xi_u}$.
Since $\ddc(\partial\xi_u+\bar\partial\overline{\xi_u})=0$, if $\gamma_0$ is Gauduchon then so is $\eta_u$, and the $(n-1)$st powers of $\gamma_0$ and $\eta_u$ have the same Aeppli class. The uniqueness in \cref{thm:stw-volume} concerns this scalar ansatz; it does not assert uniqueness among all Gauduchon metrics in the Aeppli class.
