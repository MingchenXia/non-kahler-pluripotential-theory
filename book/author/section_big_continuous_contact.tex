\section{Contact support in a big background}
\label{sec:big-continuous-contact}

Throughout this section, $\theta$ is an arbitrary smooth big background, not necessarily semipositive. For a continuous obstacle, regularization shows that the envelope is continuous off the poles of a strict analytic barrier. On a small ball where the envelope stays below the obstacle, a homogeneous replacement then shows that the envelope already solves the homogeneous equation, so its Monge--Amp\`ere measure vanishes away from the contact set.

By \cref{thm:resolvable-regularization-input}, applied to a strict barrier with a sufficiently small curvature error, we may fix $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with analytic singularities and a smooth remainder after resolution such that
\[
 \theta+\ddc\rho\geq\kappa\omega,\qquad\kappa>0.
\]
Its pole set $Z=\{\rho=-\infty\}$ is an analytic, hence pluripolar, subset of $X$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:big-continuous-envelope-contact}
Let $h\in C(X)$. Then $p=P_\theta(h)$ is a $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function, continuous on $X\setminus Z$, whose Monge--Amp\`ere measure satisfies
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{p<h\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

Here $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)$ is allowed to have infinite total mass.

\begin{proof}
Subtract a constant from $\rho$ so that $\rho+2\leq h$. Then $\rho+2$ is a competitor, and since the obstacle is continuous, the envelope lies below it everywhere; thus
\[
 \rho+2\leq p\leq h.
\]
In particular, $p$ is locally bounded off $Z$. We first prove that $p$ is continuous there; this part does not use the contact assertion.

Apply \cref{lem:smooth-background-regularization-rate} to $p-\sup_Xp$ and add back the constant. The transforms $\Phi_{c,\delta}$ of that lemma are defined on $X$ and, with fixed constants $A,K,C>0$ and $\epsilon(\delta)=C\delta$, satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-envelope-regularization}
 \theta+\ddc\Phi_{c,\delta}\geq-(Ac+\epsilon(\delta))\omega.
\end{equation}
Off $Z$ the infimum defining $\Phi_{c,\delta}$ is attained at a positive radius, and
\[
 p-K(\delta^2+\delta)\leq\Phi_{c,\delta}
       \leq R_\delta p\leq R_\delta h.
\]
For small $\delta$ the first inequality gives $\Phi_{c,\delta}\geq\rho+1$, hence $\rho\leq\Phi_{c,\delta}$ on $X$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Set
\[
 m(\delta)=\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}\|R_th-h\|_\infty,
 \qquad c=m(\delta)+\epsilon(\delta)+\delta,
 \qquad s=\frac{Ac+\epsilon(\delta)}{\kappa}.
\]
The averaging kernels are positive, have integral one and are supported in balls of radius comparable to $t$, so uniform continuity of $h$ gives $m(\delta)\to0$. Define
\[
 v_\delta=\frac{\Phi_{c,\delta}+s\rho}{1+s}.
\]
By the choice of $s$, the strict barrier and \eqref{eq:big-envelope-regularization} show that $v_\delta$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. Since $\rho\leq\Phi_{c,\delta}$,
\[
 v_\delta\leq\Phi_{c,\delta}\leq R_\delta h\leq h+m(\delta).
\]
Thus $v_\delta-m(\delta)$ is a competitor for the envelope, and $v_\delta\leq p+m(\delta)$.

Let $L\Subset X\setminus Z$ be compact. On $L$ the difference $p-\rho$ is bounded above. Since $s\leq Cc$, the identity
\[
 \Phi_{c,\delta}-p=(1+s)(v_\delta-p)+s(p-\rho)
\]
therefore gives $\Phi_{c,\delta}-p\leq C_Lc$ on $L$. At a radius $t$ where the infimum is attained,
\[
 -c\log(t/\delta)
        \leq\Phi_{c,\delta}-p+K(\delta^2+\delta)\leq C_Lc.
\]
Since $c\geq\delta$, this yields $t\geq a_L\delta$ with a fixed $a_L>0$. Because $R_tp+Kt^2$ is increasing in $t$, it follows that
\[
 p-K(a_L\delta)^2\leq R_{a_L\delta}p
       \leq\Phi_{c,\delta}+K\delta^2+K\delta.
\]
Consequently $R_{a_L\delta}p\to p$ uniformly on $L$. These averages are smooth for positive radius, even though $p$ is unbounded on $X$. Applying this to compact neighborhoods of the points of $X\setminus Z$ proves that $p$ is continuous on $X\setminus Z$.

We turn to the contact assertion. By the continuity just proved, the set $\{p<h\}\setminus Z$ is open. Let $x$ be one of its points, and choose a small coordinate ball $B_r$ centered at $x$ whose closure lies in this set. Let $H$ be the homogeneous replacement of \cref{thm:ball-homogeneous-replacement} with boundary values $p|_{\partial B_r}$. Its domination property gives $H\geq p$ on the ball.

We claim that $H\leq h$ on $B_r$ if $r$ is small enough. Indeed, in the chosen Euclidean coordinates $z$, with Laplacian $\Delta$, the inequality $\theta+\ddc H\geq0$ implies a fixed bound $\Delta H\geq-C$. Comparison with the function
$\sup_{\partial B_r}p+C'(r^2-|z-x|^2)$, with $C'$ fixed large enough, gives
\[
 H\leq\sup_{\partial B_r}p+C'r^2.
\]
As $r\to0$, the right-hand side tends to $p(x)$, whereas $\inf_{B_r}h\to h(x)>p(x)$. This proves the claim for sufficiently small $r$.

Now replace $p$ by $H$ on the ball. The resulting function is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic. To see this, fix $a>0$ and take $\max\{p,H-a\}$ inside the ball and $p$ outside. Since $H$ and $p$ are continuous with equal boundary values, $H-a<p$ on a collar of $\partial B_r$, so this is an ordinary local maximum construction. Letting $a\downarrow0$ gives the function equal to $H$ on $B_r$ and to $p$ outside, with locally uniform convergence near the boundary of the ball. This function is bounded above by $h$ on all of $X$, so the definition of the envelope forces it to be at most $p$. Thus $p=H$ on $B_r$, and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)$ vanishes there.

These balls cover $\{p<h\}\setminus Z$. There the extended measure agrees with the local bounded product, and it does not charge the pluripolar set $Z$. This proves the contact assertion on $X$; no bound on the total mass of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(p)$ was needed.
\end{proof}
