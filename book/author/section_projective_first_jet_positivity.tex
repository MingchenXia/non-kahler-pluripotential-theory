\section{Projective first jets and positive bundle metrics}
\label{sec:projective-first-jet-positivity}

The preceding constructions require a positive tangent direction for each fiber vector. Griffiths positivity requires positivity for every pair of nonzero tangent and fiber vectors. On a projective manifold, this stronger condition has a finite-dimensional test at some twist. Failure at every twist produces a positive measure annihilating a linear Levi operator. The measure lives over the dual bundle, whereas the Gauduchon tests in \cref{thm:lamari} live on the base.

Throughout this section, $X$ is a smooth compact projective manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, $E\to X$ is a holomorphic bundle of positive rank, and $F=E^*$. Fix a very ample line bundle $A$, a basis $t_1,\ldots,t_d$ of its complete space of sections, and the Fubini--Study metric $a$ determined by that basis. Fix an integer $m_0\geq0$ for which $E\otimes A^{m_0}$ is globally generated. This global-generation hypothesis is part of the data. In a local frame of $A$, put
\[
 \rho=\sum_{\alpha=1}^d|t_\alpha|^2,
 \qquad a=\mathrm e^{-\phi},\qquad\phi=\log\rho.
\]

A metric $g$ on $E$ is strictly Griffiths-positive when $g(R_g(u,\bar u)v,v)>0$ for every nonzero $u\in T_x^{1,0}X$ and $v\in E_x$, at every $x$.

For a smooth Hermitian form $h$ on $F$, not initially assumed positive, let $G_h(v)=h(v,v)$ on its total space. Write $\mathcal L G_h(\xi)$ for the complex Hessian evaluated on $\xi\in T^{1,0}F$: in local coordinates it is $\sum(G_h)_{j\bar k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k$. This uses the coefficients of $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial G_h$, without a $2\pi$ factor.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dual-bundle-strict-levi}\index{Griffiths positivity!dual bundle Levi form}
The function $G_h$ is strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section if and only if $h$ is a smooth positive Hermitian metric with strictly Griffiths-negative curvature. Equivalently, its dual metric on $E$ is strictly Griffiths-positive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On vertical tangent vectors the Levi form is $h$ itself, so strict positivity first forces $h>0$. At a fixed base point choose a holomorphic frame with matrix $H=\operatorname{Id}$ and first derivatives of $H$ zero. With the curvature convention of \cref{sec:gauduchon-directional-curvature}, $R_h(u,\bar u)=-H_{u\bar u}$ at this point. A tangent vector to $F$ has horizontal and vertical components $(u,w)$, and
\[
 \mathcal L G_h(u,w)=|w|^2+v^*H_{u\bar u}v
                  =|w|^2-h(R_h(u,\bar u)v,v).
\]
For $v\ne0$ this is positive for every nonzero $(u,w)$ precisely when the curvature evaluation is negative for every nonzero $u$. Dual curvature is the negative transpose of the original curvature, with the dual Hermitian pairing, giving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Choose any smooth Hermitian metric $k$ on $F$, let $S=\{v:k(v,v)=1\}$, and choose a smooth positive Hermitian form $b$ on the complex vector bundle $W=T^{1,0}F|_S$. The set
\[
 K=\{(v,\xi):v\in S,\ \xi\in W_v,\ b(\xi,\xi)=1\}
\]
is compact. Define the real-linear operator $D h=\mathcal L G_h|_S$, viewed as a Hermitian form on $W$. It is continuous from the $C^2$ norm of forms on $F\to X$ to the uniform norm on $W$. Indeed, its coefficients involve at most two base derivatives of $h$, and the fiber vectors range over a compact set. Fiber dilation $v\mapsto cv$ gives
\[
 G_h(cv)=|c|^2G_h(v),\qquad
 \mathcal L G_h|_{cv}(\mathrm d(v\mapsto cv)\xi)
        =|c|^2\mathcal L G_h|_v(\xi).
\]
Thus strict positivity on $K$ is equivalent to strict positivity off the zero section.

\subsection{The finite-dimensional alternative}

For $m\geq m_0$, choose a basis of $V_m=H^0(X,E\otimes A^m)$ and write $N_m=\dim V_m$. Pairing these sections with $v\in F_x$ gives a column $p_m(v)$ of local holomorphic functions with values in the local frame of $A^m$. For any Hermitian $N_m\times N_m$ matrix $B$, set
\[
 G_{m,B}(v)=\mathrm e^{-m\phi(x)}p_m(v)^*B p_m(v).
\]
This is globally defined and fiberwise quadratic. It defines a smooth Hermitian form $h_{m,B}$ on $F$. The matrix $B$ is allowed to have negative eigenvalues.

For $(v,\xi)\in K$, let $u$ be the projection of $\xi$ to $T_x^{1,0}X$ and put
\begin{align*}
 r_m&=\mathrm dp_m(\xi)-m\phi_u p_m(v),\qquad
 \kappa=\phi_{u\bar u},\\
 \Psi_m(v,\xi)&=\mathrm e^{-m\phi}
       \bigl(r_mr_m^*-m\kappa p_m p_m^*\bigr).
\end{align*}
Differentiating the weighted quadratic function gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-jet-levi-matrix}
 (D h_{m,B})_v(\xi,\xi)=\operatorname{tr}(B\Psi_m(v,\xi)).
\end{equation}
All entries use only the first jets of the section bases: if $t=(t_\alpha)$, then
\[
 \phi_u=\frac{t^*\mathrm dt(u)}{\rho},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\rho|\mathrm dt(u)|^2-|t^*\mathrm dt(u)|^2}{\rho^2}.
\]
Changing the frame of $A$ by a nowhere-zero holomorphic factor $\gamma$ changes $p_m$ to $\gamma^{-m}p_m$ and $\phi$ to $\phi-\log|\gamma|^2$. It changes $r_m$ by the same factor and leaves $\kappa$ unchanged, so $\Psi_m$ is frame independent. A change of section basis gives a congruence; whether its convex hull contains zero is also independent of that choice.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:first-jet-finite-alternative}\index{Griffiths positivity!convex hull criterion}
At each fixed $m$, exactly one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Some Hermitian matrix $B$ satisfies $\operatorname{tr}(B\Psi_m)>0$ everywhere on $K$.
 \item There is a probability measure on $K$, supported on at most $N_m^2+1$ points, such that $\int_K\Psi_m\,\mathrm d\mu=0$.
\end{enumerate}
The first alternative holds precisely when $0\notin\operatorname{conv}(\Psi_m(K))$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The real vector space of Hermitian matrices has dimension $N_m^2$. Carath\'eodory's convex-hull theorem expresses every point of the hull using at most $N_m^2+1$ points of the compact image, so the hull is compact. If it contains zero, the coefficients of such an expression define the required atomic probability measure. Its integral prevents a strictly positive separating matrix.

If the hull misses zero, let $Z_0$ be its point of least Hilbert--Schmidt norm. Differentiating the squared norm along the segment from $Z_0$ to any $Z$ in the hull gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(Z_0Z)\geq\operatorname{tr}(Z_0^2)>0.
\]
Taking $B=Z_0$ proves the first alternative, uniformly on the compact image.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Approximation with two derivatives}

Let $\mathcal H_m=\{h_{m,B}:B=B^*\}$. The passage from these finite-dimensional tests to all smooth metrics requires $C^2$ approximation, since $D$ differentiates twice.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projector-polynomial-c2-density}
The matrix-valued map $Y=tt^*/\rho$ embeds $X$ as a compact smooth submanifold of the real vector space of Hermitian $d\times d$ matrices. Restrictions of real polynomials in its real coordinates are dense in $C^2(X,\mathbb R)$ for the $C^2$ norm.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The normalized rank-one projector determines the projective line $[t]$. Its differential vanishes only when $\mathrm dt$ is proportional to $t$. Very ampleness therefore gives injectivity and injectivity of the differential; compactness makes the image embedded. A $C^2$ function on this image extends to a $C^2$ function on an ambient cube: use local coordinates in which the image is a graph, extend constantly in the normal coordinates, and combine these extensions with a finite smooth partition of unity and a cutoff. Rescale the cube to $[0,1]^q$.

For such an extension $f$, its multivariate Bernstein polynomial is
\[
 B_Nf(y)=\sum_{0\leq j_1,\ldots,j_q\leq N}
       f(j/N)\prod_{\ell=1}^q
       \binom N{j_\ell}y_\ell^{j_\ell}(1-y_\ell)^{N-j_\ell}.
\]
For a multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\leq2$, differentiation expresses $\partial^\alpha B_Nf$ as an average of $\Delta_{1/N}^\alpha f(j/N)$, with prefactor $\prod_\ell(N)_{\alpha_\ell}$ and Bernstein degrees $N-\alpha_\ell$. Here $(N)_r=N(N-1)\cdots(N-r+1)$. Repeated use of the fundamental theorem of calculus expresses $N^{|\alpha|}\Delta_{1/N}^\alpha f(j/N)$ as an average of $\partial^\alpha f$ at points within $2/N$ in each affected coordinate.

The remaining Bernstein weights are the law of independent binomial variables of degrees $N-\alpha_\ell$, divided by $N$. Their means differ from $y_\ell$ by at most $2/N$ and their variances are at most $1/(4N)$. Uniform continuity of $\partial^\alpha f$, splitting the average into a fixed small neighborhood of $y$ and its complement, and Chebyshev's inequality give uniform convergence to $\partial^\alpha f$. The prefactor divided by $N^{|\alpha|}$ tends to one. This proves convergence for every derivative of order at most two, including the function itself. Restrict to the embedded image and use the chain rule in a finite collection of charts to obtain the asserted $C^2$ approximation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:first-jet-nested-metric-density}
The spaces $\mathcal H_m$ are nested, and their union is $C^2$ dense in the space of $C^2$ Hermitian forms on $F$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For nesting, multiply the sections in $V_m$ by each $t_\alpha$ and express the products in the complete basis of $V_{m+1}$. The identity $\sum_\alpha\mathrm e^{-\phi}|t_\alpha|^2=1$ writes $G_{m,B}$ as the sum of the corresponding weighted quadratic functions at level $m+1$. This gives one Hermitian coefficient matrix at that level. Surjectivity of the multiplication map is unnecessary.

For density, write the normalized evaluation map at level $m_0$ in a local frame of $F$ as $L=\mathrm e^{-m_0\phi/2}P_0$. Global generation means that $L$ is injective, so $M=L^*L$ is positive definite. If a $C^2$ Hermitian form has matrix $H$, define
\[
 C(x)=L M^{-1}H M^{-1}L^*.
\]
Then $L^*CL=H$. A change of frame of $F$ cancels in this expression; a change of frame of $A$ multiplies $L$ by a scalar of modulus one, which also cancels. Thus $C$ is a globally defined $C^2$ Hermitian matrix function on $X$.

Apply \cref{lem:projector-polynomial-c2-density} to its real coordinates in the vector space of Hermitian matrices. This gives Hermitian matrix polynomials $C_q(Y)$ converging to $C$ in $C^2$, where their degrees may increase. Every monomial of degree $k$ in $Y$ has the form $t^I\bar t^J/\rho^k$, with $|I|=|J|=k$. For a polynomial of degree at most $q$, multiply each numerator by $\rho^{q-k}$ to use the common denominator $\rho^q$. Consequently
\[
 \mathrm e^{-m_0\phi}p_{m_0}^*C_q(Y)p_{m_0}
\]
is a quadratic expression in the product sections $\sigma_i t^I$ of $E\otimes A^{m_0+q}$, with weight $\mathrm e^{-(m_0+q)\phi}$. Express these products in the complete basis of $V_{m_0+q}$ and take the Hermitian part of the coefficient matrix. The function is unchanged because it is real. It therefore defines an element of $\mathcal H_{m_0+q}$. Multiplication by the fixed smooth evaluation map is continuous in $C^2$, proving convergence to $H$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The metric and measure alternatives}

A positive matrix-valued measure $T$ on $W\to S$ acts on continuous Hermitian forms $\Phi$ by $\langle T,\Phi\rangle\geq0$ whenever $\Phi\geq0$. Its trace mass relative to $b$ is $\langle T,b\rangle$. Write $D^*T=0$ when $\langle T,Dh\rangle=0$ for every smooth Hermitian form $h$ on $F$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:projective-first-jet-positive-metric}\index{Griffiths positivity!positive measure alternative}
With the fixed projective data above, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $E$ admits a smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metric.
 \item For some $m\geq m_0$, a Hermitian matrix $B$ makes $G_{m,B}$ strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section.
 \item For some $m\geq m_0$, $0\notin\operatorname{conv}(\Psi_m(K))$.
 \item There is no nonzero positive matrix-valued measure $T$ on $W\to S$ satisfying $D^*T=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Given the metric in the first assertion, its dual $h$ has $Dh\geq\delta b$ for some $\delta>0$ on $S$, by \cref{lem:dual-bundle-strict-levi} and compactness. The $C^2$ density in \cref{lem:first-jet-nested-metric-density} and continuity of $D$ give a form $h_{m,B}$ with $D h_{m,B}\geq\delta b/2$. This proves the second assertion. The reverse implication follows from the vertical Levi form and \cref{lem:dual-bundle-strict-levi}. The second and third assertions are equivalent by \cref{prop:first-jet-finite-alternative}.

If the first assertion holds, every nonzero positive $T$ has positive trace mass, and
\[
 \langle T,Dh\rangle\geq\delta\langle T,b\rangle>0.
\]
Such a measure cannot satisfy $D^*T=0$. Conversely, suppose that every level fails. Choose an annihilating atomic probability measure $\mu_m$ on $K$ at each level by \cref{prop:first-jet-finite-alternative}. A subsequence converges weakly to a probability measure $\mu$ on the compact set $K$. Nesting implies that this limit annihilates $Dh$ for every $h$ in each fixed $\mathcal H_j$. The $C^2$ density and continuity of $D$ extend the annihilation to every smooth Hermitian form.

Define
\[
 \langle T,\Phi\rangle=\int_K\Phi_v(\xi,\xi)\,\mathrm d\mu(v,\xi).
\]
This is a positive matrix-valued measure on $W\to S$, with $\langle T,b\rangle=1$ and $D^*T=0$. To see its measure interpretation directly, represent its entries in each local frame by scalar measures: positivity bounds the functional on a Hermitian form of uniform norm at most one by its trace mass, and scalar Riesz representation applies to the coordinate functionals. A finite partition of unity combines these local representations. This contradicts the fourth assertion and completes the equivalence.
\end{proof}

The measure equation $D^*T=0$ concerns a bundle over the fiber sphere $S\subset E^*$; it is different from $\ddc T=0$ for currents on $X$. The theorem neither bounds the first successful twist nor supplies a finite stopping rule when all levels fail.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:untwisted-evaluation-immersion}\index{Griffiths positivity!evaluation immersion}
If $E$ is globally generated and $m_0=0$, the level-zero test succeeds exactly when the complete evaluation map $\Phi:F\to H^0(X,E)^*$ is an immersion off the zero section.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At $m=0$, $\Psi_0=\mathrm d\Phi(\xi)\mathrm d\Phi(\xi)^*$. If $\mathrm d\Phi$ is injective off the zero section, $B=\operatorname{Id}$ gives a strictly positive Levi form. If it has a nonzero kernel vector there, fiber dilation and tangent normalization place such a vector in $K$, where $\Psi_0=0$. No Hermitian matrix can then separate the image from zero.
\end{proof}

Failure of this untwisted test leaves the higher twists available. The finite alternatives use all Hermitian coefficient matrices and the complete section spaces; testing only positive coefficient matrices or a selected collection of sections changes the criterion.
