\subsection{The ordinary-type topology on a curve}
\label{subsec:curve-ordinary-topology}

We now compare the polar metric with the topology on ordinary singularity types introduced in \cref{sec:ordinary-type-topology}. That topology also uses the ordinary envelope, but it measures the total variation only of the atomic part of the curvature. On ideal models the atomic part is the whole polar measure, so the two topologies agree there. On arbitrary ordinary models, diffuse polar mass can concentrate without being detected by the atomic total variation.

We keep $Y$, $\theta$, $A$ and the decomposition $\mu_u=D_u+\sigma_u$, and we use $\eta=\theta/A$ for $L^1$ norms. Let $\mathfrak S(Y,\theta)=\PSH(Y,\theta)/{\sim}$ be the space of ordinary singularity types, and write $[u]$ for the class of $u$. Recall from \cref{prop:ordinary-type-uniformity} that $\tau_{\theta,0}$ is the uniform topology on $\mathfrak S(Y,\theta)$ generated by the $L^1$ distances between ordinary envelopes and between ideal envelopes, by the total variation of divisorial Lelong numbers on each fixed modification, and by the controlled ideal relations $\mathcal U(t,a)$ of \eqref{eq:controlled-ideal-entourage}. For ordinary classes put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-ordinary-type-distance}
 d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta([u],[v])
 =\|P_\theta[u]-P_\theta[v]\|_{L^1(\eta)}
     +\|D_u-D_v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-ordinary-type-topology}\index{Singularity type!curve pseudometric}
The finite pseudometric $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$ induces $\tau_{\theta,0}$ on $\mathfrak S(Y,\theta)$. For $0\leq s\leq A$, the classes of ideal models
\[
 \mathcal C_s=\{[q_D]:D\text{ positive atomic},\ D(Y)\leq s\}
\]
form a complete metric space both for $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$ and for the distance $\|D-E\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ between $[q_D]$ and $[q_E]$, and these two metrics induce the same topology. The space $\mathcal C_s$ is not totally bounded if $s>0$, and $\mathcal C_0=\{[0]\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A smooth compact modification of a smooth curve is an isomorphism, and the divisor weight of a point is one. Hence the divisorial distance in \cref{prop:ordinary-type-uniformity} is exactly the atomic total variation, and the two summands of \eqref{eq:curve-ordinary-type-distance} are among the coordinates defining $\tau_{\theta,0}$.

Conversely, suppose that a net $[u_j]$ converges to $[u]$ for $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$. The ideal envelope of $u$ is $q_{D_u}$, by \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}. Convergence of the atomic parts in total variation implies $L^1$ convergence of these envelopes, by \cref{thm:curve-ideal-volume-distance,thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence}. These results also apply to nets: the map $D\mapsto q_D$ goes between metric spaces, so sequential continuity is equivalent to continuity. It remains to check the controlled ideal relations.

At a point $x$ with local coordinate $z$, the multiplier ideals on the curve are
\[
 \mathcal I(tu)_x=(z^{\lfloor tD_u(\{x\})\rfloor}).
\]
This follows from \cref{thm:local-i-inputs,lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}: on a smooth curve the Lelong number at $x$ determines the ideal, and the logarithmic germ with that Lelong number has the displayed ideal. Write $D_j=D_{u_j}$ and $D=D_u$, and fix $t>0$ and $a>1$. The set $F=\{x:D(\{x\})\geq1/(2t)\}$ is finite. At its points, $aD_j(\{x\})\geq D(\{x\})$ and $aD(\{x\})\geq D_j(\{x\})$ eventually. Outside $F$, the bound
\[
 \sup_x|D_j(\{x\})-D(\{x\})|\leq\|D_j-D\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\longrightarrow0
\]
makes both $\mathcal I(tu)_x$ and $\mathcal I(tu_j)_x$ unit ideals for large $j$. Hence both inclusions in \eqref{eq:controlled-ideal-entourage} hold eventually at every point. This proves that $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$ induces $\tau_{\theta,0}$.

The map $D\mapsto q_D$ is injective, so $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$ is a metric on $\mathcal C_s$. The positive atomic measures of mass at most $s$ form a closed subset of the space of finite signed measures, as in the proof of \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness}, and hence a complete space for total variation. A $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$-Cauchy sequence in $\mathcal C_s$ is Cauchy in total variation; its limit is again atomic, and \cref{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence} gives convergence of the envelope coordinate. This proves completeness for $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$ as well as the equality of the two topologies.

If $s>0$, fix $0<b\leq s$ and infinitely many distinct points $x_j$. The measures $b\delta_{x_j}$ have pairwise total-variation distance $2b$, so $\mathcal C_s$ is not totally bounded for either metric. For $s=0$ only the zero measure remains.
\end{proof}

By \cref{lem:curve-atomic-ideal-model}, for $0\leq\delta\leq A$ the set $\mathcal C_{A-\delta}$ consists exactly of the classes of ideal models of mass at least $\delta$. The whole set $\mathcal C_A$, including the models of zero mass, is complete. Its subset of positive mass is incomplete: for a point $x$, the measures $D_j=A(1-1/j)\delta_x$ form a Cauchy sequence whose limit $A\delta_x$ has zero mass.

For general ordinary classes, the corresponding statement concerns a pseudometric. Put
\[
 \mathcal G_{\theta,\delta}
 =\left\{[u]:P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\quad
       \int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I})\geq\delta\right\}.
\]
For $0\leq\delta\leq A$ this set is complete for $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$, since every class $[u]$ in it is at distance zero from $[q_{D_u}]\in\mathcal C_{A-\delta}$; limits need not be unique as ordinary classes. The set is not totally bounded when $\delta<A$. At $\delta=A$ the pseudometric vanishes identically on it, although it contains distinct classes by \cref{prop:ordinary-topology-nonseparation}. The union of these sets over all $\delta>0$ is incomplete, by the same one-atom sequence.

\subsection{Concentration of diffuse polar curvature}

The atomic total variation cannot be omitted from \cref{thm:curve-ordinary-type-topology}. On $\mathbb P^1$ with its Fubini--Study form $\theta$ of area one, fix a rational $0<\tau<1$ and take the atomic probability measures $\nu_j$ and the ideal models $r_j=P_\theta[(1-\tau)q_j]_{\mathcal I}$ of \cref{prop:positive-mass-ideal-discontinuity}, choosing the rational approximations so that the largest atom of $\nu_j$ tends to zero. The ordinary and ideal envelopes of $r_j$ both tend to zero in $L^1$, and for each $t>0$ and $a>1$, the pair $([r_j],[0])$ eventually lies in $\mathcal U(t,a)$. The currents $\theta+\ddc r_j$ converge weakly to $\theta$. Nevertheless,
\[
 d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta([r_j],[0])
 \geq\|D_{r_j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1-\tau>0.
\]
Thus these weaker conditions do not imply convergence for $\tau_{\theta,0}$, even at a fixed positive mass.

The opposite difficulty concerns diffuse polar mass: the pseudometric controls the ordinary envelope in $L^1$, but not the total variation of the diffuse measure itself.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve-ordinary-topology-incomplete}\index{Singularity type!incomplete ordinary space}
For the Fubini--Study form $\theta$ of area one on $\mathbb P^1$, the space $(\mathfrak S(\mathbb P^1,\theta),d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta)$ is incomplete. In fact, there is a $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$-Cauchy sequence without limit consisting of ordinary models of one fixed positive non-pluripolar mass, all with trivial multiplier ideals.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nonatomic polar probability measure $\sigma$ with compact support in an affine chart, by \cref{lem:polar-cantor-nonatomic-measure}. Fix $0<c<1$ and a point $x$ in that chart. Translating and shrinking the support towards $x$ gives nonatomic polar probability measures $\sigma_j$ supported in disks about $x$ with radii tending to zero. Let $p_j=p_{c\sigma_j}$ be the ordinary models of \cref{lem:curve-polar-model}. Their polar measures are $c\sigma_j$, their masses are $1-c$, and $D_{p_j}=0$. Hence their Lelong numbers vanish, their multiplier ideals are trivial, and their ideal envelopes are zero.

We claim that $p_j\to q_{c\delta_x}$ in $L^1$. Write $q_y=q_{c\delta_y}$. Rotations of $\mathbb P^1$ preserving $\theta$ and sending $x$ to $y$ can be chosen to depend continuously on $y$ near $x$. They transport $q_x$ to $q_y$, so continuity of translations in $L^1$ gives $q_y\to q_x$ in $L^1$ as $y\to x$. Set
\[
 w_j=\int_{\mathbb P^1}q_y\,\mathrm d\sigma_j(y).
\]
The common $L^1$ bound for normalized potentials makes this integral well defined. The function $w_j$ is nonpositive, its curvature current dominates $c\sigma_j$, and $w_j\to q_x$ in $L^1$. By \eqref{eq:curve-polar-domination}, $p_j$ is the upper envelope of all $v\leq0$ with $\theta+\ddc v\geq c\sigma_j$, so $w_j\leq p_j$.

By normalized compactness, every subsequence of $p_j$ has a further subsequence converging in $L^1$ to some $v\leq0$. The inequalities $\theta+\ddc p_j\geq c\sigma_j$ pass to the limit and give $\theta+\ddc v\geq c\delta_x$. Since $q_x=p_{c\delta_x}$, the same envelope description gives $v\leq q_x$. The lower bound $w_j\leq p_j$ gives $v\geq q_x$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere. This identifies every cluster point and proves the claim.

Since $P_\theta[p_j]=p_j$ and $D_{p_j}=0$, the classes $[p_j]$ form a $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta$-Cauchy sequence. A limit $[v]$ would satisfy $D_v=0$ and $P_\theta[v]=q_x$. This is impossible: the ordinary envelope preserves all multiplier ideals and hence all atoms, whereas $q_x$ has an atom of mass $c$ at $x$. The sequence therefore has no limit in $\mathfrak S(\mathbb P^1,\theta)$.
\end{proof}

On ordinary models, $\tau_{\theta,0}$ and the topology of the polar metric $d_\theta$ are also different. Choose the Cantor support of $\sigma$ inside a positive real interval in the affine coordinate $z$, away from zero. The rotations $R_j(z)=\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\varepsilon_j}z$, with $0<\varepsilon_j\to0$, move this support off itself. Put $p=p_{c\sigma}$ and $\widetilde p_j=p\circ R_j^{-1}$. By rotation invariance of the envelope, the $\widetilde p_j$ are ordinary models with polar measures $c(R_j)_*\sigma$, which have no atoms. Continuity of rotations in $L^1$ yields $d^{\mathrm{ot}}_\theta([\widetilde p_j],[p])\to0$, whereas the polar measures have disjoint supports, so $d_\theta(\widetilde p_j,p)=2c$ by \cref{thm:curve-model-volume-distance}. Conversely, polar total variation controls both terms of \eqref{eq:curve-ordinary-type-distance}, by \cref{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence}. Thus $\tau_{\theta,0}$ is strictly weaker than the polar metric topology on ordinary models, although the two agree on ideal models. The failure of continuity under smooth gauge changes with a fixed zero obstacle (\cref{prop:ordinary-topology-gauge-failure}) gives a further difference: the polar metric is invariant under such changes, by \cref{sec:curve-model-backgrounds}.
