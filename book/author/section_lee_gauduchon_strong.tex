\section{Strongly Gauduchon metrics}
\label{sec:lee-gauduchon-strong}

The closed form underlying a strongly Gauduchon metric pairs positively with every nonzero closed positive $(1,1)$-current, and this pairing depends only on the de Rham class of the current. It therefore bounds the mass of closed positive currents in terms of their de Rham classes and excludes nonzero positive de Rham-exact currents. The Lee--Gauduchon cone, by contrast, tests only the de Rham-exact Bott--Chern classes in the image of $j_X$. On the Calabi--Eckmann threefold this image vanishes while a nonzero positive exact current exists, so the two conditions differ.

\begin{definition}\label{def:strongly-gauduchon}\index{Strongly Gauduchon metric}
A Hermitian metric $\gamma$ is \emph{strongly Gauduchon} if $\gamma^{n-1}$ is the $(n-1,n-1)$-component of a smooth closed real $(2n-2)$-form.
\end{definition}

Write the closed real form in the definition as
\[
 \Theta=\gamma^{n-1}+\beta+\overline\beta,
 \qquad \beta\text{ of type }(n,n-2).
\]
Then $\partial(\gamma^{n-1})=-\bar\partial\beta$. Conversely, this equation and its conjugate give $\mathrm{d}\Theta=0$, because all other components of $\mathrm d\Theta$ have a type with an entry exceeding $n$. Thus $\gamma$ is strongly Gauduchon if and only if $\partial(\gamma^{n-1})$ is $\bar\partial$-exact. Applying $\bar\partial$ shows that a strongly Gauduchon metric is Gauduchon. Every balanced metric is strongly Gauduchon, with $\Theta=\gamma^{n-1}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lee-strong-obstruction}
If $\gamma$ is a strongly Gauduchon metric, then $[\dc(\gamma^{n-1})]=0$; in particular $0\in\operatorname{LG}(X)$. Moreover, $X$ admits a strongly Gauduchon metric if and only if there is no nonzero positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$ that is de Rham exact.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $\Gamma=\gamma^{n-1}$ and use $\beta$ as above. Then
\[
 \dc\Gamma
 =\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}
       (\bar\partial\beta-\partial\overline\beta)
 =\mathrm{d}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}
                              (\beta-\overline\beta)\right).
\]
Thus $[\dc\Gamma]=0$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Next, let $S$ be a real current of degree one such that $T=\mathrm{d}S$ is a nonzero positive $(1,1)$-current, and let $\Theta$ be a closed real form with strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-part $\Gamma$. By type considerations and Stokes' theorem for currents,
\[
 \int_XT\wedge\Gamma
 =\int_XT\wedge\Theta=0.
\]
But the first integral is strictly positive because $T\ne0$ and $\Gamma>0$, a contradiction. Thus a strongly Gauduchon metric excludes nonzero positive exact currents.

Conversely, suppose that no strongly Gauduchon metric exists. In the real Fr\'echet space of smooth $(2n-2)$-forms, let $\mathcal Z$ be the closed subspace of closed forms, and let $\mathcal P$ be the open convex cone of forms with strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-part. This cone is nonempty, since it contains $h^{n-1}$ for any Hermitian metric $h$. The two sets are disjoint, because the positive $(n-1)$st root of the $(n-1,n-1)$-part of a form in their intersection would be a strongly Gauduchon metric. The Hahn--Banach separation theorem therefore gives a nonzero real current $T$ of degree two such that
\[
 T|_{\mathcal Z}=0,\qquad T(\Theta)\geq0\quad(\Theta\in\mathcal P).
\]
Fix $\Theta_0\in\mathcal P$. If a real form $\Psi$ has zero $(n-1,n-1)$-part, then $\Theta_0+t\Psi\in\mathcal P$ for every $t\in\R$, so $T(\Psi)=0$. Thus $T$ is of type $(1,1)$. For any semipositive $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\Gamma$, we have $\Gamma+\varepsilon\Theta_0\in\mathcal P$ for $\varepsilon>0$, and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives $T(\Gamma)\geq0$; thus $T$ is positive.

Since $T$ annihilates every exact test form, it is closed. By the de Rham theorem for currents and Poincar\'e duality, its cohomology class is determined by its pairings with closed forms of complementary degree. All these pairings vanish, so $T=\mathrm{d}S$ for a real current $S$. Thus $T$ is a nonzero positive exact current, which proves the converse.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, $X$ admits a strongly Gauduchon metric if and only if every nonzero pseudoeffective Bott--Chern class has nonzero image in de Rham cohomology. Indeed, a closed positive current representing the zero Bott--Chern class has zero pairing with a Gauduchon metric and therefore vanishes, so the Bott--Chern classes of the nonzero positive de Rham-exact currents are exactly the nonzero pseudoeffective classes with zero de Rham image.

The same criterion characterizes the manifolds on which de Rham classes control the masses of closed positive currents.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:strong-gauduchon-current-compactness}\index{Strongly Gauduchon metric!current mass bound}
Fix a Hermitian metric $h$ on $X$ and a norm on $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)$. Then $X$ admits a strongly Gauduchon metric if and only if there is a constant $C>0$ such that every closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-gauduchon-class-mass}
 \int_XT\wedge h^{n-1}\leq C\|[T]_{\mathrm{dR}}\|.
\end{equation}
Under this condition, the family of closed positive $(1,1)$-currents whose de Rham classes belong to a fixed compact set is weakly compact, and the image of the pseudoeffective Bott--Chern cone in $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)$ is a closed convex cone containing no line.
\end{corollary}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that $\gamma$ is strongly Gauduchon, and choose a smooth closed real form $\Theta$ whose $(n-1,n-1)$-part is $\gamma^{n-1}$. By compactness, $h^{n-1}\leq A\gamma^{n-1}$ for some $A>0$. Positivity and type considerations then give
\[
 \int_XT\wedge h^{n-1}
 \leq A\int_XT\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 =A\langle[T]_{\mathrm{dR}},[\Theta]_{\mathrm{dR}}\rangle.
\]
The last pairing is a fixed linear functional on the finite-dimensional de Rham group, which proves \eqref{eq:strong-gauduchon-class-mass}. Conversely, this bound forces every closed positive de Rham-exact current to vanish, and \cref{prop:lee-strong-obstruction} gives a strongly Gauduchon metric.

For the compactness assertion, the bound gives uniformly bounded trace masses. A weak limit of such currents is positive and closed, and its de Rham class is the limit of their classes, as one sees by pairing with a finite basis of smooth closed forms of complementary degree and using Poincar\'e duality. Thus the family is weakly closed, and the usual weak compactness of positive currents with bounded mass proves the assertion.

Let $c_j$ be a convergent sequence in the image cone, and choose closed positive currents $T_j$ with $[T_j]_{\mathrm{dR}}=c_j$. By the compactness assertion, a subsequence converges to a closed positive current representing the limit of the classes. Its Bott--Chern class is pseudoeffective, so the image cone is closed. Convexity follows from addition and nonnegative scaling. Finally, if both $c$ and $-c$ lie in the image, choose closed positive currents $T$ and $S$ representing them. Their sum is de Rham exact, so \eqref{eq:strong-gauduchon-class-mass} gives $T+S=0$, and positivity gives $T=S=0$, hence $c=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:lee-calabi-eckmann}\index{Calabi--Eckmann manifold}
Every Calabi--Eckmann threefold $X=X_\tau$ of \cref{prop:calabi-eckmann-endpoint} satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{LG}(X)=\{0\},\qquad V_X=K_X=W_X=0,
\]
but has no strongly Gauduchon metric. Moreover, $X$ has a nonzero pseudoeffective Bott--Chern class whose de Rham image is zero; this class is not in the image of $j_X$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:calabi-eckmann-endpoint}, $X$ is a compact complex threefold diffeomorphic to $S^3\times S^3$, and the projection $\pi\colon X\to\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ is a principal elliptic fibration. Hence $X$ is connected and
\[
 H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)=H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)
 =H^5_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)=0.
\]
It follows that $V_X=K_X=W_X=0$ and $\operatorname{LG}(X)=\{0\}$; the latter cone is nonempty by Gauduchon's theorem and lies in the zero group $H^5_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)$.

With $\omega_1,\omega_2$ as in \cref{prop:calabi-eckmann-endpoint}, the form $\alpha=\pi^*(\omega_1+\omega_2)$ is closed and semipositive, nonzero because $\pi$ is a submersion, and de Rham exact because $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)=0$. By \cref{prop:lee-strong-obstruction}, $X$ admits no strongly Gauduchon metric.

The Bott--Chern class of $\alpha$ is nonzero: otherwise $\alpha=\ddc f$ for a smooth real function $f$, and the integral of $\alpha$ against any Gauduchon form would vanish, contradicting $\alpha\geq0$ and $\alpha\ne0$. The class is pseudoeffective, since $\alpha$ is a positive representative. Finally, $j_X$ has zero domain, so this class is not in its image.
\end{proof}

Thus $0\in\operatorname{LG}(X)$ does not imply the existence of a strongly Gauduchon metric, and hence does not imply the existence of a balanced metric.

On the threefold of \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric}, which carries a balanced and hence strongly Gauduchon metric, let $\alpha,\beta$ be the two pulled-back Fubini--Study forms. The nonzero class $\{\beta-\alpha\}$ has zero de Rham image, and neither it nor its negative is pseudoeffective, by \cref{prop:elliptic-bott-chern-cones}. Every Hermitian form on this threefold has zero lower mass in the sense of \cref{def:bmp}. Thus the existence of a strongly Gauduchon metric guarantees neither injectivity of the map from Bott--Chern to de Rham cohomology nor the positive lower mass needed for solvability with every finite non-pluripolar measure.
