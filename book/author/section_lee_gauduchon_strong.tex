\subsection{Strongly Gauduchon metrics}
\label{sec:lee-gauduchon-strong}

Strongly Gauduchon metrics test all positive de Rham-exact $(1,1)$-currents. Their existence is therefore a stronger condition than the presence of zero in $\operatorname{LG}(X)$, which tests only classes in the image of $j_X$. A separation argument gives the exact criterion and a bound for current masses in terms of de Rham classes. The Calabi--Eckmann threefold separates the two conditions.

\begin{definition}\label{def:strongly-gauduchon}
A Hermitian metric $\gamma$ is \emph{strongly Gauduchon} if $\gamma^{n-1}$ is the $(n-1,n-1)$-component of a smooth closed real $(2n-2)$-form.
\end{definition}

Write the closed real form in the definition as
\[
 \Theta=\gamma^{n-1}+\beta+\overline\beta,
 \qquad \beta\text{ of type }(n,n-2).
\]
Then $\partial(\gamma^{n-1})=-\bar\partial\beta$. Conversely, this equation and its conjugate give $\mathrm{d}\Theta=0$, because the remaining types exceed dimension $n$. Thus the definition is equivalent to $\partial(\gamma^{n-1})$ being $\bar\partial$-exact. Applying $\bar\partial$ also shows that $\gamma$ is Gauduchon. Every balanced metric is strongly Gauduchon by taking $\Theta=\gamma^{n-1}$.

The following criterion expresses existence through positive exact currents.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lee-strong-obstruction}
A strongly Gauduchon metric has zero Lee--Gauduchon class. A connected
compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$ admits such a metric if
and only if it has no nonzero positive de Rham-exact $(1,1)$-current.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $\Gamma=\gamma^{n-1}$ and use $\beta$ as above. Then
\[
 \dc\Gamma
 =\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}
       (\bar\partial\beta-\partial\overline\beta)
 =\mathrm{d}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}
                              (\beta-\overline\beta)\right).
\]
Thus $[\dc\Gamma]=0$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For the obstruction, let $S$ be a real current of degree one and suppose $T=\mathrm{d}S\geq0$ is nonzero and $\Theta$ is a closed real form with strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-part $\Gamma$. Type considerations and distributional Stokes give
\[
 \int_XT\wedge\Gamma
 =\int_XT\wedge\Theta=0.
\]
The first integral is strictly positive because $T\ne0$ and $\Gamma>0$,
a contradiction.

For the converse, suppose that no strongly Gauduchon metric exists.
In the real Fr\'echet space of smooth $(2n-2)$-forms, let $\mathcal Z$
be the closed subspace of closed forms and let $\mathcal P$ be the open
convex cone of forms with strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-part.
This cone is nonempty, since it contains $h^{n-1}$ for any Hermitian
metric $h$. The two sets are disjoint: the positive $(n-1)$st root of
the pure-type part of a form in their intersection would be a strongly
Gauduchon metric. Hahn--Banach separation therefore gives a nonzero
real current $T$ of degree two such that
\[
 T|_{\mathcal Z}=0,\qquad T(\Theta)\geq0\quad(\Theta\in\mathcal P).
\]
Fix $\Theta_0\in\mathcal P$. If a real form $\Psi$ has zero
$(n-1,n-1)$-part, then $\Theta_0+t\Psi\in\mathcal P$ for every
$t\in\R$, so $T(\Psi)=0$. Thus $T$ is of type $(1,1)$.
For any semipositive $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\Gamma$,
$\Gamma+\varepsilon\Theta_0\in\mathcal P$ when $\varepsilon>0$.
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves $T(\Gamma)\geq0$.

Since $T$ annihilates every exact test form, it is closed. The de Rham
theorem for currents and Poincar\'e duality identify its cohomology
class with its pairings against closed complementary forms. All these
pairings vanish, so $T=\mathrm{d}S$ for a real current $S$.
This gives the required nonzero positive exact current and proves the
converse.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, every nonzero pseudoeffective Bott--Chern class must have
nonzero de Rham image. Indeed, a positive current representing the zero
Bott--Chern class has zero pairing with a Gauduchon metric and must vanish.
The Bott--Chern classes of the nonzero positive exact currents are
therefore exactly the nonzero pseudoeffective classes with zero de Rham
image.

The criterion also characterizes when de Rham classes control the linear
masses of closed positive currents.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:strong-gauduchon-current-compactness}
Fix a Hermitian metric $h$ and a norm on $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)$.
Then $X$ admits a strongly Gauduchon metric if and only if there is a
constant $C>0$ such that every closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-gauduchon-class-mass}
 \int_XT\wedge h^{n-1}\leq C\|[T]_{\mathrm{dR}}\|.
\end{equation}
Under this condition, the family of closed positive $(1,1)$-currents
whose de Rham classes belong to a fixed compact set is weakly compact.
The image of the pseudoeffective Bott--Chern cone in
$H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)$ is a closed convex cone containing no nonzero line.
\end{corollary}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $\gamma$ is strongly Gauduchon, and choose a smooth closed real
form $\Theta$ whose $(n-1,n-1)$-part is $\gamma^{n-1}$.
Compactness gives $h^{n-1}\leq A\gamma^{n-1}$ for some $A>0$.
Positivity and type then give
\[
 \int_XT\wedge h^{n-1}
 \leq A\int_XT\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
 =A\langle[T]_{\mathrm{dR}},[\Theta]_{\mathrm{dR}}\rangle.
\]
The last pairing is a fixed linear functional on the finite-dimensional
de Rham group, proving \eqref{eq:strong-gauduchon-class-mass}.
Conversely, that bound makes every closed positive de Rham-exact current
zero. Apply \cref{prop:lee-strong-obstruction}.

For the compactness assertion, the bound gives uniformly bounded trace
mass. A weak limit of such currents is positive and closed. Its de Rham
class is the limit of their classes: this follows by pairing against a
finite smooth closed basis of complementary cohomology and using
Poincar\'e duality. Thus the family is also weakly closed, and the usual
weak compactness of positive currents proves the assertion.

Let $c_j$ be a convergent sequence in the image cone, and choose closed
positive representatives $T_j$ with $[T_j]_{\mathrm{dR}}=c_j$.
The compactness assertion gives a closed positive limit representing
the limiting de Rham class. Its Bott--Chern class is pseudoeffective,
so the image cone is closed. Convexity follows from addition and
nonnegative scaling. Finally, if both $c$ and $-c$ lie in the image,
choose positive representatives $T$ and $S$ for them. Their sum is exact,
so \eqref{eq:strong-gauduchon-class-mass} makes $T+S=0$. Positivity gives
$T=S=0$ and hence $c=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:lee-calabi-eckmann}
There is a connected compact complex threefold $X$ with
\[
 \operatorname{LG}(X)=\{0\},\qquad V_X=K_X=W_X=0,
\]
but with no strongly Gauduchon metric. Moreover, $X$ has a nonzero pseudoeffective Bott--Chern class whose de Rham image is zero and which is not in the image of $j_X$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Consider the holomorphic action of the additive group $\C$ on
\[
 \widetilde X=(\C^2\setminus\{0\})\times(\C^2\setminus\{0\}),
 \qquad
 t\cdot(z,w)=(\mathrm{e}^t z,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm i t}w).
\]
The action is free. Indeed, $\mathrm{e}^t=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm i t}=1$ forces $t=0$. It is proper: if both $(z,w)$ and $t\cdot(z,w)$ range in compact subsets of $\widetilde X$, the norms of all four vector components are bounded above and away from zero. Writing $t=a+\mathrm i b$, the ratios of the norms bound $\mathrm{e}^a$ and $\mathrm{e}^{-b}$ above and away from zero. Thus $a$ and $b$ stay bounded, which proves properness. The free proper holomorphic action has local holomorphic slices and a Hausdorff complex quotient $X$ of dimension three.

Every orbit meets $S^3\times S^3$ exactly once. The required group element is determined by
\[
 a=-\log|z|,\qquad b=\log|w|.
\]
These expressions are smooth, so the slice identifies the quotient smoothly with $S^3\times S^3$. In particular $X$ is compact and connected, and
\[
 H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)=H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)
 =H^5_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)=0.
\]
It follows directly that $V_X=K_X=W_X=0$ and $\operatorname{LG}(X)=\{0\}$: the latter cone is nonempty by Gauduchon's theorem and lies in the zero group $H^5_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)$.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
Projectivizing both vector components descends to a holomorphic submersion
\[
 p:X\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1.
\]
Its differential is surjective because the product projectivization on $\widetilde X$ is a submersion and the group orbits lie in its fibers. Let $\eta$ be the sum of the Fubini--Study forms on the two factors. The smooth form
\[
 \alpha=p^*\eta
\]
is closed, semipositive and of rank two everywhere. It is nonzero and de Rham exact, since $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)=0$. By \cref{prop:lee-strong-obstruction}, it obstructs a strongly Gauduchon metric.

Its Bott--Chern class is nonzero. Otherwise $\alpha=\ddc f$ for a smooth real function $f$, and integration against any Gauduchon form would give zero, contradicting positivity and $\alpha\ne0$. The class is pseudoeffective because it has the positive representative $\alpha$. Finally $j_X$ has zero domain, so this class is not in its image.
\end{proof}

Here the class $\{\alpha\}$ is invisible to $j_X$ because $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)=0$, but its positive representative obstructs a strongly Gauduchon metric. Thus $0\in\operatorname{LG}(X)$ does not imply existence of either a strongly Gauduchon or a balanced metric.

The balanced threefold of \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric} illustrates
another limit of the criterion. It has a strongly Gauduchon metric. For this example,
write $\alpha,\beta$ for its two pulled-back Fubini--Study forms. The nonzero Bott--Chern class of $\beta-\alpha$ has zero de Rham image,
by \cref{prop:elliptic-bott-chern-cones}. Neither this class nor its negative
is pseudoeffective: both lie outside the explicitly computed nonnegative
cone spanned by $\{\alpha\}$ and $\{\beta\}$. The criterion therefore does
not require injectivity of the full Bott--Chern-to-de-Rham map. Moreover,
the same threefold has zero lower Monge--Amp\`ere mass. Strongly Gauduchon
geometry alone does not provide the lower bound needed for solvability
with every finite non-pluripolar measure.
