\section{An \texorpdfstring{$L^1$}{L1} distance on ideal types and its completion}
\label{sec:ideal-lone-completion}

Projection gives a canonical representative of each ideal class, so the $L^1$ norm defines a metric on the classes. Normalized compactness makes it totally bounded. On a curve, finite atomic curvature measures approximate every normalized potential and determine the completion; the same construction shows that non-pluripolar mass and capacity convergence are not controlled by this metric.

Let
\[
 \mathfrak S_{\mathcal I}(X,\theta)
       =\PSH(X,\theta)/{\sim_{\mathcal I}},\qquad
 \mathfrak M_{\mathcal I}(X,\theta)
       =\{q\in\PSH(X,\theta):P_\theta[q]_{\mathcal I}=q\}.
\]
Write $[u]$ for the multiplier-ideal class of $u$. By \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection}, the assignment $[u]\mapsto P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ is a bijection from the first set to the second. Every member of the second set has supremum zero.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ideal-lone-metric}
The formula
\[
 d_{\mathcal I,\theta,\mathrm{d}V}([u],[v])
      =\int_X|P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}-P_\theta[v]_{\mathcal I}|\,\mathrm{d}V
\]
defines a finite metric on $\mathfrak S_{\mathcal I}(X,\theta)$. This metric space is totally bounded. Its metric completion is canonically the $L^1$ closure of $\mathfrak M_{\mathcal I}(X,\theta)$ inside the compact space $\mathcal K_\theta$ of \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}. Changing $\mathrm{d}V$ to another smooth positive probability volume gives a bi-Lipschitz equivalent metric.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The envelopes are integrable and depend only on the ideal classes. Symmetry and the triangle inequality are those of the $L^1$ norm. If the distance is zero, \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} identifies the two envelopes everywhere. Projection then identifies their ideal classes. Conversely, equal ideal classes have the same envelope.

The normalized compactness theorem places $\mathfrak M_{\mathcal I}$ inside compact $\mathcal K_\theta$. Every subset of a compact metric space is totally bounded, and its completion is its closure in that space. Finally the ratio of two smooth positive volume forms on compact $X$ is bounded above and below by positive constants. Multiplying the integral by these bounds proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The closure of $\mathfrak M_{\mathcal I}$ can be strictly larger than $\mathfrak M_{\mathcal I}$. On a curve, Green potentials of finite atomic measures describe the entire completion.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion}
Let $Y$ be a compact connected Riemann surface and $\theta$ a smooth positive form of total mass one. Fix a smooth positive probability volume on $Y$, again denoted by $\mathrm{d}V$, and use $\mathcal K_\theta$, the envelopes and the ideal metric on $Y$ in place of $X$. Then the normalized ideal envelopes with finitely many logarithmic poles and zero non-pluripolar mass are dense in $\mathcal K_\theta$ for $L^1$. The logarithmic coefficients can be chosen rational, so these approximants have analytic singularities in the convention of \cite{SGPT}. Consequently the completion in \cref{prop:ideal-lone-metric} is all of $\mathcal K_\theta$. The metric on ideal classes is not complete.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\delta_p$ denote the Dirac mass at $p$. For distinct points $p_1,\ldots,p_N\in Y$, consider a finitely supported probability measure
\[
 \nu=\sum_{j=1}^N a_j\delta_{p_j},\qquad a_j>0,\quad\sum_j a_j=1.
\]
The Green-kernel construction gives a potential $q_\nu$ with
\[
 \theta+\ddc q_\nu=\nu,\qquad\sup_Yq_\nu=0.
\]
Indeed, choose a kernel with $\ddc_xG(x,p)=\delta_p-\theta$. The sum $\sum_j a_jG(x,p_j)$ has this curvature, and subtracting its finite supremum gives $q_\nu$. In a coordinate $z$ centered at $p_j$, it is $a_j\log|z|^2$ plus a smooth function; it is smooth away from the finite support. All of its curvature lies on its pole set, so its non-pluripolar measure is zero.

We claim $P_\theta[q_\nu]_{\mathcal I}=q_\nu$. If $w\in\PSH(Y,\theta)$ and $w\sim_{\mathcal I}q_\nu$, the local ideal criterion gives $\nu(w,p_j)=a_j$, where $\nu(w,p_j)$ is the Lelong number. On a curve, this Lelong number is exactly the atomic mass of the positive measure $\theta+\ddc w$. That measure has total mass one by distributional Stokes, so its prescribed atoms already exhaust the mass. It follows that $\theta+\ddc w=\nu$. Thus $\ddc(w-q_\nu)=0$. The difference is a distributional harmonic function, hence smooth and constant on the compact connected curve. Among these translates the greatest one below zero is $q_\nu$. This proves the claim. It also shows that every such $q_\nu$ is an ordinary model potential by \cref{prop:p-below-i}.

Now take any $u\in\mathcal K_\theta$. Its curvature $\nu=\theta+\ddc u$ is a positive probability measure. Choose finitely supported probability measures $\nu_j$ converging weakly to $\nu$. One elementary construction partitions a compact metric space into finitely many Borel sets of diameter tending to zero, places their masses at chosen points, and omits the sets of mass zero. Uniform continuity of each continuous test function proves weak convergence.

We may make these weights rational. For a partition with weights $a_1,\ldots,a_N$, choose a large integer $M$, replace $a_i$ by $b_i=\lfloor Ma_i\rfloor/M$ for $i<N$, and put $b_N=1-\sum_{i<N}b_i$. Discard zero weights. The total absolute change is at most $2(N-1)/M$, which can be made to tend to zero along the partitions. This preserves weak convergence. Writing $b_i=m_i/M$ gives the analytic type
\[
 \left(\frac1M,\mathcal O_Y\left(-\sum_i m_i p_i\right)\right)
\]
with smooth local remainders.

Let $q_j=q_{\nu_j}$. By normalized compactness, every subsequence has an $L^1$-convergent further subsequence with limit $q\in\mathcal K_\theta$. Passing to distributions in the curvature identity gives
\[
 \theta+\ddc q=\nu=\theta+\ddc u.
\]
The difference is constant by the same harmonic argument. Since both suprema are zero, it is zero. Every cluster limit is therefore $u$, so the full sequence converges to $u$ in $L^1$. This proves the density assertion and identifies the completion.

To see incompleteness concretely, choose a nonconstant smooth real function $f$. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, $\theta+\varepsilon\ddc f>0$. Put $u=\varepsilon f-\sup_Y(\varepsilon f)$. It is smooth, normalized and nonconstant. Its multiplier ideals are all trivial, so $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=0$, because zero is an admissible competitor. Thus $u\notin\mathfrak M_{\mathcal I}(Y,\theta)$. This set is not closed in its completion, proving incompleteness.
\end{proof}

For the nonconstant smooth $u$ in the proof, let $q_j$ be the constructed ideal envelopes converging to $u$ in $L^1$. Their positive currents converge to $\theta+\ddc u$, but all their curvature is polar, so their non-pluripolar measures vanish.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
After mean normalization, the same sequence converges in the Gauduchon trace metric of \cref{cor:gaud-trace-metric}. The original sequence $q_j$ cannot converge to $u$ in capacity. Indeed, $q_j,u\leq0$, so \cref{thm:damped-continuity} would give
\[
 0=\mathrm{e}^{q_j}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(q_j)
   \rightharpoonup\mathrm{e}^{u}(\theta+\ddc u),
\]
contrary to strict positivity of the limiting measure. Thus weak trace convergence and $L^1$ convergence do not imply capacity convergence even on a curve.

The ideal projection itself is discontinuous for $L^1$: $P_\theta[q_j]_{\mathcal I}=q_j\to u$, whereas $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=0$. Distinct smooth normalized potentials give distinct completion points although their multiplier ideals are all trivial.

\Needspace{19\baselineskip}
The mass defect can also persist at a fixed positive mass.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:positive-mass-ideal-discontinuity}
In the curve setting above, for every rational $0<\tau<1$ there are normalized analytic ideal envelopes $r_j$ such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 d_{\mathcal I,\theta,\mathrm{d}V}([r_j],[0])\longrightarrow0,\\
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(r_j)(Y)=\tau,
 \qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta(r_j)\rightharpoonup\tau\theta.
 \end{gathered}
\]
In particular non-pluripolar mass is not continuous for this metric, even on a family whose masses have a fixed positive lower bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose finitely supported rational probability measures $\nu_j$ converging weakly to $\theta$, and let $q_j$ be their normalized Green potentials. The preceding proof gives $q_j\to0$ in $L^1$. Put
$a=1-\tau$ and $r_j=P_\theta[a q_j]_{\mathcal I}$. The scaling is admissible in the same background, since
\[
 \theta+\ddc(aq_j)=(1-a)\theta+a\nu_j\geq0.
\]
The function $aq_j$ is a competitor, so $aq_j\leq r_j\leq0$ and
$r_j\to0$ in $L^1$. Projection makes $r_j$ an ideal envelope. By \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type}, it has exactly the logarithmic coefficients of $aq_j$, with bounded local remainders. These coefficients are rational. Away from the finite pole set it is locally bounded. Thus, on the curve, removing the polar mass gives precisely
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(r_j)=\theta+\ddc r_j-a\nu_j.
\]
Distributional Stokes gives total mass $1-a=\tau$. The positive curvature measures $\theta+\ddc r_j$ have mass one and converge distributionally, hence weakly, to $\theta$. Since $\nu_j\rightharpoonup\theta$, the displayed identity proves the claimed measure limit. Finally
$P_\theta[0]_{\mathcal I}=0$, so the metric distance is $\int_Y|r_j|\,\mathrm{d}V$. The limiting potential has non-pluripolar measure $\theta$, of mass one.
\end{proof}

For comparison, let $d_{S,\theta}$ denote the source singularity pseudometric in background $\theta$. The ordered-distance formula in
\cite[Lemma~6.2.2]{SGPT} gives
\[
 d_{S,\theta}(r_j,0)
 =\frac12\left(\int_Y\theta-\int_Y\mathrm{MA}_\theta(r_j)\right)
 =\frac{1-\tau}{2}>0.
\]
Here $r_j\leq0$, so the ordered formula applies. The source distance retains the fixed mass gap that disappears in the elementary ideal metric.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\subsection{Comparison with scalar normalization}

The scalar pseudometric $\Delta$ of \eqref{eq:normalization-distance} records the response of the normalizing constant to changes of density. Analytic references give explicit comparisons, but in a closed background this response depends only on total mass. It therefore loses even the locations of logarithmic poles.

\begin{example}\label{ex:analytic-reference-normalization}
Let $\theta$ be semipositive and big, and let $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be analytic and normalized by $a\leq0$. Use $B_\varphi$ and $\mathcal A_\varphi$ from \cref{sec:normalization-scalar,sec:normalization-supports} with the fixed volume $\mathrm{d}V$. Set
\[
 \varphi_t=P_\theta[ta],\qquad t\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1).
\]
\Cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} gives $\varphi_t\sim ta$, so invariance under bounded changes gives $P_\theta[\varphi_t]=\varphi_t$. Moreover $ta\in\PSH(X,t\theta)$ and $ta\leq\varphi_t$, hence $P_{t\theta}(\varphi_t)$ is genuine. By \cref{prop:reference-normalization-comparison},
\[
 \sup_{G\in C(X)}|B_{\varphi_t}(G)-B_0(G)|
       \leq-n\log(1-t)\longrightarrow0
       \qquad(t\downarrow0).
\]
When $\theta$ is Hermitian and $G$ is fixed and smooth, every choice in $\mathcal A_{\varphi_t}(G)$ converges weakly in $L^1$ to the smooth Gauduchon adjoint probability, by \cref{cor:small-reference-adjoint-limits,thm:normalization-gauduchon-recovery}.
\end{example}
