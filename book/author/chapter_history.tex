\chapter{Historical notes}
\label{chap:history}

Local products and regularization extend to Hermitian backgrounds through smooth corrections. Global integration introduces torsion and variable Monge--Amp\`ere mass, so equation proofs must replace the cohomological controls of the closed theory. Gauduchon traces control linear pairings; relative envelopes control singularity types. The sources below develop these two constructions under different positivity and mass hypotheses, whose distinctions persist in the geometric applications.

\section{Plurisubharmonic functions and regularization}
\label{sec:history-psh}

Local compactness, integrability and regularization survive the passage to a nonclosed background because they apply to a psh function after a smooth local correction. The normalized $L^1$ estimate of \cref{thm:normalized-compactness}, also recorded in \cite[Proposition~6.2]{Dinew16}, follows by propagating local compactness over a connected coordinate cover. The exponential-integrability input recalled in \cite[Section~1]{Zeriahi01} becomes uniform over normalized potentials through the weighted-sum contradiction of \cref{lem:uniform-exponential}.

The comparison of convolutions in different coordinates in \cref{lem:bounded-local-regularization,cor:coordinate-convolution} is the bounded part of the argument in \cite[\S2, Lemmas~4--5]{BK07}. The full regularization theorem of \cite[Theorem~1]{BK07} permits unbounded potentials and a positive continuous background. The proof of \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization} retains the bounded case needed for the product constructions, including the decreasing diagonal and the rescaling that preserves a Hermitian background.

For singular potentials, the Chern-connection expansion of Demailly \cite[Theorem~4.1]{Dem94} applies to the closed current $\ddc p$ with an arbitrary smooth lower bound $-\theta$. In particular, the lower bound need not be closed. The monotonicity and logarithmic convexity of the regularization enter \eqref{eq:smooth-background-transform-rate}; its linear error rate follows from the curvature inequality in \cite[Lemma~4.4]{Dem94} and the expansion in \cite[Proposition~3.8, equations (3.11), (4.3), and (4.5)]{Dem94}. Here the curvature inequality requires no small $\epsilon$ term or $M_\epsilon$ correction, and local trace bounds make the constants uniform in the center and radius.

The same expansion underlies the truncated transform of \cref{lem:chern-kiselman-input}; see \cite[Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.7]{Dem94} and \cite[Lemma 4.1]{KN19}. In \cref{lem:general-kiselman-modulus}, the full base--fiber error is retained as a modulus tending to zero, with a linear-radius term absorbing the mixed and vertical errors as in \cite[Remark 4.7]{Dem94}. This formulation accommodates the unbounded, finite-$L^1$ potentials in \cref{thm:big-continuous-envelope-contact}. Localizing the averaging radius then permits the minimum-principle argument off the singular barrier.

The resolvable regularization of \cref{thm:resolvable-regularization-input} is \cite[Theorem 3.2]{DP04}. It allows an arbitrary smooth lower bound on a Hermitian manifold and supplies a smooth remainder after resolution. This is a separate regularizing sequence from the prescribed multiplier-ideal sequence in \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input}; simultaneous possession of their additional properties is not assumed.

The analyticity of positive Lelong level sets recalled in \cref{subsec:psh-lelong-levels} and used in \cref{lem:lower-mass-trace-concentration} is Siu's theorem \cite[Main Theorem, p.~53]{Siu74}. Smooth local corrections allow its application to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions.

The fixed non-pluripolar measure convergence in \cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals} uses Cegrell's classical fixed-measure argument, also written in extraction form in \cite[Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.2]{KN22}. Applying the extraction statement to each subsequence gives convergence of the whole bounded sequence.

\section{Mass and energy}
\label{sec:history-mass-energy}

On a nonclosed background, variation of the Monge--Amp\`ere mass and failure of the usual energy primitive are related manifestations of torsion. Chiose's polarization argument \cite[Theorems~1--2]{Chiose16} gives the mass-invariance characterization in \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}. Yi Li constructs torsion-corrected energies in \cite[Equation~(1.18) and Theorem~1.2]{YiLi10}. The correction changes the one-form being integrated, consistently with the obstruction for the raw one-form in \cref{thm:raw-primitive}; \cref{prop:radial-torsion-defect} makes the difference explicit by first variation.

The failure of bounded mass on a Hopf threefold is Xia--Zhang's counterexample \cite[Theorem 1.1]{XZ26}. Its Gauduchon background converts vertical Dirichlet energy into Monge--Amp\`ere mass, as developed in \cref{chap:hopf}. Combining it with \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness} produces infinite extended mass with trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight. The coefficient-integrability and convergence results of \cref{sec:integrable-infinite-mass} describe the linear current carried by this construction. The source uses $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial$ \cite[Section 1]{XZ26}; dividing its background forms by $2\pi$ and leaving its potentials unchanged preserves positivity and changes the coefficient of the mass identity.

Failure of positive lower mass is a different phenomenon. The elliptic construction in \cref{sec:vanishing-lower-mass} reconstructs the supplied manuscript \cite{LowerMass26}. Sufficient conditions for a lower bound instead come from the sign of torsion: \eqref{eq:threefold-mass-dirichlet} is the dimension-three case of \cite[Theorem~3.2]{AGL23}, where $\ddc\omega\leq0$ is called plurinegativity.

The principal elliptic bundles in \cref{sec:primitive-elliptic-bundles} are a special case of the torus bundles of Goldstein--Prokushkin \cite[Section 2]{GP04}. Their metric calculation gives an upper bound and does not decide whether lower mass vanishes.

\section{Gauduchon metrics and positivity}
\label{sec:history-gauduchon}

Gauduchon metrics replace cohomological mass conservation by a linear trace identity. In the conformal theorem recalled immediately before \cite[Lemma~2.3]{TW10}, the metric is written $\gamma=\mathrm e^f\eta$; the multiplier of \cref{thm:gauduchon} is $\rho=\mathrm e^{(n-1)f}$. The resulting trace comparison, \cref{cor:trace-bounds}, also underlies the first mixed-volume bounds of \cite[Proposition~3.3]{GL22}.

The trace identity has a dual form: positivity can be tested against Gauduchon metrics. For an arbitrary smooth background, the criterion in \cref{thm:lamari} is recorded in \cite[Lemma~1.15]{BGL25}. Its proof combines separation with a scalar elliptic right inverse. The same linear control supplies the normalization bound \eqref{eq:gauduchon-c-bound} used in the weak existence arguments of \cite[Section~4]{BGL25}.

\section{Bounded products and extended measures}
\label{sec:history-products}

The bounded product is local even when its background is not closed. Expanding against smooth local corrections, as in \cite[Section~1]{KN15} and \cite[Section~2.1]{BGL25}, gives \cref{prop:bounded-products}. Restricted truncations then define the extended measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, by the mechanism of \cite[Proposition~2.1]{BGL25}. Finiteness becomes decisive when the restrictions are removed in the category of currents: \cite[Theorem~2.2]{BGL25} uses a uniform variation bound. An infinite extended positive measure retains the local construction but is not a global current of order zero.

The metric independence and reduction to smooth potentials in \cref{prop:bmp-metric} recover the comparison recorded after \cite[Definition~1.16]{BGL25}. Exact decreasing regularization supplies the passage from smooth to bounded potentials. The Gauduchon trace bound and cancellation of the exact top wedge used in \cref{prop:gauduchon-range-growth} also appear in \cite[Proposition 3.3(i), (vi)]{GL22}. The range estimate retains their polynomial dependence on the oscillation.

The local mixed inequality in \cref{thm:local-mixed-input} is \cite[Theorem 1.2]{Dinew09}, for locally bounded psh functions and nonnegative measurable smooth-volume densities. Its extension to smooth backgrounds, proved in \cref{sec:psh-bounded-products}, is used in \cref{sec:interior-calculus}. Freezing the two smooth backgrounds gives \cref{lem:nonclosed-mixed-density} and the trace inequalities \eqref{eq:big-singular-gauduchon-trace} and \cref{lem:unbounded-gauduchon-trace-density}; the latter retains the linear trace mass on the pole set. The coefficient-freezing proof also recovers the smooth-volume inequality used in \cite[Lemma 1.9]{Nguyen16}.

\section{Envelopes and comparison}
\label{sec:history-envelopes}

Contact sets connect order of potentials to order of their measures. The semipositive big case of \cref{thm:envelope-contact} is \cite[Theorem 2.2]{ALS26}; the bounded-to-unbounded passage here removes bigness and a uniform mass assumption. For a general big background, \cite[Theorem 2.9 and Proposition 2.11]{BGL25} proves contact concentration under bounded mass. Local replacement and a strict singular barrier give \cref{thm:big-general-envelope-contact} without that assumption.

The fixed-point estimate \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite} is \cite[Proposition 2.12]{ALS26}. In the present proof, $P_\theta(\varphi+1,0)=\varphi$ and contact with the first obstacle is pluripolar, so only contact with zero contributes. For bounded potentials, the envelope proof of domination in \cref{thm:semipositive-bounded-domination} is due to Guedj--Lu \cite[Proposition 2.8]{GL22}.

Difference envelopes provide a relative class in which singular potentials can still be compared. The construction is recorded for semipositive big backgrounds in \cite[Proposition 2.4 and Lemma 2.5]{ALS26} and for arbitrary big backgrounds in \cite[Section 2.1]{XL26}. The finite-sublevel estimate turns nonempty competitors into a genuine envelope. The class in \cref{def:relative-envelope-class} follows \cite[Definition 3.2]{ALS26}; its convex stretching property, \cref{lem:relative-stretch-closure}, is the argument of \cite[Proposition 3.5]{ALS26}.

The model condition in \eqref{eq:als-model-condition} and its individual positivity consequence appear in \cite[Proposition 2.10 and the discussion after Definition 2.13]{ALS26}. That paper does not assume bounded mass for its same-type equations with $L^p$ data. Individual positivity must be distinguished from a positive infimum over the whole ordinary type. The lower-volume mechanism in \cref{thm:relative-uniform-mass-lower} is that of \cite[Proposition 3.8]{ALS26}.

Lower semicontinuous obstacles require an approximation of the contact argument. The proof of \cref{prop:lsc-obstacle-support} follows \cite[Theorem 2.3]{GL22}, using the increasing bounded convergence theorem \cite[Theorem~7.4]{BT82}. Smooth local corrections and expansion against the remaining background give the nonclosed application.

The modified comparison principle in \cref{thm:modified-comparison} is due to Ko\l odziej--Nguyen \cite[Theorem 0.2]{KN15}. The proof follows the finite induction of Guedj--Lu \cite[Theorem 1.5]{GL22}, with enlarged dimensional constants.

\section{Smooth and continuous equations}
\label{sec:history-smooth-equations}

The smooth Hermitian equation must determine a normalization constant together with the potential. For $n\geq2$, the existence theorem \cref{thm:tw-smooth} is \cite[Corollary 1]{TW10}. Their equation uses $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\varphi$; setting $u=2\pi\varphi$ gives the present convention, while a prescribed smooth volume $\mathrm dV$ is absorbed into $F+\log(\mathrm dV/\omega^n)$. The Gauduchon concavity argument for the normalization constant in \cref{thm:b-derivatives} also appears in \cite[Theorem 2.13]{Collins21}.

The weighted integration proof of the potential estimate in \cref{chap:smooth-equations} follows Cherrier and Tosatti--Weinkove \cite{Cherrier87,TW10}. A finite recurrence absorbs torsion before Sobolev iteration. The torsion calculation for the second-order estimate in \cref{chap:smooth-equations} goes back to Cherrier. Its completed-square form and the maximum argument follow \cite[Sections 3 and 9]{TW15}.

Prescribing the volume of a Gauduchon metric leads to a different equation, with first-order torsion terms. The existence result \cref{thm:stw-volume} is \cite[Theorem 1.4]{STW17}, again with the potential multiplied by $2\pi$. The potential bound of \cref{sec:stw-czero} follows \cite[Propositions~10--11 and Remark~12]{Sze18}, specialized to the Gauduchon determinant. The second-order argument in \cref{sec:stw-second-order-proof} follows \cite[Section~3]{STW17}; the identities \eqref{eq:stw-diagonal-torsion} from \cite[Section~4]{STW17} explain the structure of the first-order perturbation.

The gradient argument combines rescaling and a Liouville theorem. In \eqref{eq:stw-rescaled-matrix}, the first-order term is retained before rescaling and vanishes at rate $A_j^{-1}$, as in \cite[Section~6]{Sze18}. The Euclidean argument in \cref{sec:stw-liouville} specializes \cite[Theorem~20]{Sze18} to the Gauduchon cone. Once uniform ellipticity is available, \cref{lem:stw-higher-regularity} uses the variable-coefficient Evans--Krylov theorem \cite[Definition~3.2 and Theorem~3.4]{TWWY15}.

For $L^p$ densities, the continuous normalized and exponential equations are respectively \cite[Theorem 0.1]{KN15} and \cite[Theorem 0.1]{Nguyen16}; see \cref{thm:kn-continuous} and \eqref{eq:weak-exp}. Their operator is twice the present one, so $u=2\varphi$ and the exponential reference parameter is $2\lambda$. These results require neither bounded mass nor positive lower volume. Local capacity estimates supply the compactness: the deductions in \cite[Section 5]{KN15} and \cite[Section 6]{Dinew16} lead to \cref{thm:lp-uniform-compactness}, the statement of \cite[Corollary 5.10]{KN15}. A common density bound is essential here; normalized $L^1$ compactness alone does not suffice.

The exponential term supplies comparison even when the densities have no positive lower bound; \cref{lem:exp-order} recovers \cite[Lemma 2.3]{Nguyen16}. Letting the exponent tend to zero then identifies the normalization constant through \eqref{eq:exp-barriers}, as in \cite[Corollary 0.2]{Nguyen16}. This proves scalar uniqueness without proving uniqueness of the limiting potential. The weighted mean \eqref{eq:gauduchon-exponential-mean}, from \cite[Proposition 2.5]{Nguyen16}, controls the normalization; here its lower bound follows directly from the sublevel estimate.

Prescribed singularities require relative-envelope arguments and distinct assumptions on the reference and the ambient metric. The results of \cite{ALS26,XL26} are separated accordingly in \cref{chap:prescribed-equations,chap:relative-finite-measures,chap:bounded-mass-equations}; their provenance is discussed in \cref{sec:history-prescribed}.

\section{Uniqueness and selection}
\label{sec:history-uniqueness}

At exponent zero, uniqueness of the scalar leaves a separate question about the potential. Density degeneration affects comparison, while the positive-exponent family can impose a choice of critical solution. The restricted-density mass-concentration proof around \eqref{uni-eq:mixed-bounded} follows \cite[proof of Theorem~3.7 and Remark~3.8]{LPT20}. The exterior-ball proof of \eqref{uni-eq:normalzero} instead uses maximum propagation, following \cite[Proposition~2.4 and Theorem~2.5 in the cited preprint]{BG18}.

For smooth nonnegative densities with connected dense positive set, the bounded uniqueness theorem \cref{thm:connected-positive-uniqueness} and its level-energy and test-potential arguments reconstruct \cite[Appendix A]{SuppliedCones26}. The mixed inequality and coefficient-density passage use the local calculus in Chapter~1. Bounded continuity is obtained internally by proper comparison in \cref{cor:bounded-lp-continuity}. The smooth local Dirichlet input for \cref{prop:weak-uniform-test-potentials} is \cite[Theorem 1.1, pp.~687--688]{Guan98}; the quadratic strict subsolution verifies its hypothesis. The real contact-set estimate then gives a constant depending only on the fixed geometry and the $L^2$ bound, rather than on the height of the test density.

For measurable densities bounded above and bounded away from zero, uniqueness is proved in \cref{cor:bounded-positive-density-uniqueness} from the level-energy argument; its classical counterpart is \cite[Corollary 3.9 and Remark 3.13]{KN19}, with the locators referring to the author version specified in that entry.

When the critical equation has several normalized solutions, the exponential family can still select one. The selection theorem \cref{thm:vanishing-exponential-selection} reconstructs the supplied manuscript \cite[Theorem A, Propositions 3.1--4.1 and Lemma 5.1]{SuppliedSelection26}. The critical scalar defines a lattice norm on density roots; its norming face gives the probability family that normalizes the greatest critical subsolution. The subsolution envelopes then identify every cluster limit. The comparison and existence inputs are proved in \cref{sec:big-minimal-comparison,sec:big-minimal-existence}; \cref{thm:big-minimal-holder} gives the required common local modulus from the uniform reference interval and the bound on the actual equation densities. The scope is a fixed nonzero $L^p$ density, $p>1$, and ordinary minimal singularities in a smooth big background. This selects one critical solution without asserting uniqueness of all normalized critical solutions.

\section{Volumes under mass bounds}
\label{sec:history-volumes}

Upper and lower singularity volumes retain information that a single mass loses for a nonclosed background. The definitions used in \cref{sec:bounded-mass-volume-comparison} are \cite[Definition 3.1]{BGL25}, expressed in potentials. For closed backgrounds they coincide by invariance of non-pluripolar mass under bounded changes of singularities, as recalled in \cite[Example 3.2]{BGL25}. In the big regime, monotonicity \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity} is \cite[Proposition 3.7]{BGL25}; the lower-volume addition proof of \cref{prop:volume-addition} is \cite[Proposition 2.5]{XL26}.

The relative full-mass class \cref{def:relative-volume-class} is \cite[Definition 3.2]{XL26}. The stretching criterion \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} reconstructs \cite[Lemma 3.1]{XL26}, retaining the upper-volume deficit and choosing a representative close to its upper volume. Its zero-deficit case gives \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}. Uniform Lelong bounds turn the convex comparisons into \cref{prop:volume-deficit-lelong}.

The domination theorem \cref{thm:big-relative-domination} reconstructs \cite[Proposition 3.2]{XL26}; its proof uses positive lower volume of the reference, without requiring it to be an envelope fixed point. The relative obstacle estimate \cref{lem:relative-obstacle-contact} is \cite[Proposition 3.3]{XL26}. Together they give the upper-volume identity of \cite[Lemma 3.2]{XL26} and the lower-volume identity of \cite[Corollary 3.3]{XL26}, collected in \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}. The proof first checks that the increasing obstacle limit has the ordinary type of $P_\theta[u]$, so domination applies before volume equality is established. The resulting full-mass characterization gives the volume criterion in \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization}.

The small-stretch construction \cref{lem:positive-volume-stretch} is \cite[Proposition 3.1]{XL26}. Its mixed-mass bound gives the explicit uniform stretching scale. The additive lower-volume estimate in \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison} is the smooth-perturbation case of \cite[Proposition 3.3(ii)]{BGL25}, with the mixed bound of \cite[Lemma 2.4]{BGL25} written in potentials. The strict barrier gives the multiplicative comparison at positive lower volume, as in \cite[Proposition 3.5(ii), Step 1]{XL26}. Thus \cref{cor:ordinary-envelope-background-limit} gives lower-volume continuity for every fixed potential; upper-volume and ordinary-envelope continuity retain positive lower volume.

The increasing-limit theorem \cref{thm:increasing-envelope-volume-continuity} reconstructs \cite[Proposition 3.5(i)]{XL26}. Contact identifies the envelope limit and its upper volume; the deficit estimate then proves positivity of the approximating lower volumes. The decreasing-model theorem \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity} reconstructs \cite[Proposition 3.5(ii)]{XL26}, including its zero-lower-volume conclusion. The uniform small stretch shows that a positive lower bound along the models forces positive lower volume of their limit. Strict corrections and tail envelopes then reduce background variation to the fixed-background argument. The source states perturbations $\theta+\varepsilon_j\omega$; the proof here treats arbitrary smooth backgrounds decreasing uniformly in form order. The positive-lower-volume criterion \cref{prop:decreasing-envelope-volume-criterion} reconstructs \cite[Proposition 3.5(iii)]{XL26}. The deficit bound gives uniform Lelong convergence on each fixed compact model, and restricted analytic envelopes yield the exact traces of \cref{cor:mass-bound-exact-trace}.

The comparison function in \cref{def:model-volume-comparison} is introduced in \cite[Section 4.2]{XL26}. Its separation follows from the envelope characterization. The normalized stretching argument of \cite[Lemma 4.2]{XL26} gives the quantitative $L^1$ bound in \cref{prop:model-volume-separation}, and the three-point estimate \eqref{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus} passes convergence from a subsequence to the whole Cauchy sequence. The tail-maximum construction of \cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness} follows \cite[Theorem 4.3]{XL26}, with the closed lower bound $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\geq\delta$. The strict cutoff $>\delta$ used in version 1 of that source is not preserved at a Cauchy limit, as \cref{ex:strict-volume-cutoff-incomplete} shows. This Cauchy completeness does not assert a triangle inequality for the displayed comparison function.

The finite-rooftop criterion \cref{lem:finite-roof-volume-deficit} reconstructs \cite[Lemma 4.1]{XL26}: representatives close to their upper volumes make collapse contradict the lower volume of the common upper bound. The softened-stretch argument of \cite[Proposition 4.4]{XL26} gives \cref{prop:volume-rooftop-continuity}; only the limiting potential needs positive lower volume, and the same comparison gives lower-volume convergence of the roofs. The two stretching scales in \cite[Theorem 4.4]{XL26} produce the monotone bounds of \cref{thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}. Finite rooftops remain normalized models, so decreasing-model continuity passes their volume bounds to the infinite tail. Monotone capacity convergence then applies, and the subsequence criterion gives convergence of the original sequence.

The semipositive consequence \cref{prop:positive-volume-smaller-background} is the forward direction of \cite[Proposition 2.11]{ALS26}, deduced from the strict barrier. This direction requires neither the ordinary envelope identity nor $v_-(\theta)>0$; the latter assumption is used for the converse. Applying it to an ordinary envelope fixed point compares \eqref{eq:li-model-condition} with \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}.

The analytic approximation results of \cref{sec:ideal-volume-approximation} combine the preceding volume continuity with the dyadic ideal-envelope limit. Rational mixing with a strict analytic potential removes the curvature error in any smooth big background. Two background comparisons then give the exact upper-volume excess in \eqref{eq:fixed-big-analytic-comparison}. Conversely, stretching and concavity of the ideal envelope make its fixed points closed under upper-volume comparison at positive lower volume. This proves the analytic criterion \cref{thm:analytic-model-volume-criterion} and the inherited Cauchy completeness there. The zero-lower-volume boundary and ideal-theoretic positivity criteria in \cref{qu:hermitian-i-characterization} remain separate questions.

The background results in \cref{sec:model-background-comparison} follow from the same volume and envelope arguments. Smooth-gauge transport preserves the competitor currents, and ordinary projection corrects the zero obstacle. The explicit small-stretch scale makes the strict barrier uniform on positive lower-volume sectors. This yields \eqref{eq:vanishing-background-comparison} and extends the analytic criterion to vanishing positive background errors. These statements concern singularity volumes and canonical models; they do not identify the raw envelope mass with a gauge invariant or prescribe convergence of arbitrary representatives.

The two-sided transport in \cref{sec:two-sided-model-backgrounds} mixes the original model with a strict analytic potential. A common upper background bounds both this transport and the maximum of two transported models. The resulting volume bounds prove uniform comparison, convergence of the transported reference, and independence of the strict correction. The nearby-background estimate and the three-point modulus then reduce its analytic criterion to \cref{thm:analytic-approximation-curvature-error}.

\section{Equations with prescribed singularities}
\label{sec:history-prescribed}

Relative envelopes allow an equation to retain prescribed singularities while its measure changes. The ordinary-type equations with $L^p$ data, the general-measure equations and the equations under metric mass bounds use this framework in different ways. The distinction concerns both the estimates available and the singularity type of the resulting solution.

\subsection{Ordinary-type equations}

The ordinary-type equations of \cref{thm:als-prescribed-lp} are \cite[Theorems 5.4 and 5.7]{ALS26}. Replacing the reference volume $\omega^n$ by $\mathrm{d}V$ multiplies the density by a smooth positive factor and preserves its $L^p$ class. A positive constant change of the Hessian normalization is absorbed by rescaling both the potential and the exponential parameter.

The relative estimate \cref{thm:relative-linfty} and rooftop supersolution \cref{lem:relative-lp-supersolution} are the two constructions in \cite[Lemmas 5.5--5.6]{ALS26}. Together they control the solution within the ordinary type. For a big background, the bounded-subsolution argument of \cite[Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.4]{GL23E} gives \cref{lem:big-lp-subsolution,thm:big-bounded-exponential}.

Strict singular barriers extend the comparison argument to minimal singularities. The development from \cref{sec:big-minimal-comparison} follows \cite[Section 4]{BGL25}, where the bounded mass assumption from the earlier sections is dropped. Its minimal ordinary-type equations, culminating here in \cref{thm:big-minimal-normalized}, are \cite[Theorems 4.6--4.7]{BGL25}. The solution has finite mass, even though the whole ordinary type is not assumed to have uniformly bounded mass.

\subsection{General measures in semipositive backgrounds}

The local decomposition used in \cref{lem:local-bounded-measure-carrier} is \cite[Theorem 6.3]{Ceg98}, on a bounded hyperconvex domain in dimension at least two. For a non-pluripolar measure, the pluripolar summand vanishes. The global constructions in \cref{lem:bounded-measure-carrier,prop:relative-measure-carrier} patch these local representations.

The bounded-subsolution theorem \cref{thm:bounded-subsolution}, from \cite[Theorem 5.1]{ALS26}, solves the exponential equation for a bounded density relative to the measure of a bounded potential. Truncating the density in a measure representation brings this theorem into the general-measure construction. Domination then permits passage to the limit. The finite-measure domination theorem \cref{thm:relative-finite-domination} in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures} reconstructs \cite[Theorem 3.14]{ALS26}. The fixed finite measure prevents collapse; the other members of the envelope class need not have finite mass. The proof of \cref{thm:relative-extended-domination} also allows the compared measure to be infinite. Singular strict domination, \cref{thm:singular-strict-domination}, follows \cite[Theorem 3.11]{ALS26}.

Passing to a measure limit requires noncollapse of lower envelopes. The fixed-dominating-measure theorem \cref{thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity} reconstructs \cite[Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.1, condition (2)]{ALS26}. Positive lower mass gives noncollapse in this case; bounded upper mass is not used. The common-envelope argument preceding \cref{cor:exponential-relative-class-closure} instead supplies the first alternative of \cite[Lemma 4.2]{ALS26}. For approximants of the reference's ordinary type, that alternative requires no positive lower-volume hypothesis.

A common ordinary-type barrier also gives \cref{cor:common-barrier-continuity}, the corresponding case of \cite[Theorem 4.1]{ALS26}. The reference need not be an envelope fixed point: the smaller-background condition suffices. The moving bounded integrals are handled by the torsion calculation in \cref{lem:moving-bounded-integrals}, following \cite[Lemma 2.3]{KN22}.

The resulting general-measure equations \cref{thm:als-general-measure} are \cite[Theorem 5.8 and Corollary 5.9]{ALS26}. The minimal and prescribed-reference constructions appear in \cref{sec:general-measure-minimal,sec:general-measure-relative}; they use the density approximations of that proof. The solutions have finite mass, but arbitrary measure data do not give bounded difference from the reference. This is the regime used in \cref{chap:research-program}, with the smaller-background model condition and positive lower mass. The weighted addition theorem \cref{thm:weighted-measure-addition} reconstructs \cite[Theorem 5.2]{ALS26}, localizing the passage to the equation.

\subsection{Big backgrounds under mass bounds}

Li's general-measure theory \cite[Theorems 6.1--6.2 and Corollary 6.1]{XL26} assumes both metric volume properties. It gives \cref{thm:li-general-measure} and the model-reference constructions of \cref{sec:big-model-general-measures}. Under bounded mass, positivity of the model reference's lower volume implies the metric positive-lower-volume property. Expressing the hypothesis through the reference thus reorganizes this regime without enlarging it.

The relative foundations use the domination, stretching and obstacle estimates recorded in \cref{sec:history-volumes}. Constructing the requisite rooftop precedes the application of \cref{lem:relative-obstacle-contact} to relative projection in \eqref{eq:big-projection-contact-tail}.

Moment and continuity estimates connect these envelope operations to existence. The first-moment compactness argument using \eqref{eq:big-energy-max-bound} follows \cite[Proposition 4.2]{XL26}, by choosing an equivalent reference potential whose mass lies sufficiently close to the upper volume; the upper volume need not be attained. Fixed-measure continuity is \cite[Theorem 4.2]{XL26}: finite rooftops use only the envelope class, while the mass interval prevents collapse and controls tails.

The varying-reference closure theorem \cref{thm:big-varying-model-closure} reconstructs \cite[Corollary 4.1]{XL26}. Projection to ordered lower models provides a fixed relative class for each tail, and an exponential contact estimate prevents collapse. The positive-exponent stability in \cite[Corollary 6.2]{XL26} assumes uniformly bounded $L^p$ data and ordinary reference type. In \cref{thm:big-varying-equation-stability}, the closure argument and scalar or potential uniqueness instead give stability in the relative full-mass class. Its density hypothesis is $L^1$ convergence against a fixed non-pluripolar measure; a summable extraction produces a common dominator. The total-variation conclusion follows by identifying the right-hand sides in $L^1$.

For a minimal reference, the general-measure equations of \cref{sec:big-minimal-general-measures} are \cite[Theorems 5.2--5.3]{XL26}. The higher-moment estimate in \cref{sec:big-minimal-moments} follows the concave-weight argument of \cite[Proposition 5.1 and Lemma 5.1]{XL26}, with integrability against the fixed singular measure stated explicitly. The measure smoothing used in the existence proof is collected in \cref{lem:measure-smoothing-integrals,lem:bounded-carrier-integrability}.

The $L^p$ ordinary-type conclusion of \cref{cor:big-model-lp-type} is \cite[Corollary 6.1]{XL26}. The relative estimate \cref{prop:big-model-moment-bound} starts in the envelope class and uses a uniform moment of order $m>2n+1$. The concave-weight method is related to \cite[Theorem 5.1]{XL26}, whose stated hypothesis already assumes ordinary reference type and whose moment exponent is $m>n$. That statement is not used to infer the ordinary type of an arbitrary envelope-class solution.

Addition requires a common lower control during truncation. The weighted construction in \cref{sec:big-weighted-addition} reconstructs \cite[Proposition 6.1]{XL26}. For unrestricted addition, \eqref{eq:unrestricted-addition-cutoff} follows \cite[Theorem 6.3]{XL26}; an extra rooftop supplies the common lower bound needed to pass through the cutoff.

\section{Normalization and adjoint measures}
\label{sec:history-normalization}

The normalization constant can be studied through its adjoint probability measures even when the potential itself is not unique. The smooth differential method appears in \cite{Collins21}; \cref{thm:relative-normalization-concavity} replaces differentiation of smooth solutions by mixed products and domination.

The geometric comparison and divisorial stability in \cref{sec:geometric-normalization-stability,sec:divisorial-normalization-stability} reconstruct \cite[Theorem A, Proposition 2.2, Theorem 3.2, Proposition 4.2 and Theorems 5.1--6.1]{SuppliedGeometricNormalization26}. Relative form inequalities give a bound uniform over all bounded tests, while ordinary divisorial removal identifies the entire singularity class with bounded residual potentials. The dual compactness arguments use the preceding internal results rather than a separate construction. The residual limit uses the bounded-density uniqueness discussed in \cref{sec:history-uniqueness}.

The mass quotient and the closed and nonclosed examples in \cref{sec:normalization-mass-quotient} reconstruct \cite[Theorem 7.1, Proposition 8.1 and Section 8]{SuppliedGeometricNormalization26}. The closed prescribed-type input, including uniqueness, is \cite[Theorems 1.4(i) and 4.29, pp.~2052 and 2079--2080]{DDNL18mono}; bounded-difference mass invariance is its \cite[Proposition 2.1, p.~2055]{DDNL18mono}. Its hypotheses are a compact K\"ahler manifold, a smooth closed big class, a model reference with small unbounded locus, and positive mass. The positive-residual factorization concerns actual ordinary divisorial types and does not assert a model-envelope identity for every real weighted sum.

\section{Singularity classes and traces}
\label{sec:history-singularities}

A singularity theory for nonclosed backgrounds must retain torsion as well as divisorial information. The quotient of smooth real $(1,1)$-forms by smooth $\ddc$-terms in \cref{chap:nonclosed-bclasses} is used in \cite[Section 1]{BGL25}; fixing the exterior derivative preserves that datum on each modification.

Resolution makes addition of traces accessible to multiplier-ideal approximation. The comparison in \cref{thm:trace-data-additivity} is the argument underlying Cao's addition theorem \cite[proof of Theorem~1.4]{Cao15}; it does not require a global closed background. Recovering a potential from an entire envelope system is a further question. The ruled-surface construction of \cref{sec:envelope-boundary-example}, based on \cite[Example 1.7]{DPS94}, exhibits the boundary behavior relevant to that recovery.

The closed model-envelope continuity used in \cref{thm:kahler-target-exact-trace} is \cite[Proposition~2.1]{DX24}, also presented in \cite[Proposition~3.1.12]{SGPT}. The Lelong-number argument uses small convex corrections from \cite[Lemma~4.3]{DDNL21}. Applying this continuity on the submanifold suffices here: the restricted approximants have a common curvature bound even when the ambient background is nonclosed.

\section{Section growth and partial kernels}
\label{sec:history-sections}

Section growth links singular potentials to algebraic dimension through a projective modification. A K\"ahler modification also yields the bounded-mass conclusion of \cref{lem:kahler-modification-bounded-mass}, by the upper-volume pullback argument of \cite[Lemma 3.6]{GL22}.

For section rings constructed through algebraic reduction, see \cite{Wu24}. The flag construction in \cref{chap:flag-bodies} uses a specified compact flag on an auxiliary modification; it makes no assertion about centers of arbitrary meromorphic-field valuations.

On a fibration, singularity constraints determine which base sections are available for a partial kernel. The calculation and sharp example in \cref{sec:horizontal-center-constraints} reconstruct the supplied manuscript \cite[Theorem~1.1, Lemma~2.2 and Proposition~4.1]{SuppliedHorizontal26}. The chapter combines them with its kernel comparison.

\section{Gauduchon variational volume}
\label{sec:history-gaud-volume}

The Gauduchon variational volume extracts a homogeneous concave quantity from an elliptic normalization problem. Its method belongs to the construction of \cite[Theorem 2.13]{Collins21}; \cref{chap:gauduchon-variational-volume} develops the adjoint formula and its dependence on the chosen probability measure.

For a closed K\"ahler class, this construction meets the Hessian geometry of the K\"ahler cone. The metric of \cref{sec:gaud-volume-hessian} is $1/n$ times the Hessian of $-\log\int_X\theta^n$ used in \cite[Section 1]{Magnusson12}. Completeness and positive-volume boundaries at finite distance are studied in \cite[Proposition 4.4 and Lemma 4.5]{Magnusson12}; \cref{sec:gauduchon-metric-geometry} gives the homogeneous argument in the present normalization.

\Cref{sec:nef-boundary-subtraction} reconstructs \cite[Theorem~A, Proposition~3.1 and Section~4]{SuppliedNefSubtraction26}. The two fixed tests bound subtraction through a nef boundary, and integration by parts retains the differential data of the added form. Bigness of the closed nef positive-intersection core is \cite[Theorem~0.5]{DP04}, the classical criterion recalled in \cite{SGPT}; no such criterion for arbitrary nonclosed nef backgrounds is used.

The determinant comparison in \cite[Theorems~A--B]{SuppliedDeterminant26} has the normalization and weak-current scope of \cref{thm:gaud-determinant-endpoint,thm:weak-gaud-compact-duality}. Its geometric bound and concentrating families are reconstructed in \cref{sec:gaud-determinant-concentration}, following \cite[Theorem~A and Proposition~4.1]{SuppliedDeterminant26}. Here the geometric bound follows internally from the mass-invariance criterion and lower-mass quotient; the source uses \cite[Propositions~3.2 and 3.3(iv)]{GL22}. The example distinguishes the unique smooth maximizer for one density from maximizing sequences with divergent higher moments.

The existence of a scalar conformal factor for a weak Monge--Amp\`ere power is raised in \cite[Chapter 3, Section 5]{LuHDR22}. The finite-current representation of \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints} supplies a supporting probability without defining that weak power or its scalar factor.

\section{Closed classes and singular loci}
\label{sec:history-closed-loci}

For closed classes on a Hermitian manifold, an almost positive representative keeps cohomology separate from the auxiliary metric. The convention in \cref{sec:bc-hermitian-bounds} follows \cite[Section 2.1.2]{BouThesis02}; it does not assign a Bott--Chern class to a nonclosed Hermitian form.

The smooth and analytic regularizations in \cref{subsec:psh-closed-regularization}, used for \cref{prop:bc-non-nef}, have the lower bounds in \cite[Theorem 2.1]{Bou04Zariski}; see also \cite[Theorems 1.2.9--1.2.10]{BouThesis02}. Smooth regularization loses a multiple of a continuous approximation of the Lelong numbers; analytic regularization has vanishing uniform curvature error and does not increase Lelong numbers.

Minimal multiplicities, \cref{def:bc-minimal-multiplicity}, are defined through these almost positive approximating families as in \cite[Definition 2.1.5]{BouThesis02}. The distinction from a single minimal current is essential: the nef ruled-surface example in \cite[Section 1.2.3]{BouThesis02} has a current with minimal singularities and nonzero Lelong numbers.

The non-K\"ahler locus in \cref{def:bc-non-kahler-locus} is defined in \cite[Definition 2.1.19]{BouThesis02}. The finite-intersection and regularized-maximum proof of \cref{thm:bc-non-kahler-locus} is the argument of \cite[Theorem 2.1.20]{BouThesis02}.

The equivalence between a K\"ahler current and a K\"ahler modification, used in \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci}, is the classical criterion recalled in \cite[Theorem 1.2.11]{BouThesis02}. It is retained as an external geometric input. The equality of the non-K\"ahler and null loci for a nef and big class is the transcendental Nakamaye theorem of Collins--Tosatti \cite{CT15Loci}, also valid on compact manifolds in Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$. The discussion in \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci} recalls this external theorem.

Restricted volumes provide a numerical test for these loci. The numerical restricted volume in \eqref{eq:bc-numerical-restricted-volume} follows Collins--Tosatti \cite[Section 2]{CT22Restricted}. The smooth remainder in the analytic singularities is part of that definition.

Class volumes and masses of individual traces require different comparisons. The finite class invariant of \cref{sec:finite-restricted-invariants} and the actual-trace results of \cref{sec:resolved-trace-masses} adapt \cite[Theorems A--B]{SuppliedRestricted26}. The supplied manuscript distinguishes the supremum over a closed restricted class from the mass of a particular trace. The actual restriction, finite divisorial decomposition and bounded remainder are retained here. The source also assumes that the residual class is nef; here this follows from the bounded remainder by \cref{lem:bounded-closed-potential-nef}. The three examples are \cite[Examples 6.1--6.3]{SuppliedRestricted26}; they separate class mass, trace mass, preservation of bounded-difference type and membership in the ambient null locus.

The existence of K\"ahler models for every subvariety uses Fujiki's closure of class $\mathcal C$ under closed subspaces \cite[Lemma 4.6(2)]{Fujiki78}, followed by the K\"ahler modification theorem of Varouchas in the form stated and explained by Barlet \cite[Theorem 10, pp.~31--32]{Barlet00}. A common K\"ahler refinement follows by embedded resolution of the graph in a K\"ahler product, using \cite[Theorem 1.6 and Remark 1.7(2)]{BM97}. These are classical geometric inputs. Potential descent is already proved in \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}, so the mass-invariance proof does not repeat it.

For \cref{prop:restricted-mass-bigness}, positive non-pluripolar mass implies bigness by \cite[Proposition 1.22]{BEGZ10}. The positive-product definition, its agreement with the cup product for nef classes, and attainment by minimal currents are in \cite[Definition 1.17, p.~219 and Proposition 1.20]{BEGZ10}. The normalized diagonal current in \eqref{eq:normalized-diagonal-current} uses the mass-concentration theorem \cite[Proposition 2.6]{DP04} and spells out the coefficient in the construction of \cite[Theorem 2.12]{DP04}. These K\"ahler results remain explicit classical inputs. The bounded-residual mass identity in \cref{lem:bounded-residual-mass-polynomial} instead follows directly from the book's bounded mass formula by polynomial comparison. The nef comparison with numerical restricted volume is \cite[Lemma 2.6]{CT22Restricted}; the final equality in \eqref{eq:finite-restricted-null-union} uses the already recalled theorem \cite[Theorem 1.1]{CT15Loci}.

\section{Regularity and singular spaces}
\label{sec:history-regularity}

Regularity in a big class is first a local question on the locus where strict barriers are smooth. For a closed class, existence and uniqueness in \eqref{eq:big-class-smooth-volume} are \cite[Theorem~A, equivalently Theorem~3.1]{BEGZ10}; the minimal bounds \eqref{eq:ample-solution-minimal-bounds} are \cite[Theorem~B, quantitatively Theorem~4.1]{BEGZ10}. The nef case of ample-locus smoothness is \cite[Theorem~C]{BEGZ10}; the proof here treats smoothness without nefness.

For a possibly nonclosed big background, the local H\"older theorem \cref{thm:big-minimal-holder} reconstructs \cite[Theorem~A and Theorem~3.1]{Dang26}. This supplies the local input for the ample-locus argument while retaining the unbounded global potential and nonclosed lower curvature bound. The formulation with a positive scalar and an equation only on the regular locus is equivalent, since the analytic complement carries neither smooth volume nor non-pluripolar mass.

The local regularity proof also needs a quantitative stability estimate. The strict-barrier estimate of \cref{sec:big-reference-stability} reconstructs \cite[Theorem~3.3 and Remark~3.4]{Dang26} in the minimal case, with the comparison potential normalized explicitly. The auxiliary-subsolution construction of \cref{sec:bounded-stability} first treats a semipositive big background.

Local smoothing uses the Dirichlet theorem of Guan--Li \cite[Theorem~1.1, p.~1186]{GL10}. In \eqref{eq:local-density-determinant}, the background is zero and the metric is $(\mathrm i/2)\partial\bar\partial|z|^2$; a large quadratic correction supplies a strict subsolution for density $G$. The same input constructs the approximants in \cref{sec:interior-approximants} and starts the passage to continuous boundary values and zero density in \cref{sec:local-homogeneous-dirichlet}. In dimension one it is replaced by the Poisson Dirichlet problem. Once uniform ellipticity holds in \eqref{eq:interior-local-ma}, the variable-coefficient theorem \cite[Definition~3.2, Theorem~3.4, and the paragraph following it]{TWWY15}, followed by difference quotients and Schauder estimates, gives smoothness.

The boundary criterion and counterexamples of \cref{sec:local-density-boundary} reconstruct \cite[Theorems~1.1--1.2 and Sections~3--5]{SuppliedLocalDensity26}; the local identification uses bounded Dirichlet uniqueness \cite[Corollary~4.4, p.~17]{BT82}. Degeneration of the density also matters for global stability: \cite[Theorem 4.1]{KN21} assumes $f\geq c_0>0$ in addition to its capacity hypotheses. The nonuniqueness densities of \cref{chap:degenerate-uniqueness} lack this lower bound even when positive almost everywhere.

For compact sets in a Hermitian manifold, Ahn \cite[Theorems 1.1--1.2]{Ahn26} proves pointwise locality of H\"older continuity of the extremal function. The weighted conclusion assumes H\"older continuity of both the unweighted extremal and the weight, together with uniform density in capacity. The same density condition enters the equivalence with local and weak local H\"older properties. These are the regularity regimes referred to in \cref{chap:research-program}.

On singular spaces, regularity upstairs must be combined with descent. Dang \cite[Theorem B]{Dang26} proves continuity results for bounded solutions on resolutions under additional geometric and integrability hypotheses, notably the log terminal setting. This result does not cover arbitrary unibranch singularities in the problem posed in \cref{chap:research-program}. Pan \cite[Theorems A and A']{Pan25} constructs a Gauduchon metric on the regular locus of a compact reduced irreducible Hermitian space of dimension at least two. It is conformal and uniformly comparable to the given metric, and unique up to scale within that class. Control of conformal factors on a resolution is central to the construction. This external result motivates the singular trace questions in \cref{chap:research-program}.

The projective-cone construction in \cref{sec:projective-cone-equations} is reconstructed from \cite[Theorems A--B and Sections 3--7]{SuppliedCones26}. Its data fix the angular metric and radial density before solving the equation. Determinant-preserving deformations retain the logarithmic solution while changing the background to a nonclosed form. The closed bounded endpoint uses energy and has a different uniqueness proof. The resolution comparison includes modifications over regular points; the smooth residual need not descend when the full solution has a pole at the contracted vertex.

\section{Geometric problems}
\label{sec:history-geometry}

A closed strict current has geometric consequences that a positive nonclosed background need not have. This distinction governs the passage from equation normalizations to the geometric criteria below. The K\"ahler-current characterization of Fujiki's class used in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} is \cite[Theorem 0.7]{DP04}; the transcendental Grauert--Riemenschneider conjecture is \cite[Conjecture 0.8]{DP04}. These published locators correspond to Theorem 0.6 and Conjecture 0.7 in the arXiv version.

Under nefness and positive top self-intersection, the equivalence between bigness and bounded mass cited in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} is \cite[Theorem 4.9]{GL22}. The characterization of Fujiki's class by bounded mass and positive upper class volume is \cite[Theorem C]{BGL25}; that upper volume differs from the fixed-probability Gauduchon functional.

The closed K\"ahler transcendental Morse inequality, volume differentiability and cone-duality conclusions mentioned in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} are \cite[Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2, version 2]{Tosatti26}, retained as literature results without reconstructing their proof. The qualitative criterion under bounded mass is \cite[Theorem 4.12]{GL22}.

The non-Hermitian locus in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} uses \cite[Definition 2.12 and Theorem 2.14]{DangNull26}. Its almost analytic singularities have the exact meaning of \cite[Definition 2.8]{DangNull26}, including a smooth remainder on a suitable log resolution. Dang's qualitative null-locus equality for nef and big, possibly nonclosed backgrounds is \cite[Theorem 1.1]{DangNull26}. Theorem A of that paper gives a numerical criterion under bounded mass on the ambient manifold and positive lower volume on its positive-dimensional submanifolds. These are distinct scopes.

Theorem C of \cite{DangNull26} identifies finite-time Chern--Ricci singularities with the null locus under positive limiting lower volume, for arbitrary initial metrics in dimension two and Guan--Li initial metrics in higher dimensions. The latter satisfy $\ddc\omega_0=0$ and $\mathrm{d}\omega_0\wedge\dc\omega_0=0$. Once the limiting background is big, \cite[Theorem 4.3]{DangNull26} gives current convergence and smooth local convergence off its non-Hermitian locus. This is the parabolic input distinguished from the numerical bigness question in \cref{qu:chern-ricci-gaud-singularities}.

The maximal-time theorem \cref{thm:chern-ricci-maximal-time} is \cite[Theorem~1.2, (1.4), and Section~4, arXiv version]{TW15}. Its proof here combines the positive affine path and time-weighted potential estimates with \cref{lem:hermitian-trace-inequality} and classical parabolic regularity. Potentials are multiplied by $2\pi$; time is unchanged. The geometric smoothing conditions of \cite[Section~3.1]{DNGL26} retain the further compatibility discussed in \cref{sec:history-completeness}.

The balanced--pluriclosed conjecture referred to in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} is stated in \cite[Conjecture 1.1]{FV26}. The balanced and pluriclosed metrics are allowed to be different. The assertion that a pluriclosed manifold in Fujiki's class is K\"ahler is \cite[Theorem 0.2 in the author manuscript]{Chiose14}. The locator refers to the author manuscript used in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems}.

The proposed long-time existence and convergence of pluriclosed flow in the balanced setting with $c_1(X)=0$ is \cite[Conjecture 1.2]{FV26}. The discussion in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} distinguishes a conserved balanced trace from a pointwise metric bound.
