\chapter{Historical notes}
\label{chap:history}

The passage from K\"ahler to Hermitian pluripotential theory involves two independent changes. A closed class may be studied on a non-K\"ahler manifold, while a nonclosed background loses the cohomological control of Monge--Amp\`ere masses even on a K\"ahler manifold. Local regularization and local products survive both changes, because they only require a smooth local correction of the background. Global equations depend instead on Gauduchon duality, on relative envelopes and on whatever mass bounds are available, and these mechanisms are what connect the sources discussed below. Their hypotheses also decide whether a solution keeps the ordinary singularity type of its reference or only belongs to the relative envelope class of that reference.

These notes identify the sources of the results of the book, with precise locators, and explain how the arguments given in the book relate to the original ones. Published papers are cited in their journal versions, with the statement numbers and pages of the published text; for work available only as a preprint, the locator names the version used (v1, v2, \dots). Where needed, the conversion from the normalization of $\ddc$ or of the Chern--Ricci form used in a source to the conventions fixed at the beginning of the book is indicated next to the citation.

\section{Plurisubharmonic functions and regularization}
\label{sec:history-psh}

Compactness, integrability and regularization are local properties, and they survive the passage to a nonclosed background because a quasi-plurisubharmonic function becomes plurisubharmonic after a smooth local correction. The uniform $L^1$ bound for normalized potentials in \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} is also proved by Dinew \cite[Proposition~6.2, p.~109]{Dinew16}; the compactness follows by propagating local compactness over a connected coordinate cover. Local exponential integrability, as recalled by Zeriahi \cite[Section~1, p.~672]{Zeriahi01}, becomes uniform over normalized potentials in \cref{lem:uniform-exponential}, through a contradiction argument with a weighted sum of potentials.

The comparison of convolutions in different coordinate charts in \cref{lem:bounded-local-regularization,cor:coordinate-convolution} is the bounded part of the argument of B{\l}ocki and Ko{\l}odziej \cite[\S2, Lemmas~4--5, pp.~2091--2092]{BK07}. Their full regularization theorem \cite[Theorem~1, p.~2089]{BK07} allows unbounded potentials and a positive continuous background. The proof of \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization} treats only the bounded case, which is all that the construction of products requires; it includes the extraction of a decreasing diagonal sequence and the rescaling that keeps the regularized forms strictly positive.

The smooth regularization of closed real $(1,1)$-currents recalled in \cref{subsec:psh-closed-regularization}, which gives smooth representatives $T_j\geq\gamma-C\lambda_j\omega$ with continuous functions $\lambda_j$ decreasing to the Lelong numbers of $T$, in the notation there, is due to Demailly \cite[Main Theorem~1.1]{Dem92}; see also \cite[Theorem~4.1]{Dem94}. The analytic regularization recalled there, with $\nu(S_j,x)\leq\nu(T,x)$, is \cite[Corollary~14.13]{DemAM11}.

Demailly's regularization by the flow of the Chern connection \cite[Theorem~4.1]{Dem94} applies to $\ddc u$ for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, where $\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form: the theorem concerns a closed current with an arbitrary smooth lower bound, here $-\theta$, and this lower bound need not be closed. The monotonicity and logarithmic convexity of the regularization enter the estimate \eqref{eq:smooth-background-transform-rate}, whose linear error rate follows from the curvature inequality \cite[Lemma~4.2, p.~119]{Dem94} and the expansion in \cite[Proposition~3.1, equations (3.19), (4.21), and (4.22)]{Dem94}. In the present situation the curvature inequality needs neither a small $\epsilon$ term nor an $M_\epsilon$ correction, and local trace bounds make the constants uniform in the center and the radius.

The same expansion underlies the truncated Kiselman transform of \cref{lem:chern-kiselman-input}; see \cite[Theorem~4.1 and Remark~4.4]{Dem94} and Ko{\l}odziej--Nguyen \cite[Lemma~4.1, p.~294]{KN19}. In \cref{lem:general-kiselman-modulus}, for bounded potentials, the full base--fiber error is kept as a modulus tending to zero, and, as in \cite[Remark~4.4]{Dem94}, a term linear in the radius absorbs the mixed and vertical errors. \Cref{lem:smooth-background-regularization-rate} applies the same transform, with the linear error of \eqref{eq:smooth-background-transform-rate}, to potentials that are only locally bounded off the pole set of a strict analytic potential. This accommodates the unbounded potentials with finite $L^1$ norm that occur in \cref{thm:big-continuous-envelope-contact}: on compact subsets of the complement of the pole set the minimizing radius stays away from zero, so the minimum-principle argument is available away from the singular barrier.

For a continuous potential, the logarithmic derivative of the Chern averages tends to zero. Together with Dini's theorem and uniform continuity, this gives the uniform smooth approximation with vanishing curvature loss in \cref{lem:continuous-potential-smooth-loss}.

Demailly's regularization in the form of \cref{thm:resolvable-regularization-input}, whose approximants become divisorial with a smooth remainder after finitely many blowups, is the statement of Demailly and P{\u a}un \cite[Theorem 3.2]{DP04}. It allows an arbitrary smooth lower bound on a Hermitian manifold, and the smooth remainder after resolution is essential for its applications here. This sequence is in general different from the multiplier-ideal sequence of \cref{thm:demailly-hermitian-input}, and we do not assume that a single sequence has the additional properties of both.

The analyticity of the sets where the Lelong number is at least $c>0$, recalled in \cref{subsec:psh-lelong-levels} and used in \cref{lem:lower-mass-trace-concentration}, is Siu's theorem \cite[Main Theorem, p.~53]{Siu74}; a smooth local correction makes it applicable to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions. The non-strict inequality is essential. In \cref{ex:strict-lelong-level}, a summable series of line currents on the projective plane has the property that the set where the Lelong number exceeds one is a sequence of points accumulating at a point outside that set.

The closedness of polar curvature currents, \cref{prop:polar-curvature-closedness}, is proved by the complete pluripolar restriction argument of Boucksom, Eyssidieux, Guedj and Zeriahi \cite[Theorem~1.8, Lemma~1.9 and Remark~1.10, pp.~207--210]{BEGZ10}. The chain rule for bounded functions allows cutoffs that converge only pointwise, so the complete pluripolar set need not be closed, and a smooth local correction extends the argument to quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials with an arbitrary smooth background. Combined with the recovery of closed currents carried by a Lebesgue-null set from their traces, this removes the hypothesis that the pole set be closed from \cref{cor:closed-pole-trace-recovery,prop:closed-pole-compatible-limits}. The simultaneous realization of the limiting currents is a separate question.

The Hausdorff estimate of \cref{cor:closed-current-hausdorff-null} follows from the Lelong mass formulas in Demailly's book \cite[Chapter III, formulas (5.5)--(5.8), pp.~158--159]{DemCADG} and holds in every positive bidimension. When a closed polar current vanishes outside an exceptional locus, the support theorem for currents carried by analytic sets \cite[Chapter III, Corollaries~2.11 and 2.14, pp.~141--143]{DemCADG} identifies the remaining divisorial part. Full local Monge--Amp\`ere products at an unbounded locus of Hausdorff dimension zero rely instead on \cite[Chapter III, Theorem~4.5 and Proposition~4.9, pp.~152--156]{DemCADG}, as stated in \cref{thm:small-pole-local-products}. These products keep their top-degree mass on the pole set, whereas every lower-degree coefficient measure vanishes there. The local maximum argument of \cref{lem:small-pole-residual-invariance} compares the residual measures and shows that they are invariant under bounded perturbations. Their global consequences for masses and models are discussed in \cref{sec:history-mass-energy,sec:history-volumes}.

The convergence against a fixed non-pluripolar measure in \cref{lem:fixed-npp-integrals} rests on Cegrell's classical fixed-measure argument, which Ko{\l}odziej and Nguyen also state in a form allowing extraction of subsequences \cite[Lemma~2.1 and Corollary~2.2, p.~116]{KN22}. Applying that statement to every subsequence gives convergence of the whole bounded sequence.

Let $f$ be a holomorphic function and $\chi$ a convex nondecreasing function. The sharp Sobolev criteria for the potentials $\chi(\log|f|)$ in \cref{subsec:divisorial-sobolev} are due to Chen, Shao and Wang \cite[v1, Theorems~1.1--1.2, pp.~2--3 and 19--22]{CSW26}; their estimate in terms of the smallest multiplicity and their energy bound on small sublevel sets are \cite[v1, Lemma~4.1 and Proposition~3.8, pp.~16--20]{CSW26}. The proofs given here use the regularizations $\chi(\frac12\log(|f|^2+\varepsilon^2))$, $\varepsilon>0$, to identify the weak gradient and to keep track of the residual divisor term in the distributional Laplacian, a term that the classical Laplacian omits. The power examples and their endpoint obstruction in \cref{ex:divisorial-sobolev-endpoint} are the calculation in normal coordinates and the table of \cite[v1, Lemma~2.1 and Section~6.1, pp.~6--7 and 24--25]{CSW26}. These local criteria for derivatives should not be confused with the theorem on products at a small unbounded locus discussed above.

The concentration example of \cref{prop:smooth-dirac-concentration} is the point-mass case of Qin's \cite[v1, Corollary~3.6, p.~14]{QinLimits26}. Here the mass is computed directly from the transverse identity \eqref{eq:smooth-ridge-transverse-integral}, which gives a smooth variant of the logarithmic truncation in \cite[v1, Lemma~2.4, pp.~9--10]{QinLimits26}. The conclusion of \cref{lem:common-bound-zero-measure} for sequences with a common local bound agrees with \cite[v1, Proposition~7.2, p.~26]{QinLimits26}; in the book it follows from the range estimate \eqref{eq:local-cln-range}.

\Cref{thm:local-prescribed-excess}, which adds an arbitrary positive Radon measure to the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a fixed bounded potential in the limit, is Qin's \cite[v1, Theorem~B, p.~3]{QinLimits26}. The addition along finitely many test functions and the passage to a determining family correspond to \cite[v1, Proposition~3.2 and Theorem~3.4, pp.~11--14]{QinLimits26}. In the proof given here, the smooth ridge gives the limits of the lower-degree currents directly, finite iteration over atoms controls every mixed term, and the one-sided logarithmic bound also gives convergence in local capacity. The smoothness of the approximants is asserted for a smooth background, whereas Qin also treats continuous backgrounds.

The classification of vague cluster sets in \cref{thm:zero-local-cluster-sets}, according to which every vaguely closed set of positive measures is the full cluster set of a sequence of potentials tending to zero, is the case of the zero potential in \cite[v1, Theorem~A, pp.~2--3]{QinLimits26}. The metric for the vague topology and the concatenation of dense sequences are \cite[v1, Lemma~3.3, p.~12 and Lemma~4.1, p.~14]{QinLimits26}, and the comparison example with moving measures is \cite[v1, Example~B.1, p.~28]{QinLimits26}. In the book, finite addition of atoms gives smooth approximants that converge in local capacity directly. The construction with moving measures over a fixed bounded background in \cref{thm:bounded-background-cluster-sets} also corresponds to \cite[v1, Theorem~3.4, pp.~13--14]{QinLimits26}. The cutoff argument of \cref{lem:capacity-limit-measure-floor} shows that, under convergence in local capacity, every limit measure dominates the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of the background; this gives a complete classification of the cluster sets for bounded backgrounds approximated from below. Convergence in local capacity is a stronger requirement than the $L^1$ convergence in Qin's general classification of reachable sets, whose argument for reaching the zero measure needs additional hypotheses on maximal approximation and on the domain; that argument is not used here.

Let $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r$ be plurisubharmonic weights with exponents $p_1,\ldots,p_r>1$, as in \cref{thm:product-ideal-distance}. The division estimate with two summands, \cref{lem:two-weight-l2-division}, is the case of two weights in Takakura's \cite[v1, Theorem~1.3, pp.~2 and 5--6]{TakDivision26}. Since the relevant kernel is a line, the proof can use the Hessian formula for a scalar function instead of the general curvature theorem for subbundles. The constant in the a priori inequality \eqref{eq:sharp-twisted-apriori} follows from the twisted Bochner identity in Demailly's notes \cite[Section~13.A, pp.~123--124]{DemAM11} by a weighted Cauchy inequality. Proposition~13.4 there states the weaker constant $2$; keeping the full factor $\eta+\lambda$ gives the constant $1$ used here. For a single weight, \cref{prop:one-weight-ideal-distance} is the case $r=1$ of \cite[v1, Theorem~1.2, pp.~1--2 and 6--7]{TakDivision26}. Applying it successively gives the product constant of \cref{thm:product-ideal-distance}, with no condition on $\sum_i1/p_i$. The coordinate example in its proof extends \cite[v1, Example~3.2, p.~7]{TakDivision26} and shows that this constant is optimal when $r\leq n$. For $2\leq r\leq n$ the product is strictly smaller than $1-r^{-1}\sum_i1/p_i$, the constant stated in Takakura's theorem. For $r>n$ the product need not improve Takakura's dimension-dependent estimate, and by itself it does not determine the optimal constant.

In dimension one, \cref{thm:planar-optimal-ideal-distance} gives the optimal constant for every number $r$ of weights, including the range $\sum_i1/p_i\geq1$ in which it vanishes. When $\sum_i1/p_i<1$, it equals the dimension-one constant of \cite[v1, Theorem~1.2, pp.~1--2]{TakDivision26}, and a common logarithmic pole shows that it is sharp. The local argument in \cref{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal} uses the logarithmic fundamental solution and the characterization of positive distributions in \cite[Chapter~I, Sections~4.A--4.C, pp.~30--36]{DemCADG}; in our normalization, $\ddc\log|z|^2=\delta_0$. The global lifting in \cref{lem:planar-global-ideal-sum} is the locally free planar case of vanishing for coherent sheaves. It is proved here with the vector-valued $L^2$ estimate for complete metrics in \cite[Theorem~5.1 and its proof, pp.~33--34]{DemAM11}, whose Bochner identity is \cite[Section~4.C, p.~30]{DemAM11}. We do not determine the optimal constant when $n\geq2$ and $r>n$.

\section{Mass and energy}
\label{sec:history-mass-energy}

On a nonclosed background, the variation of the Monge--Amp\`ere mass and the failure of the usual energy to have a primitive are related manifestations of torsion. Chiose's polarization argument \cite[Theorems~1--2, pp.~576--577]{Chiose16} gives the characterization of mass invariance in \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}. Yi Li constructed energies corrected by torsion terms \cite[v1, Equation~(1.18) and Theorem~1.2, p.~5]{YiLi10}. The correction changes the one-form being integrated, in agreement with \cref{thm:raw-primitive}, which shows, for $n\geq2$, that the uncorrected one-form has no primitive unless $\omega$ is closed; \cref{prop:radial-torsion-defect} computes the difference by a first variation.

Cutoffs of diffuse poles separate this variation of the background from a fixed loss of mass at the singularity. Let $n\geq2$, and let $H$, $C$ and $M$ be the cutoff, the curvature constant and the residual mass of \cref{prop:diffuse-hermitian-mass-polynomial}; fix $0<t<1/C$ and put $A=\int_X\omega^n$. The mass of $tH$ is a polynomial in $t$ whose finite torsion coefficients come from Stokes calculations for compactly supported bounded products. By \cref{prop:diffuse-representative-mass}, every representative that is ordinarily bounded-equivalent to $tH$ loses the same top residual mass $Mt^n$, although its lower-degree coefficients can vary. If $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, invariance of the mass under bounded changes and invariance of the residual give the mass $A-Mt^n$ throughout the class (\cref{cor:diffuse-mass-invariant-models}); this applies to every compact complex surface with a Gauduchon form. On a surface, \cref{cor:diffuse-surface-mass-invariance} shows that the mass is constant on the class if and only if $\omega$ is Gauduchon, since admissible smooth bounded perturbations detect any failure of the Gauduchon condition.

The failure of the bounded mass property on a Hopf threefold is the counterexample of Xia and Zhang \cite[v1, Theorem~1.1, p.~2]{XZ26}. There a Gauduchon background converts vertical Dirichlet energy into Monge--Amp\`ere mass, a mechanism developed in \cref{chap:hopf}. Combined with \cref{thm:infinite-mass-witness}, it produces infinite extended mass with trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight, and the results on integrability and convergence of coefficients in \cref{sec:integrable-infinite-mass} describe the linear current carried by this construction. Xia and Zhang use the operator $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial$ \cite[v1, Section~1, p.~2]{XZ26}; dividing their background forms by $2\pi$ and leaving their potentials unchanged preserves positivity and changes the coefficient of the mass identity.

The failure of positive lower mass is a different phenomenon; it occurs on the elliptic threefold of \cref{sec:vanishing-lower-mass}. Sufficient conditions for a positive lower bound come instead from the sign of the torsion: \eqref{eq:threefold-mass-dirichlet} is the three-dimensional case of a formula of Angella, Guedj and Lu \cite[proof of Theorem~3.2, p.~4641]{AGL23}, who call the condition $\ddc\omega\leq0$ plurinegativity.

For $q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi}$, the principal elliptic bundles of \cref{sec:primitive-elliptic-bundles} are torus bundles of Goldstein and Prokushkin \cite[Section~2, pp.~69--71]{GP04}, built from the Chern connection of the metric on $L$ chosen there; their two closed $2$-forms are a constant multiple of $c$ and $0$. Their construction always has the square torus as fiber; other values of $q$ change only the modulus of the fiber. The metric calculation on these bundles gives an upper bound for the mass, but it does not decide whether the lower mass vanishes.

The completion energy of \cref{sec:completion-energy} is the Dinew--Popovici functional. Hanwen Liu defines it and computes its variations in \cite[v1, Section~2.1, pp.~3--5]{LiuCritical26}, and proves the identity relating energy and volume along potential variations, together with the indefiniteness of the Hessian, in \cite[v1, Theorems~5.1--5.2, pp.~16--19]{LiuCritical26}. Liu assumes that the ambient manifold is K\"ahler. The proofs in the book use only the finite-dimensional space of closed holomorphic completions and Stokes' theorem, so these statements about potential variations hold on every compact Hermitian symplectic fourfold. Liu's calculation of criticality for products \cite[v1, Lemma~2.3 and Theorem~2.4, pp.~7--9]{LiuCritical26} gives the mechanism of \cref{ex:torus-completion-critical}, where an explicit Fourier form on the square torus replaces the general existence argument on surfaces. Neither the general existence criterion of that paper in terms of the canonical bundle nor its convergence theorem for the global torsion flow is used here. Liu's convention is $\dc=\mathrm i(\bar\partial-\partial)$; rewritten with the book's $\dc$ and $\ddc$, the formulas of that paper for potential variations give \eqref{eq:completion-potential-hessian} with the coefficients shown there.

Combined with Chiose's criterion, the energy identity gives the equivalence between flatness of the energy and invariance of the mass in \cref{cor:completion-flat-invariant-mass}. The calculation with one nonclosed factor in \cref{ex:torus-flat-nonstationary} shows that this flatness along potentials implies neither criticality in the full Aeppli class nor the existence of a primitive for the Monge--Amp\`ere one-form.

\section{Gauduchon metrics and positivity}
\label{sec:history-gauduchon}

Gauduchon metrics replace the cohomological conservation of mass by a linear trace identity. In the form recalled by Tosatti and Weinkove immediately before \cite[Lemma~2.3]{TW10}, Gauduchon's conformal theorem starts, in dimension $n\geq2$, from a Hermitian form $\eta$ and constructs a smooth real function $f$ such that $\gamma=\mathrm e^f\eta$ is Gauduchon; the multiplier in \cref{thm:gauduchon} is then $\rho=\mathrm e^{(n-1)f}$. The resulting trace comparison, \cref{cor:trace-bounds}, also underlies the first mixed-volume bounds of Guedj and Lu \cite[Proposition~3.3]{GL22}.

The trace identity has a dual form: positivity can be tested against Gauduchon metrics. For an arbitrary smooth background, the generalized Lamari criterion \cref{thm:lamari} is stated by Boucksom, Guedj and Lu \cite[Lemma~1.15]{BGL25}, who derive it from the Hahn--Banach theorem; the proof given in the book combines a separation argument with a right inverse of a scalar elliptic operator. The same linear control gives the bound \eqref{eq:gauduchon-c-bound} for the normalization constant, which is used in the weak existence arguments of \cite[Section~4]{BGL25}.

The deformation obstruction for convex norms in \cref{thm:convex-norm-deformation-obstruction} is due to Liu and Wan \cite[v1, Theorem~3.2 and Sections~3.1--3.3, pp.~8--13]{LiuWanKobayashi26}, and the normalization of line metrics in \cref{lem:bundle-degree-normalization} is their Lemma~3.1, p.~8. The proof combines derivative estimates along the line axes with an average of the horizontal curvature and the formal adjoint on a fixed sphere bundle. The resulting $C^4$ neighborhood is uniform over all smooth complex norms, and the positive Gauduchon test forms may differ from one line summand to another.

The regularization of homogeneous functions in \cref{thm:homogeneous-convex-regularization} is a lemma of Kuang-Ru Wu, stated there for vector bundles over compact complex manifolds \cite[v1, Section~2.3, Lemma~13, pp.~10--13]{WuFinslerIII23}. Its gluing step uses the closure under maxima, the stability under smooth perturbations and the semilocal approximation of Greene and Wu \cite[Definitions~1.1, 1.3--1.4, pp.~48--49; Lemma~4.1 and Theorem~4.1, pp.~80--81]{GreeneWu79}, while the local positivity properties are proved directly from a holomorphic shear formula. The replacement in \cref{prop:weak-homogeneous-convex-replacement} assumes that a strictly plurisubharmonic homogeneous function is given, and \cref{cor:continuous-convex-norm-obstruction} keeps the hypothesis of local strong convexity in the fibers.

The existence statement needed here, Kobayashi's characterization of ample bundles in the form stated by Liu and Wan \cite[v1, Theorem~2.1, p.~7]{LiuWanKobayashi26}, is proved directly by the tautological construction of \cref{lem:tautological-homogeneous-levi,prop:ample-dual-strict-homogeneous-function}: a metric with positive curvature on the dual of the tautological line bundle gives a homogeneous squared norm that is strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section. When the dual vector bundle is ample, such a line metric is a root of the Fubini--Study metric pulled back by a very ample embedding. The result is the strictly plurisubharmonic homogeneous function used in their regularization \cite[v1, Lemma~3.4, pp.~13--14]{LiuWanKobayashi26}, and it gives \cref{cor:ample-dual-continuous-convex-obstruction}.

The construction of polar norms in \cref{subsec:polar-finsler-convexity} gives a proof of Liu and Wan's \cite[v1, Proposition~3.5, pp.~14--16]{LiuWanKobayashi26}. A Schur complement and a holomorphic correction of the second jet give local pluriharmonic supporting functions for the logarithm of the polar square. A uniform upper bound for the real Hessian of the primal squared metric gives strong convexity of the dual through its exact Legendre expression; neither real convexity of the primal metric nor uniqueness of a maximizer is assumed. The tautological construction for an ample dual and the homogeneous regularization then give the smooth replacement and the curvature obstruction of \cref{cor:gauduchon-semipositive-finsler-obstruction}, under the hypotheses stated there.

On Hirzebruch surfaces, the ample comparison quotient and the two prioritary points of \cref{prop:hirzebruch-ample-comparison,lem:hirzebruch-prioritary-comparison} are Liu and Wan's \cite[v1, Proposition~4.2 and Lemma~4.3, pp.~17--20]{LiuWanKobayashi26}. The facts about Hirzebruch surfaces that they need are proved directly in \cref{lem:hirzebruch-ruling-sections}, from sections along the ruling and a very ample embedding. The positive Gauduchon tests for the individual dual line bundles in \cref{prop:hirzebruch-positive-dual-tests} are the divisor calculation in their proof of Theorem~1.1, p.~23. These constructions give the ample point and the test forms used in \cref{thm:hirzebruch-stable-ample-approximation,cor:hirzebruch-stable-ample-finsler-obstruction}.

The stability of extensions in \cref{prop:hirzebruch-stable-extensions} is Liu and Wan's \cite[v1, Proposition~4.4, pp.~20--21]{LiuWanKobayashi26}, including the equality case on $\mathbb F_0$. The proof of \cref{prop:hirzebruch-extension-prioritary} uses the exact sequences of $\operatorname{Hom}$ groups and applies to every extension, while restriction to a fiber of the ruling shows that none of them is ample. The explicit family of Dolbeault operators in \cref{prop:hirzebruch-explicit-stable-degeneration} converges to the split operator in every finite $C^m$ norm. These stable, nonample approximations give the stable point used in the deformation argument of \cref{thm:hirzebruch-stable-ample-approximation}.

The approximation theorem \cref{thm:hirzebruch-stable-ample-approximation} is Liu and Wan's \cite[v1, Theorem~4.1, p.~17; proof, p.~22]{LiuWanKobayashi26}. Its input on the stack of prioritary sheaves is Walter's \cite[Proposition~2]{WalterPrioritary93}, which applies to all prioritary torsion-free sheaves of fixed rank at least two with fixed numerical Chern classes, without any semistability assumption. The required openness statements are proved directly by the family arguments in \cref{lem:hirzebruch-family-local-freeness,lem:hirzebruch-family-ample-open,lem:hirzebruch-family-stability-open}; on a Hirzebruch surface, slope stability reduces to finitely many classes of line bundles. Parallel transport and compactness, in \cref{lem:dense-family-smooth-dolbeault-convergence}, give convergence on the fixed smooth bundle and on its dual in every finite $C^m$ norm. Combining this approximation with the Gauduchon-degree obstruction and the polar construction above gives \cref{cor:hirzebruch-stable-ample-finsler-obstruction}, which is Liu and Wan's \cite[v1, Theorem~1.1, p.~2; proof, p.~23]{LiuWanKobayashi26}.

The fixed Stein hypersurface of \cref{thm:fixed-stein-finsler-hypersurface,cor:hirzebruch-fixed-stein-hypersurface} corresponds to Liu and Wan's \cite[v1, Proposition~5.1, pp.~24--27]{LiuWanKobayashi26} for explicit algebraic bundles. A positive horizontal direction and the vertical critical hyperplane give the test on the Levi index, and a holomorphic quadratic jet makes the real Hessian nondegenerate without changing the Levi form. Separability of the relevant metric space gives countably many robust local tests, which \cref{lem:separated-holomorphic-convex-compacts,lem:dilated-countable-holomorphic-tests} place on a single global graph by Runge and Oka--Weil approximation. The graph is chosen before the metric, whereas the critical point that detects the failure may depend on the metric.

The dual norm of a concave neighborhood, \cref{thm:concave-neighbourhood-dual-norm}, is Liu and Wan's \cite[v1, Lemma~5.2, pp.~27--32]{LiuWanKobayashi26}. After a correction by a pure jet, a negative tangent plane of the boundary gives a supporting holomorphic section. Balls of uniform size tangent to the boundary from inside then express the squared dual norm as a supremum of functions with a common margin of strong real convexity, and ampleness together with homogeneous regularization gives a smooth replacement. Let $E$ be an ample bundle of rank $r$, and write $U_E=\mathbb P(\mathcal O_X\oplus E)\setminus\mathbb P(\mathcal O_X)$ for the complement of the zero section in its projective completion. The argument at high levels of an exhaustion in \cref{cor:dual-norm-complement-convexity-obstruction}, which shows that $U_E$ is not $r$-convex when $E^*$ admits no smooth complex norm with plurisubharmonic square, is their \cite[v1, Corollary~5.3, p.~32]{LiuWanKobayashi26}. Combined with the norm obstruction and the stable ample approximation, it gives \cref{cor:hirzebruch-non-two-convex-complement}.

The case of positive mean curvature in \cref{thm:bundle-curvature-complement-convexity,cor:positive-mean-complement-convexity} is Liu and Wan's \cite[v1, Proposition~5.4, pp.~32--33]{LiuWanKobayashi26}. The calculation with the reciprocal of the norm gives the more general count, in which $s$ positive horizontal directions of the curvature give $(n+r-s)$-convexity of $U_E$ for a bundle $E$ of rank $r$. For stable bundles of positive degree, the application uses the existence of Hermitian--Einstein metrics, proved by Uhlenbeck and Yau \cite{UhlenbeckYau86} in the K\"ahler case and extended to Gauduchon metrics by Li and Yau \cite{LiYau87}. The degree of \cref{def:gauduchon-bundle-slope} pairs the Bott--Chern first Chern class with the Gauduchon form; with the unscaled curvature $F_h$ of a Hermitian metric $h$, the integrated trace carries the factor $2\pi n$. The resulting least convexity index three in \cref{cor:hirzebruch-exact-complement-index} is \cite[v1, Corollary~5.5, p.~33]{LiuWanKobayashi26}; the lower bound uses the dual-norm obstruction above.

Suppose that, for a holomorphic bundle $F$ of rank $r$, the complement $F^\times$ of the zero section carries a smooth positive plurisubharmonic function that is homogeneous of degree two. Taking maxima over the level spheres, \cref{prop:homogeneous-psh-sharp-complement-index} then shows that the least convexity index of $U_F$ is $n+r$: a negative radial derivative makes the Levi form restricted to the level set nonpositive on a complex hyperplane. Unitary-flat metrics give \cref{cor:unitary-flat-exact-complement-index}. For the line bundles $E_a$ on a Hopf manifold in \cref{ex:hopf-exact-complement-index}, restriction to an elliptic fiber and positive curvature give the least index two when $0<|a|<1$, whereas a homogeneous plurisubharmonic metric gives the least index $n+1$ when $|a|\geq1$. The sign of the Gauduchon degree distinguishes the three cases $|a|<1$, $|a|=1$ and $|a|>1$, although the de Rham first Chern class vanishes. A metric with Griffiths-nonpositive curvature gives the same homogeneous function in \cref{cor:griffiths-nonpositive-exact-complement-index}. For split bundles on Hopf manifolds, \cref{prop:split-hopf-pure-complement-indices} combines the weighted curvature with the lower bounds that closed submanifolds transfer to the ambient manifold in \cref{lem:closed-submanifold-complement-index-bound}. It gives the least indices $r+1$ and $n+r$ in the two pure regimes; in the mixed regime, only separate upper and lower bounds are obtained.

For external tensor products, \cref{thm:external-tensor-complement-convexity} applies the same exhaustion with one positive curvature direction from each factor. The positive mean curvatures lifted from the factors control every vector of the tensor product, including the indecomposable ones, and give \cref{cor:stable-factor-complement-convexity}. For the tangent bundle of a surface with a nonflat Ricci-flat K\"ahler metric, \cref{cor:ricci-flat-tangent-complement-convexity} combines the exhaustion instead with the directional positivity of \cref{thm:ricci-flat-surface-rc-positive}: this tangent bundle admits no metric whose mean curvature is positive definite, but its RC-positive metric still gives the required exhaustion.

For the logarithmic parabolic Monge--Amp\`ere flow on K\"ahler manifolds, the calculation of $L^p$ norms of the volume ratio is Jiyuan Han's \cite[v1, Lemma~3.2, pp.~13--14]{HanParabolic26}. The chain rule and Stokes computation in \cref{prop:hermitian-parabolic-entropy-identity} keep the additional term coming from the derivative of the Hermitian background. When the forms along the flow remain Gauduchon, convex entropies are nonincreasing (\cref{cor:gauduchon-parabolic-entropies}), and varying the target probability measure gives the exact criterion at the initial time in \cref{prop:gauduchon-initial-entropy-criterion}. These statements apply on any interval on which a smooth solution exists. Han's uniform estimates for degenerating families of Calabi--Yau manifolds, and the Green-function estimates on which they depend, are not part of this calculation.

The calculation of the total variation in \cref{prop:parabolic-total-variation-growth} is a direct variation of determinants on a flat torus, whose coefficient is given by the two Haar averages of \cref{lem:torus-total-variation-averages}. \Cref{cor:individual-entropy-pair-growth} compares this growth of the distance between two solutions with the initial derivatives of the individual entropies.

The support lemma for currents carried by a smooth hypersurface, \cref{lem:smooth-hypersurface-current-support}, is the local statement used by Dinh and Sibony in \cite[proof of Theorem~4.1, pp.~965--966]{DinhSibonyRegularization04}, where it is attributed to Bassanelli. The proof given here tests against a quadratic function of the normal coordinate and polarizes horizontal forms; it applies whenever $\ddc R$ has order zero, $R$ being the positive current supported on the hypersurface. The estimate for pullbacks in \cref{lem:smooth-pluriharmonic-pullback-mass} follows directly from the classical $\ddc$-lemma for currents on compact K\"ahler manifolds, applied to a fixed closed pushforward; the current that is paired with the distributional primitive is smooth.

The smoothing of positive pluriharmonic forms with continuous coefficients in \cref{lem:continuous-pluriharmonic-smoothing} uses the heat equation of the Hodge Laplacian, the K\"ahler identities and a positive correction tending to zero. For continuous potentials and closed backgrounds on a compact K\"ahler manifold, Dinh and Sibony construct the product of the curvature current with a positive pluriharmonic current through an energy \cite[Theorem~5.1, Proposition~5.2 and identity~(12), pp.~967--969]{DinhSibonyRegularization04}. The proofs of \cref{lem:pluriharmonic-gradient-energy,thm:continuous-pluriharmonic-trace-product} integrate by parts against the current of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$ itself, so that no auxiliary power of a K\"ahler form is needed. They give a positive measure as product, depending jointly continuously on the factors, on any compact Hermitian manifold, for every smooth real background and continuous potential.

The descent of horizontal currents in \cref{lem:horizontal-pluriharmonic-descent} follows directly from the positivity of the coefficient matrix together with the Liouville theorem for harmonic functions on the complex line, or the maximum principle on a compact connected curve. It therefore applies both to bundles with fiber $\mathbb C$ and to bundles of compact curves. On the elliptic threefold, the exact vertical trace form gives \cref{lem:elliptic-positive-current-descent} for positive $\ddc$-closed currents, and its closed case gives the calculation of the Bott--Chern cone.

The mean-curvature criterion for RC-positivity and the conformal construction in \cref{lem:semipositive-mean-rc-criterion,thm:conformal-rc-globalization} correspond to Chou and Wu's \cite[v1, Definition~5 and Lemmas~7--8, pp.~5--6]{ChouWuRC26}. The scalar equation in the construction is the Gauduchon compatibility condition of \cref{prop:adjoint-gauduchon}, and taking the curvature margin over a closed ball makes it uniform in both the base point and the unit fiber vector. \Cref{ex:local-rc-nonuniform} shows directly that the order of these two quantifiers matters, and \cref{prop:trivial-determinant-rc-obstruction} gives a global obstruction to interchanging them when the determinant bundle admits a flat Hermitian metric.

The RC-positive tangent metrics on Ricci-flat surfaces in \cref{thm:ricci-flat-surface-rc-positive} are Chou and Wu's \cite[v1, Theorem~1 and Lemmas~9--15, pp.~7--12]{ChouWuRC26}. The pointwise criterion is the invertibility of the anti-self-dual curvature operator. The identities behind \eqref{eq:ricci-flat-weyl-norm} are Derdzi{\'n}ski's \cite[equations~(33)--(35), p.~414]{Derdzinski83}, with the nonnegative Laplacian on functions used there replaced by $\operatorname{div}\nabla$ and with the same norm on endomorphisms; summing the three equations for the eigenvalues gives the coefficient $36$. The commuting curvature factors in \cite[Lemma~2, p.~412]{Derdzinski83} also show directly that a nonzero parallel operator with three distinct eigenvalues cannot occur here. This gives the required nonempty open set and a conformal tangent metric whose associated form is not closed.

The first-jet characterization of Griffiths positivity on projective manifolds, \cref{thm:projective-first-jet-positive-metric}, is due to Du and Xie \cite[v1, Theorem~1.1 and Proposition~2.1, pp.~2--5]{DuXieFirstJet26}; the identity in normal frames is written in the curvature convention used above. The density with two derivatives that the argument requires is proved directly in \cref{lem:projector-polynomial-c2-density,lem:first-jet-nested-metric-density}, through normalized projective coordinates and Bernstein polynomials. The immersion test without twist, \cref{cor:untwisted-evaluation-immersion}, is the calculation in \cite[v1, Section~3.1, pp.~7--8]{DuXieFirstJet26}. The positive measure in the dual alternative lives on the sphere bundle of the dual bundle, so the condition that it annihilates all metrics is different from Gauduchon duality on the base.

The double cover in \cref{sec:ramified-root-section-tests}, branched over $2q$ points, gives an explicit model with a computable space of sections in which a strictly positive line bundle fails the test without twist. The decomposition of sections into even and odd parts and the projective embedding are proved explicitly. A root of a very ample Fubini--Study metric gives a separately defined weighted family of tests, and \cref{prop:ramified-root-first-success} identifies its first successful level $j=q-1$, together with obstructions by one or two atoms at the lower levels. The original criterion with a fixed very ample twist $m$ follows the subsequence $j=qm$, so this calculation does not determine its first successful twist. The obstructions at a single level should also be distinguished from an adjoint measure annihilating every smooth metric.

\section{Bounded products and extended measures}
\label{sec:history-products}

The Bedford--Taylor product of bounded potentials is local even when the background is not closed: expanding against smooth local corrections, as Ko{\l}odziej and Nguyen \cite[Section~1]{KN15} and Boucksom, Guedj and Lu \cite[Section~2.1]{BGL25} do, gives \cref{prop:bounded-products}. Restricting the products of the truncations to the sets where the potential exceeds the truncation level then defines the extended measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}, by the mechanism of \cite[Proposition~2.1]{BGL25}. Finiteness becomes decisive when these restrictions are removed in the category of currents, and \cite[Theorem~2.2]{BGL25} uses a uniform bound on the variation for this purpose. An infinite extended positive measure is still given by the local construction, but it is not a global current of order zero.

The independence of the bounded mass property from the metric, and the reduction to smooth potentials, in \cref{prop:bmp-metric} recover the comparison made after \cite[Definition~1.16]{BGL25}; decreasing regularization with exact curvature bounds gives the passage from smooth to bounded potentials. The Gauduchon trace bound and the cancellation of the exact top-degree wedge product used in \cref{prop:gauduchon-range-growth} also appear in Guedj and Lu's \cite[Proposition 3.3(i), (vi)]{GL22}, and the estimate in terms of the range of the potential makes the dependence on the oscillation explicit and polynomial.

The local mixed Monge--Amp\`ere inequality, \cref{thm:local-mixed-input}, is Ko{\l}odziej's inequality in the form stated by Dinew \cite[Theorem~1.2, p.~2]{Dinew09}, for bounded plurisubharmonic functions whose Monge--Amp\`ere measures dominate nonnegative integrable densities with respect to Lebesgue measure. The inequality is local, and on relatively compact subdomains the hypotheses of \cref{thm:local-mixed-input} reduce to these. Its extension to smooth backgrounds is proved in \cref{sec:psh-bounded-products} and used in \cref{sec:interior-calculus}. Freezing the coefficients of the two smooth backgrounds gives \cref{lem:nonclosed-mixed-density} and the trace inequalities \eqref{eq:big-singular-gauduchon-trace} and \cref{lem:unbounded-gauduchon-trace-density}, the latter keeping the mass of the linear trace on the pole set. The same argument also recovers the mixed inequality for $L^1$ densities with respect to $\omega^n$ used by Nguyen \cite[Lemma~1.9, p.~255]{Nguyen16}.

\section{Envelopes and comparison}
\label{sec:history-envelopes}

Narzillaev studies weighted extremal functions of a compact set $K\subset\mathbb C^n$ with a bounded weight $\psi$ on $K$ and logarithmic growth $\delta\log^+\lVert z\rVert$, $\delta>0$. The concavity in the growth parameter and the alternatives for regular contact in \cref{thm:affine-growth-contact-ray} are stated in \cite[v1, Lemma~3.1, p.~6, and Theorem~1.1, p.~2]{NarzillaevWeighted26}; the proof given here also shows directly, by scaling, that the set of parameters at which the unregularized envelope attains the obstacle on $K$ is empty or a ray. \Cref{lem:affine-projective-growth,prop:affine-projective-envelope} derive the correspondence with compact envelopes on projective space, the boundedness of the regularized envelope and its agreement with the envelope outside a pluripolar set from the compactness and extension results proved earlier in the book. In the book's convention, the growth $\delta\log^+\lVert z\rVert$ corresponds to the projective background $(\delta/2)\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ and to the obstacle $\psi-\delta\log(1+\lVert z\rVert^2)/2$; Narzillaev's operator $\ddc=\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial/2$ is $\pi$ times the book's. None of the theorems of that preprint on products, plurithinness or weighted counterexamples is used in these proofs.

For the zero obstacle, \cref{cor:affine-polar-obstacle-invariance} gives the case of compact sets of the classical fact, recalled in \cite[v1, Section~2, equation~(4), p.~4]{NarzillaevWeighted26}, that removing a pluripolar set does not change the extremal function. For bounded weights, the proof uses the logarithmic pole barrier of \cref{lem:affine-logarithmic-pole-barrier}. \Cref{prop:compact-obstacle-polar-invariance} carries out the corresponding argument for compact sets in an arbitrary smooth big background, using the correction of pointwise competitors in \cref{lem:big-pointwise-competitor}.

The estimates for increments in the growth parameter in \cref{lem:affine-growth-increment-bounds} are \cite[v1, Lemma~3.1, p.~6]{NarzillaevWeighted26}, and at a point of regular contact \cref{prop:affine-envelope-slopes,cor:affine-slope-regular-contact} recover \cite[v1, Proposition~3.2, p.~7]{NarzillaevWeighted26}. The proof given here keeps the estimates for the initial and the later slopes at every point of $\mathbb C^n$ and every positive growth parameter, including the uniform error for large increments. The representation by probability measures and the attained derivative in \cref{thm:affine-supporting-probabilities,thm:affine-supported-growth-derivative} come from \cite[v1, Theorems~3.3--3.4, pp.~8--9]{NarzillaevWeighted26}; the same argument with the finite envelope extends them to every point and every positive parameter. The passage to weak limits of these probability measures uses the continuity of the weight. The joint response to changes of the weight and of the growth, and the criteria for differentiability, in \cref{lem:affine-active-support-stability,prop:affine-joint-directional-response,cor:affine-two-sided-growth-slopes,cor:affine-weight-differentiability}, follow directly from the attained representation and the compactness of its set of active pairs. \Cref{ex:affine-quadratic-weight-corner} gives an explicit corner in the dependence on the weight, on the closed unit disk.

The relative extremal function of \cref{def:relative-extremal-function} follows the convention for compact sets in \cite[v1, Section~2, equation~(8), p.~5]{NarzillaevWeighted26}. \Cref{lem:relative-affine-zero-value}, which shows that the relative extremal function equals $-1$ at a point exactly when the global extremal function vanishes there, is proved by an explicit logarithmic gluing and the pole barrier above. \Cref{lem:affine-local-weighted-regularity,thm:affine-local-strict-weight} give \cite[v1, Lemma~4.1 and Theorem~4.2, p.~10]{NarzillaevWeighted26}, including the local strictness hypothesis $\ddc\Psi\geq c\omega_0$ near the point, with $\omega_0=\ddc\lVert z\rVert^2$, a global weight $\Psi$ in the given growth class extending the weight on $K$, and the closed compact pieces inside the ball. Narzillaev's operator and Euclidean form are both $\pi$ times those of the book, so the coefficient $c$ and the bound for the defect are unchanged. The pointwise consequence for the Hessian in \cref{cor:affine-weight-degenerate-hessian} is the observation following that theorem on p.~10.

The planar disks and the quantitative barrier of \cref{lem:planar-contact-barrier} come from \cite[v1, Lemma~6.1, pp.~12--13]{NarzillaevWeighted26}. \Cref{prop:affine-transverse-contact-defect} proves part of the product construction in \cite[v1, Lemma~6.2, pp.~13--14]{NarzillaevWeighted26}, namely the contact defect along the exceptional fiber and the failure of plurithinness at the interior point specified there. The logarithmic series is constructed by convergence of canonical representatives on compact sets, and an explicit pole barrier produces competitors that satisfy the obstacle everywhere. The proof needs neither a general product formula nor a classification at all boundary points.

The formula on normal fibers for the pluricomplex Green function with a divisorial pole, \cref{prop:ball-divisor-green}, is the case of the unit ball of Dao, Nguyen and Tran's \cite[v1, Proposition~4.2, p.~25]{DNTGreen26}, with logarithms normalized as $\log|\zeta|^2$ in the normal coordinate $\zeta$; the proof uses the maximum principle in one variable. The bounded minorants of \cref{prop:ball-green-truncation-rate} converge at every interior point, including the points of the divisor, with an explicit error bound. This is a special model for the approximation by bounded envelopes in \cite[v1, Theorem~1.7, p.~5]{DNTGreen26}, whose general result assumes an inward deformation in a sector and uses B-regularity for continuity. The calculation of boundary cluster values in \cref{ex:ball-green-boundary-clusters} concerns the trace of the divisor on the boundary, whereas that theorem asserts continuity inside the domain.

Contact sets connect the order of potentials with the order of their Monge--Amp\`ere measures. For semipositive big backgrounds, \cref{thm:envelope-contact} is due to Alehyane, Lu and Salouf \cite[Theorem~2.2, p.~8]{ALS26}, who quote it from their earlier work and remark after the statement that the envelope may have infinite mass; the passage from bounded to unbounded potentials in the book removes the bigness assumption. For a general big background, Boucksom, Guedj and Lu \cite[Theorem 2.9 and Proposition 2.11]{BGL25} prove that the measure of the envelope is concentrated on the contact set under bounded mass, and local replacement together with a strict singular barrier gives \cref{thm:big-general-envelope-contact} without that assumption.

The measure estimate for envelope fixed points, \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite}, is the fixed-point case of \cite[Proposition~2.13, p.~14]{ALS26}, stated there for semipositive big backgrounds. Let $\theta$ be smooth and semipositive, and let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$ for the ordinary envelope of \cref{def:singularity-envelope}. The proof given here uses the identity $P_\theta(\varphi+1,0)=\varphi$, where $P_\theta(\varphi+1,0)$ is the largest $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function lying below $\min(\varphi+1,0)$ (\cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}); contact with the first obstacle occurs only on the pluripolar set $\{\varphi=-\infty\}$, so only contact with zero contributes to the measure. For bounded potentials, the proof of the domination principle by envelopes, \cref{thm:semipositive-bounded-domination}, is due to Guedj and Lu \cite[Proposition 2.8]{GL22}.

Difference envelopes give a relative class in which singular potentials can still be compared. The construction appears for semipositive big backgrounds in \cite[Proposition~2.4 and Lemma~2.6, pp.~8--9]{ALS26} and for arbitrary big backgrounds in Xuan Li's \cite[v1, Section 2.1]{XL26}; the estimate for sublevel sets gives a common upper bound for the competitors, so that the envelope is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic as soon as its family of competitors is nonempty. The relative envelope class of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class} follows \cite[Definition~3.2, p.~16]{ALS26}, and the proof of its closure under convex stretching, \cref{lem:relative-stretch-closure}, is the argument of \cite[Proposition~3.5, p.~19]{ALS26}.

The condition \eqref{eq:als-model-condition} on the reference and its consequence, positivity of the mass of each individual potential in the class, appear in \cite[Proposition~2.11, the discussion after Definition~2.14, and Remark~3.9, pp.~13, 15 and~20]{ALS26}; that paper does not assume bounded mass for its equations with $L^p$ data in a fixed ordinary type. This positivity for individual potentials must be distinguished from a positive infimum of the mass over the whole ordinary singularity type. The mechanism giving the lower bound for the mass in \cref{thm:relative-uniform-mass-lower} is that of \cite[Proposition~3.8, p.~20]{ALS26}.

For lower semicontinuous obstacles the contact argument requires an approximation. The proof of \cref{prop:lsc-obstacle-support} follows Guedj and Lu \cite[Theorem 2.3]{GL22} and uses the Bedford--Taylor convergence theorem for increasing bounded sequences \cite[Theorem~7.4, p.~27]{BT82}; smooth local corrections and an expansion against the remaining background extend it to nonclosed backgrounds.

The modified comparison principle, \cref{thm:modified-comparison}, is due to Ko{\l}odziej and Nguyen \cite[Theorem 0.2]{KN15}. The proof given here follows the finite induction of Guedj and Lu \cite[Theorem 1.5]{GL22}, with larger dimensional constants.

\section{Smooth and continuous equations}
\label{sec:history-smooth-equations}

On a Hermitian manifold, the smooth Monge--Amp\`ere equation determines a normalization constant together with the potential. For $n\geq2$, the existence theorem \cref{thm:tw-smooth} is Tosatti and Weinkove's \cite[Corollary 1]{TW10}. Their equation, for a smooth function $F$ prescribing the logarithm of the density, is written with $\mathrm i\partial\bar\partial\varphi$; the substitution $u=2\pi\varphi$ gives the convention of this book, and a prescribed smooth volume form $\mathrm dV$ is absorbed by replacing $F$ with $F+\log(\mathrm dV/\omega^n)$. The concavity argument with Gauduchon metrics for the normalization constant in \cref{thm:b-derivatives} also appears in Collins's \cite[Theorem~2.13 and its second proof, pp.~1068--1069]{Collins21}.

The proof of the uniform estimate for the potential in \cref{chap:smooth-equations} by weighted integration follows Cherrier and Tosatti--Weinkove \cite{Cherrier87,TW10}: a finite recurrence absorbs the torsion terms before the Sobolev iteration. The calculation of the torsion terms in the second-order estimate of \cref{chap:smooth-equations} goes back to Cherrier; its form with completed squares and the maximum-principle argument follow \cite[Sections~3 and~9, pp.~133--138 and 157--160]{TW15}.

Prescribing the volume form of a Gauduchon metric leads to a different equation, which contains first-order torsion terms. The existence theorem \cref{thm:stw-volume} is due to Sz{\'e}kelyhidi, Tosatti and Weinkove \cite[Theorem 1.4]{STW17}, again with the potential multiplied by $2\pi$. The bound for the potential in \cref{sec:stw-czero} follows Sz{\'e}kelyhidi's \cite[Propositions~10--11 and Remark~12, pp.~346--349]{Sze18}, specialized to the determinant of the Gauduchon equation, and the second-order argument in \cref{sec:stw-second-order-proof} follows \cite[Section~3]{STW17}. The identities \eqref{eq:stw-diagonal-torsion}, taken from \cite[Section~4]{STW17}, explain the structure of the first-order perturbation.

The gradient estimate combines a blow-up argument with a Liouville theorem. In \eqref{eq:stw-rescaled-matrix}, where $A_j$ are the rescaling factors of the blow-up, the first-order term is kept before rescaling and tends to zero at the rate $A_j^{-1}$; the blow-up itself follows \cite[Section~6, pp.~366--368]{Sze18}, where the equation has no first-order term. The Euclidean argument in \cref{sec:stw-liouville} specializes \cite[Theorem~20, p.~364]{Sze18} to the Gauduchon cone. Once the equation is uniformly elliptic, \cref{lem:stw-higher-regularity} uses the Evans--Krylov theorem for variable coefficients in the form stated by Tosatti, Wang, Weinkove and Yang \cite[Definition~3.2 and Theorem~3.4, pp.~444--445]{TWWY15}, who derive it from a theorem of Caffarelli.

For densities in $L^p$, the existence of continuous solutions of the equation with a normalization constant (\cref{thm:kn-continuous}) and of the exponential equation \eqref{eq:weak-exp} is due respectively to Ko{\l}odziej and Nguyen \cite[Theorem~0.1, p.~141]{KN15} and to Nguyen \cite[Theorem~0.1, p.~241]{Nguyen16}. Their operator is twice the one used here: for the exponent $\lambda>0$ in \eqref{eq:weak-exp}, the conversion is $u=2\varphi$, where $\varphi$ is their solution, and the exponent in their equation is $2\lambda$. These results require neither bounded mass nor positive lower volume. Compactness comes from local capacity estimates: the arguments of \cite[Section 5]{KN15} and \cite[Section~6, pp.~107--113]{Dinew16} lead to \cref{thm:lp-uniform-compactness}, which is the statement of \cite[Corollary~5.10, p.~156]{KN15}. A common bound for the densities is essential here; the $L^1$ compactness of normalized potentials alone does not suffice.

The exponential term gives a comparison principle even when the densities have no positive lower bound; \cref{lem:exp-order} recovers \cite[Lemma~2.3, p.~256]{Nguyen16}. Letting the exponent tend to zero then identifies the normalization constant through \eqref{eq:exp-barriers}, as in \cite[Corollary~0.2, p.~242]{Nguyen16}. This proves uniqueness of the constant, but not uniqueness of the limiting potential. The weighted mean \eqref{eq:gauduchon-exponential-mean}, taken from \cite[Proposition~2.5, p.~258]{Nguyen16}, controls the normalization; here its lower bound follows directly from the estimate for sublevel sets.

Equations with prescribed singularities require relative envelopes and separate assumptions on the reference potential and on the ambient metric. The results of Alehyane--Lu--Salouf and of Xuan Li \cite{ALS26,XL26} are therefore treated separately in \cref{chap:prescribed-equations,chap:relative-finite-measures,chap:bounded-mass-equations}; their sources are discussed in \cref{sec:history-prescribed}.

For fully nonlinear concave equations, the uniform estimate under a quantitative subsolution condition is due to Guo and Phong \cite[v1, Theorem~3.1 and its proof, pp.~8--11]{GP24}. The strict asymptotic gap of an admissible reference, the matrix bound, the linear stability estimate and the uniform compactness in \cref{sec:concave-strict-gap-stability} follow Guo and Song \cite[v1, Lemma~2.1, Proposition~2.8 and Lemma~2.9, pp.~4--11]{GS26}. In the book, the zero-order estimate uses the real contact-set construction of \cref{prop:stw-czero-reconstructed}, and \cref{thm:lp-potential-estimate} bounds both the auxiliary potential and its normalization constant; with these inputs the constants do not depend on a positive lower bound for the right-hand sides. Writing the potential of these sources as $u/(2\pi)$ converts their convention for smooth curvature forms into the normalization of this book.

The gradient estimate in \cref{sec:concave-gradient} is Guo and Song's \cite[v1, Theorem~1.1 and Proposition~3.1 with Lemmas~3.2--3.6, pp.~11--17]{GS26}; the earlier alternative for convex level sets is Sz{\'e}kelyhidi's \cite[Propositions~5--6, pp.~342--344]{Sze18}. The tangent-hyperplane argument in \cref{lem:concave-linearized-alternative} uses the common strict gap to keep the constants uniform at every positive level. The differentiation is carried out for $U=u/(2\pi)$, where $u$ is the solution, and the final finite net of smooth comparison functions is provided by \cref{cor:concave-uniform-compactness}.

\section{Uniqueness and selection}
\label{sec:history-uniqueness}

When the exponent is zero, uniqueness of the normalization constant leaves open the separate question of uniqueness of the potential. Degeneration of the density affects comparison, whereas the family of equations with positive exponent can select a particular solution of the critical equation. The argument by concentration of mass for densities restricted to a set, around \eqref{uni-eq:mixed-bounded}, follows Lu, Phung and T{\^o} \cite[proof of Theorem~3.7 and Remark~3.8, pp.~2033--2035]{LPT20}. The exterior-ball argument for \eqref{uni-eq:normalzero} instead propagates the maximum, following Bardi and Goffi \cite[Proposition~2.4 and Theorem~2.5, pp.~7--8]{BG18}.

The uniqueness theorem for bounded potentials, \cref{thm:connected-positive-uniqueness}, concerns a smooth nonnegative density whose positivity set is connected and dense, and is proved with energy estimates on level sets and auxiliary test potentials. The mixed inequality and the passage to coefficient densities use the local calculus of \cref{chap:framework}, and the continuity of bounded solutions is obtained in the book itself, by a comparison argument, in \cref{cor:bounded-lp-continuity}. The smooth local Dirichlet problem needed for \cref{prop:weak-uniform-test-potentials} is solved by Guan's theorem \cite[Theorem 1.1, pp.~687--688]{Guan98}, whose hypothesis is satisfied by a strictly plurisubharmonic quadratic subsolution. The estimate on the real contact set then gives a constant that depends only on the fixed geometry and the $L^2$ bound, and not on the height of the test density.

For measurable densities that are bounded above and bounded away from zero, uniqueness is derived in \cref{cor:bounded-positive-density-uniqueness} from the same energy argument on level sets. The earlier counterpart is \cite[Corollary~3.9, p.~287, and Remark~3.13, p.~289]{KN19}.

\section{Volumes under mass bounds}
\label{sec:history-volumes}

For a nonclosed background, the mass of a single potential does not determine the masses in its singularity type. The lower and upper volumes $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ and $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)$ of \cref{def:singularity-volumes}, the infimum and the supremum of the mass over the ordinary singularity type of $u$, record the information that a single mass loses. Under bounded mass, comparison of these volumes controls envelopes and defines a topology on models of positive lower volume. On curves, polar curvature describes the boundary where the lower volume vanishes.

\subsection{Singularity volumes and relative classes}

The volumes used in \cref{sec:bounded-mass-volume-comparison} are those of Boucksom, Guedj and Lu \cite[Definition 3.1]{BGL25}, expressed in terms of potentials. For closed backgrounds the two volumes coincide on every compact complex manifold with the bounded mass property: by Stokes' theorem the defect term of \cite[(2.7)]{BGL25} vanishes for a closed form, so their monotonicity of non-pluripolar masses \cite[Theorem~2.8]{BGL25} makes the mass invariant under bounded changes of the potential \cite[Example~3.2]{BGL25}. In the big case, the monotonicity theorem \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity} is \cite[Proposition 3.7]{BGL25}, and the argument for lower volumes in \cref{prop:volume-addition} is Li's \cite[v1, Proposition 2.5]{XL26}.

The relative full-mass class of \cref{def:relative-volume-class} is \cite[v1, Definition 3.2]{XL26}. The stretching criterion \cref{lem:volume-deficit-stretch} is \cite[v1, Lemma 3.1]{XL26}; the proof given here keeps track of the deficit of upper volumes and chooses a representative whose mass is close to its upper volume. The case of zero deficit gives \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}, and a uniform bound for Lelong numbers turns the convex comparisons into the estimate of \cref{prop:volume-deficit-lelong}.

The domination principle \cref{thm:big-relative-domination} is \cite[v1, Proposition 3.2]{XL26}; its proof uses that the reference has positive lower volume, but not that it is an envelope fixed point. The estimate for relative obstacles, \cref{lem:relative-obstacle-contact}, is \cite[v1, Proposition 3.3]{XL26}. Together they give the identity for upper volumes in \cite[v1, Lemma 3.2]{XL26} and the identity for lower volumes in \cite[v1, Corollary 3.3]{XL26}, both stated in \cref{thm:ordinary-envelope-volumes}. For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, the proof first shows that the increasing limit of the obstacle envelopes has the ordinary singularity type of $P_\theta[u]$, so that domination can be applied before the equality of volumes is established. The resulting characterization of full mass gives the volume criterion in \cref{prop:conditional-i-characterization}.

When the pole set is compact of Hausdorff dimension zero and the lower volume is positive, \cref{thm:small-pole-envelope-residual} shows that ordinary projection preserves the whole residual measure. Bounded mass gives uniform bounds for the traces of all local closed powers of the ordinary rooftop envelopes. Away from the pole set, convergence in capacity of bounded functions identifies the limits, and the Hausdorff estimate removes all mass of the lower-degree coefficients on the pole set; hence the lower-degree global pairings preserve the full total mass. Since the rooftop envelopes all have the same residual measure, positivity together with the local order of residuals forces the model to have this residual as well. The argument also identifies the weak limit of the full Monge--Amp\`ere measures of these rooftop envelopes. The sources of the local inputs are given in \cref{sec:history-psh}.

The construction of small stretches, \cref{lem:positive-volume-stretch}, is \cite[v1, Proposition 3.1]{XL26}, and its bound for mixed masses gives an explicit uniform scale of stretching. The additive estimate for lower volumes in \cref{prop:positive-volume-background-comparison} is the case of smooth perturbations of \cite[Proposition 3.3(ii)]{BGL25}, with the mixed bound of \cite[Lemma 2.4]{BGL25} written in terms of potentials. The strict barrier gives the multiplicative comparison at positive lower volume, as in \cite[v1, Proposition 3.5(ii), Step 1]{XL26}. Consequently, \cref{cor:ordinary-envelope-background-limit} gives continuity of the lower volume of every fixed potential as the background decreases uniformly to $\theta$, while continuity of the upper volume and of the ordinary envelope still requires positive lower volume.

The continuity along increasing sequences, \cref{thm:increasing-envelope-volume-continuity}, is \cite[v1, Proposition 3.5(i)]{XL26}: contact identifies the limit of the envelopes and its upper volume, and the deficit estimate then shows that the approximating lower volumes are positive. The continuity along decreasing sequences of models, \cref{thm:decreasing-model-volume-continuity}, is \cite[v1, Proposition 3.5(ii)]{XL26}, including its conclusion when the lower volume of the limit vanishes. The uniform small stretch shows that a positive lower bound along the models forces their limit to have positive lower volume, and strict corrections and tail envelopes reduce the variation of the background to the argument for a fixed background. Li states the result for perturbations $\theta+\varepsilon_j\omega$ with $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$, whereas the proof here treats arbitrary smooth backgrounds that decrease uniformly in the order of forms. The convergence criterion at positive lower volume, \cref{prop:decreasing-envelope-volume-criterion}, is \cite[v1, Proposition 3.5(iii)]{XL26}. The deficit bound gives uniform convergence of Lelong numbers on each fixed smooth compact modification, and the envelopes of the restricted analytic approximants give the exact traces of \cref{cor:mass-bound-exact-trace}.

For semipositive backgrounds, \cref{prop:positive-volume-smaller-background} is the forward direction of \cite[Proposition~2.12, pp.~13--14]{ALS26}, deduced here from the strict barrier. This direction requires neither the identity for ordinary envelopes nor positivity of the lower mass $v_-(\theta)$ of \cref{def:bmp}; positivity of the lower mass is used for the converse. Applied to an envelope fixed point, the proposition compares Li's condition \eqref{eq:li-model-condition} with the condition \eqref{eq:als-model-condition} of Alehyane, Lu and Salouf.

\subsection{Model topology and analytic approximation}

The comparison function $d_\theta$ of \cref{def:model-volume-comparison}, defined through the upper volumes of two potentials and of their maximum, is introduced in \cite[v1, Section 4.2]{XL26}. That it separates models follows from the characterization by envelopes. The normalized stretching argument of \cite[v1, Lemma 4.2]{XL26} gives the quantitative $L^1$ bound in \cref{prop:model-volume-separation}, and the three-point estimate \eqref{eq:model-volume-comparison-modulus} passes convergence from a subsequence to the whole Cauchy sequence. The construction by maxima of tails in \cref{thm:model-volume-cauchy-completeness} follows \cite[v1, Theorem 4.3]{XL26}, with a fixed $\delta>0$ and the non-strict lower bound $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)\geq\delta$. The strict bound $>\delta$ used in version~1 of Li's preprint is not preserved under limits of Cauchy sequences, as \cref{ex:strict-volume-cutoff-incomplete} shows. Li explicitly allows $d_\theta$ to violate the triangle inequality, and \cref{ex:model-comparison-triangle-failure} gives an example of such a failure with intersecting divisors.

The criterion for finite rooftop envelopes, \cref{lem:finite-roof-volume-deficit}, is \cite[v1, Lemma 4.1]{XL26}: after choosing representatives whose masses are close to their upper volumes, a collapse would contradict the lower volume of the common upper bound. Li's argument with softened stretches \cite[v1, Proposition 4.4]{XL26} gives \cref{prop:volume-rooftop-continuity}; only the limiting potential needs positive lower volume, and the same comparison gives convergence of the lower volumes of the rooftop envelopes. The two stretching scales in \cite[v1, Theorem 4.4]{XL26} produce the monotone bounds of \cref{thm:model-volume-capacity-convergence}. Since finite rooftop envelopes are again normalized models, continuity along decreasing models passes their volume bounds to the infinite tails; monotone convergence in capacity then applies, and the subsequence criterion gives convergence of the original sequence.

The results on analytic approximation in \cref{sec:ideal-volume-approximation} combine this continuity of volumes with the limit of the dyadic multiplier-ideal envelopes. Mixing with a strict analytic potential, with rational coefficients, removes the curvature error in any smooth big background, and two comparisons of backgrounds then give the exact excess of upper volume in \eqref{eq:fixed-big-analytic-comparison}. Conversely, stretching and concavity of the multiplier-ideal envelope show that its fixed points are closed under the upper-volume comparison at positive lower volume. This proves the analytic criterion \cref{thm:analytic-model-volume-criterion} and the Cauchy completeness inherited there. The boundary where the lower volume vanishes in higher dimension, and positivity criteria in terms of ideals, are the separate questions of \cref{qu:hermitian-i-characterization}.

The comparisons of backgrounds in \cref{sec:model-background-comparison} follow from the same arguments with volumes and envelopes. Transport by a smooth gauge preserves the competitor currents, and ordinary projection corrects the zero obstacle; the explicit scale of small stretches makes the strict barrier uniform on sets of models whose lower volumes are bounded below by a positive constant. This yields \eqref{eq:vanishing-background-comparison} and extends the analytic criterion to positive background errors that tend to zero. These statements concern singularity volumes and canonical models; they neither identify the mass of the unregularized envelope with a gauge invariant nor assert convergence of arbitrary representatives.

The two-sided transport in \cref{sec:two-sided-model-backgrounds} mixes the original model with a strict analytic potential. A common upper background bounds both this transport and the maximum of two transported models, and the resulting volume bounds give uniform comparison, convergence of the transported reference, and equivalence of the limiting comparisons obtained with different strict corrections. The estimate for nearby backgrounds and the three-point modulus then reduce the analytic criterion of that section to \cref{thm:analytic-approximation-curvature-error}.

The complete metric in \cref{sec:model-volume-metrization} is obtained by the standard construction with finite dyadic chains, carried out directly from the three-point modulus. Stretching first gives continuity of the lower volume, and the reciprocal of the lower volume controls the boundary where it vanishes; the statement about the analytic completion then follows from the approximation criterion above. This metric induces the topology of the upper-volume comparison; it does not turn the comparison function itself into a metric.

In dimension $n\geq2$, polar curvature on all modifications still does not detect all ordinary models. The Cantor construction of \cref{ex:diffuse-polar-zero-model} gives the curve potentials of \cref{lem:diffuse-product-truncation-mass}; their maximum has vanishing first polar currents, but its truncations keep a positive top-degree mass $M$ near the common pole set. A rank bound for smooth maxima gives the construction in every dimension. By \cref{prop:all-modifications-diffuse-polar-blindness,cor:diffuse-product-exact-volume}, every compact K\"ahler manifold, with K\"ahler form $\omega$ and $A=\int_X\omega^n$, carries a complete ordered interval of diffuse models $p_t$ with volumes $A-Mt^n$ and distances $M|s^n-t^n|$ between $p_s$ and $p_t$, and every choice of the first polar comparison collapses this interval. Hence the closure of the analytic models for the polar comparison contains models that are not ideal. The identities for invariant mass extend these formulas to the setting of \cref{cor:diffuse-mass-invariant-models}.

For an arbitrary Hermitian background with bounded mass and positive lower mass, the mass bounds for representatives give \cref{prop:diffuse-two-mass-model-obstruction}. This applies to every Hermitian metric on a compact complex surface, by the existence of Gauduchon metrics, the independence of the bounded mass property from the metric (\cref{prop:bmp-metric}) and \cref{cor:lower-mass-dimension}. Preservation of residuals and additivity of volumes then give \cref{cor:diffuse-hermitian-model-interval} for all parameters in the strict range. The upper volume along this interval is strictly decreasing and Lipschitz, although in general the length of the interval is not given by the power formula valid for closed backgrounds, and all first polar coordinates still vanish. In \cref{prop:disjoint-diffuse-scalar-obstruction}, two cutoffs with disjoint supports have equal total residual masses and vanishing first polar data, yet their maximum is zero and their distance is positive; in backgrounds with invariant mass, both singularity volumes coincide as well. These scalar data therefore cannot recover the spatial distribution of the residual mass.

A direct calculation with maxima in \cref{lem:anisotropic-diffuse-product} identifies the full residual measure as the tensor product of the measures on the curve factors, multiplied by the product of the positive weights; for a smooth maximum the coefficient reduces to the volume of a simplex. In \cref{prop:equal-residual-anisotropic-models}, a common cutoff and a uniform curvature bound give a compact family of models indexed by a box of positive weights. Invariance under projection identifies the comparison distance in this family, up to a fixed scale, with the Lebesgue measure of the symmetric difference of coordinate rectangles. The subfamilies on which the product of the weights is constant are complete and have real dimension $n-1$, although their members have the same full residual measure on $X$, the same two singularity volumes, the same multiplier ideals and the same first polar currents. The construction takes place in a background with invariant mass and concerns the residual measure on $X$ itself; it does not identify the full residual products on every modification. The problem of describing the complete topology remains open.

\subsection{Curves and zero-volume limits}

The inverse maximum principle in the plane, \cref{lem:planar-inverse-maximum}, is the two-dimensional case of the result stated by Brezis and Ponce \cite[Theorem 3.1]{BP04}, who attribute it to Dupaigne and Ponce; the proof given here uses harmonic barriers on shrinking neighborhoods of a compact polar set. Applied to differences of potentials, it shows that the polar curvature measures reverse the order of the potentials: a potential lying below another, up to a constant, has the larger polar measure. Subtracting a common Green potential gives the formula \eqref{eq:polar-maximum-measure} for the maximum. Since these local facts are general, they are proved in \cref{chap:framework}.

The classification of models on curves in \cref{sec:curve-zero-volume-models} combines this order of polar measures with the Green representation of \cref{sec:curve-trace-realization}. Every prescribed polar measure gives an ordinary model equivalent to its Green potential, and the characterization of envelopes of positive mass, together with uniqueness of harmonic functions at zero mass, identifies every ordinary envelope with such a model. The formula for the maximum then turns the upper-volume comparison into the total variation of measures, and completeness, geodesics and the nearest ideal model follow from the corresponding operations on measures. The exact defect of analytic approximation is the nonatomic polar mass, which for the Cantor example is the full curvature mass.

The continuity statement for curves, \cref{thm:curve-model-capacity-convergence}, uses the theorem on convergence in capacity at positive volume in the interior. At zero mass the polar curvature is the whole current, which gives convergence of the currents in total variation. The local estimate \cref{lem:planar-variation-capacity}, which follows from the Green representation on a disk, the mean-value estimate for harmonic functions and the weighted Chern--Levine--Nirenberg inequality, gives convergence in capacity at this boundary. Moving a pole distinguishes this topology from that of total variation, while the approximation by finitely many atoms in \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} distinguishes weak convergence of the curvature currents from convergence in capacity.

The correspondences between backgrounds in \cref{sec:curve-model-backgrounds} follow from the same classification by polar measures, which smooth gauges extend to every smooth background of positive area on a curve. Changes of background with equal area preserve total variation, whereas rescaling the area gives homotheties compatible with extraction of atoms and hence with ideal projection. These correspondences are realized by a smooth gauge, obtained from the scalar Poisson equation, followed by ordinary projection. Comparing the new obstacle with its maximum and minimum gives a uniform bound, which yields continuity in $L^1$ and in capacity up to the boundary of zero mass. Rationality is needed only for the assertion that analytic singularities are preserved under rescaling of the area, because analytic singularities in this book have rational coefficients. At zero area, positivity and Stokes' theorem leave a single normalized potential. The general convergence statement \cref{thm:curve-zero-area-collapse}, proved in \cref{chap:framework}, follows from compactness of normalized potentials, uniqueness of harmonic functions and the local estimate relating total variation and capacity. It also gives the collapse of the model representatives as the unscaled metric diameters tend to zero.

\section{Equations with prescribed singularities}
\label{sec:history-prescribed}

Relative envelopes allow the solution of an equation to keep prescribed singularities while the measure on the right-hand side changes. Equations with $L^p$ data in a prescribed ordinary type, equations with general measures, and equations under mass bounds on the metric use this framework in different ways. They differ both in the estimates available and in the singularity type of the solution obtained.

\subsection{Equations of prescribed ordinary type}

The equations of prescribed ordinary type in \cref{thm:als-prescribed-lp} are \cite[Theorems~5.4 and 5.7, pp.~34 and~37]{ALS26}. Replacing their reference volume $\omega^n$ by $\mathrm{d}V$ multiplies the density by a smooth positive function and preserves its $L^p$ class, and a change of the normalization of the complex Hessian by a positive constant is absorbed by rescaling both the potential and the exponent.

The two constructions of \cite[Lemmas~5.5--5.6, pp.~34--35]{ALS26} are the relative estimate \cref{thm:relative-linfty} and the rooftop supersolution \cref{lem:relative-lp-supersolution}; together they keep the solution in the ordinary type of the reference. For a big background, the argument with bounded subsolutions of Guedj and Lu \cite[Lemma~2.1, pp.~270--271, and Theorem~3.4, p.~279]{GL23E} gives \cref{lem:big-lp-subsolution,thm:big-bounded-exponential}.

The trace calculation with a barrier in \cref{sec:semipositive-degeneration} follows Guyett and Mc Gowan \cite[v1, Proposition~3.2, pp.~7--12]{GM26}. Their geometric setting is a small crepant resolution, in Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$, of a normal projective Calabi--Yau variety, and their general Theorem~3.1 treats a closed semipositive big background with an upper bound for the mass. In the book, the arguments with bounded subsolutions and exponential comparison of \cref{lem:big-lp-subsolution,thm:big-bounded-exponential} give uniform bounds for the potentials and the scalars when the density is a fixed smooth positive function. The trace argument keeps the torsion of the perturbation and also allows a fixed nonzero Chern--Ricci form. Its conclusion is convergence of the potentials along subsequences, in a semipositive background with an explicit strict analytic barrier. The assertions of that paper about metric completions and conifold contractions are not part of this argument.

The bundle and the contraction in \cref{subsec:nodal-small-resolution} form the standard model of a node used in \cite[v1, Equation~(2.1), p.~4, and Section~4, pp.~15--16]{GM26}. The logarithmic curvature identity \eqref{eq:nodal-logarithmic-curvature} gives a strict barrier for this model directly, and a cutoff in a positive collar makes it global. This proves the convergence of the potentials and the assertion about the area of the exceptional curves for these local resolutions, without using a classification of small resolutions or a theorem on metric completions. Since the canonical bundle is torsion, its local holomorphic trivializations give the Chern--Ricci-flat density.

The equation on the singular space, \eqref{eq:nodal-descended-equation}, is obtained through the general correspondence of \cref{thm:hermitian-space-descent} between potentials on a resolution and potentials on the singular space defined by disk inequalities. For closed backgrounds, the identification in \cite[v1, Lemma~3.1 and the proof of Corollary~1.1, pp.~13--14]{GM26} uses the equation on normal spaces and the estimate for adapted measures of Eyssidieux, Guedj and Zeriahi \cite[Theorem~6.3 and Lemma~6.4, pp.~632--634]{EGZ09}. In the standard model of a node, \eqref{eq:nodal-ambient-volume} gives in \cref{lem:nodal-pushforward-density} the exact range $p<3$ of integrability and the weak $L^3$ endpoint, for every smooth positive density on the resolution. The bounded energy identity already used in \cref{thm:cone-bounded-equation} proves \cref{thm:nodal-closed-full-convergence}, using smooth strict positivity only away from the exceptional curves.

The arguments for the metric completion and the distance in \cref{lem:finite-puncture-completion,prop:finite-collapse-distance} are those of \cite[v1, Lemma~5.2, pp.~23--24, and Proposition~5.1, pp.~24--26]{GM26}. Compact collars in the regular part separate the points added in the completion, and the first and last visits of a path to the finitely many contracting regions give a distance error controlled by these regions; uniform convergence of the metrics on each compact subset of the regular part suffices. The hypotheses on the diameters of punctured neighborhoods in the limit and on the intrinsic diameters for the approximating metrics are stated explicitly; the conifold estimates and the asymptotics of cones needed to verify them are separate geometric inputs.

For curves, \cite[v1, Lemma~4.1, p.~17]{GM26} bounds the diameter of $\mathbb P^1$ by a multiple of $A^{1/3}$, where $A$ is the area, under a uniform upper bound for the metric. Averaging over straight coordinate segments and splitting the kernel given by the reciprocal of the distance give the sharper logarithmic estimate of \cref{lem:conformal-area-diameter} on every fixed compact Riemann surface. The radial example in \cref{ex:conformal-log-diameter-sharpness} has the same order, and rescaling it shows that the area alone does not control the diameter. The two radial paths and the connection through the zero section in \cref{prop:radial-tube-diameter} give the mechanism of \cite[v1, Proposition~4.2, pp.~20--21]{GM26}, with the uniform bound on the restriction to the curve and the radial bounds stated as explicit hypotheses.

Strict singular barriers extend the comparison argument to potentials with minimal singularities. The development starting in \cref{sec:big-minimal-comparison} follows \cite[Section 4]{BGL25}, where the bounded mass assumption of the earlier sections of that paper is dropped. The equations with minimal singularities, culminating in \cref{thm:big-minimal-normalized}, are \cite[Theorems 4.6--4.7]{BGL25}. The solution has finite mass, although the masses in its ordinary singularity type are not assumed to be uniformly bounded.

\subsection{General measures in semipositive backgrounds}

The local decomposition of measures used in \cref{lem:local-bounded-measure-carrier} is Cegrell's \cite[Theorem~6.3, p.~209]{Ceg98}, for compactly supported measures on a bounded hyperconvex domain in $\mathbb C^n$, $n\geq2$, the standing hypothesis of \cite[Section~2, p.~188]{Ceg98}; for a non-pluripolar measure its pluripolar part vanishes. The global constructions in \cref{lem:bounded-measure-carrier,prop:relative-measure-carrier} patch these local representations together.

The bounded-subsolution theorem \cref{thm:bounded-subsolution}, taken from \cite[Theorem~5.1, pp.~27--28]{ALS26}, solves the exponential equation whose right-hand side has a bounded density with respect to the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a bounded potential. Truncating the density in such a representation of a general measure brings this theorem into the construction for general measures, and domination then allows passage to the limit. The domination theorem for finite measures, \cref{thm:relative-finite-domination} in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures}, is the case of finite $\mu_u$ in \cite[Theorem~3.14, p.~22]{ALS26}: the fixed finite measure prevents collapse, whereas the other members of the envelope class need not have finite mass. The proof of \cref{thm:relative-extended-domination} also allows the measure being compared to be infinite. The singular strict domination principle, \cref{thm:singular-strict-domination}, follows \cite[Theorem~3.11, p.~21]{ALS26}.

Passing to the limit in the measures requires that the lower envelopes do not collapse. The continuity theorem for measures dominated by a fixed measure, \cref{thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity}, is \cite[Lemma~4.2 and Theorem~4.1, condition~(2), p.~24]{ALS26}; in this case positive lower mass prevents collapse, and no upper bound on the mass is used. The argument with a common envelope preceding \cref{cor:exponential-relative-class-closure} gives instead the first assertion of \cite[Lemma~4.2, p.~24]{ALS26}, which concerns approximants of the ordinary singularity type of the reference and assumes no positive lower mass $v_-(\theta)$. Under $v_-(\theta)>0$, \cref{prop:exponential-common-rooftop} also allows approximants in $\mathcal E_\varphi$ of other singularity types.

A common barrier in the ordinary singularity type also gives \cref{cor:common-barrier-continuity}, the corresponding case of \cite[Theorem~4.1, p.~24]{ALS26}. The reference need not be an envelope fixed point; the condition in a smaller background suffices. Integrals of bounded potentials against varying mixed measures are handled by the torsion calculation in \cref{lem:moving-bounded-integrals}, which follows Ko{\l}odziej and Nguyen \cite[Lemma~2.3, pp.~116--118]{KN22}.

The resulting equations with general finite non-pluripolar measures, \cref{thm:als-general-measure}, are \cite[Theorem~5.8 and Corollary~5.9, pp.~38--40]{ALS26}. The constructions with a minimal and with a prescribed reference in \cref{sec:general-measure-minimal,sec:general-measure-relative} use the approximations of the density from that proof, and they assume both the condition \eqref{eq:als-model-condition} in a smaller background and positive lower mass. The solutions have finite mass, but for arbitrary measures they need not differ boundedly from the reference. For a Hermitian background and the minimal reference, \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-exact-threshold} identifies the scalar in the equation with the subsolution threshold of \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}, that is, the largest coefficient for which the scaled density admits a subsolution. The question that remains, \cref{qu:individual-equation-threshold} in \cref{chap:research-program}, concerns zero lower mass. The weighted addition theorem \cref{thm:weighted-measure-addition} is \cite[Theorem~5.2, p.~30]{ALS26}; the proof given here localizes the passage to the equation.

\subsection{Big backgrounds under mass bounds}

Li's theory of equations with general measures \cite[v1, Theorems 6.1--6.2 and Corollary 6.1]{XL26} assumes that the manifold has bounded mass and that the Hermitian metric has positive lower mass. It gives \cref{thm:li-general-measure} and the constructions with a model reference in \cref{sec:big-model-general-measures}. Under bounded mass, positivity of the lower volume of the model reference implies that the metric has positive lower mass, so expressing the hypothesis through the reference reorganizes this setting without enlarging it.

The relative foundations of this theory use the domination, stretching and obstacle estimates discussed in \cref{sec:history-volumes}. In \eqref{eq:big-projection-contact-tail}, the required rooftop envelope is constructed before \cref{lem:relative-obstacle-contact} is applied to the relative projection.

Moment and continuity estimates connect these operations on envelopes with existence. The compactness argument with first moments in \cref{prop:big-relative-first-moment}, using \eqref{eq:big-energy-max-bound}, follows \cite[v1, Proposition 4.2]{XL26}: one chooses a reference potential in the same singularity type whose mass is sufficiently close to the upper volume, which need not be attained. Continuity for a fixed dominating measure, \cref{thm:big-relative-fixed-continuity}, is \cite[v1, Theorem 4.2]{XL26}; there the finite rooftop envelopes use only the envelope class, while the two-sided bounds for the mass prevent collapse and control the tails.

The closure theorem for varying references, \cref{thm:big-varying-model-closure}, is \cite[v1, Corollary 4.1]{XL26}. Projection to ordered lower models gives a fixed relative class for each tail of the sequence, and an exponential contact estimate prevents collapse. The stability for positive exponents in \cite[v1, Corollary 6.2]{XL26} assumes uniformly bounded $L^p$ data and the ordinary type of the reference. \Cref{thm:big-varying-equation-stability} treats instead the relative full-mass class: the models converge for the volume comparison with a common positive lower bound for their lower volumes, and the densities converge in $L^1$ with respect to a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure. Extracting a summable subsequence produces the dominating measure needed for the closure theorem. For a positive exponent, uniqueness gives convergence in capacity of the whole sequence of potentials. For exponent zero, the scalars and the measures converge, every $L^1$ cluster point of the normalized potentials solves the limiting equation, and the corresponding subsequence converges in capacity. In both cases, identifying the right-hand sides in $L^1$ gives convergence of the measures in total variation.

For the minimal reference, the equations with general measures in \cref{sec:big-minimal-general-measures} are \cite[v1, Theorems 5.2--5.3]{XL26}. The estimate for higher moments in \cref{sec:big-minimal-moments} follows the concave-weight argument of \cite[v1, Proposition 5.1 and Lemma 5.1]{XL26}, with the integrability against the fixed singular measure stated explicitly. The smoothing of measures used in the existence proof is collected in \cref{lem:measure-smoothing-integrals,lem:bounded-carrier-integrability}.

The conclusion of \cref{cor:big-model-lp-type}, that for $L^p$ data the solutions have the ordinary type of the reference, is \cite[v1, Corollary 6.1]{XL26}. The relative estimate \cref{prop:big-model-moment-bound} starts from a solution in the envelope class and uses a uniform moment of order $m>2n+1$. The concave-weight method is related to \cite[v1, Theorem 5.1]{XL26}, which already assumes the ordinary type of the reference and uses the moment exponent $m>n$; that theorem is not used to deduce the ordinary type of an arbitrary solution in the envelope class.

Addition of measures requires a common lower bound during truncation. The weighted construction in \cref{sec:big-weighted-addition} is \cite[v1, Proposition 6.1]{XL26}. For unrestricted addition, \cref{thm:big-unrestricted-addition} follows \cite[v1, Theorem 6.3]{XL26}; an additional rooftop envelope gives the common lower bound needed to pass through the cutoff \eqref{eq:unrestricted-addition-cutoff}.

\section{Normalization and adjoint measures}
\label{sec:history-normalization}

The normalization constant can be studied through its adjoint probability measures even when the potential itself is not unique. The differential method for smooth solutions appears in Collins's work \cite{Collins21}; \cref{thm:relative-normalization-concavity} replaces differentiation of smooth solutions by mixed products and domination.

For the mass quotient and the closed examples in \cref{sec:normalization-mass-quotient}, existence and uniqueness of solutions in a prescribed singularity type are due to Darvas, Di Nezza and Lu \cite[Theorems 1.4(i) and 4.29, pp.~2052 and 2079--2080]{DDNL18mono}, and the invariance of the mass under bounded changes of the potential is their \cite[Proposition 2.1, p.~2055]{DDNL18mono}. Their hypotheses are a compact K\"ahler manifold, a smooth closed big class, a model reference with small unbounded locus, and positive mass. The decomposition $u=\varphi+w$ of an ordinary divisorial type with a positive residual form, \cref{thm:ordinary-divisor-normalization}, concerns the ordinary type itself; it does not assert an identity of model envelopes for every weighted sum of divisors with real coefficients.

The dictionary between toric potentials and convex functions used in \cref{sec:convex-body-reference-stability} is that of Berman and Berndtsson \cite[Sections~2.1--2.3, especially Proposition~2.3 and Lemma~2.5, pp.~660--664]{BBToric13}. The angular coefficient in \eqref{eq:convex-body-mass} is computed directly, in the book's conventions for the squared logarithm and the operator. The argument with finite envelopes establishes the identity for ordinary models, and a common translated simplex gives both the witness in a smaller background and the volume estimate in terms of the Hausdorff distance. The two families in \cref{sec:curved-nonanalytic-references,sec:degenerate-projective-residuals} satisfy these conditions explicitly; since their references have small unbounded locus, the theorem for prescribed singularity types in closed classes cited above applies. The blowup chart and the normal line of the exceptional divisor used to compute the rank and the mass of the residual are taken from \cite[Chapter~VII, equation~(12.2) and Proposition~12.4, pp.~356--357]{DemCADG}. These computations concern fixed modifications and closed backgrounds.

The description of the residual in \cref{prop:divisorial-residual-adjoint} uses the products of bounded plurisubharmonic functions of Bedford and Taylor \cite[Section~2, pp.~3--10]{BT82} and the extension of plurisubharmonic functions across pluripolar sets in \cite[Chapter~I, Theorem~5.24]{DemCADG}. Integration by parts for bounded potentials against a closed positive current is established in \cite[Theorem~1.14 and Lemma~1.15, pp.~214--217]{BEGZ10}; alternatively, the cutoff in \eqref{eq:divisorial-cutoff-bound} can be smoothed explicitly, which makes its estimate a direct Stokes calculation for distributions. In \cref{thm:divisorial-smoothing-adjoints}, the two radial profiles give the coefficients of the divisors before the limit \eqref{eq:divisorial-square-limit} of the squares is taken. This distinguishes the relative residual adjoint from the classical adjoints of smooth potentials whose exponential densities concentrate.

In the non-Gauduchon example of \cref{sec:nongauduchon-weak-adjoints}, the model is computed as in \cref{lem:divisorial-adjoint-model}, with the three-circles theorem on horizontal lines in place of the symmetry of the background, and the residual products are the same bounded products, evaluated by multilinearity and pullback from the first factor. The classical multipliers of the approximating residual forms come from Gauduchon's conformal theorem, recalled in \cref{sec:history-gauduchon}; since no positive representative of the background is Gauduchon, they are never constant, in contrast with \cref{prop:bounded-adjoint-persistence,thm:divisorial-smoothing-adjoints}. The uniqueness of the supporting probability is proved by an expansion in spherical harmonics along the second factor, which reduces the adjoint equation to the fact that no nonzero signed measure on the sphere annihilates $\Delta-V$ for a continuous positive function $V$; this follows from the maximum principle after smoothing $V$. Through the correspondence with bounded residual potentials, the example also gives the conclusion of \cref{conj:continuous-gaud-adjoint} for a non-Gauduchon Hermitian form at continuous densities depending on one factor.

For the normalization on split products in \cref{lem:split-product-normalization}, the smooth input is again \cite[Corollary~1]{TW10}, applied to the closed projective factor. The order of the scalars for bounded densities extends the formula to continuous densities on the factor, and the original truncations determine the threshold for every smooth reference volume. The radial flux in \cref{lem:projective-sublogarithmic-density} and a separate calculation of non-pluripolar products then give \cref{thm:zero-lower-mass-product-equation}. The elliptic factor is the threefold of \cref{sec:vanishing-lower-mass}, whose balanced metric has volume $24\pi$ by \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric}; this gives the fivefold equation with constant $240\pi$. For arbitrary densities that are merely integrable and have positive threshold, the existence of an exact solution remains the problem of \cref{qu:individual-equation-threshold}.

For a general compact K\"ahler factor, \cref{lem:closed-factor-bounded-normalization} uses on the factor the continuous solutions of Ko{\l}odziej and Nguyen, \cref{thm:kn-continuous}, instead of smooth ones, so that the bounded densities may vanish; the truncations then give the threshold of every integrable density from the factor in \cref{thm:closed-factor-product-equation}. It equals the K\"ahler expression of \cref{prop:individual-normalizer-lp}, although the product background is not closed. On the K\"ahler factor the threshold equation is the classical equation of full mass, which Guedj and Zeriahi solved for every measure that charges no pluripolar set \cite[Theorem~A, p.~443]{GZ07}; their operator is twice the one used here. The proof given here obtains the solution instead from a threshold subsolution, because in a closed background the masses of the truncations leave no room for a strict inequality, and it takes the relative envelope class from \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-exact-threshold}. The non-pluripolar measure of a potential pulled back from the factor is then computed level by level. On the resulting subspace of density roots, \cref{prop:closed-factor-root-subspace} gives the K\"ahler norm of \cref{prop:individual-density-banach}, and for the sublogarithmic densities the radial flux gives the logarithmic decay of the root tails in \cref{ex:closed-factor-logarithmic-tails}. These product densities decide neither whether $\mathcal D_h^0=\mathcal D_h$ at zero lower mass nor whether uniform root tails suffice for an exact equation.

The same radial flux gives the criterion for exponential growth in \cref{thm:product-zero-threshold-exponential-criterion}. Disjoint annuli in projective space and a rapidly convergent smooth baseline adapt the concentration in \cref{thm:zero-threshold-solvable-concentration} to every prescribed sequence of base densities whose scalars collapse. The bounded equations on the products determine the scalars of the components, and for every positive exponent the exponentially weighted pole absorbs their sum. The construction of greatest solutions then gives exact solutions at zero threshold, which are necessarily unbounded, in particular on the elliptic products of dimensions four and five. The explicit barriers there have masses $96\pi$ and $240\pi$, whereas the exact solutions have their own finite positive masses. A general criterion for solvability at zero threshold remains open.

That the set of solvable exponents is an upper interval, \cref{prop:solvable-exponents-upper-interval}, follows by translating a subsolution downward before increasing its exponent. For the collapse of mass at zero threshold in \cref{prop:zero-threshold-exponential-mass-collapse}, normalized exponential damping gives an $L^1$ approximation of the fixed density, and upper semicontinuity of the threshold forces the normalization scalars to tend to zero. This controls the masses of the exact solutions without identifying the limits of the normalized potentials.

The criterion for attaining a positive endpoint, \cref{prop:positive-exponent-endpoint-attainment}, combines the compactness of normalized potentials with the closure of subsolutions under lower bounds for the density and the existence of greatest solutions. Its common upper barrier comes from the equation of \cref{thm:nguyen-exp} with the truncated, bounded density, together with singular strict domination. The corrected order in \cref{lem:positive-exponent-uniform-upper-barrier} gives a decreasing family as the exponent decreases to a positive solvable exponent. Monotone convergence in capacity and the closedness theorem for positive exponents identify the limit from the right in \cref{prop:greatest-exponential-right-continuity}, and maximality of the greatest solutions identifies the limiting potential.

The zero-exponent limits on products with zero lower mass in \cref{thm:nonlp-selection-limit} combine three ingredients. Singular strict domination, applied to bounded solutions pulled back from the projective factor, shows that the greatest solutions are pulled back from that factor, and their maximality makes them invariant under the unitary group fixing the pole. For invariant potentials, the Monge--Amp\`ere mass of a ball around the pole is a power of the radial derivative, so the convergence of the measures in total variation, which comes with the scalar limit of \cref{cor:individual-positive-threshold-exponential}, gives uniform convergence of the derivatives; no stability theorem for full-mass solutions is used. Finally, since the exact solutions keep the mass of the threshold, the rescaled scalar error is the logarithm of an exponential damping of the density, and dominated convergence and Fatou's lemma give the first-moment criterion. In the K\"ahler class of the projective factor, the uniqueness of full-mass solutions \cite[Theorem~A, p.~200]{BEGZ10} shows that every potential whose measure dominates $q_\alpha\kappa^k$ is a translate of $u_\alpha$; with it, the order description of the selected limit extends from invariant potentials to all threshold subsolutions pulled back from the factor. The normalization by the mean against the density is the integrable counterpart of the normalizations by supporting probabilities in \cref{thm:vanishing-exponential-selection}; beyond the moment boundary it selects nothing, and the shifted solutions diverge.

\section{Singularity classes and traces}
\label{sec:history-singularities}

A theory of singularities for nonclosed backgrounds must record torsion as well as divisorial information. The quotient of the space of smooth real $(1,1)$-forms by the forms $\ddc f$, $f$ smooth, used in \cref{chap:nonclosed-bclasses}, appears in \cite[Section 1]{BGL25}; fixing the exterior derivative of the form keeps this datum on each modification.

Resolution makes additivity of traces accessible to approximation by multiplier ideals. The comparison in \cref{thm:trace-data-additivity} is the argument underlying Cao's additivity theorem \cite[proof of Theorem~1.4, pp.~118--119]{Cao15}, and it does not require a closed global background. The recovery theorem \cref{thm:positive-volume-system-recovery} combines the arguments with decreasing models and stretching with the local criterion for multiplier ideals. Uniform exponential integrability bounds each local divisorial order of the auxiliary potentials, so the proof does not require a local modification to extend over the compact manifold. The construction on a ruled surface in \cref{sec:envelope-boundary-example}, based on Demailly, Peternell and Schneider's \cite[Example 1.7]{DPS94}, exhibits the boundary behavior relevant to this recovery.

The continuity of model envelopes in closed classes used in \cref{thm:kahler-target-exact-trace} is due to Darvas and Xia \cite{DX24}; we use it in the form of \cite[Proposition~3.1.12, (3.23)--(3.24)]{SGPT}, which covers decreasing model potentials in shrinking backgrounds, the continuity of their masses and, at positive mass, of their divisorial Lelong numbers. The argument for Lelong numbers uses small convex corrections from Darvas, Di Nezza and Lu \cite[Lemma~4.3, p.~154]{DDNL21}. It suffices to apply this continuity on the submanifold, because the restricted approximants have a common curvature bound even when the ambient background is not closed.

\section{Section growth and partial kernels}
\label{sec:history-sections}

The growth of sections links singular potentials to the algebraic dimension through a projective modification. The form of Moishezon's theorem used in \cref{chap:flag-bodies} is stated in \cite[Introduction, p.~1332]{Moi67}; it is used as a classical geometric input. A K\"ahler modification also gives the bounded mass conclusion of \cref{lem:kahler-modification-bounded-mass}, by Guedj and Lu's argument pulling back upper volumes \cite[Lemma 3.6]{GL22}.

For section rings constructed through the algebraic reduction, see Xiaojun Wu's \cite{Wu24}. The flag construction in \cref{chap:flag-bodies} uses a fixed compact flag on an auxiliary modification; it makes no assertion about the centers of arbitrary valuations of the field of meromorphic functions.

The formulas for derivatives in the weight and the Gevrey bounds in \cref{prop:partial-kernel-weight-analyticity,thm:partial-kernel-parameter-derivatives,cor:partial-kernel-pointwise-response} follow the mechanism for bounded weights of Marinescu and Xing \cite[v1, Theorems~1.1--1.2, pp.~2--3, and Section~3, pp.~11--19]{MarinescuXingParameter26}, applied to the fixed space of selected sections on a compact manifold. The proof uses the inverse of a compressed Gram matrix and its finite jets, while the degree and the multiplier-ideal space stay fixed. The example \cref{ex:bergman-interior-parameter-cusp}, concerning control at the boundary, is the explicit example on the disk on p.~3 of that preprint. Marinescu and Xing point to the earlier theory of bounded perturbations of Pasternak-Winiarski; its theorems involving the distance to the boundary require additional weighted openness estimates and are not used in these compact arguments.

The estimates for the probability measures in \cref{lem:coherent-kernel-markov,prop:normalized-kernel-weight-stability,prop:normalized-kernel-uniform-radius} follow directly from the reproducing identities in finite dimension. Positivity of the symmetric coupling defined by the squared kernel gives the factor one half in the bound for infinitesimal variations. Integrating the logarithm of the odds of the probability of each Borel set then gives a sharper modulus for the global oscillation; the smooth example of rank one, \cref{ex:sharp-kernel-weight-modulus}, shows that this modulus is optimal as a supremum. Normalizing by the dimension gives a uniform radius of analyticity after rescaling. The calculation on a product in \cref{ex:inverse-degree-fiber-weight} shows that a perturbation of the norm weight of order inverse to the degree already changes the limiting fiber probability; the same survival of a fiber probability reappears in the conditional fiber probabilities of \cref{sec:horizontal-kernel-asymptotics}. Its nonzero limit of intrinsic measures and its unchanged limit of logarithmic potentials describe different parts of the kernel data.

In the fiber-integrability criterion of \cref{thm:fiber-integrability-band,cor:exact-anisotropic-fiber-series,ex:anisotropic-fiber-sharp-error}, a radial calculation on a simplex detects the strict endpoint of integrability and the weighted gain $p\sum_jb_j^{-1}$, where $p$ and $b_j$ are the exponents of the normal and the fiber variables in the anisotropic model of the selecting potential. A global lower bound and a single chart with an upper bound then give a bounded divisorial band, whose loss of sections is controlled by the dimension estimate \cref{lem:linear-range-section-bound}. This extends the isotropic theorem on sections vanishing along a divisor, without claiming that the condition is necessary for arbitrary selecting potentials.

\Cref{thm:crossing-fiber-integrability-band} extends this argument to finite families of divisors with simple normal crossings. The calculation of the ideal on the base uses separated normal coordinates, and \cref{lem:finite-family-section-bound} controls all the mixed twists arising in the restrictions. In the product model of \cref{ex:crossing-fiber-sharp-error}, integrability at points where several poles meet is established directly, by Fubini's theorem in distinct pairs of base and fiber coordinates.

The criterion on a modified base, \cref{thm:modified-base-fiber-band}, combines the same argument with the transformation rule for adjoint ideals, \cref{lem:adjoint-ideal-transform}, and the pushforward identity for non-pluripolar products, \cref{prop:birational-partial-envelopes}. The projective model with a point pole in \cref{ex:modified-point-fiber-series} makes explicit the Jacobian order $q-1$, where $q$ is the dimension of the base, and the positive residual on the blowup. The criterion assumes that the modification and its divisorial data are given; it does not assert that they exist for an arbitrary selecting potential.

\Cref{cor:semipositive-fiber-band} separates the curvature requirements in these proofs: a smooth semipositive residual gives a positive reference current, and a positive top-degree integral gives it the required mass, while the estimates for integrability and restriction need no strict positivity of the curvature. In \cref{ex:exceptional-semipositive-fiber-series} the original base is a blowup. Its contracted lines prevent any choice of gauge from making the residual strictly positive, while Hartogs extension shows that, because of the fixed exceptional divisor, the selected space and the reference space coincide.

The calculation of coefficients in \cref{prop:exceptional-coefficient-series} combines this Hartogs identification with the anisotropic local threshold; the remaining conditions are ordinary jet conditions at points of projective space. Exact counts of homogeneous monomials then identify the range of the references in which the mass is positive, the drop in degree at the boundary of this range, and the tensor power at which a more singular selecting potential removes every section.

The model with two points in \cref{ex:two-point-semipositive-fiber-series} keeps positive top-degree volume while the residual has degree zero on the curve joining the two centers. Its nonzero defect, of degree $q-1$, is computed by \cref{lem:finite-point-jet-interpolation}, which constructs simultaneous jets from affine linear factors and finite Taylor division and requires the sum of the jet orders to be at most the projective degree plus one. Thus a sharp loss of sections and the obstruction to a smooth strictly positive residual occur in the same explicit family.

The metric on projective space in \cref{sec:logarithmic-kernel-model} is the one in Chiang's \cite[v1, Example~I.15, pp.~9--10]{ChiangKernel26}. The proof of \cref{prop:projective-logarithmic-kernel} computes the global norms of monomials directly, in the convention $|e|_h^2=\mathrm e^{-\phi}$ for a local frame $e$ with weight $\phi$, and for a given integration volume; it uses neither the general spectral localization theorem nor the chain of expansions by Newton polyhedra of that preprint. The transition for growing jets on a compact curve, \cref{thm:compact-growing-jets}, is proved in \cref{sec:compact-growing-jets} by comparison with a model on a shrinking disk, and its full extension to all sublinear orders, at the boundary of the region where the curvature vanishes, is \cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets}.

The correction on shrinking scales in \cref{lem:shrinking-order-correction} uses the $L^2$ estimate with pointwise inverse curvature of \cite[Theorem~5.2 and its singular-metric extension, pp.~34--35]{DemAM11}, whose Hilbert-space estimate comes from the preceding Theorem~5.1 and its proof on pp.~33--34. Applying the estimate to $L^m\otimes K_C^{-1}$, where $L$ is the line bundle on the compact curve $C$ and $K_C$ is the canonical bundle of $C$, takes the Ricci curvature into account, and a bounded function varying on the scale $R$ of the flat region restores strictly positive curvature inside it. The particular free boundary in \cref{lem:curvature-quadrature-domain} is constructed by solving an equation for Fourier coefficients in fixed H\"older spaces of finite order and applying the implicit function theorem in Banach spaces. The argument with Gaussian annuli then gives \cref{thm:curvature-boundary-jets,thm:optimal-growing-jet-range} directly, including slowly growing orders, uniformity in the angle, sharpness on compact curves and the limitation of finite Taylor corrections.

On the explicit compact product in \cref{sec:anisotropic-growing-jets}, global orthogonal monomials turn ordinary jet conditions of total order into a selection by a half-space. The transition for one curve factor gives the marginal Gaussian laws used in \cref{thm:anisotropic-growing-jets}, and the calculation of moments at a fixed positive radius, together with the exact trace, gives convergence in total variation. The sharp radial correction in \cref{prop:anisotropic-homogeneous-center} follows from the positive first remainder assumed there. \Cref{thm:anisotropic-fiber-selector} then uses the integrability calculation at an isolated pole and constancy along compact connected fibers to transfer norms and kernels exactly; the normalization on the base and the matching of volumes are part of its statement.

The selection by an interior Newton polytope in \cref{sec:newton-selected-kernels} is set up with an integral affine offset, a polytope in the interior, a strictly convex invariant logarithmic weight and a positive invariant sampling probability on the whole compact manifold. Angular orthogonality gives exact norms, lattice approximation gives the lower bound for the envelope, and uniform concentration gives the shell measure in the relative dimension. The local argument with Hessian minors distinguishes this measure from mixed non-pluripolar curvature in lower dimension. The construction does not identify an arbitrary multiplier-ideal envelope.

The critical product of multiplier ideals in \cref{sec:ideal-rounding-kernels} is computed directly from the integrability formula for smooth centers and the identity for an atom in the plane. Exhausting the curvature mass fiber by fiber identifies its multiplier-ideal envelope. Exact norms given by beta integrals, together with concentration, give two kernel limits depending on parity, whereas dividing by the exact dimension gives a single limit of probability measures. The calculation shows that a fixed growth exponent does not imply convergence of the leading coefficient in the count of sections, even when the growth dimension is positive.

For the selection by multiplier ideals at the base points of a pencil in \cref{sec:pencil-ideal-kernels}, the lower bound for Lelong numbers implied by inclusions of multiplier ideals at all weights, together with a radial convex maximum, identifies the full classes of competitors for both the multiplier-ideal and the ordinary envelopes. Restriction to the curves of degree two in the pencil controls the competitors that are not invariant. Exact norms of truncated Gaussians keep track of the three bands of finite degree, whose count gives the limiting intrinsic mass three. The bounds at the poles also give convergence in every finite $L^p$ and uniform convergence of the exponentials, while excluding a uniform additive error on the whole manifold. The compact sampling box and the obstacle are part of the statement.

\section{Gauduchon variational volume}
\label{sec:history-gaud-volume}

The Gauduchon variational volume extracts a homogeneous concave quantity from an elliptic normalization problem. The method goes back to Collins's construction \cite[Theorem~2.13, pp.~1068--1069]{Collins21}; \cref{chap:gauduchon-variational-volume} develops the adjoint formula and its dependence on the chosen probability measure.

For a K\"ahler class represented by a closed form $\theta$, this construction meets the Hessian geometry of the K\"ahler cone: the metric of \cref{sec:gaud-volume-hessian} is $1/n$ times the Hessian of $-\log\int_X\theta^n$ used by Magn{\'u}sson \cite[v1, Section~1, pp.~2--4]{Magnusson12}. Completeness and boundary points of positive volume at finite distance are studied in \cite[v1, Proposition~4.4 and Lemma~4.5, p.~11]{Magnusson12}; \cref{sec:gauduchon-metric-geometry} gives the argument by homogeneity in the present normalization.

The cones of invariant nonclosed forms in \cref{sec:parallelizable-gauduchon-cones} are computed directly. Stokes' theorem on the compact quotient shows that invariant $(n-1,n-1)$-forms are closed, and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality for matrices then determines the quotient and its normalized minimizer. The resolvent argument of \cref{lem:positive-matrix-hessian-distance} and the normalized chord in a prescribed linear subspace give the completeness statement. The Iwasawa example comes with an explicit compact quotient and a proof, by averaging coefficients, that this quotient is not K\"ahler. The initial construction uses the normalized invariant probability measure. In dimension three, \cref{prop:invariant-threefold-upper-mass,cor:invariant-singular-coefficient-zero-mass} follow from the formula for the variation of mass proved in the book and from the polynomial continuity of bounded products. On the Iwasawa quotient, \cref{thm:iwasawa-fiber-invariant-density} extends the determinant formula to probability measures invariant along the central fibers, by means of a closed equation on the base for the Schur complement. The exact vertical Dirichlet defect characterizes all maximizing smooth densities, and the completion estimates for this larger family concern the means of the coefficients of the minimizing forms.

For the warped cones in \cref{sec:warped-gauduchon-completion}, a closed equation on the base and an explicit balanced cofactor give the full quotient. The pullback of the affine Hessian metric becomes flat, and the variance of the trace at the endpoints gives the three alternatives: a strip, a half-plane or the whole plane. Smooth existence in the interior is \cref{thm:tw-smooth}, with the scalar fixed by integration since the background is closed. That the finite faces are not big is tested against closed top-degree forms on the base, so the conclusion applies to singular current representatives as well. In the computations on projective space and on periodic tori, the density and the cone are given explicitly throughout.

The cones of currents in \cref{prop:warped-current-cones} are determined by localized closed test forms and Stokes' theorem for distributions. At the finite faces of the warped cone, \cref{prop:warped-continuous-endpoint-equation} applies \cref{thm:kn-continuous}, the existence theorem for continuous solutions with $L^p$ densities, on the closed base; it fixes the scalar by invariance of the mass under bounded perturbations and expands the pulled-back products. The obstruction to global $C^{1,1}$ solutions is the general bound of currents by determinants in \cref{prop:c11-ellipticity}, derived from the identity for coefficients in \cref{prop:c11-products}.

The convergence of the whole sequence in \cref{prop:warped-endpoint-potential-limits} combines bounds for the reciprocal densities with the compactness for a fixed background in \cref{thm:lp-uniform-compactness}. Since the base is closed, the uniqueness theorem for invariant mass, \cref{uni-thm:GL-intro}, identifies every cluster point of the potentials. For any bounded competitor at the endpoint pulled back from the base, the density forced away from the extreme set already exhausts the total mass of the closed class, so positivity excludes residual mass on that set before uniqueness is applied.

The alternatives for the trace in \cref{prop:warped-minimizer-trace,prop:warped-normalized-test-limits} follow from the explicit balanced cofactor and from integrals of reciprocal squares over tubes, while the mass on the closed base controls the remaining cofactor term. When the trace diverges, its normalization concentrates on the extreme components where the ratio is minimal; if there is a single extreme point, the whole sequence converges to a fiber current. For the trace-normalized smooth test metrics, the numerator and the denominator of the volume quotient both tend to zero, while the quotient remains equal to one.

In \cref{sec:nef-boundary-subtraction}, bigness of the closed nef core with positive top self-intersection follows from Demailly and P{\u a}un's criterion \cite[Theorem~0.5]{DP04}; no such criterion for arbitrary nonclosed nef backgrounds is used.

In the radial model on a projective manifold in \cref{sec:radial-positive-volume-nonbig}, a global bounded potential compensates the coefficient along the fibers and gives the lower bound against every smooth Gauduchon test, and explicit balanced metrics give matching values after perturbation. The test against closed top-degree forms on the base in \cref{thm:radial-positive-volume-nonbig} excludes every quasi-plurisubharmonic potential whose curvature current is strictly positive. Thus positive volume does not imply bigness for nonclosed nef backgrounds, even on a projective manifold; the construction says nothing about surfaces, or about closed nef classes on general compact complex manifolds.

The limits of test forms in \cref{sec:radial-gauduchon-test-limits} are computed from these explicit balanced metrics by Stokes' theorem for closed forms and radial integration. In dimension three the powers normalized by the trace concentrate on the degenerate fiber at a logarithmic rate, whereas in higher dimension their threshold quotient stays positive. Localized closed forms on the base, with a small K\"ahler correction, give a different balanced family approaching the fiber in every dimension of the model.

The initial metric of the Chern--Ricci flow in \cref{subsec:actual-flow-positive-volume-nonbig} is chosen so that, up to a smooth global change of gauge, the radial background is the limiting form at the maximal time, and the maximal-time theorem determines this lifetime. In the normalization $\operatorname{Ric}=-\ddc\log\omega^n$, time one corresponds to time $1/(2\pi)$ for the Chern--Ricci form $-2\pi\ddc\log\omega^n$ used in this book. A test against a closed form on the base excludes every positive representative at later times, and the support and the vertical rank of all annihilators at the endpoint follow directly from the semipositive representative and the formula for the trace matrix. The averaged trace is a special case of the trace identity for the flow at the end of \cref{subsec:chern-ricci-maximal-time}.

In the criteria of \cref{subsec:semipositive-envelope-extension}, invariance of the mass of bounded potentials and the support of the measure on the contact set prevent the stretched obstacle envelopes from collapsing, and the sublinear condition provides competitors. The uniform bound for Lelong numbers in \cref{lem:gauduchon-uniform-lelong} then gives vanishing Lelong numbers by scaling. The argument with smooth roots of rank two extends the scalar first-order equation across the analytic set, and the support theorem for currents carried by analytic sets, recalled in \cref{sec:psh-divisorial-estimates}, removes its residual current. The calculations on Hopf manifolds and quadrics give examples in which the dual problem is attained. The general implications involving moments and entropy remain separate questions.

In the example with a point pole, \cref{prop:point-pole-full-mass-nonattainment}, radial integration shows directly that the potential has full mass, and the equality case of the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality for matrices, combined with the local strong maximum principle, excludes every smooth semipositive maximizing test form. The argument does not need to identify the potential with the greatest threshold envelope. The thresholds for entropy and weighted integrability in \cref{cor:point-pole-entropy-energy-threshold} follow directly from the computed radial density. They concern the density of this single equation, whereas the question of uniform moment or entropy conditions concerns the determinant-weighted probability measures of a maximizing sequence when the supremum is attained by a smooth semipositive test.

The bound for the determinant at the endpoint, \cref{thm:gaud-determinant-endpoint}, is derived within the book from the criterion for mass invariance and the quotient by the lower mass; related criteria for the mass are \cite[Propositions~3.2 and 3.3(iv)]{GL22}. The concentrating family in \cref{sec:gaud-determinant-concentration} distinguishes the unique smooth maximizer for one density from maximizing sequences whose higher moments diverge.

The identity \eqref{eq:bounded-cubic-energy} of \cref{lem:bounded-cubic-lower-mass}, for bounded potentials in a background $g$ with $\ddc(g^2)=0$, uses the calculus of bounded products and of weighted decreasing limits in \cref{prop:bounded-products,lem:weighted-decreasing-products}, whose classical local foundations are \cite[Section~2, Theorems~2.1 and~2.4, pp.~4--9]{BT82}. That the sign $\ddc g\leq0$ gives positive lower mass is part of the criteria in terms of the sign of the torsion of Angella, Guedj and Lu \cite[Theorem~3.2, pp.~4641--4642]{AGL23}. The curvature calculation in \cref{thm:elliptic-curvature-endpoint} identifies this sign on principal elliptic threefolds, and the two quotient constructions give explicit mass constants in the present convention for the operator. The conclusion for the determinant of the full current then follows from the endpoint theorem proved in the book and a change of background.

The estimate for local families in \cref{thm:gaud-local-adjoint-endpoint} controls the solution form and its Gauduchon multiplier by two contractions. The elliptic input is the parametrix and Fredholm argument on compact manifolds in Taylor \cite[Chapter~7, Sections~4 and~10, especially (10.4)--(10.6)]{Taylor7}, together with the mapping properties on H\"older spaces in \cite[Chapter~13, (8.7), (8.17) and (8.22)]{Taylor13}. Invariance of the index is \cite[Section~7, Proposition~7.4]{TaylorFA}, and smooth regularity for nonlinear elliptic equations is also formulated in \cite[Proposition]{TaylorReg}. The estimates for the equation and for the normalized multiplier give explicit constants for the densities without assuming positive lower mass; instead, the data are restricted to smooth functions in a small $C^{2,\alpha}$ neighborhood.

The classical analytic input in \cref{sec:nef-modification-comparison} is Hironaka's elimination of indeterminacies: every modification is dominated by a composition of blowups with smooth centers. The argument by principalization and domination is explained by Abramovich, Karu, Matsuki and W{\l}odarczyk \cite[Sections 1.2.3--1.2.4, pp.~538--539, and Section 5.4, p.~564]{AKMW02}, where the analytic case is treated explicitly. The curvature of the exceptional divisors then gives the uniform bound for the pairings used here; no factorization into a sequence of blowdowns is needed for this estimate.

For the fixed projective cones in \cref{sec:nef-projective-integer-cones}, every Gauduchon test is compared with a closed form of constant rank through an explicit logarithm of an exceptional divisor, which gives the exact loss of rank when the prescribed effective divisors are removed. For a general modification whose source is K\"ahler, \cref{prop:nef-projective-modification-equality} obtains in addition two-sided bounds by powers from the intersection polynomial for closed backgrounds in \cref{sec:nef-volume-degeneration}. Fixed coefficient functions that vanish, cones of residuals without restrictions, and sources that are not K\"ahler lie outside this construction.

The theorem that the line bundle of a smooth anticanonical curve of self-intersection zero is unitary flat near the curve, \cref{thm:flat-anticanonical-neighborhood}, is due to Chen, Filip, Sun, Tosatti and Zhang \cite[v1, Theorem~1.1, p.~1, with the standing degree-zero hypothesis in Section~1.1]{CFSTZ26}. The proof given here follows their identification of the holomorphic fibers in Proposition~4.1, their elliptic inverse independent of the holonomy in Lemma~5.2, and their weighted horizontal inverse and contraction in Propositions~5.4--5.5. The cutoff in \cref{prop:flat-divisor-rank-one-curvature} is the construction at the end of their proof in Section~6. The variational bounds in rank one follow from \cref{prop:nef-maximum-rank-comparison}, and the examples obtained by blowing up nine points on a plane cubic, together with their torsion criterion, follow by restriction to the cubic. Only this local theorem and its direct consequences are used, not the global classification of surfaces in that paper.

The pencil in the case of finite holonomy and the rigidity in the non-torsion case, \cref{prop:flat-divisor-finite-holonomy,thm:flat-divisor-holomorphic-rigidity}, give the case of a smooth reduced divisor of Koike's \cite[v2, Lemma~3.5, pp.~15--16]{Koike26}. The Levi-flat level sets of the flat norm have a smooth suspension model, so the density of their leaves and the maximum modulus argument are proved directly. The hypothesis is that the line bundle of the divisor is unitary flat near the smooth compact divisor; the global criteria for semipositivity and the dichotomy between fibrations and the Hartogs phenomenon in Koike's paper are not used. Together with the theorem on anticanonical neighborhoods, these local results explain the non-Stein and Hartogs properties of the nine-point examples with non-torsion normal bundle.

The quotient construction in \cref{prop:elliptic-affine-quotient-nonstein} follows the mechanism of invariant fibers of Denisi and H\"oring \cite[v1, Lemma~2.10, p.~6, Proposition~4.2, p.~12 and pp.~14--18, and Theorem~5.1, p.~19]{DH26}. Their action has $\kappa=2\pi\mathrm{i}\rho$, where $\kappa$ is the parameter of \cref{prop:elliptic-affine-quotient-nonstein} and $\rho$ is theirs, and their function $\mu_Q$ equals $1+2\sigma$, with $\sigma$ the function defined before that proposition. The Laurent argument in \cref{lem:positive-line-puncture-hull} proves directly the obstruction for punctured line bundles used in their Example~2.1, while \cref{ex:positive-elliptic-automorphy} computes the degree of an explicit factor of automorphy by its invariant metric. The statement about the quotient includes the two exceptional parameters, which are treated with compact elliptic curves. The computation of classes in \cref{thm:split-elliptic-cotangent-torsor} gives their Proposition~4.2 and Lemmas~4.5 and 4.7--4.8, with explicit generators and conventions for projectivization. The flat unitary character in \cref{lem:elliptic-degree-automorphy-normalization} treats line bundles of every positive degree $d$ directly; the coefficient of the quotient is then $\kappa=2\pi\mathrm{i}d\rho$. This gives their non-Stein conclusion for split elliptic ruled surfaces without the reduction to a finite cover in their Lemma~5.2.

For the split elliptic ruled surface $M$, the algebra $\bigoplus_{r\geq0}H^0(M,\operatorname{Sym}^rT_M)$ in \cref{thm:split-elliptic-tangent-ring-nonfinite} has the bigness and nonfinite generation stated in \cite[v1, Theorem~1.4, p.~2, with proof on pp.~9--10]{DH26}. The decomposition by polynomial weights in \cref{lem:split-elliptic-tensor-weights} gives the exact dimensions and the tautological volume in \cref{thm:split-elliptic-symmetric-dimensions}, and its map to leading coefficients gives a direct proof of nonfinite generation through a quotient by an ideal of square zero.

For canonical extensions, \cite[v1, Remark~3.8, pp.~10--11]{DH26} also states bigness and nonfinite generation, leaving the proof to the reader. The homogeneous quadric in \cref{lem:elliptic-extension-fiber-ring} and the decomposition of coefficients under the unipotent action give the description as a polynomial algebra in \cref{thm:elliptic-extension-polynomial-ring}. The exact dimensions, the tautological volume and nonfinite generation in \cref{cor:elliptic-extension-symmetric-dimensions} follow for every extension class that is nonzero on the fibers of the ruling, including all K\"ahler canonical extensions on the split surface. The case of an extension class vanishing on the fibers of the ruling, \cref{prop:elliptic-zero-ruling-section-ring}, follows directly from the ordinary cotangent quadric and the same decomposition of coefficients. Its section algebra is the same as in the case of nonzero restriction, while \cref{prop:elliptic-zero-ruling-torsor-cover} identifies the universal cover of the corresponding torsor, which is not Stein, as the product of $\mathbb C^2$ with the total space of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)$.

The question whether a weak Monge--Amp\`ere power admits a scalar conformal factor is raised by Lu \cite[Chapter 3, Section 5]{LuHDR22}. The representation by finite currents in \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints} gives a supporting probability measure without defining that weak power or its scalar factor.

\section{Closed classes and singular loci}
\label{sec:history-closed-loci}

For closed classes on a Hermitian manifold, almost positive representatives keep the cohomology class separate from the auxiliary metric. The convention in \cref{sec:bc-hermitian-bounds} follows Boucksom's thesis \cite[Section 2.1.2]{BouThesis02}; it does not assign a Bott--Chern class to a nonclosed Hermitian form.

The smooth and analytic regularizations of \cref{subsec:psh-closed-regularization}, used for \cref{prop:bc-non-nef}, have the lower bounds of \cite[Theorem 2.1]{Bou04Zariski}; see also \cite[Theorems 1.2.9--1.2.10]{BouThesis02}. Smooth regularization loses positivity by a multiple of a continuous approximation of the Lelong numbers, whereas analytic regularization has a curvature error tending uniformly to zero and does not increase Lelong numbers.

The minimal multiplicities of \cref{def:bc-minimal-multiplicity} are defined through these almost positive approximating families, as in \cite[Definition 2.1.5]{BouThesis02}. They must be distinguished from the Lelong numbers of a single current with minimal singularities: in the example of a nef class on a ruled surface in \cite[Section 1.2.3]{BouThesis02}, a current with minimal singularities has nonzero Lelong numbers.

The non-K\"ahler locus of \cref{def:bc-non-kahler-locus} is defined in \cite[Definition 2.1.19]{BouThesis02}, and the proof of \cref{thm:bc-non-kahler-locus} by finite intersections and regularized maxima is the argument of \cite[Theorem 2.1.20]{BouThesis02}.

The equivalence between the existence of a K\"ahler current and the existence of a K\"ahler modification, used in \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci}, is the classical criterion recalled in \cite[Theorem 1.2.11]{BouThesis02}; it is used as an external geometric input. The equality of the non-K\"ahler locus and the null locus of a nef and big class is the transcendental Nakamaye theorem of Collins and Tosatti \cite[Theorem~1.1, p.~1168]{CT15Loci}, which also holds on compact manifolds in Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$; \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci} recalls this theorem from the literature.

Restricted volumes give a numerical test for these loci. The numerical restricted volume \eqref{eq:bc-numerical-restricted-volume} follows Collins and Tosatti \cite[Section 2]{CT22Restricted}; the requirement that the analytic singularities have a smooth remainder is part of that definition.

The existence of K\"ahler models for every subvariety uses Fujiki's theorem that class $\mathcal C$ is closed under passing to closed subspaces \cite[Lemma~4.6(2), p.~35]{Fujiki78}, followed by Varouchas's theorem on K\"ahler modifications in the form stated and explained by Barlet \cite[Theorem 10, pp.~31--32]{Barlet00}. A common K\"ahler refinement is obtained by embedded resolution of the graph in a K\"ahler product, using Bierstone and Milman \cite[Theorem~1.6 and Remarks~1.7(2), pp.~215--216]{BM97}. These are classical geometric inputs. The descent of potentials is proved in \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}, so the proof of mass invariance does not repeat it.

In \cref{prop:restricted-mass-bigness}, positive non-pluripolar mass implies bigness by \cite[Proposition~1.22, p.~222]{BEGZ10}. The definition of the positive product, its agreement with the cup product for nef classes, and the fact that, for big classes, it is attained by currents with minimal singularities are in \cite[Definition~1.17, pp.~218--219, and Proposition~1.20, pp.~221--222]{BEGZ10}. The normalized diagonal current \eqref{eq:normalized-diagonal-current} uses the mass-concentration theorem of Demailly and P{\u a}un \cite[Proposition 2.6]{DP04} and makes explicit the coefficient in the construction of \cite[Theorem 2.12]{DP04}. These results on K\"ahler manifolds are used as classical inputs. The identity for the mass with a bounded residual in \cref{lem:bounded-residual-mass-polynomial}, by contrast, follows directly from the book's formula for bounded masses, by comparison of polynomials. The comparison with the numerical restricted volume for nef classes is \cite[Lemma 2.6]{CT22Restricted}, and the final equality in \eqref{eq:finite-restricted-null-union} uses the theorem \cite[Theorem 1.1]{CT15Loci} recalled above.

The rigidity criterion for nef boundary classes on tori, \cref{thm:flat-torus-radial-rigidity}, develops the flat model of Fu and Ouyang \cite[v1, Section~3, pp.~5--6]{FuOuyang26}. Testing against Fourier modes proves the vanishing along the kernel directly, and the metric argument uses a finite covering by dense leaves and a character of the circle. The collapse in this theorem is along a fixed positive perturbation $\theta+t\kappa$, where $\kappa$ is a translation-invariant K\"ahler form. The calculation with mixed forms in \cref{ex:unrestricted-torus-collapse} shows that the conclusion of their Lemma~3.1 and the implication (iii)$\Rightarrow$(i) of their Proposition~3.2 fail for arbitrary sequences of translation-invariant K\"ahler forms whose classes converge to the boundary class; their Theorem~4.4, which concerns positive perturbations, and the distinction made in their Remark~6.4, p.~18, concern a different setting. Their general theorems on collapse, fibrations and lattice dynamics for K3 surfaces are not used in this argument on tori.

The nonrigid class with an irrational ray in \cref{prop:kummer-nonrigid-irrational} is the explicit Kummer construction of \cite[v1, Section~7.1, pp.~18--19, proof of Theorem~1.4(a)]{FuOuyang26}. The resolution of the quotient and its K\"ahler metric are described in local charts, and the difference of two representatives is given an explicit periodic global $\ddc$-primitive. Periods over translated real two-dimensional subtori detect the irrational ray without extending cohomology classes across the punctures. Fu and Ouyang's conclusions about diameters of Ricci-flat metrics, spectra and limit spaces require additional inputs and are not used in this construction.

The family obtained from four points and its central metric in \cref{sec:four-point-metric-nonextension} are Qin's construction \cite[v1, Theorem~A, p.~1, and Section~3, Lemmas~3.1--3.3, pp.~3--4]{QinMetricExtension26}. Qin's proof of the nonextension theorem on p.~5 uses global $L^2$ extension for adjoint bundles. \Cref{thm:four-point-semipositive-metric-nonextension} instead uses the conics of degree zero of \cref{lem:four-point-conic-degeneration}: Fubini's theorem, averages over disks and Harnack's inequality force the central weights to be harmonic away from the node, and the remaining curvature has logarithmic coefficient one, whereas the metric constructed here has coefficient one half. This shows that the metric has no semipositive extension whose central restriction is at most as singular as the metric itself, without using global adjoint extension and without asserting that this metric has minimal singularities. Varying the conic gives the exact set of restrictions in \cref{thm:four-point-metric-restriction-image}: the divisor metric of the negative line multiplied by the pullback of an arbitrary semipositive metric on the base of the pencil. The converse is constructed from the two homogeneous sections of the pencil, and \cref{cor:four-point-least-singular-extendable} concerns the least singular metrics among these restrictions.

Inoue surfaces were introduced by Inoue \cite{Inoue74}, and their higher-dimensional analogues by Oeljeklaus and Toma \cite[Section~1 and Proposition~2.5]{OT05}, who proved that the action of $\mathcal O_K\rtimes U$ used in \cref{sec:oeljeklaus-toma} is free, properly discontinuous and cocompact, and that the resulting manifolds are not K\"ahler. Ornea and Verbitsky showed that Oeljeklaus--Toma manifolds with $t=1$ contain no positive-dimensional complex subvarieties, using a semipositive closed form whose null leaves are Zariski dense \cite{OV11OT}. The decomposition of Inoue surfaces into three-dimensional tori filled by dense leaves, and a Liouville argument along the leaves for bounded harmonic functions, appear in the work of Fang, Tosatti, Weinkove and Zheng on the Chern--Ricci flow \cite[Section~2, Lemma~2.1 and its proof, pp.~3166--3168]{FTWZ16}. The Tricerri metric and the identity $\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_T)=-\alpha$ also appear there; their form $\alpha=\mathrm i\,\mathrm dw\wedge\mathrm d\overline w/(4y^2)$ equals $2\pi\theta$ in the normalization of \cref{sec:oeljeklaus-toma}, and their $\omega_T=4\alpha+\beta$ is the Tricerri metric used there. The description of all $\theta$-plurisubharmonic functions in \cref{thm:ot-transverse-potentials}, the mass computations of \cref{prop:ot-threefold-mass} and the absence of plurisigned metrics in \cref{prop:ot-no-plurisigned} are proved directly here.

The curvature criteria for finite upper and positive lower mass referred to in \cref{sec:oeljeklaus-toma} are due to Angella, Guedj and Lu, who proved that, on a compact threefold, a Hermitian metric with $\ddc\omega\geq0$ gives finite upper mass and one with $\ddc\omega\leq0$ gives positive lower mass; in particular a pluriclosed metric gives both \cite[Theorem~B, p.~4632]{AGL23}. The general question of finiteness and positivity of these masses is \cite[Problem~1.8, p.~4635]{AGL23}. Angella, Dubickas, Otiman and Stelzig showed that an Oeljeklaus--Toma manifold admits a pluriclosed metric only if $s=t$ \cite[Corollary~3, pp.~220 and~228]{ADOS24}; \cref{prop:ot-no-plurisigned} shows that for type $(s,1)$ with $s\geq2$ there is not even a metric whose $\ddc$ has a sign.

\section{Closed classes and metric completeness}
\label{sec:history-completeness}

The examples \cref{thm:incomplete-divisorial-metric,thm:nef-incomplete-cusp,thm:flow-compatible-incomplete-metric} test the completeness question posed as an open problem by Di Nezza, Guedj and Lu \cite[v1, Question~3.5, p.~14]{DNGL26}, for the solutions whose existence and uniqueness they prove in \cite[v1, Theorem~3.4, p.~14]{DNGL26}. The first uses a big class that is not nef and an ordinary solution with minimal singularities. The second has a semipositive nef and big background, a solution of full mass and finite energy with vanishing Lelong numbers, and the prescribed nonintegrable pole with smooth strictly positive coefficient; its cusp is complete, but a disjoint null curve lies at finite normal distance. The third, \cref{thm:flow-compatible-incomplete-metric}, satisfies exactly the identities of \cite[v1, Section~3.1, pp.~12--14]{DNGL26} for the initial potential, the resolution, the discrepancy, the class and the prescribed volume. Its class is not nef and has a divisorial reference with minimal singularities, while the solution adds a cusp with vanishing Lelong numbers along a disjoint curve. These examples answer the completeness question negatively for the elliptic equations, both for a big class that is not nef and for a nef and big class; Theorem~3.4 of that paper asserts existence, uniqueness, the Lelong numbers of the solution and, for nef and big classes, smoothness on a dense Zariski open set, but not completeness. Whether nefness and flow compatibility can hold simultaneously in such an example, and whether the evolving maximal flow itself is complete, remain open. The torus examples for normal lengths in \cref{sec:divisorial-normal-length} have instead logarithmically weakened poles whose coefficients tend to zero at the divisor.

The energy argument in \cref{sec:incomplete-divisorial-metric} uses the classical finite-energy class of \cite[Section~2, pp.~223--236]{BEGZ10}, relative to a reference with minimal singularities; finiteness of the mixed masses and of the relative energy comes from \cite[Proposition~1.6, p.~206, and Section~2.2, pp.~226--230]{BEGZ10}. Uniqueness follows from the comparison principle for full mass \cite[Corollary~2.3, p.~224]{BEGZ10}: the unweighted reference measure is infinite, but the two Monge--Amp\`ere measures being compared are finite.

In \cref{sec:nef-incomplete-cusp}, the potential with minimal singularities is $V_\theta=0$, and exact radial identities show that the volume form is smooth and positive across both divisors. The bounded truncations have masses $1-p(T_k)^2$ for the square and $1-p(T_k)$ for the mixed product on the transition shell, where $p$ is the slope of the radial profile and $T_k$ the radius at which the truncation at level $k$ begins; their contributions to the energy tend to zero. The products are computed by smooth convex approximation and the continuity along decreasing sequences proved by Bedford and Taylor \cite[Theorem~2.1, pp.~4--7]{BT82}. This direct computation of the energy agrees with the criterion by canonical approximation of \cite[Propositions~2.10--2.11, pp.~228--230]{BEGZ10}. Here vanishing Lelong numbers do not imply ordinary minimal singularities.

In \cref{sec:flow-compatible-incomplete-metric}, radial barriers and the inversion of a Volterra operator construct the cusp along the curve for a fixed smooth volume form, and explicit charts on the blowup give a smooth positive residual volume and a K\"ahler residual at the initial time. The reference with minimal singularities is obtained from the extension across isolated points in \cref{lem:isolated-puncture-extension}. The canonical truncations at level $k$ have cutoff energy $O(k\mathrm{e}^{-k/2})$, so the continuity of bounded products and the criteria for relative energy cited above apply. The remaining smooth pulled-back metric puts the exceptional curve at finite distance. This is an auxiliary solution of an elliptic equation; it is not identified with the evolving smoothing metric.

H\"older regularity imposes a further restriction on the metric. If a positive residual current has a H\"older continuous potential and is smooth and strictly positive outside a proper analytic set, \cref{thm:holder-current-distance} shows that every point of that set lies at finite distance in the metric completion; on a compact manifold, removing a nonempty analytic set therefore gives an incomplete metric. Continuity alone allows complete ends: for the prescribed radial densities of \cref{thm:radial-endpoints}, boundedness of the minimal remainder and finiteness of the normal length have different logarithmic thresholds. The resulting complete examples with bounded potentials solve equations with prescribed densities; they do not have the compatibility with the flow discussed above.

The mechanism of Dang \cite[v2, Proposition~5.2, pp.~16--17]{Dang26}, which bounds distances through a Dini condition on the square root of the modulus of continuity of the potential, is the subject of \cref{thm:dini-current-distance}. The trace calculation given here keeps the smooth background through the additive term in \eqref{eq:dini-distance-modulus} and needs no doubling condition. It gives logarithmic rates and uniform limits of pseudometrics along subsequences in \cref{cor:dini-distance-rates,cor:dini-family-distance-limits}; a common positive lower bound for the metrics is needed to exclude collapse.

\section{Locally conformally K\"ahler geometry}
\label{sec:history-lck}

In locally conformally K\"ahler geometry, the Lee form constrains both the rigidity of metrics and the volume forms that can be prescribed. Ornea and Verbitsky conjecture that the Lee class has positive degree with respect to every Gauduchon metric \cite[Conjecture~4.14, p.~7]{OV25Balanced}, and relate this to pseudoeffectivity of the Bott--Chern class $\{\kappa_\lambda\}$ \cite[Remark~4.16, p.~8]{OV25Balanced}. By \cref{thm:lamari}, their conjecture implies the weaker \cref{conj:lck-lee-psef}, which is the form used here; its consequence for balanced metrics is \cref{prop:lck-balanced-obstruction}. Their theorem on the volume forms of Vaisman metrics \cite[Theorem~4.1 and Lemmas~4.2--4.4]{OV24Vaisman} requires invariance under both the Lee and the anti-Lee vector fields. The proof of \cref{thm:lck-vaisman-volume} uses \cref{thm:tw-smooth}: uniqueness gives a basic potential, transverse integration by Stokes' theorem fixes the scalar, and the Lee form of the new metric is parallel because it is closed and its dual vector field is Killing. The uniqueness argument keeps the scale of the candidate metric in \eqref{eq:vaisman-volume-contraction} as an unknown until integration determines it.

The classification of radial metrics in \cref{subsec:radial-gauduchon-lck-volumes} follows from explicit identities for the Hopf forms and a periodic scalar equation. Its positive periodic solutions are strictly ordered by the constant of integration, and their Lee periods fill the negative half-line. The positive twisted potential is constructed in the gauge given by the Lee form of the metric itself. Nonconstant radial volume forms force the metric to be non-Vaisman, and uniqueness of the metric is asserted only within the radial family considered there.

Barbaro and Otiman prove that an LCK metric that is Gauduchon and Chern--Ricci flat, or more generally whose Chern--Ricci form is a suitable multiple of $\mathrm d(J\lambda)$, is Vaisman \cite[v2, Theorem~A and Section~4, pp.~11--13]{BO25LCK}, and the conjecture for manifolds, \cref{conj:lck-calabi-yau-vaisman}, is stated in \cite[v2, Introduction, p.~2]{BO25LCK}. Their formulas for the Ricci tensor keep the divergence of the Lee form \cite[v2, equations~(2)--(4), p.~12]{BO25LCK}, which is set to zero before the Bianchi and Bochner identities are integrated. Keeping this term through both integrations gives the identity of \cref{thm:lck-chern-ricci-divergence}; the Chern--Ricci-flat case of their theorem and the characterization by a holomorphic Lee field in \cref{thm:lck-chern-ricci-rigidity} follow from it. When the first Bott--Chern class vanishes, there is a Chern--Ricci-flat metric in each conformal class, unique up to scale, but it need not be the Gauduchon representative required by the rigidity argument. Barbaro and Otiman observe this by prescribing a nonconstant volume for a Vaisman metric on a Vaisman manifold with vanishing first Bott--Chern class \cite[v2, Section~4, Example, p.~13]{BO25LCK}; the Chern--Ricci-flat Vaisman metric with given Lee class used there, which they quote from the literature, is obtained in \cref{cor:lck-calabi-yau-vaisman-reduction} from \cref{thm:lck-vaisman-volume}. \Cref{prop:lck-kodaira-flat-gauge} carries out this construction explicitly on a primary Kodaira surface, which they mention as the first Vaisman example with vanishing first Bott--Chern class \cite[v2, Section~4, p.~12]{BO25LCK}; by the uniqueness of the flat gauge, the conformal classes of these Vaisman metrics contain no metric that is both Chern--Ricci flat and Gauduchon.

The dual description of the Lee--Gauduchon cone and its bimeromorphic invariance in \cref{sec:lee-gauduchon-cone} are due to Ornea and Verbitsky \cite[Theorems~3.11 and 4.5, pp.~243--244]{OV25LeeGauduchon}. The distinction between vanishing of the Lee--Gauduchon class and the strongly Gauduchon condition in \cref{sec:lee-gauduchon-strong} follows \cite[Remark~5.6, Example~5.7 and Remark~5.8, pp.~245--246]{OV25LeeGauduchon}, and the criterion for the existence of strongly Gauduchon metrics used there is due to Popovici \cite[Proposition~1.2, p.~654]{Pop13SG}.

\section{Regularity and singular spaces}
\label{sec:history-regularity}

Regularity in a big class is first a local question on the locus where strict barriers are smooth. For a closed class on a compact K\"ahler manifold, existence and uniqueness for \eqref{eq:big-class-smooth-volume} are due to Boucksom, Eyssidieux, Guedj and Zeriahi \cite[Theorem~A, p.~200, equivalently Theorem~3.1, p.~236]{BEGZ10}, and the bounds \eqref{eq:ample-solution-minimal-bounds} in terms of minimal singularities are \cite[Theorem~B, p.~201, quantitatively Theorem~4.1, p.~245]{BEGZ10}. The same uniqueness theorem for full mass identifies the solutions in closed classes in \cref{sec:prescribed,sec:blowups,sec:radial,sec:curves}. Smoothness on the ample locus in the nef case is \cite[Theorem~C, p.~201]{BEGZ10}; \cref{thm:big-class-ample-smoothness} gives this smoothness in every closed big class, for a smooth positive volume form.

For a big background that need not be closed, the local H\"older theorem \cref{thm:big-minimal-holder} is Dang's \cite[v2, Theorem~A and Theorem~3.1]{Dang26}. It gives the local input for the argument on the ample locus, while allowing the global potential to be unbounded and the lower curvature bound to be nonclosed. Formulating the equation with a positive scalar and only on the regular locus is equivalent, since the complementary analytic set carries neither smooth volume nor non-pluripolar mass.

The proof of local regularity also needs a quantitative stability estimate. The estimate with a strict barrier in \cref{sec:big-reference-stability} is \cite[v2, Theorem~3.3 and Remark~3.4]{Dang26} in the case of minimal singularities, with the comparison potential normalized explicitly; the construction with auxiliary subsolutions in \cref{sec:bounded-stability} first treats a semipositive big background.

The interior regularity theorem \cref{thm:nonclosed-interior-smoothness} combines this H\"older control with the assumption that the density is smooth and positive on the whole regular locus of one fixed strict analytic barrier. This domain is part of the hypothesis: smoothness of the density on a smaller open set does not give the same conclusion by restriction. The compact Pogorelov example \cref{ex:compact-pogorelov} has a smooth positive density on an inner chart, but its density loses regularity along the divisor in the gluing region, so it does not satisfy this hypothesis.

Local smoothing uses the theorem of Guan and Li on the Dirichlet problem \cite[Theorem~1.1]{GL10}. In \eqref{eq:local-density-determinant} the background is zero and, in local coordinates $z$, the metric is $(\mathrm i/2)\partial\bar\partial|z|^2$; for a smooth positive density $G$, a large quadratic correction gives a strict subsolution. The same theorem constructs the approximants in \cref{sec:interior-approximants} and starts the passage to continuous boundary values and to zero density in \cref{sec:local-homogeneous-dirichlet}; in dimension one it is replaced by the Dirichlet problem for the Poisson equation. Once \eqref{eq:interior-local-ma} is uniformly elliptic, the Evans--Krylov theorem for variable coefficients \cite[Definition~3.2, Theorem~3.4, and the paragraph following it, pp.~444--445]{TWWY15}, followed by difference quotients and Schauder estimates, gives smoothness.

The local identification in \cref{sec:local-density-boundary} uses Bedford and Taylor's uniqueness theorem for the bounded Dirichlet problem \cite[Corollary~4.4, p.~17]{BT82}. Degeneration of the density also matters for global stability: Ko{\l}odziej and Nguyen \cite[Theorem 4.1]{KN21} assume that the density satisfies $f\geq c_0$ for a constant $c_0>0$, in addition to their hypotheses on capacity. The densities giving nonuniqueness in \cref{chap:degenerate-uniqueness} have no such lower bound, even when they are positive almost everywhere.

For compact sets in a Hermitian manifold, Ahn \cite[v3, Theorems~1.1--1.2, p.~3]{Ahn26} proves that H\"older continuity of the extremal function is a pointwise local property. The weighted version assumes that both the unweighted extremal function and the weight are H\"older continuous, and that the set is uniformly dense in capacity; the same density condition enters the equivalence with the local and weakly local H\"older properties. These are the regularity settings referred to in \cref{chap:research-program}.

On singular spaces, regularity on a resolution must be combined with descent. Dang \cite[v2, Theorem~B]{Dang26} proves continuity results for bounded solutions on resolutions under additional geometric and integrability hypotheses, notably for log terminal singularities; this result does not cover the arbitrary unibranch singularities in the problem posed in \cref{chap:research-program}. Pan \cite[v1, Theorems~A and~A']{Pan25} constructs a Gauduchon metric on the regular locus of a compact reduced irreducible Hermitian space of dimension at least two; it is conformal and uniformly comparable to the given metric, and unique up to scale among such metrics. Control of the conformal factors on a resolution is central to the construction. This result motivates the questions about singular traces in \cref{chap:research-program}.

In the construction on unibranch curves in \cref{sec:unibranch-curve-equations}, the Green function, the exact mass of the pole, uniqueness in the full singularity type, descent through disks and the projective cusp are all obtained directly. $L^p$ data on the normalization, $p>1$, give a continuous residual even when the background coming from a local embedding pulls back to a degenerate form. The example of the cusp also computes the exact threshold for densities on the singular curve. This is a result in dimension one; it neither extends the theorem on log terminal singularities cited above nor gives a general equation on nonclosed singular spaces.

The calculation at the endpoint in \cref{thm:unibranch-curve-density-endpoint} is another direct construction from the Green function of the curve and the radial primitive of \cref{thm:curve-products}. For one logarithmic family it distinguishes three properties: finite mass of the density, a bounded residual in the ordinary singularity type, and equivalence of multiplier ideals at all weights. It does not characterize arbitrary integrable data.

The equations with nonclosed backgrounds on products with cuspidal curves in \cref{sec:nonclosed-cuspidal-products} combine a correction of the reference to a smooth positive form, quantitative Fourier inversion, and a comparison of bounded energies in a background all of whose powers are $\ddc$-closed. The weighted limit \cref{lem:weighted-decreasing-products} is proved by adding one complex variable $w$ and passing to the maxima with $\log|w|^2$, which reduces it to the ordinary convergence of mixed products along decreasing sequences, including top-degree products in one higher dimension. The corresponding classical input is Bedford and Taylor's \cite[Theorem~2.4 and its proof, pp.~7--9]{BT82}; their operator is $4\pi$ times ours, so products of degree $r$ scale by $(4\pi)^{-r}$. The resulting family with small data in all variables has H\"older continuous solutions on the ambient space, unique among all bounded competitors. The existence of bounded solutions for arbitrary nonnegative $L^p$ data on the normalization, $p>1$, follows separately from \cref{thm:li-general-measure}. These results complement the construction with Green functions on curves, without giving a general equation on singular spaces or a sharp criterion for densities on the singular space.

\section{Geometric problems}
\label{sec:history-geometry}

A closed strictly positive current has geometric consequences that a positive nonclosed background need not have, and this distinction governs the passage from the normalization of equations to the geometric criteria below. The characterization of Fujiki's class by K\"ahler currents used in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} is Demailly and P{\u a}un's \cite[Theorem 0.7]{DP04}. It shows that a big class forces $X\in\mathcal C$; conversely, if $X\in\mathcal C$, the pullback of a closed nef class with positive top self-intersection to a K\"ahler modification is big by \cite[Theorem~0.5]{DP04}, and the pushforward of a K\"ahler current in the pulled-back class is a K\"ahler current in the original class. In the same paper, the transcendental Grauert--Riemenschneider conjecture is \cite[Conjecture 0.8]{DP04}.

For nef classes with positive top self-intersection, the equivalence between bigness and bounded mass cited in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} is Guedj and Lu's \cite[Theorem 4.9]{GL22}. The characterization of Fujiki's class by bounded mass together with positive upper volume of some closed Bott--Chern class is \cite[Theorem C]{BGL25}; that upper volume is different from the Gauduchon functional for a fixed probability measure.

The transcendental Morse inequality for differences of nef classes on compact K\"ahler manifolds, and its consequences for differentiability of the volume and duality of cones, mentioned in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems}, are Tosatti's \cite[v2, Theorem~1.1 and Corollary~1.2]{Tosatti26}; they are quoted from the literature, and their proofs are not reproduced. The qualitative criterion under bounded mass is \cite[Theorem 4.12]{GL22}.

The non-Hermitian locus in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} is that of Dang \cite[Definition 2.12 and Theorem 2.14]{DangNull26}, where almost analytic singularities have the precise meaning of \cite[Definition 2.8]{DangNull26}, including a smooth remainder on a suitable log resolution. The qualitative identification of this locus with the null locus, for nef and big backgrounds that may be nonclosed, is \cite[Theorem 1.1]{DangNull26}, obtained there by observing that the proof of Collins and Tosatti does not use closedness. Theorem A of the same paper gives a numerical criterion under different hypotheses: bounded mass on the ambient manifold and positive lower volume on its submanifolds of positive dimension.

Theorem C of \cite{DangNull26} identifies the singularities of the Chern--Ricci flow at a finite time with the null locus when the limiting lower volume is positive, for arbitrary initial metrics in dimension two and for initial metrics of Guan--Li type in higher dimensions, that is, initial metrics $\omega_0$ with $\ddc\omega_0=0$ and $\mathrm{d}\omega_0\wedge\dc\omega_0=0$. Once the limiting background is big, \cite[Theorem 4.3]{DangNull26} gives convergence as currents and smooth local convergence away from its non-Hermitian locus. This conditional parabolic input should be distinguished from the implication from positive volume to bigness without further hypotheses, which fails by \cref{thm:actual-flow-positive-volume-nonbig}.

The theorem on the maximal existence time, \cref{thm:chern-ricci-maximal-time}, is Tosatti and Weinkove's \cite[Theorem~1.2 and~(1.4), pp.~126--127, and Section~4, pp.~138--142]{TW15}. The proof given here combines the positive affine path and time-weighted estimates for the potential with \cref{lem:hermitian-trace-inequality} and classical parabolic regularity. In the conversion to the book's conventions, potentials are multiplied by $2\pi$ and time is unchanged. The conditions for geometric smoothing in \cite[v1, Section~3.1, pp.~12--14]{DNGL26} include the further compatibility discussed in \cref{sec:history-completeness}.

Distance estimates for a twisted flow also depend on its parameters. In \cite[v2, Section~5.2, p.~18]{Dang26}, the twist is fixed to be $\operatorname{Ric}(\omega_X)$, for the reference metric $\omega_X$ of that paper, and Theorem~5.7 there, p.~21, allows the constants to depend on the endpoint of a finite time interval. The flat flows of \cref{ex:twisted-flow-distance-dependence} show why the formulation for arbitrary twists in Theorem~C of the introduction of that paper needs further control: a fixed initial density and a fixed lifetime do not bound the diameter independently of the twist, and a single positive twist can give unbounded diameter over an infinite lifetime.

The conjecture on balanced and pluriclosed metrics referred to in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} is stated by Fino and Vezzoni \cite[Conjecture 1.1]{FV26}; there the balanced and the pluriclosed metric are allowed to be different. That a compact manifold in Fujiki's class admitting a pluriclosed metric is K\"ahler is Chiose's \cite[Theorem~0.2, p.~3561]{Chiose14}.

The case of parallel torsion, \cref{thm:parallel-torsion-kahler}, is a theorem of Chen and Zheng \cite[v1, Theorem~1.2, pp.~2 and~8]{CZParallel26}. Their projection by integration of parallel forms and their description of the holonomy are \cite[v1, Lemmas~3.1--3.2 and Propositions~3.3--3.5, pp.~3--6]{CZParallel26}; \cref{lem:parallel-positive-projection} proves both descriptions again in a form that keeps track of positivity and of commutation with the differential. The slice of fixed trace and the maximum of the volume are \cite[v1, Propositions~4.2 and~4.5, Lemmas~4.3--4.4, pp.~7--8]{CZParallel26}. The inverse of the positive cofactor map proves the step of taking roots directly, and \cref{lem:balanced-pluriclosed-closed} proves, by linear algebra of primitive forms, that a Hermitian form which is both balanced and pluriclosed is closed. The additional obstruction of Zhao and Zheng for non-balanced metrics with Bismut-parallel torsion, used in their Corollary~1.3, is a separate ingredient.

The conjectured long-time existence and convergence of the pluriclosed flow on balanced manifolds with $c_1(X)=0$ is \cite[Conjecture 1.2]{FV26}. The discussion in \cref{sec:non-kahler-geometric-problems} distinguishes a conserved balanced trace from a pointwise bound for the metric.

The persistence of pluriclosed metrics under deformations, \cref{thm:pluriclosed-deformation-persistence}, which assumes that the Aeppli number $h_A^{1,1}$ is constant, is Cavalcanti's \cite[v5, Theorem~8.11, Section~8.3, pp.~41--42]{CavSKT13}. The fourth-order Aeppli Laplacian and the realization of Aeppli classes by harmonic forms used in \cref{lem:aeppli-smooth-projection} are due to Schweitzer \cite[v1, Section~2.c, pp.~9--10]{SchAeppli07}, who constructs the elliptic symbol in Section~2.b, pp.~7--8. The proof given here writes the projection onto $\partial\bar\partial$-closed forms explicitly and obtains its smooth dependence on the parameter from the resolvent and the constancy of the dimension of the kernel. It applies to any finite-dimensional smooth parameter space and keeps the prescribed initial metric. This is also the analytic input stated in \cite[v1, Theorem~2.1, p.~7]{BarDef26}; Barbaro's construction of deformations of Lie groups there requires a separate argument with transverse cohomology.
