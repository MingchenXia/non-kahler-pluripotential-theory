\section{Smooth equations on the ample locus}
\label{sec:ample-locus-smoothness}

We now turn to the metric geometry of solutions of Monge--Amp\`ere equations in big classes. In this section $X$ is a compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and $\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ is a big class with a smooth closed representative $\theta$. Its \emph{ample locus} is $\operatorname{Amp}(\alpha)=X\setminus E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha)$. The solution of the Monge--Amp\`ere equation with a smooth positive density has minimal singularities, and a K\"ahler current with analytic singularities provides a barrier that is smooth on the ample locus. The interior estimate of \cref{cor:big-lp-interior-smoothness} then gives smoothness there.

Let $\operatorname{vol}(\alpha)$ be the volume of $\alpha$, the non-pluripolar mass of a current with minimal singularities in $\alpha$, and let $\mathcal E(X,\theta)$ be the class of potentials $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ of \emph{full mass}, that is, with $\int_X\langle(\theta+\ddc\varphi)^n\rangle=\operatorname{vol}(\alpha)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:big-class-ample-smoothness}\index{Ample locus!smoothness}
Let $\mu$ be a smooth positive volume form with
$\mu(X)=\operatorname{vol}(\alpha)$. The normalized full-mass solution
$\varphi\in\mathcal E(X,\theta)$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-class-smooth-volume}
 \bigl\langle(\theta+\ddc\varphi)^n\bigr\rangle=\mu,
 \qquad \sup_X\varphi=0,
\end{equation}
is smooth on $\operatorname{Amp}(\alpha)$, and the restriction of
$\theta+\ddc\varphi$ to this locus is a K\"ahler form.
\end{theorem}

The existence and uniqueness of $\varphi$ are part of the classical theory of Monge--Amp\`ere equations in big classes, discussed in \cref{sec:history-regularity}; only the regularity assertion needs proof.

\begin{proof}
Fix a K\"ahler form $\omega$ and put $f=\mu/\omega^n$. Since $X$ is compact and $\mu$ is smooth, $f\in L^p(X,\omega^n)$ for every
finite $p>1$. The classical estimate for this equation, discussed in \cref{sec:history-regularity}, shows that $\varphi$ has minimal singularities: there is a constant $C>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ample-solution-minimal-bounds}
 V_\theta-C\leq\varphi\leq V_\theta.
\end{equation}
The upper bound follows from the normalization.

Let $T=\theta+\ddc\rho$ be a K\"ahler current with analytic singularities as in \cref{thm:bc-non-kahler-locus}. Its potential $\rho$ has analytic singularities with smooth remainder, and for some constant $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 T=\theta+\ddc\rho\geq\varepsilon\omega,
 \qquad \{\rho=-\infty\}=E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha).
\]
Thus $\rho$ is smooth on
$\Omega=\operatorname{Amp}(\alpha)$. After subtracting a constant we may assume
$\rho\leq0$, hence $\rho\leq V_\theta$, and
\eqref{eq:ample-solution-minimal-bounds} gives
\[
 \rho-C\leq\varphi\leq0.
\]
In particular $\varphi$ is locally bounded on $\Omega$, and by locality its non-pluripolar equation there is the equation for bounded potentials. The density $f$ belongs to $L^p$ on $X$ and is smooth and strictly positive on $\Omega$. Hence all hypotheses of \cref{cor:big-lp-interior-smoothness} hold for the strict barrier $\rho$, so $\varphi$ is smooth and $\theta+\ddc\varphi>0$ on $\Omega$. Since $\theta$ is closed, this form is K\"ahler.
\end{proof}

The proof uses that the density is smooth and positive on the entire regular locus of the barrier. The theorem says nothing about the distance from the ample locus to its complement: the metric $\theta+\ddc\varphi$ may reach the omitted set along paths of finite length. To determine that length, one must compare the normal and tangential directions of the metric. The following sections first examine zeros of the density; singular radial densities then separate boundedness of the potential from completeness of the metric.
