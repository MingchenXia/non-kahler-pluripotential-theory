\section{Pluriharmonic currents}
\label{sec:horizontal-pluriharmonic-currents}

A current is \emph{pluriharmonic} if its $\ddc$ vanishes. The normalized Gauduchon currents of \cref{sec:gauduchon-linear-duality} are positive pluriharmonic currents of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$, and positive pluriharmonic currents of other bidegrees arise when such tests are restricted, pushed forward or pulled back. This section proves four basic properties of positive pluriharmonic currents. Positivity and the second-order equation control their concentration on a smooth hypersurface. On compact K\"ahler manifolds, a closed test form bounds the mass of their pullbacks, and the heat equation smooths those with continuous coefficients. Finally, a positive pluriharmonic $(1,1)$-current with vanishing vertical trace descends along a fiber bundle whose fibers are entire lines or compact curves; this is used for elliptic fibrations in \cref{chap:bott-chern-loci}.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-hypersurface-current-support}\index{Positive current!hypersurface support}\index{Pluriharmonic current!hypersurface support}
Let $Z$ be a complex manifold of dimension $m\geq2$, and let $j:D\hookrightarrow Z$ be a smooth closed hypersurface. If a positive $(q,q)$-current $R$, $1\leq q\leq m$, is supported on $D$ and $\ddc R$ has order zero, then there is a unique positive $(q-1,q-1)$-current $S$ on $D$ such that $R=j_*S$. If $\ddc R=0$, then $\ddc S=0$. In particular, if $q=1$, $D$ is compact and connected, and $\ddc R=0$, then $R=c[D]$ for some $c\geq0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use local coordinates $(z,w_1,\ldots,w_{m-1})$ with $D=\{z=0\}$. Both $R$ and $\ddc R$ have measure coefficients supported on $D$. Suppose first that $q<m$. For every compactly supported smooth form $\eta$ of bidegree $(m-q-1,m-q-1)$,
\[
 0=\langle\ddc R,|z|^2\eta\rangle
  =\langle R,\ddc(|z|^2\eta)\rangle
  =\langle R,\ddc|z|^2\wedge\eta\rangle,
\]
where the first equality holds because $\ddc R$ is a measure supported on $D$, on which $|z|^2$ vanishes, and the last one because the terms containing $|z|^2$ or its first derivatives vanish on $D$. Thus $R\wedge\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z=0$, which removes the coefficients of $R$ containing neither normal differential.

Fix a constant positive Hermitian form $\tau$ in the $w$ variables. The current $M_\tau=R\wedge\tau^{m-q-1}$ is positive of bidegree $(m-1,m-1)$, and
$M_\tau\wedge\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z=0$.
Its Hermitian matrix of measure densities therefore has zero diagonal entry in the normal direction, and positivity removes the corresponding row and column, so
\[
 M_\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}z=0,
 \qquad M_\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z=0.
\]
Put $s=m-q-1$. The powers $\tau^s$, as $\tau$ varies over the positive Hermitian forms, span the real horizontal $(s,s)$-forms. Indeed, a linear functional vanishing on these powers vanishes on a polynomial over an open cone; polarization makes it vanish on products of arbitrary real $(1,1)$-forms, and these products span the forms of that bidegree. Their products with a horizontal one-form span the complementary test forms for the mixed coefficients of $R$. The identities above therefore remove every coefficient containing exactly one normal differential. For $q=m$, every top-degree coefficient already contains both normal differentials, and this reduction is unnecessary.

The remaining coefficients are measures on $\{z=0\}$ multiplying both normal differentials. They therefore define a unique current $S$ on $D$ with $R=j_*S$. Testing against extensions of strongly positive forms on $D$ proves positivity of $S$, and uniqueness makes these local currents agree on overlaps. Pushforward commutes with $\ddc$ and is injective for a closed embedding, so $\ddc R=0$ implies $\ddc S=0$.

When $q=1$, the current $S$ is a nonnegative distribution on $D$. The equation $\ddc S=0$ and Weyl's lemma make it a smooth nonnegative pluriharmonic function. If $D$ is compact and connected, the maximum principle makes it a constant $c\geq0$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For smooth pluriharmonic forms on compact K\"ahler manifolds, the mass of the pullback under a fixed holomorphic map is controlled by pairing with a fixed closed form. The test object is the pushforward of a power of the K\"ahler form on the source, which is closed; the form being pulled back need only be $\ddc$-closed.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-pluriharmonic-pullback-mass}\index{Pluriharmonic current!pullback mass}
Let $F:Y\to X$ be a holomorphic map between compact K\"ahler manifolds of dimensions $N\geq n$, with K\"ahler forms $\Omega$ and $\omega$. For $1\leq p\leq n$, there is a constant $C$ such that every smooth positive $(p,p)$-form $S$ on $X$ with $\ddc S=0$ satisfies
\[
 \int_Y F^*S\wedge\Omega^{N-p}
 \leq C\int_X S\wedge\omega^{n-p}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The positive closed current $\Gamma=F_*(\Omega^{N-p})$ has bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$ on $X$. The classical $\ddc$-lemma for currents on compact K\"ahler manifolds gives a smooth real closed form $\gamma$ of this bidegree and a current $U$ with $\Gamma=\gamma+\ddc U$. For $p=n$, a closed current of degree zero is already a smooth locally constant function, and one may take $U=0$. Since $S$ is smooth, pairing it with $U$ is legitimate, and
\[
 \int_Y F^*S\wedge\Omega^{N-p}
 =\langle\Gamma,S\rangle
 =\int_X\gamma\wedge S+\langle U,\ddc S\rangle
 =\int_X\gamma\wedge S.
\]
The pairing with the fixed smooth form $\gamma$ is bounded in absolute value by a constant times the trace mass $\int_XS\wedge\omega^{n-p}$ of the positive form $S$. This proves the estimate.
\end{proof}

For continuous coefficients on a compact K\"ahler manifold, the Hodge heat flow smooths a positive form while preserving the pluriharmonic equation, and a small multiple of a power of the K\"ahler form restores positivity.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:continuous-pluriharmonic-smoothing}\index{Pluriharmonic current!continuous-coefficient smoothing}
Let $(X,\omega)$ be a compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n$, and let $1\leq p\leq n$. Every continuous positive real $(p,p)$-form $R$ satisfying $\ddc R=0$ in the sense of currents is a uniform limit of smooth positive real $(p,p)$-forms $R_j$ with $\ddc R_j=0$. If $R$ is closed, the approximants can also be chosen closed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Delta$ be the real Hodge Laplacian of the K\"ahler metric. Its heat operators $H_t=\mathrm e^{-t\Delta}$ are smoothing for $t>0$. The K\"ahler identities give $\Delta=2\Delta_\partial=2\Delta_{\bar\partial}$, so $H_t$ preserves bidegree, reality and the equation $\ddc R=0$.

We recall why $H_tR\to R$ uniformly for a continuous form $R$. The Weitzenb\"ock formula writes $\Delta=\nabla^*\nabla+A$, with a bounded curvature term $A$ of order zero. For smooth initial data $\beta$, the heat equation and the maximum principle give
\[
 \|H_t\beta\|_{C^0}\leq\mathrm e^{Ct}\|\beta\|_{C^0}
 \qquad(0<t\leq1),
\]
where $C$ depends only on the fixed metric and the degree. Indeed, the scalar equation for $|H_t\beta|^2$ has a nonpositive gradient term and a curvature term bounded above by $2C|H_t\beta|^2$. The classical smooth heat solution converges uniformly to its smooth initial data. Approximating a continuous form by smooth forms and using this bound proves uniform convergence for continuous initial data. The resulting heat solution is smooth for $t>0$, and since the commutation with $\ddc$ extends to currents, $\ddc H_tR=0$.

Set $\epsilon_t=\|H_tR-R\|_{C^0}$, which tends to zero. A fixed constant $c>0$ satisfies
\[
 E+c\|E\|_{C^0}\omega^p\geq0
\]
for every continuous real $(p,p)$-form $E$; to see this, evaluate on decomposable unit $p$-vectors, on which $\omega^p$ has a uniformly positive value. Consequently
\[
 R_t=H_tR+c\epsilon_t\omega^p
\]
is smooth and positive. Since $\omega$ is K\"ahler, $\ddc\omega^p=0$, and hence $\ddc R_t=0$. Moreover, $R_t\to R$ uniformly. The Hodge heat flow also commutes with $\mathrm{d}$, and $\omega^p$ is closed, so $\mathrm{d}R=0$ implies $\mathrm{d}R_t=0$. Choosing any sequence $t_j\downarrow0$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

We next consider a positive $(1,1)$-current with vanishing vertical trace on the total space of a fiber bundle. Positivity removes its vertical and mixed coefficients. For a closed current the remaining coefficients are constant along the fibers. For a pluriharmonic current they are only harmonic along the fibers, but positivity gives the same conclusion when the fiber is the whole complex line or a compact connected curve.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:horizontal-pluriharmonic-descent}\index{Positive current!horizontal descent}\index{Pluriharmonic current!horizontal descent}
Let $Y$ be a complex manifold of dimension $\ell\geq1$, let $\eta$ be a Hermitian form on $Y$, and let $\pi:Z\to Y$ be a holomorphic fiber bundle whose fiber is either $\mathbb C$ or a compact connected Riemann surface. If $S$ is a positive real $(1,1)$-current on $Z$ with $\ddc S=0$ and $S\wedge\pi^*\eta^\ell=0$, then there is a unique positive $\ddc$-closed $(1,1)$-current $R$ on $Y$ such that $S=\pi^*R$. If $S$ is closed, then $R$ is closed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a local trivialization $\pi^{-1}(U)=U\times F$ of the bundle, with base coordinates $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_\ell)$ and a local fiber coordinate $z$. The coefficient measures of $S$ have a positive Hermitian matrix of densities relative to their trace measure. The identity $S\wedge\pi^*\eta^\ell=0$ makes the vertical diagonal entry zero, and the inequality $|s_{\alpha\bar z}|^2\leq s_{\alpha\bar\alpha}s_{z\bar z}$ then removes all mixed entries. Thus
\[
 S=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^\ell s_{\alpha\bar\beta}(w,z)\,\mathrm{d}w_\alpha\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar w_\beta,
\]
with distribution coefficients. In the equation $\partial\bar\partial S=0$, the coefficient containing both vertical differentials and the horizontal pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ gives $\partial_z\partial_{\bar z}s_{\alpha\bar\beta}=0$.

Fix $v\in\mathbb C^\ell$ and a nonnegative smooth function $\xi$ with compact support in $U$. Pairing the positive coefficient distribution $\sum_{\alpha,\beta}s_{\alpha\bar\beta}v_\alpha\bar v_\beta$, in the base variables, with $\xi$ times the Euclidean volume form defines a nonnegative harmonic distribution on the whole fiber $F$. Its definition does not depend on the fiber coordinate, since the remaining differentials are horizontal. Weyl's lemma makes it a nonnegative harmonic function. Such a function is constant on $\mathbb C$ by the Liouville theorem for harmonic functions, and on a compact connected curve by the maximum principle.

Polarization in $v$ and linearity in the base test function show that every coefficient $s_{\alpha\bar\beta}$ is the pullback of a distribution $r_{\alpha\bar\beta}$ on $U$. To identify this distribution, pair against any nonnegative smooth fiber density of total integral one; this also proves positivity of the resulting current $R_U$ on $U$ and injectivity of the pullback. Consequently $\ddc S=\pi^*(\ddc R_U)=0$ implies $\ddc R_U=0$. On overlaps, injectivity makes the currents $R_U$ agree, so they glue to a current $R$, and injectivity also proves uniqueness. If $\mathrm{d}S=0$, the same argument gives $\mathrm{d}R=0$.
\end{proof}

The assumption that the fibers are entire lines or compact curves is essential. On the bidisk $\Delta_z\times\Delta_w$, the current
\[
 S=(2+\operatorname{Re}z)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{d}w\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar w
\]
is positive and $\ddc$-closed, and its vertical trace vanishes. Its horizontal coefficient depends on $z$, so it does not descend under the projection to $\Delta_w$.
