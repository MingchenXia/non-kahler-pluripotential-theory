\section{Pluriharmonic currents}
\label{sec:horizontal-pluriharmonic-currents}

A current is \emph{pluriharmonic} if its $\ddc$ vanishes. Positivity and this second-order equation control its concentration on a smooth hypersurface. More generally, the same support conclusion holds when its $\ddc$ merely has measure coefficients.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-hypersurface-current-support}\index{Positive current!hypersurface support}\index{Pluriharmonic current!hypersurface support}
Let $Z$ be a complex manifold of dimension $m\geq2$, and let $j:D\hookrightarrow Z$ be a smooth closed hypersurface. If a positive $(q,q)$-current $R$, $1\leq q\leq m$, is supported on $D$ and $\ddc R$ has order zero, then there is a unique positive $(q-1,q-1)$-current $S$ on $D$ such that $R=j_*S$. If $\ddc R=0$, then $\ddc S=0$. In particular, if $q=1$, $D$ is compact and connected, and $\ddc R=0$, then $R=c[D]$ for some $c\geq0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use local coordinates $(z,w_1,\ldots,w_{m-1})$ with $D=\{z=0\}$. Both $R$ and $\ddc R$ have measure coefficients supported there. Suppose first that $q<m$. For every compactly supported smooth form $\eta$ of bidegree $(m-q-1,m-q-1)$, we have
\[
 0=\langle\ddc R,|z|^2\eta\rangle
  =\langle R,\ddc(|z|^2\eta)\rangle
  =\langle R,\ddc|z|^2\wedge\eta\rangle.
\]
The terms containing $|z|^2$ or its first derivatives vanish on $D$. Thus $R\wedge\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z=0$, which removes the coefficients containing neither normal differential.

Fix a constant positive Hermitian form $\tau$ in the $w$ variables. The current $M_\tau=R\wedge\tau^{m-q-1}$ is positive of bidegree $(m-1,m-1)$, and
$M_\tau\wedge\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z=0$.
Its Hermitian matrix of measure densities therefore has zero normal diagonal entry. Positivity removes the corresponding row and column, so
\[
 M_\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}z=0,
 \qquad M_\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z=0.
\]
Put $r=m-q-1$. The powers $\tau^r$, as $\tau$ varies over the positive Hermitian forms, span the real horizontal $(r,r)$-forms. Indeed, a linear functional vanishing on these powers vanishes on a polynomial over an open cone. Polarization makes it vanish on products of arbitrary real $(1,1)$-forms, and these products span the indicated bidegree. Their products with a horizontal one-form span the complementary test forms for the mixed coefficients of $R$. The displayed identities consequently remove every coefficient containing just one normal differential. For $q=m$, every top-degree coefficient already contains both normal differentials, so this reduction is unnecessary.

The remaining coefficients are measures on $\{z=0\}$ multiplying both normal differentials. They therefore define a unique current $S$ on $D$ with $R=j_*S$. Testing against extensions of strongly positive forms on $D$ proves positivity of $S$. Uniqueness makes these local currents agree on overlaps. Pushforward commutes with $\ddc$ and is injective for a closed embedding, so $\ddc R=0$ implies $\ddc S=0$.

When $q=1$, $S$ is a nonnegative distribution on $D$. The equation $\ddc S=0$ and Weyl's lemma make it a smooth nonnegative pluriharmonic function. If $D$ is compact and connected, the maximum principle makes it a constant $c\geq0$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For smooth pluriharmonic forms, K\"ahler geometry also bounds the mass of pullback under a fixed holomorphic map. The closed form supplied by the map is the test object; the form being pulled back need only be $\ddc$-closed.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smooth-pluriharmonic-pullback-mass}\index{Pluriharmonic current!pullback mass}
Let $F:Y\to X$ be a holomorphic map between compact K\"ahler manifolds of dimensions $N\geq n$, with K\"ahler forms $\Omega$ and $\omega$. For $1\leq p\leq n$ there is a constant $C$ such that every smooth positive $(p,p)$-form $S$ on $X$ satisfying $\ddc S=0$ obeys
\[
 \int_Y F^*S\wedge\Omega^{N-p}
 \leq C\int_X S\wedge\omega^{n-p}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The positive closed current $\Gamma=F_*(\Omega^{N-p})$ has bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$ on $X$. The classical K\"ahler $\ddc$ lemma for currents gives a smooth real closed form $\gamma$ of this bidegree and a distributional form $U$ with $\Gamma=\gamma+\ddc U$. For $p=n$, a closed degree-zero current is already smooth and locally constant, and one may take $U=0$. Since $S$ is smooth, pairing it with $U$ is legitimate and
\[
 \int_Y F^*S\wedge\Omega^{N-p}
 =\langle\Gamma,S\rangle
 =\int_X\gamma\wedge S+\langle U,\ddc S\rangle
 =\int_X\gamma\wedge S.
\]
The pairing with the fixed smooth form $\gamma$ is bounded in absolute value by a constant times the positive-current trace mass of $S$. This proves the estimate.
\end{proof}

For continuous coefficients on a compact K\"ahler manifold, the Hodge heat equation can be corrected by a vanishing positive form. The correction preserves the pluriharmonic equation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:continuous-pluriharmonic-smoothing}\index{Pluriharmonic current!continuous-coefficient smoothing}
Let $(X,\omega)$ be a compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n$, and let $1\leq p\leq n$. Every continuous positive real $(p,p)$-form $R$ satisfying $\ddc R=0$ in the sense of currents is a uniform limit of smooth positive real $(p,p)$-forms $R_j$ with $\ddc R_j=0$. If $R$ is closed, the approximants can also be chosen closed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Delta$ be the real Hodge Laplacian of the K\"ahler metric. Its heat operators $H_t=\mathrm e^{-t\Delta}$ are smoothing for $t>0$. The K\"ahler identities give $\Delta=2\Delta_\partial=2\Delta_{\bar\partial}$, so $H_t$ preserves bidegree, reality and the equation $\ddc R=0$.

We recall why $H_tR\to R$ uniformly for a continuous form. The Weitzenb\"ock formula writes $\Delta=\nabla^*\nabla+A$, with a bounded zeroth-order curvature term. For smooth initial data, the heat equation and the maximum principle give
\[
 \|H_t\alpha\|_{C^0}\leq\mathrm e^{Ct}\|\alpha\|_{C^0}
 \qquad(0<t\leq1),
\]
where $C$ depends only on the fixed metric and degree. Indeed, the scalar equation for $|H_t\alpha|^2$ has a nonpositive gradient term and a curvature term bounded above by $2C|H_t\alpha|^2$. The classical smooth heat solution tends to its smooth initial data uniformly. Approximating a continuous form by smooth forms and using the displayed bound proves uniform convergence for continuous initial data. The resulting heat solution is smooth for $t>0$. Commutation with $\ddc$ extends to currents, so $\ddc H_tR=0$.

Set $\epsilon_t=\|H_tR-R\|_{C^0}$, which tends to zero. A fixed constant $c>0$ satisfies
\[
 E+c\|E\|_{C^0}\omega^p\geq0
\]
for every continuous real $(p,p)$-form $E$: evaluate on unit decomposable $p$-vectors, on which $\omega^p$ has a uniformly positive value. Consequently
\[
 R_t=H_tR+c\epsilon_t\omega^p
\]
is smooth and positive. Since $\omega$ is K\"ahler, $\ddc\omega^p=0$, and hence $\ddc R_t=0$. Finally, $R_t\to R$ uniformly. The Hodge heat flow also commutes with $\mathrm{d}$, and $\omega^p$ is closed. Thus $\mathrm{d}R=0$ implies $\mathrm{d}R_t=0$. Choosing any $t_j\downarrow0$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

We next consider a positive $(1,1)$-current with vanishing vertical trace. Positivity removes its vertical and mixed coefficients. For a closed current the remaining coefficients are constant along the fibers. For a pluriharmonic current they are harmonic; positivity gives the same descent when the fiber is the whole complex line or a compact connected curve.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:horizontal-pluriharmonic-descent}\index{Positive current!horizontal descent}\index{Pluriharmonic current!horizontal descent}
Let $Y$ be a complex manifold of dimension $\ell\geq1$, let $\eta$ be a Hermitian form on $Y$, and let $\pi:Z\to Y$ be a holomorphic fiber bundle with fiber either $\mathbb C$ or a compact connected Riemann surface. If $S$ is a positive real $(1,1)$-current on $Z$ with $\ddc S=0$ and $S\wedge\pi^*\eta^\ell=0$, then there is a unique positive $\ddc$-closed $(1,1)$-current $R$ on $Y$ such that $S=\pi^*R$. If $S$ is closed, then $R$ is closed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a bundle trivialization $\pi^{-1}(U)=U\times F$, with base coordinates $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_\ell)$ and a local fiber coordinate $z$. The coefficient measures of $S$ have a positive Hermitian matrix of densities relative to their trace measure. The identity $S\wedge\pi^*\eta^\ell=0$ makes the vertical diagonal entry zero. The matrix inequality $|s_{\alpha\bar z}|^2\leq s_{\alpha\bar\alpha}s_{z\bar z}$ then removes all mixed entries. Thus
\[
 S=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^\ell s_{\alpha\bar\beta}(w,z)\,\mathrm{d}w_\alpha\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar w_\beta,
\]
with distribution coefficients. In $\partial\bar\partial S=0$, the coefficient containing both vertical differentials and the horizontal pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ gives $\partial_z\partial_{\bar z}s_{\alpha\bar\beta}=0$.

Fix $v\in\mathbb C^\ell$ and a nonnegative smooth compactly supported function $\xi$ on $U$. Pair the positive coefficient distribution $\sum_{\alpha,\beta}s_{\alpha\bar\beta}v_\alpha\bar v_\beta$ in the base variables against $\xi$ times the Euclidean volume form. This defines a nonnegative harmonic distribution on the whole fiber $F$. Its definition is independent of the fiber coordinate, since the remaining differentials are horizontal. Weyl's lemma makes it a nonnegative harmonic function. Such a function is constant on $\mathbb C$ by the harmonic Liouville theorem, and on a compact connected curve by the maximum principle.

Polarization in $v$ and linearity in the base test function show that every coefficient $s_{\alpha\bar\beta}$ is pulled back from a distribution $r_{\alpha\bar\beta}$ on $U$. To identify this distribution, pair against any nonnegative smooth fiber density of total integral one. This also proves positivity of the resulting current $R_U$ and injectivity of pullback. Consequently $\ddc S=\pi^*(\ddc R_U)=0$ implies $\ddc R_U=0$. On overlaps, injectivity makes the currents $R_U$ agree; they glue to $R$, and injectivity proves uniqueness. If $\mathrm{d}S=0$, the same argument gives $\mathrm{d}R=0$.
\end{proof}

The whole-fiber condition is essential. On the bidisc $\Delta_z\times\Delta_w$, the current
\[
 S=(2+\operatorname{Re}z)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{d}w\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar w
\]
is positive and $\ddc$-closed, and its vertical trace vanishes. Its horizontal coefficient depends on $z$, so it does not descend under projection to $\Delta_w$.
