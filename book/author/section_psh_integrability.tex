\section{Uniform exponential integrability}

For a single quasi-plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $X$, Skoda's local integrability theorem, recalled in \cite{SGPT}, applies after local smooth corrections. Taking the smallest exponent over a finite cover gives $\mathrm{e}^{-a(v)v}\in L^1(X)$ for some $a(v)>0$. Normalized compactness makes the exponent and the bound for the integral uniform: otherwise, a convergent weighted sum of counterexamples would violate this qualitative statement.

Let $K_\omega$ be the constant of \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} for the background $\omega$, so that every $v\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with $\sup_Xv=0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-lone}
 \int_X|v|\,\mathrm{d}V\leq K_\omega.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:uniform-exponential}\index{Exponential integrability}
There are constants $\alpha>0$ and $C<+\infty$, depending only on $(X,\omega,\mathrm{d}V)$, such that
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha v}\,\mathrm{d}V\leq C
 \quad\hbox{for all }v\in\PSH(X,\omega)\hbox{ with }\sup_Xv=0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that no such pair exists. Set $a_j=2^{-j}$ and choose normalized potentials $v_j\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ with
\[
 \int_X\exp\!\left(-\frac{a_j}{j}v_j\right)\mathrm{d}V\geq2^j.
\]
The partial sums $V_N=\sum_{j\leq N}a_jv_j$ decrease, since every $v_j\leq0$. They belong to $\PSH(X,\omega)$ by \cref{prop:convexity}, because $\sum_ja_j\leq1$, and their $L^1$ norms are uniformly bounded by \eqref{eq:normalized-lone}. By \cref{cor:decreasing-limit}, their limit $V$ is therefore $\omega$-plurisubharmonic. Since all summands are nonpositive, $V\leq a_jv_j$ for every $j$.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Fix $p>0$. For every $j\geq1/p$,
\[
 \int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pV}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-pa_jv_j}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq\int_X\mathrm{e}^{-(a_j/j)v_j}\,\mathrm{d}V
 \geq2^j.
\]
Thus $\mathrm{e}^{-pV}$ is not integrable for any $p>0$, which contradicts the integrability of $\mathrm{e}^{-a(V)V}$ obtained above from Skoda's theorem.
\end{proof}
