\subsection{Limits of the minimizing Gauduchon forms}
\label{sec:elliptic-gauduchon-limits}

The Gauduchon metrics that minimize the quotient defining the volume in \eqref{eq:elliptic-boundary-volume} can be identified explicitly: they are the equation metrics themselves. Their normalized squares converge smoothly at the nef boundary, although the normalized metrics may diverge. Recall from \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume} that the root volume $\lambda_{\mu_0}$, with $\lambda_{\mu_0}^3=\mathcal V_{\mu_0}$, is the infimum over Gauduchon metrics $\gamma$ of $\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^2$ divided by the denominator $D_{\mu_0}(\gamma)=\int_X(\mu_0/\gamma^3)^{1/3}\gamma^3$. For positive functions of $\epsilon$, we write $f\asymp g$ if $f/g$ is bounded above and below by positive constants as $\epsilon\downarrow0$.

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:elliptic-gauduchon-limits}\index{Elliptic fibration!limiting Gauduchon tests}
Let $a,b\geq0$, $\theta=a\alpha+b\beta$ and $\Omega_\epsilon=\theta+\epsilon\omega$. For every $\epsilon>0$, the metric $\Omega_\epsilon$ is Gauduchon, and it minimizes the quotient defining $\lambda_{\mu_0}(\Omega_\epsilon)$. Put
\[
 I_\epsilon=8\pi\bigl(ab+2(a+b)\epsilon+3\epsilon^2\bigr),
 \qquad T_\epsilon=\frac{\Omega_\epsilon^2}{I_\epsilon},
 \qquad \gamma_\epsilon=\frac{\Omega_\epsilon}{\sqrt{I_\epsilon}}.
\]
Then $\int_X\omega\wedge T_\epsilon=1$, $\ddc T_\epsilon=0$ and $T_\epsilon=\gamma_\epsilon^2$. As $\epsilon\downarrow0$, the forms $T_\epsilon$ converge smoothly to
\[
 T_0=\begin{cases}
       (4\pi)^{-1}\alpha\wedge\beta,&a,b>0,\\
       (8\pi)^{-1}\alpha\wedge(\beta+D),&a>0,\ b=0,\\
       (8\pi)^{-1}\beta\wedge(\alpha+D),&a=0,\ b>0,\\
       (24\pi)^{-1}\omega^2,&a=b=0.
      \end{cases}
\]
In every case $\theta\wedge T_0=0$. If $q$ is the number of positive coefficients among $a,b$, the denominator of the normalized minimizing metric satisfies
\[
 D_{\mu_0}(\gamma_\epsilon)
  =\frac{\bigl(24\pi\epsilon(a+\epsilon)(b+\epsilon)\bigr)^{2/3}}
         {I_\epsilon}
  \asymp\epsilon^{q/3},
\]
where the two comparison constants may depend on the fixed pair $(a,b)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $x=a+\epsilon$, $y=b+\epsilon$ and $z=\epsilon$. Since $\alpha^2=\beta^2=D^2=0$,
\[
 \Omega_\epsilon^2
   =2xy\alpha\wedge\beta+2xz\alpha\wedge D+2yz\beta\wedge D.
\]
The forms $\alpha,\beta$ are closed and $\ddc D=2\alpha\wedge\beta$. Hence the first term is closed, and $\ddc$ of each of the last two terms contains $\alpha^2$ or $\beta^2$ and therefore vanishes. Thus $\ddc(\Omega_\epsilon^2)=0$. Since the zero potential solves the normalized equation for $\Omega_\epsilon$ and $\mu_0$, the characterization of minimizing metrics in \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula} shows that $\Omega_\epsilon$ is a minimizer.

Integration along the fibers gives $\int_X\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge D=4\pi$. Hence
\[
 \int_X\omega\wedge\Omega_\epsilon^2
   =8\pi(xy+xz+yz)=I_\epsilon,
\]
which is the trace normalization. Dividing the three coefficients in the wedge expansion of $\Omega_\epsilon^2$ by $I_\epsilon$ gives each of the stated limits; the convergence holds in every smooth norm, because the forms $\alpha\wedge\beta$, $\alpha\wedge D$ and $\beta\wedge D$ are fixed. The identities $\alpha^2=\beta^2=0$ then give $\theta\wedge T_0=0$ in all four cases.

Set $c_\epsilon=24\pi\epsilon(a+\epsilon)(b+\epsilon)$. The equation for $\Omega_\epsilon$ gives
\[
 \gamma_\epsilon^3
    =\frac{c_\epsilon}{I_\epsilon^{3/2}}\mu_0.
\]
Substituting this in the definition of $D_{\mu_0}$ and using $\int_X\mu_0=1$ yields $D_{\mu_0}(\gamma_\epsilon)=c_\epsilon^{2/3}/I_\epsilon$. For fixed $(a,b)$ one has $c_\epsilon\asymp\epsilon^{3-q}$ and $I_\epsilon\asymp\epsilon^{2-q}$, so the quotient is of order $\epsilon^{q/3}$.
\end{proof}

In a coframe adapted to the three rank-one forms $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $D$, the three complementary products in the proof show that the limiting positive $(2,2)$-forms have coefficient matrices of rank $1$, $2$ and $3$ when $q=2$, $1$ and $0$, respectively. If $a,b>0$, the normalized metrics themselves converge smoothly to
\[
 \frac{a\alpha+b\beta}{\sqrt{8\pi ab}}.
\]
If $a>0$ and $b=0$, however, the coefficient of $\alpha$ in $\gamma_\epsilon$ grows like a positive constant times $\epsilon^{-1/2}$, while its other two coefficients tend to zero. Thus the metrics $\gamma_\epsilon$ are unbounded relative to the fixed metric $\omega$, although their squares converge smoothly; the same holds with $\alpha$ and $\beta$ interchanged. This divergence is unavoidable, since a bounded subsequence of the $\gamma_\epsilon$ would converge to a semipositive $(1,1)$-form whose square is the rank-two form $T_0$. In dimension three, a positive $(2,2)$-form of rank two is not the square of a semipositive $(1,1)$-form: the coefficients of such a square are the pairwise products of the eigenvalues of the $(1,1)$-form, and the number of nonzero products is three, one or zero. At the vertex $a=b=0$, the normalized metric instead stays equal to $\omega/\sqrt{24\pi}$.

For a nonzero boundary class, the smooth limit $T_0$ annihilates $\theta$, and the denominators $D_{\mu_0}(\gamma_\epsilon)$ tend to zero; no concentration of the coefficient measures occurs in this family. This degeneration of the minimizing tests for a fixed density differs from the sequence of Gauduchon forms used in \cref{lem:lower-mass-unbounded-root} to prove that the lower mass vanishes. At the vertex, the denominator stays positive, and the order three comes entirely from the scaling $\Omega_\epsilon=\epsilon\omega$ of the background.
