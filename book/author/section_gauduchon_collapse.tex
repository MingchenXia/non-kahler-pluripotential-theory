\subsection{Collapse of normalized Gauduchon tests}
\label{sec:gauduchon-collapse}

The root volume and the bigness threshold divide the same pairing by different quantities. Positive volume can fail to force bigness only if these denominators separate along tests approaching the threshold. The following calculation isolates that degeneration. The radial background of \cref{thm:radial-positive-volume-nonbig} realizes this separation. Its actual balanced tests converge to the degenerate fiber by \cref{prop:radial-localized-balanced-tests}; \cref{prop:radial-volume-test-limits} explains when the volume-minimizing metrics also approach the bigness threshold.

Let $\theta$ be a smooth pseudoeffective real $(1,1)$-form. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, abbreviate the pairings by
\[
 A_\theta(\gamma)=\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1},
 \qquad A_h(\gamma)=\int_Xh\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\]
The threshold $\kappa_h(\theta)$ of \cref{cor:gauduchon-threshold} uses the quotient $A_\theta/A_h$, whereas $\lambda_\mu(\theta)$ uses $A_\theta/D_\mu$ with the determinant denominator of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume}.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-positive-volume-collapse}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!collapse}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose $n\geq2$, $\lambda_\mu(\theta)>0$, and $\theta$ is not big. Every sequence of Gauduchon metrics satisfying
\[
 A_h(\gamma_j)=1,\qquad A_\theta(\gamma_j)\longrightarrow0
\]
then satisfies $D_\mu(\gamma_j)\to0$. Such sequences exist.
\item More generally, fix a real number $\delta>0$. If a sequence with $A_h(\gamma_j)=1$ satisfies
\[
 A_\theta(\gamma_j)\longrightarrow\kappa_h(\theta),
 \qquad \liminf_jD_\mu(\gamma_j)\geq\delta>0,
\]
then
\[
 \kappa_h(\theta)\geq\delta\lambda_\mu(\theta).
\]
In particular, positive root volume then implies bigness.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\theta$ is not big, pseudoeffectivity and the threshold criterion give $\kappa_h(\theta)=0$. Choose test metrics whose ratios approach zero. For $n\geq2$, rescaling makes $A_h=1$ without changing either quotient. The definition of the root volume gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-collapse-basic}
 \lambda_\mu(\theta)D_\mu(\gamma)\leq A_\theta(\gamma).
\end{equation}
Dividing by the positive root volume proves the first assertion. Applying the same inequality to the sequence in the second assertion and taking lower limits gives $\delta\lambda_\mu(\theta)\leq\kappa_h(\theta)$. If the root volume is positive, the threshold criterion gives bigness.
\end{proof}

There is always a uniform upper bound for $D_\mu/A_h$. Indeed, the smooth equation for $h$, or \eqref{eq:gaud-collapse-basic} with $\theta=h$, gives
\[
 D_\mu(\gamma)\leq\lambda_\mu(h)^{-1}A_h(\gamma).
\]
The missing estimate in the converse direction is a lower bound along suitable threshold-minimizing sequences. Compactness of normalized potentials does not provide compactness of these Gauduchon metrics.

In dimension one there is no such normalization issue. The root volume is $\int_X\theta$, and $\kappa_h(\theta)=\int_X\theta/\int_Xh$. Positive volume and bigness are therefore equivalent by the same threshold criterion.
