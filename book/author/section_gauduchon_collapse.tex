\subsection{Collapse of normalized Gauduchon tests}
\label{sec:gauduchon-collapse}

The root volume and the bigness threshold divide the same pairing by different quantities. Positive volume can fail to imply bigness only if these denominators separate along tests approaching the threshold, and the following calculation isolates this degeneration. The radial background of \cref{thm:radial-positive-volume-nonbig} realizes the separation: its balanced test metrics (\cref{def:balanced-pluriclosed}), converge to the degenerate fiber by \cref{prop:radial-localized-balanced-tests}, and \cref{prop:radial-volume-test-limits} explains when the metrics minimizing the volume quotient also approach the bigness threshold.

Fix a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth pseudoeffective real $(1,1)$-form. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, abbreviate the pairings by
\[
 A_\theta(\gamma)=\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1},
 \qquad A_h(\gamma)=\int_Xh\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\]
The threshold $\kappa_h(\theta)$ of \cref{cor:gauduchon-threshold} is the infimum of the quotient $A_\theta/A_h$ over Gauduchon metrics, and it is positive exactly when $\theta$ is big. The root volume $\lambda_\mu(\theta)$ is the infimum of $A_\theta/D_\mu$, with the determinant denominator $D_\mu(\gamma)$ of \cref{def:gaud-variational-volume}.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-positive-volume-collapse}\index{Gauduchon variational volume!collapse}
Let $h$, $\theta$ and $\mu$ be as above, and let $(\gamma_j)$ be a sequence of Gauduchon metrics.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose $n\geq2$, $\lambda_\mu(\theta)>0$, and $\theta$ is not big. If
\[
 A_h(\gamma_j)=1,\qquad A_\theta(\gamma_j)\longrightarrow0,
\]
then $D_\mu(\gamma_j)\to0$. Such sequences exist.
\item More generally, fix a real number $\delta>0$. If $A_h(\gamma_j)=1$ and
\[
 A_\theta(\gamma_j)\longrightarrow\kappa_h(\theta),
 \qquad \liminf_jD_\mu(\gamma_j)\geq\delta>0,
\]
then
\[
 \kappa_h(\theta)\geq\delta\lambda_\mu(\theta).
\]
In particular, positive root volume then implies bigness.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\theta$ is not big. Since $\theta$ is pseudoeffective, the threshold criterion of \cref{cor:gauduchon-threshold} gives $\kappa_h(\theta)=0$, so there are Gauduchon metrics whose quotients $A_\theta/A_h$ tend to zero. Multiplying a Gauduchon metric by a positive constant multiplies $A_\theta$, $A_h$ and $D_\mu$ by the same factor; for $n\geq2$ this factor can be chosen to make $A_h=1$, without changing either quotient. This gives the sequences in the first assertion. By the definition of the root volume, every Gauduchon metric satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-collapse-basic}
 \lambda_\mu(\theta)D_\mu(\gamma)\leq A_\theta(\gamma).
\end{equation}
Dividing by the positive root volume proves the first assertion. Applying the same inequality to the sequence in the second assertion and taking lower limits gives $\delta\lambda_\mu(\theta)\leq\kappa_h(\theta)$. If the root volume is positive, then $\kappa_h(\theta)>0$, and the threshold criterion gives bigness.
\end{proof}

The quotient $D_\mu/A_h$ is always bounded above. Indeed, the smooth equation for $h$, or \eqref{eq:gaud-collapse-basic} with $\theta=h$, gives
\[
 D_\mu(\gamma)\leq\lambda_\mu(h)^{-1}A_h(\gamma).
\]
What is missing for the converse direction is a lower bound for $D_\mu$ along suitable sequences minimizing the threshold. Compactness of normalized potentials does not give compactness of these Gauduchon metrics.

In dimension one this normalization issue does not arise: the root volume is $\int_X\theta$, and $\kappa_h(\theta)=\int_X\theta/\int_Xh$. Positive volume and bigness are therefore equivalent by the same threshold criterion.
