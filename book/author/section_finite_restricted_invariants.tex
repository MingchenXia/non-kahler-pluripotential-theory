\section{Finite restricted masses of closed classes}
\label{sec:finite-restricted-invariants}\index{Restricted volume!finite invariants}

The nonclosed error in \cref{prop:bc-zero-class-infinite-error-volume} destroys the cohomological bound on masses. On a compact K\"ahler manifold, by contrast, the non-pluripolar mass of a positive current in a closed class is bounded by a top intersection number. Pulling a class back to a K\"ahler resolution of a subvariety therefore gives a finite invariant: the supremum of the non-pluripolar masses of positive currents in the pulled-back class. It measures positivity along every subvariety, including subvarieties along which a given modification of $X$ fails to be an isomorphism.

In this section and the next, $X$ belongs to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$. Then every irreducible reduced subvariety $Y\subset X$ has a resolution $\pi\colon Z\to Y$ with $Z$ a compact K\"ahler manifold: closed subspaces of a space in class $\mathcal C$ are again in class $\mathcal C$, and the K\"ahler modification theorem applies to $Y$. These classical facts are discussed in \cref{sec:history-closed-loci}. In particular, a K\"ahler model of $Y$ is obtained directly, not as a proper transform under one fixed modification of $X$.

Two such resolutions $Z_1,Z_2$ of $Y$ admit a common K\"ahler refinement. Indeed, apply embedded resolution to the closure in $Z_1\times Z_2$ of the graph of the induced bimeromorphic map. The resulting strict transform is smooth, and it is K\"ahler because the ambient resolution is a sequence of blowups of the compact K\"ahler product along smooth centers. A blowup of a compact K\"ahler manifold along a smooth center is again K\"ahler: add to the pulled-back K\"ahler form a small positive multiple of a curvature form of $\mathcal O(-E)$, which is positive in the vertical directions along the exceptional divisor $E$.

For a closed positive $(1,1)$-current $S$ on a compact K\"ahler manifold $Z$ of dimension $k>0$, write $\langle S^k\rangle$ for its non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure, that is, $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\eta(u)$ for any decomposition $S=\eta+\ddc u$ with $\eta$ smooth and closed (\cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}). For a class $b\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R)$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-class-mass}
 \mathcal R_Z(b)=\sup_{\substack{S\in b\\S\geq0}}
                  \int_Z\langle S^k\rangle,
 \qquad \sup\varnothing=0.
\end{equation}
For a class $a\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ and an irreducible reduced subvariety $Y\subset X$ of dimension $k>0$, let $j\colon Y\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion, write $f=j\circ\pi$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-restricted-invariant}
 \mathcal R(a;Y)=\mathcal R_Z(f^*a).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite-restricted-invariance}
The quantities in \eqref{eq:closed-class-mass} and \eqref{eq:finite-restricted-invariant} are finite, and the latter is independent of the K\"ahler resolution. If $\eta\in b$ is smooth, $\kappa$ is K\"ahler and $C>0$ satisfies $C\kappa-\eta\geq0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-class-mass-bound}
 \mathcal R_Z(b+t\{\kappa\})
       \leq(C+t)^k\int_Z\kappa^k \qquad(t\geq0).
\end{equation}
Let $\mu\colon X'\to X$ be a modification and $Y'$ the proper transform of a subvariety $Y$ that meets the locus where $\mu$ is an isomorphism. Then
\[
 \mathcal R(\mu^*a;Y')=\mathcal R(a;Y).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: K\"ahler forms.} Let $\lambda$ be a K\"ahler form on $Z$ and $q\in\PSH(Z,\lambda)$ with $q\leq0$. The truncations $q_j=\max(q,-j)$ have mass $\int_Z\lambda^k$ by \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}, or by Stokes' theorem when $k=1$. Restricting their Monge--Amp\`ere measures to $\{q>-j\}$ and passing to the increasing limit gives
\[
 \int_Z\langle(\lambda+\ddc q)^k\rangle
       \leq\int_Z\lambda^k.
\]
The smooth current $\lambda$ attains equality.

\emph{Step 2: the mass bound.} Put $A=C\kappa-\eta$. For a positive current $S$ in $b+t\{\kappa\}$, the current $S+A$ is positive and belongs to $(C+t)\{\kappa\}$. Since local non-pluripolar products are positive and multilinear, $\langle S^k\rangle\leq\langle(S+A)^k\rangle$, and Step~1 applied to the K\"ahler form $(C+t)\kappa$ yields \eqref{eq:closed-class-mass-bound}. In particular $\mathcal R_Z(b)$ is finite. The definition involves only the class, so the choice of its smooth representative or of the comparison form does not change the supremum.

\emph{Step 3: modifications.} Let $\rho\colon W\to Z$ be a modification between compact K\"ahler manifolds. By \cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}, every positive current in $\rho^*b$ is the pullback of a unique positive current in $b$. Their non-pluripolar measures correspond on the locus where $\rho$ is an isomorphism, and neither charges the exceptional analytic sets. Hence their total masses agree and
\[
 \mathcal R_W(\rho^*b)=\mathcal R_Z(b).
\]
Applying this identity on a common K\"ahler refinement shows that $\mathcal R(a;Y)$ is independent of the resolution. The same argument compares resolutions of $Y$ and $Y'$, since their maps to $X$ agree on a dense open set and therefore everywhere on a common refinement.
\end{proof}

On a compact K\"ahler manifold, positivity of this mass has a classical interpretation. We use two facts from K\"ahler pluripotential theory, discussed in \cref{sec:history-closed-loci}. First, a class containing a positive current with positive non-pluripolar top mass is big. Second, in a big class a current with minimal singularities attains the volume, the largest non-pluripolar top mass, and for a nef and big class this volume is the top intersection number. For a nef class with positive top intersection number, a K\"ahler current in the class can also be obtained from the following normalized diagonal construction.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:restricted-mass-bigness}
Let $Z$ be a compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $k>0$ and $b\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R)$. Then
\[
 \mathcal R_Z(b)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b\text{ is big}.
\]
If $b$ is nef, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-restricted-mass}
 \mathcal R_Z(b)=\int_Zb^k.
\end{equation}
Suppose that $b$ is nef with $I=\int_Zb^k>0$. Fix a K\"ahler form $\kappa$, let $p_1,p_2\colon Z\times Z\to Z$ be the projections and $\Delta\subset Z\times Z$ the diagonal, and put
\[
 M=k\int_Zb^{k-1}\wedge\{\kappa\}.
\]
Then there are $d>0$ and a closed positive $(k,k)$-current $\Theta$ in $(p_1^*b+p_2^*b)^k$ with $\Theta\geq d[\Delta]$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-diagonal-current}
 K_b=M^{-1}(p_1)_*(\Theta\wedge p_2^*\kappa)\in b,
 \qquad K_b\geq(d/M)\kappa.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\mathcal R_Z(b)>0$, some positive representative has positive non-pluripolar top mass, so $b$ is big by the first fact above. Conversely, if $S\in b$ satisfies $S\geq\delta\kappa$ with $\delta>0$, then $S-\delta\kappa$ is positive and closed, and expanding the non-pluripolar product gives $\langle S^k\rangle\geq\delta^k\kappa^k$. Hence $\mathcal R_Z(b)>0$.

Suppose now that $b$ is nef. For $t>0$, the class $b+t\{\kappa\}$ is K\"ahler. Adding $t\kappa$ to an arbitrary positive representative of $b$ and using the K\"ahler mass bound of Step~1 in the proof of \cref{prop:finite-restricted-invariance} yields
\[
 \mathcal R_Z(b)\leq\int_Z(b+t\{\kappa\})^k.
\]
Letting $t$ decrease to zero gives the upper bound in \eqref{eq:nef-restricted-mass}. In particular, equality holds if $I=0$.

Let $I>0$, and choose $L>0$ such that $L\{\kappa\}-b$ is K\"ahler. Since intersection numbers of nef classes are nonnegative,
\[
 0\leq\int_Zb^{k-1}\wedge(L\{\kappa\}-b)
      =LM/k-I,
\]
so $M>0$. The class $B=p_1^*b+p_2^*b$ is nef and satisfies
\[
 \int_{Z\times Z}B^{2k}={2k\choose k}I^2>0.
\]
The classical mass concentration theorem along the diagonal gives $\Theta\in B^k$ with $\Theta\geq d[\Delta]$ for some $d>0$. When $B^k\wedge p_2^*\{\kappa\}$ is pushed forward by $p_1$, only the term containing exactly one factor $p_1^*b$ survives. Thus
\[
 (p_1)_*(B^k\wedge p_2^*\{\kappa\})=Mb,
 \qquad
 (p_1)_*([\Delta]\wedge p_2^*\kappa)=\kappa.
\]
Since $\Theta-d[\Delta]$ is positive, these identities prove \eqref{eq:normalized-diagonal-current}; the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma for currents on the compact K\"ahler manifold $Z$ identifies the cohomology class computed in this way with the Bott--Chern class $b$. Therefore $b$ is big, and by the second fact above a current with minimal singularities in $b$ has non-pluripolar mass $\int_Zb^k$. This gives the remaining lower bound.
\end{proof}

When $Y$ is smooth, the criterion descends to $Y$. Write $a_Y=j^*a$ for the restricted class, let $\pi\colon Z\to Y$ be a K\"ahler modification with K\"ahler form $\kappa$, and choose a Hermitian metric $h$ on $Y$ and $C>0$ with $\pi^*h\leq C\kappa$. If $K\in\pi^*a_Y$ satisfies $K\geq\delta\kappa$ with $\delta>0$, then descent of potentials (\cref{prop:psh-descent-modification}) makes $\pi_*K$ a positive representative of $a_Y$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-current-pushforward}
 \pi_*K\geq(\delta/C)\pi_*\pi^*h=(\delta/C)h.
\end{equation}
Conversely, let $S$ be a strict positive representative of $a_Y$. On a coordinate ball in the locus where $\pi$ is an isomorphism, $\pi^*S$ dominates a positive multiple of the closed form $\kappa$, so the local expansion of the non-pluripolar product shows that $\pi^*S$ has positive non-pluripolar mass on that ball. Consequently, $\mathcal R(a;Y)>0$ if and only if the restricted class $a_Y$ is big. The Hermitian metric $h$ enters only through inequalities between currents; the products are compared with the closed form $\kappa$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:restricted-rank-drop}
Let $W$ be a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $k>0$, let $g\colon W\to X$ be holomorphic, and suppose $\dim g(W)<k$. Then for every class $a\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$,
\[
 \mathcal R_W(g^*a)=0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If the image is a point, then $g^*a=0$ and \eqref{eq:nef-restricted-mass} gives the assertion. Otherwise, choose a K\"ahler resolution $r\colon Q\to g(W)$ and resolve the graph of the meromorphic lift of $g$ to $Q$. This gives a K\"ahler modification $\rho\colon\widehat W\to W$ and a holomorphic map $F\colon\widehat W\to Q$ with $g\circ\rho=r\circ F$, where $r$ is also viewed as a map into $X$.

Choose a smooth representative $\eta\in a$, a K\"ahler form $\kappa_Q$ on $Q$, and $C>0$ such that $C\kappa_Q-r^*\eta\geq0$. Since $\rho^*g^*\eta=F^*r^*\eta$, the forms
\[
 P=CF^*\kappa_Q,
 \qquad A=P-\rho^*g^*\eta
\]
are closed and semipositive. Since $\dim Q<k$, we have $P^k=0$. For any positive current $S\in g^*a$, the current $\rho^*S+A$ is positive and lies in the nef class $\{P\}$. Hence
\[
 \int_{\widehat W}\langle(\rho^*S)^k\rangle
 \leq\int_{\widehat W}\langle(\rho^*S+A)^k\rangle
 \leq\mathcal R_{\widehat W}(\{P\})
 =\int_{\widehat W}P^k=0.
\]
Modification invariance (Step~3 in the proof of \cref{prop:finite-restricted-invariance}) now gives the result. If the class has no positive representative, its invariant is zero by definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:finite-restricted-null-union}
Let $a$ be a nef class on $X$. For every positive-dimensional irreducible reduced subvariety $Y\subset X$,
\[
 \mathcal R(a;Y)=\int_Ya^{\dim Y}.
\]
If $\int_Xa^n>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-restricted-null-union}
 \bigcup_{\substack{Y\subset X\ \mathrm{irreducible},\ \dim Y>0\\
                         \mathcal R(a;Y)=0}}Y
       =\operatorname{Null}(a)=E_{\mathrm{nK}}(a).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The pullback of $a$ to a K\"ahler resolution of $Y$ is nef: pull back smooth representatives with arbitrarily small negative part, and bound the pullback of $\omega$ by a multiple of a K\"ahler form on the resolution. Since the intersection number $\int_Ya^{\dim Y}$ is defined through the same pullback, \eqref{eq:nef-restricted-mass} gives the first assertion. The first equality in \eqref{eq:finite-restricted-null-union} then follows from the definition of the null locus. For the second, the first assertion with $Y=X$ gives $\mathcal R(a;X)=\int_Xa^n>0$, so $a$ is big by the criterion for smooth subvarieties preceding \cref{prop:restricted-rank-drop}. The second equality is therefore the classical theorem on nef and big classes recalled earlier in this chapter.
\end{proof}

When $X$ is K\"ahler and $a$ is nef, the numerical restricted volume \eqref{eq:bc-numerical-restricted-volume} along $Y$ is also $\int_Ya^{\dim Y}$, so the two restricted invariants agree. Outside the nef case this comparison does not identify them: \eqref{eq:finite-restricted-invariant} is a supremum over all positive currents on a resolution of $Y$, and these currents need not extend to $X$.
