\subsection{Upper and lower masses}
\label{sec:gaud-volume-extrema}

As the probability measure varies, the equation realizes every smooth strictly positive potential mass. Exact-background regularization gives the same extrema over bounded potentials. The infimum has a further simplification: maximizing the denominator at each Gauduchon metric removes the density entirely.

Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$, and let $\mathscr M$ be the set of smooth strictly positive probability measures on $X$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!extrema by duality}
One has
\[
 \inf_{\mu\in\mathscr M}\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)=v_-(\omega),
 \qquad
 \sup_{\mu\in\mathscr M}\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)=v_+(\omega).
\]
The supremum is allowed to be infinite. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula}
 v_-(\omega)^{1/n}
 =\inf_{\gamma\text{ Gauduchon}}
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_X\omega\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}
      {\displaystyle\left(\int_X\gamma^n\right)^{(n-1)/n}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The smooth normalized equation realizes every value $\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)$ as the mass of a smooth strictly positive potential. Conversely every such potential $v$ realizes its mass by the probability measure $\mu_v$ in the proof of \cref{prop:gaud-volume-density-independence}. Thus the two ranges, as subsets of $(0,+\infty)$, are equal.

We justify replacing bounded potentials by smooth strictly positive ones in the extrema defining $v_\pm$. If $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^\infty$, \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization} gives uniformly bounded smooth strictly $\omega$-plurisubharmonic $u_j$ decreasing to $u$. Their Monge--Amp\`ere measures converge by bounded monotone continuity, including their total masses on compact $X$. Every bounded mass is therefore a limit of strictly positive smooth masses. The reverse inclusion is immediate. This proves the first two identities, without a uniform bound on those masses.

For the last formula, take the $n$th root and commute the infima over $\mu$ and $\gamma$ in the dual formula. At fixed $\gamma$, H\"older's inequality and $\int_X\mu=1$ give
\[
 D_\mu(\gamma)
 \leq\left(\int_X\gamma^n\right)^{(n-1)/n}.
\]
Equality holds for the smooth probability measure
$\mu=\gamma^n/\int_X\gamma^n$. The numerator is positive and does not depend on $\mu$, so this maximization of the denominator computes the infimum of the quotient. Substitution gives \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula}. For $n=1$ both denominators are one, and the same argument applies.
\end{proof}

Thus $v_-(\omega)>0$ is equivalent to a positive lower bound for
$\int_X\omega\wedge\gamma^{n-1}$ among Gauduchon metrics of volume one. This lower bound fails on the threefold constructed in \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}. By \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-solvability}, it is also equivalent to solvability of the normalized equation for every finite non-pluripolar measure in a Hermitian background. Likewise every fixed smooth density gives a finite normalization, while their supremum can be infinite.

\Needspace{25\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower-mass-smooth-attainment}
For a Hermitian $\omega$, the infimum in \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula} is attained if and only if the infimum of smooth strictly positive Monge--Amp\`ere masses is attained. Every such minimizing potential has a Gauduchon metric $\omega+\ddc v$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a test metric $\gamma$ attains the first infimum, and put $m=v_-(\omega)^{1/n}$. Its numerator is strictly positive, so $m>0$. For $\mu_\gamma=\gamma^n/\int_X\gamma^n$, the same test metric gives
$\lambda_{\mu_\gamma}(\omega)\leq m$.
The mass-extrema theorem gives the opposite inequality. Hence the smooth equation for $\mu_\gamma$ has mass $m^n=v_-(\omega)$, giving a minimizing smooth potential. For $n\geq2$, equality in the dual formula makes its solution conformal to $\gamma$. The fixed proportional volume forms make the conformal factor constant, so the solution metric is Gauduchon.

Conversely, at a smooth strictly positive mass minimizer $v$, both signs of every smooth variation are admissible for sufficiently small parameters. The first variation of the raw mass therefore gives
\[
 \int_X f\,\ddc\bigl((\omega+\ddc v)^{n-1}\bigr)=0
 \quad\hbox{for every smooth real }f.
\]
Thus $\Omega=\omega+\ddc v$ is Gauduchon. Using $\gamma=\Omega$ in the quotient and Gauduchon Stokes gives
\[
 \frac{\int_X\omega\wedge\Omega^{n-1}}
      {(\int_X\Omega^n)^{(n-1)/n}}
             =(\int_X\Omega^n)^{1/n}=v_-(\omega)^{1/n},
\]
so the test infimum is attained. In dimension one all metrics are Gauduchon, and all masses equal $\int_X\omega$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
The density infimum also has a definition beyond the positive cone. For a pseudoeffective smooth real $(1,1)$-background $\theta$ on $X$, put
\[
 \lambda_*(\theta)=
 \inf_{\gamma\text{ Gauduchon}}
 \frac{\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}
      {(\int_X\gamma^n)^{(n-1)/n}}.
\]
The same H\"older argument proves
\[
 \lambda_*(\theta)=\inf_{\mu\in\mathscr M}\lambda_\mu(\theta).
\]
It is finite, nonnegative, concave, homogeneous, and invariant under smooth $\ddc$ changes. If $\theta$ is nef, fix a Hermitian form $h$ and, for real $\epsilon>0$, choose a smooth Hermitian representative $\omega_\epsilon$ of $\theta+\epsilon h$. Commuting the infima over the test metric and $\epsilon>0$ also gives
\[
 \lambda_*(\theta)
 =\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\lambda_*(\theta+\epsilon h)
 =\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}v_-(\omega_\epsilon)^{1/n}.
\]
Gauge invariance makes the last expression independent of that representative. This extends the lower-mass formula to the nef boundary. It does not identify the resulting value with every lower-volume definition for an arbitrary singular class.
