\subsection{Upper and lower masses}
\label{sec:gaud-volume-extrema}

As the probability measure varies, the smooth equation realizes the mass of every smooth strictly positive potential, by the proof of \cref{prop:gaud-volume-density-independence}. The extrema of the volume over all measures are therefore the extrema of these masses, and regularization of bounded potentials identifies them with the lower and upper masses $v_-(\omega)$ and $v_+(\omega)$ of \cref{def:bmp}. The infimum simplifies further: at each Gauduchon metric an explicit measure maximizes the denominator, and this removes the density from the formula entirely.

Throughout this subsection $\omega$ is a Hermitian form on $X$, and $\mathscr M$ is again the set of smooth strictly positive probability measures on $X$, as in \cref{sec:invariant-mass-reference-densities}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!extrema by duality}
One has
\[
 \inf_{\mu\in\mathscr M}\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)=v_-(\omega),
 \qquad
 \sup_{\mu\in\mathscr M}\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)=v_+(\omega),
\]
where the supremum may be infinite. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula}
 v_-(\omega)^{1/n}
 =\inf_{\gamma\text{ Gauduchon}}
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_X\omega\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}
      {\displaystyle\left(\int_X\gamma^n\right)^{(n-1)/n}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $\mu\in\mathscr M$, the smooth normalized equation realizes $\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)$ as the mass $\int_X(\omega+\ddc v)^n$ of a smooth potential $v$ with $\omega+\ddc v>0$. Conversely, every such potential realizes its mass as $\mathcal V_{\mu_v}(\omega)$ for the measure $\mu_v$ in the proof of \cref{prop:gaud-volume-density-independence}. Thus the set of volumes $\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)$, $\mu\in\mathscr M$, coincides with the set of these smooth masses.

It remains to show that the smooth masses have the same extrema as the masses of bounded potentials, which define $v_\pm(\omega)$. If $u\in\PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^\infty$, \cref{thm:bounded-exact-regularization} gives uniformly bounded smooth strictly $\omega$-plurisubharmonic functions $u_j$ decreasing to $u$. Their Monge--Amp\`ere measures converge by the monotone continuity of bounded products, and so do their total masses, since $X$ is compact. Every bounded mass is therefore a limit of smooth strictly positive masses. Conversely, smooth potentials are bounded. This proves the first two identities; no uniform bound on the masses is needed.

For the last formula, take $n$th roots and interchange the infima over $\mu$ and over $\gamma$ in \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula}. For a fixed Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, H\"older's inequality and $\int_X\mu=1$ give
\[
 D_\mu(\gamma)
 \leq\left(\int_X\gamma^n\right)^{(n-1)/n},
\]
with equality for the smooth probability measure
$\mu=\gamma^n/\int_X\gamma^n$. The numerator is positive and does not depend on $\mu$, so the infimum of the quotient over $\mu$ is obtained by maximizing the denominator. This gives \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula}. For $n=1$ both denominators equal one, and the same argument applies.
\end{proof}

Thus $v_-(\omega)>0$ if and only if $\int_X\omega\wedge\gamma^{n-1}$ has a positive lower bound over all Gauduchon metrics $\gamma$ with $\int_X\gamma^n=1$. This lower bound fails on the threefold constructed in \cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass}. By \cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-solvability}, positivity of $v_-(\omega)$ is also equivalent to solvability, in the background $\omega$, of the normalized equation for every nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure. In the same way, each individual volume $\mathcal V_\mu(\omega)$ is finite, while their supremum $v_+(\omega)$ can be infinite.

\Needspace{25\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower-mass-smooth-attainment}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!smooth minimum}
The infimum in \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula} is attained if and only if the infimum of the masses $\int_X(\omega+\ddc v)^n$ over smooth functions $v$ with $\omega+\ddc v>0$ is attained. For every such minimizing potential $v$, the metric $\omega+\ddc v$ is Gauduchon.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$ attains the infimum in \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula}, and put $m=v_-(\omega)^{1/n}$. The numerator of its quotient is strictly positive, so $m>0$. For the measure $\mu_\gamma=\gamma^n/\int_X\gamma^n$, equality holds in the H\"older inequality above, so the test $\gamma$ gives
$\lambda_{\mu_\gamma}(\omega)\leq m$.
\Cref{thm:gaud-volume-mass-extrema} gives the opposite inequality. Hence the solution of the smooth equation for $\mu_\gamma$ has mass $m^n=v_-(\omega)$, which gives a minimizing smooth potential. For $n\geq2$, $\gamma$ also minimizes the quotient for $\mu_\gamma$, so by \cref{thm:gaud-volume-dual-formula} the solution form is conformal to $\gamma$. Since both have volume forms proportional to $\mu_\gamma$, the conformal factor is constant, and the solution metric is Gauduchon.

Conversely, let $v$ be a smooth potential minimizing the mass. For every smooth real $f$, the potentials $v\pm tf$ are admissible for sufficiently small $t>0$. The vanishing of the first variation of the mass therefore gives
\[
 \int_X f\,\ddc\bigl((\omega+\ddc v)^{n-1}\bigr)=0
 \quad\hbox{for every smooth real }f.
\]
Thus $\Omega=\omega+\ddc v$ is Gauduchon. Taking $\gamma=\Omega$ in the quotient and using Stokes' theorem for the Gauduchon metric $\Omega$ gives
\[
 \frac{\int_X\omega\wedge\Omega^{n-1}}
      {(\int_X\Omega^n)^{(n-1)/n}}
             =(\int_X\Omega^n)^{1/n}=v_-(\omega)^{1/n},
\]
so the infimum in \eqref{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula} is attained. In dimension one all metrics are Gauduchon and all masses equal $\int_X\omega$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
The infimum over densities also makes sense beyond the positive cone. For a pseudoeffective smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ on $X$, put
\[
 \lambda_*(\theta)=
 \inf_{\gamma\text{ Gauduchon}}
 \frac{\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}
      {(\int_X\gamma^n)^{(n-1)/n}}.
\]
The H\"older argument above proves
\[
 \lambda_*(\theta)=\inf_{\mu\in\mathscr M}\lambda_\mu(\theta).
\]
Like each $\lambda_\mu$, the function $\lambda_*$ is finite, nonnegative, concave, positively homogeneous, and invariant under smooth $\ddc$ changes of $\theta$. Suppose $\theta$ is nef, fix a Hermitian form $h$, and for real $\epsilon>0$ choose a Hermitian representative $\omega_\epsilon$ of $\theta+\epsilon h$. Interchanging the infima over the test metric and over $\epsilon>0$, as in \cref{thm:gaud-volume-nef-limit}, gives
\[
 \lambda_*(\theta)
 =\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\lambda_*(\theta+\epsilon h)
 =\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}v_-(\omega_\epsilon)^{1/n}.
\]
By invariance under smooth $\ddc$ changes, the last expression does not depend on the choice of the representatives $\omega_\epsilon$. This extends the lower-mass formula to the nef boundary. We do not compare $\lambda_*$ with other notions of lower volume for singular classes.
