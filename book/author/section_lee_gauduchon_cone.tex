\section{The Lee--Gauduchon cone}
\label{sec:lee-gauduchon-cone}

For the rest of the chapter, $X$ is an arbitrary connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq2$, not necessarily LCK. By \cref{thm:lamari}, a class in the image of the Lee map $j_X$ of \eqref{eq:lck-lee-bc-map} is pseudoeffective exactly when it pairs nonnegatively with every Gauduchon metric. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, the form $\dc(\gamma^{n-1})$ is closed, and these pairings depend only on its de Rham class. The classes obtained in this way lie in the annihilator of $\ker j_X$, where they form an open convex cone, and strict positivity against this cone detects every nonzero pseudoeffective class in the image of $j_X$.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
Set $V_X=H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)$ and
\[
 K_X=\ker j_X,\qquad
 W_X=K_X^\perp\subset H^{2n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R),
\]
where the annihilator is taken with respect to the Poincar\'e pairing
\[
 \langle[\lambda],a\rangle=\int_X\lambda\wedge a
 \qquad\bigl(a\in H^{2n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)\bigr).
\]
Let
\[
 \mathcal C_X
 =\{[\lambda]\in V_X:j_X([\lambda])\text{ is pseudoeffective}\}.
\]
This is a convex cone, since pseudoeffective classes form a convex cone. The image of $j_X$ lies in the kernel of the natural map from Bott--Chern to de Rham cohomology, and it can be strictly smaller than this kernel, as \cref{ex:lee-calabi-eckmann} will show.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lee-gauduchon-kernel}
The space $K_X$ consists of the de Rham classes of real parts of closed holomorphic one-forms. Moreover,
\[
 \mathcal C_X\cap(-\mathcal C_X)=K_X.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $j_X([\lambda])=0$, write $\dc\lambda=\ddc f$ for a smooth real function $f$. Since $\dc\mathrm{d}=-\mathrm{d}\dc$, the form $\lambda+\mathrm{d}f$ is both $\mathrm{d}$-closed and $\dc$-closed. Since the two equations separate the $\partial$- and $\bar\partial$-derivatives, its $(1,0)$-part is holomorphic and closed. Thus $\lambda+\mathrm{d}f$ is the real part of the closed holomorphic form $2(\lambda+\mathrm{d}f)^{1,0}$. Conversely, the real part of a closed holomorphic one-form is closed for both operators, so its image under $j_X$ is zero.

By \cref{prop:psef-cone-compact-base}, the pseudoeffective Bott--Chern cone contains no line. Thus $j_X(\xi)$ and $-j_X(\xi)$ are both pseudoeffective exactly when $j_X(\xi)=0$, which is the identity $\mathcal C_X\cap(-\mathcal C_X)=K_X$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\begin{definition}\label{def:lee-gauduchon-cone}\index{Lee--Gauduchon cone}
The \emph{Lee--Gauduchon cone} is
\[
 \operatorname{LG}(X)
 =\bigl\{[\dc(\gamma^{n-1})]:
                    \gamma\text{ is a Gauduchon metric on }X\bigr\}.
\]
\end{definition}

For a closed real one-form $\lambda$ and any smooth real $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\Gamma$ with $\ddc\Gamma=0$, integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lck-lee-gauduchon-pairing}
 \int_X\lambda\wedge\dc\Gamma
 =\int_X\kappa_\lambda\wedge\Gamma.
\end{equation}
The form $\dc\Gamma$ is closed, and both sides depend only on the cohomology classes involved. In particular, the class $[\dc\Gamma]$ annihilates $K_X$, so $\operatorname{LG}(X)\subseteq W_X$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lee-gauduchon-open}
The cone $\operatorname{LG}(X)$ is nonempty, convex and open in $W_X$. Its linear span is $W_X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal Z_X$ be the real vector space of smooth $(n-1,n-1)$-forms $\Gamma$ satisfying $\ddc\Gamma=0$, and set
\[
 q_X(\Gamma)=[\dc\Gamma].
\]
We first show that $q_X(\mathcal Z_X)=W_X$. The inclusion into $W_X$ follows from \eqref{eq:lck-lee-gauduchon-pairing}. If $[\lambda]$ annihilates the image of $q_X$, then by \eqref{eq:lck-lee-gauduchon-pairing} the class $\{\kappa_\lambda\}$ has zero pairing with every Gauduchon form. The generalized Lamari criterion, \cref{thm:lamari}, implies that both $\{\kappa_\lambda\}$ and $-\{\kappa_\lambda\}$ are pseudoeffective, so $[\lambda]\in K_X$ by \cref{lem:lee-gauduchon-kernel}. Thus the image of $q_X$ and $W_X$ have the same annihilator in $V_X$, and since the Poincar\'e pairing between $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and $H^{2n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is nondegenerate, they are equal.

Let $\mathcal Z_X^+$ be the set of strictly positive forms in $\mathcal Z_X$. By the linear-algebra construction preceding the proof of \cref{thm:lamari}, every such form is the $(n-1)$st power of a unique Hermitian form, which is then Gauduchon. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{LG}(X)=q_X(\mathcal Z_X^+).
\]
By Gauduchon's theorem, $\mathcal Z_X^+$ is nonempty, and it is a convex cone; hence so is $\operatorname{LG}(X)$.

For openness, choose forms $\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_r\in\mathcal Z_X$ whose images form a basis of $W_X$. If $\Gamma\in\mathcal Z_X^+$, then $\Gamma+\sum_i t_i\Gamma_i$ remains strictly positive for all sufficiently small real $t_i$, and the images of these forms cover a neighborhood of $q_X(\Gamma)$ in $W_X$. This includes the case $W_X=\{0\}$, with the empty basis. Since a nonempty open subset spans the ambient vector space, the assertion about the span follows.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lee-gauduchon-duality}\index{Lee--Gauduchon cone!duality}
The cone $\mathcal C_X$ is closed, and
\[
 \mathcal C_X
 =\{\xi\in V_X:\langle\xi,a\rangle\geq0
                         \text{ for every }a\in\operatorname{LG}(X)\}.
\]
Conversely,
\[
 \operatorname{LG}(X)
 =\{a\in W_X:\langle\xi,a\rangle>0
                         \text{ for every }\xi\in\mathcal C_X\setminus K_X\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first equality is \cref{thm:lamari} applied to the smooth closed form $\kappa_\lambda$, together with \eqref{eq:lck-lee-gauduchon-pairing}. It expresses $\mathcal C_X$ as an intersection of closed half-spaces, so this cone is closed.

If $a=[\dc(\gamma^{n-1})]\in\operatorname{LG}(X)$ and $\xi=[\lambda]\in\mathcal C_X\setminus K_X$, choose a closed positive current $T=\kappa_\lambda+\ddc u$. It is nonzero because it represents the nonzero class $j_X(\xi)$. Therefore
\[
 \langle\xi,a\rangle
 =\int_XT\wedge\gamma^{n-1}>0.
\]

Conversely, suppose that $a\in W_X\setminus\operatorname{LG}(X)$. Separating the point $a$ from the nonempty open convex cone $\operatorname{LG}(X)$ in the finite-dimensional space $W_X$ gives a nonzero linear functional $\ell$ on $W_X$ such that
\[
 \ell(a)\leq0,\qquad \ell(b)\geq0
                   \quad(b\in\operatorname{LG}(X)).
\]
Indeed, in the usual separating inequalities the constant can be taken to be zero, by scaling the elements of the cone. By Poincar\'e duality, $\ell=\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle$ for a class $\xi\in V_X$ that is unique modulo $K_X$, and $\xi\notin K_X$ because $\ell\neq0$. The first equality of the theorem gives $\xi\in\mathcal C_X$, and then $\langle\xi,a\rangle\leq0$ shows that $a$ does not belong to the right side of the second equality. This proves the second equality; if $\operatorname{LG}(X)=W_X$, there is nothing to prove.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Bimeromorphic invariance}

A modification induces an isomorphism on degree-one cohomology, and a class on the base is pseudoeffective exactly when its pullback is. These two facts identify the cones $\mathcal C_X$, and duality then identifies the Lee--Gauduchon cones.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lee-gauduchon-modification}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a modification between connected compact complex manifolds, and let $\theta$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then
\[
 \{\theta\}\text{ is pseudoeffective}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{\pi^*\theta\}\text{ is pseudoeffective}.
\]
The pullback on real Bott--Chern cohomology in bidegree $(1,1)$ is injective.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:psef-cone-compact-base}, both pullback and pushforward preserve pseudoeffectivity; the pullback of potentials is the one used in \cref{lem:modification-lone-pullback}. Since $\pi$ has degree one,
\[
 \pi_*\pi^*\{\theta\}=\{\theta\}.
\]
Indeed, the identity of currents $\pi_*\pi^*\theta=\theta$ follows by integration over the locus where $\pi$ is an isomorphism, whose complement has volume zero. This left inverse proves injectivity and the reverse implication for pseudoeffectivity.
\end{proof}

Degree-one cohomology is compared by moving cycles away from the image of the exceptional set.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lee-gauduchon-blowup-cohomology}
If $\pi\colon Y\to X$ is a modification between connected compact complex manifolds, then
\[
 \pi^*:H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)\longrightarrow
                    H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(Y,\R)
\]
is an isomorphism. Its Poincar\'e dual is the isomorphism
$\pi_*\colon H^{2n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(Y,\R)\to H^{2n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a proper analytic subset $A$ of a connected complex manifold $M$, the restriction map $H^1(M,\R)\to H^1(M\setminus A,\R)$ is injective, and it is an isomorphism if $A$ has complex codimension at least two. Indeed, choose a Whitney stratification of $A$. General position moves every one-cycle off the strata, which have real codimension at least two. If those codimensions are at least four, it also moves every two-chain with boundary in $M\setminus A$ off the strata, relative to its boundary. Thus the map $H_1(M\setminus A,\R)\to H_1(M,\R)$ is surjective in the first case and bijective in the second, and dualizing over $\R$ gives the assertions about restriction.

Since $X$ is smooth, there is an analytic subset $A\subset X$ of complex codimension at least two such that $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $U=X\setminus A$. Restriction from $X$ to $U$ is therefore an isomorphism, whereas restriction from $Y$ to $\pi^{-1}U$ is injective. Every class on $\pi^{-1}U\simeq U$ extends to $X$ and hence pulls back to a class on $Y$. The commutative diagram of restriction maps now shows that $\pi^*$ is an isomorphism, and Poincar\'e duality and the projection formula give the assertion for $\pi_*$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lee-gauduchon-bimeromorphic}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be bimeromorphic connected compact complex manifolds. Under the natural identification of their first de Rham cohomology groups, and the Poincar\'e-dual identification of their de Rham cohomology groups of degree $2n-1$, the spaces $K_X,W_X$ and the cones $\mathcal C_X,\operatorname{LG}(X)$ correspond to those of $Y$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a modification. Holomorphic pullback commutes with $\dc$, so
\[
 j_Y(\pi^*\xi)=\pi^*j_X(\xi).
\]
By \cref{lem:lee-gauduchon-modification}, a class $\xi$ lies in $\mathcal C_X$ if and only if $\pi^*\xi$ lies in $\mathcal C_Y$, and likewise for $K_X$ and $K_Y$, since the pullback is injective on Bott--Chern classes. By \cref{lem:lee-gauduchon-blowup-cohomology}, every degree-one class on $Y$ is of the form $\pi^*\xi$. Thus the isomorphism $\pi^*$ in degree one identifies the two cones and the two kernels. Its dual $\pi_*$ identifies $W_Y$ with $W_X$, and \cref{thm:lee-gauduchon-duality} gives
\[
 \pi_*\operatorname{LG}(Y)=\operatorname{LG}(X).
\]

For a bimeromorphic map between $X$ and $Y$, resolve its graph to obtain modifications $p\colon Z\to X$ and $q\colon Z\to Y$ from a compact complex manifold $Z$. The preceding argument applies to both maps and identifies the cones and kernels via $(q^*)^{-1}p^*$ and its Poincar\'e dual. These identifications do not depend on the resolution: they are compatible with restriction to a common open set on which the original map is an isomorphism, and by the injectivity of degree-one restriction proved above, this determines each class. The dual identifications are therefore canonical as well.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Balanced metrics and the Hopf cone}

If zero belongs to the Lee--Gauduchon cone, then the image of $j_X$ contains no nonzero pseudoeffective class; this is the case when $X$ carries a balanced metric. On the Hopf threefold, by contrast, the Lee form spans a ray of classes with pseudoeffective image under $j_X$, and the Lee--Gauduchon cone is an open half-line.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lee-gauduchon-zero}
The following conditions are equivalent:
\[
 0\in\operatorname{LG}(X),\qquad
 \operatorname{LG}(X)=W_X,\qquad
 \mathcal C_X=K_X.
\]
A balanced metric implies these conditions. On a manifold in Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$, one has $W_X=\{0\}$ and $\operatorname{LG}(X)=\{0\}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
An open cone in a finite-dimensional vector space that contains zero is the entire space, since scaling a neighborhood of zero reaches every point. The two dual descriptions in \cref{thm:lee-gauduchon-duality} now prove the equivalences, including the case $W_X=\{0\}$. If $b$ is a balanced metric, the form $b^{n-1}$ is closed and of pure type, hence also $\dc$-closed. Thus $b$ is Gauduchon and gives the element $[\dc(b^{n-1})]=0$ of $\operatorname{LG}(X)$.

On a compact K\"ahler manifold the $\partial\bar\partial$-lemma gives $j_X=0$, since every $\kappa_\lambda$ is closed and de Rham exact. Thus $K_X=V_X$ and $W_X=\{0\}$, and since $\operatorname{LG}(X)$ is nonempty, $\operatorname{LG}(X)=\{0\}$. By \cref{thm:lee-gauduchon-bimeromorphic}, this conclusion extends to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, a nonzero pseudoeffective class in the image of $j_X$ obstructs balanced metrics on every compact complex manifold bimeromorphic to $X$. For LCK manifolds, \cref{conj:lck-lee-psef} would provide such a class.

For the Hopf threefold of \cref{chap:hopf}, with the Lee form $\lambda$ of \cref{prop:lck-hopf-vaisman}, the covering description identifies the underlying manifold with $S^1\times S^5$, so $V_X=\R[\lambda]$. The class $j_X([\lambda])=\{\alpha\}$ is nonzero by \eqref{eq:lck-lee-degree}, and it is pseudoeffective because $\alpha\geq0$. Its negative multiples are not pseudoeffective, since their pairing with the Gauduchon metric $\omega$ is negative. Thus
\[
 K_X=0,\qquad \mathcal C_X=\R_{\geq0}[\lambda],\qquad
 \operatorname{LG}(X)=\{a\in H^5_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\R):
                                  \langle[\lambda],a\rangle>0\}.
\]
The same manifold has unbounded Monge--Amp\`ere masses by \cref{thm:hopf-unbounded}. The positive ray detected here controls pairings with cohomology classes, which are linear and do not bound those nonlinear masses.
