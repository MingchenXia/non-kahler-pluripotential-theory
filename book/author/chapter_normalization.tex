\chapter{Normalization and weak adjoint measures}
\label{chap:normalization}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$ with a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. Function spaces are real, and Lebesgue norms are taken with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$. Until \cref{sec:geometric-normalization-stability}, $\theta$ is a smooth semipositive big real $(1,1)$-form and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ is a model reference in the sense of \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}: it is a fixed point of the ordinary singularity envelope $P_\theta[\cdot]$ of \cref{def:singularity-envelope}, and its envelope $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)$ in a smaller background is not identically $-\infty$ for some $0<\varepsilon<1$. We write $u\sim\varphi$ when $u$ has the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi$ (\cref{def:singularity-order}), and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta$ for the extended non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}; when its total mass is finite, it is the usual non-pluripolar measure, which we also denote by $\mathrm{MA}_\theta$.

In the ordinary type of $\varphi$, the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)=c\,\mathrm{e}^F\mathrm{d}V$ determines the constant $c>0$ uniquely, even when it has several normalized solutions. As a function of the logarithmic density $F$, the logarithm of this constant is concave. Its supporting functionals are probability measures that describe the first-order response of the constant to a change of density; for smooth Hermitian equations they are the Gauduchon adjoint probabilities of the linearized operator. The convex dual of the functional controls the concentration of these measures through entropy, and this gives compactness when the density, the reference or the positive background varies. The same measures provide the integral normalizations that single out the limit of the exponential equations as the exponent tends to zero. The scalar functional does not, however, determine the singularities of the solutions: examples with moving divisors have identical functionals but different limiting potentials. On closed projective backgrounds, convex geometry gives quantitative criteria for varying references, including references with nonanalytic poles or degenerate residuals.

\section{The scalar functional}\label{sec:normalization-scalar}\index{Normalization scalar!functional}

The domination principle makes the constant decrease as the density increases, and mixed Monge--Amp\`ere products strengthen this monotonicity to concavity in the logarithmic density. Compactness of the solutions then gives continuity under uniformly bounded $L^1$ perturbations.

For a bounded real measurable function $F$, part (2) of \cref{thm:als-prescribed-lp}, applied to the bounded density $\mathrm{e}^F$, gives $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\sim\varphi$ and a unique constant $c_\varphi(F)>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-normalization}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
       =c_\varphi(F)\mathrm{e}^F\,\mathrm{d}V.
\end{equation}
The \emph{normalization scalar} is
\[
 B_\varphi(F)=\log c_\varphi(F).
\]
Since the constant is unique, $B_\varphi$ is well defined; it depends on $\varphi$ only through its ordinary singularity type.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:relative-normalization-order}
Let $F,G\in L^\infty(\mathrm{d}V)$ be real. The functional $B_\varphi$ is decreasing, and for every constant $a\in\R$,
\[
 B_\varphi(F+a)=B_\varphi(F)-a.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-normalization-lipschitz}
 -\mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_X(G-F)
 \leq B_\varphi(G)-B_\varphi(F)
 \leq-\mathop{\rm ess\,inf}_X(G-F),
\end{equation}
and $|B_\varphi(G)-B_\varphi(F)|\leq\|G-F\|_\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that $F\leq G$ almost everywhere and, to reach a contradiction, that $c_\varphi(F)<c_\varphi(G)$. Let $u,v\sim\varphi$ solve the two equations. Since $F\leq G$ and the extended measure is unchanged by adding constants, for every constant $a$,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
 \leq\frac{c_\varphi(F)}{c_\varphi(G)}
             \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(v+a),
\]
and the ratio is strictly less than one. As $u\sim\varphi$, the envelope $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)$ dominates a translate of $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)$ and is therefore not identically $-\infty$; moreover $v+a\preceq u$. The singular strict domination principle, \cref{thm:singular-strict-domination}, gives $u\geq v+a$ for every $a$, which is impossible at any point where both potentials are finite. Thus $B_\varphi(F)\geq B_\varphi(G)$.

Replacing $F$ by $F+a$ and the constant by $\mathrm{e}^{-a}c_\varphi(F)$ leaves the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:relative-normalization} unchanged, so uniqueness of the constant proves the translation rule. Finally, $G$ lies between the constant translates $F+\mathop{\rm ess\,inf}_X(G-F)$ and $F+\mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_X(G-F)$; monotonicity and the translation rule give \eqref{eq:relative-normalization-lipschitz}.
\end{proof}

The scalar does not depend on the smooth representative of the background: for a smooth real $h$, replacing $\theta$ by $\theta+\ddc h$ and each potential $u$, including $\varphi$, by $u-h$ preserves the currents and the prescribed ordinary type, and hence leaves the equation and its constant unchanged.

Concavity comes from mixed products. A convex combination of two solutions stays in the prescribed ordinary type, and \cref{lem:singular-convex-density} bounds its measure below by the corresponding mixed density.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-normalization-concavity}\index{Normalization scalar!concavity}
The functional $B_\varphi$ is concave on the real space $L^\infty(\mathrm{d}V)$: for $F,G$ in that space,
\[
 B_\varphi(tF+(1-t)G)
 \geq tB_\varphi(F)+(1-t)B_\varphi(G),\qquad0\leq t\leq1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $u,v\sim\varphi$ solve the equations for $F$ and $G$, and set
$H=tF+(1-t)G$ and $D=c_\varphi(F)^t c_\varphi(G)^{1-t}$. \Cref{lem:singular-convex-density} and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(tu+(1-t)v)
 &\geq\bigl(t(c_\varphi(F)\mathrm{e}^F)^{1/n}
       +(1-t)(c_\varphi(G)\mathrm{e}^G)^{1/n}\bigr)^n\,\mathrm{d}V\\
 &\geq D \mathrm{e}^H\,\mathrm{d}V.
 \end{split}
\]
The averaged potential $tu+(1-t)v$ has the ordinary type of $\varphi$. If $c_\varphi(H)<D$, its measure dominates the measure of a solution $w\sim\varphi$ for $H$ by the factor $D/c_\varphi(H)>1$. Applying the singular strict domination principle to $w$ and to every constant translate of the averaged potential, as in the proof of \cref{prop:relative-normalization-order}, leads to the same contradiction. Hence $c_\varphi(H)\geq D$, and taking logarithms proves concavity.
\end{proof}

For smooth Hermitian data, this concavity agrees with the second-variation formula of \cref{thm:b-derivatives}.

Uniformly bounded logarithmic densities give the solutions a common lower barrier, and compactness of the solutions identifies the limiting scalar even when the densities converge only in $L^1$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:normalization-bounded-lone}
If $F_j,F$ are uniformly bounded real measurable functions and $F_j\to F$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, then
\[
 B_\varphi(F_j)\longrightarrow B_\varphi(F).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose solutions $u_j\sim\varphi$ normalized by $\sup_Xu_j=0$, and write $c_j=c_\varphi(F_j)$. If $|F_j|\leq M$, monotonicity and the translation rule of \cref{prop:relative-normalization-order} give
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-M}c_\varphi(0)\leq c_j\leq \mathrm{e}^M c_\varphi(0).
\]
In particular, the Monge--Amp\`ere measures of the $u_j$ are dominated by a fixed multiple of $\mathrm{d}V$, and \cref{thm:relative-linfty} gives a constant $C$ such that
\[
 \varphi-C\leq u_j\leq\varphi.
\]
From any subsequence, extract a further subsequence with $c_j\to c>0$ and $u_j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$. By \cref{cor:common-barrier-continuity}, the convergence also holds in capacity, $u\sim\varphi$, and the measures converge weakly. On the other hand, the exponential function is Lipschitz on the common range of the $F_j$, so $c_j\mathrm{e}^{F_j}\to c\mathrm{e}^F$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. Therefore
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)=c\mathrm{e}^F\,\mathrm{d}V,
\]
and uniqueness of the constant gives $c=c_\varphi(F)$. Since every subsequence has a further subsequence along which $c_j\to c_\varphi(F)$, the whole sequence converges, and so do the logarithms.
\end{proof}

\section{Supporting probability measures}\label{sec:normalization-supports}

A supporting functional of the concave function $B_\varphi$ describes the first-order response of the scalar to a change of logarithmic density. Monotonicity and the translation rule force such a functional to be minus the integral against a probability measure. We work first on the space $C(X)$ of continuous real functions, with the uniform norm. For $F\in C(X)$, let $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ be the set of Borel probability measures $\nu$ on $X$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint-probability-set}
 B_\varphi(G)\leq B_\varphi(F)-\int_X(G-F)\,\mathrm{d}\nu
 \qquad\text{for every }G\in C(X).
\end{equation}
Thus $G\mapsto-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu$ is a supergradient of $B_\varphi$ at $F$. We call the members of $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ the \emph{supporting probabilities} at $F$.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:weak-adjoint-probabilities}\index{Adjoint measure!supporting probability}
For every $F\in C(X)$, the set $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ is nonempty, convex and weakly compact. Every one of its measures is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The function $C_\varphi=-B_\varphi$ is convex and Lipschitz. Fix $F\in C(X)$. For a direction $h\in C(X)$, the limit
\[
 p(h)=\lim_{t\downarrow0}
        \frac{C_\varphi(F+th)-C_\varphi(F)}{t}
\]
exists and is finite: convexity makes the difference quotients monotone in $t>0$, and their absolute values are at most $\|h\|_\infty$. Clearly $p$ is positively homogeneous. Given another direction $k\in C(X)$, apply convexity at the midpoint of $F+2th$ and $F+2tk$ and let $t\downarrow0$; this gives $p(h+k)\leq p(h)+p(k)$. Thus $p$ is sublinear.

By the Hahn--Banach theorem, the zero functional on $\{0\}$ extends to a linear functional $\ell\leq p$. Since $p(h)\leq\|h\|_\infty$, applying this bound to $h$ and $-h$ shows that $\ell$ is continuous. Monotonicity of the convex difference quotients gives
\[
 C_\varphi(F+h)\geq C_\varphi(F)+\ell(h),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:adjoint-probability-set} once $\ell$ is represented by a probability. Testing the translation rule with positive and negative constants gives $\ell(1)=1$. If $h\geq0$, monotonicity of $B_\varphi$ gives
$C_\varphi(F-th)\leq C_\varphi(F)$ for $t>0$, whereas the inequality just proved gives the lower bound $C_\varphi(F)-t\ell(h)$. Hence $\ell(h)\geq0$. By the Riesz representation theorem, $\ell(h)=\int_Xh\,\mathrm{d}\nu$ for a probability measure $\nu$, and $\nu\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$. Each inequality in \eqref{eq:adjoint-probability-set} defines a weakly closed half-space in the compact space of probability measures, so their intersection is closed and convex, hence weakly compact.

It remains to prove absolute continuity. Let $K$ be compact with $\mathrm{d}V(K)=0$, and choose continuous functions $0\leq\chi_j\leq1$, equal to one on $K$, whose supports have volumes tending to zero. For any $\nu\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$, the supporting inequality with $G=F+\chi_j$ gives
\[
 0\leq\nu(K)\leq\int_X\chi_j\,\mathrm{d}\nu
 \leq B_\varphi(F)-B_\varphi(F+\chi_j).
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero by \cref{thm:normalization-bounded-lone}, so $\nu(K)=0$. By inner regularity of finite Borel measures on $X$, the same holds for every Borel set of zero volume. The Radon--Nikodym theorem now gives $\mathrm{d}\nu=h_\nu \mathrm{d}V$ with $h_\nu\geq0$ and $\int_Xh_\nu \mathrm{d}V=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:weak-adjoint-stability}\index{Adjoint measure!weak stability}
Let $F_j,F\in C(X)$ with $F_j\to F$ uniformly, and let $\nu_j\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F_j)$. Then every weak cluster point of $(\nu_j)$ belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$. In particular, if the latter set consists of a single measure, $\nu_j$ converges weakly to it.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take a weakly convergent subsequence and fix $G\in C(X)$. In the inequality
\[
 B_\varphi(G)\leq B_\varphi(F_j)-\int_X(G-F_j)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_j,
\]
the Lipschitz continuity of $B_\varphi$, the uniform convergence of $F_j$ and the weak convergence of the probabilities allow passage to the limit. The limiting inequality holds for every $G$, so the limit measure belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$. If that set is a singleton, every convergent subsequence has the same limit, and compactness of the space of probability measures proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Directional derivatives}

As $\nu$ runs through the supporting probabilities at $F$, the integral $-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu$ ranges exactly between the two one-sided derivatives of $B_\varphi$ at $F$ in the direction $G$. Consequently, differentiability is equivalent to uniqueness of the supporting probability. For $F,G\in C(X)$, write $D^+B_\varphi(F)[G]$ and $D^-B_\varphi(F)[G]$ for the limits of the difference quotient $(B_\varphi(F+tG)-B_\varphi(F))/t$ as $t\downarrow0$ and $t\uparrow0$, respectively.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:normalization-directional}
For $F,G\in C(X)$, the one-sided derivatives exist, and
\begin{align*}
 D^+B_\varphi(F)[G]&=-\max_{\nu\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F)}\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu,\\
 D^-B_\varphi(F)[G]&=-\min_{\nu\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F)}\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\end{align*}
Consequently $B_\varphi$ is G\^ateaux differentiable at $F$ if and only if $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ is a singleton. Its derivative is then $G\mapsto-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The one-sided limits exist and are finite by concavity and the Lipschitz bound. For $t>0$, every supporting probability $\nu$ at $F$ gives
\[
 \frac{B_\varphi(F+tG)-B_\varphi(F)}{t}
 \leq-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu.
\]
Conversely, choose $\nu_t\in\mathcal A_\varphi(F+tG)$ and apply its supporting inequality with the test function $F$; this bounds the same quotient below by $-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu_t$. Along a sequence $t\downarrow0$, the measures $\nu_t$ have a weak limit point, which belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ by \cref{prop:weak-adjoint-stability}. The two bounds identify the right derivative with minus the maximum. Applying this identity to $-G$ gives the formula for the left derivative.

If $\mathcal A_\varphi(F)$ is a singleton $\{\nu\}$, the two derivatives coincide for every $G$ and equal $-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\nu$, a continuous linear functional of $G$. Conversely, if the two one-sided derivatives agree for every $G$, all supporting probabilities have the same integral against every continuous function, and the Riesz representation theorem shows that they coincide.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gauduchon adjoints}

For a smooth Hermitian equation, differentiating the scalar identifies the supporting probability with the adjoint probability of the linearized operator.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:normalization-gauduchon-recovery}
Let $\theta=\omega$ be a Hermitian form, let $\varphi=0$, and let $F$ be a smooth real function. Let $u$ be the smooth solution of \eqref{eq:relative-normalization}, unique up to an additive constant by \cref{cor:bounded-positive-density-uniqueness}, and let $\mu_u$ be its Gauduchon adjoint probability from \cref{prop:adjoint-gauduchon}; for $n=1$, this is $\mu_u=(\omega+\ddc u)/\int_X\omega$. Then
\[
 \mathcal A_0(F)=\{\mu_u\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $n\geq2$. By uniqueness of the constant, the normalization scalar of the smooth equation in \cref{thm:b-derivatives} is $B_0$, and that theorem gives, for every smooth real $G$,
\[
 \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}B_0(F+tG)
 =-\int_XG\,\mathrm{d}\mu_u.
\]
Let $\nu\in\mathcal A_0(F)$. Testing the supporting inequality at $F+tG$ for both signs of $t$ and letting $t\to0$ shows that $G$ has the same integral against $\nu$ and $\mu_u$. Since smooth functions are uniformly dense in $C(X)$, it follows that $\nu=\mu_u$, and the assertion follows from nonemptiness of $\mathcal A_0(F)$. In dimension one, Stokes' theorem gives
$c_0(F)=\int_X\omega/\int_X\mathrm{e}^F \mathrm{d}V$; differentiating its logarithm in the same way yields the stated measure, since $\omega+\ddc u=c_0(F)\mathrm{e}^F\mathrm{d}V$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, in the Hermitian case the supporting probabilities at a continuous $F$ can be approached through smooth data. If smooth $F_j$ converge uniformly to $F$, then by \cref{prop:weak-adjoint-stability} every weak cluster point of the Gauduchon adjoint probabilities of the solutions for $F_j$ belongs to $\mathcal A_0(F)$, and is therefore absolutely continuous. If $\mathcal A_0(F)$ is a singleton, which holds in particular for every $F\in\mathscr U_0$, these probabilities converge to its unique member, independently of the approximation. We do not know whether $\mathcal A_0(F)$ is a singleton for every continuous $F$, even in this Hermitian setting; the same question is open for the singular references considered above.

For $n\geq2$, the Hermitian zero-reference case admits a quantitative strengthening: \cref{cor:gaud-adjoint-lq} bounds the densities of all supporting probabilities uniformly in $L^q$, for $1<q<n/(n-1)$, when $\|F\|_\infty$ is bounded, and also for $q=n/(n-1)$ when $\omega$ has positive lower mass $v_-(\omega)>0$ in the sense of \cref{def:bmp}. The proof uses the Gauduchon-current formulation of \cref{chap:gauduchon-variational-volume}, and we do not claim such a bound for an arbitrary singular reference.

A weak linearized operator requires more than a supporting probability. In the same Hermitian zero-reference setting, \cref{thm:gaud-current-all-adjoints} represents each supporting probability by a determinant-weighted positive $\ddc$-closed current. Identifying that current with a scalar multiple of a weak power $(\omega+\ddc u)^{n-1}$ would require a well-defined product and its compatibility with approximation.

A similar representation holds for a functional defined by subsolutions rather than by an equation in a prescribed singularity type. Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero with $f^{1/n}\in\mathcal D_\omega^0$, the space of densities with uniformly vanishing Gauduchon tails from \cref{subsec:bounded-root-approximation}. Then \cref{prop:integrable-threshold-adjoints} describes the supporting probabilities of $F\mapsto\log b_\omega(\mathrm{e}^Ff)$, where $b_\omega(\cdot)$ is the density threshold of \cref{thm:individual-density-subsolution}.
