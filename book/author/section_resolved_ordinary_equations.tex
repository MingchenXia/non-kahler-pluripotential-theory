\section{Equations with resolved logarithmic poles}\label{sec:prescribed}

We now prescribe a singularity type with logarithmic poles. If the poles become a divisor on a modification, the singular part can be removed there, and what remains is a smooth residual form. When this residual form is strictly positive, the bounded Hermitian theory on the modification provides the remaining bounded potential, and pushing forward solves the equation in the prescribed type.

In this section the background is a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$. Let $\psi$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function that is smooth outside a nonempty analytic set $A$, its pole set. Assume that there is a modification $\pi\colon Y\to X$, biholomorphic over $X\setminus A$, such that $\pi^{-1}(A)$ is the support $|D|$ of a simple normal crossing divisor $D=\sum_jD_j$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolved-type}
 \psi\circ\pi=\sum_j c_j\log|s_j|^2+b,
 \qquad
 \beta=\pi^*h+\ddc b-\sum_jc_j\Theta_j>0,
\end{equation}
where $c_j>0$, $b$ is smooth on $Y$, $s_j$ is a holomorphic section of $\mathcal O(D_j)$ defining $D_j$, and $\Theta_j$ is the curvature form of a smooth Hermitian metric on $\mathcal O(D_j)$, so that $\ddc\log|s_j|^2=[D_j]-\Theta_j$. Thus $\beta$ is the smooth part of the current $\pi^*h+\ddc(\psi\circ\pi)$, which we call the \emph{residual form}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:resolved-type}\index{Singularity type!resolved logarithmic poles}
For every nonzero nonnegative $f\in L^p(X,h^n)$, $p>1$, there are $\kappa>0$ and a normalized $u\in\PSH(X,h)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolved-equation}
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=\kappa f h^n,
 \qquad u=\psi+O(1).
\end{equation}
The lifted remainder $v=(u-\psi)\circ\pi$ extends H\"older continuously over $Y$, so $u$ is locally H\"older on $X\setminus A$. Its current is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolved-current}
 \pi^*h+\ddc(u\circ\pi)
 =\sum_jc_j[D_j]+\beta+\ddc v.
\end{equation}
The positive residual current $\beta+\ddc v$ has zero Lelong numbers. If $h$ is K\"ahler, then $\kappa=\int_Y\beta^n/\int_X f h^n$, and the normalized solution is unique among all normalized solutions $u'$ of \eqref{eq:resolved-equation} with $u'=\psi+O(1)$. If the density $g=(f\circ\pi)\,\pi^*h^n/\beta^n$ is smooth and strictly positive on $Y$, the residual current $\beta+\ddc v$ may be chosen smooth and strictly positive.
\end{theorem}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}
Define $J=\pi^*h^n/\beta^n$ and $g=(f\circ\pi)J$ off the exceptional set, assigning any value to $g$ on that volume-zero set. The smooth nonnegative $J$ is bounded. Change of variables on the biholomorphic locus gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prescribed-transport}
 \int_Yg\beta^n=\int_Xfh^n,
 \qquad
 \int_Yg^p\beta^n\leq \|J\|_\infty^{p-1}\int_Xf^ph^n.
\end{equation}
Thus $g$ is a nonzero $L^p$ density. \Cref{thm:kn-continuous,thm:hermitian-holder-elementary} give a bounded H\"older $\beta$-plurisubharmonic $v$ and $\kappa>0$ with $(\beta+\ddc v)^n=\kappa g\beta^n$. If $h$ is K\"ahler, then $\beta$ is K\"ahler and the mass identity fixes the stated scalar. For smooth positive $g$, \cref{thm:tw-smooth} gives a smooth solution instead.

Set $u=\psi+v\circ\pi^{-1}$ on $X\setminus A$ and $u=-\infty$ on $A$. Push forward the positive current $\sum c_j[D_j]+\beta+\ddc v$. Pushforward commutes with $\ddc$, $\pi_*\pi^*h=h$, and the pushed locally integrable potential is $u$ almost everywhere. Its quasi-plurisubharmonic representative equals $\psi+v\circ\pi^{-1}$ off $A$ and tends to $-\infty$ at $A$ because $v$ is bounded. Hence $h+\ddc u\geq0$ and $u=\psi+O(1)$ in the two-sided ordinary sense. The pulled-back identity is \eqref{eq:resolved-current}; bounded residuals have zero Lelong number. The equation holds off $A$ because $g\beta^n=(f\circ\pi)\,\pi^*h^n$ off the exceptional set, and \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma} extends it as \eqref{eq:resolved-equation}. Subtracting $\sup_Xu$ changes neither the equation nor the ordinary type.

For uniqueness when $h$ is K\"ahler, let $u'$ be a normalized solution with $u'=\psi+O(1)$. On $Y\setminus D$, $w=(u'-\psi)\circ\pi$ is bounded and $\beta$-plurisubharmonic. Since $w$ is bounded, \cref{lem:qpsh-removable-analytic} extends it across $D$ to a bounded $\beta$-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$. On each relatively compact subset of $Y\setminus D$, sufficiently deep truncations of $u'$ equal $u'$. Thus locality in \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma} gives the bounded equation $\kappa g\beta^n$ there; both sides give $D$ zero mass. The global equation follows, its mass fixes $\kappa$, and closed full-mass uniqueness gives $w=v+$constant as recalled in \cref{sec:history-regularity}. Normalization fixes that constant.
\end{proof}

The H\"older constants depend on the fixed geometry and divisor metrics, the $c_j$, a lower bound for the positivity of $\beta$, $p$, and the $L^p$ norm of the density $\kappa g$ itself. In particular, the argument gives no estimate that is uniform as $\beta$ degenerates or as $\kappa$ becomes unbounded. In simple normal crossing coordinates a density comparable to $\prod_j|z_j|^{-2a_j}$ belongs locally to $L^p$ exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:snc-integrability}
 pa_j<1\quad\hbox{for every }j,
\end{equation}
by the factors $\int_0^\epsilon r_j^{1-2pa_j}\,\mathrm{d} r_j$. A smooth positive density $f$ on $X$ need not give a strictly positive $g$ on $Y$, since the Jacobian factor $J=\pi^*h^n/\beta^n$ of the proof can vanish on the exceptional set.

\begin{example}\label{ex:projective-point-nonminimal}
Take $X=\mathbb P^n$ with $n\geq2$, $h=\lambda\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, $\int_X\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}^n=1$, and $0<c<\lambda$. Put
\[
 \psi_c=c\log\frac{\sum_{j=1}^n|Z_j|^2}{\sum_{j=0}^n|Z_j|^2}.
\]
Its pole is $P=[1:0:\cdots:0]$. On the point blowup $\pi\colon Y\to X$, let $q\colon Y\to\mathbb P^{n-1}$ be the direction map. In affine blowup coordinates $x=(z,zw)$, Poincar\'e--Lelong gives
\[
 \pi^*h+\ddc(\psi_c\circ\pi)
 =c[E]+(\lambda-c)\pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}+cq^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}.
\]
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
The residual form $\beta=(\lambda-c)\pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}+cq^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is positive: $\pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is positive in the directions normal to $E$, and $q^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ in the directions tangent to $E$. Write $H=\pi^*[\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}]$. Since $H|_E=0$ and $E^n=(-1)^{n-1}$, its mass is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-residual-mass}
 \int_Y\bigl((\lambda-c)\pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}+cq^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}\bigr)^n=\lambda^n-c^n.
\end{equation}
Hence, for every nonzero nonnegative $f\in L^p$ with $\int_Xfh^n=\lambda^n-c^n$, \cref{thm:resolved-type} gives $\kappa=1$ and a solution of $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=fh^n$ with the nonminimal type $u=\psi_c+O(1)$.

For an explicit solution, choose a smooth function $v$ on $Y$ with $v|_E$ nonconstant, small enough that $S=\beta+\ddc v$ is still positive, and put $u=\psi_c+v\circ\pi^{-1}$ off $P$. The density $f$ of its measure, given by $f\circ\pi=S^n/\pi^*h^n$ off $E$, lies in every $L^p$ with $1<p<n/(n-1)$: $\pi^*h^n$ vanishes to order $|z|^{2(n-1)}$ along $E$, so the local integral of $f^p$ is finite precisely when $(n-1)(p-1)<1$. Different directions of approach to $P$ give different limits of $u-\psi_c$. The divisor $c[E]$ of the current upstairs leaves no divisor downstairs, since $E$ is contracted to a point. On $X\setminus\{P\}$, where $u$ is smooth and strictly $h$-plurisubharmonic, the Hermitian metric $h+\ddc u$ is isometric to $(Y\setminus E,S)$. Its metric completion is $Y$ with the metric $S$, so the whole divisor $E$ is restored: removing the smooth real codimension-two submanifold $E$ does not change the infimum of path lengths in $Y$. Finally, if $h$ is replaced by a sufficiently small nonclosed perturbation, both $h$ and the residual form remain positive, so the existence part of \cref{thm:resolved-type} still applies.
\end{example}

\subsection{Residual mass}\label{sec:mass-deficit}

When $h$ is not K\"ahler, the scalar in \cref{thm:resolved-type} is still determined by masses if $h$ satisfies the power condition $\ddc(h^k)=0$, $1\leq k\leq n-1$, of \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization}, which fixes the mass of every bounded solution. The condition persists under pullback and under addition of a smooth closed form: expand each power and apply the original identities to its terms. For $n=2$ the condition is simply $\ddc h=0$, so it includes the nonclosed Gauduchon metric $\alpha+\zeta$ on the Hopf surface $X_2$ of \cref{sec:hopf-homogeneous-sections}. The residual form then has strictly smaller mass than $h$, the difference being carried by the divisor.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mass-deficit}\index{Monge--Ampere mass@Monge--Amp\`ere mass!divisorial deficit}
Under \eqref{eq:resolved-type}, assume $\ddc(h^k)=0$ for $1\leq k\leq n-1$, and put $H=\pi^*h$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass-deficit}
 \int_Xh^n-\int_Y\beta^n
 =\sum_jc_j\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
   \int_{D_j}H^i\wedge\beta^{n-1-i}>0.
\end{equation}
Thus the constructed solution of \cref{thm:resolved-type} has $\kappa=\int_Y\beta^n/\int_Xfh^n$. If $\int_Xfh^n=\int_Xh^n$, its scalar is strictly less than one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $C=\sum_jc_j\Theta_j-\ddc b$, so $\beta=H-C$ with $C$ closed. The mixed forms $Q_i=H^i\wedge\beta^{n-1-i}$ satisfy $\ddc Q_i=0$: expand $\beta=H-C$ and apply the power condition to each $H^k$. With $\Psi=\psi\circ\pi$, $\ddc\Psi=\sum_jc_j[D_j]-C$. Distributional integration by parts against the smooth $\ddc$-closed $Q_i$ gives
\[
 \int_Y(H^n-\beta^n)=\sum_i\int_Y C\wedge Q_i
   =\sum_jc_j\sum_i\int_{D_j}Q_i.
\]
Change of variables gives $\int_YH^n=\int_Xh^n$. Every integrand on the right is nonnegative, since $H$ is semipositive and $\beta$ positive; the $i=0$ term on each nonempty $D_j$ is strictly positive. The same expansion of $\beta=H-C$ gives $\ddc(\beta^k)=0$ for $1\leq k\leq n-1$, so by \cref{thm:mass-invariance-characterization} the residual solution has mass $\int_Y\beta^n$, giving the scalar.
\end{proof}
