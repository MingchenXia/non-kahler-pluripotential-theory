\chapter{Uniqueness for degenerate equations}
\label{chap:degenerate-uniqueness}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, let $\omega$ be a Hermitian form, and let $\mathrm dV$ be a smooth positive volume form. For a nonzero nonnegative density $f\in L^p(X,\mathrm dV)$, $p>1$, the preceding chapter shows that $f$ determines the constant $c$ in $(\omega+\ddc u)^n=cf\,\mathrm dV$. We now ask whether the potential is determined up to a constant.

For real potentials $u,v$, with the regularity stated in each result, set
\[
 A=\omega+\ddc u,\qquad B=\omega+\ddc v,\qquad w=u-v,
 \qquad T=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j\wedge B^{n-1-j}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{uni-eq:linearization}
 \ddc w\wedge T=A^n-B^n.
\end{equation}

If $A,B\geq0$ and $A^n=B^n$, then $w$ solves the linear equation $\ddc w\wedge T=0$. Its polarized cofactor $T$ is semipositive, but it may be degenerate and need not be closed. Coefficient products for Sobolev potentials are interpreted almost everywhere. For $C^{1,1}$ solutions with a common continuous density, perturbing $w$ locates its extrema in regions where this equation is uniformly elliptic, and the maximum principle propagates the extremal values over the corresponding components.

For $C^2$ potentials, a continuous weight $h>0$ with $\ddc(hT)=0$ in distributions gives a global energy identity forcing $\mathrm dw$ to vanish in the positive directions of the cofactor.

For bounded solutions, an energy measure replaces the pointwise gradient calculation. A continuous density whose positive set is connected and dense then gives uniqueness. When the background has invariant mass, ordinary comparison proves uniqueness for nonzero nonnegative $L^p$ densities, $p>1$, even if the density vanishes on an open set.

The examples on $\mathbb P^3$ have the bounded mass property and a smooth nonclosed Hermitian background, yet distinct smooth normalized solutions share one density. Its zero set separates the elliptic regions: it can be an open band or two hypersurfaces with quadratic vanishing, even for a real analytic density. Finite upper mass therefore does not ensure uniqueness.
