\chapter{Uniqueness for degenerate equations}
\label{chap:degenerate-uniqueness}

The normalization constant is unique, but two potentials with the same density can differ by more than a constant. Their difference solves a linear equation whose cofactor can lose ellipticity where the density vanishes. Extremal values propagate through its elliptic regions, while a $\ddc$-closed weighted cofactor gives a global energy identity. For bounded potentials, an energy measure replaces the pointwise gradient calculation.

Connectedness of the positive-density region gives uniqueness under the regularity hypotheses below, whereas two zero hypersurfaces can separate regions carrying different extremal values. Radial constructions on projective three-space realize this separation even for smooth solutions and a real analytic density with quadratic zeros.

Throughout the uniqueness arguments, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, $\omega$ is a Hermitian form, and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive volume form. For real potentials $u,v$, of the regularity stated in each result, set
\[
 A=\omega+\ddc u,\qquad B=\omega+\ddc v,\qquad w=u-v,
 \qquad T=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j\wedge B^{n-1-j}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{uni-eq:linearization}
 \ddc w\wedge T=A^n-B^n.
\end{equation}
When $A,B\geq0$, the polarized cofactor $T$ is semipositive. It may be degenerate and need not be closed. For Sobolev or $C^{1,1}$ potentials, coefficient products in these identities are understood almost everywhere.
