\chapter{Uniqueness for degenerate equations}
\label{chap:degenerate-uniqueness}

The preceding chapter proves uniqueness of the normalization constant for continuous solutions of the Hermitian Monge--Amp\`ere equation. The normalized potential need not be unique. Degeneracy can separate the regions on which a difference of solutions attains its extreme values. The first part of this chapter gives conditions that prevent this separation or make the difference constant by integration: connectedness of the positive-density region for both differentiable and bounded potentials, propagation along the polarized cofactor, low-dimensional identities, and bounded comparison under the Guan--Li condition.

The second part constructs nonunique solutions on projective three-space. An open region of zero density first gives freedom in a scalar equation. We then reduce the zero set to two hypersurfaces, make the vanishing quadratic, and finally prescribe a real analytic density. These examples distinguish positivity almost everywhere from the hypotheses needed for uniqueness and show that the square-root density threshold proved below is sharp.

Throughout the uniqueness arguments, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, $\omega$ is a Hermitian form, and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive volume form. For real potentials $u,v$, of the regularity stated in each result, set
\[
 A=\omega+\ddc u,\qquad B=\omega+\ddc v,\qquad w=u-v,
 \qquad T=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j\wedge B^{n-1-j}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{uni-eq:linearization}
 \ddc w\wedge T=A^n-B^n.
\end{equation}
When $A,B\geq0$, the polarized cofactor $T$ is semipositive. It may be degenerate and need not be closed. For Sobolev or $C^{1,1}$ potentials, coefficient products in these identities are understood almost everywhere.
