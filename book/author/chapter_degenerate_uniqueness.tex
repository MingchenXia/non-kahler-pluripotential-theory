\chapter{Uniqueness for degenerate equations}
\label{chap:degenerate-uniqueness}

Equality of Monge--Amp\`ere measures need not determine a Hermitian potential up to a constant. The obstruction persists for smooth densities that are positive almost everywhere. This chapter studies the additional hypotheses that restore uniqueness: the geometry of the positive-density region, a condition on the polarized cofactor, low-dimensional integration identities, or comparison on the equation background.

We first prove the positive results and then construct examples with progressively more restrictive zero sets, ending with a prescribed real analytic density. The regularity of the potential and that of the density are stated separately in each result. Throughout, $\omega$ is Hermitian and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive volume form. Set
\[
 A=\omega+\ddc u,\qquad B=\omega+\ddc v,\qquad w=u-v,
 \qquad T=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j\wedge B^{n-1-j}.
\]
The factorization
\begin{equation}\label{uni-eq:linearization}
 \ddc w\wedge T=A^n-B^n
\end{equation}
relates the nonlinear equation to a linear one. The positive form $T$ is its polarized cofactor; it may be degenerate and need not be closed.

\section{Regularity and the remaining questions}
The connected positive-density criterion above applies to $C^{1,1}$ potentials and a continuous density. Its extension to arbitrary bounded potentials is a different question: the proof uses second-order contact and a locally bounded measurable cofactor. The Sobolev argument has its own dimension and torsion assumptions. The bounded comparison theorem, in contrast, allows the density to vanish on an open set but imposes the Guan--Li condition on $\omega$ itself.

The analytic example has three positive-density components. It therefore does not contradict the connectedness criterion. It also leaves the unique scaling constant intact. These distinctions will remain essential when equations are considered in a prescribed singularity class.

The same analytic examples belong to the minimal envelope class and have positive lower and finite upper volume. \Cref{cor:analytic-nonuniqueness-envelope-class} verifies these assertions after the relative classes have been defined.
