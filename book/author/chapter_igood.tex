\chapter{Multiplier-ideal envelopes and \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal I$}{I}-goodness}
\label{chap:hermitian-igood}

An ordinary singularity type determines all multiplier ideals, but the converse can fail. The two associated envelopes measure this difference: the ordinary envelope retains the singularity bound, while the ideal envelope retains only integrability at every weight. Analytic singularities give the same envelope in both constructions. More generally, the envelopes of dyadic analytic regularizations decrease to the ideal envelope. This convergence gives a criterion for envelope equality in a Hermitian background. The curve examples explain why neither the $L^1$ distance between ideal envelopes nor the normalizing scalars of the preceding chapter recover the singularity topology of the K\"ahler theory.

\section{The K\"ahler characterization}

Let $X$ be a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $n$, let $\theta$ be a smooth closed big real $(1,1)$-form, and let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have positive non-pluripolar mass. Write $\mathrm{MA}_\theta$ for the non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure. Use the ordinary envelope $P_\theta[u]$ of \cref{def:singularity-envelope} and the multiplier-ideal envelope $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ defined in \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope}. Let $d_S$ be the singularity pseudometric of \cite[Section~6.2]{SGPT}. The characterization of $\mathcal I$-good singularities in \cite[Theorem~7.1.1]{SGPT} states the equivalence of
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 u\text{ lies in the }d_S\text{-closure of analytic singularities},\\
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
  =\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}),\\
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The last condition involves envelopes alone. It therefore gives a starting point for the Hermitian problem even when the metric and mass characterizations are unavailable.

The integral on the right in the middle condition is the \emph{multiplier-ideal volume} in \cite{SGPT}. The upper and lower volumes of \cref{def:singularity-volumes} instead vary the potential within its ordinary type. This distinction matters in a nonclosed background, where even bounded changes can alter total mass.

\section{An envelope defined by multiplier ideals}

From now on, let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. We use the ordinary and ideal singularity orders from \cref{chap:framework}. Fix a smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$ for $L^1$ norms, and write $\mathrm{MA}_\theta$ for the extended non-pluripolar measure. The ideal envelope chooses the greatest potential below zero in a prescribed ideal class.

\begin{definition}\label{def:hermitian-i-envelope}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ define
\[
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}
 :=\left(\sup\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):v\leq0,
                   \ v\sim_{\mathcal I}u\}\right)^*.
\]
\end{definition}

The family is nonempty because $u-\sup_Xu$ belongs to it; bounded perturbations do not change multiplier ideals. It is bounded above by zero, so its regularized envelope is a genuine $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function. It depends only on the multiplier-ideal equivalence class of $u$.

The local ideal calculus of \cref{thm:local-i-inputs,lem:hermitian-i-calculus} shows that taking this supremum preserves every multiplier ideal.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-i-projection}
For every smooth real background $\theta$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}\sim_{\mathcal I}u,\qquad
 P_\theta[P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}]_{\mathcal I}=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
The regularization in \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope} does not change the pointwise supremum. Moreover,
\[
 \sup_X P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. By Choquet's lemma choose a sequence from the defining family whose regularized supremum is $q$. Replace it by successive finite maxima. By \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus}, the resulting sequence $v_j$ remains in the family, is increasing, and converges to $q$ almost everywhere.

Fix $x\in X$ and $\lambda>0$. On a coordinate ball about $x$, choose one smooth function $r$ such that every $v_j+r$ and $q+r$ is plurisubharmonic. This is possible because they all have the same smooth curvature lower bound $-\theta$. The increasing-limit assertion of \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} gives
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda(q+r))_x
   =\mathcal I(\lambda(v_j+r))_x
   =\mathcal I(\lambda u)_x
\]
for large $j$; smooth additions give the second equality. Hence $q\sim_{\mathcal I}u$.

Since $q\leq0$, it now belongs to its own defining family. The pointwise supremum is at least $q$ and at most its regularization $q$, proving the assertion about regularization. The defining families for $P_\theta[q]_{\mathcal I}$ and $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ coincide, which proves idempotence. Finally, if $\sup_Xq<0$, the function $q-\sup_Xq$ would be a larger competitor, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:p-below-i}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 u-\sup_Xu\leq P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}\leq V_\theta.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Only the middle inequality needs explanation. Let $h\leq0$ be $\theta$-plurisubharmonic with $h\preceq u$, and set
\[
 h'=\max(h,u-\sup_Xu).
\]
It belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$, lies below zero, dominates $h$, and differs from $u$ by a bounded amount. Hence $h'\sim_{\mathcal I}u$, so $h\leq P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Take the regularized supremum over $h$. The last inequality holds because every competitor for $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ is a competitor for $V_\theta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 P_\theta[u]\sim_{\mathcal I}u,\qquad
 P_\theta[P_\theta[u]]_{\mathcal I}=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 P_\theta[P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The inequalities of \cref{prop:p-below-i} and monotonicity of local integrability give, for every $\lambda>0$,
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda u)
 \subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda P_\theta[u])
 \subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I})
 =\mathcal I(\lambda u).
\]
This proves the first assertion, and equality of the defining families proves the second. For $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, the inequalities $q\leq P_\theta[q]\leq P_\theta[q]_{\mathcal I}=q$ prove the last assertion; the first inequality uses $q\leq0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:i-envelope-finite}
If $\theta$ is semipositive and big, then $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ has finite extended Monge--Amp\`ere mass for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The last identity in \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i} makes $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ an ordinary envelope fixed point. Apply the fixed-point mass estimate in \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite}.
\end{proof}

The finiteness statement concerns the envelope. It does not imply finiteness for every function with the same multiplier ideals, as \cref{prop:goodness-infinite-mass} below demonstrates.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:i-envelope-operations}
For $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 u\preceq_{\mathcal I}v\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}\leq P_\theta[v]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
For $a>0$ one has $P_{a\theta}[a u]_{\mathcal I}=aP_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. For smooth real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta_i$ and potentials $u_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i)$, $i=1,2$,
\[
 P_{\theta_1}[u_1]_{\mathcal I}+P_{\theta_2}[u_2]_{\mathcal I}
 \leq P_{\theta_1+\theta_2}[u_1+u_2]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $h$ is a competitor for $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, the reverse Lelong inequalities in \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} show that $\max(h,v-\sup_Xv)$ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $v$. It is below zero and dominates $h$. Taking the supremum proves order preservation. Scaling gives a bijection of the two defining families. For addition, the sum of the two envelopes is below zero, is $(\theta_1+\theta_2)$-plurisubharmonic, and is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u_1+u_2$ by projection and \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus}. It is therefore an admissible competitor on the right.
\end{proof}

For an analytic singularity, the ideal bound recovers the ordinary bound by \cref{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance}. This yields the following extension of \cite[Proposition~3.2.10]{SGPT}.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:analytic-envelope-type}
Let $\theta$ be any smooth real background, and let $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have analytic singularities. Then
\[
 a\sim P_\theta[a]=P_\theta[a]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
In particular the common envelope has analytic singularities with the same coefficient and ideal as $a$, up to a bounded remainder.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $q=P_\theta[a]_{\mathcal I}$. Projection gives $q\sim_{\mathcal I}a$, so \cref{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance} gives $q\preceq a$. Since $q\leq0$, it is a competitor for $P_\theta[a]$. Combine this with \cref{prop:p-below-i} to obtain
\[
 a-\sup_Xa\leq P_\theta[a]\leq q\leq P_\theta[a].
\]
The two envelopes are equal and ordinarily equivalent to $a$. Their bounded difference from $a$ preserves the analytic presentation.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:i-envelope-gauge}
Let $\theta$ be a smooth real background, $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, and $f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$. Set $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$, $u'=u-f$, $m=\min_Xf$ and $M=\max_Xf$. Then
\[
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}+m
 \leq P_{\theta'}[u']_{\mathcal I}+f
 \leq P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}+M.
\]
The identical inequalities hold for ordinary envelopes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $v$ is a competitor for $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, then $v-f+m$ is $\theta'$-plurisubharmonic, below zero, and multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u'$. Therefore
$v\leq P_{\theta'}[u']_{\mathcal I}+f-m$.
Taking the envelope gives the first inequality. Conversely, a competitor $v'$ in the new background gives $v'+f-M$ in the old one, which proves the second inequality. For $P$, use the ordinary singularity order instead of multiplier-ideal equivalence in the same calculation.
\end{proof}

Thus a smooth change of representative preserves the ordinary singularity type of the enveloped current. The constants in the comparison come from transporting the zero obstacle.

\section{Dependence of envelope mass on the background}

For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, put
\[
 W_\theta(u)=\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I})\in[0,+\infty].
\]
The gauge comparison preserves the ordinary type of the enveloped current. In a nonclosed background, this is insufficient to preserve its mass. The failure already occurs for smooth potentials.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:raw-i-volume-gauge-failure}
There are two smooth decompositions of the same positive current for which the corresponding values of $W$ are different.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the Hermitian form $\omega$ and smooth function $g=u_t$ from \cref{ex:torus-mass}, choosing $t\ne0$ sufficiently small. Both $\omega$ and $\omega'=\omega+\ddc g$ are Hermitian, and
\[
 \omega+\ddc0=\omega'+\ddc(-g).
\]
For a smooth potential in a Hermitian background, the multiplier ideals at all weights are trivial and zero is an admissible competitor for its multiplier-ideal envelope. That envelope is therefore zero. Consequently
\[
 W_\omega(0)=\int_X\omega^2,\qquad
 W_{\omega'}(-g)=\int_X(\omega+\ddc g)^2.
\]
The two integrals differ by the explicit formula \eqref{eq:torus-mass}.
\end{proof}

The underlying torus is K\"ahler; it is the chosen nonclosed background that causes this dependence. In the closed theory, invariance of mass under bounded changes supplies the further step after \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge}.

The comparison with finite upper and lower volumes under bounded mass is developed in \cref{chap:mass-bound-volumes}.

\section{Envelope equality and infinite mass}

The equality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background-goodness}
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}
\end{equation}
is meaningful for each $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. We call it \emph{envelope goodness in the fixed background}. Its relation to mass differs from the K\"ahler characterization: it can hold even when the potential has infinite mass and its envelope has finite mass.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:goodness-infinite-mass}
If a Hermitian metric $\omega$ fails bounded mass, there exists $U\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ such that
\[
 P_\omega[U]=P_\omega[U]_{\mathcal I}=0,
 \qquad U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0),
 \qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(U)(X)=+\infty.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $U$ from \cref{prop:infinite-envelope-class}. Its ordinary envelope is zero and its multiplier ideals are trivial at every weight. Zero is consequently a competitor for $P_\omega[U]_{\mathcal I}$, and all such competitors are below zero. Thus $P_\omega[U]_{\mathcal I}=0$. The other properties have already been proved.
\end{proof}

For this potential, the envelope mass $W_\omega(U)=\int_X\omega^n$ is finite, while the mass of $U$ is infinite. Thus \eqref{eq:background-goodness} does not imply the mass identity in the K\"ahler characterization for arbitrary Hermitian potentials.

Positive divisorial currents give envelope equality through an ordinary upper bound: preserving their Lelong numbers forces the ideal envelope to have the original singularity type.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:divisorial-envelope-equality}
Let $S$ be a closed positive divisorial current of finite mass on a connected compact complex manifold $Z$, and choose a smooth residual decomposition $S=\beta+\ddc v$ as in \cref{cor:divisorial-current-ordinary-type}. For every Hermitian form $\Omega$ with $v\in\PSH(Z,\Omega)$,
\[
 P_\Omega[v]=P_\Omega[v]_{\mathcal I}\sim v.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $S=\sum_jc_j[D_j]$ with distinct prime divisors $D_j$ and $c_j>0$.
By \cref{thm:hermitian-i-projection}, the ideal envelope preserves all multiplier ideals, so its generic Lelong number on each $D_j$ is still $c_j$. \Cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination} gives $P_\Omega[v]_{\mathcal I}\preceq v$. Also $P_\Omega[v]_{\mathcal I}\geq v-\sup v$, so these functions are ordinarily equivalent. Consequently $P_\Omega[v]_{\mathcal I}$ is admissible in the ordinary envelope of $v$, giving $P_\Omega[v]_{\mathcal I}\leq P_\Omega[v]$. The reverse inequality is \cref{prop:p-below-i}. This proves the envelope identity and ordinary equivalence.
\end{proof}

The current may have infinitely many components. Exact curve-fibration trace types are examples; \cref{prop:exact-trace-infinite-representatives} still gives representatives of the same ideal class with different finiteness of Monge--Amp\`ere mass.

\section{A topology on ordinary singularity types}

In a Hermitian background $\omega$, the preceding construction approximates every ideal class by analytic classes. Envelope goodness asks for more: the same approximation must recover the ordinary envelope. A topology on ordinary singularity types should record this additional requirement.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{problem}\label{qu:i-good-no-bmp}
Construct a topology on ordinary singularity types in arbitrary Hermitian backgrounds for which the closure of analytic singularities is exactly the set satisfying $P_\omega[u]=P_\omega[u]_{\mathcal I}$. It should recover the dyadic-envelope criterion of \cref{cor:dyadic-goodness-criterion,cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density}, remain meaningful for infinite extended mass, and specify how convergence behaves under smooth changes of representative and background.
\end{problem}

The dyadic criterion concerns approximants whose ideals are tied to those of the limit. For arbitrary families, the ideal metric cannot distinguish envelope goodness, since analytic classes are dense everywhere by \cref{cor:fixed-hermitian-analytic-density}. Appending weak convergence of the damped measures from \cref{chap:damped-measures} to capacity convergence also adds no condition: their capacity-continuity theorem already gives that weak convergence. A suitable topology must retain further control of singularities, such as ideal inclusions at each fixed weight. Its construction and the behavior of envelope goodness under changes of background remain open.
