\chapter{Multiplier-ideal envelopes and \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal I$}{I}-goodness}
\label{chap:hermitian-igood}\index{I-goodness@$\mathcal I$-goodness}

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, and let $\theta$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Fix a smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$ for $L^1$ norms. We use the extended non-pluripolar measure.

Multiplier ideals record weighted holomorphic integrability. They can agree for two potentials whose ordinary envelopes differ, so passing from an ordinary type to an ideal class loses information. Dyadic analytic approximation makes this loss visible: the associated envelopes converge to the greatest representative of the ideal class below zero, which may exceed the ordinary envelope of the original potential.

In the K\"ahler theory, equality of these envelopes also has a mass characterization. For a nonclosed background, their equality remains meaningful even when the original potential has infinite mass. We therefore track both envelopes when studying analytic approximation. Curve examples show why this still does not ensure continuity of mass or completeness: diffuse polar curvature can disappear in the limit. They also explain the role of the obstacle when the background changes.

\section{The K\"ahler characterization}

First suppose $X$ is K\"ahler, $\theta$ is a smooth closed big real $(1,1)$-form, and $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ has positive non-pluripolar mass. Use $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ for the ideal envelope defined in \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope}, and let $d_S$ be the singularity pseudometric of \cite[Section~6.2]{SGPT}. The characterization of $\mathcal I$-good singularities in \cite[Theorem~7.1.1]{SGPT} states the equivalence of
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 u\text{ lies in the }d_S\text{-closure of analytic singularities},\\
 \int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)
  =\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}),\\
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The last condition involves envelopes alone. It therefore gives a starting point for the Hermitian problem even when the metric and mass characterizations are unavailable.

The integral on the right in the middle condition is the \emph{multiplier-ideal volume} in \cite{SGPT}. The upper and lower volumes of \cref{def:singularity-volumes} instead vary the potential within its ordinary type. This distinction matters in a nonclosed background, where even bounded changes can alter total mass.

\section{Ideal projection}

Return to the general compact complex setting at the chapter's opening. Taking the supremum over an ideal class could enlarge its multiplier ideals. Increasing-limit stability prevents this and makes the envelope a projection.

\begin{definition}\label{def:hermitian-i-envelope}\index{Envelope!multiplier ideal}\index{Multiplier ideal!envelope}
For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ define
\[
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}
 :=\left(\sup\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):v\leq0,
                   \ v\sim_{\mathcal I}u\}\right)^*.
\]
\end{definition}

The family is nonempty because $u-\sup_Xu$ belongs to it; bounded perturbations do not change multiplier ideals. It is bounded above by zero, so its regularized envelope is a genuine $\theta$-plurisubharmonic function. It depends only on the multiplier-ideal equivalence class of $u$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hermitian-i-projection}\index{Envelope!ideal projection}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}\sim_{\mathcal I}u,\qquad
 P_\theta[P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}]_{\mathcal I}=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
The regularization in \cref{def:hermitian-i-envelope} does not change the pointwise supremum. Moreover,
\[
 \sup_X P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. By Choquet's lemma choose a sequence from the defining family whose regularized supremum is $q$. Replace it by successive finite maxima. By \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus}, the resulting sequence $v_j$ remains in the family, is increasing, and converges to $q$ almost everywhere.

Fix $x\in X$ and $\lambda>0$. On a coordinate ball about $x$, choose one smooth function $r$ such that every $v_j+r$ and $q+r$ is plurisubharmonic. This is possible because they all have the same smooth curvature lower bound $-\theta$. The increasing-limit assertion of \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} gives
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda(q+r))_x
   =\mathcal I(\lambda(v_j+r))_x
   =\mathcal I(\lambda u)_x
\]
for large $j$; smooth additions give the second equality. Hence $q\sim_{\mathcal I}u$.

Since $q\leq0$, it now belongs to its own defining family. The pointwise supremum is at least $q$ and at most its regularization $q$, proving the assertion about regularization. The defining families for $P_\theta[q]_{\mathcal I}$ and $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ coincide, which proves idempotence. Finally, if $\sup_Xq<0$, the function $q-\sup_Xq$ would be a larger competitor, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:p-below-i}\label{cor:ordinary-preserves-i}
For every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 u-\sup_Xu\leq P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}\leq V_\theta.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_\theta[u]\sim_{\mathcal I}u,\qquad
 P_\theta[P_\theta[u]]_{\mathcal I}=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\\
 P_\theta[P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a competitor $h\leq0$ with $h\preceq u$ in the ordinary envelope, the function $\max(h,u-\sup_Xu)$ is below zero, ordinarily equivalent to $u$, and dominates $h$. It is therefore admissible for the ideal envelope. Taking regularized suprema gives $P_\theta[u]\leq P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$; the two outer bounds follow from the defining families.

Projection and monotonicity of local integrability now give, for every $\lambda>0$,
\[
 \mathcal I(\lambda u)
 \subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda P_\theta[u])
 \subseteq\mathcal I(\lambda P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I})
 =\mathcal I(\lambda u).
\]
Thus the ordinary envelope preserves all multiplier ideals and has the same ideal envelope as $u$. For $q=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, the already proved bounds and idempotence give $q\leq P_\theta[q]\leq P_\theta[q]_{\mathcal I}=q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:i-envelope-finite}
If $\theta$ is semipositive and big, then $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ has finite extended Monge--Amp\`ere mass for every $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The last identity in \cref{cor:ordinary-preserves-i} makes $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ an ordinary envelope fixed point. Apply the fixed-point mass estimate in \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:i-envelope-operations}
For $u,v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$,
\[
 u\preceq_{\mathcal I}v\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}\leq P_\theta[v]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
For $a>0$ one has $P_{a\theta}[a u]_{\mathcal I}=aP_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$. For smooth real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta_i$ and potentials $u_i\in\PSH(X,\theta_i)$, $i=1,2$,
\[
 P_{\theta_1}[u_1]_{\mathcal I}+P_{\theta_2}[u_2]_{\mathcal I}
 \leq P_{\theta_1+\theta_2}[u_1+u_2]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $h$ is a competitor for $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, the reverse Lelong inequalities in \cref{thm:local-i-inputs} show that $\max(h,v-\sup_Xv)$ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $v$. It is below zero and dominates $h$. Taking the supremum proves order preservation. Scaling gives a bijection of the two defining families. For addition, the sum of the two envelopes is below zero, is $(\theta_1+\theta_2)$-plurisubharmonic, and is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u_1+u_2$ by projection and \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus}. It is therefore an admissible competitor on the right.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Analytic and divisorial singularities}

For an analytic singularity, the ideal bound forces ordinary domination by \cref{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance}. The ideal envelope is then a competitor for the ordinary envelope, giving their equality. This extends \cite[Proposition~3.2.10]{SGPT} to smooth backgrounds.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:analytic-envelope-type}\index{Envelope!analytic singularities}
Let $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ have analytic singularities. Then
\[
 a\sim P_\theta[a]=P_\theta[a]_{\mathcal I}.
\]
In particular the common envelope has analytic singularities with the same coefficient and ideal as $a$, up to a bounded remainder.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $q=P_\theta[a]_{\mathcal I}$. Projection gives $q\sim_{\mathcal I}a$, so \cref{lem:analytic-ideal-dominance} gives $q\preceq a$. Since $q\leq0$, it is a competitor for $P_\theta[a]$. Combine this with \cref{prop:p-below-i} to obtain
\[
 a-\sup_Xa\leq P_\theta[a]\leq q\leq P_\theta[a].
\]
The two envelopes are equal and ordinarily equivalent to $a$. Their bounded difference from $a$ preserves the analytic presentation.
\end{proof}

The same argument applies to a countable divisorial current of finite mass, using \cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:divisorial-envelope-equality}
Let $S$ be a closed positive divisorial current of finite mass on a connected compact complex manifold $Z$, and choose a smooth residual decomposition $S=\beta+\ddc v$ as in \cref{cor:divisorial-current-ordinary-type}. For every Hermitian form $\Omega$ with $v\in\PSH(Z,\Omega)$,
\[
 P_\Omega[v]=P_\Omega[v]_{\mathcal I}\sim v.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $q=P_\Omega[v]_{\mathcal I}$. Projection gives $q\sim_{\mathcal I}v$, and \cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination} gives $q\preceq v$. Thus $q$ is admissible for $P_\Omega[v]$. Together with \cref{prop:p-below-i}, this yields $v-\sup_Zv\leq P_\Omega[v]=q\preceq v$.
\end{proof}

\section{Changes of background and envelope mass}

A smooth change of representative transports the current but changes the zero obstacle. The resulting envelopes remain ordinarily equivalent after transport. For a nonclosed background, their masses can nevertheless differ.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:i-envelope-gauge}
Let $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and $f\in C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)$. Set $\theta'=\theta+\ddc f$, $u'=u-f$, $m=\min_Xf$ and $M=\max_Xf$. Then
\[
 P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}+m
 \leq P_{\theta'}[u']_{\mathcal I}+f
 \leq P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}+M.
\]
The identical inequalities hold for ordinary envelopes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $v$ is a competitor for $P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$, then $v-f+m$ is $\theta'$-plurisubharmonic, below zero, and multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u'$. Therefore
$v\leq P_{\theta'}[u']_{\mathcal I}+f-m$.
Taking the envelope gives the first inequality. Conversely, a competitor $v'$ in the new background gives $v'+f-M$ in the old one, which proves the second inequality. For $P$, use the ordinary singularity order instead of multiplier-ideal equivalence in the same calculation.
\end{proof}

For $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, put
\[
 W_\theta(u)=\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\theta(P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I})\in[0,+\infty].
\]
The following example shows that this quantity depends on the decomposition of the current, even for smooth potentials.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:raw-i-volume-gauge-failure}
There are two smooth decompositions of the same positive current for which the corresponding values of $W$ are different.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the Hermitian form $\omega$ and smooth function $g=u_t$ from \cref{ex:torus-mass}, choosing $t\ne0$ sufficiently small. Both $\omega$ and $\omega'=\omega+\ddc g$ are Hermitian, and
\[
 \omega+\ddc0=\omega'+\ddc(-g).
\]
For a smooth potential in a Hermitian background, the multiplier ideals at all weights are trivial and zero is an admissible competitor for its multiplier-ideal envelope. That envelope is therefore zero. Consequently
\[
 W_\omega(0)=\int_X\omega^2,\qquad
 W_{\omega'}(-g)=\int_X(\omega+\ddc g)^2.
\]
The two integrals differ by the explicit formula \eqref{eq:torus-mass}.
\end{proof}

The underlying torus is K\"ahler; it is the chosen nonclosed background that causes this dependence. In the closed theory, invariance of mass under bounded changes supplies the further step after \cref{prop:i-envelope-gauge}.

Singularity volumes instead range over the entire ordinary type, so the gauge bijection of competitors preserves them. Under bounded mass, \cref{prop:model-comparison-gauge} consequently transports positive-volume models while preserving their upper-volume comparison.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\subsection{Envelope equality and infinite mass}

The equality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background-goodness}
 P_\theta[u]=P_\theta[u]_{\mathcal I}
\end{equation}
is meaningful for each $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. We call it \emph{envelope goodness in the fixed background}.\index{I-goodness@$\mathcal I$-goodness!envelope equality} Its relation to mass differs from the K\"ahler characterization: it can hold even when the potential has infinite mass and its envelope has finite mass.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:goodness-infinite-mass}
If a Hermitian metric $\omega$ fails bounded mass, there exists $U\in\PSH(X,\omega)$ such that
\[
 P_\omega[U]=P_\omega[U]_{\mathcal I}=0,
 \qquad U\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega,0),
 \qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega(U)(X)=+\infty.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $U$ from \cref{prop:infinite-envelope-class}, which supplies its ordinary envelope, class membership and infinite mass. Since $P_\omega[U]=V_\omega=0$, \cref{prop:p-below-i} gives $P_\omega[U]_{\mathcal I}=0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Complete model topologies}

On a curve, total variation of polar curvature gives a complete metric on ordinary models, and ideal projection takes the atomic part. The diffuse polar mass is exactly the distance to the analytic closure, by \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness,prop:curve-analytic-distance-defect}. This topology is stronger than $\tau_{\omega,0}$ on ordinary models: \cref{prop:curve-ordinary-topology-incomplete} exhibits the diffuse concentration lost by the latter.

For a smooth big background under bounded mass, \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric} gives a complete topology on models of positive lower volume. Its ideal-model locus is the completion of the positive-volume analytic models by \cref{cor:positive-ideal-metric-completion}.

\Cref{prop:divisorial-product-polar-metric} gives a restricted higher-dimensional extension at zero volume. On its divisorial product locus, polar curvature on the curve base supplies a complete metric with analytic closure exactly at the ideal models, although the top-degree volume comparison vanishes identically. \Cref{cor:nonclosed-product-polar-metric} gives the same restricted metric and analytic closure in a positive nonclosed projective background. \Cref{cor:degenerate-product-polar-metric} preserves this locus, metric and ideal projection when the second curve coefficient vanishes and the limiting background is not big. \Cref{prop:separated-product-polar-metric} gives a complete sum metric on the locus induced by several independent curve models, with the same ideal-projection and analytic-closure properties. \Cref{cor:nonclosed-separated-product-metric} preserves that several-curve locus and metric through a nonclosed auxiliary coefficient and its nonbig zero limit. \Cref{prop:weighted-product-polar-metric} gives compatible weighted metrics when the curve coefficients vary, discarding zero coordinates and proving uniform $L^1$ collapse when all curve areas vanish. Extending this metric to general ordinary models and preserving background compatibility remains open.

\begin{problem}\label{qu:i-good-no-bmp}
Extend the complete topology of \cref{thm:positive-model-complete-metric} to ordinary models of zero lower volume and beyond bounded mass, retaining analytic closure exactly at the ideal models. Recover the curve topology of \cref{cor:curve-model-completeness} and the background compatibility of \cref{sec:model-background-comparison,sec:two-sided-model-backgrounds}, including higher-dimensional limits that lose bigness or positive lower volume.
\end{problem}
