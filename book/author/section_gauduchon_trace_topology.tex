\section{Finite trace measures and the topology of potentials}
\label{sec:gauduchon-trace-topology}

A fixed Gauduchon metric gives more than a bound for the linear mass. Its trace measure determines the potential up to a constant. This provides finite measure coordinates on the entire normalized potential space, even when top-degree Monge--Amp\`ere mass is infinite. The coordinates retain the full potential, so they do not descend to ordinary or multiplier-ideal singularity classes.

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$. Fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-background $\theta$ on $X$ with $\PSH(X,\theta)\ne\varnothing$, a Gauduchon metric $\gamma$, and a smooth positive probability measure $\mathrm{d}V$. The discussion also applies when $n=1$, in which case every Hermitian metric is Gauduchon. Use the mean normalization
\[
 \mathcal K^0_{\theta,\mathrm{d}V}
   =\left\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):\int_Xu\,\mathrm{d}V=0\right\}.
\]
For such a potential put
\[
 \mathcal T_\gamma(u)
     =(\theta+\ddc u)\wedge\gamma^{n-1},
 \qquad M_{\theta,\gamma}=\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.
\]
The trace theorem makes this a positive finite measure of mass $M_{\theta,\gamma}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gaud-trace-homeomorphism}
The map $\mathcal T_\gamma$ is an affine homeomorphism from the compact convex space $\mathcal K^0_{\theta,\mathrm{d}V}$, with its $L^1$ topology, onto a compact convex subset of the positive measures of mass $M_{\theta,\gamma}$, with their weak topology. In particular
\[
 u_j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal T_\gamma(u_j)\rightharpoonup\mathcal T_\gamma(u).
\]
If $M_{\theta,\gamma}=0$, both spaces consist of a single point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The mean-normalized space is convex. It is compact because it is the continuous image of the supremum-normalized compact space in \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} under
\[
 v\longmapsto v-\int_Xv\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
This map is onto the mean-normalized space, and continuity is immediate for $L^1$.

If $u_j\to u$ in $L^1$, their trace measures converge in distributions, by differentiating against smooth test functions. All these positive measures have the same finite mass. Uniform approximation of continuous functions by smooth functions therefore upgrades this to weak convergence of measures.

For injectivity, equality of traces gives
\[
 \ddc(u-v)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}=0.
\]
The scalar operator $L_\gamma f=n\ddc f\wedge\gamma^{n-1}/\gamma^n$ is elliptic. Elliptic regularity makes the distribution $u-v$ smooth; the maximum principle on connected $X$ makes it constant. Its mean is zero, so it vanishes. The canonical representatives then agree everywhere.

A continuous injection from a compact space to the Hausdorff space of finite measures is a homeomorphism onto its image. Affineness gives convexity of the image. If its common mass is zero, every trace is the zero measure, and injectivity makes the potential space a singleton. In this case positivity also forces $\theta+\ddc u=0$; elliptic regularity shows that the unique normalized potential is smooth.
\end{proof}

The inverse can be described by a scalar equation, but positivity imposes a further constraint on its datum. For a finite signed measure $\sigma$ of total mass zero, let $G_\gamma\sigma$ denote the mean-zero distribution solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaud-trace-green}
 \ddc(G_\gamma\sigma)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}=\sigma.
\end{equation}
This is the usual distributional Green inverse. To recall why it exists, the scalar elliptic operator above has kernel the constants and index zero. Since $\gamma$ is Gauduchon, integration against $\gamma^n$ annihilates its range, so the adjoint kernel is also generated by the constants. The Fredholm alternative extends to distributions, by its Green operator on smooth test functions, and the only compatibility condition is $\int_X\sigma=0$. Normalization with $\mathrm{d}V$ fixes its additive constant. No regularity beyond the distributional solution is asserted for arbitrary measure data.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-trace-admissible-data}
A positive measure $\nu$ of mass $M_{\theta,\gamma}$ belongs to the image of $\mathcal T_\gamma$ if and only if the distribution
\[
 w=G_\gamma\bigl(\nu-\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\bigr)
\]
satisfies $\theta+\ddc w\geq0$. In that case its canonical representative is the corresponding member of $\mathcal K^0_{\theta,\mathrm{d}V}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\nu$ is the trace of a potential, uniqueness of the mean-zero solution of \eqref{eq:gaud-trace-green} identifies the two distributions. Conversely, positivity after adding a local smooth strictly plurisubharmonic correction gives a plurisubharmonic distribution. Its canonical representative is locally integrable and quasi-plurisubharmonic. It therefore belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$, and the prescribed distributional mean is zero. The Green equation gives its trace. These arguments prove both directions.
\end{proof}

In dimension one the positivity condition is automatic: $\theta+\ddc w=\nu$. Thus every positive measure of the specified mass occurs. In higher dimension, positivity of the scalar trace alone does not imply positivity of the full complex Hessian; the displayed additional condition is part of the characterization.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gaud-trace-metric}
Fix a smooth Riemannian distance $d$ on $X$. For a real Lipschitz function $f$ on $X$, set $\operatorname{Lip}_d(f)=\sup_{x\ne y}|f(x)-f(y)|/d(x,y)$. For $u,v\in\mathcal K^0_{\theta,\mathrm{d}V}$, the formula
\[
 d_{\gamma,d}(u,v)
 =\sup_{\substack{\|f\|_\infty\leq1\\\operatorname{Lip}_d(f)\leq1}}
       \left|\int_X f\,\mathrm{d}\bigl(\mathcal T_\gamma(u)-\mathcal T_\gamma(v)\bigr)\right|
\]
is a bounded complete metric on $\mathcal K^0_{\theta,\mathrm{d}V}$, inducing its $L^1$ topology. Changing $\gamma$ or $d$ gives the same topology and a uniformly equivalent metric on this compact space.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The displayed bounded-Lipschitz distance metrizes weak convergence on measures of a fixed bounded mass over a compact metric space. One can see this directly: Lipschitz functions are uniformly dense in continuous functions, and the test family in the supremum is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. The first fact separates measures and gives weak convergence from metric convergence. The Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem supplies finite uniform nets for the test family and gives the converse. Symmetry and the triangle inequality follow directly from the formula, and its value is at most $2M_{\theta,\gamma}$.

Pull back this metric by the injective map in the theorem. The resulting metric space is compact, hence complete. For two choices of the auxiliary data, both metrics induce the same compact $L^1$ topology, so the identity maps in both directions are uniformly continuous. This proves uniform equivalence, without asserting a Lipschitz comparison.
\end{proof}

The equivalence with the $L^1$ topology admits quantitative bounds. In the following statement all $L^1$ norms are taken with respect to the fixed probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gaud-trace-quantitative}
With $X,\theta,\gamma,\mathrm{d}V$ and $d$ as above, there is a constant $C\geq1$, depending only on these data, such that
\[
 C^{-1}\|u-v\|_{L^1}
 \leq d_{\gamma,d}(u,v)
 \leq C\|u-v\|_{L^1}^{1/2}
 \qquad(u,v\in\mathcal K^0_{\theta,\mathrm{d}V}).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $w=u-v$, $\sigma=\mathcal T_\gamma(u)-\mathcal T_\gamma(v)$ and $M=M_{\theta,\gamma}$, and write $\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ for the total variation of $\sigma$. Then $\int_Xw\,\mathrm{d}V=0$, $\sigma=\ddc w\wedge\gamma^{n-1}$ and $\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq2M$. If $M=0$, \cref{thm:gaud-trace-homeomorphism} gives $u=v$, so both estimates hold.

For the first estimate, consider the scalar elliptic operator
\[
 A\phi=\frac{\ddc\phi\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}{\mathrm{d}V}
\]
and its formal adjoint $A^*$ with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$. The kernel of $A$ consists of constants by the maximum principle. The Gauduchon identity gives $A^*1=0$; since the scalar elliptic operator has index zero, the adjoint kernel also consists of constants. Thus, for a smooth real function $g$, the equation
\[
 A^*\phi=g-\int_Xg\,\mathrm{d}V,\qquad
 \int_X\phi\,\mathrm{d}V=0
\]
has a unique smooth solution. Fix $p>2n$. The global elliptic $W^{2,p}$ estimate on the mean-zero subspace and the Sobolev embedding into $C^1$ give
\[
 \|\phi\|_\infty+\operatorname{Lip}_d(\phi)
 \leq C\|g\|_\infty.
\]
This is the scalar elliptic theory used for \eqref{eq:gaud-trace-green}, applied to the adjoint. Pairing the distributional equation $Aw\,\mathrm{d}V=\sigma$ with $\phi$ yields
\[
 \left|\int_Xwg\,\mathrm{d}V\right|
 =\left|\int_X\phi\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|
 \leq C\|g\|_\infty d_{\gamma,d}(u,v).
\]
Taking the supremum over smooth real $g$ with $\|g\|_\infty\leq1$ gives $\|w\|_{L^1}$ and proves the first estimate.

For the second estimate, let $f$ be any test function in the definition of $d_{\gamma,d}$. A fixed finite coordinate cover, smooth partition of unity and convolution at scale $0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0$ give smooth functions $f_\epsilon$ satisfying
\[
 \|f-f_\epsilon\|_\infty\leq C\epsilon,
 \qquad \|A^*f_\epsilon\|_\infty\leq C\epsilon^{-1}.
\]
The constants are uniform over this test family. To see the second bound, localize $f$ by the fixed partition. The localized functions have uniformly bounded values and first weak derivatives. In each second derivative of their convolution, move one derivative onto the mollifier; its $L^1$ norm is $O(\epsilon^{-1})$. The lower-order terms of $A^*$ have fixed smooth coefficients and satisfy the same bound. Hence
\[
 \left|\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|
 \leq C\bigl(M\epsilon+\epsilon^{-1}\|w\|_{L^1}\bigr).
\]
If $0<\|w\|_{L^1}<\epsilon_0^2$, choose $\epsilon=\|w\|_{L^1}^{1/2}$. For larger norms use $d_{\gamma,d}(u,v)\leq2M$, and for $w=0$ use injectivity. Taking the supremum over $f$ proves the asserted bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:gaud-trace-sharp-exponents}
Let $X=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm{i}\mathbb Z)$, with $z=x+\mathrm{i}y$, $\theta=\gamma=\eta=(\mathrm{i}/2)\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar z$, $\mathrm{d}V=\eta$ and the flat distance $d$. For integers $k\geq1$, set
\[
 u_k=\frac{\cos(2\pi kx)}{2\pi k^2}.
\]
These are smooth mean-zero potentials, and our normalization gives
\[
 \theta+\ddc u_k=\left(1-\frac12\cos(2\pi kx)\right)\eta>0,
 \qquad \|u_k\|_{L^1}=\frac1{\pi^2k^2}.
\]
The function $f_k=\cos(2\pi kx)/(2\pi k)$ has supremum norm at most one and Lipschitz constant at most one. It follows that
\[
 d_{\gamma,d}(u_k,0)
 \geq\left|\int_Xf_k\,\ddc u_k\right|
 =\frac1{8\pi k}.
\]
Consequently, the exponent $1/2$ in \cref{prop:gaud-trace-quantitative} cannot be replaced by any larger exponent, even for smooth strictly positive potentials on a fixed curve. The exponent one in the reverse estimate is also optimal: for $0<t\leq1$, both $\|tu_1\|_{L^1}$ and $d_{\gamma,d}(tu_1,0)$ equal $t$ times their positive values at $t=1$.
\end{example}

These finite coordinates must be distinguished from the non-pluripolar top-degree measure. On a curve, take the finite atomic Green potentials $q_j$ of \cref{thm:curve-ideal-lone-completion} converging in $L^1$ to a nonconstant smooth strictly $\theta$-plurisubharmonic potential $u$. Their trace measures converge weakly to $\theta+\ddc u$, so the normalized potentials converge in the metric above. Yet their non-pluripolar measures are all zero. The limit has a strictly positive non-pluripolar measure.

In particular this convergence is not convergence in capacity. Otherwise, after subtracting one common upper bound to make all potentials nonpositive, the finite exponential damping theorem would make their zero damped measures converge to the nonzero damped measure of the smooth limit. That is impossible. Supremum normalization gives a common upper bound directly. For mean-normalized $v$, put $w=v-\sup_Xv$. Then \cref{thm:normalized-compactness} gives $\sup_Xv=-\int_Xw\,\mathrm{d}V\leq C_\theta$, so the same conclusion holds. Thus even a complete finite metric supplied by Gauduchon traces does not resolve the nonlinear convergence or singularity-quotient problems.

There is a different obstruction when bounded mass fails. The potentials in \cref{cor:strong-linear-infinite-mass} have full currents with integrable coefficients and trace measures converging in total variation to the trace of a smooth metric. Their extended top-degree masses are nevertheless infinite. Thus strengthening weak convergence of the finite traces to total-variation convergence still does not control nonlinear mass.
