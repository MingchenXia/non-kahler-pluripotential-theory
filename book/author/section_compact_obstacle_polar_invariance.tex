For an obstacle prescribed only on a compact set, the same perturbation shows that pluripolar exceptions in the constraint do not change the regularized envelope.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:compact-obstacle-polar-invariance}
Let $\theta$ be big, let $K\subseteq X$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $\psi$ be a bounded real function on $K$. Set
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R&=\sup\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\leq\psi\text{ on }K\},\\
 Q&=\sup\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\leq\psi\text{ q.e. on }K\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Then $R^*=Q^*$ belongs to $\PSH(X,\theta)$ and has minimal singularities; it is bounded if $\theta\geq0$. Moreover, this regularized envelope does not change if the pointwise constraint is imposed only on $K\setminus E$, for any pluripolar subset $E\subseteq K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $m=\inf_K\psi$ and $M=\sup_K\psi$. Since $\theta$ is big, $\PSH(X,\theta)$ is nonempty, and $V_\theta\leq0$; hence $V_\theta+m$ belongs to the family defining $R$. Applying \cref{lem:compact-obstacle-upper-bound} with the constant obstacle $M$ gives a common upper bound $C$ for the larger family defining $Q$. The upper-envelope argument at the beginning of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes} therefore shows that $R^*$ and $Q^*$ belong to $\PSH(X,\theta)$.

Now fix a competitor $u$ for $Q$ and define a bounded function on $X$ by
\[
 f(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 \psi(x),&x\in K,\\
 \max\{\sup_Xu,M\},&x\notin K.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. By \cref{lem:big-pointwise-competitor}, there are $u_t\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfying $u_t\leq f$ everywhere and $u_t\to u$ almost everywhere. Each $u_t$ is a competitor for $R$, so $R^*\geq u$ almost everywhere, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} gives this inequality everywhere. Taking the supremum over $u$ and regularizing yields $R^*\geq Q^*$; the inclusion of the competitor families gives the reverse inequality.

Write $F=R^*=Q^*$. Since $F\leq C$, the potential $F-C$ is a competitor for $V_\theta$. Thus
\[
 V_\theta+m\leq F\leq V_\theta+C,
\]
which proves minimal singularities and, when $\theta\geq0$, boundedness.

Finally, if $E\subseteq K$ is pluripolar, the family constrained on $K\setminus E$ lies between the families defining $R$ and $Q$, so its regularized supremum also equals $F$.
\end{proof}
