\subsection{Divisorial products and zero volume}
\label{subsec:zero-volume-product-obstacles}

A divisorial pole can exhaust the curvature in the fiber direction while leaving a curve potential on the base. Every envelope competitor then has the same divisorial pole and descends after its removal. A smooth obstacle on the product becomes a continuous obstacle on the curve by taking a fiber minimum. This reduction gives an all-obstacle criterion even when the ideal envelope has zero top-degree volume.

Let $C$ be a compact connected curve with a smooth positive form $\eta$, and let $Z$ be a connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension $k\geq1$. Suppose that $D\subset Z$ is a nonempty smooth reduced divisor, $\kappa$ is a K\"ahler form, and $\psi\in\PSH(Z,\kappa)$ is smooth off $D$ and satisfies
\[
 \kappa+\ddc\psi=[D],\qquad \sup_Z\psi=0.
\]
Thus $\psi$ is locally $\log|z_1|^2$ plus a smooth function at $D$. On $M=C\times Z$, write $p:M\to C$ and $r:M\to Z$ for the projections, and put
\[
 \Theta=p^*\eta+r^*\kappa,\qquad
 \Psi=\psi\circ r,\qquad H=C\times D.
\]
For $w\in\PSH(C,\eta)$ set $u=\Psi+w\circ p$. For a continuous real obstacle, use the same competitor definitions for the ordinary and ideal envelopes as for a smooth obstacle.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:divisorial-product-obstacles}\index{Envelope!divisorial product}\index{Obstacle!fiber minimum}
For $F\in C^\infty(M,\mathbb R)$, the function
\[
 \widehat F(y)=\min_{z\in Z\setminus D}\{F(y,z)-\psi(z)\}
 \qquad(y\in C)
\]
is finite and continuous. The two envelopes satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divisorial-product-obstacles}
 \begin{split}
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]&=\Psi+P_{\eta,\widehat F}[w]\circ p,\\
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
     &=\Psi+P_{\eta,\widehat F}[w]_{\mathcal I}\circ p.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $z_0\notin D$. The bound $F(y,z_0)-\psi(z_0)\leq\max_MF-\psi(z_0)$ is uniform in $y$. Since $\psi$ tends to $-\infty$ at $D$, every minimum can consequently be taken on one compact subset of $Z\setminus D$. On that subset, $F-\psi$ is continuous. Uniform continuity there proves continuity of its minimum as a function of $y$.

Let $v$ be an ordinary competitor for $u$, or an ideal competitor satisfying $v\sim_{\mathcal I}u$. Its generic Lelong number along each component of $H$ is at least one. For ordinary competitors this follows from $v\preceq u$; for ideal competitors it follows from \cref{thm:local-i-inputs}. Divisorial removal in \cref{lem:nonclosed-divisorial-removal} gives
\[
 \Theta+\ddc v-[H]\geq0.
\]
As $\Theta+\ddc\Psi=p^*\eta+[H]$, the distribution $v-\Psi$ has a quasi-plurisubharmonic representative $a$ with $p^*\eta+\ddc a\geq0$. By \cref{prop:psh-descent-submersion}, $a=b\circ p$ for a unique $b\in\PSH(C,\eta)$. The equality
$v=\Psi+b\circ p$ holds almost everywhere and hence for the canonical representatives. Conversely, every potential of this form is $\Theta$-plurisubharmonic.

Ordinary order is now exactly $v\preceq u$ if and only if $b\preceq w$: test away from $H$ and use surjectivity onto $C$. Ideal equivalence is likewise exactly $b\sim_{\mathcal I}w$. Indeed, if $\mathcal J_H$ denotes the ideal sheaf of $H$, then for every $t>0$ the local product calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divisorial-product-ideals}
 \mathcal I\bigl(t(\Psi+b\circ p)\bigr)
   =\mathcal J_H^{\lfloor t\rfloor}
                   \bigl(\mathcal I(tb)\mathcal O_M\bigr).
\end{equation}
Near $H$, write its equation as $z_1=0$. Integrability in this variable forces a holomorphic germ to have order at least $\lfloor t\rfloor$. After factoring that power, the remaining weight $|z_1|^{-2(t-\lfloor t\rfloor)}$ is integrable and bounded below by a positive constant on a small polydisc. Weighted integrability therefore implies unweighted integrability in the fiber variables, so \cref{lem:submersion-multiplier-pullback} puts the remaining germ in the extension of $\mathcal I(tb)$. Conversely, the local generators of that extended ideal and integrability of the remaining one-variable weight give weighted integrability by Fubini. Away from $H$ the same pullback lemma applies directly. Equality of the product ideals also implies equality on $C$, by pulling back along a local section with fixed $z\notin D$.

Finally, $v\leq F$ is equivalent to $b\leq\widehat F$. The two competitor families therefore give \eqref{eq:divisorial-product-obstacles} off $H$. Regularization commutes with pullback by $p$ in product coordinates there. The uniform upper bound $b\leq\max_C\widehat F$, together with $\Psi\to-\infty$ at $H$, prevents regularization from changing the pole on $H$.
\end{proof}

Write $T_w=\eta+\ddc w$, let $\mu_w=\mathbf1_{\{w=-\infty\}}T_w$ be its polar part, and let $D_w=\sum_{y\in C}\nu(w,y)\delta_y$ be its atomic part. The curve classification in \cref{lem:curve-polar-model,prop:curve-all-obstacle-topology} relates these measures to the two envelopes.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:zero-volume-product-obstacles}\index{Singularity volume!zero-volume product}
For the product potential $u$ above, the following conditions are equivalent:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_\Theta[u]=P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I},\qquad \mu_w=D_w,\\
 P_{\Theta,F}[u]=P_{\Theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}
             \quad\text{for every smooth real }F.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Both singularity volumes of $u$ and of $P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}$ are zero.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $F=0$, the normalization of $\psi$ gives $\widehat F=0$. The preceding proposition and the curve classification identify the first condition with $\mu_w=D_w$. Under this equality, the ordinary and ideal curve envelopes agree below every smooth obstacle. The obstacle comparison extends their equality to every continuous obstacle by uniform smooth approximation. Apply \eqref{eq:divisorial-product-obstacles} to $\widehat F$. The converse follows by taking $F=0$.

The extended measure $\mathrm{MA}_\kappa(\psi)$ is zero: its curvature vanishes off $D$, and its pole set is discarded. The product formula in \cref{prop:curve-product-extended-ma} therefore gives
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\Theta(\Psi+b\circ p)=0
                 \qquad(b\in\PSH(C,\eta)).
\]
Divisorial removal and descent, as in the preceding proof, put every potential ordinarily equivalent to $u$ in this form. The same applies to every potential ordinarily equivalent to its ideal envelope, which is $\Psi+P_\eta[w]_{\mathcal I}\circ p$. Taking the infimum and supremum of their zero masses proves the volume assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:zero-volume-nonanalytic-product}
Take $C=\mathbb P^1$ and $Z=\mathbb P^k$, $k\geq1$, with their standard Fubini--Study forms, and let $D$ be a hyperplane. There is an unbounded $u$ with no analytic ordinary type in this product setting and with
\[
 P_\Theta[u]=P_\Theta[u]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi,
 \qquad u\not\sim\Psi,
\]
for which the two envelopes agree below every smooth obstacle. Their common zero-obstacle model has zero singularity volume.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Use the unbounded curve potential $w$ constructed in the proof of \cref{prop:ordinary-topology-nonseparation}, whose two envelopes equal zero. A logarithmic hyperplane potential on $Z$ supplies $\psi$ with $\kappa+\ddc\psi=[D]$ and supremum zero. Set $u=\Psi+w\circ p$ and apply the proposition and corollary. Fixing $z\notin D$ shows that $u-\Psi=w\circ p$ is unbounded, proving $u\not\sim\Psi$. If $u$ had analytic singularities, \cref{prop:analytic-envelope-type} would give $u\sim P_\Theta[u]=\Psi$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
