\subsection{A positive-volume endpoint that is not big}
\label{subsec:actual-flow-positive-volume-nonbig}

The radial background of \cref{sec:radial-positive-volume-nonbig} occurs, up to a smooth gauge change, at the maximal time of an actual Chern--Ricci flow. Thus the flow-endpoint condition does not restore the implication from positive variational volume to bigness. Its annihilating currents can also be described explicitly.

Use the notation of that section: $n=m+1\geq3$, $t=(m-1)/m$, $a=x/t$, and
\[
 X=\mathbb P^m\times\mathbb P^1,\qquad
 \theta=\omega+a w,\qquad \mu=\omega^m\wedge w,
 \qquad Z=\{p\}\times\mathbb P^1.
\]
Here $\omega$ and $w$ are the normalized Fubini--Study forms pulled back from the two factors. In particular, $\mu$ is a probability form. Start the flow from the Hermitian metric
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-flow-initial-metric}
 \omega_0=(n+1)\omega+(a+2)w.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:actual-flow-positive-volume-nonbig}\index{Chern--Ricci flow!nonbig positive-volume endpoint}
The maximal smooth time for this initial metric, in the Ricci convention of \cref{subsec:chern-ricci-maximal-time}, is $\tau=1/(2\pi)$. Its limiting background $\alpha_\tau=\omega_0-\tau\operatorname{Ric}(\omega_0)$ is nef and not big, and
\[
 \alpha_\tau=\theta+\ddc\log(a+2),\qquad
 \mathcal V_\mu(\alpha_\tau)=n.
\]
Its variational volume is positive for every smooth strictly positive probability form on $X$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The initial metric is strictly positive and has volume
\[
 \omega_0^n=n(n+1)^m(a+2)\mu.
\]
The normalized Fubini--Study Ricci forms are $2\pi n\omega$ and $4\pi w$ in the book convention. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}(\omega_0)
   =2\pi\bigl(n\omega+2w-\ddc\log(a+2)\bigr).
\]
Write $s$ for flow time and put $r=2\pi s$. The affine background is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-flow-affine-background}
 \alpha_s=(n+1-nr)\omega+(a+2-2r)w
                          +r\ddc\log(a+2).
\end{equation}
For $0\leq r<1$, the smooth potential $-r\log(a+2)$ gives a strictly positive representative: the horizontal coefficient is at least one and the vertical coefficient is at least $2(1-r)>0$. At $r=1$, the asserted gauge identity follows. The logarithm is globally smooth, so the bijection $u\mapsto u-\log(a+2)$ between the potential spaces preserves nefness and bigness. Gauduchon Stokes preserves the variational volume. \Cref{thm:radial-positive-volume-nonbig} therefore makes this endpoint nef, nonbig and of volume $n$.

For $r>1$, choose a nonzero smooth nonnegative function $f$ on the base supported where $a+2-2r<0$. The pullback $S=f\omega^m$ is a closed semipositive $(n-1,n-1)$-form. For every quasi-psh potential $v$, distributional Stokes gives
\[
 \int_X(\alpha_s+\ddc v)\wedge S
       =\int_{\mathbb P^m}f(a+2-2r)\omega^m<0.
\]
Hence $\alpha_s$ is not even pseudoeffective after $r=1$. At $r=1$ a smooth positive representative would contradict nonbigness. \Cref{thm:chern-ricci-maximal-time} now proves the exact lifetime and produces the actual smooth flow on $[0,\tau)$. The reference representatives used above need not solve the flow equation. Finally, \cref{prop:gaud-volume-density-comparison} gives positivity for every smooth positive probability form.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:radial-flow-annihilator-support}\index{Gauduchon current!flow endpoint support}
The current $[Z]/2$ belongs to $\mathscr G_{\omega_0}$ and annihilates $\alpha_\tau$. Every $R\in\mathscr G_{\omega_0}$ with $\int_X\alpha_\tau\wedge R=0$ has trace probability $\sigma_R=\omega_0\wedge R$ supported on $Z$. In the coefficient-matrix convention of \cref{sec:weak-gauduchon-duality}, its matrix is
\[
 P_R=n\Pi_{\mathrm{vert}}\qquad\sigma_R\text{-almost everywhere},
\]
where $\Pi_{\mathrm{vert}}$ is the $\omega_0$-orthogonal projection onto the tangent line to the fiber. In particular, its matrix has rank one and its determinant-weighted measure vanishes for every smooth positive reference density.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Only smooth forms need to be restricted to $Z$. Their restrictions satisfy $\omega_0|_Z=2w$ and $\alpha_\tau|_Z=0$, proving normalization and annihilation of $[Z]/2$.

For a general annihilator $R$, smooth-gauge Stokes gives $\int_X\theta\wedge R=0$. The measure $\theta\wedge R$ is nonnegative, so it vanishes. On every compact set $K\subset X\setminus Z$, the explicit form $\theta$ satisfies $\theta\geq c_K\omega_0$ for some $c_K>0$. Thus $\sigma_R(K)=0$, and a countable compact exhaustion gives its support on $Z$.

Relative to $\omega_0$, the matrix formula for a smooth test form $\beta$ is
\[
 \beta\wedge R=\frac1n\operatorname{tr}(B_\beta P_R)\sigma_R,
 \qquad P_R\geq0,\qquad\operatorname{tr}P_R=n.
\]
On $Z$, $\theta$ is positive on the horizontal $m$-plane and has the vertical line as kernel. Its zero pairing forces all horizontal diagonal entries of $P_R$ to vanish. The positive-matrix inequality $|(P_R)_{ij}|^2\leq(P_R)_{ii}(P_R)_{jj}$ then removes their rows and columns. The remaining vertical entry is $n$, proving the formula and determinant vanishing.
\end{proof}

For each of these annihilators the preceding flow identity specializes to
\[
 \int_X\omega(s)\wedge R=1-2\pi s,
 \qquad 0\leq s<1/(2\pi).
\]
This is an averaged trace statement. The support conclusion uses the explicit semipositive endpoint representative; determinant vanishing alone would not give it. Neither conclusion asserts pointwise collapse, local convergence of the metrics or uniqueness of the scalar trace probability.
