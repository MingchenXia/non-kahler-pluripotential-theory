\subsection{Distance for several hyperplane weights}
\label{subsec:psh-hyperplane-ideal-distance}

In this subsection, $n\geq2$ and $B\subset\mathbb C^n$ is the unit ball. If the weights have logarithmic poles along several hyperplanes through the origin, every admissible remainder must take a prescribed value at their common zero. On the other hand, the input norm stays finite as long as the combined radial exponent is below the dimension. Together these observations give a positive distance even in part of the range where the planar distance of \cref{thm:planar-optimal-ideal-distance} vanishes.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:hyperplane-positive-ideal-distance}\index{Multiplier ideal!hyperplane distance}
Fix $r>n$ and $p>r/n$. There are negative plurisubharmonic functions $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r$ on $B$ for which
\[
 I=\int_B\mathrm e^{-\sum_i\phi_i}\,\mathrm{d}V<+\infty
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperplane-positive-ideal-distance}
 \inf\left\{\int_B|g|^2\,\mathrm{d}V:g\in\mathcal O(B),\quad
       1-g\in\sum_{i=1}^r\Gamma(B,\mathcal I(p\phi_i))\right\}
       =\frac{\pi^n}{n!}>0.
\end{equation}
In particular, for $r/n<p\leq r$, the distance is positive although $\sum_i1/p=r/p\geq1$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Choose distinct real numbers $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ and unit linear forms
\[
 \ell_i(z)=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}t_i^kz_{k+1}}
                    {(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}t_i^{2k})^{1/2}}.
\]
Any $n$ of these forms are linearly independent, since the determinant of their unnormalized coefficients is a nonzero Vandermonde determinant. Put $\phi_i=p^{-1}\log|\ell_i|^2$. Since the coefficient vectors of the $\ell_i$ have norm one, $|\ell_i(z)|<1$ on $B$, so the $\phi_i$ are negative plurisubharmonic functions. By \cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}, $\mathcal I(p\phi_i)=(\ell_i)$.

At a nonzero point, at most $n-1$ of the forms vanish. These forms are linearly independent and can be used as part of a holomorphic coordinate system, while the other forms are bounded away from zero on a small neighborhood of the point. Hence
\[
 W(z)=\prod_{i=1}^r|\ell_i(z)|^{-2/p}
\]
is locally integrable off the origin, because each factor $|\ell_i|^{-2/p}$ has exponent $2/p<2$ (note that $p>r/n>1$). By compactness, $W$ is integrable on every closed annulus. Set $s=r/p$ and let $\mathrm{d}\sigma$ denote surface measure on the unit sphere. Homogeneity and polar integration over such an annulus show that
\[
 A=\int_{|\zeta|=1}\prod_i|\ell_i(\zeta)|^{-2/p}\,
                                     \mathrm{d}\sigma(\zeta)<+\infty.
\]
Tonelli then gives
\[
 I=A\int_0^1\rho^{2n-1-2s}\,\mathrm{d}\rho
       =\frac{A}{2(n-s)}<+\infty,
\]
since $s<n$. This takes care of the common zero at the origin as well as of the individual hyperplanes.

Every global section of $(\ell_i)$ vanishes at the origin. Thus every admissible $g$ in \eqref{eq:hyperplane-positive-ideal-distance} satisfies $g(0)=1$. For each $z\in B$, the holomorphic function $\lambda\mapsto g(\lambda z)$ is defined on a disc of radius greater than one, and the submean inequality for the subharmonic function $|g(\lambda z)|^2$ gives
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|g(\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\tau}z)|^2\,
                         \mathrm{d}\tau\geq|g(0)|^2=1.
\]
Integrating in $z$ and using the rotation invariance of $B$ proves $\int_B|g|^2\,\mathrm{d}V\geq\operatorname{Vol}(B)=\pi^n/n!$, also when the integral is infinite. The constant $g=1$ is admissible and attains this bound.
\end{proof}

Hence, for these equal exponents, the optimal universal constant in an estimate of the distance by a multiple of $I$ is at least $\pi^n/(n!I)>0$. In particular, the vanishing of the optimal constant for $s\geq1$ in \cref{thm:planar-optimal-ideal-distance} does not extend to higher dimensions. The example does not determine the optimal constant for $r>n$.
