\subsection{Distance for several hyperplane weights}
\label{subsec:psh-hyperplane-ideal-distance}

Let $n\geq2$ and let $B\subset\mathbb C^n$ be the unit ball. The common zero of several hyperplanes imposes a point value on every admissible holomorphic remainder. The input norm is finite as long as the combined radial exponent is below the dimension. This gives a positive distance even in part of the range where the planar distance vanishes.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:hyperplane-positive-ideal-distance}\index{Multiplier ideal!hyperplane distance}
Fix $r>n$ and $p>r/n$. There are genuine negative psh weights $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r$ on $B$ for which
\[
 I=\int_B\mathrm e^{-\sum_i\phi_i}\,\mathrm{d}V<+\infty
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperplane-positive-ideal-distance}
 \inf\left\{\int_B|g|^2\,\mathrm{d}V:g\in\mathcal O(B),\quad
       1-g\in\sum_{i=1}^r\Gamma(B,\mathcal I(p\phi_i))\right\}
       =\frac{\pi^n}{n!}>0.
\end{equation}
In particular, for $r/n<p\leq r$, the distance is positive although $\sum_i1/p=r/p\geq1$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
Choose distinct real numbers $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ and unit linear forms
\[
 \ell_i(z)=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}t_i^kz_{k+1}}
                    {(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}t_i^{2k})^{1/2}}.
\]
Every $n$ of these forms are independent, since their unnormalized coefficient determinant is the nonzero Vandermonde product. Put $\phi_i=p^{-1}\log|\ell_i|^2$. The unit normalization gives $|\ell_i(z)|<1$ on $B$, so these are genuine negative psh functions. By \cref{lem:smooth-center-multiplier-ideal}, $\mathcal I(p\phi_i)=(\ell_i)$.

At a nonzero point, at most $n-1$ forms vanish. These forms are independent and can be used as part of a holomorphic coordinate system. The other forms are bounded away from zero on a smaller neighborhood. Hence
\[
 W(z)=\prod_{i=1}^r|\ell_i(z)|^{-2/p}
\]
is locally integrable off the origin: each normal factor has exponent $2/p<2$. Compactness gives integrability on a closed annulus. Set $s=r/p$ and let $\mathrm{d}\sigma$ denote surface measure on the unit sphere. Homogeneity and polar integration on that annulus show that
\[
 A=\int_{|\zeta|=1}\prod_i|\ell_i(\zeta)|^{-2/p}\,
                                     \mathrm{d}\sigma(\zeta)<+\infty.
\]
Tonelli then gives
\[
 I=A\int_0^1\rho^{2n-1-2s}\,\mathrm{d}\rho
       =\frac{A}{2(n-s)}<+\infty,
\]
since $s<n$. This checks the intersection at the origin as well as the individual hyperplanes.

Every global section of $(\ell_i)$ vanishes at zero. Thus an admissible $g$ in \eqref{eq:hyperplane-positive-ideal-distance} has $g(0)=1$. For each $z\in B$, the holomorphic function $\lambda\mapsto g(\lambda z)$ is defined on a disc of radius greater than one. Its circle mean gives
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|g(\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\tau}z)|^2\,
                         \mathrm{d}\tau\geq|g(0)|^2=1.
\]
Integrating in $z$ and using rotation invariance of $B$ proves $\int_B|g|^2\,\mathrm{d}V\geq\operatorname{Vol}(B)=\pi^n/n!$, including the case of infinite norm. The constant $g=1$ is admissible and attains this bound.
\end{proof}

For these equal exponents, the universal optimal constant is therefore at least $\pi^n/(n!I)>0$. The example excludes extending the zero regime of \cref{thm:planar-optimal-ideal-distance} to higher dimensions. It leaves the optimal constant in the range $r>n$ undetermined.
