\subsection{A nef class with an incomplete null end}
\label{sec:nef-incomplete-cusp}

Nefness of the class does not prevent incompleteness. In the next construction, the pole of the measure along a divisor $D$ does produce a complete cusp, but a disjoint curve $E$, on which the class has degree zero, lies at finite distance.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nef-incomplete-cusp}\index{Metric completeness!nef big counterexample}
There are a smooth projective surface $Y$, disjoint smooth divisors $E,D$, a smooth closed semipositive form $\theta$ with nef and big class, a smooth Hermitian norm on $\mathcal O_Y(D)$, its defining section $\sigma$, and a smooth strictly positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V_Y$, such that the equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-incomplete-cusp-equation}
 \left\langle(\theta+\ddc\rho)^2\right\rangle
 =\mathrm{e}^{\rho}|\sigma|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}V_Y
\end{equation}
has a unique solution $\rho\in\mathcal E^1(Y,\theta)$. The solution has zero Lelong numbers and is smooth on $Y\setminus(E\cup D)$, where its current $\Omega=\theta+\ddc\rho$ is strictly positive. The curve $E$ lies in the null locus of $\{\theta\}$. Every dense Zariski open set on which $\Omega$ is smooth and strictly positive is incomplete, although every path in $Y\setminus(E\cup D)$ approaching $D$ has infinite length.
\end{theorem}

The measure $|\sigma|^{-2}\mathrm{d}V_Y$ has a nonintegrable pole along $D$ with smooth strictly positive coefficient. The solution is unbounded near $D$ and therefore does not have minimal singularities: since $\theta$ is semipositive, the envelope $V_\theta$ is zero. The theorem concerns nefness and bigness only; the additional compatibility identities of the geometric smoothing problem in \cref{sec:flow-compatible-incomplete-metric} are a separate question.

The solution will be a radial function on $\C^2\setminus\{0\}$, identified with $Y\setminus(E\cup D)$. Its current is $\ddc F(\log|z|^2)$ for a convex function $F$ whose derivative increases from $0$ at $E$ to $1$ at $D$, and the volume form $\mathrm{d}V_Y$ is defined from $F$. The following lemma constructs the profile $F$; its two end identities make the volume form smooth and positive across $E$ and $D$.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nef-cusp-radial-profile}
There is $F\in C^\infty(\R)$ with $0<p:=F'<1$ and $p'>0$ such that, for $c=1/100$, some $B\in\R$ and $K=(9c^2/8)\mathrm{e}^{-B}>0$,
\begin{align}
 F(t)&=B-3\log(1-c\mathrm{e}^{t/2}),&
 pp'&=K\mathrm{e}^{F+t} &&(t\leq-2),\label{eq:nef-cusp-left-profile}\\
 F(t)&=t+\log H(p(t)),&
 pp'&=\mathrm{e}^{F-t} &&(t\geq2),\label{eq:nef-cusp-right-profile}
\end{align}
where $H(p)=(1-p)^2(1+2p)/6$. At the two ends,
\begin{align*}
 F(t)&=B+3c\mathrm{e}^{t/2}+O(\mathrm{e}^t),&
 p'(t)&\sim(3c/4)\mathrm{e}^{t/2} &&(t\to-\infty),\\
 1-p(t)&\sim2/t,&p'(t)&\sim2/t^2 &&(t\to+\infty),
\end{align*}
and $F(t)-t=-2\log t+\log2+o(1)$ as $t\to+\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $t\leq-2$ put $L(t)=-3\log(1-c\mathrm{e}^{t/2})$. With $q=c\mathrm{e}^{t/2}$,
\[
 p_L=L'=\frac{3q}{2(1-q)},\qquad
 p_L'=\frac{3q}{4(1-q)^2},\qquad
 p_Lp_L'=\frac{9c^2}{8}\mathrm{e}^{t}(1-c\mathrm{e}^{t/2})^{-3}.
\]
This proves the left identity for every additive constant $B$.

Since $H'(p)=(p-1)p$, the increasing function
\[
 \mathcal T(p)=2+\int_{3/4}^{p}\frac{q}{H(q)}\,\mathrm{d}q
\]
tends to $+\infty$ as $p\to1$, because its integrand is asymptotic to $2/(1-p)^2$ there. Hence it has an inverse $p_R(t)$, defined for $t\geq2$ and on a neighborhood of $2$, with $p_R(2)=3/4$ and $p_R'=H(p_R)/p_R$. The function $F_R=t+\log H(p_R)$ has derivative $p_R$ and satisfies the right identity. For $q=1-p_R$,
\[
 (1/q)'=\frac{3-2q}{6(1-q)}\longrightarrow\frac12.
\]
Hence $q\sim2/t$ and $H(p_R)\sim2/t^2$, and the asymptotics at the right end follow.

It remains to interpolate the derivatives. Set $d=p_R(2)-p_L(-2)>0$. Choose disjoint short collars in $[-2,2]$ at the two endpoints, and cutoffs $\alpha_L,\alpha_R$ equal to one on smaller collars. For small $\varepsilon>0$, the function
\[
 Q_0=\alpha_Lp_L'+\alpha_Rp_R'
       +\varepsilon(1-\alpha_L-\alpha_R)
\]
is positive and agrees with the derivative germs at the endpoints. The collars and $\varepsilon$ can be chosen so that $\int_{-2}^2Q_0<d$. Adding a nonnegative smooth bump in the interior, with integral $d-\int_{-2}^2Q_0$, gives a positive function $Q$, and we put
\[
 p(t)=p_L(-2)+\int_{-2}^tQ(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.
\]
This function matches both endpoint germs, is increasing and takes values in $(0,1)$. Integrating $F'=p$ with $F(2)=F_R(2)$ gives the required $F$ and fixes the constant $B$ at the left end. The left expansion follows directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:nef-incomplete-cusp}]
\emph{The background and the pole.}
Use the incidence surface of \cref{thm:incomplete-divisorial-metric}, with blowup map $\pi:Y\to\mathbb P^2$ and exceptional curve $E$ over $[1:0:0]$, and let $\beta,\gamma$ be the pulled-back Fubini--Study forms as there. Put $D=\pi^{-1}(Z_0=0)$ and $A=\pi^*c_1(\mathcal O(1))$. Then $E\cap D=\varnothing$, $A^2=1$ and $A\cdot E=0$. The class $A$ is nef, since it has the smooth semipositive representative $\beta$. Moreover $\{\gamma\}=A-[E]$, and $\beta+\varepsilon\gamma$ is K\"ahler for $\varepsilon>0$. Thus
\[
 \frac{\beta+\varepsilon\gamma}{1+\varepsilon}
       +\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}[E]
\]
is a K\"ahler current in $A$, which proves bigness.

Choose an even nonnegative smooth function $\zeta$, supported in $[-1,1]$, positive on its interior and of integral one. The smooth convex function
\[
 U(t)=\int_\R\max(0,t-x)\zeta(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
\]
satisfies $U=0$ for $t\leq-1$, $U=t$ for $t\geq1$, and $U',U''\geq0$. On $Z_0\ne0$, write $z=(Z_1/Z_0,Z_2/Z_0)$, $s=|z|^2$ and $t=\log s$, and give the section $Z_0$ of $\mathcal O(1)$ the squared norm $\mathrm{e}^{-U(t)}$; this norm is constant near the affine origin. Near the line at infinity, use
\[
 w=Z_0/Z_1,\qquad v=Z_2/Z_1,\qquad h=1+|v|^2,
 \qquad s=h/|w|^2.
\]
The section $Z_1$ then has squared norm $\mathrm{e}^{-U}/|w|^2=1/h$ near $w=0$. These formulas define a smooth Hermitian metric on $\mathcal O(1)$. Pull it back to $\mathcal O_Y(D)$ and let $\sigma=\pi^*Z_0$. Its curvature form $\theta$ and the norm of $\sigma$ are
\[
 \theta=\ddc U(t),\quad |\sigma|^2=\mathrm{e}^{-U(t)},\qquad
 \theta=\ddc\log h,\quad |\sigma|^2=|w|^2/h\quad\hbox{near }D.
\]
The radial Hessian shows that $\theta$ is semipositive. It vanishes near $E$ and has rank one near $D$. In particular $V_\theta=0$, and the nef class has volume $A^2=1$.

\emph{Extension of the potential.}
On $Y\setminus(E\cup D)\simeq\C^2\setminus\{0\}$, set
\[
 \rho=F(t)-U(t),\qquad \Omega=\ddc F(t),
\]
so that $\theta+\ddc\rho=\Omega$. The eigenvalues of the Hermitian matrix of $\Omega$ are $p/s$ and $p'/s$, which are positive. Extend $\rho$ to $E$ by $B$ and to $D$ by $-\infty$.

Near the affine origin, the smooth functions $F(\log(|z|^2+\varepsilon))$ are plurisubharmonic and decrease to the continuous extension of $F(\log|z|^2)$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Indeed, their two Hessian eigenvalues are $p/(s+\varepsilon)$ and $(s p'+\varepsilon p)/(s+\varepsilon)^2$, with $p,p'$ evaluated at $\log(s+\varepsilon)$. Since $\theta=0$ there, pulling back to $Y$ proves positivity near $E$.

Near $D$ put $t_\varepsilon=\log h-\log(|w|^2+\varepsilon)$ and $g(t)=F(t)-t$. The local potentials $g(t_\varepsilon)+\log h$ decrease to $\rho+\log h$, and their Hessians are
\[
 p(t_\varepsilon)\ddc\log h
 +(1-p(t_\varepsilon))\ddc\log(|w|^2+\varepsilon)
 +p'(t_\varepsilon)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}
       \partial t_\varepsilon\wedge\bar\partial t_\varepsilon\geq0.
\]
Thus $\rho$ is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic on $Y$. Since $\rho$ is bounded near $E$ and grows like a double logarithm near $D$, all its Lelong numbers vanish.

\emph{The volume form and full mass.}
With Euclidean area $\mathrm{d}A$ in each complex variable, the radial determinant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nef-cusp-radial-determinant}
 \Omega^2=\frac{2}{\pi^2}\frac{pp'}{s^2}
                  \mathrm{d}A_{z_1}\mathrm{d}A_{z_2}.
\end{equation}
Define $\mathrm{d}V_Y=\mathrm{e}^{-F}\Omega^2$ off the two divisors. In an exceptional chart $z=(u,uv)$, the real Jacobian is $|u|^2$ and $s=|u|^2h$, so the left profile identity gives
\[
 \mathrm{d}V_Y=\frac{2K}{\pi^2h}\,\mathrm{d}A_u\mathrm{d}A_v.
\]
At infinity, the map $z=(1/w,v/w)$ has real Jacobian $|w|^{-6}$, and the right profile identity gives
\[
 \mathrm{d}V_Y=\frac{2}{\pi^2h^3}\,\mathrm{d}A_w\mathrm{d}A_v,
 \qquad
 |\sigma|^{-2}\mathrm{d}V_Y
 =\frac{2}{\pi^2|w|^2h^2}\,\mathrm{d}A_w\mathrm{d}A_v.
\]
The analogous second charts cover each divisor. The extensions are smooth and strictly positive, and they agree on overlaps because they agree on the punctured overlaps. The last expression exhibits the nonintegrable pole with smooth positive coefficient.

On the complement of the two divisors, $\mathrm{e}^{\rho}|\sigma|^{-2}\mathrm{d}V_Y=\Omega^2$; we extend this density by zero on $D$. Both measures give zero mass to the two divisors. In polar coordinates on $\C^2$, the Euclidean volume is $\pi^2s\,\mathrm{d}s$ times the normalized angular measure, so
\[
 \int_Y\langle\Omega^2\rangle
 =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}2pp'\,\mathrm{d}t
 =p(+\infty)^2-p(-\infty)^2=1.
\]
This proves the global equation and shows that $\rho$ has full mass.

\emph{Bounded truncations and energy.}
Near $D$ write $\alpha=\ddc t=\ddc\log h$ and $\beta_t=(\mathrm{i}/2\pi)\partial t\wedge\bar\partial t$. These forms have rank one, and
\[
 \theta=\alpha,\quad \Omega=p\alpha+p'\beta_t,\quad
 \Omega^2=2pp'\alpha\wedge\beta_t,\quad
 \Omega\wedge\theta=p'\alpha\wedge\beta_t,\quad \theta^2=0.
\]
Integration of $\alpha\wedge\beta_t$ over the angular and projective variables gives $\mathrm{d}t$. Since $|\rho|=O(\log t)$ and $p'=O(t^{-2})$, the integrals of $|\rho|$ against $\Omega^2$ and $\Omega\wedge\theta$ near $D$ are finite. Near $E$ the potential is bounded and $\theta=0$, and the remaining region is compact with smooth data.

Choose $M$ so that $\phi=\rho-M\leq0$, and set $\phi_k=\max(\phi,-k)$. For large $k$ the truncation changes $\phi$ only in the cusp near $D$, where $\phi=\log H(p(t))-M$ is strictly decreasing. If $\phi(T_k)=-k$, then $k=2\log T_k+O(1)$. On $\C^2\setminus\{0\}$, the radial local potential of $\theta+\ddc\phi_k$ is
\[
 G_k(t)=\max(F(t)-M,t-k).
\]
Near $D$, in the frame used above, the local potential of $\theta+\ddc\phi_k$ is $G_k(t)+\log|w|^2$, which equals the smooth function $\log h-k$; the term $\log|w|^2$ cancels the divisorial part of $\ddc G_k(t)$. At $t=T_k$, the derivative of $G_k$ jumps upward from $p(T_k)$ to $1$.

Convolving $G_k$ in $t$ with an even nonnegative smooth kernel gives convex functions decreasing to $G_k$ as the scale decreases. The corresponding bounded local potentials converge uniformly on compact sets, so their Bedford--Taylor products converge. Integrating the smooth radial measures through the transition shows that the transition carries mass $1-p(T_k)^2$ for $(\theta+\ddc\phi_k)^2$ and mass $1-p(T_k)$ for $(\theta+\ddc\phi_k)\wedge\theta$. Beyond the transition, $G_k$ is linear and both top-degree measures vanish. Since $\phi_k=-k$ on the transition, its energy contributions have absolute values $k(1-p(T_k)^2)$ and $k(1-p(T_k))$, and both tend to zero because $1-p(T_k)\sim2/T_k$. The contributions of the remaining region converge absolutely by the bounds above. At $E$, bounded potentials give no mass to the curve, in accordance with the vanishing radial boundary mass $p(-\infty)^2=0$. Consequently
\[
 E_\theta(\phi_k)=\frac13\sum_{j=0}^2
       \int_Y\phi_k(\theta+\ddc\phi_k)^j\wedge\theta^{2-j}
\]
has a finite limit, and in particular is bounded below. This proves $\rho\in\mathcal E^1(Y,\theta)$.

Uniqueness follows from the full-mass comparison argument in the proof of \cref{thm:incomplete-divisorial-metric}, now with $\mathrm{d}\mu=|\sigma|^{-2}\mathrm{d}V_Y$ off $D$, extended by zero on $D$. Its total mass is infinite, but each exponential density $\mathrm{e}^{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ and $\mathrm{e}^{u}\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ is a finite measure of mass $1$. Comparison on the two strict sublevel sets forces equality almost everywhere, and \cref{lem:qpsh-almost-everywhere-order} gives equality of the potentials everywhere.

\emph{The two metric ends.}
Fix $v=v_0$ in an exceptional chart and write $h_0=1+|v_0|^2$. The restriction of $\Omega$ to the normal line $\{v=v_0\}$ is
\[
 \Omega|_{v=v_0}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}
       \frac{p'(\log(|u|^2h_0))}{|u|^2}
       \,\mathrm{d}u\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar u.
\]
Its coefficient is asymptotic to $(3c\sqrt{h_0}/4)|u|^{-1}$, so the current has no smooth extension at any point of $E$. The radial path $u=r>0$ has finite remaining length, bounded by $C\int_0^{r_0}r^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}r$. At $D$ the normal coefficient is asymptotic to $2/(|w|^2t^2)$ and also diverges. Thus every open set on which $\Omega$ is smooth and strictly positive misses both curves.

Let $W$ be a dense Zariski open set on which $\Omega$ is smooth and strictly positive. Its complement $Y\setminus W$ is a proper analytic set containing $E$, and by local finiteness of irreducible components and compactness it has finitely many components, which are curves or points. Each curve other than $E$ meets $E$ in finitely many points. Choose a point of $E$ that avoids all these intersections and all point components. In a sufficiently small chart around it, the complement of $W$ is exactly $E$, so a tail of the radial path above lies in $W$. Its points approaching $E$ form a Cauchy sequence for the intrinsic metric with no limit in $W$: the smooth positive metric induces the manifold topology of $W$, and the only ambient limit is the removed point. Hence $W$ is incomplete.

Finally, on the cusp $\Omega=p\ddc t+p'\,\mathrm{d}t\wedge\dc t$, both terms are nonnegative, and $p'\geq c_0/t^2$ for large $t$ and some $c_0>0$. The length of any path with $t\to\infty$ is therefore bounded below by a positive multiple of $\int|\mathrm{d}t|/t$, which diverges. Thus the end at finite distance is the null curve $E$, disjoint from the pole.
\end{proof}
