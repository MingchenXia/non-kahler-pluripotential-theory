\chapter{Equations with prescribed singularities}
\label{chap:prescribed-equations}

There are two ways to prescribe singularities in a Monge--Amp\`ere equation. One can require the solution to differ from a reference potential by a bounded function. Alternatively, one can require it to belong to a relative full mass class; the solution may then be strictly more singular than the reference. The distinction is already present in the closed theory. In the Hermitian theory it interacts with the choice of upper and lower volume, and with the positivity assumptions needed for domination.

We first solve $L^p$ equations in semipositive big backgrounds and develop the comparison and compactness arguments used later. These also describe what the positive-exponent approximation selects in the nonuniqueness example. For arbitrary smooth big backgrounds, \cref{sec:big-minimal-comparison,sec:big-minimal-existence} use a strict singular barrier, resolution and the Gauduchon trace to solve both equations in the minimal ordinary singularity type, without either metric volume property. The next step prescribes a general ordinary type in a semipositive background.

In a Hermitian background, \cref{sec:lower-mass-solvability} explains why positive lower mass is necessary for solvability for every finite non-pluripolar measure. The general-measure existence theorems under uniform mass hypotheses are collected in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures,chap:bounded-mass-equations}. The present chapter treats the admissibility and approximation of individual right-hand sides without imposing either metric mass property.

For an individual $L^1$ density, \cref{sec:individual-density-subsolutions,sec:integrable-gauduchon-tests,sec:individual-density-stability} replace this uniform hypothesis by an exact subsolution threshold and its Gauduchon formula. The final sections construct the greatest exponential solution at a positive threshold and separate convergence of the scalar, the right-hand-side measure and the normalized potential. Throughout, uniqueness of the scalar is distinguished from uniqueness of the potential.

\section{The same ordinary singularity type}

Throughout this section $\theta$ is semipositive and big, and $\varphi$ satisfies
\[
 P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi,\qquad
 P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty
 \quad\text{for some }0<\varepsilon<1.
\]
Thus the model condition is exactly \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:als-prescribed-lp}
Let $\mathrm{d}V$ be a smooth positive volume form and let $\mu=f\,\mathrm{d}V$, where $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and $\mu(X)>0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $\lambda>0$ there is a unique $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\simeq\varphi$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu.
\]
\item There are $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\simeq\varphi$ and a uniquely determined constant $c>0$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The assertions hold without bounded mass or a positive uniform lower mass hypothesis on $X$.
\end{theorem}

These are \cite[Theorems 5.4 and 5.7]{ALS26}; their proofs follow below after the uniform estimate and supersolution construction. Replacing the reference $\omega^n$ there by $\mathrm{d}V$ changes the density by a positive smooth factor and preserves its $L^p$ class. The theorem is independent of a positive constant convention for $\ddc$: rescale the potentials and the exponential parameter together.

Only $c$ is asserted to be unique in the second part. Uniqueness of the potential up to a constant is false under these hypotheses: \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness} already gives a counterexample in the minimal singularity type of a Hermitian background. Positive-exponent uniqueness does not imply zero-exponent uniqueness.

We first bound the density norm from below in the prescribed ordinary type.

The next estimate explains how the same singularity type is retained in the limit.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-linfty}
Under the hypotheses on $(X,\theta,\varphi)$ above, suppose $u\simeq\varphi$, $\sup_Xu=0$, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)\leq f\,\mathrm{d}V,
 \qquad0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V),\quad p>1.
\]
There is a constant $C$, depending on the fixed geometry, $\varphi$, $p$, and an upper bound for $\|f\|_{L^p}$, such that
\[
 \varphi-C\leq u\leq\varphi.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We reconstruct \cite[Lemma 5.5]{ALS26}. The upper bound follows directly from the fixed-point property: $u\leq0$ and $u\preceq\varphi$, so $u$ is a competitor for $P_\theta[\varphi]$.

Fix $0<\varepsilon<1$ for which $\varphi_\varepsilon=P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)$ is genuine. Since $\varphi_\varepsilon\leq\varphi\leq0$, uniform exponential integrability for normalized $u$ and exponential integrability for this fixed $\varphi_\varepsilon$ allow a choice of $\gamma>0$ and $1<q<p$ such that
\[
 g=\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma(\varphi_\varepsilon+u)}f
\]
has a uniform $L^q$ bound. More explicitly, choose $q<p$ first, apply H\"older with exponents $p/q$ and $p/(p-q)$, and then choose $\gamma$ small enough to control the exponential factor by Cauchy--Schwarz and the two exponential integrability estimates.

Apply \cref{thm:big-bounded-normalized} in background $(1-\varepsilon)\theta$ to the density $1+g$. We obtain a bounded $v$, with $\sup_Xv=0$, and $c>0$ such that
\[
 ((1-\varepsilon)\theta+\ddc v)^n=c(1+g)\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Both $\|v\|_\infty$ and $c^{-1}$ are uniformly bounded. The added constant one ensures a uniform positive root integral, even if no lower bound for the integral of $f$ was specified.

Put $h=\varphi_\varepsilon+v\leq\varphi$ and define
\[
 \chi(t)=\frac1{1-t}\quad(t\leq0),\qquad
 \psi=\varphi+\chi(h-\varphi).
\]
Then $0\leq\chi\leq1$, $0<\chi'\leq1$, and $\chi''>0$. The formula is interpreted off the pole sets and then by the canonical representative. We justify it as follows. The function
$G(x,y)=x+\chi(y-x)$ on $y\leq x$ is convex and nondecreasing in each variable: its first derivatives are $1-\chi'$ and $\chi'$, and its Hessian is
\[
 \chi''\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\geq0.
\]
For bounded ordered potentials the local chain rule therefore gives
\[
 \theta+\ddc G(\varphi,h)
 \geq(1-\chi'(h-\varphi))\theta_\varphi
       +\chi'(h-\varphi)\theta_h\geq0.
\]
This bounded chain rule follows by local convolution after a common smooth correction; $G(x+r,y+r)=r+G(x,y)$ preserves that correction, and positivity follows by the same constant-form freezing used for bounded products. For unbounded potentials apply the bounded assertion to
$\varphi_k=\max(\varphi,-k)$ and $h_k=\max(h,-k)\leq\varphi_k$. Monotonicity of $G$ makes $G(\varphi_k,h_k)$ decrease. Its limit is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic and lies between $\varphi$ and $\varphi+1$, so it is genuine and equals $\psi$ as defined above. Locality on common finite levels and bounded product positivity give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\psi)
 \geq(\varphi-h+1)^{-2n}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(h)
 \geq c(\varphi-h+1)^{-2n}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma\varphi_\varepsilon}
             \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Pole sets have zero smooth volume, so the last inequality extends by exhaustion to all Borel sets.

Write $T=-\varphi_\varepsilon\geq0$. If $C\geq1+\|v\|_\infty$, then
$\varphi-h+1\leq T+C$. Since
\[
 A:=\inf_{T\geq0}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\gamma T}}{(T+C)^{2n}}>0,
\]
we obtain, with a uniform $d>0$,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\psi)
 \geq d \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u).
\]
Choose $K=2+\gamma^{-1}|\log d|$. On $\{u<\psi-K\}$ we have $u<1-K$, hence
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
 \leq d^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma(1-K)}
            \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\psi-K)
 \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\psi-K).
\]
The two potentials have the same ordinary singularity type. Moreover $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)$ is genuine, since $u\geq\varphi-C_u$ for some constant $C_u$. Singular strict domination yields $u\geq\psi-K\geq\varphi-K$. All constants used to choose $K$ are uniform in the asserted family. The possibly nonuniform $C_u$ is used only to provide a genuine envelope, not in any estimate.
\end{proof}

The estimate controls $u-\varphi$, rather than $u$ itself. The smaller-background condition supplies $\varphi_\varepsilon$ and enables the final domination argument; the bounded auxiliary equation is now supplied by the preceding sections.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-lp-supersolution}
For nonzero $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ and $\lambda>0$, there exists $w\simeq\varphi$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(w)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda w}f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be the bounded solution of \cref{thm:big-bounded-exponential}, and write $M=\sup_Xv$, $a=v-M\leq0$. The rooftop $p=P_\theta(a,\varphi)$ satisfies
$\varphi-\|a\|_\infty\leq p\leq\varphi$, so $p\simeq\varphi$. By the rooftop inequality,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{p=a\leq\varphi\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a)
   +\mathbf1_{\{p=\varphi<a\}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\varphi).
\]
The second term vanishes: \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite} carries the measure of $\varphi$ on $\{\varphi=0\}$, while $\varphi<a\leq0$ excludes that set. On the first contact set, $a=p$ and
$\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda(a+M)}f\,\mathrm{d}V$. Thus
$\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(p)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda(p+M)}f\,\mathrm{d}V$. Take $w=p+M$. This is the argument of \cite[Lemma 5.6]{ALS26}.
\end{proof}

\section{The zero-exponent limit and its topology}
\label{sec:zero-exponent-topology}

The uniform limit $\lambda u_\lambda\to\log c$ proved for continuous bounded potentials in \cref{chap:weak-equations} cannot be transferred unchanged to an unbounded prescribed singularity type.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:singular-no-uniform-limit}
Suppose $\varphi$ is unbounded below and $u_\lambda\simeq\varphi$ for every $\lambda>0$. Then $\lambda u_\lambda$ cannot converge uniformly on $X$ to a finite constant as $\lambda\downarrow0$. Its distance to any finite constant is also infinite in $L^\infty(\mathrm{d}V)$ for each fixed $\lambda>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Bounded equivalence preserves unboundedness below. Every nonempty strict sublevel set of the upper semicontinuous function $u_\lambda$ is open and has positive smooth volume. Thus $u_\lambda$ is essentially unbounded below, as is $\lambda u_\lambda$. The stated conclusions follow.
\end{proof}

A fixed finite non-pluripolar measure is uniformly controlled by capacity.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:npp-uniform-capacity}
Let $\mu$ be a finite non-pluripolar Borel measure on $X$. Then
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\sup\{\mu(E):E\text{ Borel},\
                      \operatorname{Cap}_\omega(E)\leq\delta\}=0.
\]
Consequently, capacity convergence implies convergence in $\mu$-measure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the first assertion failed, there would be $\epsilon>0$ and Borel sets $E_j$ with capacity at most $2^{-j}$ and $\mu(E_j)\geq\epsilon$. Countable subadditivity of capacity gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_\omega\left(\limsup_j E_j\right)=0.
\]
The limsup is therefore pluripolar and has zero $\mu$-measure. On the other hand, finiteness of $\mu$ and continuity from above applied to $\bigcup_{j\geq k}E_j$ show that the limsup has measure at least $\epsilon$, a contradiction. Apply the first assertion to the sets on which the difference of two converging potentials exceeds a fixed positive number to get the consequence.
\end{proof}

For solutions of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}\mu$, write
\[
 v_\lambda=u_\lambda-\sup_Xu_\lambda,\qquad
 c_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_\lambda}.
\]
There are three separate limit questions: whether $c_\lambda$ has a finite limit, whether the measures $c_\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda}\mu$ converge, and whether a limit of $v_\lambda$ solves the resulting equation. The next lemma passes from the first question to the second for every fixed finite non-pluripolar measure. The third requires a continuity theorem for the Monge--Amp\`ere operator. For $L^p$ densities, the measure estimate is quantitative.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:normalized-exponential-lp}\label{lem:normalized-exponential-npp}
Let $v_j\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ satisfy $\sup_Xv_j=0$, and let $\lambda_j>0$ decrease to zero. For every finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu$,
\[
 \int_X\left|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_jv_j}-1\right|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\longrightarrow0.
\]
If $f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ for some $p>1$, then more precisely
\[
 \int_X\left|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_jv_j}-1\right|\,|f|\,\mathrm{d}V
 \leq C_p\lambda_j\|f\|_{L^p(\mathrm{d}V)}.
\]
The constant depends on the fixed background, $p$ and $\mathrm{d}V$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega\geq\theta$. By \cref{lem:global-capacity-estimates}, for every $T>1$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_\Omega(\{v_j<-T\})\leq C/T
\]
uniformly in $j$. Since $v_j\leq0$, splitting at this sublevel gives
\[
 \int_X(1-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_jv_j})\,\mathrm{d}\mu
 \leq\lambda_jT\mu(X)+\mu(\{v_j<-T\}).
\]
The preceding lemma makes the second term uniformly small when $T$ is large. For this fixed $T$, the first term tends to zero as $j\to\infty$. This proves the general assertion. The value of the exponential is zero at a pole; pole sets have zero $\mu$-measure.

For the quantitative assertion, \cref{lem:uniform-exponential}, applied in background $\Omega$, gives a uniform $L^q$ bound for $-v_j$, where $q=p/(p-1)$. H\"older's inequality and
\[
 0\leq1-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_jv_j}\leq\lambda_j|v_j|
\]
give the claimed estimate for every $j$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, if $u_j$ solve $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_j)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_ju_j}f\,\mathrm{d}V$, put
\[
 v_j=u_j-\sup_Xu_j,\qquad c_j=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j\sup_Xu_j}.
\]
If $c_j\to c<+\infty$, then the right-hand sides
$c_j\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_jv_j}f\,\mathrm{d}V$ converge in total variation to $cf\,\mathrm{d}V$. This conclusion follows from the lemma alone. To conclude that an $L^1$ limit of $v_j$ solves the limiting equation still requires continuity of the Monge--Amp\`ere operator and preservation of the relative class.

Here is one precise continuity input that supplies this step in a common regime.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-dominated-continuity}
Assume the hypotheses on $\theta,\varphi$ of \cref{thm:als-general-measure}. Suppose $u_j\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$, $u_j\to u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ in $L^1(X)$, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_j)\leq\nu
\]
for one finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu$. Then $u\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$, the convergence holds in capacity, and
$\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_j)\rightharpoonup\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)$.
\end{theorem}

This is the fixed-measure case of \cite[Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.1, condition (2)]{ALS26}. A complete reconstruction is given in \cref{thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity}. Its proof first constructs a common rooftop envelope after extraction, establishes finite mass of the limiting lower roofs, and then applies domination. Mere $L^1$ convergence, even for uniformly bounded potentials, is insufficient for the Monge--Amp\`ere passage to the limit. The common dominating measure and the positive lower mass assumption are the substantive hypotheses here.

\section[Exponential approximation without bounded mass]{Closing an exponential approximation without bounded mass}
\label{sec:exponential-closedness}

The estimates in \cref{chap:damped-measures} give a passage to the limit that requires neither bounded mass nor a positive lower volume. Its hypothesis is convergence in capacity. Establishing that convergence in an existence argument remains a separate task.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:exponential-closedness}
Fix a Hermitian metric on $X$, let $\theta$ be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form, and let $\mu$ be a finite non-pluripolar measure. Suppose $f_j\geq0$, $f_j\to f$ in $L^1(\mu)$, and $\lambda_j>0$ with $\lambda_j\to\lambda>0$. Let $u_j,u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be nonpositive, suppose $u_j\to u$ in capacity, and assume
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)
     =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j u_j}f_j\,\mu.
\]
Then $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$ is finite and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mu.
\]
The measures on the left converge weakly; the right-hand sides converge in total variation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Discard finitely many indices so that $\lambda_j\geq\lambda/2$. Their masses are at most $\|f_j\|_{L^1(\mu)}$, hence uniformly bounded. On a deep sublevel,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)(\{u_j<-T\})
 \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda T/2}\|f_j\|_{L^1(\mu)}.
\]
The tail criterion \cref{thm:undamped-tail-continuity}, in its general-background form \cref{thm:general-background-damping}, shows that the limiting Monge--Amp\`ere measure is finite and that the left-hand sides converge weakly to it.

It remains to identify this weak limit. By \cref{lem:npp-uniform-capacity}, $u_j\to u$ in $\mu$-measure. The functions $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}$ are bounded by one and converge in $\mu$-measure to $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$. The change of exponent is uniform for nonpositive arguments, because the mean-value theorem gives
\[
 \sup_{t\leq0}|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_jt}-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}|
 \leq |\lambda_j-\lambda|
       \sup_{s\geq0}s \mathrm{e}^{-(\lambda/2)s}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j u_j}\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ in $\mu$-measure and, by absolute continuity, in $f\mu$-measure as well. Boundedness by one and finiteness of $f\mu$ give convergence in $L^1(f\mu)$. Therefore
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j u_j}f_j-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f|\,\mathrm{d}\mu
 &\leq\|f_j-f\|_{L^1(\mu)}\\
 &\quad+\int_X|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j u_j}-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}|f\,\mathrm{d}\mu
 \longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
\]
This proves total-variation convergence and identifies the equation for $u$.
\end{proof}

Normalizing constants can be absorbed into $f_j$ whenever the resulting densities converge in $L^1(\mu)$. The theorem does not assert that an arbitrary $L^1$-convergent sequence converges in capacity or stays in a prescribed envelope class.

This argument also pinpoints the difficulty as $\lambda_j\downarrow0$: the tail bound $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_jT}\|f_j\|_1$ no longer tends to zero uniformly in $j$ as $T\to\infty$. A zero-exponent approximation needs another source of uniform tail control, such as the integrability condition in \cref{cor:integrability-continuity}. Global bounded mass would not by itself supply that missing tail estimate.

\section{Consequences for the singularity program}

Ordinary singularity volumes are defined by optimizing mass within a bounded-equivalence class. They are therefore invariants of that class and may be infinite; their finiteness and comparison properties retain the hypotheses of \cref{chap:envelopes}. Multiplier-ideal equivalence is coarser. The $\mathcal I$-goodness problem asks which envelope and volume identities survive under this coarser relation.

The equation proofs separate three ingredients: relative envelopes, finite damped measures and continuity of the undamped operator. The first two are available without bounded mass. The third requires estimates for the actual approximating sequence, as in \cref{thm:exponential-closedness}.

Gauduchon metrics give an invariant linear trace and the adjoint measure controlling the smooth scalar. In \cref{chap:normalization}, logarithmic variation of the density gives supporting probabilities for prescribed-type equations. Their construction does not identify them with a linearized adjoint at a singular solution; that identification requires additional regularity.
