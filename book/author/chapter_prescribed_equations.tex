\chapter{Equations with prescribed singularities}
\label{chap:prescribed-equations}

Throughout this chapter we fix a Hermitian metric $\omega$ on $X$ and a smooth positive probability volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. Unless another measure is named, $L^p$ norms, volumes and the phrase ``almost everywhere'' refer to $\mathrm{d}V$, while capacity and convergence in capacity refer to $\operatorname{Cap}_\omega$ from \cref{sec:hermitian-capacity}. The background $\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form whose positivity is specified in each section. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha$ and $u\in\PSH(X,\alpha)$ we write $\alpha_u=\alpha+\ddc u$, and we call $\{u=-\infty\}$ the \emph{pole set} of $u$; it is pluripolar and has zero volume. We write $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)$ for the extended non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$ of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}. It equals $(\theta+\ddc u)^n$ when $u$ is bounded, charges no pluripolar set, and may have infinite total mass; we keep the superscript where this possibility matters. Finally, $s_+=\max(s,0)$ for real $s$.

We use the singularity order $\preceq$, the equivalence $\sim$ and the ordinary singularity types of \cref{def:singularity-order}; the envelopes $P_\theta(f)$, the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta(u,v)$, the envelope $V_\theta=P_\theta(0)$ and minimal singularities of \cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}; and the ordinary envelope $P_\theta[u]$ and its fixed points of \cref{def:singularity-envelope}. A potential $\rho\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $\theta_\rho\geq\delta\omega$ for some $\delta>0$ is called a \emph{strict barrier}; it exists exactly when $\theta$ is big, and by \cref{thm:resolvable-regularization-input} it can be chosen with analytic singularities.

We seek potentials $u$ in the ordinary singularity type of a given reference $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ solving
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu\quad(\lambda>0)
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu,
\]
where $\mu$ is a given measure, typically $f\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $f\in L^p$ and $p>1$, and the constant $c>0$ is part of the unknown. When $\varphi$ is unbounded, the comparison principles for bounded potentials no longer apply, and a limit of approximate solutions may leave the prescribed type. For a semipositive big background, both difficulties are overcome by a smaller background. If $P_{\delta\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty$ for some $0<\delta<1$, then the part $\delta\theta$ of the background can carry the singularity of $\varphi$, while bounded envelopes in the remaining big background $(1-\delta)\theta$ have Monge--Amp\`ere measures of positive mass. This gives a domination principle for singular potentials (\cref{thm:singular-strict-domination}), and hence uniqueness. Existence starts from bounded equations with $L^p$ densities, solved with a subsolution built from a strict barrier. A compactness criterion with a common lower barrier identifies limits of Monge--Amp\`ere measures, and an addition theorem for weighted measures in a fixed type removes a possible deficit of mass in a limit. Together with a uniform estimate relative to the reference (\cref{thm:relative-linfty}), these results solve both equations in the type of an envelope fixed point that admits such a smaller-background envelope (\cref{thm:als-prescribed-lp}). At exponent zero, comparison determines the constant $c$ but, in general, not the potential.

If $\theta$ is big but not semipositive, $V_\theta$ may be unbounded and the smaller-background construction is not available; a strict analytic barrier $\rho$ takes over its role in comparison. Resolving the singularities of analytic approximants of $V_\theta$ reduces the equations to semipositive big backgrounds. Stability above the barrier then produces solutions with minimal singularities which are locally H\"older continuous off the poles of $\rho$ (\cref{thm:big-minimal-exponential,thm:big-minimal-normalized,thm:big-minimal-holder}). They are smooth off these poles when the density is smooth and strictly positive on the whole complement (\cref{thm:nonclosed-interior-smoothness}).

The same barrier controls smooth equations as a semipositive background degenerates (\cref{thm:semipositive-smooth-degeneration}). For small resolutions of threefold nodes with a closed background, the potentials converge smoothly away from the exceptional curves, and comparing the limiting and approximating metrics reduces to controlling the diameters of neighborhoods of these curves. In the projective example of \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness}, where the normalized potential is not unique, symmetry selects the zero-exponent limit, while local examples show that a positive density on a small open set does not by itself give regularity. Resolved logarithmic poles and minimal types on blowups give explicit equations whose data are determined by the residual form and the Jacobian of the modification.

The last part of the chapter concerns a Hermitian background $h$ and general right-hand sides. If the masses of bounded $h$-plurisubharmonic functions have no positive lower bound, some probability measure $\mu$ with density bounded below by a positive constant admits no solution of $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_h(u)=c\mu$; with such a bound, every nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure admits one (\cref{cor:positive-lower-mass-solvability} in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures}). Without such a bound, a measure with bounded density with respect to the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a H\"older potential still admits a normalized H\"older solution of the homogeneous equation (\cref{thm:holder-reference-homogeneous-equation}). For singular data supported on shrinking coordinate spheres, a bounded-density test against a fixed probability supplies a strict H\"older reference, a normalized H\"older solution of the homogeneous equation and bounded exponential solutions (\cref{prop:fixed-spherical-reference,cor:fixed-spherical-homogeneous,thm:fixed-spherical-exponential}), even when the lower mass vanishes. For an individual nonnegative density $f\in L^1$, bounded truncations and Gauduchon tests determine the largest coefficient $a$ for which $\mathrm{MA}_h(u)\geq af\,\mathrm{d}V$ has a solution.

Passing from this threshold to an exact equation at exponent zero requires control of the potentials as well as of their measures. On products $X_0\times Y$, exact threshold equations are obtained, also when the lower mass vanishes, for the product of a fixed smooth measure on $X_0$ with an integrable density on a closed K\"ahler factor $Y$, or with a measure of continuous logarithmic potential on $Y=\mathbb P^1$ (\cref{thm:zero-lower-mass-product-equation,thm:closed-factor-integrable-equation,thm:continuous-potential-product-threshold}). The roots of the closed-factor densities form a closed subspace of the density roots approximable by bounded ones (\cref{prop:closed-factor-density-roots}). A density whose measure is dominated by a power $\operatorname{Cap}_h^{1+\alpha}$ of the capacity, $\alpha>0$, also admits an exact threshold equation (\cref{thm:power-capacity-critical-equation}), and such densities exist outside every $L^p$ with $p>1$ (\cref{thm:logarithmic-critical-density}). At a positive exponent, exponential decay controls the mass on deep sublevels (\cref{thm:exponential-closedness}); this control is lost as the exponent tends to zero. For an individual density, the greatest solutions of the exponential equations have one-sided limits in the exponent and jump at no more than countably many exponents (\cref{prop:greatest-exponential-right-continuity,prop:greatest-exponential-left-limit,prop:greatest-exponential-countable-jumps}). For the sublogarithmic densities on products with a projective space, a radial flux identifies the limit of the supremum-normalized greatest solutions as the exponent tends to zero (\cref{thm:radial-integrable-selection}). On projective space itself, the radial singularity types of these densities separate the regularity of the residual from the pole, both when the density is prescribed (\cref{thm:radial-residual-regularity}) and when an envelope of a singular radial obstacle selects the measure (\cref{thm:singular-radial-envelope}).

\section{Equations in a prescribed ordinary type}\label{sec:ordinary-type-equations}

We return to a semipositive big background $\theta$ and now require the solution to have the ordinary singularity type of a fixed reference $\varphi$. Throughout this section, $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ is a model reference in the sense of \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}: an envelope fixed point, $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$, that admits a smaller-background envelope. We fix $0<\varepsilon<1$ with $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)\not\equiv-\infty$. Note that $\varphi\leq0$, since every competitor for $P_\theta[\varphi]$ is nonpositive. For a normalized solution $u$, the fixed-point property gives the upper bound $u\leq\varphi$, while the smaller-background envelope $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)$ yields a uniform lower bound $u\geq\varphi-C$. A supersolution and the addition theorem then prove existence in this type.

\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:als-prescribed-lp}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!prescribed singularities}
Let $\theta$ and $\varphi$ be as above, and let $\mu=f\,\mathrm{d}V$, where $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and $\mu(X)>0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $\lambda>0$ there is a unique $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\sim\varphi$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu.
\]
\item There are $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $u\sim\varphi$ and a uniquely determined constant $c>0$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The potential in part (2) need not be unique up to an additive constant, even for a Hermitian background and the reference $\varphi=0$; see the counterexample in \cref{thm:degenerate-hermitian-nonuniqueness}.

The proof uses two estimates in the prescribed type. The first is a lower bound for the norm of a Monge--Amp\`ere density.

\input{author/section_relative_density_lower_bound}

The second estimate bounds a normalized solution from below by $\varphi$ minus a uniform constant. It is what keeps limits of solutions in the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi$.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:relative-linfty}
Let $\theta$ and $\varphi$ be as above. Suppose $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, $u\sim\varphi$, $\sup_Xu=0$, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)\leq f\,\mathrm{d}V,
 \qquad0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V),\quad p>1.
\]
There is a constant $C$, depending only on the fixed geometry, $\varphi$, $p$, and an upper bound for $\|f\|_{L^p}$, such that
\[
 \varphi-C\leq u\leq\varphi.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows directly from the fixed-point property: since $u\leq0$ and $u\preceq\varphi$, the function $u$ is a competitor for $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$.

For the lower bound we construct a subsolution $\psi$ of the type of $\varphi$ whose measure dominates a multiple of $\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)$, and then apply singular domination. Put $\varphi_\varepsilon=P_{\varepsilon\theta}(\varphi)$, so that $\varphi_\varepsilon\leq\varphi\leq0$. Uniform exponential integrability (\cref{lem:uniform-exponential}) for the normalized potentials $u$ and exponential integrability for the fixed function $\varphi_\varepsilon$ allow us to choose $\gamma>0$ and $1<q<p$ such that
\[
 g=\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma(\varphi_\varepsilon+u)}f
\]
has a uniform $L^q$ bound. Explicitly, we first choose $q<p$ and apply H\"older's inequality with exponents $p/q$ and $p/(p-q)$; then we choose $\gamma$ so small that the exponential factor is controlled by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and the two exponential integrability estimates.

\Cref{thm:big-bounded-normalized}, applied in the semipositive big background $(1-\varepsilon)\theta$ to the density $1+g$, gives a bounded $v$ with $\sup_Xv=0$ and a constant $c>0$ such that
\[
 ((1-\varepsilon)\theta+\ddc v)^n=c(1+g)\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Both $\|v\|_\infty$ and $c^{-1}$ are uniformly bounded. Adding the constant one to $g$ makes the root integral of the density at least one, so no lower bound for the integral of $f$ is needed.

Put $h=\varphi_\varepsilon+v\leq\varphi$, which is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic, and define
\[
 \chi(t)=\frac1{1-t}\quad(t\leq0),\qquad
 \psi=\varphi+\chi(h-\varphi),
\]
where the formula for $\psi$ is evaluated off the pole sets. Then $0\leq\chi\leq1$, $0<\chi'\leq1$, and $\chi''>0$. For bounded potentials with $h\leq\varphi$, \cref{lem:bounded-convex-pair}, applied with $G(x,y)=x+\chi(y-x)$, gives
\[
 \theta+\ddc G(\varphi,h)
 \geq(1-\chi'(h-\varphi))\theta_\varphi
       +\chi'(h-\varphi)\theta_h\geq0.
\]
For unbounded potentials we apply this bounded assertion to $\varphi_k=\max(\varphi,-k)$ and $h_k=\max(h,-k)\leq\varphi_k$. Since $G$ is nondecreasing in both variables, $G(\varphi_k,h_k)$ decreases as $k$ increases. Its limit is $\theta$-plurisubharmonic and lies between $\varphi$ and $\varphi+1$; hence it is not identically $-\infty$, and it equals $\psi$. Locality on common finite levels and positivity of bounded products give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\psi)
 \geq(\varphi-h+1)^{-2n}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(h)
 \geq c(\varphi-h+1)^{-2n}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma\varphi_\varepsilon}
             \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}f\,\mathrm{d}V,
\]
where the second inequality uses $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(h)\geq((1-\varepsilon)\theta+\ddc v)^n\geq cg\,\mathrm{d}V$. These inequalities are first obtained off the pole sets; since pole sets have zero volume, they extend by exhaustion to all Borel sets.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Write $T=-\varphi_\varepsilon\geq0$. If $C_1\geq1+\|v\|_\infty$, then $\varphi-h+1\leq T+C_1$. Since
\[
 A:=\inf_{T\geq0}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\gamma T}}{(T+C_1)^{2n}}>0,
\]
we obtain, with a uniform $d>0$,
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\psi)
 \geq d \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u).
\]
Choose $K=2+\gamma^{-1}|\log d|$. On $\{u<\psi-K\}$ we have $u<1-K$, because $\psi\leq\varphi+1\leq1$; hence
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)
 \leq d^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma(1-K)}
            \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\psi-K)
 \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\psi-K).
\]
The potentials $u$ and $\psi-K$ have the same ordinary singularity type. Moreover, $u\geq\varphi-C_u$ for some constant $C_u$, so $P_{\varepsilon\theta}(u)\geq\varphi_\varepsilon-C_u$ is not identically $-\infty$. \Cref{thm:singular-strict-domination} therefore yields $u\geq\psi-K\geq\varphi-K$. All constants used to choose $K$ are uniform over the family in the theorem; the possibly nonuniform constant $C_u$ serves only to show that the smaller-background envelope of $u$ is not identically $-\infty$, and it enters no estimate.
\end{proof}

The existence proof starts from a supersolution in the prescribed type. It is obtained from a bounded solution by a rooftop envelope with $\varphi$.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relative-lp-supersolution}
For nonzero $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and $\lambda>0$, there exists $w\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $w\sim\varphi$ such that
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(w)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda w}f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be the bounded solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}f\,\mathrm{d}V$ given by \cref{thm:big-bounded-exponential}, and write $M=\sup_Xv$ and $a=v-M\leq0$. The rooftop envelope $r=P_\theta(a,\varphi)$ satisfies $\varphi-\|a\|_\infty\leq r\leq\varphi$, so $r\sim\varphi$. By the rooftop inequality of \cref{prop:rooftop-measure},
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(r)
 \leq\mathbf1_{\{r=a\leq\varphi\}}\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a)
   +\mathbf1_{\{r=\varphi<a\}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(\varphi).
\]
The second term vanishes: by \cref{prop:fixed-point-finite}, the measure of $\varphi$ is carried by $\{\varphi=0\}$, whereas $\varphi<a\leq0$ on the set in question. On the first contact set, $a=r$ and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda(a+M)}f\,\mathrm{d}V$. Thus $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(r)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda(r+M)}f\,\mathrm{d}V$, and $w=r+M$ has the required properties.
\end{proof}

\section{Limits of exponential equations}
\label{sec:zero-exponent-topology}

In this section $\theta$ is an arbitrary smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $\mu$ a finite non-pluripolar measure. We consider solutions $u_\lambda\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\theta(u_\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}\mu\qquad(\lambda>0).
\]
As $\lambda\downarrow0$, one expects suitably normalized solutions to converge to a solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu$ with a scalar $c>0$. For an $L^p$ density with $p>1$ and a Hermitian background, the continuous solutions satisfy $\lambda u_\lambda\to\log c$ uniformly (\cref{prop:vanishing-exponent}). For solutions in a fixed unbounded ordinary type, no such uniform convergence is possible: by \cref{prop:singular-no-uniform-limit}, the $L^\infty$ distance from $\lambda u_\lambda$ to every finite constant is infinite for each $\lambda>0$. The limit must instead be formulated through normalized potentials and their measures. Write
\[
 v_\lambda=u_\lambda-\sup_Xu_\lambda,\qquad
 c_\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\sup_Xu_\lambda},
\]
so that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(v_\lambda)=c_\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda}\mu$. By \cref{lem:normalized-exponential-npp}, $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda}\to1$ in $L^1(\mu)$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$; hence, if $c_\lambda\to c<+\infty$, the right-hand sides converge in total variation to $c\mu$. If $\mu=f\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $f\in L^p$ for some $p>1$, \cref{lem:normalized-exponential-lp} gives an error linear in $\lambda$.

To identify the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of an $L^1$ limit of the $v_\lambda$, one needs in addition a continuity theorem for the Monge--Amp\`ere operator. For potentials of a fixed ordinary type, \cref{cor:common-barrier-continuity} identifies the limiting measure under a common barrier and a domination hypothesis. In a relative envelope class, \cref{thm:relative-fixed-measure-continuity} instead constructs a common lower envelope under positive lower mass. For a semipositive big $\theta$ and a reference satisfying \eqref{eq:als-model-condition}, it shows that an $L^1$-convergent sequence in the class whose measures are dominated by one finite non-pluripolar measure converges in capacity, and that its measures converge weakly. That argument, with its mass hypothesis, is developed in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures}.

\subsection{A positive limiting exponent}
\label{sec:exponential-closedness}

When the exponents converge to a positive limit, the factor $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ makes the Monge--Amp\`ere mass of deep sublevels uniformly small. If the potentials converge in capacity, the tail criterion of \cref{thm:undamped-tail-continuity} then identifies the limit of their Monge--Amp\`ere measures, and convergence of the right-hand sides identifies the equation.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:exponential-closedness}
Let $\theta$ be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $\mu$ a finite non-pluripolar measure. Suppose $f_j\geq0$, $f_j\to f$ in $L^1(\mu)$, and $\lambda_j>0$ with $\lambda_j\to\lambda>0$. Let $u_j,u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ be nonpositive, suppose $u_j\to u$ in capacity, and assume
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)
     =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j u_j}f_j\,\mu.
\]
Then $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$ is finite and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mu.
\]
Moreover, $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)\rightharpoonup\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$ weakly, and the right-hand sides converge in total variation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Discarding finitely many indices, we may assume that $\lambda_j\geq\lambda/2$. The measures $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)$ have masses at most $\|f_j\|_{L^1(\mu)}$, which are uniformly bounded, and since $u_j\leq0$, their deep sublevels satisfy
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)(\{u_j<-T\})
 \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda T/2}\|f_j\|_{L^1(\mu)}.
\]
The tail criterion \cref{thm:undamped-tail-continuity}, which holds for an arbitrary smooth background by \cref{thm:general-background-damping}, therefore shows that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$ is finite and that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u_j)\rightharpoonup\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta(u)$.

It remains to identify this limit with $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\,\mu$. By \cref{lem:npp-uniform-capacity}, $u_j\to u$ in $\mu$-measure. Since $t\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}$ is $\lambda$-Lipschitz for $t\leq0$, the functions $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_j}$, which are bounded by one, converge in $\mu$-measure to $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$. The change of exponent is uniform for nonpositive arguments, because the mean value theorem gives
\[
 \sup_{t\leq0}|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_jt}-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}|
 \leq |\lambda_j-\lambda|
       \sup_{s\geq0}s \mathrm{e}^{-(\lambda/2)s}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j u_j}\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ in $\mu$-measure, and hence in $f\mu$-measure, since the finite measure $f\mu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu$. As these functions are bounded by one, the convergence also holds in $L^1(f\mu)$. Therefore
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_X|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j u_j}f_j-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f|\,\mathrm{d}\mu
 &\leq\|f_j-f\|_{L^1(\mu)}\\
 &\quad+\int_X|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_j u_j}-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}|f\,\mathrm{d}\mu
 \longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
\]
This proves the total-variation convergence of the right-hand sides, and comparing the two limits gives the equation for $u$.
\end{proof}

Additive normalizations of the potentials can be absorbed into the densities: translating each $u_j$ by a constant multiplies $f_j$ by an exponential factor, and the theorem applies as long as the modified densities still converge in $L^1(\mu)$. Convergence in capacity is a hypothesis; the theorem says nothing about sequences that converge only in $L^1$, and it does not keep the limit in a prescribed envelope class.

The proof also shows where the difficulty lies as $\lambda_j\downarrow0$: the tail bound $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_jT}\|f_j\|_{L^1(\mu)}$ no longer tends to zero uniformly in $j$ as $T\to\infty$. A zero-exponent limit therefore needs another source of uniform tail control, such as the integrability condition of \cref{cor:integrability-continuity}; a uniform bound on the total masses does not by itself control these tails.
