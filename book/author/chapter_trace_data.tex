\chapter{Restriction and intrinsic trace data}
\label{chap:trace-data}

Analytic approximation and restriction need not commute: the restriction may have logarithmic poles although every ambient approximant is locally bounded. To retain the singularities detected by the approximants, we restrict their defining multiplier ideals and take asymptotic vanishing orders. These orders are finite, additive and intrinsic to the ambient multiplier-ideal class.

On a curve, they are the atoms of a finite measure. Its Green potential defines an ordinary singularity type, and the remaining curvature is a smooth measure whose mass is fixed by Stokes. In every dimension, the divisorial inequalities define an envelope in the original restricted background, obtained as the limit of the restricted analytic envelopes. This envelope pulls back to the envelope of the inherited data on every compact modification. Pullback from a curve gives exact realizations of all the trace numbers, while product examples show why even exact divisorial data does not determine the Monge--Amp\`ere mass.

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold, let $Y\subset X$ be a connected compact complex submanifold of positive dimension, and fix $u\in\QPSH(X)$ with $\nu(u,Y)=0$. Choose a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$ with $u\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ and a Hermitian form $\omega$. We use the analytic approximants $u_m$ and errors $\epsilon_m$ of \cref{cor:nonclosed-analytic-approximation}. Their restrictions are genuine by \cref{cor:restricted-analytic-divisorial-bound}.

\section{Restricted ideals and trace data}\label{sec:restricted-ideals}\label{sec:trace-additivity}

The image of a multiplier ideal on $Y$ records the vanishing of its local generators along $Y$. Its order on a modification can grow with the weight, even when the ideal is generically trivial on $Y$.

For $t>0$ put
\[
 \mathfrak a_t(u;Y)=\mathcal I(tu)\mathcal O_Y.
\]
Here we take the image of the restriction map in $\mathcal O_Y$. It is coherent and nonzero. Indeed, choose $y\in Y$ with $\nu(u,y)<1/t$; Skoda integrability gives $\mathcal I(tu)_y=\mathcal O_{X,y}$.

A \emph{divisor over $Y$} in this chapter means a prime divisor $E$ on a connected smooth compact modification $\pi:Z\to Y$. For a nonzero coherent ideal $\mathfrak a$ on $Y$, let $\operatorname{ord}_E(\mathfrak a)$ be the generic vanishing order of its image on $Z$. It is a finite nonnegative integer.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intrinsic-trace-profile}
For every divisor $E$ over $Y$, the limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-profile}
 \tau_{Y,u}(E)
 :=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{ord}_E\mathfrak a_t(u;Y)}{t}
 =\sup_{t>0}\frac{\operatorname{ord}_E\mathfrak a_t(u;Y)}{t}
\end{equation}
exists and is finite. It also equals the limit along positive integers. The numbers depend only on the multiplier-ideal equivalence class of $u$ on $X$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the modification $\pi:Z\to Y$ carrying $E$ and write $b(t)=\operatorname{ord}_E\mathfrak a_t(u;Y)$ for $t>0$. For $s,t>0$, the second inclusion in \eqref{eq:trace-local-ideal-inputs}, followed by restriction, gives
\[
 \mathfrak a_{s+t}\subseteq\mathfrak a_s\mathfrak a_t,
 \qquad b(s+t)\geq b(s)+b(t).
\]
Moreover $b(t)\geq0$. For fixed $s>0$ and $t=ks+r$, $0\leq r<s$, repeated superadditivity gives $b(t)\geq k b(s)$; for $r=0$ the same conclusion follows without a remainder. Hence
\[
 \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{b(t)}t\geq\frac{b(s)}s.
\]
Taking the supremum over $s$ proves existence and the supremum formula, initially allowing $+\infty$. In particular the positive-integer subsequence has the same limit.

For integer $m$ sufficiently large, \cref{cor:nonclosed-analytic-approximation} and analytic restriction give
\[
 \frac{b(m)}m
 =\nu\bigl((u_m|_Y)\circ\pi,E\bigr).
\]
These numbers are uniformly bounded by \cref{cor:restricted-analytic-divisorial-bound}. The integer limit, and therefore the full supremum, is finite. Finally all the image ideals in the definition are unchanged when $u$ is replaced by a multiplier-ideal equivalent potential. This proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

We call $\tau_{Y,u}$ the \emph{divisorial trace data}. Its realization by a potential on $Y$ will be the main question of this chapter.

\section{Comparison with ordinary restriction}

The restriction theorem for multiplier ideals bounds the trace data by the Lelong numbers of a genuine restriction. Analytic singularities give equality, because their approximants also admit an ordinary upper bound with asymptotically the same logarithmic coefficients.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:trace-naive-comparison}
If $v=u|_Y$ is genuine, then
\[
 \tau_{Y,u}(E)\leq\nu(v\circ\pi,E)
\]
for every divisor $E$ over $Y$. If $u$ has analytic singularities, equality holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The restriction theorem and \cref{lem:bergman-divisorial-error}, applied on $Y$, give for every $t>0$
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_E\mathfrak a_t(u;Y)
 \leq\operatorname{ord}_E\mathcal I(tv)
 \leq t\nu(v\circ\pi,E).
\]
Divide by $t$ and use \cref{thm:intrinsic-trace-profile}.

If $u$ has analytic singularities, apply \cref{lem:analytic-minorant-product-ideals} with $v=0$ and $s=u$, taking $V_m=0$ and $U_m=u_m$. It gives
\[
 u_m\leq(1-\delta_m)u+O(1),\qquad\delta_m\downarrow0.
\]
Restrict to $Y$, pull back by $\pi$, and take the generic Lelong number along $E$. Since $u_m$ has type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$,
\[
 \frac1m\operatorname{ord}_E\mathfrak a_m(u;Y)
 \geq(1-\delta_m)\nu((u|_Y)\circ\pi,E).
\]
Let $m\to\infty$. This proves the reverse inequality and hence equality. All restrictions are genuine because $\nu(u,Y)=0$.
\end{proof}

The inequality can be strict. On a small bidisc in $\mathbb C^2$ with coordinates $(z,w)$, write
\[
 u(z,w)=\max\{-\log(-\log|z|^2),\ \log|w|^2\},
 \qquad Y=\{z=0\}.
\]
The bidisc is chosen with $|z|<\mathrm{e}^{-1}$, and the first function is extended by $-\infty$ at $z=0$. It is psh: the function $s\mapsto-\log(-s)$ is increasing and convex for $s<0$. The maximum is psh as well. For every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathrm{e}^{-tu}\leq(-\log|z|^2)^t,
\]
which is locally integrable. Thus all ambient multiplier ideals are trivial and all the trace data is zero. But $u|_Y=\log|w|^2$ has Lelong number one at the origin. This example is from \cite[Example~8.1.2]{SGPT}.

\section{Realization on a compact curve}\label{sec:curve-trace-realization}

Assume until \cref{sec:trace-envelope} that $Y$ is a curve. Its divisors are points, and the trace numbers become atomic masses. Stokes bounds their sum; the Green operator then turns this measure into a potential with a smooth residual curvature measure.

Set
\[
 D_u=\sum_{p\in Y}\tau_{Y,u}(p)\,\delta_p.
\]
We first prove that this is a finite measure; the displayed sum is initially understood through its finite subsums.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-trace-realization}
Put $M=\int_Y\theta|_Y$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-trace-budget}
 0\leq D_u(Y)\leq M.
\end{equation}
The finite atomic measure $D_u$ defines a canonical ordinary quasi-plurisubharmonic singularity type on $Y$. For every smooth positive form $\eta$ on $Y$ with $\int_Y\eta=1$, that type has a representative $v\in\PSH(Y,\theta|_Y)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-trace-current}
 \theta|_Y+\ddc v=D_u+(M-D_u(Y))\eta.
\end{equation}
Its extended Monge--Amp\`ere measure is $(M-D_u(Y))\eta$. The ordinary type is independent of $\eta$, of the chosen smooth background admitting the construction, and of the ambient representative within its multiplier-ideal class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For integer $m$ let
\[
 D_m=\frac1m\sum_{p\in Y}
       \operatorname{ord}_p\mathfrak a_m(u;Y)\,\delta_p.
\]
A nonzero coherent ideal on a compact smooth curve has finitely many zeros, so this is a finite sum. It is the atomic part of the positive current
$(\theta+\epsilon_m\omega)|_Y+\ddc(u_m|_Y)$.
Stokes on the curve gives
\[
 D_m(Y)\leq M+\epsilon_m\int_Y\omega|_Y.
\]
For any finite subset of $Y$, pass to the limit in the sum of its coefficients using \cref{thm:intrinsic-trace-profile}. Then take the supremum over finite subsets. This gives \eqref{eq:curve-trace-budget}. In particular the positive coefficients have at most countable support, and they define the claimed finite measure. Write $d=D_u(Y)$.

Choose a Green kernel for $\ddc$ on the curve, normalized so that
\[
 \ddc_xG(x,p)=\delta_p-\eta,\qquad G(x,p)\leq0.
\]
The Green kernel of the scalar Laplacian on the compact Riemann surface has the following properties: its singularity is $\log|z(x)-z(p)|^2$, it is bounded above uniformly, and $\sup_p\int_Y|G(x,p)|\eta(x)<\infty$. Subtracting one uniform constant gives the indicated sign. Fubini therefore shows that
\[
 w(x)=\int_YG(x,p)\,\mathrm{d}D_u(p)
\]
is an $L^1$ function, with its canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representative satisfying
$\ddc w=D_u-d\,\eta$. It has Lelong number $\tau_{Y,u}(p)$ at every point $p$.

Since $M\eta-\theta|_Y$ is smooth and has integral zero, the scalar Poisson equation gives a smooth $s$ with $\ddc s=M\eta-\theta|_Y$. Then $v=w+s$ satisfies \eqref{eq:curve-trace-current}. Positivity follows from $M\geq d$. At every atom of $D_u$, the positive Lelong number forces $v=-\infty$. The remaining pole set has zero smooth volume. Removing the pole set from the current consequently leaves exactly $(M-d)\eta$, proving the assertion about the extended measure.

If another smooth volume form or another admissible smooth background is used, the difference of the two constructed representatives has smooth $\ddc$: their atomic parts are the same, and all other terms in their equations are smooth. Elliptic regularity makes their difference smooth, hence bounded. This proves independence of these choices at the level of ordinary type. Independence of the ambient multiplier-ideal representative follows from equality of the numbers defining $D_u$.
\end{proof}

We denote this ordinary type by $\operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{at}}_Y(u)$, the \emph{atomic trace on a curve}. All potentials on a curve having these Lelong numbers have the same multiplier ideals, but they need not have the same ordinary type. The construction selects the type with a smooth residual curvature measure. Additivity of the trace data and linearity of the Green operator give
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{at}}_Y(u+v)
 \sim\operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{at}}_Y(u)+\operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{at}}_Y(v),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{at}}_Y(au)\sim a\operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{at}}_Y(u)
 \quad(a>0).
\]
Here $v\in\QPSH(X)$ also has zero generic Lelong number along $Y$.

Keep $D_m,G,w$ from the proof and put $w_m=\int_YG(\cdot,p)\,\mathrm{d}D_m(p)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve-trace-approximation}
The measures $D_m$ above satisfy $D_m\leq D_u$ and converge to $D_u$ in total variation. Along dyadic indices they increase. With a fixed nonpositive Green kernel, the corresponding potentials $w_{2^j}$ decrease to $w$ and converge in $L^1(Y)$. Each $w_m$ has the ordinary singularity type of $u_m|_Y$.

If the ordinary restriction $u|_Y$ is genuine, it is more singular than the atomic trace:
\[
 u|_Y\preceq \operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{at}}_Y(u).
\]
For analytic $u$, ordinary equivalence holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The supremum formula \eqref{eq:trace-profile} gives $D_m\leq D_u$ coefficient by coefficient. Since $D_u$ is a finite atomic measure, dominated convergence of its coefficients proves $D_m(Y)\to D_u(Y)$ and hence total-variation convergence. Superadditivity at $m+m$ gives $D_{2m}\geq D_m$. The sign of $G$ then makes $w_{2^j}$ decrease. Monotone convergence for $-G$ identifies its pointwise limit with $w$, including possible poles. The uniform $L^1$ bound for the kernel also gives
\[
 \|w_m-w\|_{L^1(\eta)}
 \leq C\|D_m-D_u\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Each $D_m$ is finitely supported. Locally both $w_m$ and $u_m|_Y$ are its logarithmic singular part plus a bounded remainder. Thus their ordinary types agree.

If $a=u|_Y$ is genuine, \cref{prop:trace-naive-comparison} gives $\nu(a,p)\geq\tau_{Y,u}(p)$ at every point. Since $\ddc w=D_u-D_u(Y)\eta$, \cref{lem:divisorial-potential-domination} implies $a\preceq w$. If $u$ is analytic, \cref{prop:trace-naive-comparison} identifies its finitely many restricted logarithmic coefficients with those of $D_u$. Its bounded local remainders then give ordinary equivalence.
\end{proof}

\section{The Poisson equation in the atomic trace type}

Write $d=D_u(Y)$ and let $w$ be the Green representative above. In its ordinary type, the atoms of the full curvature measure are fixed. Prescribing the extended measure therefore reduces to a Poisson equation for the bounded remainder, with total mass $M-d$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-atomic-lp-equation}
Let $\mathrm{d}V$ be a smooth positive volume form on $Y$, and let $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, with $\int_Yf\,\mathrm{d}V>0$.
There is a solution in the atomic trace type to
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\theta|_Y}(v)=c f\,\mathrm{d}V,
 \qquad c>0,
\]
if and only if $M>d$. In that case
\[
 c=\frac{M-d}{\int_Yf\,\mathrm{d}V},
\]
and $v$ is unique up to an additive constant. If $M=d$, there is instead a unique such ordinary-type solution up to constants with extended measure zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume $M>d$ and use the Green potential $w$ above. Choose the displayed $c$ and solve
\[
 \ddc s=d\,\eta+c f\,\mathrm{d}V-\theta|_Y.
\]
The right side has integral zero. The scalar elliptic estimate gives $s\in W^{2,p}(Y)$, unique up to constants. In real dimension two the Sobolev embedding for $p>1$ makes $s$ continuous and bounded. The potential $v=w+s$ has the atomic trace type and satisfies
\[
 \theta|_Y+\ddc v=D_u+c f\,\mathrm{d}V\geq0.
\]
Its extended measure is $c f\,\mathrm{d}V$, since this density does not charge the pole set while the atoms do. For $M=d$ the identical construction with $c=0$ has a smooth right side and produces the asserted zero-measure solution.

To prove necessity and uniqueness, let $a$ be any $\theta|_Y$-plurisubharmonic potential in the ordinary type of $w$, and put $\mu=\theta|_Y+\ddc a$. Its Lelong numbers, and hence its atoms, are exactly those of $D_u$. Thus $\mu-D_u$ is a positive measure and
\[
 \ddc(a-w)=\mu-D_u-\theta|_Y+d\,\eta
\]
has a smooth lower bound. The difference has a bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic representative, because the ordinary types agree. The curvature measure of a bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic function on a curve does not charge polar sets, by \cref{prop:bounded-products}. After addition of smooth forms the same assertion applies to $\mu-D_u$. Consequently
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\theta|_Y}(a)=\mu-D_u,
 \qquad
 \int_Y\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\theta|_Y}(a)=M-d.
\]
Integrating any proposed equation forces the displayed value of $c$, so a positive $c$ requires $M>d$. If two potentials solve with the same density and scalar, their full currents both equal $D_u+c f\,\mathrm{d}V$. Their difference has zero $\ddc$ and is therefore constant on the compact connected curve. The argument also proves uniqueness in the zero-measure case.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact realization in higher dimension}

For a general submanifold $Y$, the remaining realization problem is to find $v\in\QPSH(Y)$ such that
\[
 \nu(v\circ\pi,E)=\tau_{Y,u}(E)
\]
for every divisor on every smooth compact modification. The envelope of \cref{thm:trace-profile-model-envelope} has Lelong numbers at least this large. An exact representative would force equality for that envelope in every sufficiently large background. Analytic ambient singularities and the curve fibrations of \cref{thm:curve-fibration-exact-trace} supply such representatives. For arbitrary data, \cref{thm:restricted-analytic-envelope-limit} identifies the limit of the restricted analytic envelopes, including in the original background $\theta|_Y$. Its proof gives lower bounds for the limiting divisorial numbers; equality remains the realization question.

For pulled-back backgrounds, \cref{thm:trace-envelope-modification} gives equality of the envelopes on different compact models and identifies the limit after a vanishing Hermitian perturbation. Comparing ordinary types under arbitrary changes of background is a separate problem. For curve fibrations, sufficiently large backgrounds give one ordinary type, compatible with addition and positive homogeneity. For general data, inclusion of competitor families orders the envelopes but does not identify their ordinary types. Additivity of the numerical data alone does not settle this question.

The K\"ahler trace of \cite[Section~8.1]{SGPT} is defined through a singularity topology and takes values in $P$-equivalence classes. The curve identification in \cref{prop:curve-trace-source-distance} uses that topology on $Y$. Extending this comparison to higher-dimensional Hermitian manifolds requires a corresponding topology and control of the restricted approximations in it. The exceptional-divisor compatibility proved in \cref{thm:trace-exceptional-difference} concerns the numerical data. The product example in \cref{prop:exact-trace-infinite-representatives} shows that even exact realization and equality of all multiplier ideals leave the extended mass of a representative undetermined. The mass formula in \cref{sec:resolved-trace-masses} applies instead to an actual restriction in a closed background, with a finite divisorial part and bounded remainder on a K\"ahler model, whose residual class is then nef; it does not resolve the general intrinsic-trace problem.
