\subsection{Second derivatives at positive exponent}
\label{sec:numa-positive-exponent-regularity}

On the band where the density vanishes, the exponential factor plays no role, and the radial equation determines the slope of the solution independently of the exponent. Consequently the positive-exponent solutions have the same failure of second differentiability as the selected limit $u_*$. We keep the notation $A$, $\Phi$, $k_\lambda$ and the transition radius $t_*$ of \cref{sec:numa-selected-limit}.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:numa-positive-exponent-nonc2}
For the symmetric projective data of \cref{thm:numa-selected-limit}, let $u_\lambda$ be the bounded solution of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_\omega(u_\lambda)
       =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda}f\omega_X^3,
 \qquad\lambda>0.
\]
On the open band $\{-1<r<1\}$, $u_\lambda-u_*$ is constant. Both potentials are $C^{1,1}$ there and fail to be $C^2$ at every point of the two hypersurfaces $r=\pm t_*$. In particular, none of these positive-exponent solutions is a globally $C^2$ potential.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:symmetric-equation-clusters}, $u_\lambda$ is $K$-invariant, so \cref{lem:numa-radial-flux} applies to it; write $u_\lambda+\rho=G_\lambda(r)$ on $U$ and $h_\lambda=G_\lambda'$. On $-1<r<1$ the coefficient $k_\lambda$ is identically zero, and exchange invariance makes $h_\lambda$ odd. On the positive half-band, the first-contact and no-departure arguments in the proof of \cref{thm:numa-selected-limit}, which use only these two facts and the lemma, apply verbatim: the first branch solves $h'=-z_0$, reaches $-p$ at $t_*$, and cannot leave that lower boundary to its right because $(h+p)'=p'-z_0<0$ on every proposed departure interval. Therefore
\[
 h_\lambda(r)=
 \begin{cases}
  p(r),&-1<r\leq-t_*,\\
  -\displaystyle\int_0^r z_0(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,&-t_*\leq r\leq t_*,\\
  -p(r),&t_*\leq r<1.
 \end{cases}
\]
This is the derivative of $u_*+\rho$. Its primitive differs from $G_\lambda$ by one constant on the connected band. The claimed local $C^{1,1}$ regularity follows from the explicit slope.

At $t_*\in(0,1)$ the two one-sided derivatives of this slope are
\[
 h_\lambda'(t_*-)=-z_0(t_*)<0,
 \qquad
 h_\lambda'(t_*+)=-p'(t_*)>0.
\]
These unequal derivatives prevent the scalar primitive from having a second derivative at $t_*$. Through every point of that level set, the submersion $r$ provides a smooth curve parametrized by $r$, transferring the failure to $u_\lambda$. Smooth $\rho$ cannot cancel the jump. Oddness gives the conclusion at $-t_*$, and the identical slope proves it for $u_*$.
\end{proof}

The zero set of $f$ contains the whole band. This example therefore does not concern the smooth strictly positive densities in the Hermitian Calabi--Yau theorem. It shows that smoothness of a nonnegative density alone does not give second-order regularity, even when the equation includes a strictly increasing exponential factor.
