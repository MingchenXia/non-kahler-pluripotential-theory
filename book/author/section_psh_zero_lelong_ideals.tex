A quasi-plurisubharmonic function with zero Lelong numbers can still be
unbounded, but adding it to another potential changes no multiplier ideal.
The integrability of $\mathrm e^{-tq}$ in the next lemma is a case of
Skoda's theorem \cite[Lemma~5.6]{DemAM11}; the proof below derives it
directly from the local Bergman estimates.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:zero-lelong-ideal-neutrality}\index{Multiplier ideal!zero Lelong residual}
Let $q$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on a complex manifold and suppose that
$\nu(q,x)=0$ at every point. Then $\mathrm e^{-tq}$ is locally integrable
for every $t>0$. For every quasi-plurisubharmonic $v$ and every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathcal I\bigl(t(v+q)\bigr)=\mathcal I(tv).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a point $x$ and add a smooth correction to make $q$ plurisubharmonic
on a bounded pseudoconvex coordinate neighborhood. Form the weighted
Bergman space of \cref{prop:bergman-local-estimates} at the real weight $t$,
as allowed by the remark following that proposition.
If every member vanished at $x$, the Cauchy estimates, together with the
local upper bound for $q$, would give
\[
 \sup_{\|g\|_t\leq1}|g(z)|\leq C|z-x|
\]
near $x$. The Bergman potential $t^{-1}\log\sup_{\|g\|_t\leq1}|g|^2$ would
consequently have Lelong number at least $1/t$ at $x$, contradicting
\eqref{eq:bergman-lelong-bounds}.
There is therefore a weighted square-integrable holomorphic function
nonzero at $x$. On a smaller neighborhood it is bounded away from zero,
so its weighted norm proves integrability of $\mathrm e^{-tq}$.
Smooth corrections do not change this conclusion. Thus $q\sim_{\mathcal I}0$;
the addition rule in \cref{lem:hermitian-i-calculus}, whose proof is local, gives
$v+q\sim_{\mathcal I}v$, which is precisely the asserted equality at every weight.
\end{proof}
