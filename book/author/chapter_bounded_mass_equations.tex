\chapter{Big backgrounds under bounded mass}
\label{chap:bounded-mass-equations}

\begingroup
\predisplaypenalty=10000

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq1$, let $\theta$ be a smooth big real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, not necessarily closed or semipositive, and let $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. As in \cref{chap:relative-finite-measures}, we solve the equations $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$, $\lambda>0$, and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu$, $c>0$, for an arbitrary nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu$, with $u$ in the relative class of $\varphi$. Since $\theta$ need not be semipositive, the smaller-background model condition used there is replaced by upper and positive lower mass bounds.

Throughout this chapter we make two standing assumptions. First, $X$ has the bounded mass property of \cref{def:bmp}: $v_+(\omega)<+\infty$ for one, and hence every, Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$ (\cref{prop:bmp-metric}). Second, $\varphi$ satisfies \eqref{eq:li-model-condition}: it is an ordinary model, $P_\theta[\varphi]=\varphi$ (\cref{def:singularity-envelope}), and its lower singularity volume $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)$ (\cref{def:singularity-volumes}) is positive. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ for capacity and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$.

The two assumptions give the two bounds needed for approximation: every member of the relative class of $\varphi$ has Monge--Amp\`ere mass at least $\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0$ and at most $\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)<\infty$. With these bounds, first moments relative to a reference prevent collapse and keep limits in the relative class, while higher moments control convergence against a smoothed measure. This first gives solutions in the class of the potential $V_\theta$ with minimal singularities.

To pass to the reference $\varphi$, we project a solution in the relative class of $V_\theta$ onto the relative class of $\varphi$. The projection keeps only part of the exponentially weighted measure, but weighted addition can fill any remaining deficit; maximizing the weighted mass therefore gives the required equation. The same contact estimates keep limits in the relative class when the model reference varies in the volume comparison of \cref{sec:model-volume-comparison}.

For general finite non-pluripolar data the solutions lie in the relative full-mass class; for $L^p$ densities, a stronger moment estimate shows that they differ from $\varphi$ by a bounded function. A positive exponent determines the solution uniquely; at exponent zero only the scalar is unique.

For $a\in\PSH(X,\theta)$, write
\[
 \mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
                  =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi),
 \qquad \theta_a=\theta+\ddc a,\qquad \mu_a=\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a).
\]
Here $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$ is the relative full-mass class of \cref{def:relative-volume-class}, $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi)$ is the relative envelope class of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}, and the two classes coincide by \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}. The measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a)$ is the non-pluripolar Monge--Amp\`ere measure of \cref{thm:arbitrary-extended-ma}; it is finite by \cref{prop:bmp-finite}. Put
\[
 \ell=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0,
 \qquad M=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)<\infty.
\]
By \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}, every $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-relative-mass-interval}
 \ell\leq\mu_u(X)\leq M.
\end{equation}
We use the stretching envelopes $Q_b(u,w)=P_\theta(bu-(b-1)w)$ introduced before \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}, the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta(u_1,\ldots,u_N)=P_\theta(\min_iu_i)$, and $V_\theta=P_\theta(0)$ (\cref{sec:obstacle-envelopes}). For $a\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, \cref{prop:relative-envelope-equality} gives $P_\theta[a]=\varphi$, and hence, as in \eqref{eq:relative-normalized-model-bound},
\[
 a-\sup_Xa\leq\varphi.
\]
We refer to this consequence of the identity $P_\theta[a]=\varphi$ as the envelope identity. Finally, \emph{zero domination} refers to \cref{thm:big-relative-domination} with coefficient zero: if $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$, $v\preceq\varphi$ and $\mu_u(\{u<v\})=0$, then $u\geq v$.

\section{Equations for finite measures}
\label{sec:big-general-equation-setting}

A positive exponent determines a unique solution in the relative class. At exponent zero, the equation determines its positive scalar, but normalized potentials may still differ, as in \cref{cor:analytic-nonuniqueness-envelope-class}.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:li-general-measure}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!bounded-mass regime}
For every nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu$ on $X$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item There are $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and a unique $c>0$ such that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu$.
\item For each $\lambda>0$ there is a unique $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ such that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
If $\mu=f\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ for some $p>1$, the solutions in both parts may be taken with $u\sim\varphi$.
\end{theorem}

Part (1) is \cref{thm:big-model-normalized}, part (2) is \cref{thm:big-model-exponential}, and the assertion for $L^p$ densities is \cref{cor:big-model-lp-type}.
