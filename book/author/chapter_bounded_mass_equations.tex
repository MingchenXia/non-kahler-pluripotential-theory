\chapter{Big backgrounds under bounded mass}
\label{chap:bounded-mass-equations}

A minimal-class solution can be projected to a prescribed model, but projection may discard part of its weighted measure. Weighted addition restores the missing part, so a maximal supersolution solves the equation. Bounded upper mass and positive lower reference volume provide the moment and compactness estimates needed in this construction. Higher moments of the datum recover ordinary reference type for $L^p$ densities.

Assume that $X$ has bounded mass, fix a smooth big real $(1,1)$-form $\theta$, and let $\varphi$ satisfy \eqref{eq:li-model-condition}. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ for capacity and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. Write
\[
 \mathcal E_\varphi=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta,\varphi)
                  =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,\varphi),
 \qquad \theta_a=\theta+\ddc a,\qquad \mu_a=\mathrm{MA}_\theta(a),
\]
where the equality of classes is \cref{thm:relative-equivalence}, and put
\[
 \ell=\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)>0,
 \qquad M=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)<\infty.
\]
Every $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:big-relative-mass-interval}
 \ell\leq\mu_u(X)\leq M.
\end{equation}
We use the stretching envelopes $Q_b$ of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}.

\section{The general-measure equations}
\label{sec:big-general-equation-setting}

A positive exponent determines a unique solution in the relative class. At exponent zero, the equation determines its positive scalar; normalized potentials may still differ, as in \cref{cor:analytic-nonuniqueness-envelope-class}.

\Needspace{22\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:li-general-measure}
For every nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item There are $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ and a unique $c>0$ such that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=c\mu$.
\item For each $\lambda>0$ there is a unique $u\in\mathcal E_\varphi$ such that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
If $\mu=f\,\mathrm{d}V$ with $0\leq f\in L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ for some $p>1$, the solutions in both parts may be taken with $u\simeq\varphi$.
\end{theorem}

These assumptions also give $v_-(\omega)>0$. Indeed, choose $C>0$ with $\theta\leq C\omega$. Since $\varphi\preceq0$, \cref{thm:volume-monotonicity} gives
\[
 0<\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(\varphi)
 \leq\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_{C\omega}(0)
 =C^n v_-(\omega).
\]
