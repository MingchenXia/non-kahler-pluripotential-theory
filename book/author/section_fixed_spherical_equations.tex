\section{Singular measures on shrinking spheres}
\label{sec:fixed-spherical-equations}

A measure with bounded density with respect to the Monge--Amp\`ere measure of a bounded potential admits exponential equations by \cref{thm:bounded-subsolution}. The following construction turns this analytic hypothesis into a test against a probability measure fixed by a coordinate ball. It applies to singular data on every Hermitian background, including backgrounds with zero lower mass (\cref{def:bmp}).

Fix a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, with $n\geq2$, a holomorphic coordinate ball centered at a point $p$, and a smooth cutoff $0\leq\chi\leq1$ supported in that ball and equal to one near a smaller closed ball. Choose $r_0>0$ so small that all spheres of radii $r_j=r_0 2^{-j}$, $j\geq1$, lie in the smaller ball. Let $\sigma_j$ denote the normalized Euclidean surface measures of these spheres, which are probability measures, extended by zero to $X$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-spherical-probability}
 S_n=\sum_{j\geq1}r_j^{n/2},\qquad
 \rho=\frac1{S_n}\sum_{j\geq1}r_j^{n/2}\sigma_j.
\end{equation}
For a finite positive measure $\nu$, define
\[
 M_\rho(\nu)=\left\|\frac{\mathrm d\nu}{\mathrm d\rho}\right\|_{L^\infty(\rho)},
\]
with value $+\infty$ when $\nu\not\ll\rho$. The measure $\rho$ is chosen before $\nu$ or the exponent in the equation. The test depends on the chart and the radii only through the measure $\rho$ on $X$. Requiring $M_\rho(\nu)<\infty$ for the measure $\rho$ of some coordinate ball therefore gives a sufficient condition that does not depend on a specified chart.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fixed-spherical-reference}\index{Non-pluripolar measure!shrinking spheres}\index{Subsolution!singular measure}
The measure $\rho$ is a singular non-pluripolar probability. There are $k>0$ and $u_\rho\in\PSH(X,h)\cap C^{0,1/2}(X)$ with $\sup_Xu_\rho=0$ such that
\[
 h+\ddc u_\rho\geq h/2,\qquad
 \mathrm{MA}_h(u_\rho)\geq k\rho,\qquad
 0<\mathrm{MA}_h(u_\rho)(X)<\infty.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the coordinate ball write $R=|z|$ and set
\[
 \psi_j=\chi\log\max\{R^2,r_j^2\}+(1-\chi),
\]
extending $\psi_j$ by one outside it. On the transition region of $\chi$, $R$ is bounded away from zero and exceeds all $r_j$. Hence all $\psi_j$ agree there with the same smooth function. Where $\chi=1$ they are plurisubharmonic, and outside its support they are constant. One fixed $K\geq1$ therefore satisfies $\ddc\psi_j\geq-Kh$ for every $j$. Put
\[
 Z=2K\sum_{j\geq1}\sqrt{r_j},\qquad
 d_j=\frac{\sqrt{r_j}}Z,\qquad u_0=\sum_{j\geq1}d_j\psi_j.
\]
The estimate $\|\psi_j\|_\infty\leq C(1+|\log r_j|)$ gives uniform convergence, and $\sum d_j=1/(2K)$ yields $h+\ddc u_0\geq h/2$.

For two points of the inner ball at Euclidean distance $t$, the reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
 |\log\max\{|z|^2,r_j^2\}-\log\max\{|z'|^2,r_j^2\}|
 \leq2\log(1+t/r_j).
\]
The contribution of $r_j\geq t$ is at most $Ct\sum_{r_j\geq t}r_j^{-1/2}\leq C\sqrt t$. For $r_j<t$, summing the geometric tail gives at most $C\sqrt t\sum_{\ell\geq0}2^{-\ell/2}(1+\ell)$. On the cutoff transition and outside, the series is a fixed smooth function. These estimates give a local $1/2$-H\"older bound near every point. A finite cover and the bound on the oscillation give the global bound.

On the smaller ball, the currents $\ddc\psi_j$ are closed and positive. Expand the $n$th power of $h+\sum_{j\leq N}d_j\ddc\psi_j$ there. All terms are positive, and we retain the diagonal terms $d_j^n(\ddc\psi_j)^n$, which equal $d_j^n\sigma_j$ by \cref{lem:spherical-bounded-product}. The partial sums $\sum_{j\leq N}d_j\psi_j$ are $h$-plurisubharmonic and converge uniformly to $u_0$, so their Monge--Amp\`ere measures converge weakly to $\mathrm{MA}_h(u_0)$. Testing against nonnegative functions, and retaining first any fixed finite number of diagonal terms, therefore gives
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(u_0)\geq\sum_{j\geq1}d_j^n\sigma_j
                       =\frac{S_n}{Z^n}\rho.
\]
Here the right-hand side is supported strictly inside the smaller ball, so the inequality extends to $X$. Each $\sigma_j$ is non-pluripolar by the same lemma; countable additivity gives that property for $\rho$. The countable union of the spheres has zero smooth volume, which proves singularity. Local bounded-product estimates on a finite cover make the mass of $u_0$ finite, and the retained inequality makes it positive. Set $k=S_n/Z^n$ and subtract $\sup_Xu_0$ to obtain $u_\rho$.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fixed-spherical-homogeneous}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!singular spherical data}
Let $\nu$ be a finite nonzero positive measure with $M_\rho(\nu)<\infty$. There are $c>0$ and a H\"older continuous $w\in\PSH(X,h)$ satisfying
\[
 \sup_Xw=0,\qquad\mathrm{MA}_h(w)=c\nu,
 \qquad0<\mathrm{MA}_h(w)(X)<\infty.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:fixed-spherical-reference}, $\nu\leq (M_\rho(\nu)/k)\mathrm{MA}_h(u_\rho)$, where $u_\rho$ is H\"older continuous with exponent $1/2$. Apply \cref{thm:holder-reference-homogeneous-equation}. The equation and $0<c<\infty$ give its positive finite mass.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:fixed-spherical-exponential}\index{Monge--Ampere equation@Monge--Amp\`ere equation!singular spherical data}
Let $\nu$ be a finite nonzero positive measure with $M_\rho(\nu)<\infty$. Then $\nu$ is non-pluripolar and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(u_\rho)\geq\frac{k}{M_\rho(\nu)}\nu.
\]
For every $\lambda>0$ there is a unique bounded $U_\lambda\in\PSH(X,h)$ satisfying
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_h(U_\lambda)=\mathrm e^{\lambda U_\lambda}\nu,
 \qquad0<\mathrm{MA}_h(U_\lambda)(X)<\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $\nu\leq M_\rho(\nu)\rho$, the non-pluripolarity of $\rho$ and the inequality $\mathrm{MA}_h(u_\rho)\geq k\rho$ of \cref{prop:fixed-spherical-reference} prove the first assertion. Write $\alpha=\mathrm{MA}_h(u_\rho)$. Then $\nu=f\alpha$ with $0\leq f\leq M_\rho(\nu)/k$. The form $h>0$ is semipositive and big, so \cref{thm:bounded-subsolution} gives the unique bounded solution. Its boundedness and the equation give positive finite mass.
\end{proof}

The scalar in the exponential equation is fixed: adding a constant to $U_\lambda$ changes its right-hand side. The theorem imposes no supremum normalization and does not assert uniqueness among unbounded potentials. For example, it applies to $\nu=g\sigma_j$ for every $j$ and every bounded nonnegative function $g$ with $\nu\neq0$. The test is only a sufficient condition: its failure is no obstruction to solvability. The homogeneous equation is supplied separately by \cref{cor:fixed-spherical-homogeneous}, with a positive coefficient obtained as part of the solution. The construction does not identify a largest admissible coefficient or establish uniqueness for that equation.
