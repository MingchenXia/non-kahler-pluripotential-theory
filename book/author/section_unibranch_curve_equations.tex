\section{Logarithmic equations on normalized curves}
\label{sec:unibranch-curve-equations}
\index{Unibranch space!torus normalization}

On a curve the pulled-back background can be degenerate, but its total mass still determines the equation. A periodic Green function on the normalization compensates for the degeneracy. The normalization also gives direct control of descent, of the atoms at the poles and of continuity.

Let $X$ be a connected compact reduced unibranch curve whose normalization is a torus:
\[
 \pi:E=\mathbb C/\Lambda\longrightarrow X,
\]
a finite bijective holomorphic map, where $\Lambda\subset\mathbb C$ is a lattice. It is biholomorphic outside a finite set. Let $h$ be a Hermitian form on $X$ in the sense of the beginning of this appendix. In the flat coordinate $z=x+\mathrm iy$ on $E$, set
\[
 dA=dx\,dy,\qquad V=\int_EdA,\qquad
 \beta_0=dA/V,\qquad \beta=\pi^*h=b\beta_0.
\]
Here $b$ is smooth and nonnegative, positive over $X_{\rm reg}$, and may vanish at the other points. Put $H=\int_E\beta>0$. Every two-form on a curve is closed, and this is essential for the following construction.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:torus-logarithmic-green}
There is a real periodic distribution $G$ with mean zero, smooth off zero, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:torus-logarithmic-green}
 \ddc G=\delta_0-\beta_0,\qquad
 G(z)=\log|z|^2+\text{a smooth function near }0.
\end{equation}
For $r\in L^p(E,\beta_0)$, $p>1$, its convolution satisfies
\[
 \ddc\bigl(G*(r\beta_0)\bigr)
       =\left(r-\int_Er\beta_0\right)\beta_0.
\]
The convolution is bounded and continuous, and it is smooth if $r$ is smooth.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With the normalization of $\ddc$ fixed in the Conventions, $\ddc v=(\Delta v)/(4\pi)\,dA$ and $\ddc\log|z|^2=\delta_0$, as in \cref{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal}. Cutting off this logarithm in a coordinate disk, with a cutoff equal to one near zero, gives a function $L$ on $E$ with $\ddc L=\delta_0+q\beta_0$, where $q$ is smooth and $\int_Eq\beta_0=-1$ by Stokes' theorem.

Let $e_\xi$ be the characters of the torus, with $-\Delta e_\xi=\lambda_\xi e_\xi$. The nonconstant characters have $\lambda_\xi>0$, growing quadratically on the dual lattice. Define the correction $S$, of mean zero, by its Fourier coefficients
\[
 S_\xi=\frac{4\pi}{V}\frac{(q+1)_\xi}{\lambda_\xi}
       \quad(\xi\ne0),\qquad S_0=0,
\]
where $(q+1)_\xi$ is the Fourier coefficient of $q+1$. Integration by parts shows that these coefficients decay faster than any power of $\lambda_\xi$. Hence the series and all its derivatives converge uniformly, and $\ddc S=-(q+1)\beta_0$. Conjugate symmetry of the coefficients makes $S$ real. Subtracting the mean of $L+S$ from $L+S$ proves \eqref{eq:torus-logarithmic-green}. In particular, $G$ belongs to $L^r$ for every finite $r$.

The convolution uses the group law and the Haar probability $\beta_0$. Write $K=G*(r\beta_0)$. Testing against smooth functions and using Fubini's theorem proves its distributional equation. Smooth input gives smooth output by the same Fourier argument. For $p'=p/(p-1)$, H\"older's inequality gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|K\|_\infty&\leq\|G\|_{p'}\|r\|_p,\\
 |K(z+s)-K(z)|
     &\leq\|r\|_p\|G(\cdot+s)-G\|_{p'}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Approximating $G$ by smooth functions shows that translation is continuous in $L^{p'}$, and hence that $K$ is continuous.
\end{proof}

Choose finitely many distinct points $q_1,\ldots,q_k\in E$ and weights $a_j>0$, possibly none, with
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A&=\sum_j a_j<H,& M&=H-A,\\
 \Phi&=G*((H-b)\beta_0),&
 \Psi&=\Phi+\sum_j a_jG(\cdot-q_j).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The function $\Phi$ is smooth, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unibranch-curve-reference}
 \beta+\ddc\Psi=M\beta_0+\sum_j a_j\delta_{q_j}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\Psi$ is a $\beta$-plurisubharmonic function, not identically $-\infty$, whose logarithmic poles are exactly the points $q_j$, with coefficients $a_j$ measured on the normalization. The proper bijection $\pi$ is a homeomorphism, so we may define $\psi=\Psi\circ\pi^{-1}$; its disk inequalities are proved below. The ordinary type of $\psi$ consists of the potentials $w\in\PSH(X,h)$ with
\[
 w\leq\psi+C,\qquad \psi\leq w+C
\]
for a finite constant $C$, the inequalities being understood for extended real values.

\Needspace{15\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation}
Let $0\leq f\in L^p(E,\beta_0)$ for some finite $p>1$, with $F=\int_Ef\beta_0>0$, and set $\nu=\pi_*(f\beta_0)$. Every potential in the ordinary type of $\psi$ has extended non-pluripolar mass $M$. In this type there is a unique normalized solution of
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,h}(u)=c\nu,
 \qquad \sup_Xu=0,
 \qquad c=\frac{H-A}{F}>0.
\]
Every member of the type that solves $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,h}(w)=c'\nu$ for some constant $c'>0$, with no regularity assumed, has $c'=c$ and differs from the normalized solution by a constant.

The solution is continuous with real values off its finite pole set, and continuous at the poles as a map into $\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$ with the order topology. Its residual relative to $\psi$ extends to a continuous real function on $X$. If there are no poles, the solution itself is continuous with real values everywhere. The type and the equation do not depend on the choice of a resolution or of a common refinement. The measure $\nu$ is non-pluripolar.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We construct the solution on the normalization with the Green function of \cref{lem:torus-logarithmic-green} and descend it by lifting disks. The mass and uniqueness assertions then follow from the atoms of the curvature currents at the poles. Set $c=M/F$ and
\[
 v=G*((cf-M)\beta_0),\qquad U=\Psi+v.
\]
The lemma gives a bounded continuous $v$ and the positive current
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unibranch-curve-solution-current}
 \beta+\ddc U=cf\beta_0+\sum_j a_j\delta_{q_j}.
\end{equation}
In each disk, adding a smooth local primitive of $\beta$ gives a function with positive distributional Laplacian, and hence a subharmonic representative. Continuity off the poles and the explicit logarithmic values identify this representative with $U$ as defined above. The same argument applies to $\Psi$. The supremum $S=\sup_EU$ is finite, and we put $u=(U-S)\circ\pi^{-1}$.

Every holomorphic disk $g\colon \Delta\to X$ lifts holomorphically to $\widetilde g=\pi^{-1}\circ g$. Indeed, for a nonconstant disk the inverse image of the finite singular set is discrete, and off this set the lift is holomorphic. Since $\pi^{-1}$ is a homeomorphism, $\widetilde g$ is continuous on the whole disk. Near an omitted point its image therefore stays in a bounded coordinate neighborhood in $E$, and the removable singularity theorem in one variable extends it holomorphically. A constant disk lifts to a constant map, and a nonconstant disk cannot have image in a finite set.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Choose a smooth local primitive $\eta$ of $\beta$ near the lifted image, that is, $\ddc\eta=\beta$. Then $\eta\circ\widetilde g$ is a primitive of $g^*h$, and
\[
 u\circ g+\eta\circ\widetilde g=(U-S+\eta)\circ\widetilde g
\]
is subharmonic or identically $-\infty$. That the composition is subharmonic follows from decreasing smooth local regularizations and the holomorphic chain rule for the Laplacian. Any other primitive on the disk differs by a harmonic function. These are exactly the disk tests of \cref{def:space-theta-psh}, and the same argument shows that $\psi\in\PSH(X,h)$. A constant disk at a pole has the permitted restriction $\equiv-\infty$. A nonconstant lift meeting $q_j$ with order $\ell$ gives the pole coefficient $\ell a_j$.

The homeomorphism $\pi$ gives continuity off the poles. Near a pole, the smooth logarithmic remainder of $\Psi$ and the bounded continuous function $v$ give continuity with extended values. The residual is the descent of $v-S$, including at the poles, so no difference of two infinite values is involved. The solution belongs to the stated ordinary type.

For the mass and uniqueness assertions, let $w$ be any member of the type and put $W=w\circ\pi$. Pulling back along coordinate disks of the normalization shows that $W$ is $\beta$-plurisubharmonic and not identically $-\infty$. The two type inequalities make $W$ locally bounded away from $Q=\{q_j\}$, and at each pole they give
\[
 W=a_j\log|z-q_j|^2+O(1),
\]
with value $-\infty$ at $q_j$. The positive Radon measure $T_W=\beta+\ddc W$ has total mass $H$ by Stokes' theorem. Its atom at $q_j$ has weight exactly $a_j$. This follows from the planar atom identity of \cref{lem:planar-multiplier-ideal}. Explicitly, choose a smooth local primitive $\eta$ of $\beta$ near $q_j$, and let $m(r)$ be the circular mean of $W+\eta$ around $q_j$. Since $\ddc(W+\eta)=T_W$, it satisfies
\[
 m'(r)=\frac{2T_W(B_r)}r
\]
for almost every $r$, by the divergence theorem and regularization. If $\tau=T_W(\{q_j\})$, integration gives $m(r)/\log r^2\to\tau$, while the bounded logarithmic remainder gives the limit $a_j$.

Hence the non-pluripolar product of $W$, defined in dimension one by truncation, is
\[
 \langle\beta+\ddc W\rangle=\mathbf1_{E\setminus Q}T_W.
\]
Indeed, on each relatively compact set avoiding $Q$, a sufficiently low truncation equals $W$, and the indicator removes the poles. Its mass is $H-A=M$ for every member of the type. For \eqref{eq:unibranch-curve-solution-current}, removing the atoms leaves $cf\beta_0$. The definition \eqref{eq:space-extended-ma} gives the asserted equation on $X$. A locally pluripolar set pulls back to a set on which some quasi-subharmonic function, not identically $-\infty$, equals $-\infty$; such sets have zero flat area. Hence $\nu$ is non-pluripolar.

Suppose that $w$ solves the equation with a constant $c'>0$. Comparing total masses gives $c'F=M$, so $c'=c$. Since $\pi$ is a Borel homeomorphism and the atoms are fixed, $T_W=T_U$. Thus $\ddc(W-U)=0$, where $W-U$ is an integrable function. Every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of $W-U$ vanishes, so the difference is constant almost everywhere. Subharmonic functions are determined by their averages over shrinking disks, so the values of $W$ and $U$ differ by the same constant everywhere, and the same holds on $X$. This includes members of the type with no regularity assumed.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Finally, a resolution $\rho\colon Y\to X$ factors through $E$ by the disk-lifting argument. The resulting proper holomorphic map $Y\to E$ has generic degree one. For smooth compact curves, the multiplicities in a fiber sum to the degree, so every fiber is a single point of multiplicity one, and the map is biholomorphic. The same holds for every common refinement, which proves the independence assertion directly.
\end{proof}

The integrability hypothesis on $f$ refers to flat area on $E$. On $X_{\rm reg}$, the density of $\nu$ with respect to $h$ is $(f/b)\circ\pi^{-1}$; both measures give the finite singular set zero mass. No positive lower bound for $b$ and no bound for the density on $X$ is required.

\subsection{A projective cusp with a degenerate pullback}
\label{subsec:unibranch-projective-cusp}

The following example has a normalization of genus one and a singularity that is not normal. Its background and its equation can both be computed explicitly.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{example}\label{ex:unibranch-elliptic-cusp}\index{Unibranch space!projective cusp}
There is a projective unibranch curve with one ordinary cusp, normalized by $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$, for which the restricted Fubini--Study background has mass $H=6$. For the reference with one pole of weight one at the preimage of the cusp, every potential of its ordinary type has mass five. With
\[
 f(z)=1+\tfrac12\cos(2\pi\operatorname{Re}z),\qquad
 \nu=\pi_*(f\beta_0),
\]
the normalized equation has $c=5$. Its residual is continuous on the curve, although the density on the regular locus with respect to the background is unbounded at the cusp. That density belongs to $L^r(X,h)$ exactly for $1\leq r<2$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
For the square lattice define
\[
 x(z)=z^{-2}+\sum_{\substack{\lambda\in\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z\\\lambda\ne0}}
       \bigl((z-\lambda)^{-2}-\lambda^{-2}\bigr),
 \qquad y=x'.
\]
The summands are $O(|\lambda|^{-3})$ on compact sets away from the lattice, so the series converges normally. Pairing opposite lattice points shows that $x$ is even. The differentiated series is absolutely and normally convergent, and reindexing shows that $y$ is periodic. Thus $x(z+\omega)-x(z)$ is constant for $\omega=1,\mathrm i$; evaluating at $-\omega/2$ and using evenness shows that this constant is zero. Both functions are meromorphic on $E$, with their only poles at zero and expansions
\[
 x=z^{-2}+a_2z^2+a_4z^4+\cdots,
 \qquad y=-2z^{-3}+2a_2z+4a_4z^3+\cdots.
\]
Cancelling the Laurent expansions leaves an elliptic function with no poles and zero constant term, which vanishes by the maximum principle. This gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unibranch-elliptic-cubic}
 y^2=4x^3-g_2x-g_3,
 \qquad g_2=20a_2,\quad g_3=28a_4.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
The argument principle on a translated fundamental parallelogram shows that $x-c$ has two zeros, counted with multiplicity, for every finite $c$. Its fibers are therefore of the form $\{z,-z\}$. If $y(z)=0$, the local multiplicity is at least two. Distinct points $z,-z$ would then give at least four zeros, so $z=-z$ on $E$, the multiplicity is exactly two, and $x''(z)\ne0$. Since $y$ is odd, the map $(x,y)$ is injective and immersive away from zero: use $dx$ where $y\ne0$, and $dy=x''dz$ otherwise.

Define
\[
 \pi(z)=[1:x:y:x^2:x^3],\qquad
 \pi(0)=[0:0:0:0:1].
\]
In the last affine chart its holomorphic coordinates are
\[
 [s^3:s^2:t:s:1],\qquad
 s=1/x=z^2+O(z^6),\qquad t=y/x^3=-2z^3+O(z^7).
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:unibranch-elliptic-cubic} becomes
\[
 t^2=s^3(4-g_2s^2-g_3s^3).
\]
Dividing $t$ by an analytic square root of the nonvanishing factor gives the ordinary cusp $t'^2=s^3$. Choose the local parameter $\zeta$ so that $s=\zeta^2$ and $t'=\zeta^3$. The local ring of the cusp is $\mathbb C\{\zeta^2,\zeta^3\}$, with normalization $\mathbb C\{\zeta\}$. To see this directly, note that division by $t'^2-s^3$ writes every germ as $A(s)+t'B(s)$, and the even and odd parts identify the image ring. Its fraction field contains $\zeta$, which is integral since $\zeta^2=s$, and the larger ring is a finite module generated by $1$ and $\zeta$. An integral meromorphic germ cannot have negative order: in a monic equation, its highest power would have strictly smallest order. Hence $\mathbb C\{\zeta\}$ is the full integral closure.

The image is the reduced projective locus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Z_1^2&=Z_0Z_3,& Z_1Z_3&=Z_0Z_4,\\
 Z_3^2&=Z_1Z_4,&
 Z_2^2&=4Z_0Z_4-g_2Z_0Z_1-g_3Z_0^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
On $Z_0\ne0$ these equations describe the graph of the affine cubic, and since the fibers of $x$ have degree two, both square-root values of $y$ occur. On $Z_0=0$ they force $Z_1=Z_3=Z_2=0$, leaving only $\pi(0)$. In the last chart they give exactly the cusp displayed above. The finite local module at the cusp and the biholomorphic charts elsewhere show that $\pi$ is finite. Hence $\pi$ is the bijective normalization of a connected curve that is smooth away from this one cusp.

For the restricted Fubini--Study form, $\beta=b\beta_0$ is positive away from zero and
\[
 C_1|z|^2\leq b(z)\leq C_2|z|^2
\]
near zero. Indeed, the derivatives of the coordinates in the last chart vanish at zero, and $ds=2z\,dz+O(z^5)dz$ gives the leading term. On the complement of zero, the Fubini--Study potential is
\[
 L=\log(1+|x|^2+|y|^2+|x|^4+|x|^6)
   =-6\log|z|^2+\text{a smooth function near }0.
\]
Globally $\ddc L=\beta-6\delta_0$, so integrating gives $H=6$. Since the normalization has generic degree one, the degree two of $x$ contributes no factor to the measure.

For the pole of weight one, the reference and the continuous correction are
\[
 \Psi=G*((6-b)\beta_0)+G,
 \qquad v=-\frac5{2\pi}\cos(2\pi\operatorname{Re}z).
\]
Direct differentiation gives $\beta+\ddc\Psi=5\beta_0+\delta_0$ and $\ddc v=(5f-5)\beta_0$. Since $F=1$, \cref{thm:unibranch-torus-curve-equation} gives $c=5$, the mass, and uniqueness in the whole type. The residual is the descent of $v-\sup_E(\Psi+v)$. Finally, $f/b$ is comparable to $|z|^{-2}$ near zero, and the integral of its $r$th power against $\beta=b\beta_0$ is comparable to
\[
 \int_0^\epsilon \rho^{3-2r}\,d\rho.
\]
This is finite exactly when $r<2$, with logarithmic divergence at $r=2$.
\end{proof}

The cusp is not normal, so the example is not covered by hypotheses requiring normal log-terminal singularities. Nor is the curve a projective cone: an irreducible reduced projective cone of dimension one has a single point as base and is a projective line, whereas the normalization here has genus one. Without poles, the same data give $c=6$ and $v=-(3/\pi)\cos(2\pi\operatorname{Re}z)$, and the solution is continuous with real values even at the cusp. These are sufficient conditions in dimension one; the general problem for singular spaces in \cref{qu:singular-space-equations-program} remains open.
