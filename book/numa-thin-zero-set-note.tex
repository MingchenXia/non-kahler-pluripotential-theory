\chapter{Smooth solutions across a degenerate hypersurface}

This note collects constructions and proofs from the working manuscript \emph{Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory}. Using three rank-one directions, it gives a smooth nonclosed Hermitian background on $\mathbb P^3$ and a smooth density which vanishes exactly quadratically on two real hypersurfaces, is positive elsewhere, and has uncountably many distinct normalized smooth solutions.

The original construction is reproduced first so that the note can be read without the rest of the book. A first extension retains its background and removes the open zero-density band, with infinite-order vanishing on the remaining hypersurfaces. A second extension changes the background coefficients to achieve quadratic vanishing. Both arguments use a scalar ordinary differential equation, two endpoint barriers and estimates at every derivative order. No numerical approximation is used as a proof step.

We use $\ddc=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial$ and $\dc=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}(\bar\partial-\partial)$. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\omega$, a function is $\omega$-plurisubharmonic when $\omega+\ddc u\geq0$ as a current. For the smooth potentials constructed here, $\mathrm{MA}_\omega(u)=(\omega+\ddc u)^3$ is an ordinary smooth top form. When $\omega>0$, its canonical minimal potential $V_\omega=(\sup\{v\in\PSH(X,\omega):v\leq0\})^*$ equals zero, so every bounded potential has minimal singularities. The bounded-mass observation at the end of the original construction also uses standard bounded Bedford--Taylor products and closed Stokes.

This is a working proof prepared for mathematical review. The accompanying book records its verification history and the limits of the conclusions.

\section{A remaining question}
Finite-order vanishing does not restore uniqueness, as the last construction shows. A different question concerns the topology of the positive-density region. On a connected compact complex manifold, let $\omega$ be Hermitian and $f\geq0$ smooth with $\{f>0\}$ connected and dense. Must two bounded solutions of $(\omega+\ddc u)^n=cf\,\mathrm{d}V$, with the same $c>0$ and supremum zero, coincide? The book proves uniqueness for $C^{1,1}$ potentials under this connectedness hypothesis. The extension to bounded potentials remains a question. The positive-density sets of both hypersurface constructions above have three components.
