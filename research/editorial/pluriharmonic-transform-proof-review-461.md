The decisive space distinction is checked by a complete geometric example. Blow up the disjoint planes $A=\{u=0\}$ and $B=\{v=0\}$ in $\mathbb P^5$. Resolving both projections gives a $\mathbb P^1$-bundle over $\mathbb P^2_u\times\mathbb P^2_v$, the projectivization of $\mathcal O(-1,0)\oplus\mathcal O(0,-1)$. For $h=\Pi^*\sigma_5$, $\theta_u=\rho_u^*\sigma_2$ and $\theta_v=\rho_v^*\sigma_2$, put $Q=h^4$ and $L=\theta_u^2\wedge\theta_v^2$. Fiber integration gives both pushforward trace masses one. On the exceptional section $E_A$, $Q$ integrates to zero and $L$ to one. Thus $Q-L$ is not $dd^c$-exact on $E$, even as a distribution. This counters the exactness claim interpreted on $E$ in source 307/PDF 13–14. The corresponding pushed classes on $\mathbb P^5$ agree; a proof on that space still needs a justified singular pairing, since the cited integration-by-parts lemma tests smooth forms and the ensuing primitive is only $L^1$.

A third complete proof fills the smooth-family mass interface. For a holomorphic map $F:Y\to X$ between compact Kähler manifolds, the closed pushforward $\Gamma=F_*(\Omega^{N-p})$ has a smooth closed representative $\gamma$ and a distributional $dd^c$ primitive. Pairing only with a smooth positive $dd^c$-closed $(p,p)$-form $S$ is legitimate and yields $\int_Y F^*S\wedge\Omega^{N-p}=\int_X\gamma\wedge S\leq C\|S\|$. At $p=n$, $\Gamma$ is smooth locally constant and the primitive is zero. Exterior closedness of $S$ is unnecessary. The classical Kähler $dd^c$ lemma for currents is an explicitly retained input, without a new complete secondary proof-tree claim.
