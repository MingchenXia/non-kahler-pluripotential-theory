# Nef divisors and maximizing Gauduchon currents

22 September2026. Unaudited continuation after the pending semipositive-divisor supplement. This is not production book content and is not covered by either current audit.

There is a short additional consequence worth checking against the existing nef-annihilator theorem. Suppose f>=0 is nonzero in L1, p=f^(1/n) lies in D_omega^0, and a threshold subsolution satisfies omega+dd^c u >= [E]. The separately audited divisorial proposition supplies a maximizing current T with integral beta_E wedge T=0.

If the class of E is nef and has positive fixed-density Gauduchon variational volume, prop:nef-annihilator-determinant in section_smooth_cone_duality.tex gives Gamma_0(T)=0. This would contradict D_p(T)=rho_omega(p)>0. Consequently an effective nef divisor class appearing at the global threshold should have zero Gauduchon variational volume. This would use the existing proposition directly, without solving the conjecture relating positive nef volume to bigness and without assuming BMP.

The argument is complementary to the semipositive representative criterion. For the Hopf hyperplane class alpha, the fixed-density volume is zero, but its smooth semipositive representative is nowhere zero, so the latter criterion still excludes a positive divisorial component. Conversely a nef class with no usable semipositive representative could be covered by the volume criterion if its volume is positive.

Before integration, check the exact definition and homogeneity of lambda_mu for closed nef classes, that its determinant-vanishing conclusion is independent of the positive smooth reference density, and the completed divisorial audit dependency. Neither criterion proves an exact equation for a general L1 density or classifies all possible divisors. General singularity loci, higher-codimensional Lelong numbers and a realization theorem remain separate.

There is also an explanatory link: Lamari's smooth Gauduchon tests detect pseudoeffectivity, whereas all positive dd^c-closed currents test nefness by thm:smooth-cone-current-duality. The exceptional curve example makes their different weak closures explicit. Do not confuse the determinant supremum equality for fixed densities with equality of the two test sets or equality of their pairings with arbitrary smooth backgrounds.
