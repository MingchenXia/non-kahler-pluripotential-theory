## Mathematical reconstruction and second pass

Section 28.9 first proves the metric argument for an arbitrary smooth positive (1,1)-form on the complement of a smooth divisor in a compact complex manifold. It then deduces the exponential-equation criterion for a smooth, possibly nonclosed background. This is an explicitly proved extension of the supplied paper's big nef closed setting, not an attribution of the more general statement to that source. No BMP, PVP, minimality or finite-energy hypothesis enters the length estimate.

1. The defining section's norm gives a global tau. In a finite divisor cover, its differential has size comparable to R inverse; the bounded smooth frame correction cannot cancel the pole sufficiently near the divisor. This also proves that its kernel is a complex hyperplane. The norm convention agrees with the book's ddc normalization and the displayed real metric.
2. The positive tangential block has Schur complement det(T)/det(B). Completing the square controls the component normal to ker partial tau even when mixed coefficients are present. The upper bound for the dual norm of partial tau cancels the R inverse-square factor. The real differential loses only the harmless factor four. The empty tangential determinant in dimension one is explicitly one.
3. The truncated integral of t^(-p/2) is globally Lipschitz for length distance, including crossing the cutoff level, and is proper exactly in the stated sufficient range p<=2. A Cauchy sequence stays in a compact subset of the complement; local smooth positivity gives convergence in distance. This proves metric completeness without assuming radial or monotone escape paths, or a single coordinate chart along a path.
4. The equation converts the density and potential lower bounds into det(T)>=c R^(-2) tau^(-q-a). The tangential determinant upper bound supplies the remaining exponent b. Integrability and the global upper bound of a quasi-psh potential ensure finite right-side measure, but are not needed for the abstract metric proposition. Neither cohomological positivity nor closedness is covertly used.
5. For the two torus profiles, G(L) has mixed derivative G''(L)/r^2. The densities are locally integrable. Both punctured-disc boundary terms, epsilon times |G| and |G'|, vanish, excluding an atom at the divisor. The cutoff's negative Hessian is bounded, so a common sufficiently large C works for the two fixed profiles. This does not claim a uniform C for all beta at once.
6. The one-dimensional positive measure alpha has its exact compact Stokes mass and no atom. Its product with the smooth remaining torus factors gives the full smooth top power off the divisor and has a finite zero extension. The already proved non-pluripolar locality/noncharging statement identifies this with the global extended measure. This is a shorter internal proof than repeating the source's truncation/mollification passage. It relies on Theorem 6.4.1 and Proposition 6.3.1 as inspected in the source, not on a new external BEGZ import.
7. The complete model has density L^(lambda A)+A/(C r^2 L^(2-lambda A)), integrable when 0<lambda A<1. Its exponents add to two. The bounded model has density asymptotic to a positive multiple of r^(-2)L^(-2-beta). The explicit normal path has finite length, with integral L0^(-beta/2)/beta. Its smallest allowable sum is 2+beta, since q>=2+beta, a>=0 and the constant tangential determinant forces b>=0. This verifies sharpness under the given inequalities.
8. The continuous bounded model has minimal ordinary singularities; the complete model cannot dominate the smooth potential zero. Both have full mass. Their coefficients F=R^2 f are positive and smooth only off the divisor and tend to zero at it. They do not supply a counterexample for a prescribed pole whose coefficient extends smoothly and positively across the divisor, or for the DNGL geometric-flow compatibility conditions.
