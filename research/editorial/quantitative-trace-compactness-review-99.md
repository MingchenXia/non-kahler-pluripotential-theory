## Proof and exposition

Proposition 5.2.2 quantifies the finite trace homeomorphism on the entire mean-normalized theta-psh space: the bounded-Lipschitz distance of Gauduchon traces is bounded below by a constant times the L1 distance and above by a constant times its square root. Constants depend only on the fixed geometry, background, probability volume and distance. The zero-trace-mass singleton is handled separately. Potentials need not be bounded, and no Monge–Ampère mass bound is used.

The reverse estimate solves the correctly normalized formal-adjoint Poisson equation. The Gauduchon identity, index zero and the maximum principle identify both kernels. Classical global W2p estimates with p>2n give a uniform Lipschitz test. The forward estimate smooths bounded-Lipschitz tests at scale epsilon, using one weak derivative of the test and one derivative of the mollifier to obtain an epsilon^-1 bound. The fixed finite linear mass bounds the total-variation error. Optimization gives exponent 1/2.

Example 5.2.1 checks the constants on the flat unit elliptic curve in the book's dd^c convention. The potential cos(2 pi kx)/(2 pi k^2) has L1 norm 1/(pi^2 k^2), positive background at least eta/2, and trace distance at least 1/(8 pi k). No forward exponent above 1/2 is possible even on this fixed smooth family. Scaling the first potential shows that the inverse modulus cannot be superlinear. These are elementary deductions, not novelty claims.
