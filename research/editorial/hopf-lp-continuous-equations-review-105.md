## Hopf result and proof boundary

Fix the Chapter 8 Hopf threefold, its displayed form omega and probability nu=omega^3/6. For nonnegative nonzero f in Lp(X,nu), p>1, let c_f be the unique continuous-solution normalization scalar and I_f=int f dnu. Proposition 10.10.1 proves

    c_f I_f >= 1,
    ||u-Au||_L2(nu)^2 <= 4(c_f I_f-1).

Equality holds exactly when f is almost everywhere a pullback from the projective base. In that case every continuous solution is a continuous base pullback. The hypothesis allows vanishing, discontinuous and unbounded Lp densities. It does not concern unbounded solution potentials.

The direct proof integrates the equation and applies the already proved bounded mass-minimizer characterization. Equality first gives invariance of each solution; naturality then gives invariance of the finite measure f nu for each fixed fiber translation. Haar averaging is performed at the measure level, tested against continuous functions, before concluding f=Af almost everywhere. Fubini and Jensen give a well-defined Lp function on the base. No common pointwise representative under an uncountable family of translations is assumed.

For the converse, continuous existence on the Kahler base and bounded Stokes give the scalar I_f^-1. Smooth strictly positive decreasing approximations on the base yield the cube identity on X. In a local trivialization, wedging with the base top-degree form removes all horizontal components of beta, leaving beta restricted to the fiber equal to 2 times probability Haar area. The coefficient is therefore 3 times 2=6. Product-measure convergence in local trivializations and bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity on both manifolds pass the identity to the continuous pullback. Scalar uniqueness finishes the proof.
