## Fixed-space inverse

Fix a closed reproducing subspace H of an L2 space with base inner product linear in its first variable. Bounded real weight increments f preserve the underlying subspace, closedness and point evaluation, since the two norms are equivalent. Let P0 be the base orthogonal projection and T(f)=P0 M_exp(-f)|H. It represents the changed inner product, is positive selfadjoint for real f, and satisfies exp(-||f||) I <= T(f) <= exp(||f||) I. In particular, it is invertible. For complex bounded f, the same formula is an operator-valued entire map and ||T(f)-I|| <= exp(||f||)-1. If ||f|| <= log(3/2), the Neumann inverse exists and has norm at most 2. This complexification is a bounded operator construction, not a positive complex-weight inner product.

For an evaluation functional ell_x and its base Riesz vector k0,x, real increments give k_f,x=T(f)^(-1) k0,x: selfadjointness verifies the Riesz identity. Consequently the diagonal kernel has holomorphic continuation ell_x(T(f)^(-1) k0,x). Evaluation is linear, so complex conjugation of the moving parameter is not introduced. Changing a unit evaluation covector by a phase changes the Riesz vector by the opposite phase; this expression is invariant. Uniform evaluation bounds give local uniform kernel analyticity. On the compact book manifold and fixed finite selected space they hold over all X, including base points. The weighted diagonal adds exp(-f(x)).

For the book norm, f=m(v-v0), with m fixed. Thus v in C(X,R) maps real analytically to B_m,v in C(X), and then to its measure in total variation. The zero-dimensional space gives the identically zero map. The common zero locus of the selected space is independent of v. A common zero gives a zero Riesz vector, so no division by a zero kernel is used. No assertion is made about varying multiplier ideals, a common radius as m tends to infinity, or differentiation of a limiting envelope.

## All derivatives

Let v_t be C-infinity with values in C(X,R), keeping m, h, nu and the selecting potential fixed. At each real base t use its own inner product and projection P_t. Let Bell_j be the complete exponential Bell polynomial, characterized by d^j exp(g)/dt^j=exp(g) Bell_j(g',...,g^(j)), and put A_t,j=-P_t M_Bell_j(-m v_t)|V_m. The local Gram operator H(s), with reference inner product at t, has H(t)=I and H^(j)(t)=-A_t,j. The Riesz vector is H(s)^(-1) k_t,x. Its kth inverse jet follows by truncating the Neumann inverse after k terms: H(s)-I=O(|s-t|) makes the remaining tail O(|s-t|^(k+1)). No convergence of the smooth weight curve's Taylor series is assumed. The resulting identity is

    d^k k_t,x/dt^k = sum_{alpha composition of k} (k!/alpha!) A_t,alpha_r ... A_t,alpha_1 k_t,x.

The order is retained; the operators are not presumed to commute. Smooth parameter dependence in a fixed norm follows first from the smooth Gram entries and inverse, so coefficient differentiation does not assume the conclusion. Applying evaluation gives the source formula in the fixed compact selected-space setting. At orders one and two this reads k'=m P_t(v'_t k) and k''=2m^2 P_t(v'_t P_t(v'_t k))+P_t((m v''_t-m^2(v'_t)^2)k), matching the signs in pp.12–14.

If ||v_t^(j)||_infinity <= A R^j(j!)^s on a compact parameter interval, s>=1, then each Bell term is bounded by a constant to power j times (j!)^s. Indeed, a partition with sum l n_l=j has product (l!)^((s-1)n_l) <= (j!)^(s-1). The remaining coefficient sum is the jth coefficient of exp(u/(1-u)); at u=1/2 it is at most e 2^j. Thus ||Bell_j(-m v_t)||_infinity <= L_m,J^j(j!)^s for one finite L_m,J. Orthogonal-projection contractivity bounds each ordered product by L_m,J^k (alpha!)^s. Since alpha!<=k! and there are 2^(k-1) ordered compositions, the full vector derivative is bounded by C_m,J (2L_m,J)^k(k!)^s. Uniform evaluation and bounded weight factors give the same factorial order for the weighted diagonal in C(X), by the ordinary Leibniz formula. The beta measure has the same order in total variation. Constants depend on the fixed degree and parameter interval.

## Boundary-control example

On the unit disk, rho_t(z)=1+t^2/((1-|z|^2)^2+t^2) lies in [1,2]. The density is radial, so constants are orthogonal to all nonconstant holomorphic monomials and its center reproducing kernel is 1/integral rho_t dA. Polar coordinates and s=1-r^2 give integral rho_t dA=pi(1+|t| arctan(1/|t|)) for t!=0, and pi at zero. Therefore K_t(0,0)=pi^(-1)-|t|/2+O(t^2), which is not differentiable at zero. Each interior spatial point has analytic parameter dependence of its weight, but ||-log rho_t||_infinity=log 2 for every nonzero t. The weight curve is not even continuous in the uniform norm at zero. This is the explicit p.3 example and does not contradict the bounded-norm result. No boundary-distance estimate is needed for this calculation.
