# Real parameters and rational analytic types

Focused editorial and proof review for checkpoint71, based on checkpoint70 commit 61c317edbc066942ca25ce958c1418be48531fa6. The scope is Chapters16 and19 and their use of the source book's rational-coefficient convention.

The local Bergman construction, multiplier ideals and generic divisorial estimate permit every real m>0. An analytic type in the convention stated in Chapter19 instead uses a positive rational coefficient. The old consequence of the divisorial lemma called the coefficient-1/m presentation analytic while m was allowed to be real. The revision writes the presentation explicitly and states its Lelong-number convergence without that terminology. It then says when the presentation has a rational coefficient. Chapter19 makes the corresponding distinction when extending its local construction to real parameters. The integer global regularization and the rationally rescaled Hermitian approximants are unchanged.

The proof was reread directly. At a generic point of a fixed divisor E, factor the common power z_1^k from local ideal generators, where k is their minimum vanishing order. At least one residual generator is a unit there. The logarithm of their squared norms is therefore k log|z_1|^2 plus a bounded term, so the coefficient-1/m presentation has Lelong number k/m. This is the identity now stated at the end of the proof. Applying the unchanged two-sided divisorial estimate proves convergence for real m tending to infinity. No arithmetic restriction is used in either inequality.

The distinction is substantive terminology. On a disk, u=log|z|^2 and m=sqrt(2) give I(mu)=(z) in the book's normalization. The coefficient-1/m presentation has irrational Lelong number 1/sqrt(2), whereas a rational-coefficient analytic presentation has rational Lelong number at a point. One must therefore neither call every real-parameter presentation analytic nor restrict the useful real-parameter estimate merely to preserve that word. This local calculation is review evidence, not a new numbered result.
