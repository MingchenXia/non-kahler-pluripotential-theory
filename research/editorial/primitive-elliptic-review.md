## Mathematical content

The construction starts with a connected compact Kähler surface (B,eta) and a holomorphic line bundle of eta-degree zero. A scalar Poisson equation makes its curvature primitive. In the book's normalization, the quotient of the punctured bundle by multiplication by q>1 has fiber period T=2 log q. The metric omega=pi*eta+dt wedge d^c t is balanced, has volume 3TA, and has dd^c omega=-pi*(c^2)>=0. Expansion and two integrations by parts prove its exact smooth mass formula. The previously proved equality of smooth and bounded mass extrema gives v_+=3TA. Nonzero real Chern class obstructs both pluriclosed metrics and closed Kähler currents by pairing with a smooth positive exact (2,2)-form. The canonical bundle is the pullback of the base canonical bundle. These claims do not require a lower mass hypothesis.

The sharp periodic variance lemma treats both T<=2M and T>=2M. Its endpoint calculation does not assume monotonicity. The extremizing piecewise profiles include a positive jump at the period join; periodic convolution and a strict scaling preserve the derivative bound and mean zero. At T=2M the two constructions agree. The separated class u=f(t)+pi*v has a positive mass bound using its necessary cohomological amplitude bound. Sharpness is asserted only for the radial subclass v=0, where the pointwise curvature eigenvalue gives the exact admissible amplitude. No claim that one common base potential realizes all positive cohomology classes is made. The earlier P^1 x P^1 example is recovered with restricted infimum 8.

The K3 example uses standard period, lattice and Kähler-cone inputs. A primitive square-minus-four class and a generic period perpendicular to it give Pic(B)=Z[L]. Adjunction excludes every irreducible curve, including singular curves. The orthogonal positive-square class is Kähler because there are no rational-curve walls. The total space has trivial canonical bundle and no divisors; projection and Stokes exclude a divisor dominating B, while the base and fiber dimensions exclude other images. Every compact irreducible curve is an elliptic fiber. This is a geometric test of the lower-mass problem, not a value for its unrestricted lower mass. No novelty or globally open status is claimed for that problem.
