Several intermediate arguments are directly reconstructed. For a symmetric real matrix with nonnegative off-diagonal entries and connected positive-entry graph, a maximal Rayleigh eigenvector can be replaced by its absolute value. Its nonnegative coordinates are all positive: a zero coordinate propagates to all adjacent coordinates in the eigenvalue equation. Opposite signs across a positive edge would strictly increase the Rayleigh quotient. The maximal eigenspace is simple, since two independent positive eigenvectors admit a linear combination with a zero coordinate. Pairing the positive eigenvector with Am determines the sign of the maximal eigenvalue for a positive vector m satisfying a strict sign condition. For integer entries, rational approximation preserves strict inequalities, and a positive rational kernel vector exists in a rational zero-eigenspace. This supplies Proposition2.5 and Lemma2.6 at their exact interface without newly relying on their external proof. Applying it to the intersection matrix gives the proportionality in Lemma3.1: positive effective divisors on the same connected support, numerically trivial there, have proportional multiplicity vectors.

The cutoff in Lemma2.8 is reconstructed with a threshold strictly above the boundary maximum. The canonical logarithmic pole cancels in the frame metric; convexity of the increasing cutoff gives semipositive curvature. In the locally flat case this agrees with the concave logarithmic cutoff already proved in402. Lemma2.7(i) is read as classification of line bundles with specified flat structures. On a noncompact space this is not classification of their underlying holomorphic line bundles: C-star has many unitary characters and trivial holomorphic Picard group. For a compact connected smooth base, two flat metrics on a holomorphically trivial line differ by a globally pluriharmonic logarithm, hence by a constant. A nonzero trivializing section of a unitary-flat power is parallel. Thus finite holonomy is equivalent to holomorphic torsion in the smooth compact setting needed next.

The regularity and flux mechanisms are also reconstructed at stated interfaces. On a log resolution, the logarithm is minus a positive integral linear combination of log|w_j|^2 plus a smooth function. Near each crossing, at least one derivative is -m_j/w_j plus a bounded term; compactness gives regular sufficiently small levels. Resolution is an isomorphism off the support, so this proves the original regular-level assertion. For a smooth closed (n-1,n-1)-form alpha, distributional Stokes on the tube W_s gives

    integral_{psi=s} dc psi wedge alpha = integral_D alpha - integral_{W_s} B wedge alpha.

The orientation is that of the boundary of {psi<s}. One can justify this identity by smoothing the logarithmic pole and applying a smooth boundary cutoff before passing to the limit; the divisor term must be retained. Smoothness of B and shrinking tube volume give the limit. With alpha=B^k wedge kappa^(n-k-1), semipositivity gives nonnegative flux, and ordinary Stokes on slabs gives monotonicity. Its limit is the divisor intersection. If the latter vanishes, the nonnegative flux vanishes identically on late levels. Diagonalizing the tangential Levi form gives a positive factor times its kth elementary symmetric polynomial; consequently its rank is at most k-1. If the limit is positive, some point on every sufficiently late level has rank at least k. These are local conclusions, distinct from the unreviewed global Hartogs implication.

The local core of Theorem4.1 is reconstructed. Vanishing square intersection makes the connected late levels Levi flat. A positive semidefinite Hermitian form whose tangential block vanishes has vanishing cross terms, so dd^c psi=f dpsi wedge dc psi for a smooth nonnegative f. Taking d of this identity shows that f is leafwise constant. If there is no nonconstant smooth leafwise-constant function on any sufficiently late level, f=G(psi). At one regular point of support multiplicity m, psi=-m log|w|^2+phi and the normal-normal coefficient gives f=|w|^2 times a smooth function. Evaluating on a positive real transverse ray and inverting eta=exp(-psi) gives G(s)=O(exp(-s/m)). The estimate at this one point is enough because G is already a function of the level.

Writing t=eta and g(t)=(1-G(-log t))/t, choose

    rho(t)=t^-1 exp(integral_0^t tau^(-1+1/m) Bhat(tau^(1/(2m))) d tau),
    chihat(t)=log t + integral_0^t (rho(tau)-1/tau) d tau.

Here Bhat is bounded and continuous at zero. Both integrals converge, rho'=-g rho, and psi_hat=-chihat(eta) is pluriharmonic off the support with bounded difference from psi. Adding the local defining logarithm gives a bounded pluriharmonic function, which extends across the divisor by the removable-singularity theorem applied to both signs. It defines a smooth flat local divisor metric. If a nonconstant smooth leafwise-constant level function instead exists, a nonempty interval of regular values is supplied by Sard's theorem; proper submersion over that interval gives a smooth family of disjoint compact complex hypersurfaces. This reconstructs the local alternatives. It does not manufacture the later global fibration.
