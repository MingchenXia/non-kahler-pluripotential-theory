The general proposition does not require analytic approximants or their L1 convergence. The fixed target ideal envelope p is below every varying-background envelope q_j. Since all q_j have supremum zero and a common smooth curvature bound, normalized compactness gives cluster limits q. The vanishing background error makes q theta-psh. Pullback L1 convergence and pointwise Lelong semicontinuity imply nu_E(q) >= limsup nu_E(q_j); every point on E has at least its generic Lelong number. The inequality p <= q supplies the opposite bound. Blowups of points on local modifications recover the pointwise valuative data, hence the ideal class. Then q is a competitor for p, forcing equality. Analyticity is used only to identify ordinary and ideal envelopes.

In the fixed-weight corollary, ideal inclusion reverses orders of vanishing. The Bergman inequalities give (1+delta)m nu_E(a_j) >= m nu_E(u)-A_U(E). First take the lower limit in j, then m to infinity and delta to zero. The ideal order supplies nu_E(a_j) <= nu_E(u). No uniform estimate over all divisors or uniform choice of j over all weights is claimed. The stated sheaf inclusion is a sufficient condition, not asserted equivalent to divisorial convergence.

For the counterexample, choose a nonconstant smooth normalized potential u with positive curvature on a compact curve. Atomic rational approximations to this smooth probability have largest atom tending to zero: the largest cell mass in a partition of vanishing diameter tends to zero, and the total rational-rounding error does too. Their Green potentials are analytic ideal envelopes and converge to u in L1. Every pointwise Lelong number tends to zero; local modifications of a smooth curve are isomorphisms. Both envelopes of u are zero, whereas the envelopes of the approximants are the approximants themselves. The one-sided ideal order fails at their poles. This is a refinement of the existing curve construction, not a new source dependency.
