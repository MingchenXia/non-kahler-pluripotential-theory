## Mathematical scope

The new section proves ordinary/ideal envelope equality for analytic singularities in any smooth real background with nonempty potential space. The essential local comparison is that ideal domination by an analytic singularity implies ordinary domination. Principalization resolves the analytic ideal. The valuative ideal criterion bounds the generic divisorial coefficients, and Siu removal is applied to a locally closed positive complex Hessian after smooth correction. Properness and a finite cover give the required global additive constant. No pullback commutation for envelopes is assumed, and the analytic remainder need only be bounded.

The local Bergman estimate is strengthened to every fixed local prime divisor E, with discrepancy error A(E)/m. In the book's normalization the integrability weight is exp(-m u), the logarithmic pole is log|z|^2, and A(E)=1+ord_E Jac(pi). Pullback of integrability forces k+A(E)>m nu_E(u). This is a necessary condition, not a sufficient integrability criterion. The other inequality comes from the proved local Bergman lower bound. The estimate holds at real positive parameters locally; the global approximation uses integer levels. No error uniform over all divisors is asserted.

For dyadic Demailly approximants a_j of exact ideal type (1/2^j,I(2^j u)), subadditivity gives decreasing ordinary types. Their values are not asserted to decrease. Choosing decreasing curvature errors makes their ordinary envelopes actual competitors for the preceding envelope, hence decreasing. The ideal envelope Q_theta[u] is a common lower barrier. Squeezing each fixed divisor's Lelong number identifies the limit with Q_theta[u]. Point blowups recover the exact local-modification pointwise data required by the ideal criterion. The argument never invokes continuity of Q on arbitrary L1 sequences.

The equality P_theta[u]=Q_theta[u] is therefore equivalent to convergence of these particular ordinary envelopes to P_theta[u], both pointwise and in L1. The nonnegative L1 gap has an explicit limiting integral. This is a criterion for a specified approximation, not a topology on arbitrary ordinary singularity types, a mass identity, or the whole SGPT characterization.

For a Hermitian background, positive rational decreasing curvature errors allow rational scaling back into the same background. The rational analytic-singularity convention is preserved. After normalizing u<=0, the scaled approximants still dominate u. The same divisorial argument proves that their envelopes decrease to Q_omega[u]. Thus analytic classes are dense in the entire elementary ideal-type metric space already constructed in Section16.5. Density in this metric imposes no restriction on the ideal class and by itself cannot reproduce the source's ordinary-singularity d_S characterization. No background-invariance or unrestricted mass convergence is deduced.

None of these conclusions uses BMP, bigness, closedness of the background or a positive-volume condition. Compactness is used explicitly for finite covers, upper bounds, canonical integrability and nested maximum sets. The fixed-background scaling corollary uses positivity of the Hermitian background.
