## Mathematical checks

- Half-line duality: a closed decreasing concave function bounded above by zero is finite arbitrarily far to the left once it is finite somewhere. Its transform is infinite for negative time, and its value at zero is the limit from positive time. Biconjugacy therefore uses positive times alone. For the reverse direction, supporting slopes are at most the linear bound c; a slope equal to c is recovered by slopes increasing strictly to c. The endpoint identity follows from the same duality. Identically minus-infinity vertical functions are covered separately.
- Parameter closure: a decreasing left limit dominates an interior potential. Outside the pole set of a later interior member, finite concavity supplies continuity; almost-everywhere equality then gives equality everywhere. This proves closure of every parameter function and justifies restriction to rational parameters in a supremum.
- Generality of the moved statements: the closure and upper-semicontinuity arguments require only a pointwise concave decreasing family of theta-psh functions bounded above by zero, not envelope fixed points or a prescribed initial potential. In this generality the member at c may be identically minus infinity. The Fubini argument consequently uses the pole set of a fixed interior member, not the cutoff member. The constant family Gamma_tau = -1/(c-tau) provides a check of this distinction: its cutoff member collapses, while its transform is ct-2 sqrt(t).
- Pointwise upper semicontinuity: regularize on the product, use the interior member as a genuine lower bound, and obtain equality at all positive times outside one null set by Fubini and finite convexity. Kiselman's principle and almost-everywhere order identify each inverse potential everywhere. The half-line duality then identifies the original supremum everywhere, including the originally exceptional set. Every slice remains genuine by the same fixed lower potential.
- Test curve to ray: supremum normalization gives exactly ct and the decreasing family U_t-ct. Envelope order and the regularized parameter supremum give P[U_t]=phi. Compactness and pointwise order identify every initial L1 cluster point with phi. A Perron replacement on [a,b] exists with the specified endpoints and glues by the joint boundary inequalities. Duality recovers the original ray.
