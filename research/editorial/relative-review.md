## Chapter15 checks

Concavity follows from the local mixed inequality on finite levels, not from a global mass identity. Bounded-density continuity uses the relative bound and one common barrier. The supporting-probability construction includes the Hahn–Banach and Riesz arguments. Right derivatives are the negative maximum and left derivatives the negative minimum of directional integrals. Uniqueness gives Gateaux differentiability, not Frechet differentiability or an already-defined weak linearized operator. In the smooth regime the supporting probability agrees with the Gauduchon adjoint probability. The later quantitative zero-reference result is cross-referenced without extending it to arbitrary singular references.

The convex dual is lower semicontinuous; bounded sublevels are uniformly absolutely continuous. The proof chooses the amplitude of a continuous cutoff before choosing the small set. This justifies extension to bounded measurable densities and weak L1 stability of minimizing probabilities. No strong L1 convergence is claimed. The entropy computation retains the closed-background mass identity as an explicit hypothesis and handles infinite entropy and non-unit reference volume. Generic uniqueness follows from countably many directional widths and Baire's theorem; it does not prove uniqueness at every density.
