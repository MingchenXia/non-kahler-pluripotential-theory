The disc example on the primary's page 3 is reconstructed directly. For `s=1-|z|^2`, let the density be `1+t^2/(s^2+t^2)`, with value 1 at t=0. It stays between 1 and 2. Its negative logarithm is jointly smooth and locally real analytic in the parameter at interior points, and each fixed-parameter weight is smooth up to the boundary. Radial symmetry nevertheless gives

`K_t(0,0) = 1 / [pi * (1+|t| arctan(1/|t|))]`

for nonzero t, with `K_0(0,0)=1/pi`. The radial substitution has `dA=pi ds`; the primitive `s+t arctan(s/t)` for positive t verifies the norm. Thus `K_t(0,0)=1/pi−|t|/2+O(t^2)`. The kernel is not differentiable at zero. The missing hypothesis is uniform parameter control up to the boundary: the weights are not even continuous at zero in the L-infinity norm. Uniform equivalence of norms and pointwise analytic dependence alone do not repair that failure.
