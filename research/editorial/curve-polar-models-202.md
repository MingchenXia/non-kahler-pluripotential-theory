The local inverse maximum proof starts from a compact polar set K. Harmonic functions equal to one on nested smooth neighborhoods and zero on the outer disc are continuous, bounded and superharmonic. Their bounded distributional limit has Laplacian supported on K, hence zero by the bounded subharmonic nonpolar property, and vanishes by its boundary values. The functions converge smoothly off K. Testing a nonpositive L1 function against a compactly supported cutoff times their mollifications gives nonnegative mass on K. The limits are taken first in the smoothing parameter and then in the neighborhood index; derivatives of the cutoff stay away from K. Signed-measure inner regularity gives positivity of the whole polar part. Applying this to differences gives order. Subtracting the Green potential of the common minimum polar measure gives the reverse inequality for the maximum formula.

For a polar measure mu of mass at most A, the Green potential w has curvature mu+(A-mu(Y))eta. A potential v is more singular than w exactly when its current dominates mu: polar order gives necessity, and the distribution v-w is globally quasi-subharmonic for sufficiency. This current inequality survives maxima and increasing envelope limits. The model p=P[w] differs boundedly from w, so its residual curvature charges no polar set and its polar part is exactly mu. Ordinary equivalence proves fixedness before it is used. For arbitrary u, the same domination puts u below the model for its polar part. Equality of positive masses invokes the existing full-mass characterization; at zero mass the polar measure exhausts the full current, and harmonic uniqueness gives the envelope directly. Thus zero-mass idempotence is not assumed in the proof.

The polar maximum identity gives d(u,v)=norm(mu_u-mu_v)_TV. Positive polar measures of mass at most A form a closed subset of finite signed measures: a countable union of polar carriers carries every sequential total-variation limit. Linear interpolation gives constant-speed geodesics. Atomic measures form a closed convex subspace; finite rational truncations of mass below A give analytic density there. Atomic extraction is contractive. Its complement is mutually singular to every atomic signed measure, proving both unique nearest projection and the exact analytic defect.
