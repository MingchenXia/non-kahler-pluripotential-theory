## Mathematical checks and correction

The old sentence saying that a Gauduchon background need not remain Gauduchon after changing its potential omitted the complex-surface exception. Since dd^c(omega+dd^c u)=dd^c omega, all positive perturbations of a Gauduchon surface retain that condition. A normalized Gauduchon multiplier is constant exactly when the solution metric is Gauduchon; the former assertion that it is nonconstant for a general solution metric is replaced by this precise criterion.

Proposition 4.5.2 identifies persistence for every smooth positive potential perturbation with invariant smooth Monge–Ampère mass. Its proof uses the first variation in every smooth direction and the convex segment joining zero to a potential. The differential characterization in Theorem 26.15.1 remains a forward reference to the book's existing Chiose polarization proof.

Example 4.5.1 works on the three-dimensional complex torus with omega=eta_1+b eta_2+b^(-1) eta_3 and b=exp(cos(2 pi x_1)). The base is Gauduchon, but arbitrarily small cosine perturbations in x_2 are not. Positivity, all factors of pi in the book's dd^c convention, and the nonzero second derivative are checked explicitly. The example does not assert that mass changes along that particular one-parameter line: its determinant there is affine with constant integral. It shows failure of the Gauduchon condition at the perturbed metric, and hence failure of mass invariance over the entire potential space.

Proposition 4.6.1 extends the adjoint probabilities in the persistence regime to bounded omega-psh potentials by MA_omega(u)/integral omega^n. Exact decreasing Hermitian regularization passes the smooth total mass to the bounded limit. Local Bedford–Taylor capacity continuity for uniformly bounded corrected psh functions and the finite nonclosed-background expansion give weak continuity. Uniform boundedness and capacity convergence are explicit. This applies to Gauduchon surfaces. It does not define a weak linearized operator or assert independence for unbounded approximations in an arbitrary background.

On a non-Kahler Gauduchon surface, the normalized raw Monge–Ampère one-form still has no primitive, by the book's exact primitive criterion. Thus the finite adjoint probability and smooth mass invariance do not remove the first-order torsion obstruction.
