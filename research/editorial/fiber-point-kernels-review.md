## Proof scope

The starting point is the original SGPT convention: a singular weight selects sections through I(m phi), while a separate continuous weight determines their Hilbert norm. The latter is the same for the two nested subspaces being compared. Extending an orthonormal basis makes the kernel difference positive and gives its exact integral, the codimension. Integrating the finite determinant first variation gives the corresponding functional comparison. These statements include empty subspaces and require no asymptotic section-generation theorem.

For a compact connected-fiber holomorphic submersion X^n -> B^q and L=pi*A, every holomorphic section descends. An isolated pole a log|x|^2 in dimension n imposes order max(floor(ma)-n+1,0): the radial integrability condition is STRICTLY k+n>ma, including integral values of ma. In product coordinates a descended coefficient has the same order at the upstairs point and its base image. Several poles in one fiber impose the maximum of these orders, not their sum. The selected space therefore is exactly a base fat-point space.

The corresponding radial pole on B has multiplier-ideal exponent max(floor(ma)-q+1,0). The difference is at most n-q jet orders. Injection of the quotient of section spaces into the finite jet quotient proves codimension O(m^(q-1)); no jet surjectivity is asserted. This includes q=1 and the zero fiber-dimension case.

The general convergence theorem RETAINS a Kähler base and a theta-psh base reference with positive non-pluripolar mass and the specified projected poles. The already proved SGPT transfer theorem applies to the BASE with its identity modification. It is never applied to the non-descended Hermitian-positive weight upstairs. Kernel comparison gives total-variation distance O(1/m) from the lifted base partial kernels; the limiting equilibrium convergence itself is weak. The determinant functionals converge by the same codimension estimate. Positive growth has degree q; ambient n-degree normalization gives zero if n>q. Small-coefficient cutoff constructions give genuine Hermitian-positive selecting weights without BMP, and positive base references for a positive line-bundle curvature when the coefficients are sufficiently small.

Moving isolated poles inside a fiber, or adding poles without increasing that fiber's largest coefficient, preserves EVERY selected space. With fixed norms all kernels and determinants are exactly identical in every degree. The ideal types upstairs differ at large weights. Under the base hypotheses, this loss of information coexists with positive q-dimensional section growth.

## The explicit Hopf calculation

For X_n -> P^q, q=n-1, one pole over [1:0:...:0], and zero continuous weight, the selected sections are projective monomials of affine degree at least r_m=max(floor(ma)-q,0). Direct Gaussian integration proves their Fubini–Study norms. The kernel is D_m times a binomial tail, D_m=binomial(m+q,q); the dimension is D_m-binomial(r_m+q-1,q). Its q-normalized limit is 1-a^q.

Chebyshev estimates and the probability density q t^(q-1) dt prove total-variation convergence of the normalized kernel measures to 1_{t>a} nu_X. An independent entropy estimate proves convergence of m^(-1) log B_m to e_a(t): it is zero for t>=a and a log(t/a)+(1-a)log((1-t)/(1-a)) below a. The proof includes the interface, uniformity near t=1, and eventual minus-infinity values at t=0. Fixed-curvature compactness then proves L1 convergence. Measure convergence is not used as a substitute for a potential lower estimate.

The local potential inside the contact sphere is a log|z|^2 plus a constant; outside it is log(1+|z|^2). Matching values and first derivatives gives C1,1 regularity near the sphere, positivity and absence of a hypersurface mass. Convolution and Bedford–Taylor continuity identify the top measure, including q=1. A complex-line maximum principle proves envelope maximality. The analytic ideal-domination lemma identifies the complete competitor families upstairs and downstairs. The selecting weight phi need not be alpha-psh; no envelope theorem requiring that hypothesis is applied to it. The Hopf identity gives the exact factor 1/(2n) in the Hermitian measure of the equilibrium potential.

For sufficiently small a the selecting weight is omega-psh on the Hopf threefold failing BMP. Its only pole is a point, but the limit has a pole on the whole elliptic fiber. A small cutoff gives an open set on which the raw selecting-weight Monge–Ampère measure is omega^n and the kernel limit vanishes. Thus the limiting equilibrium measure is explicitly distinguished from the raw measure of phi.
