# The strongly Gauduchon existence criterion

A connected compact complex manifold of dimension at least two admits a strongly Gauduchon metric exactly when it has no nonzero positive de Rham-exact (1,1)-current. ## Reconstructed proof

Only the three possible components of a real degree-(2n-2) form occur. Closedness is consequently equivalent to the bar-partial-exact condition on the partial derivative of the positive pure-type component. The existing explicit d^c primitive has the stated sign and normalization. Distributional Stokes proves the forward obstruction, including the balanced case.

For the converse, separate the closed linear space of closed complementary smooth real forms from the open convex cone of forms with strictly positive (n-1,n-1)-part. A point in their intersection would give a strongly Gauduchon metric by the positive-root construction. The separator is therefore a nonzero real degree-two current annihilating every closed complementary form and nonnegative on the open cone. Free variation of every other type component forces type (1,1). Epsilon addition proves positivity on all semipositive tests. Vanishing on exact tests makes the current closed; vanishing on all closed tests and current de Rham duality make it exact. The latter step is essential and is written explicitly.

A positive current in the zero Bott--Chern class vanishes, because its Gauduchon trace is zero. Thus the criterion is equivalent to the assertion that every nonzero pseudoeffective Bott--Chern class has nonzero de Rham image. This concerns the set of represented classes; it does not assert a one-to-one correspondence between currents and classes.
