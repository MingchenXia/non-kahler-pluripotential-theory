The induction in the proof of Theorem 1.2, pp.11--12, contains an omitted whole-fiber argument. For a projective bundle pi:D->A with irreducible fibres P^s and a proper analytic subset B of D, let F={x in A: pi^-1(x) is contained in B}. Because pi restricted to B is proper, the proper fiber-dimension theorem makes {x:dim(B intersect pi^-1(x))>=s} analytic. This equals F: an analytic subset of the irreducible s-dimensional fibre has dimension s precisely when it is the whole fibre. If F were all of A, B would equal D. Thus F is proper; on a connected manifold A it has strictly smaller dimension. For s=0 this is just the bundle isomorphism. This uses the stated classical fiber-dimension theorem, not a new reconstruction of that theorem.

Apply this to B=E_(c-a)(R) on the exceptional projective bundle, or to the identity bundle in the divisor case. The source inequalities give a<c and put every fibre over each high-Lelong base point inside B. Those points therefore lie in F. Proper image under the embedded resolution, together with the original singular locus, supplies a proper analytic subset of the original irreducible component, contradicting minimality of its analytic hull. The image of B alone is insufficient: a section of A times P^1 is proper yet projects onto every point of A. Compact curves give the induction's atomic base case. Bidimensions zero and n require their elementary atomic-measure and constant-pluriharmonic-function cases before using the Lelong--Skoda reduction.

The finite-Hausdorff-measure step in Corollary 1.4 can be made explicit. If a finite measure mu has zero upper d-density on a set Z inside A with finite H^d(A), fix epsilon>0 and let Z_m consist of points of Z satisfying mu(B(x,r))<=epsilon r^d for every r<1/m. Cover Z_m by small balls centered on Z_m, with the sum of diameter powers arbitrarily close to a fixed dimensional multiple of H^d(A). Countable subadditivity gives mu(Z_m)<=C epsilon H^d(A); taking the increasing union and then epsilon->0 gives mu(Z)=0. Local smooth trace comparisons convert zero Lelong number into zero upper 2q-density. With the analytic residual superlevels and the support theorem, this gives the corollary. These are trace-measure concentration statements, not assertions about topological support or algebraic embeddings in an arbitrary Kähler manifold.
