# Checks before using results of Popovici

The user's standing rule is recorded in PLAN.md: consult the supplied private working reference first, then independently check the complete proof and its dependencies before relying on a result of Popovici. That document is not incorporated into the book or source package. This record describes our own checks of the primary mathematical sources; it does not adopt another document's assessments as premises.

## Existing use: nef-difference bigness

On 22 September 2026 the complete seven-page primary version of Dan Popovici, *Sufficient Bigness Criterion for Differences of Two Nef Classes*, [arXiv:1405.2518v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2518v2), was read. This includes the full proof of Theorem1.1, its reduction in Section2, both Lemmas3.1 and3.2 and the final contradiction in Section3. The book's two explicit uses of Pop16 are the theorem attribution in Chapter30 and the attribution of the integrated Cauchy--Schwarz estimate in its nested proof. The book proves the compact Kähler case in full.

The primary proof has the stronger ambient hypothesis ddc(omega^j)=0 for every j=1,...,n-1. Expanding each mixed product into closed class representatives, exact Hessians and powers of omega shows that the required ddc derivatives vanish. Integration by parts then makes the total and mixed integrals independent of the potential and of the Gauduchon test. Polynomial continuity as epsilon tends to zero suffices for every limiting step. No monotone choice of nef potentials or monotone arrow for these integrals is needed.

For each fixed epsilon the positive form beta_epsilon permits normalization of a Gauduchon test by integral beta_epsilon gamma^(n-1)=1, when n>=2. It also bounds the mass of the normalized test currents, giving the needed weak subsequence. Lamari's strict criterion converts failure of bigness to tests with the limiting pairing at most one. The Monge--Ampere equation and the trace inequality yield the opposite bound c_epsilon/(n M_epsilon). The two normalizations of the wedge-power trace were checked, including their factors n. Passing to the limit gives c_0<=n M_0, the desired contradiction. The one-dimensional normalization is handled separately by the Poisson equation; the book already includes this branch explicitly.

The reconstructed book proof is shorter: fix eta and then epsilon before choosing any test; put H=(a_tilde^(k-1) b_epsilon)/mu; use the positive eigenvalue inequality a_tilde>=c_epsilon b_epsilon/(kH), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz. Closedness gives integral H mu=N_epsilon. The resulting positive pairing for the fixed class A-B' and Lamari's criterion provide bigness after adding eta omega. All these steps and the changed numerical application were reread. The prior bounded report nef_schwarz_01 is correct and remains unchanged.

Dependencies actually used are the smooth Hermitian Monge--Ampere theorem, the proved Gauduchon--Lamari criterion, Stokes' theorem, finite-dimensional positive-matrix algebra, Cauchy--Schwarz and the Poisson equation on a compact curve. The book states the smooth equation as a standard reference and reconstructs the duality needed here. There is no reliance on a further theorem of Popovici, on a deformation statement, or on an unrestricted Hermitian bigness criterion. This check does not certify the other results in his publications.

## Checked use: the strongly Gauduchon criterion

The criterion is restated as Proposition1.2 in [arXiv:1009.5408v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.5408v1), *Stability of Strongly Gauduchon Manifolds Under Modifications*. Its complete proof is traced to Lemma3.2 and the argument for Proposition3.3, pages16--17 of [arXiv:0910.2032v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.2032v1). Both the definition equivalence and the entire separating-current argument were read. The version and locator matter: the later deformation arguments are separate and are not used here.

For dimension at least two, a positive (n-1,n-1)-form is the (n-1)st power of a unique Hermitian form. The strongly Gauduchon condition is therefore equivalent to existence of a closed real (2n-2)-form with strictly positive pure-type part. A nonzero positive exact (1,1)-current rules this out by distributional Stokes.

For the converse in the primary proof, the space of exact degree-two currents is weakly closed: a weak limit is closed and pairs to zero with every closed complementary smooth form, so the current de Rham theorem and Poincaré duality make it exact. Positive (1,1)-currents normalized by a fixed positive smooth test form constitute a weakly compact convex set. Separation from the linear space of exact currents supplies a smooth complementary form that is closed and has strictly positive pure-type part. Strict positivity follows by testing rank-one Dirac currents. The linearity of the exact-current space is essential for the separating functional to vanish on it.

The book proof gives the same criterion by separating in the real Fréchet space of smooth complementary forms: the closed subspace of closed forms and the open cone of forms with positive pure-type part are disjoint if no strongly Gauduchon metric exists. The separating current vanishes on the subspace. Arbitrary variation of all other type components forces it to have type (1,1); adding epsilon times a fixed positive form proves positivity on semipositive tests. The current de Rham theorem and Poincaré duality then make it exact. These arguments use only Hahn--Banach, current de Rham theory, Poincaré duality, Stokes and the positive-root algebra. No Popovici theorem is accepted as a black-box premise.

The definition, primitive, separation, exactness and Bott--Chern reformulation are checked; production and source reconstruction are recorded in the checkpoint audits. The proof check above concerns only this existence criterion. In particular it supplies no endorsement or use of the modification or deformation theorems in the same papers.

The forward mass estimate follows directly from the defining closed complementary form. Its separate bounded report strong_current_compactness_01 is correct; no additional result of Popovici is used.
