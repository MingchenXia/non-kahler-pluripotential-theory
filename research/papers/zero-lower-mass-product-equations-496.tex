\section{Exact equations on zero-lower-mass products}
\label{sec:zero-lower-mass-product-equations}

Let $(X_0,h_0)$ be a connected compact Hermitian manifold of dimension $m\geq1$ with $v_-(h_0)=0$. A singular potential pulled back from a closed factor gives explicit exact equations on the product, although the product background also has zero lower mass. The coefficient is determined by the bounded normalization constants, while exactness follows separately from the product measure.

Fix $Y=\mathbf P^k$, $k\geq2$, with the normalized Fubini--Study form $\kappa$, so that $\int_Y\kappa^k=1$. On $X=X_0\times Y$ put $N=m+k$, $H=h_0+\kappa$ and
\[
 V_0=\int_{X_0}h_0^m,\qquad C=\binom Nm,\qquad
 \mu=V_0^{-1}h_0^m\wedge\kappa^k,\qquad R=\frac{\mu}{\mathrm{d}V},
\]
where $\mathrm{d}V$ is any smooth strictly positive probability volume form on $X$. Pullbacks of factor forms and functions are understood. Thus $R$ is smooth and bounded above and below by positive constants. We use the normalization scalar and threshold of \cref{sec:individual-density-subsolutions}, and the relative envelope class of \cref{def:relative-envelope-class}.

For $0<\alpha<1$ define on $Y$
\[
 \psi([z_0:\cdots:z_k])=\log\frac{\sum_{i=1}^k|z_i|^2}{\sum_{i=0}^k|z_i|^2},
 \qquad u_\alpha=1-(1-\psi)^\alpha.
\]
Its only pole is $o=[1:0:\cdots:0]$, where its canonical value is $-\infty$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projective-sublogarithmic-density}
The function $u_\alpha$ is genuine $\kappa$-plurisubharmonic, with supremum zero, and
\[
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\kappa(u_\alpha)=q_\alpha\kappa^k,
 \qquad \int_Yq_\alpha\kappa^k=1.
\]
The density is smooth and strictly positive off $o$, belongs to $L^1(\kappa^k)$ and to no $L^p(\kappa^k)$ for $p>1$. Defining $q_\alpha(o)=+\infty$, every $\min(q_\alpha,s)$, $s>0$, is strictly positive and continuous on $Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Locally $\kappa+\ddc\psi=\ddc\log\sum_{i=1}^k|z_i|^2\geq0$. The function $\chi(t)=1-(1-t)^\alpha$, $t\leq0$, is increasing and convex, with $0<\chi'\leq\alpha$. Its chain rule gives $\kappa+\ddc u_\alpha\geq(1-\alpha)\kappa$ off $o$. Local psh extension across the pole proves the global assertion, with the stated canonical value. Along $z_0=0$, $\psi=0$ and is smooth, so $u_\alpha$ is smooth there and has supremum zero.

In the affine chart $z_0=1$ put $r=\sum_{i=1}^k|z_i|^2$, $s(r)=1-\log(r/(1+r))$ and
\[
 F(r)=\log(1+r)+1-s(r)^\alpha,\qquad
 Q(r)=rF'(r)=\frac{r+\alpha s(r)^{\alpha-1}}{1+r}.
\]
The tangential Levi eigenvalue of $F$ is $F'=Q/r$, with multiplicity $k-1$, and its radial eigenvalue is
\[
 Q'(r)=\frac{1-\alpha s(r)^{\alpha-1}+\alpha(1-\alpha)r^{-1}s(r)^{\alpha-2}}{(1+r)^2}>0.
\]
Radial integration gives $\int_{a<|z|^2<b}(\ddc F)^k=Q(b)^k-Q(a)^k$. The constant is fixed by $F(r)=\log(1+r)$, whose total mass is one in the chosen normalization. Since $Q(0)=0$ and $Q(\infty)=1$, this proves mass one on the punctured affine chart. The omitted hyperplane is smooth and has zero volume; the non-pluripolar measure also gives zero mass to $o$.

Relative to $\kappa^k$, the density on this chart is
\[
 q_\alpha(r)=(1+r)^{k+1}(Q(r)/r)^{k-1}Q'(r)
 \asymp r^{-k}(1-\log r)^{-1-k(1-\alpha)}\quad(r\downarrow0).
\]
The radial smooth volume is comparable to $r^{k-1}\mathrm{d}r$. Its product with this density is integrable. For its $p$th power, $p>1$, substituting $L=1-\log r$ gives an exponentially increasing factor $\mathrm{e}^{k(p-1)L}$ times a power of $L$, so the integral diverges. The same asymptotic gives uniform divergence to $+\infty$ at $o$, proving continuity of every capped density. Strict positivity follows from the strict background lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:split-product-normalization}
For a strictly positive continuous function $g$ on $Y$, with $M_g=\int_Yg\kappa^k$, the bounded-density scalar on $X$ is
\[
 c_H(Rg)=\frac{CV_0}{M_g}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First suppose $g$ is smooth. Apply \cref{thm:tw-smooth} on $Y$. Closed integration determines the normalization, giving a smooth $\phi_g$ with $A_g=\kappa+\ddc\phi_g>0$ and $A_g^k=(g/M_g)\kappa^k$. The pullback satisfies
\[
 (H+\ddc\phi_g)^N=C h_0^m\wedge A_g^k
                    =(CV_0/M_g)Rg\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
Uniqueness of the bounded equation scalar identifies $c_H(Rg)$. For continuous $g>0$, choose smooth $g_l>0$ converging uniformly to $g$. Its positive minimum permits $(1-\varepsilon_l)g\leq g_l\leq(1+\varepsilon_l)g$ with $0<\varepsilon_l<1$ tending to zero. The order and homogeneity in \cref{lem:bounded-normalizer-subsolution} yield
\[
 (1-\varepsilon_l)c_H(Rg_l)\leq c_H(Rg)
                         \leq(1+\varepsilon_l)c_H(Rg_l).
\]
Since $M_{g_l}\to M_g$, the smooth formula proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:zero-lower-mass-product-equation}
The genuinely nonclosed Hermitian background $H$ has $v_-(H)=0$. For every $0<\alpha<1$, $f=Rq_\alpha$ is a probability density in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ and in no $L^p(\mathrm{d}V)$ for $p>1$, and
\[
 b_H(f)=CV_0>0,\qquad
 \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u_\alpha)=b_H(f)f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
The pulled-back canonical potential has supremum zero and finite mass $CV_0$. It belongs to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,H,0)$, and $P_{\delta H}(u_\alpha)$ is genuine for every $0<\delta<1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $a=\min_XR>0$, $A=\max_XR$, and $M_s=\int_Y\min(q_\alpha,s)\kappa^k$. The capped densities are positive and continuous, and $M_s\uparrow1$. The original truncation satisfies
\[
 R\min(q_\alpha,j/A)\leq\min(Rq_\alpha,j)
                         \leq R\min(q_\alpha,j/a).
\]
The preceding lemma and scalar monotonicity give
\[
 \frac{CV_0}{M_{j/a}}\leq c_H(\min(f,j))
                         \leq\frac{CV_0}{M_{j/A}}.
\]
Both bounds tend to $CV_0$. This proves the threshold with the original truncations for the arbitrary reference $\mathrm{d}V$.

For $u_{\alpha,j}=\max(u_\alpha,-j)$, bounded local products and the factor dimensions give
\[
 \mathrm{MA}_H(u_{\alpha,j})
       =C h_0^m\wedge\mathrm{MA}_\kappa(u_{\alpha,j}).
\]
This identity follows by the binomial expansion of local bounded psh products with smooth-background corrections. Multiplying by $\mathbf1_{\{u_\alpha>-j\}}$ and passing to the defining non-pluripolar limit gives the exact equation, of mass $CV_0$. Smooth upper and lower bounds on $R$ transfer the density's integrability properties to $X$. Separately, pull back bounded $h_0$-psh potentials with masses tending to zero. The same factor expansion gives their $H$-masses as $C$ times the factor masses, hence $v_-(H)=0$. A closed positive $h_0$ would have every bounded mass equal to $V_0$ by Stokes, contradicting $v_-(h_0)=0$. Thus $h_0$, and hence $H$, is nonclosed.

For a stretching parameter $t\geq1$, choose $\alpha<\beta<1$ and $\varepsilon>0$ with $\varepsilon\beta\leq1$. The number
\[
 K_t=\sup_{s\geq1}\{t s^\alpha-\varepsilon s^\beta\}-t
\]
is finite. The genuine potential $a_t=-\varepsilon(1-\psi)^\beta-K_t$ has $\ddc a_t\geq-\kappa$ and satisfies $a_t\leq t u_\alpha$, including their common poles. Its pullback is $H$-psh, proving membership in the relative envelope class. For $0<\delta<1$, take $t=1$ and $\varepsilon\beta\leq\delta$. Then $a_1$ is a genuine $\delta H$-psh competitor below $u_\alpha$. The upper-regularized envelope lies below the usc obstacle and above this competitor, proving genuineness.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:elliptic-fivefold-exact-threshold}
Let $(X_0,\omega)$ be the elliptic threefold with its balanced metric from \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric}. On $X_0\times\mathbf P^2$, in background $H=\omega+\kappa$, the preceding densities satisfy
\[
 v_-(H)=0,\qquad b_H(f)=240\pi,
 \qquad\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_H(u_\alpha)=240\pi f\,\mathrm{d}V.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:vanishing-lower-mass} gives $v_-(\omega)=0$, and \cref{prop:lower-mass-balanced-metric} gives $V_0=24\pi$. The product theorem applies with $m=3$, $k=2$ and $C=\binom53=10$, yielding the displayed identities.
\end{proof}

These explicit equations distinguish a positive individual threshold from positive lower mass. They do not identify the limit of normalized exponential solutions for these data; exactness here comes from the factor product. The unrestricted problem in \cref{qu:individual-equation-threshold} still concerns all merely integrable densities at a positive threshold.
