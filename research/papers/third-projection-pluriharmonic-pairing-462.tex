\begin{lemma}\label{lem:third-projection-pairing}
Let $k\geq3$ and $1\leq p\leq k-2$. Write
\[
 M=\mathbb P(\mathbb C^k_u\oplus\mathbb C^k_v),\qquad
 A=\{u=0\},\quad B=\{v=0\},\quad C=\{v=u\},
\]
and let $\Pi:Z\to M$ be the blow-up of these three disjoint linear subspaces. The maps $[u:v]\mapsto[u]$, $[u:v]\mapsto[v]$ and $[u:v]\mapsto[v-u]$ resolve to holomorphic maps $\rho_u,\rho_v,f:Z\to\mathbb P^{k-1}$. With $\sigma$ the normalized Fubini--Study form on $\mathbb P^{k-1}$, put
\[
 \theta_u=\rho_u^*\sigma,\qquad \theta_v=\rho_v^*\sigma,
 \qquad \Phi=f_*(\theta_u^{p+1}\wedge\theta_v^{k-1}).
\]
Then $f$ is a holomorphic fiber bundle with fiber the blow-up of $\mathbb P^k$ at two distinct points. The form $\Phi$ is smooth, strongly positive and closed, and its de Rham class is $[\sigma^p]$. Consequently every positive $\ddc$-closed current $S$ of bidegree $(k-p-1,k-p-1)$ on $\mathbb P^{k-1}$ satisfies
\[
 \int_Z f^*S\wedge\theta_u^{p+1}\wedge\theta_v^{k-1}
 =\int_{\mathbb P^{k-1}}S\wedge\sigma^p.
\]
The pullback on the left is the ordinary distributional pullback under the submersion $f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First blow up $C$. The graph of the projection $[u:v]\mapsto[v-u]$ is
\[
 \mathbb P\bigl(\mathcal O^{\oplus k}\oplus\mathcal O(-1)\bigr)
 \longrightarrow\mathbb P^{k-1},
\]
where projectivization parametrizes lines. Over a line $\ell\subset\mathbb C^k$, its points are $[u:w]$ with $w\in\ell$, and its map to $M$ is $[u:w]\mapsto[u:u+w]$. On a chart meeting $C$, the incidence equations for $w$ are the ordinary blow-up of its $k$ transverse coordinates. Off $C$ the map is an isomorphism. Thus this graph is indeed $\operatorname{Bl}_C M$.

The strict transforms of $A$ and $B$ are disjoint sections, respectively $[0:w]$ and $[-w:w]$. Choose a local holomorphic generator $e$ of $\mathcal O(-1)$ and a holomorphic change of frame sending $e$ to the first standard vector $e_1$. The resulting fiber coordinates send the two sections to the fixed points $[0:1]$ and $[-e_1:1]$ of $\mathbb P^k$. Blowing up both sections therefore gives a locally trivial holomorphic bundle with the asserted fiber. Since $A,B,C$ are disjoint in $M$, their blow-ups commute. This bundle is $Z$, and the other two blow-ups resolve $\rho_u$ and $\rho_v$.

The relative dimension of $f$ is $k$. Fiber integration of the smooth closed form $\theta_u^{p+1}\wedge\theta_v^{k-1}$ therefore gives a smooth closed $(p,p)$-form $\Phi$. Each factor is semipositive, so their wedge and its fiber integral are strongly positive.

Let $H=\Pi^*c_1(\mathcal O_M(1))$, and let $D_A,D_B,D_C$ denote the exceptional divisor classes. The linear systems defining the three projections give
\[
 [\theta_u]=H-D_A,\qquad [\theta_v]=H-D_B,
 \qquad [f^*\sigma]=H-D_C.
\]
The exceptional divisors are disjoint, so every product involving two distinct $D$'s vanishes. For each center, the restriction of $H$ to its exceptional divisor comes from that center, which has dimension $k-1$. Hence
\[
 \int_Z H^{2k-1-j}D^j=0\quad(1\leq j\leq k-1),
 \qquad \int_Z H^{2k-1}=1.
\]
Indeed, the first integral is computed on $D$ after taking out one divisor factor, and the restricted power of $H$ has exponent at least $k$. All exceptional powers appearing in the following product are at most $k-1$, by the prescribed range of $p$. Thus
\[
 \int_{\mathbb P^{k-1}}\Phi\wedge\sigma^{k-p-1}
 =\int_Z(H-D_A)^{p+1}(H-D_B)^{k-1}(H-D_C)^{k-p-1}=1.
\]
The group $H^{2p}(\mathbb P^{k-1},\mathbb R)$ is generated by $[\sigma^p]$, so this identity proves the asserted class equality.

The smooth K\"ahler $\ddc$ lemma on projective space gives a smooth real $(p-1,p-1)$-form $U$ with $\Phi-\sigma^p=\ddc U$. Pairing the current $S$ with this smooth form gives
\[
 \langle S,\Phi-\sigma^p\rangle=\langle\ddc S,U\rangle=0.
\]
Since $f$ is a proper submersion, distributional pullback and fiber integration satisfy
\[
 \int_Z f^*S\wedge\theta_u^{p+1}\wedge\theta_v^{k-1}
 =\langle S,\Phi\rangle.
\]
Together these two identities prove the claim. Every test form paired with $S$ is smooth; no pairing with a distributional or $L^1$ primitive is used.
\end{proof}
