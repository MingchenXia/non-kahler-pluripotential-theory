## Uniqueness, regularity and selection

Section 11.6 proves that two bounded solutions of the same equation in a Hermitian background differ by a constant when the common density is smooth, nonnegative and has a connected dense positive set. Thus the old bounded connected-positivity question is settled in this smooth-density regime. The earlier C1,1 criterion allows continuous density. Surface identities, the threefold torsion-sign criterion, the square-root sublevel condition and Guan–Li comparison remain separate results. The projective examples still disprove unrestricted normalized uniqueness, even for smooth densities with thin or real analytic zero sets.

Section 14.12 identifies the entire vanishing-exponent family for a fixed nonzero Lp density, p > 1, in ordinary minimal type on a smooth big background. Convergence is in L1 and locally uniform off the strict barrier's poles; for a Hermitian background it is uniform on X. Selection identifies one normalized critical solution. It does not imply that every normalized critical solution equals it. General singular references and merely integrable data retain separate selection questions.

Sections 13.19–13.22 reconstruct fixed-reference compactness, local Hölder estimates and interior smoothness. Global Lp data supply the Hölder premise in the minimal type. The earlier need to import that particular Hölder conclusion is superseded; the original adverse report on the broader local-density-only source statement is preserved. Section 13.23 supplies a compact Kähler counterexample showing that a smooth positive density on an arbitrary open subset alone need not even imply continuity there. Smooth boundary traces give a separate local closed-background criterion. Neither nonminimal regularity nor behavior across the strict barrier's pole set is settled by the global-Lp theorem.

Section 27.3.1 applies the internally available regularity to the closed big-class ample-locus equation with a smooth positive global volume. Its classical closed existence and minimal-singularity inputs remain explicit. The non-nef incomplete-metric example in Section 27.7 and the sharp divisorial normal-length examples in Section 27.8 distinguish smoothness from completeness; they do not settle the separate nef or flow-compatible completeness questions.
