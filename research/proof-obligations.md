# Proof boundary and remaining obligations

Current ledger, 16 September 2026. The earlier chronological record is retained in `audits/proof-obligations-history-through-2026-09-15-0450.md`. The main theory does not assume the bounded mass property. A successful bounded audit is evidence for its specified argument and inputs; it is not formal certification or a novelty review.

## Standard reference boundary

The book retains local Bedford–Taylor theory, local capacity and weighted Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimates, local Skoda integrability, strong openness, valuative multiplier-ideal facts, classical Demailly regularization, basic current theory, and the indicated SGPT results. Standard functional analysis and elliptic/parabolic theory are stated where used. Nonclosed extensions of local formulas are justified rather than subsumed under a standard closed theorem.

## Closed loci and LCK proof boundary

The new closed-loci chapter reconstructs minimal multiplicities, the non-nef characterization, analytic attainment and the no-isolated-points argument. Classical Demailly regularization, Siu analyticity, the Kähler ddbar lemma and the Kähler-current/modification criterion are standard inputs. The nef-and-big null-locus equality is attributed to Collins–Tosatti; their numerical restricted-volume definition keeps its smooth-remainder and Kähler-error requirements.

The LCK chapter proves the Lee/Gauduchon identities, the balanced obstruction under Lee-curvature pseudoeffectivity, the ordinary closed-bigness obstruction, the twisted-potential and coupled-volume formulas, the Vaisman Hopf identification and basic transverse uniqueness. The full Chern–Ricci-flat Gauduchon rigidity proof has been reconstructed using standard conformal Ricci, Bianchi and Bochner identities, and separately verified. It concerns one metric satisfying both conditions.

Lee–Gauduchon duality and bimeromorphic invariance are now reconstructed in Section31.8. The proof identifies the kernel with real parts of closed holomorphic one-forms, proves openness in its annihilator, and makes the strict tests only outside that kernel. Closed positive currents are pulled back through local psh potentials and descended by pushforward; Thom theory supplies degree-one blowup invariance. Analytic weak factorization, AKMW Theorem0.3.1(2), is a standard external input. The independently constructed Calabi–Eckmann example proves that the zero cone does not imply a strongly Gauduchon or balanced metric, and that the image of j_X can be strictly smaller than the de Rham-exact Bott–Chern classes. Neither result assumes BMP.

The transverse Calabi–Yau existence theorem underlying the invariant-volume Vaisman theorem remains an explicit geometric input. No arbitrary-volume Vaisman existence is claimed. The definition of Vaisman and its volume theorem exclude zero Lee form.

Chapter 30 now contains the complete adaptation of the supplied Vu–Xia general Nakamaye proof, using the stated SGPT trace and singularity results and the precise Collins–Tosatti restricted-volume inputs. Its new weak nef-difference proof follows from the already reconstructed smooth Monge–Ampère equation and Lamari duality. The quantitative regularization and global ideal-potential lemmas are proved for a Hermitian reference metric; the former retains a closed background and only one-sided ideal control. The Kähler numerical theorem is not asserted for a nonclosed background. The bounded integration audit accepted all mathematics but recorded one stale map checksum; that metadata-only finding was repaired and deterministically checked, with the original adverse report preserved. See `editorial/nakamaye-integration-review.md` and the refreshed adaptation map.

## General-measure reconstruction and its hypotheses

XL26 Theorems 6.1–6.3 and Corollary 6.1 are now reconstructed for every smooth big real background under BMP and positive lower model-reference volume. Sections14.12–14.19 establish the relative compactness and restricted addition inputs; Sections14.20–14.23 prove cross-class projection, supersolutions, both general-measure equations, unrestricted addition and the ordinary-type Lp estimate. The cross-class rooftop is explicitly constructed; it is not inferred from same-class closure.

The reference's positive lower volume implies metric PVP under BMP: choose theta<=C omega and use lower-volume monotonicity to obtain 0<lowerVol_theta(phi)<=C^n v_-(omega). Thus the book's hypothesis package is equivalent to the source's stated regime once such a reference is fixed. No extension by deleting PVP is claimed. The relative Lp estimate starts in E_phi and uses an unrooted uniform moment with exponent m>2n+1. It does not invoke the source's m>n estimate, which already assumes ordinary type.

This closes these known-result proof obligations. It does not establish arbitrary-model general-measure existence for arbitrary big backgrounds without BMP. Such a theory must replace the mixed-mass bounds, finite upper relative volumes and their compactness uses, and prove finiteness of the actual equation measure. Minimal ordinary-type Lp equations already require neither BMP nor PVP in Sections13.17–13.19. See `editorial/big-model-equations-review.md` for the exact audit dependencies and source changes.

The semipositive prescribed-reference theorem stated in the book is now fully reconstructed in Chapter14. Its positive and zero-exponent existence assertions agree with ALS26 Theorem C and the corresponding assertions of Theorem5.8/Corollary5.9. The book proves the displayed uniform lower bound `(1-epsilon)^n v_-(theta)`; this should not be relabeled as a reconstruction of every additional relative-volume assertion in that paper.

Standard parabolic Chern–Ricci flow existence is explicitly cited in the geometry chapter; that chapter does not purport to reconstruct the entire parabolic theory.

## How the moving-measure compactness step is closed

A common lower envelope in the relative class need not have finite mass. The new zero-domination lemma avoids assuming that it does: its auxiliary envelope has measure supported where the potential is nonnegative, hence has finite mass by locality with one bounded truncation. Only that auxiliary measure enters the previously proved finite-measure domination theorem. Scaling its background preserves positive lower volume.

For moving bounded reference measures, weighted lower semicontinuity is applied after monotone capacity convergence of finite tail roofs. The zero-domination lemma identifies the lower limit without an upper mass bound. Uniform raw tails then give finiteness and weak convergence of the equation measures. Both stages were checked separately; the downstream audit was conditional on the exact zero lemma, whose separate audit passed.

The prescribed-type carrier representation remains a different assertion: its carrier can be unbounded and have infinite extended mass. The equation proof instead uses a bounded Hermitian carrier and the independently reconstructed minimal-reference theorem for comparison potentials. It never applies the same-background bounded-subsolution theorem directly to a different-background carrier.

Neither `MA(max(u,v)) <= MA(u)+MA(v)` nor unrestricted L1 lower semicontinuity of non-pluripolar mass is available. Capacity convergence is not total-variation convergence; an envelope-class condition is not a mass bound; BMP alone is not a uniform tail bound.

## Singularity approximation proof boundary

Section16.6 reconstructs the ideal part of SGPT Proposition3.2.10 and Theorem7.1.1 in the stated nonclosed setting. It uses a fixed-divisor discrepancy estimate and a common ideal-envelope lower barrier; neither mass continuity nor arbitrary L1 continuity of Q is invoked. The resulting P=Q criterion concerns envelopes of a specified dyadic analytic regularization. Under the explicit hypotheses of Proposition16.7.1 it also characterizes the upper volume equality, by combining that proposition with the new corollary. Analytic classes are dense everywhere for the elementary ideal metric in a Hermitian background, so its density condition supplies no restriction on an ideal class. A general topology on ordinary types, smooth-background compatibility and a volume characterization beyond BMP remain research obligations. ## Partial-kernel proof boundary

Section28.6 proves the relative-canonical multiplier-ideal formula by change of variables and Hartogs extension. Its all-weight ideal-order proof uses a local composite map that need not be proper over a downstairs coordinate chart; generic injectivity and compact-patch integrability suffice for the discrepancy estimate. Closed local potentials are retained in the envelope descent proof. The exact kernel isometry uses a fixed auxiliary line bundle and a smooth volume-density ratio, including at zeros of the Jacobian. These identities assume no BMP.

The asymptotic theorem imports the verified SGPT final-edition Theorem14.3.1 on a compact Kähler modification, with arbitrary fixed smooth auxiliary line bundle. This geometric hypothesis implies BMP and positive ambient growth forces Moishezonness. The determinant limit follows by integrating finite-dimensional variations and dominated convergence. No raw nonclosed Monge–Ampère primitive or lower Fubini–Study potential convergence is inferred. Outside this regime, lower asymptotics and relationships with Hermitian mass remain research obligations. See `editorial/birational-partial-kernels-review.md`.

Sections28.10–28.11 extend the fibration calculation to finite isolated logarithmic poles. The difference between the ambient and base multiplier thresholds is controlled by a finite jet quotient, not by global generation. Codimension O(m^(q-1)) yields O(1/m) total-variation distance between normalized selected kernels and the lifted base kernels. The base equilibrium limit remains weak in the general theorem. The explicit Hopf model separately proves total-variation convergence to the limit and an entropy-based lower potential estimate. It identifies the limiting envelope and the exact Hermitian mass factor, and distinguishes the raw selecting-weight measure. The new Problem32.12.2 asks when general fiberwise singularities admit positive-mass base approximants with codimension o(m^q). See `editorial/fiber-point-kernels-review.md`.

Sections20.8–20.9 give exact higher-dimensional traces over an ambient curve problem under a smooth connected-fiber holomorphic submersion. A Green pullback realizes every compact-model divisor order and its ordinary type is independent of a sufficiently large background. A product formula for extended Monge–Ampère measures includes infinite total mass and gives separated Lp equations. Products with a factor failing BMP have finite- and infinite-mass representatives with all the same multiplier ideals and exact trace numbers. Corollary24.5.1 combines the Gauduchon mass bound with a smooth residual current to determine a canonical ordinary type on each fixed model; this alone does not recover the trace data on further modifications. See `editorial/curve-fibration-traces-review.md`. The half-background condition is retained for the general ordinary-type equation; the explicit separated equation only needs A greater than the total atomic curve mass. No zero-exponent potential uniqueness is inferred.

## Mathematical research questions

The book leaves explicit questions on arbitrary exact higher-dimensional trace realization, a general singularity topology and I-goodness equivalences, lower partial Bergman asymptotics, positive-volume recovery of envelope systems, birational volume compatibility, weak adjoint uniqueness at all continuous logarithmic densities, and the geometric implications of positive Gauduchon volume. These require new arguments, rather than merely filling in the proof of a cited existence theorem.

Both smooth hypersurface extensions passed two independent audits and are included. The stronger one has exactly quadratic density zeros, while differences of potentials agree to infinite order with their separate constant interface values. The newly supplied positive uniqueness criteria and analytic-density example passed two further bounded audits, with their exact statements and dependencies recorded. Integration into the book changes some proof interfaces; those changes are listed in `editorial/uniqueness-integration-map.json` and were checked separately by the main agent.

Connectedness of the positive-density region is now a proved sufficient condition for C1,1 potentials with continuous common density. Its extension to arbitrary bounded potentials remains a question. C2 uniqueness on a surface no longer requires the Gauduchon condition. The threefold Sobolev result retains its stated torsion sign; there is no unrestricted higher-dimensional uniqueness claim. The analytic-density example has three positive-density components and therefore does not contradict the connectedness theorem. Selection for general degenerate data remains open in this manuscript.
