\section{Local corrections and a capacity criterion}

Work on a relatively compact holomorphic coordinate ball $U$, with the
restriction of the chapter's Hermitian form $\omega$. Let
$\mathcal H_m(U,\omega)$ denote the bounded canonical upper
semicontinuous functions $f$ for which every local smooth upper test
$q$ satisfies $\ddc q\in\overline{\Gamma_m(\omega)}$.
The equivalent linear distributional inequalities use the same
$\omega$ and the same cone tests as admissibility, but omit the additive
$\omega$ in the first factor. The classical linear
viscosity--distributional equivalence is retained as in the chapter.

\begin{lemma}\label{draft619:local-correction}
There is a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function $\rho$ on a
neighborhood of $\overline U$, with $\tau=\ddc\rho\geq2\omega$, such
that every bounded admissible $u$ on $X$ satisfies
$u+\rho\in\mathcal H_m(U,\omega)$. The smooth decreasing admissible
approximations of $u$, after addition of $\rho$, are smooth decreasing
approximations in this local class. A uniform bound on $u$ gives a
uniform local bound on the corrected functions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In the coordinate chart take $\rho=C|z|^2$. Compactness of
$\overline U$ and positivity of $\ddc|z|^2$ allow $C$ to be chosen so
that $\ddc\rho\geq2\omega$. If $q$ is an upper test for $u+\rho$ at
$x$, then $q-\rho$ is an upper test for $u$ there. Consequently
\[
 \ddc q=(\omega+\ddc(q-\rho))+(\ddc\rho-\omega)
                    \in\overline{\Gamma_m(\omega)}.
\]
Here the second summand is positive, and addition of a nonnegative
form preserves the cone. Smooth admissible approximations satisfy the
same identity pointwise. Adding the fixed smooth function preserves
decrease and pointwise convergence, and
$\|u+\rho\|_{L^\infty(U)}\leq\|u\|_{L^\infty(X)}+\|\rho\|_{L^\infty(U)}$.
The canonical representative is preserved under smooth addition, as
is also seen by the transformation of all the local linear tests.
\end{proof}

The correction gives an inclusion, not an identification of the two
classes. Moreover $\tau$ is closed, whereas
$A=\tau-\omega$ satisfies $\mathrm dA=-\mathrm d\omega$.
Thus the correction does not turn the Hermitian background into a
closed one.

For the remaining statements assume the local ordered construction
\[
 T_0=1,\qquad
 T_q(f_1,\ldots,f_q)=\ddc\bigl(f_qT_{q-1}(f_1,\ldots,f_{q-1})\bigr)
\]
for $f_i\in\mathcal H_m(U,\omega)$ has been established through degree
$m$, with its iterated smooth limits and smooth approximants on $U$
whose uniform bounds depend only on the potential bound and the fixed
local data. Assume its coefficient bounds
below degree $m$, its local Chern--Levine--Nirenberg bounds, and
positivity of mixed traces. These are explicit inputs here.
Put $r=m-1$ and define, for Borel $E\subset U$,
\[
 c_r(E)=\sup\left\{\int_E T_r(h,\ldots,h)\wedge\omega^{n-r}:
             h\in\mathcal H_m(U,\omega),\ -1\leq h\leq0\right\}.
\]
This set function is monotone and countably subadditive, by positivity
of each competitor measure. It is finite on compact subsets by the
local mass bounds. Put $c_r^*(E)=\inf_{G\supset E}c_r(G)$, where $G$
ranges over open subsets of $U$. Open supersets and subadditivity show that $c_r^*$
is monotone and countably subadditive, and $c_r^*(G)=c_r(G)$ for
open $G$. No quasicontinuity assertion is part of this construction.

\begin{lemma}\label{draft619:capacity-domination}
Fix $K\Subset U$ and $0\leq M<\infty$. For $0\leq q\leq r$ and local
potentials $f_1,\ldots,f_q$ of norm at most $M$, every coefficient
measure of $T_q(f_1,\ldots,f_q)$ satisfies
\[
 |\nu|(E)\leq C_{K,U,\omega,m,M}\,c_r^*(E),\qquad E\subset K.
\]
The coefficient norm is taken in a fixed coordinate frame.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For smooth functions put $S=\sum_{i=1}^q\ddc f_i$, with $S=0$ when
$q=0$. The absolute mixed-form estimate below degree $m$ bounds each
coefficient variation by
$C S^q\wedge\omega^{n-q}$, where the latter means $\omega^n$ for
$q=0$. Set $s=\sum_i f_i+\rho$. There are constants $B$ and $L\geq1$,
depending only on the fixed data and $M$, such that
$h=(s-B)/L$ lies between $-1$ and zero. It belongs to the local class.
In the expansion of
\[
 H_r(h)=L^{-r}(S+\tau)^r\wedge\omega^{n-r}
\]
all mixed terms are nonnegative. Since $\tau\geq\omega$, polarized
cone positivity also gives
\[
 H_r(h)\geq L^{-r}\binom rq
                  S^q\wedge\omega^{n-q}.
\]
Indeed, replacing any of the $r-q$ copies of $\tau$ by $\omega$
changes the mixed term by a nonnegative trace, because
$\tau-\omega$ is nonnegative. The remaining copies of $\omega$
complete a polarized product of at most $m$ cone forms.
For smooth factors, this bounds each coefficient variation on a Borel
set by $C c_r(E)$.

For rough factors choose the assumed iterated smooth approximations,
with the assumed common local bound. Apply the smooth estimate on a
slightly larger compact set $K\Subset K'\Subset U$. At each limit below
degree $m$, the coefficients converge weakly as signed measures by
the assumed coefficient bounds. Lower semicontinuity of total
variation on open subsets therefore preserves the estimate
$|\nu|(G\cap V)\leq Cc_r(G)$, where $K\subset V\Subset K'$
and $G$ is any open subset of $U$. For $E\subset K$, take open
$G\supset E$ and then their infimum. This proves the outer-capacity
assertion. No simultaneous limit or diagonal expansion for rough
functions is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{draft619:weighted-measures}
Let $c$ be a monotone countably subadditive set function on Borel subsets
of $U$. Let signed Radon measures $\nu_j$ converge weakly to $\nu$,
with locally uniformly bounded total variations. Assume that on each
compact $K\Subset U$ they and $\nu$ satisfy
$|\nu_j|(E),|\nu|(E)\leq C_Kc(E)$ for Borel $E\subset K$.
Let bounded Borel functions $v_j\downarrow v$ be uniformly bounded.
Suppose every member of the countable family $v,v_1,v_2,\ldots$ is
quasicontinuous for $c$: for every $\epsilon>0$ it is continuous
outside an open set of capacity less than $\epsilon$.
Then $v_j\nu_j\to v\nu$ weakly.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\phi\in C_c(U)$ and a compact $K\Subset U$ containing its
support. Allocate exceptional capacities less than
$2^{-a-1}\epsilon$ to the members of the countable family. The union
$G$ is open, $c(G)<\epsilon$, and all functions are continuous on
$F=K\setminus G$. This set is compact, so Dini's theorem gives
$\|v_j-v\|_F\to0$. Choose a bounded continuous extension $g$ of
$v|_F$ to $U$, with $\|g\|_\infty\leq M$, where $M$ bounds the
functions. If $F$ is empty, take $g=0$.
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 \left|\int\phi(v_j\,\mathrm d\nu_j-v\,\mathrm d\nu)\right|
 &\leq \|\phi\|_\infty\|v_j-v\|_F|\nu_j|(K)
       +\left|\int\phi g\,\mathrm d(\nu_j-\nu)\right|\\
 &\quad+2M\|\phi\|_\infty
                  \bigl(|\nu_j|(K\cap G)+|\nu|(K\cap G)\bigr).
 \end{split}
\]
For fixed $G$, the first two terms tend to zero; the last is at most
$4MC_K\|\phi\|_\infty\epsilon$. Letting $\epsilon$ tend to zero
proves the assertion. In particular, no convergence of integrals
over $F$ with its discontinuous indicator is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{draft619:conditional-transfer}
Assume in addition that every bounded function in
$\mathcal H_m(U,\omega)$ is quasicontinuous for $c_r^*$.
Then its ordered products are symmetric and simultaneously continuous
under uniformly bounded decreasing sequences, in distributions through
degree $m$ and weakly with measure coefficients below degree $m$.
Below degree $m$, the same convergence holds with an additional bounded
decreasing weight in this local class.

If these hypotheses hold on a finite coordinate cover of $X$, the
book's currents $D_p$ for bounded admissible potentials have the same
symmetry and simultaneous
convergence. The positive currents $Q_p$ converge weakly for
$1\leq p\leq m$; at $p=m$ this is weak convergence of positive Radon
measures. No order-zero assertion for the unwedged $D_m$ is made.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the local class, argue by induction on degree. Degree zero is
constant, and the capacity domination lemma includes its smooth
coefficient measure. Suppose $T_{p-1}(f_1^j,\ldots,f_{p-1}^j)$
converges weakly with measure coefficients to
$T_{p-1}(f_1,\ldots,f_{p-1})$. Capacity domination and the weighted
measure lemma, applied coefficientwise with weight $f_p^j$, give
convergence of their weighted products. Applying $\ddc$ proves
simultaneous distributional convergence at degree $p\leq m$.
For $p<m$, the uniform coefficient bounds upgrade distributional
convergence to weak convergence of Radon coefficients: smooth
approximation of a continuous compactly supported test has uniformly
small error in the pairings. The same capacity and weighted lemmas
then give the additional weighted conclusion at degree $p<m$.

Choose simultaneous smooth decreasing approximations for all factors.
The smooth wedge products are symmetric. Their just established limit
is the recursive product for any ordering, which proves symmetry.

For the book's potentials put $f_i=u_i+\rho$. The closed smooth form
$\tau=\ddc\rho$ gives the exact ordered expansion
\[
 D_p(u_1,\ldots,u_p)
 =\sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,p\}}
       (-1)^{p-|I|}\tau^{p-|I|}\wedge T_{|I|}(f_i:i\in I),
\]
with inherited order in each subset. To verify the identity, apply the
recursion and expand $u_p=f_p-\rho$. Every preceding term is closed
under both $\mathrm d$ and $\dc$, so
$\ddc(\rho T)=\tau\wedge T$ and smooth closed factors commute with
$\ddc$. This proves the formula inductively, without replacing
$\tau$ by the nonclosed form $A$ inside the recursion.
The local simultaneous limits and symmetry pass through this finite
sum. A finite cover of $X$ and a smooth partition of unity give the
global distributional and coefficient conclusions.

The subset formula defining $R_p$ uses fixed smooth powers of
$\omega$, so it also preserves these distributional limits and
symmetry. Finally $Q_p=R_p\wedge\omega^{n-m}$ is positive with uniform
local mass bounds by the existing ordered construction. Its
convergence therefore holds weakly as an order-zero positive current;
when $p=m$ it is convergence of Radon measures. This uses positivity
of $Q_p$, not an unproved coefficient bound for $D_m$.
\end{proof}

In addition to the stated local construction inputs, the analytic
input is quasicontinuity for this Hessian capacity. Its proof requires
mixed energy estimates and localization;
the Sobolev-capacity estimate for first Hessians does not supply it.
For a closed current $\eta$, a compactly supported smooth $\phi$ and
a smooth $v$, the exact Stokes identity is
\[
 \int\phi\,\ddc v\wedge\eta\wedge\omega^k
 =-\int\mathrm d\phi\wedge\dc v\wedge\eta\wedge\omega^k
   +k\int\phi\,\dc v\wedge\eta\wedge\mathrm d\omega
                                              \wedge\omega^{k-1},
\]
with the last term absent for $k=0$. These background terms must be
retained in the missing mixed energy argument.
