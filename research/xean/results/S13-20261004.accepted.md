## w7-1/n1

RADIAL PROFILE LEMMA

All derivatives below are with respect to the real variable t. We construct F in C-infinity(R) with
  0<F'(t)<1,  F''(t)>0,
and with the following exact models.

1. Blow-up end. Fix c=1/100. For t near and below -2 set
  L(t)=-3 log(1-c exp(t/2)),
  p_L(t)=L'(t)=(3/2)q/(1-q),  q=c exp(t/2).
Then
  p_L'(t)=(3/4)q/(1-q)^2,
  p_L p_L'=(9c^2/8) exp(t)(1-c exp(t/2))^(-3).
Consequently, if F=B+L on this end, then the exact identity is
  F' F''=K exp(F+t),  K=(9c^2/8)exp(-B)>0.
Also, as t tends to minus infinity,
  F=B+3c exp(t/2)+O(exp(t)),
  F''~(3c/4)exp(t/2).
The denominators are positive on a neighborhood of (-infinity,-2]. In particular p_L(-2)<1/50, say.

2. Cusp end. For 0<p<1 put
  H(p)=integral from p to 1 of (1-q)q dq
      =1/6-p^2/2+p^3/3
      =(1-p)^2(1+2p)/6.
This is positive, and H'(p)=(p-1)p. Define
  T(p)=2+integral from 3/4 to p of q/H(q)dq.
It is smooth and strictly increasing. The integral tends to infinity as p tends to 1, because q/H(q)~2/(1-q)^2. Thus T has a smooth inverse p_R(t) on [2,infinity), extending smoothly to a neighborhood of t=2. It satisfies
  p_R(2)=3/4,  p_R'=H(p_R)/p_R>0,
  p_R(t) tends to 1.
Define
  F_R(t)=t+log H(p_R(t)).
Direct differentiation, using H'=(p-1)p, gives
  F_R'=1+(H'/H)(H/p)=p_R,
  F_R''=H(p_R)/p_R,
  F_R' F_R''=H(p_R)=exp(F_R-t).
This proves existence and the exact ODE identity on the entire cusp end without a tuning or shooting argument.

For the asymptotics set q(t)=1-p_R(t). Then q decreases to zero and
  (1/q)'=p_R'/q^2=(3-2q)/(6(1-q)) tends to 1/2.
Integration and division by t imply 1/(tq) tends to 1/2. Hence
  1-p_R~2/t,
  H(p_R)~2/t^2,
  F_R''~2/t^2,
  F_R-t=-2 log t+log 2+o(1).
These are all endpoint asymptotics needed below. Higher derivative control at the divisor is unnecessary because the volume coefficient will cancel exactly, not just asymptotically.

3. Interpolation. We describe a rigorous positive-derivative interpolation between p_L at -2 and p_R at 2. Write d=p_R(2)-p_L(-2)>0. Choose disjoint sufficiently short endpoint collars in [-2,2], and smooth cutoffs alpha_L, alpha_R supported in those collars, respectively equal to 1 on still shorter endpoint collars. They take values in [0,1]. The endpoint functions p_L and p_R are defined on these collars. Choose a small number eta>0 and initially set
  Q_0=alpha_L p_L'+alpha_R p_R'+eta(1-alpha_L-alpha_R).
This is strictly positive and agrees with the required endpoint derivatives near the endpoints. By first shortening the collars and then reducing eta, its integral can be made less than d. Choose a nonzero nonnegative smooth bump b compactly supported in (-2,2), and set
  Q=Q_0+C b,
  C=(d-integral Q_0)/(integral b)>0.
Then Q>0, its integral is exactly d, and it retains the endpoint germs. Define on [-2,2]
  p(t)=p_L(-2)+integral from -2 to t of Q(x)dx.
Because its endpoint values and all derivative germs match, it patches smoothly with p_L for t<=-2 and p_R for t>=2. It is strictly increasing and satisfies 0<p<1 everywhere.

Finally define F by F(2)=F_R(2) and F'=p. Then F=F_R for t>=2 and F=B+L for t<=-2, with B determined by the integration. All stated endpoint identities hold exactly. The interpolation introduces no restriction on B, and K is strictly positive for every resulting B.

This completes the profile construction and independently proves existence and the asymptotics of the only auxiliary ODE used in the counterexample.

## w7-1/n2

A COMPLETE NEF-AND-BIG COUNTEREXAMPLE

We use dd^c=(i/(2 pi)) partial barpartial throughout. The profile F, constants c and B, and positive constant K are those constructed and proved in n1. Write p=F'. Thus 0<p<1 and p'=F''>0 everywhere, and
  pp'=K exp(F+t) for t<=-2,
  pp'=exp(F-t) for t>=2.
At the two ends,
  F=B+3c exp(t/2)+O(exp(t)),  p'~(3c/4)exp(t/2) as t tends to minus infinity,
  1-p~2/t,  p'~2/t^2,
  F-t=-2 log t+log 2+o(1) as t tends to infinity.

I. THE COMPACT MANIFOLD AND THE NEF-BIG CLASS

Let X=P^2 with homogeneous coordinates [Z0:Z1:Z2], let P=[1:0:0], and let pi:Y->X be its blow-up at P. For an explicit projective model,
  Y={([Z0:Z1:Z2],[ell1:ell2]) in P^2 x P^1 : Z1 ell2=Z2 ell1}.
This is the smooth incidence surface, and its embedding in the product makes it compact, connected, and Kähler. Let f:Y->P^1 be the second projection. Let E=pi^(-1)(P), and let
  D=pi^(-1)({Z0=0}).
D is a smooth nonempty divisor, isomorphic to P^1, disjoint from E. In particular it is simple normal crossing.

Write H=pi^*c1(O_P2(1)). It is nef, since it admits the smooth semipositive representative pi^*omega_FS; equivalently adding any positive multiple of a Kähler form gives a Kähler representative of the perturbed class. It is big by the following explicit current construction. If L=f^*c1(O_P1(1)), then L=H-[E]: the pullback of a line through P consists of its strict transform, a fiber of f, plus E. For epsilon>0 the form
  pi^*omega_FS+epsilon f^*omega_FS
is Kähler on Y, since (pi,f) is an embedding and this form is the restriction of a product Kähler form. Its class is H+epsilon L=(1+epsilon)H-epsilon[E]. Consequently
  (pi^*omega_FS+epsilon f^*omega_FS)/(1+epsilon)
       +(epsilon/(1+epsilon))[E]
is a Kähler current in H. Thus H is big. Also H^2=1, either by two generic lines missing P, or by the degree-one birational pullback computation.

II. A SMOOTH CURVATURE FORM AND THE EXACT HERMITIAN NORM

Choose an even, nonnegative, smooth function eta, positive on (-1,1), supported in [-1,1], with integral 1. Set
  U(t)=integral max(0,t-x) eta(x)dx.
Then U is smooth and convex,
  U=0 for t<=-1,  U=t for t>=1,
  U'>=0, U''>=0.
On the affine chart Z0!=0 put z=(Z1/Z0,Z2/Z0), s=|z|^2, and t=log s. Define a Hermitian metric on O_P2(1) by giving the frame Z0 squared norm exp(-U(t)). This is smooth at z=0 because U is identically zero there. At infinity, in the chart Z1!=0 write
  w=Z0/Z1,  v=Z2/Z1,  h=1+|v|^2.
Then s=h/|w|^2 and, near w=0, U=t, so the frame Z1 has squared norm
  exp(-U)/|w|^2=1/h.
This proves smoothness and strict positivity of the Hermitian metric across the line at infinity. The analogous chart with Z2!=0 covers its other points.

Pull this metric back to O_Y(D)=pi^*O_P2(1). Let sigma=pi^*Z0 be the canonical defining section of D, and let theta be its smooth Hermitian curvature. On the affine chart,
  |sigma|^2=exp(-U(t)),  theta=dd^c U(t).
On the infinity chart,
  |sigma|^2=|w|^2/h,  theta=dd^c log h.
The curvature is semipositive: away from the origin this follows from U'>=0 and U''>=0 in the radial Hessian, and near the origin it is zero. Thus theta is a globally smooth closed real (1,1)-form, semipositive, in the nef-big class H. It vanishes on a neighborhood of E. The constant potential 0 has minimal theta-psh singularities: any theta-psh function is bounded above on compact Y, and 0 itself is theta-psh. In particular the reference minimal potential can be taken to be 0, and the volume of the class is the mass of theta^2, namely 1.

III. THE POTENTIAL AND ITS POSITIVITY

On Y minus E union D, identified with C^2 minus {0}, define
  rho=F(t)-U(t),  Omega=theta+dd^c rho=dd^c F(t).
Extend rho to E by its limit B, and set rho=-infinity on D. It is globally upper semicontinuous and theta-psh, as we now check at the missing divisors.

The radial Hessian on C^2 minus {0} has eigenvalues p/s and p'/s; both are strictly positive. Hence Omega is smooth and strictly positive on
  U_0=Y minus (E union D),
a dense Zariski open subset.

Near E, use blow-up coordinates z1=u, z2=u v. Then s=|u|^2 h and t=log(|u|^2h). Since theta=0 there, the local potential is
  F(t)=B-3 log(1-c |u| sqrt(h)).
The function F(log|z|^2) on the base is psh off the origin and bounded above near it. Its upper semicontinuous extension is psh by the removable-singularity submean-value argument for locally bounded-above psh functions across an analytic set. Pulling back gives the stated continuous psh function near E. Thus there is no positivity problem at E.

Near D, theta has local potential log h, and rho+log h is psh off w=0 because its dd^c is Omega. It tends to minus infinity there, is locally bounded above, and therefore has the psh extension taking value minus infinity on w=0. Explicitly
  rho=F(t)-t=log H(p(t))=-2 log t+log 2+o(1),
where t=log(h/|w|^2). This proves positivity of the global current across D. The standard psh extension fact here can also be obtained directly by applying the submean inequality on transverse disks and taking an upper limit across w=0; local boundedness above is the necessary hypothesis.

All Lelong numbers of rho are zero. This is immediate from boundedness near E and smoothness elsewhere away from D. At D its growth is of log-log type, so rho/log|w| tends to zero. Thus rho has the same Lelong numbers as the minimal potential 0.

IV. THE GLOBALLY SMOOTH STRICTLY POSITIVE VOLUME FORM

On U_0 define
  dV_Y=exp(-F(t)) Omega^2.
We prove that this extends to a globally smooth strictly positive volume form, rather than merely one whose coefficient decays at D.

Let dLambda denote Euclidean Lebesgue volume in the indicated complex coordinates. The exact radial determinant formula in C^2 is
  Omega^2=(2/pi^2) pp'/s^2 dLambda_z.
The factor follows from our normalization dd^c=(i/(2pi)) partial barpartial.

At E, z=(u,uv), so dLambda_z=|u|^2 dLambda_(u,v) and s=|u|^2 h. Thus
  Omega^2=(2/pi^2) pp'/(|u|^2 h^2) dLambda_(u,v).
For all sufficiently small |u| the first exact endpoint identity gives pp'=K exp(F) s. Therefore
  dV_Y=(2K/pi^2) h^(-1) dLambda_(u,v).
This is smooth and strictly positive across u=0 and independent of u. The analogous exceptional chart covers the rest of E.

At D, z=(1/w,v/w), so dLambda_z=|w|^(-6)dLambda_(w,v), s=h/|w|^2, and
  Omega^2=(2/pi^2) pp'/(|w|^2 h^2) dLambda_(w,v).
For all sufficiently small |w|, the second exact endpoint identity gives exp(-F)pp'=exp(-t)=|w|^2/h. Hence
  dV_Y=(2/pi^2) h^(-3)dLambda_(w,v).
This is smooth and strictly positive across w=0. The chart with Z2!=0 gives the same conclusion at the other infinity points. These extensions agree on overlaps because they agree on the dense punctured overlaps, and smooth forms that agree there agree everywhere. On the intervening compact region dV_Y is already smooth and positive because F is smooth and Omega is Kähler. We have therefore constructed the required globally smooth strictly positive dV_Y.

In particular the prescribed pole is genuinely nonintegrable, with an exact smooth positive coefficient. Near D,
  |sigma|^(-2)dV_Y
      =(2/pi^2)|w|^(-2)h^(-2)dLambda_(w,v).
The coefficient (2/pi^2)h^(-2) is smooth and strictly positive at w=0, and the transverse integral of |w|^(-2) diverges logarithmically.

V. THE ACTUAL EQUATION AND FULL MASS

On U_0, since |sigma|^2=exp(-U),
  exp(rho)|sigma|^(-2)dV_Y
    =exp(F-U)exp(U)exp(-F)Omega^2
    =Omega^2.
This is the displayed equation with exactly the data just defined, not an asymptotic equation or a weakened logarithmic pole.

The non-pluripolar measure of the global current agrees with Omega^2 on U_0 and puts no mass on E or D. The right-hand side has no mass on E because it is absolutely continuous with respect to the smooth volume form there and rho is finite; it is extended by zero on D as required. Thus the equality on U_0 proves equality of the global measures.

For its mass, polar integration in C^2 gives
  integral Omega^2=integral from minus infinity to infinity of 2p(t)p'(t)dt
                  =p(infinity)^2-p(-infinity)^2
                  =1.
For completeness, dLambda_z has radial density pi^2 s ds; substitution in the determinant formula above gives exactly 2pp'dt after integrating the angular probability measure. Since the minimal-potential mass is integral theta^2=H^2=1, rho has full mass.

VI. FINITE ENERGY, CHECKED DIRECTLY

We give both the endpoint integrability estimates and a bounded-truncation energy argument, so that no specialized existence or finite-energy theorem is needed.

Near E, rho is bounded and theta=0. The only nonzero top-degree mixed measure relevant to the energy is Omega^2, whose density there is exp(F)(2K/pi^2)h^(-1), locally integrable. Hence all energy contributions near E are finite. On the compact region away from D and E everything is smooth and bounded.

Near D put
  alpha=dd^c t=dd^c log h,
  beta=(i/(2pi)) partial t wedge barpartial t.
Here alpha and beta have rank one, theta=alpha, and
  Omega=p alpha+p' beta,
  Omega^2=2pp' alpha wedge beta,
  Omega wedge theta=p' alpha wedge beta,
  theta^2=0.
Integration of alpha wedge beta over the angular and projective directions gives dt. Consequently the endpoint integrability reduces to
  integral_T^infinity |rho| p' dt<infinity,
  integral_T^infinity |rho| 2pp' dt<infinity,
which follow from |rho|=O(log t) and p'=O(t^(-2)).

To verify the E^1 definition by truncations, choose a constant M with phi=rho-M<=0, and set phi_k=max(phi,-k). For all large k, the truncation changes phi only sufficiently near D, since phi is bounded on the complement of any fixed neighborhood of D. On the cusp end phi=log H(p(t))-M is strictly decreasing to minus infinity. Let T_k be its unique crossing of -k. Thus
  k=2 log T_k+O(1).
Near D the local potential for theta+dd^c phi_k is
  G_k(t)=max(F(t)-M,t-k).
Its derivative equals p(t) before T_k and 1 after T_k, with an upward jump from p(T_k) to 1. Smooth convex regularization of this kink gives the radial mass formulas d((G_k')^2) for its square and d(G_k') for its wedge with theta. Therefore the kink contributes shell masses
  1-p(T_k)^2 to (theta+dd^c phi_k)^2,
  1-p(T_k) to (theta+dd^c phi_k) wedge theta.
Beyond the shell these top-degree mixed measures vanish because G_k is linear and theta has rank one. The potential is -k on the shell, so the absolute energy contributions from it are respectively
  k(1-p(T_k)^2),  k(1-p(T_k)),
both tending to zero, since 1-p(T_k)~2/T_k and k=O(log T_k). The formula for the shell square follows also by integrating 2q dq from p(T_k) to 1 during convex smoothing; it does not assume an ill-defined product of delta currents.

All interior energy integrals converge absolutely by the estimates already proved. Hence each of
  integral phi_k theta^2,
  integral phi_k (theta+dd^c phi_k) wedge theta,
  integral phi_k (theta+dd^c phi_k)^2
has a finite limit. In particular the usual relative Aubin-Mabuchi energies
  E_theta(phi_k)=(1/3) sum over j=0,1,2 of
     integral phi_k (theta+dd^c phi_k)^j wedge theta^(2-j)
are uniformly bounded below and have a finite limit. This is precisely finite E^1 energy relative to the minimal potential 0. Adding back M does not change finiteness. Thus rho belongs to E^1(Y,theta).

The bounded-potential wedge products used in this computation are the usual Bedford-Taylor products. Their radial formulas can be checked by the convex smoothings just described. At E the bounded psh extension has no mass on the analytic curve E (equivalently the radial boundary mass tends to zero because p(t) tends to zero). Thus there is no omitted boundary energy contribution. We have constructed the full-mass finite-energy solution itself; by the uniqueness stipulated in the question it is the solution denoted there by rho.

VII. FINITE DISTANCE TO AN UNAVOIDABLY MISSING EXCEPTIONAL DIVISOR

The form Omega is not smooth at any point of E. In the exceptional chart fix v=v_0 and put h_0=1+|v_0|^2. Its restriction to the transverse punctured disk is
  Omega|_(v=v_0)=(i/(2pi)) p'(log(|u|^2 h_0)) |u|^(-2) du wedge dbar u.
Since p'~(3c/4)exp(t/2), its normal coefficient is asymptotic to
  (3c sqrt(h_0)/4)|u|^(-1),
which diverges at every point u=0. Thus no point of E can belong to an open locus where the induced metric is smooth. Likewise at D the normal coefficient is asymptotic to 2/(|w|^2 t^2), which diverges, so D is not a smooth locus either. Consequently every dense Zariski open positive regular locus U is contained in U_0.

Nevertheless the radial approach to E has finite length. Along u=r>0, v=v_0, one has t=2 log r+log h_0. With the real Riemannian metric associated to Omega, the length is, up to the displayed fixed normalization,
  (1/(2 sqrt(pi))) integral from minus infinity to t_0 of sqrt(p'(t))dt.
This is finite because sqrt(p')=O(exp(t/4)) at the negative end. Equivalently the normal metric coefficient is of order 1/r, giving length integral of r^(-1/2)dr.

It remains essential to show incompleteness for every candidate dense Zariski open regular locus, rather than merely for U_0. Let U be any such locus, and let A=Y minus U be its proper closed analytic complement. Since E is wholly nonsmooth, E is contained in A. On the compact complex surface Y, the analytic set A has finitely many curve and point components. Any curve component other than E meets E in finitely many points; otherwise it would contain E as a component. Choose e in E avoiding those finite intersections and all point components. D is already disjoint from E. A sufficiently small coordinate neighborhood V of e therefore has
  A intersect V=E intersect V.
Choose exceptional coordinates (u,v) centered along that point, fix v=v_0, and take the radial curve u=r, 0<r<=r_0, entirely inside V. It lies in U and converges to e as r tends to zero. Its intrinsic metric length is finite by the preceding estimate. Points r_j tending to zero form a metric Cauchy sequence because the remaining tail lengths tend to zero. They have no limit in U: the smooth positive metric induces the ordinary manifold topology at every point of U, whereas their only limit in compact Y is the missing point e. Hence U is incomplete.

VIII. CONCLUSION AND SCOPE

These data satisfy every condition in the nef-and-big question: Y is compact connected Kähler; theta is globally smooth, closed, real and nef-big; D is nonempty smooth SNC; sigma has a smooth Hermitian squared norm; dV_Y is globally smooth and strictly positive; the prescribed density has an exact nonintegrable divisor pole with coefficient smooth and strictly positive across D; rho solves the actual exponential equation, is full mass and E^1, and has the minimal Lelong numbers; and its induced metric is smooth and strictly positive on U_0. Nevertheless every dense Zariski open positive regular locus is incomplete because of the unavoidable exceptional boundary.

Thus mere nefness and bigness do not force the asserted completeness. This is a complete negative resolution only of that branch. No specific geometric-smoothing flow data, discrepancy conditions, semipositive residual, or flow-prescribed volume have been verified here, so the separate flow-compatible branch remains explicitly unclosed.
