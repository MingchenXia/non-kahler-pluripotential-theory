# S07: divisorial smoothing and adjoint obstruction

A precise counterexample to unrestricted smooth recovery of relative non-pluripolar adjoints. On P1 x P1 an admissible unbounded ordinary model type has singleton support dV and the meaningful residual adjoint beta/4. Two monotone, uniformly positive smooth regularizations converge canonically, in capacity and smoothly off the pole divisor, with log-densities converging in all finite Lp, but have different normalized classical adjoint probability limits, each placing mass one half on the divisor. Fixed-type, mass-preserving or uniformly integrable-exponential extensions are not refuted; broader S07 remains open.

Accepted note: w7-1/n2

Immutable verified transitive argument:

## w7-1/n1

Explicit admissible divisorial model on P1×P1: theta=2ω1+2ω2, psi=log|s|², D={p}×P1, and phi=P_theta[psi]~psi. For this reference B_phi(G)=log4−log∫e^G dV, so every continuous-density support is singleton. At F=0, u=psi has non-pluripolar residual beta=ω1+2ω2 and normalized weak adjoint rho=1/4, nu=dV. This supplies the exact singular target for n2, not an assertion about arbitrary smooth regularizations.

Take normalized Fubini–Study forms ωi on X=P1×P1, dV=ω1∧ω2, theta=2ω1+2ω2, and a section s of O(1) on the first factor vanishing at p, with r=|s|²∈[0,1]. Then dd^c log r=[D]−ω1, beta=theta−ω1=ω1+2ω2, beta²=4dV. The model envelope phi=P_theta[log r] is boundedly equivalent to log r: subtracting log r from each obstacle envelope produces bounded beta-psh functions, whose suprema have a uniform bound by a compact Green-function estimate and the obstacle on a fixed open set away from D. Type invariance then proves P_theta[phi]=phi and sup phi=0. Since (3/4)theta−ω1>0, log r itself lies below phi and is (3/4)theta-psh, proving the additional admissibility condition. Every ordinary-type solution has a bounded beta-psh remainder. Bounded Bedford–Taylor integration by parts gives total residual mass 4; a displayed logarithmic-cutoff argument proves specifically that this measure does not charge D. Consequently the prescribed scalar is 4/∫e^GdV, independently of potential uniqueness. Jensen and directional differentiation prove A_phi(F)={e^F dV/∫e^F dV}. For u=log r and F=0, the non-pluripolar first and second products are beta and beta². Multiplication by rho=1/4 is unambiguous, dd^c(rho beta)=0, ∫rho beta²=1, and the weak linearization h↦2dd^ch∧beta has this adjoint. No external singular Monge–Ampère existence theorem is used: ordinary-type solvability/scalar uniqueness are premises of the task.

This note constructs and checks a genuine singular-reference target used in the approximation obstruction in n2. All constants use dd^c=(i/(2π))∂bar∂.

1. Geometry and singular potential.
Let X=P1×P1. Let ω1 and ω2 be the pullbacks of the Fubini–Study form, each normalized to integral 1 on its factor. Put
  dV=ω1∧ω2,   theta=2ω1+2ω2.
Thus dV is a smooth positive probability and theta is Kähler (in particular smooth semipositive and big). Fix p on the first P1. For the section s of O(1) vanishing at p, use its standard Fubini–Study metric, scaled so r=|s|² has maximum 1. Define
  D={p}×P1,   psi=log r,   beta=theta−ω1=ω1+2ω2.
Here psi is the genuine canonical logarithmic representative, equal to −infinity on D. Poincaré–Lelong, with the displayed convention, gives
  dd^c psi=[D]−ω1,
so theta+dd^c psi=beta+[D]. On X\D it is the smooth positive form beta. Directly from the non-pluripolar definition, which excludes D, its extended products are
  <theta+dd^c psi>=beta,   <(theta+dd^c psi)^2>=beta²=4dV.
The smooth form beta and its powers put no mass on D, so these extensions do not introduce any unspecified boundary product.

2. Checking the ordinary model and the extra admissibility hypothesis.
Put phi=P_theta[psi]. We verify phi~psi, rather than assuming preservation of analytic singularities.
For C≥0 set v_C=P_theta(min(psi+C,0)). The obstacle and the competitor psi imply
  psi≤v_C≤0,   v_C≤psi+C.
These inequalities hold for canonical representatives; near D the upper bound tends to −infinity, so usc regularization does not change it. On X\D let w_C=v_C−psi. Then 0≤w_C≤C and beta+dd^c w_C≥0. Since beta is closed, it has local smooth potentials. The usual removable-singularity statement for locally upper-bounded psh functions across a complex hypersurface, applied after adding such a local potential, extends w_C as a bounded beta-psh function on X. This removable statement follows locally by defining the extension by limsup and applying the submean inequality on complex lines; boundedness above prevents a negative boundary defect. In particular no external result about singular envelopes is required.

There is a bound sup_X w_C≤K independent of C. Here are details of the compact estimate used for this claim. Fix ω=ω1+ω2 and its Riemannian Laplacian with the subharmonic sign. The inequality beta+dd^c w≥0 implies Δ_ω w≥−K0, with K0 fixed. A normalized Green kernel G(x,y) for this Laplacian can be chosen nonpositive and with uniformly bounded absolute integral. For a smooth w,
  w(x)−average(w)=∫G(x,y)Δw(y)dy≤K1.
Indeed Δw+K0 is a positive measure and G≤0. The same inequality for bounded quasi-psh w follows by Laplacian heat regularization and canonical limits. Hence, if S=sup w, then ∫(S−w)≤K1 Vol(X). On the fixed positive-volume set E={r≥1/2}, the obstacle v_C≤0 gives w_C≤log2. Therefore
  (S_C−log2)Vol(E)≤∫_E(S_C−w_C)≤K1 Vol(X),
which is the promised uniform bound. It follows that
  psi≤phi≤psi+K.
Also phi≤0 and phi≥psi with sup psi=0, so sup phi=0.

The operator P_theta[·] depends only on bounded-equivalence type: shifting its input by a bounded amount merely shifts the parameter C between two bounded translates, and squeezing the increasing limits proves equality. Since phi~psi, this gives P_theta[phi]=P_theta[psi]=phi. Thus phi is an ordinary model reference.

Take epsilon=3/4. Then
  epsilon theta−ω1=(1/2)ω1+(3/2)ω2>0.
Consequently psi is epsilon theta-psh and psi≤phi. It is a genuine competitor for P_(epsilon theta)(phi). This envelope is not identically −infinity and is bounded above by 0. Thus phi satisfies exactly the additional admissible-reference condition in the question.

3. Scalar functional and singleton supports in this singular class.
If v~phi, then v~psi. On X\D, w=v−psi is bounded and beta-psh. Adding local potentials of beta and using the same removable argument extends it as a bounded beta-psh function on X. The equality v=psi+w holds canonically, including their common poles. Set T=beta+dd^c w. On X\D the extended Monge–Ampère product is T², in the bounded Bedford–Taylor sense.

For completeness one can check specifically that T² does not charge D, without invoking a general theorem about arbitrary pluripolar sets. Shift w by a constant so 0≤w≤M. Define the bounded quasi-psh cutoff
  f_t=max(psi/t,−1),  t>0.
Then dd^c f_t≥−ω1/t, −f_t=1 on D, and f_t→0 on X\D. Bounded Bedford–Taylor integration by parts and closedness of T+beta give
  ∫(−f_t)T²
  =∫(−f_t)beta²+∫w dd^c(−f_t)∧(T+beta)
  ≤∫(−f_t)beta²+(M/t)∫ω1∧(T+beta).
The last mixed mass is finite and independent of w: Stokes gives ∫ω1∧T=∫ω1∧beta=2. The first integral tends to zero by dominated convergence against a smooth volume. Since ∫(−f_t)T²≥T²(D), this proves T²(D)=0. All identities here are bounded-potential identities: they follow from the local definition dd^c(wS) for closed Bedford–Taylor currents and integration by parts, or from bounded regularization followed by that definition. No multiplication of an unbounded function by a measure is used.

The same bounded-current Stokes identity gives
  ∫T²=∫beta²=4;
for example T²−beta²=dd^c(w(T+beta)). Thus MA_ext_theta(v)=T² globally, and its mass is 4 for every v of the reference type.

The task supplies existence of ordinary-type solutions and uniqueness of their scalar for all bounded logarithmic densities in this admissible regime. Integrating their equation therefore yields
  c_phi(G)=4/(∫e^G dV),
  B_phi(G)=log4−log(∫e^G dV).
This use of solvability is exactly a premise of the problem, not an unverified external singular Monge–Ampère theorem.

For continuous F put Z_F=∫e^F dV and nu_F=e^F dV/Z_F. Jensen proves
  log Z_G−log Z_F≥∫(G−F)dnu_F,
so nu_F belongs to A_phi(F). Conversely, if nu belongs to A_phi(F), apply its defining inequality to G=F±th for an arbitrary continuous real h and let t tend to zero. Differentiation under the integral gives ∫h dnu=∫h dnu_F. Therefore
  A_phi(F)={nu_F}.
In particular A_phi(0)={dV}.

4. An explicitly defined adjoint at the singular target.
The potential u=psi is an ordinary-type solution for F=0 with c_phi(0)=4. Its non-pluripolar residual is beta. The first weighted product is, by ordinary smooth multiplication,
  Q=rho <theta+dd^c u>=beta/4,   rho=1/4.
It satisfies dd^c Q=0 and ∫rho <(theta+dd^c u)^2>=1. The associated probability is rho beta²=dV, precisely the singleton support just calculated.
For a smooth test perturbation h, the residual linearization is the measure-valued operator
  L_u h=2dd^c h∧beta.
It is the derivative of the non-pluripolar square for u+th while beta+tdd^c h remains positive. Stokes gives ∫rho L_u h=0. Hence the product, normalization, operator identity and supporting-probability relation are all meaningful at this unbounded potential.

This note does not assert that this adjoint is recovered by arbitrary smooth regularizations of u. The difference between its non-pluripolar residual mass 4 and the smooth theta-class mass 8 is central to n2.

## w7-1/n2

Complete obstruction to unrestricted smooth-approximation adjoints in the admissible singular regime. On the target in n1, smooth uniformly theta-positive u_delta^g=log(r+delta e^g) decrease canonically to the same unbounded solution and converge in capacity, C∞ off D, and L1; their smooth logarithmic densities converge to F=0 in every finite Lp. Nevertheless their normalized classical adjoint probabilities have g-dependent limits: (1/2)dV+(1/2)[D]∧ω2+(1/8)[D]∧dd^c g. None equals the singleton support dV. The normalized adjoint currents converge to (beta+[D])/8, not beta/4, and cannot be a function times the non-pluripolar residual beta. No claim is made against fixed-type or uniformly bounded-density approximations.

The explicitly formulated assertion refuted is: for an admissible singular-reference solution, arbitrary smooth strictly theta-psh regularizations converging canonically and in capacity should yield an approximation-independent normalized classical adjoint, whose probability is the singleton support and whose current is a weighted non-pluripolar residual. It remains false even if one additionally assumes uniform lower positivity, C∞ convergence away from the poles, and convergence of smooth logarithmic densities in all finite Lp. On P1×P1 use n1's theta=2ω1+2ω2, D, r, psi, beta, F=0 and c=4. For any fixed smooth g on the second factor with dd^c g≥−ω2, set u_delta^g=log(r+delta e^g). Writing lambda=r/(r+delta e^g) and h=log r−g−log delta, the exact formula is Omega_delta=(2−lambda)ω1+2ω2+(1−lambda)dd^c g+lambda(1−lambda)dh∧d^c h≥ω1+ω2. Each form is Kähler and has mass 8, so its normalized adjoint weight is the constant 1/8. The normal-spike measure lambda(1−lambda)dlog r∧d^c log r concentrates on D, with lambda asymptotically uniform on [0,1]; averaging the extra factor 1−lambda gives 1/2. Consequently Omega_delta² converges to 4dV+4[D]∧ω2+[D]∧dd^c g. Choosing g=0 and a small nonconstant g gives different limits despite the same limiting canonical potential. The currents (1/8)Omega_delta converge to (beta+[D])/8, containing a divisorial part impossible for any locally integrable multiplier of smooth beta. The density F_delta=log(Omega_delta²/(4dV)) is smooth, satisfies the exact equation with scalar 4, and converges to zero in finite Lp, but ∫e^{F_delta}=2 for every delta. Thus exponentiated densities are not uniformly integrable. The obstruction is mass concentration at poles and loss of non-pluripolar mass, not a failure of conformal-multiplier estimates. Uniform L∞ logarithmic-density or fixed singularity-type/mass-preserving approximation theorems are explicitly outside its scope.

VERIFIED OBSTRUCTION; this is a complete resolution through the obstruction alternative in the task, not an unrestricted existence theorem.

A. The unrestricted assertion being tested.
Consider the following proposed extension of the smooth adjoint construction:
(U) Let theta and phi be in the problem's admissible singular-reference regime. Let u be an ordinary-type solution at a smooth logarithmic density F, and suppose A_phi(F) is singleton. Whenever smooth potentials u_j with smooth strictly positive forms Omega_j=theta+dd^c u_j converge canonically to u, in capacity and smoothly off its pole set, the normalized classical adjoint measures and currents
  nu_j=rho_j Omega_j^n,   Q_j=rho_j Omega_j^(n−1),
  dd^c Q_j=0,   ∫rho_j Omega_j^n=1,
should produce an approximation-independent adjoint of u. In particular nu_j should converge to the singleton support, and the limiting current should be a justified weighted non-pluripolar residual rho <(theta+dd^c u)^(n−1)> with the corresponding normalization.

Here the approximations are unrestricted smooth regularizations: no uniform upper bound on their logarithmic densities and no preservation of relative non-pluripolar mass is assumed. We will disprove (U), even with the additional conditions Omega_j≥a fixed Kähler form and convergence of logarithmic densities in every finite Lp. This is not a bounded-potential Gauduchon continuity example: the limiting potential has a genuine logarithmic divisorial pole and the reference is a genuine admissible ordinary model.

B. The singular solution and its support.
Use exactly the example fully checked in n1:
  X=P1×P1,   theta=2ω1+2ω2,   dV=ω1∧ω2,
  D={p}×P1,   r=|s|² with max r=1,   u=psi=log r,
  beta=ω1+2ω2,   theta+dd^c u=beta+[D].
The reference phi=P_theta[psi] is an ordinary model with phi~psi, and P_((3/4)theta)(phi) is genuine. The ordinary-type equation is
  MA_ext_theta(u)=beta²=4dV,
so F=0 and c_phi(0)=4. Moreover
  A_phi(0)={dV}.
The meaningful normalized non-pluripolar adjoint at u is beta/4, with weight rho=1/4 and supporting probability dV. These facts, including the scalar formula for all continuous densities, are established in n1.

C. A family of strictly positive smooth regularizations.
Let g be any fixed smooth real function on the second P1 such that
  gamma:=dd^c g≥−ω2.
In formulas on X, g and gamma are pulled back from that factor. Both g=0 and any sufficiently small nonconstant g satisfy this condition. For delta>0 define the globally smooth potential
  u_delta^g=log(r+delta e^g).
As delta decreases to zero, u_delta^g decreases pointwise to the same genuine canonical u=log r, including the value −infinity on D. On every compact subset of X\D the convergence is C∞. These are actual monotone smooth regularizations, not merely a sequence of measures declared to represent an adjoint.

Define, on X\D,
  alpha=log r,
  lambda=r/(r+delta e^g),
  h=alpha−g−log delta.
Choose d^c=(i/(4π))(bar∂−∂), so dd^c has the required convention and dh∧d^c h is a positive rank-one (1,1) form. The chain rule for log(e^alpha+delta e^g), using dd^c alpha=−ω1 off D, gives the exact identity
  Omega_delta^g=theta+dd^c u_delta^g
  =A_delta+R_delta,
  A_delta=(2−lambda)ω1+2ω2+(1−lambda)gamma,
  R_delta=lambda(1−lambda)dh∧d^c h.
Hence
  A_delta≥ω1+ω2,   R_delta≥0,
so Omega_delta^g≥ω1+ω2 on X\D and, by continuity of the smooth form, on all X. Thus every approximation is uniformly strictly theta-positive. Each form is closed. Smooth Stokes gives
  ∫(Omega_delta^g)^2=∫theta²=8.

Since the forms are Kähler, their classical adjoint weights are constant: dd^c(rho Omega)=dd^c rho∧Omega=0 is the elliptic harmonic equation for rho, whose smooth solutions on this connected compact manifold are constant by the maximum principle. With the required normalization,
  rho_delta^g=1/8,
  nu_delta^g=(Omega_delta^g)^2/8,
  Q_delta^g=Omega_delta^g/8.
This explicitly controls the conformal multipliers; the counterexample does not hide an unproved assertion about their behavior.

D. Exact limiting probabilities and their dependence on g.
We now compute the weak limit. The computation also verifies all normalization constants.
Because R_delta has rank one, R_delta²=0. Thus
  (Omega_delta^g)^2=A_delta²+2A_delta∧R_delta.
The coefficients of A_delta are uniformly bounded. Since lambda→1 off D,
  A_delta² → beta²=4dV
by dominated convergence.

Put Q_N=dalpha∧d^c alpha and Q_T=dg∧d^c g. Because alpha depends only on the first factor and g only on the second, the cross terms in dh∧d^c h vanish when wedged with the diagonal form A_delta. Consequently
  A_delta∧R_delta
  =lambda(1−lambda)Q_N∧(2ω2+(1−lambda)gamma)
   +lambda(1−lambda)(2−lambda)ω1∧Q_T.
The second term tends to zero as a measure: its smooth density is uniformly bounded and tends to zero almost everywhere.

For the normal term, the following elementary concentration identities hold:
  lambda(1−lambda)Q_N ⇀ [D],
  lambda(1−lambda)^2 Q_N ⇀ (1/2)[D].
They are uniform in the second coordinate. Here is a direct verification, so no general theorem about products of unbounded potentials is needed. Near p use the affine coordinate z in which
  r=|z|²/(1+|z|²).
For fixed second coordinate write delta'=delta e^g. After z=sqrt(delta') zeta, the first normal measure converges to
  (i/(2π)) d zeta∧d bar zeta /(1+|zeta|²)^2.
It has total mass 1. Its radial variable y=|zeta|² has measure dy/(1+y)^2, and lambda converges to y/(1+y). Therefore lambda is uniformly distributed on [0,1] with respect to this normal spike, and the extra factor 1−lambda has average 1/2. Outside a fixed neighborhood of p the normal measure is O(delta'); outside a shrinking rescaled disk its tails are bounded by the tail of dy/(1+y)^2. These bounds justify both weak limits for arbitrary smooth test functions. Since delta' lies between two fixed positive multiples of delta, the argument is uniform over the compact second factor.

It follows that
  (Omega_delta^g)^2 ⇀ 4dV+4[D]∧ω2+[D]∧gamma,
and hence
  nu_delta^g ⇀ nu^g
  :=(1/2)dV+(1/2)[D]∧ω2+(1/8)[D]∧dd^c g.
The displayed measure is positive: its divisorial component is [D]∧(4ω2+gamma)/8, and 4ω2+gamma≥3ω2. Its total mass is 1 because ∫gamma=0 on P1. In particular it has mass 1/2 on D.

For g=0 the limit is
  nu^0=(1/2)dV+(1/2)[D]∧ω2.
Choose a sufficiently small smooth nonconstant g. On P1, dd^c g is then not identically zero (a real globally harmonic function would be constant), so nu^g differs from nu^0. Thus two monotone, uniformly strictly positive smooth approximation sequences of precisely the same canonical unbounded solution have different normalized classical adjoint probability limits. Neither limit can be the singleton support dV; both charge D, whereas dV does not.

E. The limiting weighted current is also incompatible with the required product.
The functions u_delta^g converge to psi in L1. For example 0≤u_delta^g−psi≤log(1+delta e^(max g)/r) off D, and logarithmic integrability near the smooth divisor proves L1 convergence. Therefore distributional differentiation gives
  Q_delta^g=Omega_delta^g/8 ⇀ (beta+[D])/8.
This current is independent of g, but it is not the normalized non-pluripolar adjoint beta/4. More fundamentally it cannot equal rho beta for any locally integrable function rho giving ordinary multiplication of this smooth positive form: rho beta has locally integrable coefficients and no divisorial component, while (beta+[D])/8 has a nonzero singular component on D. Thus a weak limit of the classical weighted currents does not automatically define the displayed weighted non-pluripolar product, even when all conformal multipliers are perfectly controlled constants. The top products retain additional, approximation-dependent information, as the two probabilities above demonstrate.

F. Convergence and solvability properties of the approximation class.
Each regularization is a genuine global smooth theta-psh potential with uniformly positive form. It is an explicit classical solution, so there is no hidden solvability hypothesis. Define its smooth logarithmic density by
  F_delta^g=log((Omega_delta^g)^2/(4dV)).
Then exactly
  (Omega_delta^g)^2=4 exp(F_delta^g)dV.
Each F_delta^g is bounded (separately for each delta) and smooth. On X\D it tends to F=0 in C∞.

In fact F_delta^g→0 in Lp(dV) for every finite p. Uniform positivity gives F_delta^g≥−log2. For the upper bound, |dalpha|² with respect to ω1+ω2 is at most C/r, while g and all its derivatives are bounded. Since lambda(1−lambda)≤1/4, the exact formula gives
  Omega_delta^g≤C(1+1/r)(ω1+ω2)
on X\D, uniformly for small delta. It follows that
  |F_delta^g|≤C+2|log r|.
The right side belongs to every finite Lp(dV), and pointwise convergence off D then proves the claim by dominated convergence.

The potentials also converge in the usual compact Kähler capacity. Indeed, for any eta>0,
  {u_delta^g−psi>eta}⊂{r<delta e^(max g)/(e^eta−1)},
apart from D itself. These are shrinking neighborhoods of the pluripolar divisor. Their capacity tends to zero. In this particular setting this last elementary capacity fact can be checked directly: the bounded logarithmic cutoffs max(psi/t,−1) used in n1, tested against the defining bounded omega-psh capacity potentials and integrated by parts, give a uniform bound tending to zero for the capacity of {psi<−t}. Thus the capacity assertion does not posit convergence at noncanonical values on D. If desired one can impose sup-normalization on the smooth potentials; subtracting their suprema, which tend to zero, leaves all forms and conclusions unchanged.

There is an essential missing density condition:
  ∫exp(F_delta^g)dV=8/4=2
for every delta, whereas ∫exp(0)dV=1. Consequently exponentiated densities are not uniformly integrable and do not converge in L1. The smooth measures preserve theta-class mass 8; the target's non-pluripolar mass is only 4. This explicitly locates the obstruction.

G. Scope of the conclusion.
Assertion (U) is false in a completely verified admissible singular-reference example, even though the supporting probability is singleton and the target itself has a well-defined normalized weak non-pluripolar adjoint. It is false for two different admissible smooth-regularization paths, not merely because a particular sequence lacks a subsequential limit. Uniform lower positivity, monotonic canonical convergence, capacity convergence, smooth convergence off the poles, and finite-Lp convergence of logarithmic densities do not repair it.

The construction does NOT refute a restricted theorem whose approximation class preserves the divisorial singularity type, discards the divisorial concentration before normalization, preserves full relative mass, or requires uniform L∞ bounds on the logarithmic densities or uniform integrability of their exponentials. Smooth approximants in this note are necessarily bounded and are not individually of the singular reference type. That distinction is stated, not suppressed: the assertion refuted is precisely the unrestricted use of smooth regularizations of an unbounded ordinary-type potential. Any positive extension must control or prohibit this concentration mechanism. This is beyond the existing bounded Gauduchon continuity result because its failure is entirely at genuine unbounded poles.
