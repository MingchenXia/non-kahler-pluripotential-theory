## 1. Anisotropic integrability lemma
Let p,b_1,...,b_r be positive integers, λ>0, m≥1, and k≥0 an integer. Put S=Σ_{j=1}^r 1/b_j. Near the origin in C^{r+1},

I=∫ |z|^{2k} (|z|^{2p}+Σ_j |w_j|^{2b_j})^{-mλ} dV(z,w)

is finite if and only if

(k+1)/p+S>mλ.                                             (1)

The same assertion holds after adding auxiliary complex variables ranging in a polydisc. Replacing the logarithmic weight by that weight plus a bounded function does not change integrability for any fixed m. Multiplying z^k by a holomorphic unit also does not change the criterion.

Proof. Integrate out the arguments of z,w_j and set x_0=|z|^{2p}, x_j=|w_j|^{2b_j}. Up to positive constants the integral becomes

∫ (x_0+...+x_r)^{-mλ} x_0^{(k+1)/p-1} Π_{j=1}^r x_j^{1/b_j-1} dx_0...dx_r.

All exponents a_0=(k+1)/p and a_j=1/b_j are positive. A sufficiently small simplex {Σx_i<δ} lies inside the integration box, and the box lies in a larger simplex. With t=Σx_i and x_i=t u_i, the simplex integral is a finite positive beta-integral in u times

∫_0^δ t^{Σa_i-1-mλ}dt.

The beta-integral is finite because every a_i>0. Thus finiteness is exactly (1). Equality in (1) replaced by equality of the two sides gives logarithmic divergence.

Equivalently, with c=pλ and a=pS, the condition on k is k+1>mc-a. Therefore the smallest nonnegative permissible integer is

K_m=max{0,floor(mc-a)}.                                   (2)

This formula includes integer endpoints: if mc-a is an integer N≥0, k=N-1 fails and k=N succeeds.

## 2. Uniform dimension bound without projectivity
Let Y be a compact complex manifold of dimension t, and let F,G be fixed holomorphic line bundles. There is a constant C such that, for all integers a,b,

h^0(Y,F^a⊗G^b)≤C(1+|a|+|b|)^t.                           (3)

Proof for t≥1. Choose smooth Hermitian metrics on F,G and a smooth positive volume form on Y. Use finitely many coordinate charts, with relatively compact smaller charts covering Y, and holomorphic frames for F,G. Write N=1+|a|+|b|. In such frames the logarithm of the metric on F^a⊗G^b has all real second derivatives bounded in absolute value by C_0N, uniformly on the charts.

At any point y in a smaller chart, multiply the frame by a nonvanishing holomorphic exponential of an affine-linear function. Normalize its length at y to one and cancel the real linear term of its log metric there. Every real linear function of complex coordinates is the real part of a holomorphic affine-linear function, so this is possible. Taylor's theorem then shows that the metric in this frame is bounded above and below by uniform positive constants on the Euclidean ball centered at y of radius R=cN^{-1/2}, with c fixed sufficiently small.

For a holomorphic section s=f e in this frame, the Euclidean submean inequality and equivalence of the volume forms give

|s(y)|^2≤C_1N^t ∫_Y |s|^2 dV.                            (4)

Equip the finite-dimensional space H^0(Y,F^a⊗G^b) with this L^2 inner product. Evaluation at y is a linear map to a one-dimensional Hermitian space. Its squared operator norm equals Σ_i |s_i(y)|^2 for any orthonormal basis {s_i}; (4) consequently bounds this sum by C_1N^t, not by C_1N^t times the dimension. Integrating over Y gives

h^0(Y,F^a⊗G^b)=∫_Y Σ_i |s_i(y)|^2dV≤C_1 Vol(Y)N^t.

For t=0 the manifold is a finite set of points and the assertion is immediate. This proves (3). The auxiliary L^2 norm is used only to estimate a dimension; no section space is defined by a norm.

## 3. Bounded divisorial bands
Let B be a compact complex manifold of dimension q≥1, D a smooth connected effective divisor, and A a fixed line bundle. Suppose integers 0≤K_m≤L_m satisfy L_m=O(m) and L_m-K_m≤M for a fixed M. Then

0≤h^0(B,A^m(-K_mD))-h^0(B,A^m(-L_mD))=O(m^{q-1}).         (5)

Indeed, for each integer j between K_m and L_m-1 the exact sequence

0→A^m(-(j+1)D)→A^m(-jD)→A^m(-jD)|_D→0

gives a bound on the successive dimension difference by h^0(D,A^m(-jD)|_D). Apply (3) on D, of dimension q-1, to F=A|_D and G=O(-D)|_D. Since j=O(m), this bound is O(m^{q-1}), uniformly in the band, and there are at most M steps. When q=1, a smooth connected divisor is a single point, each restriction space has dimension one, and the defect is at most L_m-K_m.

## w7-1/n2

# A sufficient anisotropic criterion, with genuinely non-descended examples

Write d=dim X-dim B. The following gives sufficient conditions on the fixed data in the question. It does not assert that arbitrary selecting weights admit a base reference.

## The criterion
Assume the following additional conditions.

**Base positivity.** There is a nonempty smooth connected effective divisor D⊂B, with defining section s_D of O(D) and a smooth Hermitian metric H_D. Write η=c_1(O(D),H_D). There are a real number c>0 and a smooth real function u on B such that

β=θ+dd^c u-cη

is a Kähler form. This is a geometric positivity condition on the residual class c_1(A)-c{D}. On a Kähler base the condition that this class be Kähler also gives such a u by the dd^c lemma. In particular, if A is ample, it holds for every sufficiently small c>0: choose a positive metric on A, compare it with the fixed metric h to obtain u, and then subtract the sufficiently small smooth form cη.

**No stronger unrelated selecting singularities.** For some constant C,

φ≥c log||π^*s_D||^2-C                                   (6)

on X, with the inequality interpreted in the extended-real sense.

**One anisotropic fiber witness.** There is an adapted coordinate polydisc in X, with coordinates

(z,t_1,...,t_{q-1},w_1,...,w_r,v_1,...,v_{d-r}),

where 1≤r≤d, π(z,t,w,v)=(z,t), and D is {z=0} in the base chart. For positive integers p,b_1,...,b_r and a constant C', throughout this polydisc,

φ≤(c/p) log(|z|^{2p}+Σ_{j=1}^r|w_j|^{2b_j})+C'.          (7)

The upper-bound chart need only lie over a nonempty open part of D. Neither a global section nor a global normal-coordinate splitting is required for this criterion.

Define

ψ=u+c log||s_D||^2,
S=Σ_j 1/b_j,  a=pS,
L_m=floor(mc),  K_m=max{0,floor(mc-a)}.

Then, for every positive integer m,

W_m=H^0(B,A^m(-L_mD)) ⊂ V_m ⊂ H^0(B,A^m(-K_mD)).         (8)

Moreover,

ψ∈PSH(B,θ),
∫_B ⟨(θ+dd^cψ)^q⟩=∫_B β^q∈(0,∞),
0≤dim V_m-dim W_m=O(m^{q-1})=o(m^q).                     (9)

Thus the requested inclusion actually holds for all m, not merely for sufficiently large m.

## Proof
### Holomorphic descent and the meaning of the spaces
A holomorphic function on a compact connected fiber is constant. Locally trivialize A on B. A section of π^*A^m then has a holomorphic coefficient on the inverse image which is constant on every fiber. A holomorphic submersion has local holomorphic sections, so the descended coefficient is holomorphic on the base. Surjectivity gives uniqueness, and these coefficients glue. Hence

H^0(X,π^*A^m)=H^0(B,A^m),

and V_m is exactly the subspace of descended sections whose pullbacks are locally integrable against e^{-mφ}. Smooth local metrics and smooth positive volume forms do not change local integrability. No choice of an L^2 norm changes V_m or W_m.

### Positivity and mass of the base potential
With the convention in the question, Poincaré–Lelong reads

dd^c log||s_D||^2=[D]-η.

Therefore

θ+dd^cψ=β+c[D]≥0.

The displayed ψ is upper semicontinuous and quasi-psh, so ψ∈PSH(B,θ). Off D the current is the smooth form β. The non-pluripolar product discards the locus where ψ=-∞, namely D, and therefore is the extension by zero of β^q from B\D. Since a smooth volume form gives D zero measure, this is just β^q on all of B. Consequently its mass is ∫_Bβ^q, which is finite by compactness and strictly positive because β is Kähler. This argument also applies when q=1: the non-pluripolar part is β, not β+c[D].

### The base multiplier ideal and the first inclusion
Near D, ψ=c log|z|^2 plus a smooth function. A holomorphic germ having order k in z is locally integrable against e^{-mψ} precisely when

k+1>mc.

Thus

I(mψ)=O_B(-floor(mc)D).                                  (10)

At an integer endpoint mc=N, order N-1 is not integrable, whereas order N is. In particular (10) has the floor, with no endpoint adjustment.

Because u is bounded on compact B, (6) gives φ≥π^*ψ-C'' for another constant. For each fixed m,

|π^*f|^2e^{-mφ}≤e^{mC''}|π^*f|^2e^{-mπ^*ψ}

almost everywhere. In local submersion coordinates, local integrability of the right-hand side follows from local integrability on the base and integration over a bounded fiber polydisc. Therefore W_m⊂V_m for every m.

### The witness gives the second inclusion
Take a nonzero descended section s∈V_m and let k be its vanishing order along the connected smooth divisor D. In the witness chart its local coefficient can be written z^k g(z,t), where g(0,t) is not identically zero on D in that chart. Indeed, otherwise the identity theorem on the connected smooth divisor would contradict the definition of k. Choose a point in this chart where g(0,t)≠0 and shrink around it, so that g is a unit. When q=1 there are no t variables, and the leading coefficient is already nonzero.

By (7), integrability of this pulled-back section implies integrability of

|z|^{2k}(|z|^{2p}+Σ_j|w_j|^{2b_j})^{-mc/p}.

The anisotropic lemma proved in n1 says this requires

(k+1)/p+S>mc/p,

or k+1>mc-pS. Its smallest permissible nonnegative integer is K_m. Thus every section in V_m vanishes at least to order K_m along D, proving the second inclusion in (8). Only a witness over an open part of D was needed to detect this divisorial order.

### The dimension defect
Since a=pS>0,

0≤L_m-K_m≤ceil(a).                                      (11)

Indeed, floor(x)-floor(x-a)≤ceil(a), and truncating the second floor at zero can only decrease this difference when x=mc≥0. Both orders are O(m). By (8),

0≤dim V_m-dim W_m
 ≤h^0(B,A^m(-K_mD))-h^0(B,A^m(-L_mD)).

The bounded-divisorial-band estimate proved in n1 gives O(m^{q-1}). This estimate requires no projectivity assumption: its proof bounds sections on the smooth compact (q-1)-fold D by local submean estimates for metrics whose weights have second derivatives O(m).

For q=1, D is one point, every successive restriction space has dimension one, and the sharper bound is

0≤dim V_m-dim W_m≤L_m-K_m≤ceil(a).

This is O(1)=o(m). All formulas remain valid when mc or mc-a is an integer. In the anisotropic condition equality k+1=mc-a diverges logarithmically, which is exactly accounted for by K_m=max(0,floor(mc-a)). This proves (9).

## Exact local models give an exact description of V_m
Here is a useful stronger geometric version. Suppose Z⊂π^{-1}(D) is a compact smooth submanifold projecting submersively onto D, and at each point of Z there are adapted coordinates as above in which

Z={z=w_1=...=w_r=0},
φ=(c/p)log(|z|^{2p}+Σ_j|w_j|^{2b_j})+O(1),               (12)

with the same p,b_j,c. Suppose φ is locally bounded outside Z. Then (6) follows by a finite covering of compact X, because the model in (12) dominates c log|z|^2 up to a constant; (7) is immediate. Furthermore,

V_m=H^0(B,A^m(-K_mD))                                   (13)

for every m. To prove the reverse inclusion in (13), a section vanishing to order at least K_m has coefficient z^{K_m} times a bounded holomorphic function near every point of Z. The anisotropic lemma gives local integrability there. Outside Z, the weight is locally bounded. The other inclusion was already proved. Thus the transverse weighted dimension produces a bounded, explicitly computed relaxation of the base vanishing order.

## A nonempty family of genuinely non-descended selectors
Take a projective Kähler base B, an ample A with the given arbitrary smooth metric h, and a smooth connected effective divisor D. Let

X=B×(P^1)^d,   d≥1,

and let π be the first projection. Choose 1≤r≤d and in the first r fiber factors choose marked points o_j. Let E_j be the divisor where the j-th fiber coordinate equals o_j, with defining section s_{E_j} and a smooth metric. Give O(D) a smooth metric as well. For any positive integers p,b_1,...,b_r put

Q=||π^*s_D||^{2p}+Σ_{j=1}^r||s_{E_j}||^{2b_j},
φ=λ log Q,   c=pλ.                                     (14)

The common zero locus is

Z=D×{o_1}×...×{o_r}×(P^1)^{d-r}.

Outside Z, φ is smooth. Near Z, smooth positive metric factors show

φ=λlog(|z|^{2p}+Σ_j|w_j|^{2b_j})+O(1).

Also Q≥||π^*s_D||^{2p}, so (6) holds with C=0.

For completeness, φ can be made ω-psh for the fixed arbitrary Hermitian form ω on X. View Q as the squared norm of a holomorphic section of the direct sum of the line bundles π^*O(pD), O(b_1E_1),...,O(b_rE_r), with the direct-sum metric. There is a constant C_0 such that

dd^c log Q≥-C_0ω.

One way to check this is to use local metric weights ρ_j: away from component zeros the log-sum-exp Hessian of Σ|f_j|^2e^{-ρ_j} is a weighted average of -dd^cρ_j plus a nonnegative variance term. The finitely many smooth curvature forms have a uniform lower bound by -C_0ω. The same inequality extends across individual zeros by continuity and across common zeros by the locally bounded-above psh extension after adding a sufficiently positive local quadratic function. Thus choosing λC_0≤1 gives ω+dd^cφ≥0.

Choose a positive metric on A and let u be the smooth comparison function with the fixed h. For λ>0 small enough, c=pλ is also small enough that β=θ+dd^c u-cη is Kähler. Both required smallness conditions can be imposed simultaneously. Equations (9) and (13) consequently apply to (14).

These selectors are genuinely non-descended even at the level of singularity type. Over a point of D, φ is -∞ on a proper subset of the fiber and finite on its complement. A pullback base weight, even after adding a bounded function, cannot have this behavior: an infinite base singularity is infinite on the entire fiber, while a finite base value plus a bounded function is finite everywhere on the fiber.

For a completely explicit nonempty family in every base dimension q≥1, take B=P^q, A=O(1), and D a hyperplane (a point when q=1). Use Fubini–Study for the positive comparison metric and for O(D). Then β=(1-c)ω_FS for 0<c<1, and the base mass is (1-c)^q under the normalization ∫ω_FS^q=1. The given metric h is accommodated by u, and the given ω on the product is accommodated by taking λ sufficiently small.

## What features of the fiber singularities are sufficient?
The proof isolates the following sufficient features, rather than a classification.

1. **A common horizontal logarithmic slope c.** Global domination (6) means vanishing with the base multiplier-ideal order always suffices upstairs; it excludes unrelated stronger constraints.
2. **A singularity of that slope visible over an open part of D.** The upper witness (7) detects the leading divisorial order. A singularity at merely an isolated exceptional base point would not supply this divisorial detection argument.
3. **Finite weighted transverse volume.** For the model, the transverse fiber quantity is S=Σ1/b_j. Integrating in these fiber directions changes the required order by the constant a=pS, not by another term linear in m. This bounded rebate is why the difference of section spaces lives in only finitely many divisorial layers and has O(m^{q-1}) dimension.
4. **Control of any remaining singularities.** The global lower bound suffices for the sandwich theorem. For exact equality (13), boundedness away from Z and the bounded-error local model ensure that no additional constraints remain. Bounded perturbations of these weights do not affect the conclusions.

The isotropic section-over-divisor model is the specialization p=b_1=...=b_r=1 and r=d, with Z a section over D. Its order is max(0,floor(mc-d)). The anisotropic result here permits different powers p,b_j, and also r<d; its order is max(0,floor(mc-pΣ1/b_j)). It is therefore not merely a restatement of the isotropic model. No necessity-and-sufficiency assertion is made for arbitrary fiber singularities.

If d=0, the connected-fiber submersion is a biholomorphism and no genuinely non-descended weight can exist. Descended weights, or bounded perturbations of them, give the familiar exact equality of multiplier-ideal section spaces whenever their base potential satisfies the required positivity. The genuinely non-descended family above applies whenever fiber directions are present.

Finally, additional positivity hypotheses are indispensable in some form. For example, if A is trivial with its flat metric, then θ=0. A nontrivial member of PSH(B,0) on compact connected B is constant by the maximum principle, and its current has zero top mass for q≥1. Thus arbitrary data in the question cannot always yield a positive-mass base reference, regardless of the upstairs selector. The result proved above is a complete sufficient-condition answer, not a claim of universal approximation.
