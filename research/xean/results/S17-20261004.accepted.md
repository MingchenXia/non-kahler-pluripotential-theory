# S17: local critical bounds for exact Hermitian adjoints

For every fixed compact connected Hermitian background with zero lower Monge--Ampere mass, an explicit positive-radius ball of smooth probability log-densities around log(h^n/(V_h*dV)) in C^(2,alpha), 0<alpha<1, has unique exact Gauduchon maximizing currents with uniformly bounded probability and determinant densities in Linfinity and therefore at n/(n-1). The family is infinite dimensional, contains distinct data with distinct exact maximizers, and imposes no bounds on higher derivatives. Broader continuous or large bounded families remain open.

Accepted note: w7-1/n2

The following immutable verified transitive chain supports the article.

## w7-1/n1

Quantitative local construction at every smooth positive Hermitian metric: all smooth real logarithmic densities in a positive-radius C^{2,alpha} neighborhood of F*=log(h^n/(V_h dV)) have smooth solution forms uniformly comparable to h and uniformly controlled positive Gauduchon factors. Two contractions give an explicit radius in terms of linear elliptic inverse norms; no lower-MA-mass assumption is used.

Fix 0<alpha<1, H=h^n/dV, V=integral h^n, and F*=log(H/V). Linear elliptic Fredholm theory and the maximum principle give a positive smooth chi_0 with dd^c(chi_0 h^{n-1})=0 and integral chi_0 h^n=1. The maps (v,c)->L_h v-c, with integral v dV=0 and L_h v=n dd^c v wedge h^{n-1}/h^n, and eta->dd^c(eta h^{n-1})/dV, with integral eta h^n=0, have bounded inverses K and G in the indicated Hölder spaces. Constants D,Q,C_A control dd^c, the quadratic log-determinant remainder, and the perturbation of the Gauduchon operator. Set m=min chi_0, C_0=||chi_0||_{C^{2,alpha}}, r=min{1/(2D),1/(4KQ),m/(4G C_A D C_0)}, and epsilon=r/(2K). For every smooth F with ||F-F*||_{C^{2,alpha}}<=epsilon, contraction yields a smooth u, integral u dV=0, and c, with ||u||_{C^{4,alpha}}+|c|<=r, solving (h+dd^c u)^n=exp(F+log V+c)dV. The form omega=h+dd^c u satisfies h/2<=omega<=3h/2. A second, linear contraction produces the unique normalized solution chi of dd^c(chi omega^{n-1})=0, integral chi h^n=1, with ||chi-chi_0||_{C^{2,alpha}}<=m/2. In particular m/2<=chi and chi<=3 max(chi_0)/2. All constants are finite for every smooth positive h, including any h with v_-(h)=0. No global Hermitian Monge-Ampere estimate or positive lower mass theorem is invoked.

QUANTITATIVE LOCAL SOLUTION AND GAUDUCHON CONSTRUCTION

Fix the connected compact complex manifold and smooth positive h,dV in the question. Fix 0<alpha<1. All functions and forms below are real where appropriate. Set
  H=h^n/dV,   V=integral_X h^n,   F*=log(H/V),   b*=log V.
Thus exp(F*)dV=h^n/V is a probability measure. The convention is exactly dd^c=(i/(2 pi))partial barpartial.

We prove the following local assertion, without using v_-(h). There is a quantitatively specified epsilon>0 such that every smooth F satisfying ||F-F*||_{C^{2,alpha}}<=epsilon has a smooth solution
  omega=h+dd^c u,   omega^n=exp(F+b)dV,
with integral u dV=0, b=b*+c, and uniform bounds on u,c. Moreover omega has a uniformly controlled positive factor chi with dd^c(chi omega^{n-1})=0. The constants and proof follow.

1. Linear elliptic facts and the baseline factor.

Use fixed Hölder norms from a finite smooth atlas and background connections. We use the foundational facts for real smooth scalar uniformly elliptic second-order operators on a compact manifold without boundary: the Schauder estimate
  ||v||_{C^{s+2,alpha}} <= C (||Pv||_{C^{s,alpha}}+||v||_{C^0}),
distributional elliptic regularity, and the strong maximum principle. The relevant hypotheses are checked below. Here is also the Fredholm argument needed, rather than an extra complex-geometric existence theorem. The Schauder estimate, compact embedding into C^0, and local elliptic regularity give finite-dimensional kernel, closed range, and finite-dimensional cokernel represented by smooth solutions of the formal adjoint. A scalar operator with positive-definite principal symbol has index zero: deform its principal coefficients through positive-definite symbols to those of a real Laplacian and its lower-order coefficients to those of that Laplacian. The operators remain elliptic Fredholm throughout, their index is constant, and the Laplacian has index zero. The usual local Schauder input itself is obtained by the constant-coefficient Hölder estimate, freezing principal coefficients on sufficiently small coordinate balls, absorbing the coefficient error, and using a partition of unity. These are foundational real elliptic facts; no global complex Monge-Ampere regularity or mass result is needed here.

Define
  L_h v = n (dd^c v wedge h^{n-1})/h^n.
Its principal symbol is positive definite since h is positive, its coefficients are smooth, and it has no zeroth-order term. Connectedness and the strong maximum principle imply that its kernel consists precisely of constants. Its index is zero, so its cokernel is one dimensional. Let p be a nonzero real smooth generator of the kernel of its formal adjoint relative to dV.

The function p has one sign. Indeed, the range of L_h contains no nonzero nonnegative smooth function: L_h v>=0 on a compact manifold implies v is constant by the strong maximum principle. If p took both signs, one could choose nonnegative smooth functions supported where p is positive and negative, and a nonzero nonnegative linear combination g with integral g p dV=0. The one-dimensional Fredholm compatibility condition would give g=L_h v, a contradiction. Choose the sign so p>=0. It is actually strictly positive. In any real coordinate patch the adjoint has the form a^{ij}partial_ij+b^i partial_i+c_0 with positive-definite a. Subtract a constant C>=max{0,sup c_0} from its zeroth-order coefficient. The resulting operator P has nonpositive zeroth-order coefficient and Pp=-Cp<=0. If p vanished at an interior point, the strong minimum principle would make p identically zero; connectedness contradicts its choice. Normalize integral p dV=1.

Define chi_0=p/H. Integration by parts gives
  integral L_h v p dV = n integral v dd^c(chi_0 h^{n-1}),
so
  dd^c(chi_0 h^{n-1})=0,   integral chi_0 h^n=1.
In particular chi_0 is smooth and strictly positive. This reconstructs the baseline Gauduchon factor, rather than requiring a specialized external Gauduchon existence citation. The probability mu_0=chi_0 h^n is the compatibility measure for L_h.

Let E_4={v in C^{4,alpha}: integral v dV=0}. The map
  A:E_4 x R -> C^{2,alpha},   A(v,c)=L_h v-c,
is an isomorphism. For a prescribed g, necessarily c=-integral g dmu_0; then L_h v=g-integral g dmu_0 is solvable by the Fredholm compatibility condition, and its constant is uniquely fixed by the dV-mean-zero normalization. Schauder estimates and the bounded inverse theorem give a finite inverse norm K. Take K>=1 and the product norm ||v||_{C^{4,alpha}}+|c|.

For a positive smooth (1,1)-form omega define
  A_omega eta = dd^c(eta omega^{n-1})/dV.
At omega=h its formal adjoint relative to dV is
  eta -> (dd^c eta wedge h^{n-1})/dV = (H/n)L_h eta.
This has kernel constants and index zero. Therefore A_h has one-dimensional kernel, spanned by chi_0, and range
  Z_0={g in C^{0,alpha}: integral g dV=0}.
Its restriction to
  E_chi={eta in C^{2,alpha}: integral eta h^n=0}
is an isomorphism onto Z_0. Let R be its inverse and G>=1 a bound for its operator norm. All the operators here have smooth coefficients and uniformly elliptic principal symbols, so all the elliptic hypotheses just used hold.

2. Quantitative nonlinear constants.

Choose the form and endomorphism Hölder norms so endomorphism composition is submultiplicative and their norms dominate pointwise operator norms. Scaling the usual norms by their multiplication constants achieves this. Let D>=1 satisfy, for every v,
  ||dd^c v||_{C^{2,alpha}} <= D ||v||_{C^{4,alpha}},
  ||h^{-1}dd^c v||_{C^{2,alpha},alg} <= D ||v||_{C^{4,alpha}}.
Let C_tr>=1 bound the trace map in these norms and set Q=2 C_tr D^2. For ||v||_{C^{4,alpha}}<=1/(2D), define
  N(v)=log det(I+h^{-1}dd^c v)-L_h v.
The convergent Banach-algebra power series
  N(v)=sum_{j>=2} (-1)^{j+1} tr((h^{-1}dd^c v)^j)/j
shows
  ||N(v)||_{C^{2,alpha}} <= Q ||v||_{C^{4,alpha}}^2,
and, for v,w in this ball,
  ||N(v)-N(w)||_{C^{2,alpha}}
    <= Q (||v||_{C^{4,alpha}}+||w||_{C^{4,alpha}})
           ||v-w||_{C^{4,alpha}}.
For example, the difference estimate follows by telescoping each matrix power and summing sum_{j>=2}rho^{j-1}=rho/(1-rho), with rho<=1/2. These constants depend only on the fixed norms and h.

Choose C_A>=1 so that whenever ||omega-h||_{C^{2,alpha}}<=1,
  ||A_omega-A_h||_{C^{2,alpha}->C^{0,alpha}}
       <= C_A ||omega-h||_{C^{2,alpha}}.
This is a direct polynomial estimate, not an unproved regularity assumption: expand omega^{n-1}-h^{n-1}, multiply by eta, and apply dd^c. The Hölder product estimates use at most two derivatives of omega. A finite C_A is determined by n,h,dV and the fixed norms.

Set
  m=min_X chi_0>0,   C_0=||chi_0||_{C^{2,alpha}},
  r=min{1/(2D), 1/(4KQ), m/(4G C_A D C_0)},
  epsilon=r/(2K).
The norms dominate supremum norms, so m<=C_0. These formulas specify positive constants solely from the fixed background. In particular they contain no v_-(h) term or inverse lower-mass bound.

3. First contraction: the solution form.

Put f=F-F*. On the closed radius-r ball in E_4 x R consider
  Psi_f(v,c)=A^{-1}(f-N(v)).
The norm estimates above show
  ||Psi_f(v,c)|| <= K epsilon+KQ r^2 <= 3r/4,
and its Lipschitz constant is at most 2KQr<=1/2. The contraction theorem yields (u,c) with
  ||u||_{C^{4,alpha}}+|c|<=r,
  log(omega^n/h^n)-c=f,   omega=h+dd^c u.
Consequently omega^n=exp(F+b*+c)dV. Since the pointwise operator norm of h^{-1}dd^c u is at most Dr<=1/2,
  (1/2)h <= omega <= (3/2)h.
For smooth F this solution is smooth. To verify the bootstrap, differentiate the local log-determinant equation: its linearization has principal coefficients omega^{-1}, uniformly positive, and the initial coefficients are C^{2,alpha}. Schauder estimates on differentiated equations improve regularity of u; iteration with smooth F,h gives all derivatives. This uses only the same local linear elliptic theory. No estimates uniform in higher derivatives of F are asserted or needed.

4. Second contraction: the Gauduchon factor.

Let delta=||A_omega-A_h||<=C_A D r. Since each A_omega has integral zero, the right side below lies in Z_0. Write chi=chi_0+eta with eta in E_chi and solve
  eta=-R(A_omega-A_h)(chi_0+eta).
The affine map is a contraction on E_chi, since
  G delta <= m/(4C_0)<=1/4.
It therefore has exactly one solution, and
  ||eta||_{C^{2,alpha}}
    <= G delta C_0/(1-G delta)
    <=2G C_A D r C_0 <=m/2.
Hence
  dd^c(chi omega^{n-1})=0,   integral chi h^n=1,
  chi>=m/2,   chi<=chi_0+m/2 <=(3/2)max_X chi_0.
The factor is smooth by linear elliptic bootstrapping because omega is smooth. Moreover it is the unique C^{2,alpha} solution with integral chi h^n=1: the same global affine contraction equation holds for any such solution.

This finishes the local construction. Every prerequisite has been obtained from smooth positivity, compactness, connectedness, and foundational linear elliptic theory. In particular the construction is equally valid on the given h when v_-(h)=0.

## w7-1/n2

Complete restricted endpoint theorem at zero lower mass: all smooth probability log-densities in the explicit small C^{2,alpha} ball from n1 have uniformly L-infinity bounded exact-maximizer densities, both k_T and dnu/dV. AM-GM plus Stokes proves exact maximality and uniqueness. The normalized family is infinite-dimensional and contains an explicit path of distinct data and distinct exact maximizers on the same zero-mass h. No conclusion for arbitrary C^0-bounded data, all tests, near-maximizers, or P16 supports follows.

For the h assumed to have v_-(h)=0, fix alpha and the explicit epsilon of n1, and take F_epsilon={smooth real F: integral exp(F)dV=1, ||F-log(h^n/(V_h dV))||_{C^{2,alpha}}<=epsilon}. All data are smooth; boundedness is in the specified C^{2,alpha} norm, with no bounds on higher derivatives. Write omega=h+dd^c u and chi as constructed in n1, Z=integral chi h wedge omega^{n-1}=integral chi omega^n, and T_F=(chi/Z)omega^{n-1}. For every test S, the pointwise matrix AM-GM inequality gives Gamma_F(S)<=exp(-b/n)omega wedge S and hence D_F(S)<=exp(-b/n). Equality holds for T_F. Any exact maximizer satisfies equality almost everywhere, so is rho omega^{n-1}; elliptic regularity and the normalized-factor uniqueness prove it equals T_F. Thus nu=(chi/Z)omega^n, q=dnu/dV=(chi/Z)(omega^n/dV), and k_T=(chi/Z)(omega^n/dV)^((n-1)/n). If a=max chi_0 and Hmax=max h^n/dV, then ||q||_infinity<=3^(n+1)a Hmax/4 and ||k_T||_infinity<=3^n a Hmax^((n-1)/n)/2. In particular p=n/(n-1) gives ||q||_p<=Cnu^(1/n) and ||k_T||_p<=Ck. Nonvacuity at the given zero-mass h is explicit: use a global smooth bump potential whose Hessian at a point is non-scalar, set omega_t=h+t dd^c psi and F_t=log(omega_t^n/((integral omega_t^n)dV)). For small t these are normalized data in the family, pairwise distinct even modulo constants, and have distinct exact maximizing currents. The sufficient condition is local strong-topology smallness; it does not imply positive global lower mass or a uniform estimate for general bounded continuous data.

A RESTRICTED UNIFORM CRITICAL ESTIMATE FOR EXACT MAXIMIZERS AT ZERO LOWER MASS

Theorem. Let X,h,dV be exactly as in the question, including v_-(h)=0 and n>=3. Fix 0<alpha<1. Put V=integral_X h^n, H=h^n/dV, and F*=log(H/V). Use the explicit epsilon>0 constructed in n1. Specify the density family
  F_epsilon={F in C^infinity(X,R): integral_X exp(F)dV=1,
                         ||F-F*||_{C^{2,alpha}}<=epsilon}.
The topology and quantitative bound are those of the fixed C^{2,alpha} norm in n1. All members are smooth; there is no uniform bound on derivatives of order greater than two. In particular this is also an L-infinity-bounded family of logarithmic densities, with uniformly positive upper and lower bounds on exp(F). Its normalized nature rules out nonzero constant shifts within the family.

For every F in this family every exact maximizing current has the same determinant density k_T and probability density q_T=dnu_(T,F)/dV, and
  ||q_T||_{L^infinity(dV)} <= C_nu,
  ||k_T||_{L^infinity(dV)} <= C_k,
where, writing a=max_X chi_0 and H_max=max_X H for the baseline factor of n1,
  C_nu=(3^{n+1}/4) a H_max,
  C_k=(3^n/2) a H_max^{(n-1)/n}.
Consequently, for the requested critical exponent p=n/(n-1),
  ||q_T||_{L^p(dV)} <= C_nu^{1/n},
  ||k_T||_{L^p(dV)} <= C_k.
These are uniform exact-maximizer estimates over a genuinely nontrivial infinite-dimensional specified family at the given zero-lower-mass h. The same bounds also hold if the probability-normalization condition on F is dropped and the entire small smooth C^{2,alpha} ball is used.

Proof.

1. Construction and normalization.

Apply n1 to F. It gives smooth u and b=log V+c with
  omega=h+dd^c u,   omega^n=exp(F+b)dV,
  (1/2)h<=omega<=(3/2)h,
and a smooth positive chi satisfying
  dd^c(chi omega^{n-1})=0,   integral chi h^n=1,
  chi<=3a/2.
Define
  Z=integral_X chi h wedge omega^{n-1},
  T_F=(chi/Z)omega^{n-1}.
It is a strictly positive smooth member of G_h. Since omega-h=dd^c u, integration by parts gives
  integral chi(omega-h) wedge omega^{n-1}
    =integral u dd^c(chi omega^{n-1})=0,
so
  Z=integral chi omega^n.
Pointwise positivity and comparison of omega with h give
  2^{-(n-1)}<=Z<=(3/2)^{n-1}.
These comparisons follow, for example, by diagonalizing omega relative to h: h wedge omega^{n-1}/h^n is the average of the products of n-1 eigenvalues.

2. Exact maximality, not merely near maximality.

Let S be any current in G_h. Since u is smooth and dd^c S=0,
  integral omega wedge S=integral h wedge S=1.
In the h-unitary frame let W be the positive Hermitian matrix of omega and let P=P_S be the matrix density supplied in the question. Then
  exp(F)dV/h^n=exp(-b)det W.
For positive semidefinite P and positive definite W, matrix AM-GM gives
  det(W)^{1/n}det(P)^{1/n}
    =det(W^{1/2}PW^{1/2})^{1/n}
    <=tr(WP)/n.
It follows as an inequality of measures that
  Gamma_F(S)<=exp(-b/n)omega wedge S,
and hence D_F(S)<=exp(-b/n). This argument applies equally to singular test currents.

For T_F equality holds everywhere. Indeed, for a current of the form t omega^{n-1} with smooth t>0, the trace-normalized matrix in the question is
  P=n W^{-1}/tr(W^{-1}).
Thus W^{1/2}PW^{1/2} is a scalar matrix. Consequently
  Gamma_F(T_F)=exp(-b/n)omega wedge T_F,
  D_F(T_F)=exp(-b/n).
Therefore T_F is an actual exact maximizer and M(F)=exp(-b/n). The supplied identity M(F)=exp(-B_0(F)/n) also identifies b=B_0(F). No global PDE existence theorem is needed for this identification: exact maximality was just proved directly.

For completeness, all exact maximizers equal T_F, independently of the smooth uniqueness fact supplied in the question. If S has D_F(S)=M(F), the nonnegative AM-GM deficit measure has integral zero and is zero. Therefore, sigma_S-almost everywhere,
  W^{1/2}P_S W^{1/2}=lambda I.
Since tr P_S=n, lambda=n/tr(W^{-1})>0. This is exactly the pointwise ray of omega^{n-1}, so globally S=rho omega^{n-1} for a nonnegative scalar distribution rho (a positive measure coefficient). The equation dd^c S=0 is the distributional elliptic equation A_omega rho=0, where A_omega is the operator of n1. Smooth-coefficient elliptic regularity makes rho smooth. Divide rho by integral rho h^n, which is positive and finite. The resulting factor has integral one against h^n and solves A_omega=0; the second contraction in n1 proves that it is chi. The normalization integral h wedge S=1 now forces S=(chi/Z)omega^{n-1}=T_F. This uniqueness comes from equality and ellipticity, not from the endpoint estimate.

3. Which densities are estimated.

The exact equality of measures in step 2 gives
  nu_(T_F,F)=Gamma_F(T_F)/M(F)=omega wedge T_F
            =(chi/Z)omega^n.
Thus the estimated adjoint probability density is
  q_F=dnu_(T_F,F)/dV=(chi/Z)(omega^n/dV),
and its integral is one because Z=integral chi omega^n.

Writing
  gamma=(chi/Z)^{1/(n-1)}omega,
we have T_F=gamma^{n-1}, with the required h-trace normalization. The determinant density appearing in the question is therefore
  k_{T_F}=(gamma^n/dV)^{(n-1)/n}
         =(chi/Z)(omega^n/dV)^{(n-1)/n}.
Equivalently, using omega^n/dV=exp(F+b) and M(F)=exp(-b/n),
  k_{T_F}=exp(-F/n)M(F)q_F,
which agrees exactly with the definition valid at actual maximizers. There is no conflation of k_T with the probability density q_F.

4. Uniform bounds and critical exponent.

The bounds from step 1 imply
  chi/Z <=(3a/2)2^{n-1},
  omega^n/dV <=(3/2)^n H_max.
Multiplication yields
  q_F <= (3a/2)2^{n-1}(3/2)^n H_max
        =(3^{n+1}/4)a H_max=C_nu,
and
  k_{T_F} <=(3a/2)2^{n-1}(3/2)^{n-1}H_max^{(n-1)/n}
            =(3^n/2)a H_max^{(n-1)/n}=C_k.
Since dV is a probability volume, ||k_{T_F}||_p<=C_k. Since q_F is a probability density, integral q_F^p dV<=C_nu^{p-1}; with p=n/(n-1), this gives ||q_F||_p<=C_nu^{1/n}. The bounds are actually stronger than the requested critical estimate.

5. Nonvacuity on the same h with v_-(h)=0.

The radius epsilon is strictly positive for every smooth positive h by n1. It is not a hypothesis that might secretly imply positive lower Monge-Ampere mass. No part of its construction used v_-(h)>0. Thus the family exists on every h supplied by the zero-mass premise itself.

Here is an explicit varying family inside it. Fix a point p and h-unitary holomorphic coordinates at p. Choose a smooth global real bump potential psi, adjusted to have dV-mean zero, whose complex Hessian at p is a nonzero non-scalar matrix Q. A cutoff equal to one near p times |z_1|^2-|z_2|^2 supplies such a psi. For small real t set
  omega_t=h+t dd^c psi,   V_t=integral omega_t^n,
  F_t=log(omega_t^n/(V_t dV)).
For sufficiently small t these are smooth positive forms and smooth probability log-densities, and F_t tends to F* in C^{2,alpha}. Hence an interval of these data lies in F_epsilon. Also u=t psi and b=log V_t solve the equation; taking the interval smaller makes them lie in the local contraction neighborhood of n1.

The data are pairwise distinct, not merely constant shifts. If F_s=F_t, then log(omega_s^n/omega_t^n)=log(V_s/V_t) is constant. At a maximum and a minimum of (s-t)psi, positivity and the sign of dd^c((s-t)psi) show that this constant is respectively <=0 and >=0, so it is zero. The mean-value formula for log determinants then gives
  integral_0^1 tr_{omega_t+tau(omega_s-omega_t)}
                  dd^c((s-t)psi) d tau=0.
This is a uniformly elliptic scalar equation with no zeroth-order term on the compact connected manifold. The maximum principle forces (s-t)psi constant, so s=t. Nonzero constant shifts between probability-normalized F_s,F_t are also impossible.

The exact maximizing currents in this path are themselves distinct. If T_s=T_t, their positive smooth factors show that omega_s^{n-1} and omega_t^{n-1} are on the same ray at p. The cofactor matrix identity implies that positive (1,1)-forms whose (n-1)st powers are proportional are themselves proportional. But at p the matrices are I+sQ and I+tQ; because Q is non-scalar, proportionality forces s=t. Thus this is not even a family of different data with one fixed exact current.

The entire specified normalized family is infinite-dimensional, not just this path: any sufficiently small smooth perturbation f can be normalized explicitly by
  F=F*+f-log integral exp(F*+f)dV.
The derivative of this normalization at zero is f minus its mean against exp(F*)dV, and its only constant-direction kernel is the constants. Arbitrarily many independent smooth nonconstant directions remain inside the epsilon neighborhood.

6. Scope and remaining boundary.

The proved sufficient density condition is smallness in a specified strong C^{2,alpha} topology around the explicit reference probability density h^n/V. All data in the stated family are smooth, although their higher derivatives need not be uniformly bounded. The result applies under the assumed zero lower mass because it is local in solved data and imposes no global lower mass bound on all bounded h-plurisubharmonic potentials. It does not assert an endpoint bound for arbitrary L-infinity- or C^0-bounded continuous logarithmic densities, a large C^{2,alpha} family, all normalized Gauduchon tests, or near-maximizing sequences. The full broader exact-maximizer problem in those weaker families is left unresolved. Nor is any general P16 singleton-support conclusion inferred from the endpoint estimate: the particular smooth exact-current uniqueness used here was proved separately by AM-GM equality and elliptic regularity.

This proves the sufficient-condition alternative in the task with a quantitative, genuinely nontrivial family on the given zero-lower-mass background.
