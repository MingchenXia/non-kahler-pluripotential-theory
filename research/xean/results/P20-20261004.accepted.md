## w7-1/n1

This note fixes an explicitly selected series and proves its global selection constraint and its target envelope. No claim about multiplier-ideal-selected spaces is made.

SETUP. Fix n>=1 and integers k_j>=1. Let X=(P^1)^n, L=boxtimes_j O(k_j), and let h be the product Fubini--Study metric with potential
  phi_0(r)=sum_j k_j log(1+exp r_j),   r_j=log|z_j|^2,
in the standard frame e on the affine chart C^n. Its Chern curvature theta=dd^c phi_0 is closed. Let T=(C*)^n. Set P=product_j [0,k_j]. Fix a nonempty rational polytope Q compactly contained in int P. Write d=dim Q and assume aff Q=b+W, where b is in Z^n and W is a rational vector subspace. Set Lambda=W intersect Z^n; its rank is d. (When d=0 this requires Q={b} with integral b.) Write H_Q(r)=max_{q in Q} q.r.

Let w be a smooth real torus-invariant function on X. Its restriction is a function of r. Assume
  F(r):=phi_0(r)+w(r),   D^2 F(r)>0 for every r in R^n.
These hypotheses are nonempty: w=0 works. The ambient Hermitian form on X need not be theta or closed.

GLOBAL SPACES AND THE ENTIRE CONSTRAINT. Define
  V_m=span{ z^alpha e^m : alpha in mQ intersect Z^n }.
Each displayed section extends holomorphically across every chart: 0<=alpha_j<=m k_j. Conversely all global sections of L^m are polynomials in the z_j of degree at most m k_j in the jth variable, as is seen by changing to z_j^{-1} at infinity. Thus the displayed monomials are actual global sections with precisely the asserted selection, not germs requiring globalization.

More intrinsically within the chosen toric frame, V_m consists exactly of the sections s for which there is a finite constant C_s with
  |s/e^m|^2 <= C_s exp(m H_Q(r)) on T.                 (1)
Indeed, write s/e^m=sum_alpha c_alpha z^alpha. For support in mQ, the triangle inequality proves (1) with C_s=(sum |c_alpha|)^2. Conversely angular averaging of (1), using Haar probability d tau on (S^1)^n, gives
  sum_alpha |c_alpha|^2 exp(alpha.r) <= C_s exp(m H_Q(r)).
If a nonzero coefficient had alpha not in mQ, finite-dimensional separation would provide a vector a with alpha.a>m H_Q(a). Setting r=t a and letting t increase contradicts this inequality. This proves the exact equivalence. Also V_m V_l is contained in V_{m+l}, by convexity of Q.

THE PRECISE ENVELOPE FOR THIS SERIES. Define C_Q to be the class of genuine global theta-psh functions v, not identically minus infinity, such that for some finite C_v
  v(z)+phi_0(r) <= H_Q(r)+C_v on T.
This is a logarithmic Newton-body growth constraint; it is not a condition defined by the desired kernel asymptotics. Define
  E_Q=(sup{v in C_Q : v<=w quasi-everywhere on X})^*.
The selected series in (1) has this constraint. In particular we are not substituting the multiplier-ideal envelope from the question.

CONVEX PREPARATION. Since w is bounded on X, F-phi_0 is bounded on R^n. For any p in int P,
  F(r)-p.r >= delta_p sum_j |r_j|-||w||_infinity,
where delta_p=min_j(p_j,k_j-p_j)>0. Thus this function has a unique minimizer, by coercivity and strict convexity. It satisfies grad F(r)=p. The image of grad F is contained in P: a supporting plane to F and the asymptotic slopes of phi_0 imply p.a<=H_P(a) for every a. The inverse-function theorem and positive Hessian make this image open, so it lies in int P. Hence grad F:R^n->int P is a bijection with smooth inverse r(p). Define
  F*(p)=p.r(p)-F(r(p)),   p in int P.
It is smooth, grad F*=r(p), and D^2 F*=(D^2 F(r(p)))^{-1}>0. In particular F* and its first two derivatives are bounded on a neighborhood of Q, and r(Q) is compact.

EXPLICIT ANSWER. Put
  G_Q(r)=max_{q in Q} {q.r-F*(q)}.                    (2)
Then
  E_Q=G_Q-phi_0 on T,                                (3)
and its canonical extension is minus infinity on X\T.

First construct the candidate globally. On a chart where some coordinates are inverted, the local potential G_Q minus the frame-change linear term is
  max_{q in Q} [sum_j c_j(q) log|zeta_j|^2-F*(q)],
where c_j(q)=q_j for a noninverted coordinate and c_j(q)=k_j-q_j for an inverted coordinate. Compact containment Q in int P gives a uniform delta>0 with all c_j(q)>=delta. Each summand is a genuine psh function on the chart. The family is uniformly bounded above on compact subsets. Its supremum is continuous off the coordinate hyperplanes, and tends uniformly to minus infinity on any such hyperplane while the other chart coordinates remain bounded. Thus it is upper semicontinuous everywhere. It is psh directly by the submean inequality: use a maximizing q at a finite-valued center, apply the submean inequality to that summand, and bound its average by the average of the supremum; at a minus-infinity center the inequality is automatic. These chart potentials glue with the line-bundle frame changes. Consequently (3) is a genuine global theta-psh function with its canonical representative, finite on T and minus infinity on the boundary. Because F* is bounded on Q, G_Q-H_Q is bounded. Fenchel's inequality gives G_Q<=F. Hence this function belongs to the defining envelope class and is <=w everywhere.

Now let v be any competitor. Its quasi-everywhere obstacle is an everywhere obstacle. A pluripolar set is Lebesgue-null locally (it lies in the minus-infinity set of a nontrivial locally integrable psh function). Thus v+phi_h<=w+phi_h almost everywhere in each frame. The psh submean inequality on shrinking balls, followed by continuity of w+phi_h, gives the same inequality at every point. Genuine canonical representatives are essential here.

On T take the circular maximum
  M(r)=max_{tau in (S^1)^n} [v(z(r,tau))+phi_0(r)].
It is upper semicontinuous by compactness of the torus and upper semicontinuity of v. It is psh: rotate a point attaining the maximum and apply its submean inequality; the rotated integrand is bounded by the circular maximum. It is torus-invariant, hence a convex function of r. To see this without a toric representation theorem, pull back by the exponential map to logarithmic complex coordinates. A psh function depending only on the real coordinates has positive-semidefinite distributional real Hessian, by restriction to complex lines (or local convolution). Thus its real restriction is convex. It is not identically minus infinity and is finite everywhere: a convex function locally bounded above on all of R^n cannot have a minus-infinity value at an interior point without being identically minus infinity.

We have M<=F and M<=H_Q+C_v. Every supporting slope p of M lies in Q. Otherwise separation provides a with p.a>H_Q(a), and the supporting affine function for M at a fixed point contradicts M(t a)<=t H_Q(a)+C_v as t tends to infinity. A finite convex function on R^n has a supporting slope at every point, by separation of its epigraph. At a point r take such a slope p. Then
  M(r)=p.r-M*(p),   M*(p)=sup_x(p.x-M(x))>=F*(p),
and p is in Q. Therefore M(r)<=G_Q(r), and v<=G_Q-phi_0 on T. On the toric boundary v<=G_Q-phi_0 follows by the canonical psh submean extension, or by approaching through full-measure points in a chart where the candidate potential tends uniformly to minus infinity. More explicitly, v+phi_h is bounded above by that candidate potential almost everywhere on each chart, so submean passes this upper bound to every point. Thus the pointwise supremum and its upper regularization both equal the already upper-semicontinuous candidate. This proves (2)--(3).

This argument uses no Bergman or equilibrium asymptotic theorem. The elementary separation, submean, chart, and strict-convexity arguments above supply the required envelope identification even when d<n.

## w7-1/n2

Use the spaces, metric, weight, convex function, envelope, and lattice of n1. Let nu be any smooth strictly positive torus-invariant Borel probability on X. It has no mass on the toric boundary. On T write
  dnu=a(r) dr d tau,
where d tau is Haar probability, a is smooth and positive on R^n, and integral a(r)dr=1. No uniform lower bound for a at infinity is required. The norm is the one in the question, with K=X.

EXACT HILBERT TRANSFORMATIONS. For alpha in mQ intersect Z^n set sigma_alpha=z^alpha e^m. Its pointwise squared norm is
  |sigma_alpha|_{h^m}^2=exp(alpha.r-m phi_0(r)).
Consequently
  A_m(alpha):=||sigma_alpha||_m^2
    =integral_{R^n} exp(alpha.r-mF(r)) a(r) dr.       (4)
Different exponents are orthogonal, because angular integration of exp(i(alpha-beta).theta) is zero. All A_m are finite and positive. Thus the exact orthonormal basis is sigma_alpha/sqrt(A_m(alpha)), and the norm of sum c_alpha sigma_alpha is sum |c_alpha|^2 A_m(alpha). This proves positive definiteness on the entire selected space. Equivalently the original weighted norm is the unweighted norm for the smooth metric h exp(-w), whose local potential is F; its curvature is still a closed dd^c potential. This identity is only a metric transformation and makes no identification with the ambient Hermitian form.

In particular, on T,
  B_m=sum_{alpha in mQ intersect Z^n}
       exp(alpha.r-m phi_0(r))/A_m(alpha),            (5)
  exp(-mw)B_m=sum_alpha exp(alpha.r-mF(r))/A_m(alpha).
These formulas account for every norm and normalization factor.

UNIFORM ELEMENTARY LAPLACE BOUNDS. Write q=alpha/m and r(q)=(grad F)^{-1}(q). Then
  f_q(r):=F(r)-q.r
has unique minimum -F*(q) at r(q). The points r(q) lie in a compact set. On a fixed compact neighborhood of this set the density a is bounded below by a positive constant, and the Hessian of F is bounded above. Integrate (4) over a ball of radius m^{-1/2} around r(q). Taylor's formula yields, uniformly in q in Q,
  A_m(alpha)>=c m^{-n/2} exp(mF*(q)).
Since f_q(r)>=-F*(q) everywhere and integral a=1, the upper estimate is
  A_m(alpha)<=exp(mF*(q)).
Hence, for all sufficiently large m and all selected alpha,
  c m^{-n/2} exp(mF*(q)) <= A_m(alpha)
       <= exp(mF*(q)).                               (6)
This proves uniform logarithmic norm asymptotics directly; no stationary-phase, peak-section, or external kernel theorem is invoked.

LATTICE GROWTH AND GRID APPROXIMATION. Since aff Q=b+W with integral b,
  (m aff Q) intersect Z^n=m b+Lambda.
Normalize Lebesgue volume dq_Lambda on b+W so a fundamental cell of Lambda has volume 1. For d>=1, fundamental-cell comparison gives
  N_m:=dim V_m=#(mQ intersect Z^n)
       =m^d vol_Lambda(Q)+O(m^{d-1}).                 (7)
Here is a direct justification. After translating by m b, count Lambda cells anchored in m(Q-b). Cells fully inside or outside are counted exactly up to their unit volume. The discrepancy is confined to a bounded-width neighborhood of the finitely many facets of m(Q-b); its volume and cell count are O(m^{d-1}). This also proves lattice Riemann-sum convergence for continuous functions on Q. For d=0, Q={b}, so N_m=1 exactly. Therefore the intrinsic section-growth dimension, computed from the actual spaces, is
  lim_{m->infinity} log N_m/log m=d
(with dimension zero interpreted by the constant sequence). The leading volume in (7) is positive.

Moreover Q_m=Q intersect (b+m^{-1}Lambda) is within Hausdorff distance C/m of Q for large m. Choose a relative interior point q_0 with a positive inscribed radius, move any q a distance O(1/m) toward q_0, and round the moved point to the nearest lattice-grid point. A fixed lattice covering radius ensures the rounded point remains in Q. This construction works for all sufficiently large m, not only a divisible subsequence. Thus there are globally selected sections for every slope needed in the lower bound. The d=0 case is immediate.

MATCHING ENVELOPE LOWER BOUND AND TOPOLOGIES. Let
  G_{Q,m}(r)=max_{q in Q_m} {q.r-F*(q)}.
The Lipschitz bound for F* on Q and the grid approximation give
  0<=G_Q(r)-G_{Q,m}(r)<=C(1+sum |r_j|)/m.            (8)
Equations (5)--(6) imply
  G_{Q,m}-phi_0 <= U_m
      <=G_{Q,m}-phi_0+[(n/2)log m+log N_m-log c]/m. (9)
The lower inequality already uses one explicit global selected monomial approximating a maximizing q. Hence it is a genuine envelope lower bound respecting the entire constraint, rather than a local section construction. By n1, E_Q=G_Q-phi_0. Combining (7)--(9),
  |U_m-E_Q|<=C(1+sum |r_j|+log m)/m on T.           (10)
It follows that U_m converges locally uniformly on T, and pointwise there, to E_Q. On the toric boundary all selected monomials vanish for sufficiently large m, because Q has a positive distance from every face of P; thus U_m and E_Q both have canonical value minus infinity there. The extended pointwise liminf lower bound holds on all X. For every smooth positive volume and every 1<=p<infinity, the functions sum |log|z_j|^2| have finite L^p norm on X. Equation (10), ignoring the measure-zero boundary, therefore gives U_m->E_Q in L^p(X). In particular this gives L^1 convergence with respect to the volume of any ambient smooth Hermitian form. No global uniform convergence across logarithmic poles is asserted.

DERIVED BERNSTEIN--MARKOV CONDITION. For q in Q, Fenchel's inequality gives q.r-F(r)<=F*(q). Equations (5)--(6) therefore show
  sup_X exp(-mw)B_m <= C N_m m^{n/2}.
At the boundary the summands vanish for large m. Cauchy--Schwarz in the exact orthonormal basis gives
  sup_X |s|_{h^m} exp(-mw/2)
     <=C m^{d/2+n/4} ||s||_m,   s in V_m.
The finitely many small m are absorbed by changing C (zero spaces cause no issue). A polynomial is bounded by C_epsilon exp(epsilon m), so the weighted Bernstein--Markov condition in the question holds for every epsilon>0. It is proved, not postulated.

KERNEL-MEASURE LIMIT. Define the probability on X associated with one monomial by
  dP_{m,q}=exp(m(q.r-F(r))) a(r)dr d tau/A_m(alpha),
where q=alpha/m. The angular factor is exactly Haar. Its radii concentrate uniformly in q in Q at r(q). To prove this, for fixed eta>0 there is c_eta>0 such that
  f_q(r)+F*(q)>=c_eta whenever |r-r(q)|>=eta,
uniformly in q. On a large compact set this follows from uniqueness and continuity of the minima. Outside that set, use the uniform coercive bound
  F(r)-q.r>=delta sum |r_j|-||w||_infinity,
with delta>0 uniform for q in Q, and boundedness of F*. The numerator outside the eta-ball is at most exp(mF*(q)-m c_eta), while (6) gives a lower denominator c m^{-n/2} exp(mF*(q)). Its probability tends to zero uniformly.

For a continuous function f on compact X define
  f_bar(r)=integral_{(S^1)^n} f(exp(r/2)tau)d tau.
Uniform continuity near the compact set r(Q), the preceding concentration, and boundedness of f prove
  integral f dP_{m,q}->f_bar(r(q))
uniformly for the selected q. From the exact Hilbert identity,
  mu_m:=d!m^{-d} exp(-mw)B_m nu
       =d!m^{-d} sum_{alpha in mQ intersect Z^n} P_{m,alpha/m}.
Lattice Riemann sums from (7) now yield weak-* convergence of finite Borel measures on X:
  mu_m -> mu_Q :=Psi_*(d! dq_Lambda d tau),          (11)
  Psi(q,tau)=exp(r(q)/2)tau,  q in Q.
For d=0 the integral over Q is the single point, with zero-dimensional volume 1. All test functions in (11) are continuous on X. The exact mass and its limit are
  mu_m(X)=d!m^{-d}N_m -> d!vol_Lambda(Q)>0.           (12)
Thus the intrinsic normalization is essential. If d<n, replacing it by n!m^{-n} would give the zero measure. In that case (11) is supported on a compact moment shell of real dimension n+d, and is singular with respect to every smooth volume on X.

The integral-offset assumption is significant: a rational affine hull without an integral offset can have empty selected spaces in infinitely many degrees. For example a half-integral singleton has no sections in odd degrees. Our hypotheses exclude this congruence obstruction. Polytope faces touching the boundary of P and weights failing strict convexity are not covered by the argument.

## w7-1/n3

COMPLETE SCOPED SUFFICIENT-CONDITION ANSWER.

The following theorem is a condition-finding solution, not a universal theorem for multiplier ideals or selecting series. Its primary nontrivial cases have n>=2 and 1<=d<n. They are sparse globally constrained section series with transverse radial concentration, not merely a restatement of a positive-mass big-line-bundle theorem or a descended submersion limit.

THEOREM. Assume all of the following explicit conditions.
(1) X=(P^1)^n; L=boxtimes O(k_j), k_j positive integers; h is the product Fubini--Study metric. In the dense torus T=(C*)^n write r_j=log|z_j|^2 and phi_0=sum k_j log(1+exp r_j). Thus theta=c_1(L,h)=dd^c phi_0 is closed. The ambient Hermitian form omega may be nonclosed.
(2) K=X. The obstacle w is smooth and torus-invariant, and F=phi_0+w has positive-definite real Hessian at every r in R^n.
(3) nu is a smooth strictly positive torus-invariant probability on X. In particular one may take nu=omega^n/integral_X omega^n when omega is a torus-invariant Hermitian form, whether or not omega is closed. For a non-invariant omega, an independent invariant nu is allowed, but its volume is not asserted to satisfy (3).
(4) Q is a nonempty rational polytope compactly contained in the interior of P=product [0,k_j]. Its affine hull is b+W with b in Z^n and W rational. The specified selection is exactly
  V_m=span{z^alpha e^m : alpha in mQ intersect Z^n}.
Set Lambda=W intersect Z^n and normalize relative volume dq_Lambda by covolume 1. Put d=dim Q.

For this series the precise target is
  E_Q=(sup{v genuine global theta-psh, v+phi_0<=H_Q+C_v on T
                 for some finite C_v, and v<=w q.e. on X})^*,
where H_Q(r)=max_{q in Q} q.r. It is not the ideal envelope unless some separate argument proves such an identification. Let F* be the convex conjugate on int P and r(q)=(grad F)^{-1}(q). Then
  E_Q= max_{q in Q}[q.r-F*(q)]-phi_0 on T,
with canonical value minus infinity on X\T.

The weighted norm is positive definite on V_m. Its weighted Bernstein--Markov condition is valid. The actual dimensions satisfy
  dim V_m=m^d vol_Lambda(Q)+O(m^{d-1}) for d>=1,
and dim V_m=1 for d=0. Therefore d is the intrinsic section-growth dimension, not automatically dim X. For the orthonormal kernel in the question,
  U_m -> E_Q locally uniformly on T,
  U_m -> E_Q in L^p(X,dvol_omega) for every 1<=p<infinity,
and the extended pointwise envelope lower bound liminf U_m>=E_Q holds on X. In fact there is two-sided convergence on T and canonical minus-infinity values on the toric boundary for large m.

The finite Borel kernel measures converge weak-* (against every continuous test function on X):
  d!m^{-d} exp(-mw)B_m nu -> mu_Q,
  mu_Q=Psi_*(d!dq_Lambda d tau),
  Psi(q,tau)=exp(r(q)/2)tau.
Their limiting mass is d!vol_Lambda(Q)>0. When d=0 the volume of the singleton is 1 and the limit is Haar probability on one n-dimensional real torus. When d<n the n-dimensional normalization would instead yield zero.

PROOF OF THE ANALYTIC AND GLOBALIZATION CLAIMS. All of these assertions, including the exact target identification, are proved in n1 and n2. In particular n1 proves the global section space and exact equivalence between selection and the bound |s/e^m|^2<=C_s exp(mH_Q). It also proves the envelope formula using the circular maximum of each genuine psh competitor, rather than assuming competitors invariant. Its chart construction proves a genuine global theta-psh candidate, with no pointwise representative changes. In n2 the exact orthogonal basis, all norm transformations, and the elementary uniform estimates
  c m^{-n/2} exp(mF*(alpha/m)) <= ||z^alpha e^m||_m^2
             <= exp(mF*(alpha/m))
are established. A lattice grid approximation supplies global selected sections for every maximizing slope. The resulting estimate
  |U_m-E_Q|<=C(1+sum |r_j|+log m)/m
proves the stated topologies. Counting the actual selected lattice points establishes the intrinsic dimension and normalization. Finally individual monomial probabilities concentrate uniformly at r(alpha/m), and lattice Riemann sums prove the stated limit. These arguments do not assume any desired kernel asymptotic or rely on an external Bergman, toric quantization, or Monge--Ampere asymptotic theorem.

RELATION TO AN ARBITRARY AMBIENT HERMITIAN FORM. Let g_omega(u,v)=omega(u,Jv), with Riemannian volume dvol_omega=omega^n/n!. For d>=1 disregard the relative boundary of Q, which has zero dq_Lambda measure. The image
  S_Q=Psi(relint Q x (S^1)^n)
is a smooth embedded real submanifold of dimension n+d. Indeed grad F is a diffeomorphism, so its inverse restricted to aff Q is an immersion and embedding; angular coordinates give the additional n independent directions. For d=0 use the single embedded torus. Let dvol_{omega,S_Q} be the induced Riemannian volume. There is a unique smooth positive Jacobian J_omega satisfying
  Psi^*(dvol_{omega,S_Q})=J_omega(q,tau)dq_Lambda d tau.
Consequently the exact geometric density relation is
  dmu_Q=(d!/J_omega)dvol_{omega,S_Q} on S_Q.          (13)
No closedness of omega is required in this statement. It follows just by change of variables on an embedded submanifold. This is a direct and precise relation of the limit to a possibly nonclosed background; it does not replace theta by omega. The limit is independent of the choice of smooth positive invariant nu, although the Hilbert norms themselves depend on that choice.

For d<n, (13) is a submanifold density, not a density with respect to omega^n. The support S_Q has real codimension n-d>0 and mu_Q is singular with respect to smooth ambient volume. In particular there is no justification for identifying it with a universal mixed measure involving a nonclosed omega.

CLOSED-CURVATURE IDENTIFICATION AND THE LOWER-DIMENSIONAL OBSTRUCTION. These claims can also be made precise without importing an external asymptotic theorem. Put G=G_Q. On Q the conjugate F* is uniformly strongly convex in W. Hence the maximizer q(r) in the definition of G is unique. The variational inequalities at q(r) and q(s) imply
  (r-s).(q(r)-q(s))
     >=(grad F*(q(r))-grad F*(q(s))).(q(r)-q(s))
     >=c|q(r)-q(s)|^2.
Thus q(r) is Lipschitz. Comparing the optimizing values at r and r+h shows grad G=q(r), so G is C^{1,1}. Since its gradient takes values in b+W, its almost-everywhere Hessian has rank at most d. On T the closed curvature current of the envelope is
  T_Q=theta+dd^c E_Q=dd^c G.
The local bounded-psh powers of this current are given by Hessian minors. For completeness, mollify G in r on a relatively compact logarithmic chart. The mollifications are smooth convex, converge in C^1, and have uniformly bounded Hessian. Their Hessians converge almost everywhere and locally in finite L^p to the weak Hessian of G. All minors therefore converge in L^1. The corresponding wedge currents converge to those minors; the identity with the bounded-psh wedge follows inductively from dd^c(G T_Q^{j-1}) by integration by parts and uniform convergence of potentials. The nonpluripolar extension agrees with this local product on T and gives no mass to the toric boundary, which is pluripolar. This justifies all powers used here directly in our C^{1,1} situation.

It follows, when d<n, that
  <T_Q^{d+1}>=0,
and in particular the nonpluripolar n-th power is zero despite the positive kernel-limit mass. Also <T_Q^d> wedged with any smooth omega^{n-d} is locally absolutely continuous with respect to ambient volume on T, since its coefficients there are Hessian minors with locally bounded densities. It therefore gives zero mass to S_Q, whereas mu_Q(S_Q)=d!vol_Lambda(Q)>0. This proves that the kernel limit cannot equal that mixed measure, for any smooth ambient Hermitian omega. Closedness of the ambient form would not repair this mismatch. It is the selection and its intrinsic dimension, not an arbitrary background wedge, that determine the limit.

When d=n, the answer simplifies to the usual closed-curvature interpretation, but here it is proved by explicit Hessians. On r(int Q), the optimizing q is interior, the optimality equation is r=grad F*(q), and G=F. Elsewhere the optimizing q lies in the union of the facets of Q. The Lipschitz map q(r) then has derivative of rank less than n almost everywhere there: on the measurable set where it lies in any fixed facet, the normal component is constant and its derivative is zero almost everywhere on that set. A finite union of facets suffices. Thus
  det D^2 G=1_{grad F(r) in Q} det D^2 F(r) a.e.
The logarithmic-angular normalization of dd^c=(i/(2pi))partial barpartial is
  (dd^c F)^n=n! det D^2 F(r) dr d tau.
The boundary of Q is null and grad F is a diffeomorphism; changing variables q=grad F(r) in the explicit limit yields
  mu_Q=<(theta+dd^c E_Q)^n>.                         (14)
This is the nonpluripolar product, not an undefined ordinary wedge of divisorial currents. For a smooth ambient omega write dvol_omega=a_omega(r,tau)dr d tau on T. The density form of (14) is
  dmu_Q=[n! 1_{grad F(r) in Q} det D^2 F(r)/a_omega(r,tau)]dvol_omega.
Again omega may be nonclosed; theta remains the Chern curvature throughout.

EXPLICIT NON-SUBMERSION, LOWER-GROWTH EXAMPLE. Take n=2, L=O(4,4), product Fubini--Study h, w=0, and
  Q={(2+t,2-t): -1/2<=t<=1/2}.
Its integral affine offset is b=(2,2), W=R(1,-1), Lambda=Z(1,-1), d=1, and dq_Lambda=dt (not sqrt(2)dt). The entire globally selected space is
  V_m=span{z_1^{2m+j} z_2^{2m-j} e^m:
                         j integer, |j|<=m/2}.
Therefore N_m=2 floor(m/2)+1 and the limit mass is 1. Here
  F(r)=4log(1+e^{r_1})+4log(1+e^{r_2}),
  r_1(t)=log((2+t)/(2-t)),   r_2(t)=-r_1(t),
and
  mu_Q=integral_{-1/2}^{1/2}
          [Haar probability on {|z_1|^2=(2+t)/(2-t),
                                |z_2|^2=(2-t)/(2+t)}] dt.
Thus the limit is supported on a compact portion of |z_1 z_2|=1, of real dimension 3, and is singular with respect to any smooth volume on the complex surface. Its potential is explicitly the formula above with
  F*(2+t,2-t)=2c(2+t),
  c(p)=p log(p/4)+(4-p)log((4-p)/4).
Adjacent section ratios are z_1/z_2. The rational map [v_1u_2:u_1v_2], with z_j=v_j/u_j, has indeterminacy at (0,0) and (infinity,infinity); it is not a global submersion on X. The explicit norms concentrate in a transverse radial direction and do not simply integrate against a fixed fiber probability. This shows concretely why this theorem is not just a pullback-submersion model.

One may also make the sampling background genuinely nonclosed in this example. Let omega_0 be the sum of the two Fubini--Study forms, choose a nonconstant smooth torus-invariant f, for instance a nonzero constant times |z_1|^2/(1+|z_1|^2), and put omega=exp(f)omega_0.
