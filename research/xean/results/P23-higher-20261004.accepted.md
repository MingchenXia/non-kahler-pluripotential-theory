A CIRCULAR-LIFT PROOF OF WEIGHTED DECREASING CONVERGENCE.

The purpose is to remove the specifically weighted convergence theorem from the external-input list in n3. Assume only the ordinary foundational Bedford–Taylor statement: locally uniformly bounded decreasing psh sequences have weakly convergent unweighted products, with the product of the limiting functions as limit. Mixed products follow from single-sequence products by polarization of positive sums. The extra-dimensional argument below proves the needed weighted variant, including the exact normalization.

Lemma. On a ball B in C^m let a_j and b_{1,j},…,b_{m,j} be locally uniformly bounded decreasing psh sequences, with limits a and b_1,…,b_m. Put
μ_j=dd^cb_{1,j}∧…∧dd^cb_{m,j}, μ=dd^cb_1∧…∧dd^cb_m.
Then a_jμ_j→aμ weakly on compact subsets. Smooth fixed coefficient factors and products of lower degree follow by wedging with dummy fixed flat psh potentials and testing current coefficients.

Proof. Work on a relatively compact base ball B' so every a_j and a lies between constants L and U. Choose 0<r<R with log r^2<L−1 and log R^2>U+1. On B'×{|w|<R}, define
v_j(z,w)=max{a_j(z),log|w|^2},
v(z,w)=max{a(z),log|w|^2}.
These are psh, locally uniformly bounded (including at w=0 because of the a_j lower bound), and v_j decreases to v. The b_{r,j} are pulled back independently of w. Therefore the ordinary unweighted mixed decreasing theorem in complex dimension m+1 gives
T_j=μ_j∧dd^cv_j → T=μ∧dd^cv.

We claim a direct distributional formula for these currents, which does not itself use weighted convergence. The defining bounded-product recursion gives
μ∧dd^cv=dd^c(vμ)
and likewise for μ_j. The current μ has top bidegree (m,m) in the z variables. Thus all terms of dd^c involving a z differential vanish by bidegree; only the vertical dd^c_w survives. Equivalently this equality can be verified by testing and Fubini in the w variable, with μ a finite measure on compact subsets. For every fixed z, the vertical function is
w↦max{a(z),log|w|^2}.
With dd^c=(i/(2π))∂bar∂, dd^c log|w|^2 is unit point mass at 0. Hence
 dd^c_w max{t,log|w|^2}
is unit probability mass uniformly distributed on the circle |w|=e^{t/2}. To check the coefficient without relying on convention: for a radial smooth test, integrate the planar Laplacian of the piecewise harmonic function; its outward radial derivative jumps from 0 to 2/r at the circle, so the total Euclidean Laplacian mass is 4π, and dd^c is (1/(4π)) times Euclidean Laplacian measure. The resulting mass is exactly one.

Consequently
T=∫_{z∈B'} [probability circle at |w|=e^{a(z)/2}] dμ(z),
and the identical formula holds for T_j with a_j,μ_j. This formula is meaningful for the bounded canonical Borel representatives, including if μ charges a discontinuity set: it follows from the distribution dd^c_w(vμ) and Fubini, not from an assumed continuity of a.

Choose a smooth radial compactly supported function θ(w) on the annulus {r<|w|<R} which equals log|w|^2 on a neighborhood of every circle with radius between e^{L/2} and e^{U/2}. For a smooth compactly supported base test ξ(z),
⟨T_j,ξ(z)θ(w)⟩=∫B' ξ a_j dμ_j,
⟨T,ξ(z)θ(w)⟩=∫B' ξ a dμ.
The unweighted convergence T_j→T proves the claimed weighted convergence. Local mass bounds and the uniform bound on a_j extend the test class to continuous compactly supported ξ if desired.

This also proves variants in which the weighted psh sequence and the psh sequences defining the measure are different. For a quasi-psh weight, add a fixed smooth psh quadratic locally, apply the lemma, then subtract its unweighted smooth contribution. For lower-degree currents in base dimension d, wedge with fixed flat dd^c potentials to reach top degree; polarization of such test factors, together with scalar test functions, recovers all current coefficients. For smoothly varying fixed coefficient forms, uniform convergence of the coefficients and uniform local masses allow replacement by their limit.

APPLICATION TO N3. The convolved U_j+b r_j^2 plus a fixed local quadratic is a bounded decreasing psh sequence. All positive closed currents in the expansion of A_j^2 are dd^c of such bounded decreasing psh functions (or fixed flat quadratic potentials). Thus the lemma proves precisely the weighted convergence of ∫U_jA_j^2 used in the rough energy proof. No separate weighted BT theorem is now needed. The ordinary unweighted mixed decreasing theorem in one extra dimension suffices, and mixed convergence itself reduces by polarization to the usual single-sequence theorem. All hypotheses—local uniform bounds, genuine decreasing canonical limits, and psh rather than merely positive nonclosed currents—have been explicitly arranged.

Construct a genuinely nonclosed ambient-smooth Hermitian h with dd^c(pi*h)=0; its normalization pullback is degenerate, but an explicit bounded continuous reference repairs it to a positive pluriclosed metric on a flat complex torus.

Let C be obtained from an elliptic curve E1 by replacing a coordinate disk at p by the cusp y^2=x^3, using z↦(z^2,z^3). Its normalization E1→C is finite, degree one, and a homeomorphism. X=C×E2 is compact, reduced, connected, pure dimensional, locally irreducible, and has complex dimension two. A background h0=hC+κ2 is ambient-smooth and positive; on Y=E1×E2 its pullback H0=η+κ2 is closed and degenerates along {p}×E2. CONSTRUCTION AND GEOMETRY. Throughout dd^c=(i/(2π))∂bar∂, and d^c=(i/(4π))(bar∂−∂), so dd^c=d d^c.

1. The singular compact curve. Fix elliptic curves E1=C/Λ1 and E2=C/Λ2, and a point p∈E1. Choose a holomorphic coordinate z centered at p on a small disk. Replace that disk by the reduced cusp chart {y^2=x^3}, identified on the punctured disk by x=z^2,y=z^3. Glue the punctured cusp chart to E1\{p} by this identification. The resulting compact complex curve C has underlying topological space E1. Its only singularity q is a cusp. Its local ring at q is C{z^2,z^3}, which is a domain and has one analytic branch. The map π1:E1→C is finite: locally z is integral over C{z^2,z^3}, since z^2=x, and globally the map is proper. It is degree one and bijective, hence a homeomorphism. It is the normalization, and is an isomorphism away from p.

Set X=C×E2 and Y=E1×E2, with π=π1×id. Then X is compact, connected, reduced, pure of dimension two, and locally irreducible. Its singular locus is the elliptic curve {q}×E2. Y is a smooth compact complex torus and π is a resolution in the sense of a proper smooth birational model, although here it is finite. This is a cuspidal pinching construction, not a projective-cone construction.

2. A positive local-embedding background. Choose a smooth Hermitian form hC on C in the local-embedding sense. It can be constructed by finite chart partitions from positive ambient Euclidean forms. Near q take specifically hC=dd^c(|x|^2+|y|^2)|C. Away from q use positive forms on the smooth curve and glue with smooth local-embedding partitions; on the overlap all tangent metrics are positive and positive ambient extensions can be retained. Let κj be fixed flat positive (1,1)-forms on Ej. Put η=π1*hC. This is a smooth nonnegative form on E1, positive away from p, and near p
η=(i/(2π))(4|z|^2+9|z|^4) dz∧dbar z.
In particular it vanishes at p; this degeneracy must not be ignored. Write η=ρκ1, with ρ smooth, ρ≥0, ρ>0 outside p. Set λ=(∫E1η)/(∫E1κ1)>0.

The product background h0=prC*hC+prE2*κ2 is positive in the local-embedding sense on X. Its pullback H0=η+κ2 is smooth, nonnegative, and closed on Y, but has rank one along D={p}×E2.

3. A nonclosed pluriclosed perturbation. Choose a real smooth function A on C whose support is contained in Creg and misses a neighborhood of q. Choose a real smooth function B on E2. Suppress the product pullback notation and set
α=∂A∧bar∂B+bar∂A∧∂B.
This is a real (1,1)-form, globally ambient-smooth on X, and zero on a neighborhood of its singular locus. It also has the expression
α=∂(A bar∂B)+bar∂(A∂B),
since the two A∂bar∂B terms cancel. Therefore dd^cα=0. It need not be d-closed. In fact
∂α=−∂A∧∂bar∂B+∂bar∂A∧∂B.
Choose a point r∈Creg at which ∂A(r)=0 and ∂bar∂A(r)≠0, and a point s∈E2 at which ∂B(s)≠0. Such functions are supplied by local quadratic and linear functions times cutoffs. Then ∂α(r,s)≠0. Thus dα≠0.

Write α~ for its smooth pullback to Y. Let K0=λκ1+κ2. Define the finite constants
rH=sup over Y\D of the operator norm of α~ relative to H0,
rK=sup over Y of the operator norm of α~ relative to K0.
The first is finite because α~ vanishes near D. For any nonzero ε with |ε|rH<1/2 and |ε|rK<1/2, set h=h0+εα on X and H=π*h=H0+εα~ on Y. Then h is a strictly positive ambient-smooth background. Near the cusp it equals h0; on the support of α it is an ordinary positive smooth surface metric, which also gives positive local ambient extensions. It is genuinely nonclosed because dh=εdα≠0 on Xreg. Upstairs H remains degenerate along D, and dd^cH=0.

4. Explicit repair, not a positivity assumption. The function λ−ρ has zero κ1-integral. On the flat torus E1, Fourier inversion of the mean-zero Laplacian produces a unique smooth real mean-zero v satisfying
dd^c v=(λ−ρ)κ1.
Concretely, each nonzero Fourier coefficient of v is the corresponding coefficient of λ−ρ divided by the nonzero negative eigenvalue of dd^c/κ1; smoothness follows from rapid decay. Set v0=pr1*v on Y. Then
k=H+dd^c v0=K0+εα~ ≥ K0/2.
Thus k is genuinely positive, smooth, pluriclosed, and nonclosed. The positivity was obtained by adding an explicit reference potential; it was not attributed to π*h.

5. Genuine disk descent. Every holomorphic disk into the cusp lifts holomorphically. If its components satisfy y^2=x^3 and x is not identically zero, set z=y/x off {x=0}. Then z^2=x, z^3=y, and |z|^2=|x|, so z extends holomorphically over the zeros by the removable-singularity theorem. If x is identically zero, so is y, and the lift is the constant disk. Together with the unchanged E2 component this lifts every disk into X to Y. Since π is a homeomorphism, any continuous U on Y has a unique continuous descended function u=U∘π^{-1}. If H+dd^cU≥0 for a C^2 U, lifted disks show exactly the required h-subharmonic disk condition downstairs: on a disk f, the chosen p_f has dd^cp_f=f*h, and dd^c(U∘f~+p_f)=f~*(H+dd^cU)≥0. In particular v0 descends to a bounded continuous genuine h-psh function. All values are finite canonical values. No assertion that an arbitrary smooth solution on an arbitrary resolution descends is being used.
