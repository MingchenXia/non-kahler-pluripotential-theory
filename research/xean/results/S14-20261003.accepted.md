## w7-1/n1

RADIAL GLOBAL MOMENT LEMMA AND PROOF.

Fix an integer r>=2 and a number epsilon>0. Put d=r+1,
P(t)=1+t^r+epsilon t^(r+1), psi(t)=log P(t), a(t)=(1+t)^(-2),
H(t)=t psi'(t), G(t)=t H'(t), and g(t)=G(t)/t=psi'(t)+t psi''(t).
For integers m>=1 and 0<=k<=dm set
J_{m,k}=pi integral_0^infinity t^k exp(-m psi(t)) a(t) dt,
and w_{m,k}(t)=t^k exp(-m psi(t))/J_{m,k}.
All these integrals are finite, including k=dm, since their integrands at infinity are O(t^(k-dm-2)). Let K_m(t)=sum_{k=0}^{dm}w_{m,k}(t).

CLAIMS.
(A) If t=t_m->0 and mu=mH(t)->infinity, then, with V=mG(t),
K_m(t)=V/(pi a(t)t)(1+o(1)). The probability weights p_{m,k}=w_{m,k}/K_m satisfy
(k-mu)/sqrt(V) => N(0,1).
There is no further restriction: mu=o(m) follows from t->0. The statements are uniform along all sequences with these properties.
(B) For each fixed t in (0,infinity), K_m(t)=m g(t)/(pi a(t))(1+o(1)). The same result is locally uniform on (0,infinity).
(C) If k->infinity and k=o(m), and s is the unique solution H(s)=k/m, then
J_{m,k}=pi a(s)s sqrt(2pi/(mG(s))) exp(k log s-m psi(s))(1+o(1)).

We provide the tail details because uniformity in growing order is essential.

1. Elementary convexity facts. Set F(u)=psi(e^u). Its derivative is H(e^u), and its second derivative is G(e^u). If the exponents 0,r,d have weights respectively 1,t^r,epsilon t^d, divided by P(t), then H is their weighted mean and G is their variance. Consequently G>0 on (0,infinity), H increases from 0 to d, and there is a unique saddle for every 0<k<dm. Near zero,
H(t)=r t^r+(r+1)epsilon t^(r+1)+O(t^(2r)),
G(t)=r^2 t^r+(r+1)^2 epsilon t^(r+1)+O(t^(2r)).
In particular H(t) is comparable to t^r and G/H tends to r. Each fixed positive-order u derivative of F is O(H) near zero. Choose b>0 small and numbers rho_-=r-eta>1 and rho_+=r+eta, with eta>0 small, so that
rho_- <= G(t)/H(t) <= rho_+ for 0<t<=b.
The amplitude A(u)=a(e^u)e^u has bounded logarithmic derivatives and, on e^u<=b, is comparable to e^u.

2. The uniform saddle formula. In u coordinates,
J_{m,k}=pi integral_R A(u) exp(ku-mF(u)) du.
Let v=log s and E(h)=m[F(v+h)-F(v)]-kh. Choose a small constant eta_0>0 so that k<=eta_0 m implies s<=b/e^2 and eta_0<H(b)/2. On |h|<=1, E(h)>=c k h^2; also E(h)=mG(s)h^2/2+O(k|h|^3). The amplitude ratio A(v+h)/A(v) has uniformly bounded logarithmic derivative there. On the part h<=-1, the elasticity bounds give
H(se^h)/H(s)<=exp(rho_- h),
E(h)>=k[|h|-(1-exp(rho_- h))/rho_-].
On 1<=h<=log(b/s), they give
E(h)>=k[(exp(rho_- h)-1)/rho_- -h].
The amplitude ratio on these ranges is at most C exp(h). Thus, outside any fixed small neighborhood of zero but before reaching b, the normalized saddle integral has exponentially small tails in k. After reaching b, E'(h)>=mH(b)-k>=c m. By taking eta_0 still smaller, E(log(b/s))>=c m: indeed m psi(b)>0 is fixed times m, while m psi(s)=O(k) and k log(b/s)<=C m eta_0 log(1/eta_0) uniformly for k<=eta_0m. The remaining amplitude integral is bounded by C(b/s)exp(-cm)/(cm-1). Since s is comparable to (k/m)^(1/r), this is a polynomial factor times exp(-cm).

Now set h=y/sqrt(mG(s)). For bounded y the exponent tends uniformly to y^2/2, and the amplitude ratio tends uniformly to one. The preceding quadratic and tail bounds justify extending the Gaussian integral over all y. This proves (C), uniformly for k->infinity, k=o(m). They also give, for every integer 1<=k<=eta_0m, the useful lower bound
J_{m,k}>=c a(s)s k^(-1/2) exp(k log s-m psi(s)).
For this lower bound alone, integrate over |h|<=1/sqrt(k); the exponent is bounded and the amplitude comparable. The constants do not depend on m,k.

3. Central index asymptotics. Suppose now t->0 and mu=mH(t)->infinity, and write V=mG(t)~r mu. For |k-mu|<=M sqrt(V), the saddle s satisfies log(s/t)=(k-mu)/V+O((k-mu)^2/mu^2). The saddle formula and Taylor expansion yield, uniformly on this set for each fixed M,
w_{m,k}(t)=sqrt(V)/(pi a(t)t sqrt(2pi)) exp(-(k-mu)^2/(2V))(1+o(1)).
Indeed the exponent loss is
I(k)=m[psi(t)-psi(s)]-k log(t/s),
and I(mu)=I'(mu)=0, I''(k)=1/(mG(s)); this gives the displayed quadratic expansion. All amplitude ratios tend to one. Summing on a fixed standardized interval is a Gaussian Riemann sum, with total natural scale
T=V/(pi a(t)t).

4. Summable tails; no hidden lower bound on the rate of growth of mu. For 1<=k<=eta_0m, the saddle lower bound gives
w_{m,k}/T <= C (sqrt(k)/mu)(t/s) exp(-I(k)).
We can suppose mu<=eta_0m. Along the interval between k and mu the elasticity bounds give I''(k)>=1/(rho_+ k). Hence
I(k)>=rho_+^(-1)D(k,mu),
D(k,mu)=k log(k/mu)-k+mu.
If k<=mu, t/s<=(mu/k)^alpha with alpha=1/rho_-<1; if k>=mu, t/s<=1. For mu/2<=k<=2mu, this implies the bound
w_{m,k}/T <= C mu^(-1/2) exp(-c(k-mu)^2/mu).
Its sum outside |k-mu|<=M sqrt(V) is bounded by C integral_{cM}^infinity exp(-c y^2)dy+o(1). For 1<=k<mu/2, D>=c mu and the sum is at most
C exp(-c mu) mu^(alpha-1) sum_{k<mu/2} k^(1/2-alpha)
<=C sqrt(mu) exp(-c mu).
For 2mu<k<=eta_0m, D>=c k, so the sum of C sqrt(k) exp(-ck)/mu tends to zero.

For k=0, integration on 0<=s<=m^(-1/r) gives J_{m,0}>=c m^(-1/r). Therefore
w_{m,0}/T <= C mu^(1/r-1) exp(-c mu)->0.
For k>eta_0m choose a fixed s_0>0. Integrating the original moment over a t-interval of length c/m next to s_0 gives, uniformly for 0<=k<=dm,
J_{m,k}>=c m^(-1) s_0^k exp(-m psi(s_0)).
Here the exponent and amplitude change by bounded factors since k<=dm. For our observations t->0, summing the resulting geometric bound gives
sum_{k>eta_0m}w_{m,k} <= C m exp(m psi(s_0))(t/s_0)^(eta_0m)/(1-t/s_0).
Dividing by T still gives zero: log(s_0/t)->infinity, so the negative exponent -eta_0m log(s_0/t) dominates both m psi(s_0) and all polynomial prefactors. In particular this argument does not multiply exp(-c mu) by m, which would be invalid for very slowly growing mu.

The central Riemann sums and these tails prove K_m/T->1 and convergence of the normalized index distribution to the standard Gaussian. This proves (A).

5. Fixed-positive-t statement. On a compact t-interval in (0,infinity), mu/m stays in a compact subinterval of (0,d), G(t) is bounded below, and V is comparable to m. The same saddle proof, now on a compact saddle interval, gives the central formula with m in place of mu. For indices whose k/m stay in that interval, I''(k)=1/(mG(s)) is bounded below by c/m, giving summable Gaussian tails. Remaining indices are exponentially suppressed without using endpoint saddle estimates: for k/m<=H(t)-delta, evaluate the moment on a width-1/m interval about s_-=t exp(-h), with h>0 small. The exponent gain over evaluating at t is at least m(delta h-C h^2)>=cm, uniformly in those indices. Use s_+=t exp(h) for k/m>=H(t)+delta. Thus the remaining weights are bounded by Cm exp(-cm), and there are only dm+1 of them. The central sum gives (B), uniformly on compact t-intervals. This completes the lemma using only Taylor expansion, convexity, Gaussian integration and elementary integral bounds.

## w7-1/n2

A PRECISE HIGHER-DIMENSIONAL SUFFICIENT-CONDITION THEOREM.

The sufficient conditions below deliberately specify a tractable global class. They do not assert a universal law for arbitrary higher-dimensional degeneracies.

Fix n>=2, integers r_1,...,r_n>=2 with at least two unequal, and constants epsilon_j>0. Put d_j=r_j+1. Let
Y=(CP1)^n, x=(0,...,0), L=external tensor product_j O(d_j).
In the product affine chart z=(z_1,...,z_n), set t_j=|z_j|^2 and use the holomorphic product frame with
|e|_h^2=exp(-phi),
phi(z)=sum_j psi_j(t_j), psi_j(t)=log(1+t^(r_j)+epsilon_j t^(r_j+1)).
Use the smooth positive Hilbert volume
nu_Y=product_j a_j(t_j)dA_j, a_j(t)=(1+t)^(-2).
In particular its affine density at x is one. Write
H_j=t psi_j', G_j=tH_j', g_j=G_j/t=psi_j'+t psi_j'',
a(t)=product_j a_j(t_j).
For q=q_m->infinity, q=o(m), let
V_m=H0(Y,L^m tensor m_x^q),
with the smooth norm integral_Y |s|_(h^m)^2 nu_Y, and let B_m be its diagonal partial Bergman kernel.

These are genuine compact examples, not an entire-space assumption. To check the metric at infinity on each factor, with u=|w|^2=1/t, subtract d_j log t from psi_j(t). The resulting potential is
log(epsilon_j+u+u^(d_j)),
which is smooth at w=0 and has positive curvature there. The curvature is semipositive everywhere. For 0<t<infinity it is strictly positive because H_j is the weighted mean of distinct exponents with positive weights, so G_j is their strictly positive variance. At zero
psi_j(t)=t^(r_j)+epsilon_j t^(r_j+1)+O(t^(2r_j)),
g_j(t)=r_j^2 t^(r_j-1)+(r_j+1)^2 epsilon_j t^(r_j)+O(t^(2r_j-1)).
Thus the local leading weight is sum_j |z_j|^(2r_j), with specified, genuinely nonzero smooth anisotropic remainders epsilon_j|z_j|^(2r_j+2). The product curvature has rank loss along the coordinate divisors, and is positive where all z_j are nonzero; no isolated-degeneracy assumption is made. The underlying L is positive as a holomorphic line bundle. The volume extends smoothly and positively at every infinity chart.

THE LOCAL TRANSITION.
Consider any observation sequence with t_j->0 and
mu_j=mH_j(t_j), mu_j/q->alpha_j>0, sum_j alpha_j=1.
Set V=sum_j mG_j(t_j). Suppose
(sum_j mu_j-q)/sqrt(V)->tau in R.
Then
[pi^n a(t) B_m(z)]/[m^n product_j g_j(t_j)] -> Phi(tau).
There is no phase restriction: every choice of arguments of z_j obeys this limit. The restriction alpha_j>0 excludes faces of the anisotropic observation cone.

This is an all-q=o(m) result when the center is corrected by the actual weight. For an explicit moving scale choose positive alpha_j summing to one, put R=sum_j alpha_j r_j, and set
T_m=q+tau sqrt(Rq),
t_j=H_j^(-1)(alpha_j T_m/m).
Then T_m>0 eventually, these inverse functions are well-defined, mu_j=alpha_j T_m, V~Rq, and the stated transition applies. Physical radii are |z_j|=sqrt(t_j); their leading orders are (alpha_j q/(r_j m))^(1/(2r_j)).

HOMOGENEOUS CENTER, VALIDITY RANGE, AND ITS FAILURE.
If instead the radii are prescribed solely by the leading homogeneous term,
t_j=(alpha_j T_m/(r_j m))^(1/r_j), T_m=q+tau sqrt(Rq),
put r_*=max_j r_j. In this model the unshifted law is valid in the precise small-remainder range
q=o(m^(2/(r_*+2))).
One may use the homogeneous denominator: on these observations
[pi^n B_m(z)]/[m^n product_j r_j^2 |z_j|^(2r_j-2)] -> Phi(tau)
in that range, since a(t)->1 and g_j/(r_j^2 t_j^(r_j-1))->1.
If q/m^(2/(r_*+2))->c in (0,infinity), the same normalized expression instead tends to
Phi(tau+C_alpha c^((r_*+2)/(2r_*))),
where
C_alpha=(1/sqrt(R)) sum_{j:r_j=r_*} (r_*+1)epsilon_j (alpha_j/r_*)^(1+1/r_*).
If that ratio tends to infinity, while still q=o(m), the limit is 1 for every fixed tau. More generally the subsequential behavior is described by the actual saddle center and variance above; an oscillating shift need not have a limit. In particular, in this class with positive epsilon_j, q=o(m^(2/(r_*+2))) is necessary as well as sufficient for the unshifted homogeneous-center law to hold for all fixed tau along the whole sequence. This is a statement about the specified radial class, not an assertion about the original arbitrary one-dimensional background.

GLOBAL SECTION PRODUCTION AND EXACT KERNEL FORMULA.
Holomorphic sections on a CP1 factor of O(d_jm) have affine coefficients polynomial of degree at most d_jm: this follows by expanding an entire coefficient in powers of z and imposing holomorphicity of z^(-d_jm)f(z) at infinity. Applying the same expansion one coordinate at a time gives a basis on the product indexed by
0<=k_j<=d_jm.
A polynomial belongs to m_x^q exactly when its Taylor coefficients of all total degrees below q vanish. Consequently V_m has the basis
z^k e^m, 0<=k_j<=d_jm, sum_j k_j>=q.
The ideal is the coherent point ideal m_x^q; in a neighborhood of x it is generated by the finitely many degree-q monomials, and away from x it is the unit ideal. Unlike a product of divisorial ideals, it imposes ordinary total-order vanishing at the single point.

Angular integration makes these monomials exactly orthogonal. If J_{j,m,k} is the moment from n1 with r=r_j, epsilon=epsilon_j, their squared norms are product_j J_{j,m,k_j}. Thus the globally normalized kernel is exactly
B_m(z)=sum_{0<=k_j<=d_jm, sum k_j>=q} product_j w_{j,m,k_j}(t_j).
No local sections need to be extended by an unproved analytic estimate: every monomial here is already global, its norm is the full compact norm, and n1 proves that this global norm has the required uniform moving-scale asymptotic.

For q=o(m), eventually q<=min_j(d_jm)+1. All jets of total degree below q are then supplied by distinct global monomials. Hence exactly
N_m=dim V_m=product_j(d_jm+1)-binomial(q+n-1,n).
In particular N_m/m^n->D=product_j d_j>0, and the intrinsic section-growth exponent is
lim_m log N_m/log m=n.
This exponent, rather than the dimension of any later total space, dictates the m^n normalization. If kernels are instead divided by N_m, the corresponding global limit below is divided by D.

PROOF OF THE LOCAL TRANSITION.
Write K_{j,m}=sum_{k=0}^{d_jm}w_{j,m,k} and p_{j,m,k}=w_{j,m,k}/K_{j,m}. The exact kernel identity becomes
B_m=(product_j K_{j,m}) P(sum_j K_j>=q),
where here K_j are independent integer variables with laws p_{j,m,k}. (This probabilistic notation is simply a finite product sum.) At the observations in the theorem, mu_j->infinity and mu_j=o(m). Lemma n1 gives
product_j K_{j,m}=m^n product_j g_j(t_j)/(pi^n a(t))(1+o(1)),
and (K_j-mu_j)/sqrt(mG_j(t_j)) converges to a standard Gaussian. Moreover mG_j(t_j)~r_j alpha_j q, so all the variance fractions have positive limits.

For completeness, the conclusion for the sum does not require invoking an external central limit theorem. On a product of bounded standardized intervals, the central index formula in n1 turns the joint product probabilities into Riemann sums for the product Gaussian density. Its complement has arbitrarily small probability by the summable tails in n1. A half-space has boundary of zero Gaussian measure, so the same Riemann-sum argument applies to the inequality defining the sum, by enclosing its boundary in a strip of arbitrarily small Gaussian mass. A linear combination of independent standard Gaussian densities with coefficients whose squared sum is one has standard Gaussian density, as follows directly by an orthogonal change of variables in exp(-|v|^2/2). It follows that
(sum_j K_j-sum_j mu_j)/sqrt(V) => N(0,1).
The lattice spacing is 1/sqrt(V)->0, and the assumed threshold is asymptotically -tau. Therefore P(sum K_j>=q)->Phi(tau), proving the theorem.

For the failure calculation, on homogeneous radii use
sum mu_j=T_m+Delta_m,
Delta_m=sum_j (r_j+1)epsilon_j m t_j^(r_j+1)+O(sum_j m t_j^(2r_j)),
V~Rq.
Each error is little-o of its own displayed positive correction because r_j>=2 and t_j->0. Therefore
Delta_m/sqrt(Rq)=(1+o(1)) sum_j [(r_j+1)epsilon_j(alpha_j/r_j)^(1+1/r_j)/sqrt(R)] q^(1/2+1/r_j)m^(-1/r_j).
At q~c m^(2/(r_*+2)), precisely the indices r_j=r_* survive; the others have a negative power of m. Below that scale all terms vanish. Above it the positive r_* term diverges. In the latter case the tightness already proved for the normalized sum shows the probability of the selected half-space tends to one. The curvature-denominator ratios tend to one in all these cases. Positivity of all epsilon_j also proves the claimed necessity by taking a subsequence on which the growth ratio is bounded away from zero and then a further subsequence with a finite positive or infinite limit.

A GLOBAL KERNEL-MEASURE LIMIT, WITH INTRINSIC NORMALIZATION.
Let omega=c1(L,h), with the convention dd^c=(i/(2pi))partial barpartial. In the affine chart,
omega=sum_j g_j(t_j)dA_j/pi,
omega^n/n!=product_j g_j(t_j)dA_j/pi.
The single-factor mass is integral_0^infinity g_j(t)dt=[H_j(t)]_0^infinity=d_j; thus the total mass is D=product_j d_j.
For the full space H0(Y,L^m), its kernel is product_j K_{j,m}. Part (B) of n1 shows that the full normalized kernel density tends pointwise to product g_j/pi^n on the dense chart with all coordinates nonzero and finite. This excluded set is a union of coordinate divisors, which has zero volume and zero omega^n mass. The total mass of the full normalized kernel measure is product_j(d_jm+1)/m^n->D, by integrating each orthonormal basis element.

Here is an elementary justification that these facts imply weak convergence, without asserting a global uniform estimate at the degeneracies. Choose compact subsets exhausting the chart just used. On each, Fatou's lemma gives the lower mass bound for nonnegative continuous test functions. Since the limiting measure gives arbitrarily small mass to the complement of sufficiently large compact subsets and the total masses converge, the limsup of the complementary masses is correspondingly small. Apply Fatou also to a constant minus a given bounded test function on the compact part, and use this complementary-mass bound. Equivalently, lower semicontinuity for every nonnegative continuous test function, together with total mass convergence, gives the reverse bound by subtracting the function from a constant. This proves
m^(-n) B_full,m nu_Y => omega^n/n!.
For the partial kernel, B_full,m-B_m is nonnegative and its integral is precisely binomial(q+n-1,n)=O(q^n)=o(m^n). Consequently
m^(-n) B_m nu_Y => omega^n/n!.
All constants have therefore been fixed by both the local saddle calculation and the global trace.

LIMITATIONS. The theorem assumes separability, radial invariance, the indicated polynomial global compactification and a product radial smooth volume. It handles nonzero anisotropic remainders of the specified class, but does not control mixed or angular remainders, arbitrary compact bases, directions where some alpha_j vanish, or arbitrary monomial/weighted ideals. None of those extensions follows merely by invoking fixed-order localization. The growing-order global comparison in this theorem is furnished instead by the explicit monomial norms and the uniform estimates of n1.

## w7-1/n3

FIBRATION TRANSFER WITH A NON-DESCENDING SINGULAR SELECTOR.

Together with n1 and n2, this is a complete sufficient-condition solution of the requested model research subtask. The unrestricted programme, and the original arbitrary-weight one-dimensional problem, are not claimed to be solved.

THEOREM. Use the compact anisotropic base (Y,x,L,h,nu_Y) and growing q_m from n2. Let pi:X->Y be a holomorphic submersion of compact complex manifolds, with connected fibers of positive complex dimension f, and let N=n+f=dim_C X. Endow X with any Hermitian manifold structure. Let E=pi*L and h_E=pi*h. Choose a smooth positive volume nu_X satisfying the explicit matched-norm condition
pi_*nu_X=nu_Y.
Fix one point p in pi^(-1)(x).

In holomorphic submersion coordinates (z,w) centered at p, with pi(z,w)=z, choose a real function chi_p which equals
log(|z_1|^2+...+|z_n|^2+|w_1|^2+...+|w_f|^2)
in a neighborhood of p, is smooth on X minus {p}, and is smoothly cut off to zero outside a larger coordinate neighborhood. Put
Psi_m=(q_m+N-1)chi_p,
I_m=I(Psi_m),
where I(Psi_m) is the sheaf of holomorphic germs locally integrable for the density |F|^2 exp(-Psi_m) against a smooth positive volume. The convention is exp(-Psi_m), rather than exp(-2Psi_m). Then
I_m=m_p^(q_m),
and this ideal and selector do not descend from Y.

Use the selected section space
W_m=H0(X,E^m tensor I_m)
with the smooth Hilbert norm integral_X |S|_(h_E^m)^2 nu_X. In particular Psi_m selects the space but is NOT included in its Hilbert weight. Let B_X,m be the corresponding partial diagonal kernel.

There are exact equalities
W_m=pi*H0(Y,L^m tensor m_x^(q_m)),
||pi*s||_X=||s||_Y,
B_X,m=B_m composed with pi.
Consequently all local laws and homogeneous-center corrections in n2 transfer to every sequence P_m in X with pi(P_m)=z_m satisfying the stated base observations. No condition on the positions in the fibers is needed. In particular, under the corrected-center conditions,
[pi^n a(t(pi(P_m))) B_X,m(P_m)]/[m^n product_j g_j(t_j(pi(P_m)))] -> Phi(tau).
The intrinsic section-growth exponent on X is n, not N:
dim W_m=product_j(d_jm+1)-binomial(q_m+n-1,n),
dim W_m/m^n->D=product_j d_j.

There is also the global measure limit
m^(-n) B_X,m nu_X => [(omega^n/n!)/nu_Y] composed with pi times nu_X,
where omega=c1(L,h). The quotient on the right is the smooth nonnegative base density of omega^n/n! relative to nu_Y. Its total mass is D. Normalizing by dim W_m instead gives the probability measure obtained by dividing this limit by D.

PROOF OF THE SELECTOR AND ITS NON-DESCENT.
The specified cutoff is well-defined: use a smooth bump equal to one on a smaller coordinate ball and zero near the boundary of a larger one, multiply log of the squared coordinate norm, and extend by zero. It is quasi-plurisubharmonic. Near p the logarithm of the squared norm of a holomorphic coordinate vector is plurisubharmonic. On the annulus affected by the cutoff it is smooth with Hessian bounded below. Relative to any fixed Hermitian form this gives a global lower Hessian bound; multiplication by the finite positive coefficient preserves quasi-plurisubharmonicity.

A nonzero holomorphic germ in N variables has an initial homogeneous Taylor term of degree ell. Its squared modulus integrated over the unit sphere after substituting R times the variable is asymptotic to c R^(2ell), with c>0: the initial homogeneous polynomial is not identically zero, and the higher Taylor terms are uniformly lower order on that sphere. The weighted radial integrability at p is therefore exactly the integrability at zero of
R^(2ell) R^(-2(q+N-1)) R^(2N-1)dR
=R^(2ell-2q+1)dR.
This is finite if and only if ell>=q. Smooth positive metric or volume factors are bounded above and below and do not affect the criterion. Thus I(Psi_m)=m_p^q, including for the zero germ. Away from p the weight is smooth, and the ideal is the unit ideal. It is a coherent ideal, locally generated near p by all coordinate monomials of degree q.

The singularity is not a pullback singularity: it is infinite at p but finite at other points in the same positive-dimensional fiber. Similarly m_p^q cannot be a pullback ideal from Y; its stalk is a proper ideal at p but the unit ideal at any other point of pi^(-1)(x), whereas a pulled-back ideal would impose the same base vanishing condition at each such point. This is an explicit fiber-selected singularity that does not descend.

PROOF OF SECTION DESCENT AND THE SELECTED SPACE.
First, for any m, every holomorphic section S of pi*L^m is a pullback section. On the inverse image of a local trivializing open set U for L^m, write S=F pi*e. Its restriction to any fiber is a holomorphic function on a connected compact complex manifold, hence constant. To recall why, its modulus attains a maximum; the maximum modulus principle in a coordinate ball forces local constancy, and connectedness continues this constancy over the fiber. Thus F is constant on each fiber. A holomorphic submersion has local holomorphic sections through each point, obtained by setting the fiber coordinates to constants in the local submersion chart. Composing F with such a section proves that the resulting base function is holomorphic. Constants on entire connected fibers make the locally defined base functions agree. They glue in the transitions for L^m, proving S=pi*s uniquely.

Now use submersion coordinates (z,w) near p. The coefficient of a pullback section is f(z), independent of w. Its order at (0,0) is exactly its order at z=0: the initial Taylor polynomial has no w terms and retains its original degree. Consequently
pi*s belongs to m_p^q if and only if s belongs to m_x^q.
There are no constraints away from p from I_m. This proves the exact selected-space equality. The non-descending selector therefore imposes precisely the descending growing point-jet condition on this particular pullback section family, even though its multiplier ideal on all germs is not descending.

HILBERT-NORM DESCENT AND CONSTRUCTION OF MATCHED VOLUMES.
For a pullback section the pointwise norm is |pi*s|_(h_E^m)^2=|s|_(h^m)^2 composed with pi. By the definition of the pushforward of a measure,
integral_X |pi*s|^2 nu_X=integral_Y |s|^2 pi_*nu_X.
The matched-norm hypothesis proves the asserted isometry. Pulling back an orthonormal basis then proves the exact pointwise kernel equality, not just an asymptotic comparison.

The matched-volume hypothesis is nonempty for every such submersion. Start with any smooth positive top-degree form mu_X, for example the volume of a Hermitian metric. Its pushforward is b(y)nu_Y with b a smooth positive function. One can see smoothness without using a global fibration-trivialization theorem: take finitely many submersion coordinate boxes on the compact X, insert a smooth partition of unity with supports compactly contained in these boxes, and in each box integrate the smooth compactly supported density in the w variables. The resulting functions of z are smooth by differentiation under the integral, and their sum is the pushforward density. Positivity follows because each fiber is nonempty and the original density is everywhere positive. The coordinate transformations give the same base top form on overlaps. Set
nu_X=mu_X/(b composed with pi).
Then pi_*nu_X=nu_Y, as required. Such a volume is itself a legitimate Hermitian volume: multiply the starting Hermitian metric by the appropriate positive scalar function if desired.

More generally, with an arbitrary smooth positive nu'_X, the exact transformed base norm is
||pi*s||_(nu'_X)^2=integral_Y |s|_(h^m)^2 (pi_*nu'_X).
If pi_*nu'_X=b nu_Y, the transformed volume is b nu_Y, not the original volume. This identity is proved, but one must not conclude the precise n2 limit for general b from it. One may either use the explicit matched normalization above, or take as base volume the transformed product radial volume in a version of the radial moment argument with that volume. Our asserted numerical constants use precisely nu_Y from n2. No singular norm involving exp(-Psi_m) is silently identified with this smooth norm.

PROOF OF THE GLOBAL FIBRATION MEASURE LIMIT.
For a continuous function A on X define
T_A(y) nu_Y=pi_*(A nu_X).
The same local submersion-box integration proves T_A is continuous (uniform approximation by smooth functions, or dominated convergence in the boxes suffices). Positivity and the matched-volume condition give |T_A|<=||A||_infinity, and T_1=1. The exact kernel equality implies
m^(-n) integral_X A B_X,m nu_X
=m^(-n) integral_Y T_A B_m nu_Y.
Apply the global measure convergence proved in n2. The result is
integral_Y T_A omega^n/n!
=integral_X A [(omega^n/n!)/nu_Y] composed with pi nu_X,
which proves the claimed weak limit. This proof uses the intrinsic exponent n from the exact selected dimension; using m^N would instead give the zero measure when f>0.

COMPACT REALIZATIONS AND SCOPE.
For instance take any compact connected Hermitian manifold F of dimension f>=1, X=Y times F, pi the projection, and E=pi*L. With nu_F a smooth positive volume of mass one, nu_X=nu_Y times nu_F is matched. More generally nu_X=W(y,u)nu_Y nu_F is matched whenever W>0 is smooth and integral_F W(y,u)nu_F=1 for every y; this allows nonproduct total norms before integration. One explicit choice is W=1+delta b(y)c(u), where c has fiber mean zero and delta is small enough for positivity. The selector above at (x,p_F) is isolated and non-descending even in this product realization. The volume-normalization construction also applies to arbitrary nontrivial compact holomorphic submersions with connected fibers; no product decomposition of X is used in the descent proof.

The transfer theorem requires a pullback line bundle and pullback metric, connected compact fibers, and a matched or explicitly transformed smooth Hilbert volume. For arbitrary fiber-selected singularities, the intersection of the multiplier ideal with pullback sections can produce a different base ideal, or a different space altogether; the Taylor-order calculation here does not extend without further hypotheses. Disconnected fibers may contribute additional sections. Non-pullback metrics, mixed anisotropic base remainders, and singular Hilbert norms need new analysis. Thus this theorem supplies an explicit successful interface rather than assuming that all singular selectors descend.

DEPENDENCIES AND COMPLETENESS. The only substantial analytic estimates are independently proved in n1 and used in n2; n2 proves the compact section production, constants, center corrections, dimension, and global comparison. The present note proves the selector, descent, norm identity, construction of volumes and transferred measure limits. There are no unverified external localization, Bergman-kernel, extension, or multiplier-ideal theorems being invoked. The remaining elementary inputs (maximum modulus, Taylor expansion, finite partitions of unity, Gaussian integration and differentiation of compactly supported integrals) have their relevant arguments explicitly given or are the ordinary elementary constructions. These three notes establish all claims for the specified sufficient-condition class, including nonzero smooth anisotropic remainders and a compact realization, and make no claim outside that class.
