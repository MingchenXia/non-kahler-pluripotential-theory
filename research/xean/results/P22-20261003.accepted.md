PRECISE GEOMETRIC INPUT, PROVED DIRECTLY

Write ρ=φ_zbarz. With the convention of the question, dd^cφ=(ρ/π)dA. Fix r≥1. Put
  t=q/m,   R=(t/r)^(1/(2r)),   Q_R(ζ)=R^(-2r)φ(Rζ).
The original smooth Taylor hypothesis implies that Q_R extends jointly smoothly to signed R near zero on every fixed ζ-disk, and
  Q_R(ζ)=|ζ|^(2r)+R E(R,ζ),
with E jointly smooth. Indeed all Taylor terms of φ below degree 2r vanish, its degree-2r term is |z|^(2r), and the integral Taylor remainder can be taken along R↦φ(Rζ). Thus Q_R→Q_0=|ζ|^(2r) with O(R) error in every fixed Ck norm. Every Q_R is subharmonic; its Laplacian is strictly positive on a fixed annulus about |ζ|=1 for small R. Work on a fixed outer disk |ζ|<b, b>1, for example b=3.

Here is a construction requiring only the usual Banach implicit-function theorem and boundedness of the circle Cauchy projections on Hölder spaces. Fix k≥3 and 0<α<1. Let X_k be the real Banach space of holomorphic h on the unit disk with C^(k,α) boundary values and h(0) real. Put
  F(u)=u(1+h(u)),
  K(R,h)(u)=F(u)(Q_R)_ζ(F(u))−r,   |u|=1.
Let P_- project onto strictly negative Fourier modes. Consider
  T(R,h)=(P_-K(R,h), Re[K(R,h)]_0).
The target is the negative-mode Hölder subspace times ℝ. Composition with the smooth Q_R is a smooth map on these fixed Hölder spaces. In particular no derivative of F occurs in this equation, and there is no loss of derivatives.

At (R,h)=(0,0), T=0. If v∈X_k is a variation, then
  D_hK(0,0)v=r²(v+bar v),
since ζ(Q_0)_ζ(ζ)=r|ζ|^(2r). Consequently
  D_hT(0,0)v=(r² overline{v−v(0)}, 2r²v(0)).
This is a bounded real-linear isomorphism. The implicit-function theorem gives a unique nearby normalized F_R, smooth in R in C^(k,α), with
  F_R(0)=0, F_R'(0)>0, F_R=u+O(R).
For small R it is univalent: sup|F_R'−1|<1 implies injectivity on the convex unit disk. Write Ω_R=F_R(D).

The equation has not omitted a residue constraint. P_-K=0 means that K has a holomorphic extension to D, while its constant coefficient is purely imaginary. Stokes gives
  (1/(2πi))∮ (Q_R)_ζ(F_R(u))F_R'(u)du
    =(1/π)∫_{Ω_R}(Q_R)_{ζbarζ}dA ∈ ℝ.
The integrand is (r+K)F_R'/F_R. Its residue at zero is r+K(0). Thus K(0) is real as well as purely imaginary, and K(0)=0. It follows that
  S_R(ζ)=[r+K(F_R^(-1)(ζ))]/ζ
is meromorphic on Ω_R with principal part r/ζ and agrees with (Q_R)_ζ on the boundary. Set H_R=S_R−r/ζ, which is holomorphic at zero, and define
  U_R(ζ)=2r log|ζ|+2 Re ∫_0^ζ H_R(w)dw+c_R.
Because (U_R)_ζ=(Q_R)_ζ on the boundary, Q_R−U_R is constant there. Choose c_R to make this constant zero. Then Q_R and U_R match in both value and gradient.

At R=0, F_0=u, H_0=0 and c_0=1, so U_0=1+2r log|ζ|. The interior gap
  |ζ|^(2r)−1−2r log|ζ|
is strictly positive for 0<|ζ|<1. For small R the perturbed gap is positive throughout Ω_R. To check the boundary part of this assertion, let n be the normal. Since the gap and its gradient vanish on ∂Ω_R, its tangential Hessian and mixed Hessian vanish there. U_R is harmonic off the pole, so its normal second derivative at the boundary is ΔQ_R, bounded below by a positive constant. A uniform C3 bound gives strict positivity in a fixed inner boundary collar. Continuity gives positivity on compact interior sets away from zero and that collar. Near zero the term −2r log|ζ| dominates all uniformly bounded regular parts. These three regions prove the assertion.

Patch W_R=U_R on Ω_R and W_R=Q_R outside Ω_R. It is C1 across the boundary and subharmonic in distributions: its Laplacian is 4πrδ_0 inside and ΔQ_R outside, with no boundary measure. It is an admissible minorant with pole 2r log|ζ|+O(1), and equals Q_R near the outer boundary. It is the maximal such minorant. Indeed, for any other minorant v, v−U_R is subharmonic on Ω_R\{0}, bounded above near zero, and nonpositive on the boundary. Its removable subharmonic extension satisfies the maximum principle. Outside Ω_R, v≤Q_R=W_R already. Thus the noncontact set is exactly Ω_R, with no additional components.

## w47-1/n4

Inspected source: [Demailly, Multiplier ideal sheaves and analytic methods in algebraic geometry](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~demailly/manuscripts/trieste.pdf), Theorem (5.2) and its following paragraph, printed p. 33. On a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifold, a positive Hermitian line bundle F with curvature eigenvalues γ_j admits solutions ∂̄f=g for closed F-valued (n,q)-forms satisfying the finite inverse-curvature integral, with

∫|f|²≤∫(γ₁+⋯+γ_q)^{-1}|g|².

The following paragraph explicitly extends this to “a singular metric with positive curvature in the sense of currents.” Corollary (5.3) gives the uniform bound 1/(qε) under iΘ(F)≥εω. The preceding page includes compact manifolds among the allowed spaces.

The section formulation is obtained as follows. Let E have singular squared norm e^{-ψ}, put ω=ν, and equip F=E⊗K_C^{-1} with the induced metric. Require globally

iΘ(E)+Ric(ω)=iΘ(F)≥λω,

where λ is a continuous strictly positive lower bound, interpreted as an inequality of currents. Through the canonical isometric identification of E-valued (0,j)-forms with F-valued (1,j)-forms, the estimate becomes

∂̄u=η,   ∫_C|u|_E²ω≤∫_C λ^{-1}|η|_{E,ω}²ω.

In a coordinate with ω=a dA, Ric(ω)=−i∂∂̄log a. On a smooth locus, the curvature eigenvalue is

λ_actual=(2/a)[ψ_{z z̄}−(log a)_{z z̄}].

For η=b d z̄⊗e, |η|²=(2/a)|b|²e^{-ψ}. Thus, when this actual eigenvalue is positive, the local contribution to the right side is

a|b|²e^{-ψ}/[ψ_{z z̄}−(log a)_{z z̄}] dA.

For Euclidean area this recovers the denominator ψ_{z z̄}, with constant one. Equivalently, c₁(E)+Ric(ω)/(2π)≥κω gives λ=2πκ and the inverse factor 1/(2πκ). Curvature atoms are positive measures, not pointwise eigenvalues.

Applicability to an obstacle metric requires checking this global twisted curvature condition. The quoted strict-positive version does not directly address a metric whose curvature vanishes throughout the hole; an auxiliary metric or a justified semipositive extension is still needed in that case.

## w103-1/n2

GLOBAL SINGULAR METRIC AND THE COMPACT SECTION ESTIMATE

Use the hole and obstacle from n1. The locally defined φ_t patches to a singular metric on L because it equals the original potential outside a small chart. It satisfies φ_t≤φ and is C1 across Γ_t. Its curvature current is zero on D_t\{x}, the original curvature outside the hole, and tδ_x at x. Formula (1) of n1 verifies preservation of total curvature mass. For L^m, mφ_t has logarithmic singularity 2q log|z|+O(1).

Choose a fixed smooth β on the plane, equal to |ζ|² for |ζ|≤1.4 and zero for |ζ|≥2.5. Let b_R(z)=β(z/R), extended by zero on C. The hole lies within |z|<1.1R for small R. The function b_R is uniformly bounded, its Hessian is O(R^−2), and its negative Hessian is supported outside |z|≤1.4R, hence outside the hole. On the inner disk, (b_R)_zbarz=R^−2.

The Taylor hypothesis gives, on a fixed punctured neighborhood,
  ρ(z)=r²|z|^(2r−2)(1+O(|z|)).
Thus original curvature near exterior points with |z|≈R is comparable to q/R², because mR^(2r)=q/r. On the bump's negative annulus the same is true, and q→∞ absorbs the O(R^−2) negative part. On all remaining points of the small chart outside radius 1.1R, the original curvature is at least c q/R². Off a fixed neighborhood of x it is at least c m. Finally
  mR²=r^(−1/r)m^(1−1/r)q^(1/r)→∞
for every r≥1, including r=1, when this equals q. Consequently the metric with potential
  ψ=mφ_t+b_R
has globally strictly positive twisted curvature after inclusion of the bounded Ricci term. It is at least c/R² everywhere, and at least cq/R² on exterior annuli |z|≈R. The positive atom at x only improves the current inequality.

The section estimate used here is the classical singular-metric positive-curvature ∂bar estimate in the exact formulation recorded in w47-1/n4. Equip F=L^m⊗K_C^−1 with the metric induced by ψ and ν. The condition is
  iΘ(L^m,ψ)+Ric(ν)≥λν,
where λ is a continuous positive lower bound. Such a λ can be chosen ≥c/R² globally and ≥cq/R² on the source region. On a smooth chart ν=a dA its actual eigenvalue is
  (2/a)[ψ_zbarz−(log a)_zbarz].
The Ricci term is therefore bounded independently of m,R; the preceding curvature estimates check the theorem's global hypothesis, rather than applying a strict-positive theorem to a flat hole.

For a smooth L^m-valued (0,1)-form α, the theorem gives a linear minimal-weighted-norm solution u of ∂bar u=α with
  ∫|u|²e^(−ψ)dν ≤ ∫ λ^(−1)|α|²_(ψ,ν)dν.          (2)
In dimension one there is no further closedness obstruction for a (0,1)-form. Singular metrics with positive curvature currents are included in that theorem. For α supported outside the hole at |z|≈R, φ_t=φ on its support and b_R is bounded. Moreover φ_t≤φ everywhere implies e^(−mφ)≤e^C e^(−ψ). The volume and form-metric factors are uniformly bounded on this region. Therefore
  ||u||²_original ≤ C(R²/q)||α||²_original.       (3)
There is no factor depending polynomially on m. This remains useful even when q grows slower than every power of m.

If α vanishes near x, the solution is distributionally holomorphic there. Its singular weighted norm controls its ordinary local L² norm, so Weyl's lemma gives a holomorphic extension at x. A holomorphic term z^ℓ is integrable against |z|^−2q dA exactly when ℓ≥q. Therefore u vanishes to order at least q. Subtracting u from a cutoff local holomorphic section of order at least q produces a global section in V_m.

The same bounded bump added to the ORIGINAL metric mφ, instead of the obstacle metric, provides the identical estimates near |z|≈R for construction of full Bergman peaks. The singular obstacle version is used when the correction must enforce the order-q condition.

The only external analytic theorem in this note is (2), supported by w47-1/n4; all its compactness, strict positivity, Ricci, source-support and integrability hypotheses have been checked here.

## w103-1/n3

CORRECTED MESOSCOPIC EDGE THEOREM, WITH A COMPLETE KERNEL PROOF

Use n1 and n2. Throughout q→∞, q=o(m), t=q/m, R=(q/(mr))^(1/(2r)), and ρ=φ_zbarz. Let d_t(z) be signed Euclidean distance to Γ_t, positive outside, and p_t(z) its nearest boundary point in the local tubular neighborhood. For every fixed M,
  π a(z) B_m(z)/(mρ(z))
    =Φ(2√(mρ(p_t(z))) d_t(z))+o(1)               (4)
uniformly for |d_t(z)|≤MR/√q and in all angular variables. Also, if d_t(z)=o(R) and √q d_t(z)/R→−∞ or +∞, that normalized density tends respectively to 0 or 1. These two statements imply the sequential corrected formula with difference tending to zero for every d_t(z)=o(R), even when the Gaussian argument is unbounded.

1. Straightening and the boundary gap.
Write the interior obstacle in scaled coordinates as
  U_R(ζ)=2r log|ζ|+2 Re G_R(ζ),
and let F_R:D→Ω_R be the normalized map of n1. Define the holomorphic function
  A_R(w)=G_R(F_R(w))+r log(F_R(w)/w),
using the branch of the logarithm near 1. Then F_R=w+O(R) and A_R=1/2+O(R) in every specified finite boundary norm. On the unit disk define
  Ψ_R(w)=Q_R(F_R(w))−2 Re A_R(w)−2r log|w|.
This is exactly Q_R−U_R pulled back by F_R. It is positive inside, has zero value and gradient on the circle, and for w=e^(v+iθ),
  Ψ_R=(2r²+O(R))v²+O(v³).                       (5)
The coefficient more precisely is
  2(Q_R)_{ζbarζ}(F_R(e^(iθ)))|F_R'(e^(iθ))|².
Indeed the normal Hessian of the gap is ΔQ_R=4(Q_R)_{ζbarζ}, and the Taylor coefficient is half of this times the conformal length factor. All estimates are uniform in θ.

2. Smooth boundaries do not give holomorphic exterior extension; use polynomials.
Set K=⌊q^(1/16)⌋ and δ=c/K with a fixed small c>0. Truncate the Taylor series of F_R,A_R at K, giving F_K,A_K. For any fixed prescribed integer N and derivative order, smooth Fourier coefficient decay gives errors O(q^−N) on the closed disk, provided a sufficiently high but finite Ck norm is used. Such a norm is uniformly available eventually by n1. Thus there is no assumption of an analytic weight or holomorphic continuation of F_R across the circle.

On |w|≤1+δ, the polynomials and every required finite derivative are uniformly bounded; moreover F_K−w and A_K−1/2 are O(R). To see this, Taylor coefficient estimates are O(R)(1+j)^−k for the differences, and (1+c/K)^j≤e^c for j≤K. In particular sup|F_K'−1|=O(R), so F_K is univalent there. Put
  Ψ_K=Q_R(F_K)−2 Re A_K−2r log|w|.
Choosing N=10, for example, its value and first normal derivative on the circle are O(q^−N), and
  Ψ_K=(2r²+O(R)+O(|v|))v²+O(q^−N)(1+|v|),       (6)
for |v|≤δ. On the closed unit disk it differs from Ψ_R by O(q^−N). The true interior gap has uniform positive quadratic growth in a circle collar, is uniformly positive on interior compact sets away from zero, and grows like −2r log|w| at zero.

3. The Hardy representation and Gaussian annulus.
In the coordinate z=R F_K(w), any local section of order at least q can be uniquely written, in the given frame, as
  s=w^q exp((q/r)A_K(w)) f(w),                    (7)
where f is holomorphic and has only nonnegative Taylor powers. The pointwise norm is
  |s|²_h^m=|f(w)|² exp(−(q/r)Ψ_K(w)).             (8)
The volume element is
  R² a(RF_K(w))|F_K'(w)|²dA_w=R²(1+O(R))dA_w.
The identity mR^(2r)=q/r has been used in (8).

Let η=√(rq)v and consider an observation with η_0 bounded. On each fixed band |η|≤L, (6) gives
  (q/r)Ψ_K=2η²+o(1),
uniformly in angle. The actual norm restricted to this band is consequently 1+o(1) times a diagonal quadratic form on f=∑_{j≥0}b_jw^j with monomial norms
  n_{j,L}=(2πR²/√(rq)) I_L(j/√(rq)),
  I_L(s)=∫_{−L}^L exp(2sη−2η²)dη.               (9)
The Euclidean area factor e^(2v) can be suppressed by a relative 1+o(1) on this fixed band, uniformly for all f.

4. Upper kernel bound.
Restricting the global norm gives an upper bound for its evaluation kernel. Using (8) and (9), division by rq/(πR²) and passage to a Riemann sum of mesh 1/√(rq) give
  limsup πR² B_m/(rq)
    ≤(1/2)e^(−2η_0²)∫_0^∞ e^(2sη_0)/I_L(s)ds.  (10)
This passage controls all j, not merely bounded j/√q. Fix L_0 larger than the observation bound by at least two. Integrating I_L(s) on an outer subinterval of [−L_0,L_0], for L≥L_0, gives a uniform exponential majorant for the integrand as s→∞. The same majorant controls the Riemann-sum tails. Now increase L. Since
  I_∞(s)=√(π/2)e^(s²/2),
the right side of (10) tends to
  (1/√(2π))∫_0^∞ exp(−(s−2η_0)²/2)ds=Φ(2η_0).  (11)
The argument is uniform in θ and bounded η_0; for varying bounded observations one can equivalently pass to convergent η_0 subsequences.

5. Lower bound and operator-norm control.
Fix T. Use (7) with f=w^j, 0≤j≤T√(rq), multiplied by a radial cutoff equal to one for |w|≤1+δ/2 and zero for |w|≥1+δ. The source of ∂bar is outside the true hole: F_K differs from F_R on the circle by O(q^−N), whereas δ≫q^−N. Its derivative has size C/(Rδ).

  n_j=(2πR²/√(rq))√(π/2)exp(j²/(2rq)).           (12)
There is no dimension factor.

The same radial envelopes on the cutoff annulus, where qδ²→∞ and jδ≤T√(rq)δ≪qδ², give

    ≤C(Rδ)^−2 exp(−c qδ²)||s||²_trial
as a quadratic-form inequality on the whole span. Polynomial factors, if introduced in the tail estimate, are absorbed by decreasing c. All factors involve only q,δ,R, not m separately. Applying n2 gives a linear correction u, vanishing to order q, with

  |v(z)|²_h^m≤C(q/R²)||v||²_original.            (14)
For clarity, take a disk of radius R/√q. Remove by a holomorphic exponential the constant, linear, and pure quadratic harmonic Taylor terms of mφ.   v=d_t(z)/(R|F_R'(e^(iθ))|)+O(d_t(z)²/R²)+O(q^−N).

  mρ(z)/a(z)=(rq/R²)(1+o(1)).

7. Full density and saturation.
For reference, uniformly at |z|≈R the full Bergman kernel is
  B_m^full(z)=mρ(z)/(πa(z))(1+o(1)).              (15)
A direct proof uses the same weighted Taylor expansion as (14), now rescaling by (mρ(z))^−1/2. Restriction to a fixed Gaussian disk and the radial evaluation kernel at its center give the upper bound; then the disk radius is increased.   B_m(z)≤C(√q/R²)(1−|w|²)^−1 exp(−cqu²).
After division by q/R² this is bounded by C(√qu)^−1 exp(−cqu²), tending to zero.

For exterior saturation take z outside with d=o(R) and √qd/R→∞. Center the Gaussian peak just used at z and choose

Then ε→0, √qε→∞, and its entire cutoff disk lies outside D_t. Apply the SINGULAR obstacle estimate of n2 to its source. Equation (15) supplies the upper bound, and the ratio tends to one.

All error estimates are uniform in angle. Every required smoothness order in the truncation and Gram argument is finite and fixed in advance, so the precise finite-Ck geometry of n1 suffices. The theorem therefore applies to arbitrary original smooth weights and every sublinear q, including q slower than any power of m and q approaching m at any sublinear rate. It uses no separate edge-kernel theorem and no unrestricted free-boundary stability theorem.

  √q ||c_R−1||_∞→0.                            (18)
Sufficiency follows exactly as above. For necessity, if this fails choose a subsequence and angles where the absolute graph displacement attains its maximum. At τ=0 the observations are on |z|=R. If √q(c_R−1) tends to a finite nonzero number along a further subsequence, n3 gives a nonzero Gaussian shift and hence a limit different from 1/2. If it is unbounded, take a further one-sign subsequence; the saturation conclusion of n3 gives 0 or 1. At a graph extremum the radial and normal distances agree to the requisite leading order; alternatively the uniformly O(R) graph slope gives the same conclusion. All these points are an o(R) distance from Γ. Complete the observations arbitrarily on remaining indices. This violates the asserted every-sequence limit and proves necessity.

II. Sharp compact counterexamples for all r and every excluded sequence.

On CP1, in coordinate w, choose L=O(d) for a sufficiently large integer d. Construct a smooth radial potential f(u), u=|w|², such that f(u)=u^r near zero and F(u)=uf'(u) is strictly increasing for u>0, tending to d. Arrange F(u)=d−ε/u for large u. A strictly increasing smooth interpolation exists with d sufficiently large. Then
  f(u)=d log u+constant+ε/u
near infinity. In the infinity coordinate v=1/w and the corresponding O(d) frame, the potential is constant+ε|v|², so curvature is strictly positive there. At every finite w≠0 it is positive since ρ=F'(u)>0. Near zero it is r²|w|^(2r−2); r=1 is allowed and is positive also at zero. Choose a smooth positive radial volume equal to dA near zero.

Now use the local coordinate z defined by w=z+cz², with c>0 real. Its derivative at zero is one. Consequently a_z(0)=1 and
  φ(z)=|z+cz²|^(2r)=|z|^(2r)+O(|z|^(2r+1)),
so all original hypotheses hold. In the w-coordinate the exact small-t obstacle hole is |w|<R, since f(u)=u^r there. In the z-coordinate its boundary is |z+cz²|=R. At the raw observation z=R on the positive real axis, τ=0, the boundary lies at R−cR²+O(R³), and
  2√(mρ_z(p))d=2c√r (√qR)(1+O(R)).             (19)
If q is not in (16), pass to a subsequence with √qR→κ∈(0,∞]. By n3 the normalized density tends to Φ(2c√r κ), or to 1 for κ=∞, instead of 1/2. Its original denominator differs from the curvature denominator only by a factor 1+O(R). This disproves universality for EVERY excluded growth sequence, including oscillatory sequences by subsequences, and for EVERY r≥1. This is not restricted to a Fubini–Study example.

III. Complete correction theorem for arbitrary smooth weights.

For every q→∞, q=o(m), use the center Γ_{q/m} defined by the actual local obstacle. It satisfies the weighted quadrature identities
  ∫_{D_{q/m}}ρ(z)f(z)dA=π(q/m)f(0).
At a boundary point p the normal transition length is
  ℓ(p)=1/(2√(mρ(p))),                          (20)
and the leading density is mρ(z)/(πa(z)). The theorem n3 says, for bounded corrected Gaussian argument and uniformly in angle,
  πa(z)B_m(z)/(mρ(z))−Φ(d_{q/m}(z)/ℓ(p))→0.    (21)
It also gives saturation to 0 or 1 on the inner or outer side when d=o(R) but |d|/ℓ→∞. Together these give the sequential formula (21) for every d=o(R), including unbounded argument. This is a complete correction theorem for arbitrary original smooth weights throughout all sublinear growth. The center can be noncircular. Further terms can move it by many transition widths. Curvature determines the scale; its relative change from the leading scale tends to zero because ρ(p)=r²R^(2r−2)(1+O(R)). The smooth volume changes leading density by 1/a and can give lower-order effects, but does not change this limiting center or scale.

IV. Explicit first critical correction and all finite effective jets.

Write the degree-2r+1 Taylor expansion as
  φ(z)=|z|^(2r)+P_{2r+1}(z,bar z)+O(|z|^(2r+2)),
and let P(θ) be the restriction of that homogeneous polynomial to the unit circle. Define Λ(e^(inθ))=|n|e^(inθ), the circle Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator. The boundary expansion is
  c_R(θ)=1+Rb_1(θ)+O(R²),
  b_1=−[((2r+1)−Λ)P]/(4r²).                   (22)
To derive it, write the interior potential as 1+2r log s+R H+O(R²), where H is harmonic, and the boundary as s=1+Rb_1. Value matching at order R gives H|_{s=1}=P: the b_1 terms cancel because the leading weight and logarithmic obstacle have the same derivative 2r. Thus H_s|_1=ΛP. Derivative matching gives
  4r²b_1+(2r+1)P−ΛP=0.
Harmonic Taylor terms, the modes of frequency ±(2r+1), disappear as they must because they can be removed by a holomorphic frame change.

If q=O(m^(1/(r+1))), then √qR is bounded and √qR²→0. At the original observation (17), the corrected argument is uniformly
  τ−2√(rq)Rb_1(θ)+o(1)
   =τ+[√qR/(2r^(3/2))][((2r+1)−Λ)P](θ)+o(1).  (23)
Consequently, without requiring the observation angles to converge,
  πB_m(z)/(mr²|z|^(2r−2))
   −Φ(τ+[√qR/(2r^(3/2))][((2r+1)−Λ)P](θ))→0.  (24)
The coordinate example has P=2r Re(ce^(iθ)), b_1=−Re(ce^(iθ)), and (23) agrees with (19). A smooth radial weight has no degree-2r+1 radial term, explaining why radial examples alone miss the sharp universal threshold.

For each fixed J, the finite-order parameter dependence proved in n1 gives
  c_R=1+∑_{j=1}^J R^j b_j+O(R^(J+1))           (25)
in every fixed required norm. The b_j depend only on the Taylor jet of φ through degree 2r+j and are unaffected by addition of harmonic terms. They can be computed recursively, not merely defined by a formal extrapolation: expand the harmonic interior part as 1+2r log s+∑R^jH_j. At order j, value matching determines H_j on the unit circle from the current Taylor term and the lower coefficients; harmonic extension determines H_j. The new b_j does not enter this value equation because of the leading first-order contact. Derivative matching is then
  4r² b_j+(known forcing)=0.
All finite-order forcings are finite trigonometric polynomials, so their harmonic extensions cause no exterior-extension issue in this recursion.

Replacing Γ by the curve containing b_1,...,b_J has physical center error O(R^(J+2)), negligible relative to (20) whenever
  √q R^(J+1)→0,
  equivalently q=o(m^((J+1)/(r+J+1))).          (26)
Using its signed normal distance in (21) is therefore a complete finite-jet correction in this range. At larger growth the next omitted effective coefficient need not be negligible.

If the first nonzero effective boundary coefficient is b_j, then (18) and (25) give the sharp fixed-weight all-direction uncorrected range
  √qR^j→0, equivalently q=o(m^(j/(r+j))).       (27)
At critical growth the shift is −2√(rq)R^j b_j; above it some direction saturates. If all earlier b_k vanish, their Taylor terms are harmonic after the earlier harmonic terms have been removed, and the first coefficient is the simple linear expression
  b_j=−[((2r+j)−Λ)P_{2r+j}]/(4r²).
In general the recursion includes nonlinear contributions from lower nonharmonic Taylor terms. A rule based solely on the first nonzero raw Taylor term, without these effective coefficients, would be incorrect.

V. Radial formulas, and why finite jets are insufficient for all sublinear q.

If φ(z)=f(u), u=|z|², then ρ(u)=f'(u)+uf''(u). The exact geometric center is the disk of squared radius u_* solving
  m u_* f'(u_*)=q.                            (28)
Its curvature mass is u_*f'(u_*). A convenient equivalent corrected argument near this center is
  [m u f'(u)−q]/√(m uρ(u)).                    (29)
For bounded (29), Taylor expansion of F(u)=uf'(u), whose derivative is ρ(u), shows that (29) equals 2√(mρ(u_*))(|z|−√u_*)+o(1). Hence (29) and (28) give a complete radial correction theorem throughout q=o(m), by (21).

The quantity σ²(u)=m uρ(u) is the leading mode variance, or saddle Hessian, not an assertion about the exact finite-m probabilistic variance. In the radial monomial norm, with v=log u, the exponent is (k+1)v−mf(e^v). Its second derivative is −m uρ(u), explaining this variance and the harmless order-one mode-centering offset. For f=u^r, (28) yields mrR^(2r)=q and σ²(R²)=rq. For f=u^r+c u^(r+1)+... the exact center equation and leading variance are
  m[r u_*^r+c(r+1)u_*^(r+1)+...]=q,
  σ²(u)=m[r²u^r+c(r+1)²u^(r+1)+...].
If c≠0 the first radial critical growth is q of order m^(2/(r+2)); the raw-observation argument acquires shift
  c(r+1)r^(−3/2)√qR².

No fixed finite Taylor jet determines the edge to its width for every q=o(m). For example, near zero take
  f(u)=u^r+c u^r exp(−u^(−1/2)),  c>0,
with the additional term extended smoothly as zero at zero. Its jets are identical to those of u^r. Curvature is positive in a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood; a monotone radial interpolation as in II makes a compact example. At u=R² the relative correction to uf'(u) is
  c exp(−1/R)[1+1/(2rR)].
For q of order m/log m, R is of order (log m)^(-1/(2r)), and √q exp(−1/R)/R→∞. The raw τ=0 circle is an o(R) distance outside the true center but many transition widths away, so its normalized density tends to 1 rather than 1/2. Every finite Taylor model predicted the unchanged center. The exact obstacle correction (21), unlike those finite models, remains valid.

VI. Constant and convention checks, including r=1.

The constants established in n3, also checked by radial integration, are:
  dd^cφ=(ρ/π)dA;
  dd^c(2t log|z|)=tδ_x;
  full Bergman density mρ/(πa);
  leading ρ=r²|z|^(2r−2);
  ∫_{|z|<R}ρ dA=πrR^(2r);
  center mrR^(2r)=q;
  leading mode variance at the radial center rq;
  Gaussian argument 2√(rq)(|z|/R−1)=τ+o(1);
  normal transition length 1/(2√(mρ));
  ∫_ℂ |z|^(2k)e^(−m|z|^(2r))dA
     =(π/r)m^(-(k+1)/r)Γ((k+1)/r).
In the conformal Gaussian-ring calculation the exponent is 2η², the ring norm integral is √(π/2)e^(s²/2), and the limiting half-line integral is precisely Φ(2η). Thus there is no missing π, r or factor-two in the displayed law. All estimates and constructions apply for r=1 without change. Since deg L>0 and q=o(m), Riemann–Roch also ensures V_m≠0 eventually, so the zero-space convention has no asymptotic effect.

The formerly known q=o(m^(1/(2r+1))) range compares the weight error mR^(2r+1)≈qR directly. This misses the holomorphic gauge and first-order boundary cancellation. The relevant edge displacement is instead √qR, yielding (16).

Conclusion: (16) is the optimal universal range, (18) is the exact metric-dependent all-direction criterion, and (20)–(21) are the complete general smooth-weight center-and-scale correction for all sublinear q. Equations (22)–(29) are rigorous finite-jet and radial refinements; the compact and flat examples demonstrate sharpness and limitations, rather than substituting for the general correction theorem.
