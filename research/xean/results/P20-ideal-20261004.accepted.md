NORMALIZATIONS AND ANALYTIC INPUTS.
Set p=(0,∞), q=(∞,0), and write dd^c=(i/(2π))∂bar∂. Thus dd^c log|ζ|²=δ_0 in one variable, and ω_FS=dd^c log(1+|ζ|²) has integral 1 on CP1. A local metric potential φ_h means |e|_h²=e^{-φ_h}; consequently a local genuine psh potential of v is v+φ_h. Adding a smooth function does not change a multiplier ideal locally.

The standard elementary psh facts used have the following scope: on a CONNECTED complex manifold, a genuine psh function that is not identically -∞ is locally integrable; its canonical representative satisfies submean inequalities and is determined by its almost-everywhere values. Restrictions to connected complex submanifolds are psh or identically -∞; we only use their local integrability after establishing the former alternative. A psh function that is -∞ on a nonempty open subset of a connected domain is identically -∞. Thus, for a nontrivial global θ-psh v on connected X, no frame ball has identically -∞ potential. The identically -∞ global competitor is harmless and satisfies both constraints below.

Here is the precise non-elementary analytic input required. Weighted point extension on balls: if B_R⊂C^n is a Euclidean ball, 0<r<R, Φ is genuine psh on B_R, and a∈B_r with Φ(a)>-∞, then there is F∈O(B_R) with F(a)=1 and
 ∫_{B_R}|F|²e^{-Φ}dλ ≤ C_{n,R,r}e^{-Φ(a)}.                    (A)
The constant does not depend on Φ or a. This is a consequence of the weighted L² hyperplane extension theorem of T. Ohsawa and K. Takegoshi, “On the extension of L² holomorphic functions,” Math. Z. 195 (1987), 197–204. The exact primary-source formulation needed is: square-integrable holomorphic data on a complex hyperplane in a bounded pseudoconvex domain extend with their e^{-Φ} norm bounded by a constant independent of the psh weight, controlled by the domain's bounded normal coordinate. Only Euclidean balls and their affine slices are needed here. To derive (A), choose a flag of affine complex subspaces through a and extend successively from dimension zero to dimension n. Every slice is a connected ball, and every restricted weight is non-identically -∞ because it is finite at a. Their diameters are bounded by 2R, and a stays a uniformly positive distance from their boundaries when |a|≤r. The starting zero-dimensional norm is e^{-Φ(a)}. This proves the stated corollary with uniform constants.

This specifies the external theorem and the entire reduction actually used; no optimal extension constant is needed. All weights in this argument are exactly e^{-Φ}. If a source writes its weight as e^{-2φ}, its application here is φ=Φ/2. This relabeling leaves plurisubharmonicity unchanged. The curvature normalization dd^c does not enter (A), and there is no unexplained factor of two in the multiplier-ideal exponent. The primary-source dependency is the hyperplane extension theorem just stated, not a Bergman asymptotic, valuative ideal theorem, or envelope convergence theorem.

1. EXACT MULTIPLIER IDEALS OF ψ.
Near p use coordinates u=z1, v=1/z2 and frame T1 Z2. The two pencil sections T1T2 and Z1Z2 have coefficients v and u, respectively. The metric potential is
 φ_h=log(1+|u|²)+log(1+|v|²),
so
 ψ=log max(|u|²,|v|²)-φ_h.
The same formula holds at q after exchanging the two factors. Elsewhere ψ is locally bounded.

The maximum squared norm and |u|²+|v|² are comparable. If a nonzero holomorphic germ f has order k, its spherical squared mean is comparable, for small radius, to r^{2k}: the leading homogeneous polynomial has strictly positive spherical squared integral, and higher terms contribute lower-order errors. Integrability against e^{-tψ} is therefore equivalent to convergence of
 ∫_0^ε r^{2k-2t+3}dr,
which occurs exactly when k+2>t, with logarithmic divergence at equality. Hence
 I(tψ)_p=m_p^{k(t)},  I(tψ)_q=m_q^{k(t)},
 k(t)=max(0,floor(t)-1),                                      (1)
and I(tψ)=O away from p,q. In particular, I(mψ) has order m-1 at both poles for each integer m≥1.

2. RECOVERING THE LOGARITHMIC POLE FROM IDEAL CONTAINMENT.
We give the local argument, including its endpoint. Let φ be non-identically -∞ genuine psh on a connected ball centered at 0. Shrink the ball so that φ≤M on it; its closure lies in the original domain. Suppose every germ in I(tφ)_0 has order at least k. Every holomorphic f of finite weighted norm
 ||f||_t²=∫_B |f|²e^{-tφ}dλ
then has its jets of orders below k equal to zero. Since ||f||_{L²(B)}²≤e^{tM}||f||_t², the holomorphic L² Cauchy estimate and its Taylor remainder give, on a smaller fixed ball,
 |f(z)|²≤A_{t,k}|z|^{2k}||f||_t².                            (2)
For example this follows by expanding in Taylor monomials orthogonal on the ball: the terms of degrees <k vanish, and the remaining evaluation sum is bounded by a constant times |z|^{2k} on a smaller ball.

For φ(z)>-∞ apply (A) with Φ=tφ and a=z. Substitute the resulting f_z, with f_z(z)=1, into (2). This yields
 φ(z)≤(k/t)log|z|²+C_{t,k}.                                  (3)
At points with φ=-∞ it is automatic. If k>0, φ(0) cannot be finite: extension at 0 would contradict the forced vanishing. Thus the conclusion has the correct pointwise value at 0 also. No constant uniform in t or k is asserted or needed.

For the endpoint, put M_φ(s)=sup_{|z|≤e^{s/2}}φ(z), for sufficiently negative s. This is finite and nondecreasing. The one-variable three-circles theorem on each complex line through 0 shows it is convex: it is the supremum of the convex radial maxima on these lines. Restrictions that are identically -∞ do not affect that supremum. Let ν be its limiting left slope as s→-∞. It exists, is nonnegative and finite by convexity. If (3) holds with coefficient c, then M_φ(s)≤cs+C, which implies ν≥c. Also convexity gives
 M_φ(s)≤M_φ(s0)+ν(s-s0),  s<s0:                              (4)
the secant slope ending at s0 is at least the limiting left slope. In particular, if the coefficients in (3) approach 1, then ν≥1, and (4) implies φ(z)≤log|z|²+C. This is an endpoint bound; it does not take a limit of the uncontrolled constants in (3).

3. FULL IDEAL CLASS EQUALS THE ORDINARY BOUNDED-ABOVE TYPE CLASS.
Let v be any nontrivial genuine θ-psh function with I(tv)⊂I(tψ) for every real t>0. At either pole set φ=v+φ_h on its connected frame ball. The smooth metric potential leaves its multiplier ideals unchanged. Equations (1)–(3) apply with k=k(t), and k(t)/t→1 as t→∞. The endpoint argument gives
 v+φ_h≤log(|u|²+|v|²)+C.
Since log(|u|²+|v|²) differs from log max(|u|²,|v|²) by at most log 2, this is v≤ψ+C near each pole. On a compact complement of smaller pole neighborhoods, ψ is continuous and finite and v is bounded above. Combining these bounds gives one finite global C_v with v≤ψ+C_v.

Conversely, v≤ψ+C_v implies
 e^{-tψ}≤e^{tC_v}e^{-tv}.
Every germ integrable against the latter is integrable against the former. Consequently, for ALL genuine θ-psh functions, with no invariance assumption,
 {v:v≤_Iψ}={v:v≤ψ+C_v for some finite C_v}.                    (5)
Both sets contain the identically -∞ function if it is allowed. Equality holds before imposing any obstacle, so all their obstacle-restricted suprema and USC regularizations agree. In particular their envelopes agree for every smooth obstacle on all X, as well as for the compact-obstacle problem in the question.

4. COMPLETE NON-TORIC PENCIL DESCRIPTION.
Off p,q define
 F=[T1T2:Z1Z2],
 ψ_FS=log(|T1T2|_h²+|Z1Z2|_h²).
Then ψ≤ψ_FS≤ψ+log 2. On the torus, with Q=z1z2, s=log|Q|² and φ_X=log(1+e^{x1})+log(1+e^{x2}),
 ψ_FS=log(1+|Q|²)-φ_X.

For Q∈C*, the closed curve C_Q={Z1Z2=Q T1T2} is a connected smooth rational (1,1) curve, parameterized by z1 with z2=Q/z1. It passes through p and q. Since each projection has degree 1, ∫_{C_Q}θ=2. If v|C_Q is not identically -∞, it is a genuine θ|C_Q-psh function on a connected compact curve, hence locally integrable. The bound v≤ψ+C implies in a local parameter at either pole a bound log|ζ|²+O(1) for its local psh potential. The atom of the one-variable curvature measure is therefore at least 1 at each pole, by the elementary Jensen/Lelong slope formula. The curvature measure θ|C_Q+dd^c(v|C_Q) has total mass 2 by Stokes. It must be exactly δ_p+δ_q.

On the same curve, T1T2 has precisely two simple zeros, p and q, and the ratio Z1Z2/(T1T2) is constant. Thus θ|C_Q+dd^c(ψ|C_Q)=δ_p+δ_q also. It follows that v|C_Q-ψ|C_Q is distributionally harmonic and therefore a constant on the compact connected curve. Canonical representatives make this equality pointwise. Equivalently, v+φ_X is constant along its torus fiber.

The local integrability of v on the torus and Fubini in holomorphic coordinates (z1,Q) show that almost every fiber has non-identically -∞ restriction. Let S={z1=1}, identified with CP1 by Q=z2. Each of those good fibers has a finite value at its point on S, by the just-proved constancy. Therefore v|S is a nontrivial genuine ω_FS-psh function on connected S. Define u=v|S+log 2. Here θ|S=ω_FS and ψ_FS|S=-log 2, so the constants are fixed by the actual product metric.

The fiber calculation gives, almost everywhere on the torus,
 v=ψ_FS+u∘F.                                                (6)
The right side has genuine psh local potentials off p,q: its curvature is F*(ω_FS+dd^c u). Both sides have canonical representatives, hence (6) holds everywhere off p,q. At either base point, u is bounded above on compact CP1 and ψ_FS has a logarithmic pole; their psh removable extension has value -∞. The bound already proved for v gives the same value there. Thus (6) holds globally with these extensions.

Conversely, any nontrivial ω_FS-psh u on CP1 gives this construction. In a frame with pencil coefficients a,b, its local potential off the common zeros is
 log(|a|²+|b|²)+u([a:b]),
which is psh and bounded above by log(|a|²+|b|²)+C. The standard removable-extension principle for a locally bounded-above psh function across an isolated analytic set gives its canonical extension at the common zeros, with value -∞ by this bound. It satisfies v≤ψ+C and so belongs to both classes in (5). Thus (6) describes the entire class, including arbitrary angular dependence on the base. In affine base notation it reads
 v=χ(Q)-φ_X,
where χ(Q)=log(1+|Q|²)+u(Q) is subharmonic on C and χ(Q)≤max(0,log|Q|²)+C on C*.

## w97-1/n2

1. ENTIRE ACTUAL SPACES AND GROWTH.
For m≥1 a basis of H0(X,O(m,m)) is
 s_ab=Z1^a T1^{m-a} Z2^b T2^{m-b},  0≤a,b≤m.
At p, in the frame T1^m Z2^m, this is u^a v^{m-b}; at q it has order b+m-a. By n1 the required ideal orders are m-1 at both points. The conditions are exactly
 a+m-b≥m-1,  b+m-a≥m-1,
i.e. |a-b|≤1. For a general section, the local monomial exponents are distinct; terms of insufficient degree cannot cancel as polynomials. Therefore
 V_m=span{s_ab:0≤a,b≤m, |a-b|≤1}.                            (7)
There are m+1 diagonal elements and m elements on each adjacent diagonal, hence
 N_m=dim V_m=3m+1,  m≥1.                                   (8)
With the natural V_0=C this formula also gives N_0=1. The intrinsic Hilbert growth exponent lim(log N_m)/(log m) is 1, and N_m/m→3. Equivalently the leading dimension coefficient with this growth normalization is 3.

The selection at the single exponent m must not be confused with sections whose own potential (1/m)log|s|_{h^m}² belongs to n1's ordinary class. Those must have order at least m at both poles and are exactly the diagonal span, of dimension m+1. Equation (5) of n1 does not change this fact about the single-exponent section spaces. In particular the V_m are not a multiplicative graded ring: the square of Z1T2∈V_1 has offset 2 and is not in V_2. Ratios of suitable elements of V_m recover both z1 and z2 on the torus, so their rational-map images can have dimension 2. That does not change the growth exponent 1; graded-ring Iitaka assertions do not apply to this nonmultiplicative family.

2. EXACT ORIGINAL NORMS AND THE KERNEL.
Let
 φ_X(x)=log(1+e^{x1})+log(1+e^{x2}),
 H(x)=(x1²+x2²)/2,
 w=H-φ_X.
For s_ab one has
 |s_ab|_{h^m}²=exp(a x1+b x2-mφ_X).
Independent normalized angular Haar makes all distinct monomials orthogonal. Since the metric part of w cancels exactly, the ORIGINAL squared norm is
 A_m(a,b)=||s_ab||²=(1/9)I_m(a/m)I_m(b/m),                   (9)
 I_m(p)=∫_{-1}^2 exp(m(px-x²/2))dx
       =exp(mp²/2)J_m(p),
 J_m(p)=∫_{-1}^2 exp(-m(x-p)²/2)dx.
Thus s_ab/√A_m(a,b) over the complete set (7) is an orthonormal basis and
 B_m=exp(-mφ_X)∑_{(a,b) in (7)} exp(a x1+b x2)/A_m(a,b).     (10)
All angles disappear from the kernel.

For p∈[0,1] the center is at distance at least 1 from both endpoints of [-1,2]. Each omitted Gaussian tail is at most e^{-m/2}/m. Consequently, uniformly in all relevant p,
 J_m(p)=√(2π/m)(1+O(m^{-1/2}e^{-m/2})).                    (11)
The following nonasymptotic bounds, valid for every m≥1, are useful:
 c/√m≤J_m(p)≤√(2π/m),  c=∫_{-1}^1e^{-y²/2}dy.
For the lower bound integrate over [p-1/√m,p+1/√m], which is contained in [-1,2]. In particular
 (c²/(9m))exp((a²+b²)/(2m))≤A_m(a,b)
 ≤(2π/(9m))exp((a²+b²)/(2m)).                              (12)
No substituted Hilbert structure has been used.

3. NORMALIZED KERNEL-MEASURE LIMIT.
The probability trace measure associated with this Hilbert norm is
 μ_m=N_m^{-1}B_m e^{-mw}ν.                                  (13)
The weight e^{-mw} is essential; its total mass is exactly 1 by the orthonormal basis identity. Substitution of (9)–(10), including the original factor 1/9 in ν, shows that the contribution of each basis vector is precisely
 [e^{-m(x1-a/m)²/2}/J_m(a/m)]
 [e^{-m(x2-b/m)²/2}/J_m(b/m)] dx1 dx2
 ×dθ1/(2π)dθ2/(2π),
restricted to [-1,2]^2. Thus μ_m is the arithmetic average of these N_m product truncated-Gaussian probability measures.

Their masses outside any fixed positive-radius neighborhood of their centers tend to zero uniformly in the selected indices, by Gaussian tail bounds and the lower bound for J_m. For a continuous test function on K, uniform continuity reduces the integral to the average of its angularly averaged values at the centers, with an error tending to zero. The three bands have centers
 (j/m,j/m), 0≤j≤m;
 ((j+1)/m,j/m), 0≤j≤m-1;
 (j/m,(j+1)/m), 0≤j≤m-1.
Their three Riemann sums have the same limiting integral over t∈[0,1], and their relative cardinalities tend to 1/3 each. Therefore, weakly on X,
 μ_m→μ,
 ∫f dμ=∫_0^1∫_0^{2π}∫_0^{2π}
 f(e^{t/2+iθ1},e^{t/2+iθ2}) dθ1/(2π)dθ2/(2π)dt.             (14)
This is the probability measure dt times independent normalized angular Haar on x1=x2=t, 0≤t≤1. The measures normalized instead by the intrinsic power m satisfy
 m^{-1}B_m e^{-mw}ν→3μ.
Normalization by the ambient power m² yields zero. These statements account for both the intrinsic leading coefficient and the probability normalization.

## w97-1/n3

This note completes the exact task using only the fresh notes n1 and n2. It does not use any dead note. The only non-elementary existence input in those notes is the precise weighted hyperplane-extension theorem recorded in n1; the hypotheses, weight convention, point corollary, and connectedness restrictions used here are all specified there.

1. THE FULL IDEAL ENVELOPE, INCLUDING NON-TORIC COMPETITORS.
Put φ_X(x)=log(1+e^{x1})+log(1+e^{x2}) and s=x1+x2. Define
 g(s)=sup_{0≤p≤1}(ps-p²)
 =0 for s≤0,
 =s²/4 for 0≤s≤2,
 =s-1 for s≥2.                                             (15)
The answer on the torus is
 P(x)=g(x1+x2)-φ_X(x).                                      (16)
It has the canonical global θ-psh extension described below, with value -∞ at the two fixed poles.

To prove the upper bound for the FULL competitor class, let v be any nontrivial ideal competitor. By n1 it has the possibly non-toric form
 v=χ(Q)-φ_X,  Q=z1z2,
where χ is subharmonic in the affine base coordinate and has upper growth χ(Q)≤max(0,log|Q|²)+C. The identically -∞ competitor needs no argument.

Let Ω be the interior of K, given by -1<x_i<2. The quasi-everywhere obstacle inequality implies an almost-everywhere inequality on Ω, since pluripolar sets have zero Euclidean volume. In the torus frame, the genuine psh local potential v+φ_X is therefore at most H=(x1²+x2²)/2 almost everywhere. Submean on balls contained in Ω, followed by shrinking them and using continuity of H, proves this inequality at every point of Ω. This step uses the canonical representative explicitly; it would be false for arbitrary artificially raised representatives of a curvature distribution.

For any base point with s=log|Q|²∈(-2,4), choose |z1|²=|z2|²=e^{s/2} and angles with product Q. The resulting point lies in Ω and gives
 χ(Q)≤s²/4.                                                (17)
Let c(s)=max_{|Q|²=e^s}χ(Q). It is finite and convex by the subharmonic three-circles theorem. It cannot be -∞ on an entire circle: upper semicontinuity would bound χ by arbitrarily negative constants in a neighborhood of that circle, and the maximum principle would make it identically -∞ inside and then on its connected domain. Its growth satisfies c(s)≤max(0,s)+C. Every supporting slope therefore lies in [0,1]: a negative slope contradicts the bound at -∞, and a slope greater than 1 contradicts the bound at +∞. Continuity of finite convex functions extends (17) to [-2,4].

For any real s, select p∈∂c(s). Since 2p∈[0,2], the supporting-line inequality and (17) give
 c(s)+p(2p-s)≤c(2p)≤p².
Thus c(s)≤ps-p²≤g(s), and χ(Q)≤g(log|Q|²). This proves v≤P on the torus without assuming angular invariance of v. Away from the poles, P is continuous, so the canonical submean property extends the inequality across the coordinate divisors. At the poles both v and P have value -∞.

Conversely, g is convex with slopes between 0 and 1. The function χ_g(Q)=g(log|Q|²) is subharmonic on C*, is constant near 0, and near ∞ differs from log|Q|² by the constant -1. It therefore gives an ω_FS-psh function
 u_g=χ_g-log(1+|Q|²)
on CP1. Use n1's pencil construction to extend (16) globally as a genuine θ-psh function. The elementary bounds
 -1≤g(s)-max(0,s)≤0
show ψ-1≤P≤ψ, so P belongs to the ordinary class and, by n1, the full ideal class. Finally
 g(s)≤s²/4≤(x1²+x2²)/2,
so P≤w pointwise on K. It is an admissible competitor, while every competitor is at most P. Hence the actual supremum, and its USC regularization, equal (16). Near each pole P=log r²+O(1), so the regularization also has value -∞ there. This proves the full ideal-envelope identification, not merely a toric-restricted one.

2. GLOBAL LOWER BOUND FROM THE ACTUAL SECTION SPACES.
Use the original norms and the complete three-band basis in n2. Its diagonal elements are among the actual selected elements, and (12) gives
 A_m(j,j)≤(2π/(9m))e^{j²/m}.
Consequently
 B_m≥(9m/(2π))e^{-mφ_X}max_{0≤j≤m}exp(js-j²/m).
For all real s,
 max_{0≤j≤m}[(j/m)s-(j/m)²]≥g(s)-1/(4m²):
for s≤0 and s≥2 the exact maximizing endpoint belongs to the grid; for 0≤s≤2 choose a nearest grid point to s/2 and lose at most its squared distance 1/(4m²). Therefore
 U_m≥P+log(9m/(2π))/m-1/(4m²).                             (18)
This is a global full-envelope lower bound on the torus. By continuity it extends to X minus the poles, and at a pole its right side is -∞. The correction tends to zero independently of the point.

Using diagonal elements for this lower bound does not replace the actual spaces by a diagonal subseries. Their dimensions, norms, measure limits, and the upper bound below concern all three bands. Indeed U_m itself is generally not an ideal/ordinary competitor: its pole coefficient is 1-1/m. Thus one cannot obtain the required upper bound by treating each U_m as admissible for the envelope.

3. UPPER BOUND FOR ALL THREE BANDS.
The lower norm bound (12) implies
 B_m≤(9m/c²)e^{-mφ_X}
       ∑_{0≤a,b≤m, |a-b|≤1}exp(a x1+b x2-(a²+b²)/(2m)).
Diagonal exponents are at most m g(s). For a=j+1,b=j, 0≤j≤m-1, comparison with diagonal index j gives an upper bound m g(s)+x1; comparison with index j+1 gives m g(s)+1-x2. Therefore the whole exponent is bounded by
 m g(s)+log min(e^{x1},e^{1-x2}).
The opposite band has the bound obtained by exchanging x1 and x2. Set
 R(x)=1+min(e^{x1},e^{1-x2})+min(e^{x2},e^{1-x1}).           (19)
Counting the terms gives
 B_m≤(9m(m+1)/c²)R(x)e^{mP(x)},
and hence, with a fixed constant C,
 U_m≤P+[C+2log(m+1)+log R(x)]/m.                            (20)
This uses every actual selected section.

The function R has a finite continuous extension across the coordinate divisors away from the poles and is bounded on each compact subset of X\{p,q}. For example at z1=0 with z2 finite its value is 1+|z2|², and at z1=∞ with z2 finite it is 1+e/|z2|². At (0,0) and (∞,∞) it is 1. Near p write u=z1, v=1/z2, A=|u|², B=|v|². Then
 R=1+min(A,eB)+min(1/B,e/A).
The first minimum is bounded and
 min(1/B,e/A)≤2e/(A+B).
Thus R≤C/r² for r²=|u|²+|v|² sufficiently small; the same estimate holds near q. In particular log R is integrable against every smooth volume form on X. Estimates (18) and (20) extend off the torus by continuity wherever both potentials are finite.

4. VALID CONVERGENCE TOPOLOGIES AND THE POLE LIMITATION.
The preceding bounds prove:
(a) U_m→P uniformly on every compact subset of X\{p,q}, with O(log m/m) error there. This includes coordinate-divisor points away from the poles, not just dense-torus compact sets.
(b) U_m→P globally in L¹ with respect to any smooth volume form, at rate O(log m/m), by integration of (18) and (20) and the integrability of log R. Values at the two points do not affect this assertion.
(c) U_m→P pointwise on all X as extended-real functions. At a pole, all selected sections vanish for m≥2, so U_m=-∞; P=-∞ also. For m=1 the actual space is all of H0(O(1,1)) and U_1 is finite there, which is immaterial to the limit.
(d) exp(U_m)→exp(P) uniformly on all X. To see this genuine global uniform statement, near a pole P≤log r²+C and R≤C/r². Estimate (20) gives, for m≥2,
 U_m≤(1-1/m)log r²+C+[C+2log(m+1)]/m.
The exponential is bounded by C r for r<1, uniformly in m≥2, while e^P≤Cr². These uniform smallness bounds near the poles, together with uniform convergence on their complement, establish (d).

There is NO global additive uniform error estimate for U_m-P. At p, the band b=a+1 consists locally of all degree-(m-1) binary monomials u^a v^{m-1-a}, 0≤a≤m-1, and all other selected sections have at least that order. Their positively weighted sum of squared leading forms is comparable to r^{2(m-1)} in every direction, with constants allowed to depend on m. The same holds at q using the opposite band. Thus, for fixed m≥2,
 U_m=(1-1/m)log r²+O_m(1),
 P=log r²+O(1).
On punctured approaches to either pole U_m-P tends to +∞. This is the precise reason a purported uniform additive boundary/pole error would fail for the actual multiplier ideals of order m-1. The statements (a)–(d) do not make that false assertion.

5. FINAL OUTPUTS AND INTRINSIC NORMALIZATION.
The entire actual spaces are (7), with N_m=3m+1 and Hilbert growth dimension 1. The full ideal and ordinary competitor classes coincide for arbitrary non-toric genuine θ-psh functions by n1, so their envelopes coincide for every smooth obstacle on all X. For the stated compact K and continuous obstacle w, the full envelope is (16), and the actual original-norm kernels satisfy the global lower bound (18) and the convergence statements above.

The normalized kernel trace probability measure is
 (3m+1)^{-1}B_m e^{-mw}ν,
and its weak limit is exactly (14) of n2: dt on x1=x2=t∈[0,1] times independent normalized angular Haar. Its support lies on the contact set, since g(2t)=t²=H(t,t). With the intrinsic power normalization m^{-1}, the limit is three times that probability measure. The ambient m^{-2} normalization gives zero. No multiplicative-series theorem, substitute monomial family, or identification restricted to toric competitors is used. These facts provide the actual ideal-selected spaces, their intrinsic growth, the full envelope identification and lower convergence, the original Hilbert normalization, and the normalized kernel-measure limit required in the problem.
