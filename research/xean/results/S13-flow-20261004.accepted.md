## w7-1/n1

Auxiliary cusp lemma. Throughout dd^c=(i/(2π))∂bar∂. On P1, write z=Z1/Z0 and put α=dd^c log(1+|z|²), so ∫α=1. The standard squared norm of the section Z1 of O(1) is h=|z|²/(1+|z|²); its zero divisor is {0}. For every c>2 there is a rotational function u, smooth on P1\{0}, such that

    g1:=cα+dd^c u=e^u h^{-1}cα.                    (1)

It extends as a cα-psh function on P1, has zero Lelong number at 0, and has full mass and finite energy. More precisely, if s=-log|z|² tends to +∞, then for some A∈R,

    u=log(2/c)-2log(s+A)+O(e^{-s}),
    du/dt=2/(s+A)+O(e^{-s}),  t=log|z|²,
    d²u/dt²=2/(s+A)²+O(e^{-s}).                  (2)

Existence by explicit barriers. Set r=|z|², L=1-log h=1+log(1+1/r), and w=-2log L. Away from 0, dd^c L=α and

    dd^c w=(-2/L+2/(rL²))α.

Consequently g_w=cα+dd^c w is strictly positive for c>2. Its ratio to the desired right-hand side at w is

    Q(r)=g_w/(e^w h^{-1}cα)
        =(c rL²-2rL+2)/(c(1+r)).

This is positive, extends continuously to r=0 and r=∞, and has limits 2/c and 1-2/c respectively. Thus 0<q_-≤Q≤q_+<∞. Choose a≤min(0,log q_-) and b≥max(0,log q_+). Then l=w+a and H=w+b satisfy

    cα+dd^c l ≥ e^l h^{-1}cα,
    cα+dd^c H ≤ e^H h^{-1}cα,
    l≤w≤H.                                                   (3)

On Ω_ε=P1\{|z|≤ε}, solve (1) with Dirichlet boundary value w. Here everything is smooth and the domain has a smooth boundary. For completeness, the usual sub/supersolution construction can be reduced to elementary linear elliptic Dirichlet problems: write the equation as Δu=f e^u-g in a fixed smooth background metric. On the bounded interval [l,H] choose k≥sup f e^H. Invert -Δ+k with boundary value w, iterating

    (-Δ+k)u_{j+1}=ku_j-f e^{u_j}+g,

starting from l. Inequalities (3), the linear maximum principle, and monotonicity of the right-hand side in u_j show that the iterates increase and remain below H. Their limit solves the equation. The nonlinear maximum principle gives uniqueness on Ω_ε. Since the data and domain are rotational, so is this solution.

The bounds l≤u_ε≤H are uniform on compact subsets of P1\{0}. Interior elliptic estimates, followed by a diagonal subsequence, give a smooth rotational limit u satisfying (1) and

    u=-2log(-log r)+O(1)  as r→0.                             (4)

The exhaustion includes a neighborhood of ∞, so the resulting function is smooth there. This argument establishes existence, not just a formal asymptotic solution.

Sharper asymptotics. For a radial function use t=log r. Equation (1) is

    u''(t)=c e^{u(t)}/(1+e^t)-c e^t/(1+e^t)².

Set v(t)=u(t)+c log(1+e^t), V(s)=v(-s). Then

    V''(s)=k(s)e^{V(s)},
    k(s)=c(1+e^{-s})^{-(c+1)},
    V(s)=-2log s+O(1).

Convexity and the last bound imply V'(s)→0 and V'(s)<0 for all sufficiently large s. Also k'(s)>0 and k'(s)=O(e^{-s}). The quantity F=(V')²-2k e^V obeys F'=-2k'e^V and tends to zero at infinity. Therefore

    F(s)=2∫_s^∞ k'(τ)e^{V(τ)}dτ=O(e^{-s}s^{-2}).

Since e^V is comparable to s^{-2}, it follows that

    (d/ds)e^{-V/2}=sqrt(c/2)(1+O(e^{-s})).

Integration gives e^{-V/2}=sqrt(c/2)(s+A)+O(e^{-s}) for a real constant A. Substitution proves the first statement in (2); the derivative statements follow from the same identity and the ODE. In particular g1 is asymptotic to the usual positive cusp form, with its radial coefficient asymptotic to 2/(r s²) in the normalization (i/(2π))dz∧dbar z.

Extension, mass, and energy. Locally u+c log(1+r) is subharmonic on the punctured disk and bounded above near 0; it extends subharmonically. Bound (4) implies zero Lelong number, hence no Dirac mass at 0. The density in (1) is integrable and comparable near 0 to the cusp density 1/(r s²). Globally, distributional Stokes gives ∫g1=c, so this is full mass. Moreover

    ∫_{P1}|u|α<∞,
    ∫_{P1}|u|g1<∞,

because the only potentially singular second integral is bounded by a constant times ∫^∞ (log s)/s² ds. These integrals give the usual E1 condition on a compact curve. Finally, the right-hand coefficient cα is smooth and strictly positive, and h^{-1}cα by itself is nonintegrable: the pole has not been weakened by a vanishing coefficient.

## w7-1/n2

Exact compact geometric data. Let X=P1_Z×P1_W, with z=Z1/Z0 and w=W1/W0. Denote the normalized Fubini–Study forms on the two factors by α and β, and choose

    ω=6(α+β),             dV_X=ω²=72 α∧β.

Let H={Z1=0}={z=0}, and let p=([0:1],[1:0])=(∞,0), which is disjoint from H. Define globally smooth nonnegative functions

    h_H=|Z1|²/(|Z0|²+|Z1|²),
    η=|Z0|²/(|Z0|²+|Z1|²)+|W1|²/(|W0|²+|W1|²).

Here h_H is the standard squared norm of the canonical section of O(H). The zero set of η is exactly {p}. Put

    φ0=2log h_H+2log η,

with value -∞ on H∪{p}.

Let π:Y=Bl_p X→X be the ordinary blowup. Write H_Y=π^{-1}H and E for its exceptional curve. Then D=H_Y+E is a disjoint union of two smooth divisors, hence is SNC. The blowup is an isomorphism off p, and in particular off the singular locus H∪{p}.

Explicit smooth divisor metrics. Use |σ_H|²=πh_H, giving Θ_H=πα. Use

    |σ_E|²=πη.                                                (5)

Equation (5) really defines a smooth Hermitian metric on O(E), not only a function off E. For example, near p use coordinates x=Z0/Z1, y=W1/W0. Then

    η=|x|²/(1+|x|²)+|y|²/(1+|y|²).

In the blowup chart x=t, y=tv, this becomes

    πη=|t|² A(t,v),
    A(t,v)=1/(1+|t|²)+|v|²/(1+|tv|²)>0.

Thus the local metric factor A is smooth and strictly positive across t=0; the other chart is identical with x,y interchanged. These local factors transform by the Hermitian metric rule.

A useful globally exact curvature formula follows from the three sections

    S0=Z0W0,  S1=sqrt(2) Z0W1,  S2=Z1W1

of L=O(1,1), equipped with the product FS metric. Their summed squared norm is η. Their common base locus is only p, and in the coordinates above their ideal is (x,sqrt(2)xy,y)=(x,y). After blowing up and dividing by σ_E, they have no common zero and define a morphism F:Y→P2. If γ is the normalized FS form on P2, the induced FS metric on πL−E gives

    F*γ=π(α+β)−Θ_E.                                         (6)

This is also obtained locally by taking dd^c of the logarithm of the sum of squared holomorphic coordinates. On E, F is an isomorphism onto a line: in the first blowup chart F=[1:sqrt(2)tv:v], so on E it is [1:0:v].

Analytic singularities and positivity. Near H, φ0=2log|z|² plus a smooth term. Near p,

    η=(|x|²+|y|²+2|xy|²)/((1+|x|²)(1+|y|²)),

so φ0 has analytic singularities from the holomorphic ideal (x,y), with coefficient 2. On Y there is the exact identity

    πφ0=2log|σ_H|²+2log|σ_E|²,

so ell0=0 and m_H=m_E=2. Poincaré–Lelong gives

    π(ω+dd^c φ0)=2[H_Y]+2[E]+R0,
    R0=πω−2Θ_H−2Θ_E
      =2πα+4πβ+2F*γ.                                        (7)

The last expression is smooth, closed, and semipositive. In fact it is Kähler: away from E the first two summands are already positive; on E they are positive in the nonvertical normal direction and F*γ is positive on TE. Thus φ0 is ω-psh and (7) verifies the required residual semipositivity directly, rather than assuming a suitable analytic pole exists.

Discrepancies and Arnold multiplicity. The usual blowup Jacobian in the chart x=t,y=tv has complex determinant t. Hence K_Y=π*K_X+E; b_H=0 and b_E=1. Therefore

    λ=max{m_H/(1+b_H),m_E/(1+b_E)}=max{2,1}=2.

This also follows directly from exponential integrability: at H, e^{-qφ0} is integrable exactly for q<1/2; the isolated point singularity in complex dimension two has threshold q<1. Thus the global threshold is 1/2 and its inverse is 2.

Exact theta identity. The task's Θ is Θ_H+2Θ_E, so

    θ=λΘ+R0=2Θ_H+4Θ_E+R0=πω+2Θ_E.                         (8)

Consequently {θ}=π{ω}+2{E}, precisely the required class formula: the added coefficient at H is 2−2=0, and that at E is 4−2=2. This class is non-nef, since its intersection with E is −2. It is big: the smooth form πω−Θ_E=5π(α+β)+F*γ is Kähler, by the same tangent-space argument as above, and

    θ+dd^c(3log|σ_E|²)=(πω−Θ_E)+3[E]

is a Kähler current in its class.

Exact smooth positive volume identity. Set

    dV_Y=πω²/|σ_E|²                                         (9)

off E. In the chart x=t,y=tv, the numerator is |t|² times a smooth strictly positive volume coefficient, because the Jacobian is t. The denominator is |t|²A(t,v), with A strictly positive and smooth. Thus (9) extends to a globally smooth strictly positive volume form on Y. The other blowup chart verifies the extension everywhere on E; away from E the quotient was already smooth positive. We have identically on Y

    π*dV_X=|σ_H|^{2b_H}|σ_E|^{2b_E}dV_Y=|σ_E|²dV_Y.

This checks the volume pushforward/discrepancy identity with the requested dV_X=ω², not an independently chosen unrelated volume. Finally, dV_Y/(|σ_H|²|σ_E|²) is genuinely nonintegrable near each component: locally it has a positive smooth coefficient times the ordinary unweighted |t|^{-2} pole. No logarithmic weakening or vanishing pole coefficient is present.

## w7-1/n3

Complete counterexample to the geometric-smoothing-compatible completeness question.

Use exactly the compact data of n2. In particular X=P1×P1, ω=6(α+β), dV_X=ω², Y=Bl_(∞,0)X, D=H_Y⊔E, m_H=m_E=2, b_H=0,b_E=1, λ=2, ell0=0, R0 is Kähler, and

    θ=πω+2Θ_E,
    πω²=|σ_E|²dV_Y.

All divisor metrics and dV_Y are globally smooth Hermitian/volume metrics with the strictly positive coefficients specified there.

Actual solution and equation. Take the function u on the first P1 from n1 with c=6, pull it to X by the first projection, and put

    g_X=(6α+dd^c u)+6β.

On X\H this is a smooth strictly positive Kähler metric. Since p=(∞,0) is not on H, it is smooth strictly positive on a whole neighborhood of p. Its exact equation is

    g_X²=e^u h_H^{-1}ω²,                                    (10)

as follows by wedging the one-dimensional equation with 6β. Define on Y

    P=2log|σ_E|²,
    ρ=P+πu.                                                  (11)

Poincaré–Lelong and the exact form identity for θ give

    θ+dd^cρ=2[E]+πg_X.                                      (12)

Thus ρ is θ-psh. Outside D its current is the smooth positive metric πg_X. The non-pluripolar square discards the divisorial mass and is the zero-extension from Y\D of πg_X². On that open set the required right-hand side is exactly

    e^ρ |σ|^{-2}dV_Y
      =(|σ_E|²)² e^{πu} (|σ_H|²|σ_E|²)^{-1}dV_Y
      =e^{πu}|σ_H|^{-2}|σ_E|²dV_Y
      =π(e^u h_H^{-1}ω²)
      =πg_X².                                               (13)

Both sides have no mass on D and the prescribed right-hand side is extended by zero there. Thus (13) proves the actual global divisorial equation, not an equation for a modified pole coefficient. Notice that the pole measure before multiplication by e^ρ remains the genuinely nonintegrable unweighted pole from n2. The exceptional pole is suppressed by the positive minimal Lelong coefficient of the solution, not by weakening the data.

Minimal singularities of P. Here is a direct description of all positive currents in this particular big class. If T is any positive closed (1,1) current on Y in π{ω}+2{E}, push it forward to S=π_*T. Then S is positive closed in {ω}. The pullback π*S is well-defined and positive: locally pull back a plurisubharmonic potential of S. On Y\E it equals T. Hence T−π*S is a closed order-zero (1,1) current supported on the smooth irreducible divisor E. The elementary support theorem says that such a current is a constant multiple of [E]. One can see this locally by testing tangential and normal components: closedness rules out all components except integration in the tangential variables, whose coefficient must be constant along the connected divisor; order zero excludes normal derivatives of delta distributions. The coefficient is 2 by cohomology, since {π*S}=π{ω} and {E} is nonzero. Consequently

    T=π*S+2[E].                                              (14)

Using the dd^c lemma on the compact Kähler X, write S=ω+dd^c v with v ω-psh. Equations (14) and θ=πω+2Θ_E show that every θ-psh potential is P+πv plus a constant. Since every ω-psh potential on compact X is bounded above, every such potential is at most P+C. Therefore P has minimal singularities. This argument also confirms the fixed coefficient 2 along E; it does not insert nefness into the theta class.

Volume and full non-pluripolar mass. The minimal current at P is 2[E]+πω, whose non-pluripolar square has total mass

    ∫_Y〈(θ+dd^cP)²〉=∫_Xω²=72.

This is the volume of the big class. In particular the volume is 72, not its cup-product square 68; the difference matters because the class is non-nef. By n1, the first-factor metric has mass 6 and no atom at its cusp, so

    ∫_Y〈(θ+dd^cρ)²〉=∫_Xg_X²=72.

Thus ρ has full non-pluripolar mass. There is no hidden mass at the exceptional curve or at the cusp divisor: all these measures are the extensions of their displayed densities and assign zero mass to those divisors.

Relative E1 energy. The difference ρ−P=πu is bounded above. One may add a constant to the reference minimal potential to make the difference nonpositive. On Y\D all mixed non-pluripolar products of the currents at ρ and P are pullbacks of the corresponding mixed products of g_X and ω. The extensions charge neither E nor H_Y. For j=0,1,2 their energy integrals are therefore

    ∫_Y |πu|〈(θ+dd^cρ)^j∧(θ+dd^cP)^{2-j}〉
      =∫_X |u|g_X^j∧ω^{2-j}.

Writing g1=6α+dd^c u, the measures on the right are linear combinations with positive constant coefficients of g1∧β and α∧β: ω²=72αβ, g_X∧ω=6g1β+36αβ, and g_X²=12g1β. Both ∫|u|g1 and ∫|u|α are finite by n1. All three energy integrals are finite. This verifies the relative E1 condition with respect to the minimal potential P, hence also with respect to any boundedly equivalent minimal reference.

Minimal Lelong numbers. Near E, πu is smooth, because u is smooth at z=∞. Thus ρ has coefficient exactly 2 along E, the same as P. At H_Y, u has logarithmic-cusp growth −2log(−log|z|²)+O(1), whose Lelong number is zero. Everywhere else it is smooth. Hence all the Lelong numbers of ρ agree with those of the minimal potential P. We have now exhibited a full-mass relative E1 solution of the exact equation specified in the question. By the uniqueness stipulated there (or the full-mass comparison principle with the strictly increasing factor e^ρ), it is its designated solution.

Regular locus. The current (12) is smooth and strictly positive exactly on

    U0=Y\(H_Y∪E).

Outside these two curves, π is a biholomorphism and g_X is smooth positive. At H_Y, its normal density has the cusp divergence furnished by n1, so the current is not smooth. At E, the current has its nonzero integration-current component 2[E], so again it is not smooth. Even if one considered only the non-pluripolar metric part, its smooth extension across E would be the pullback of g_X, which is degenerate in TE and not strictly positive there.

Incompleteness on every candidate dense Zariski open set. Let U be any dense Zariski open subset of Y on which this current is smooth and strictly positive. Necessarily U⊂U0. The analytic complement B=Y\U has finitely many curve and point components. It contains E. Every other curve component meets E only at finitely many points unless it is E itself. Choose q∈E away from all those intersections and away from the other point components. A sufficiently small coordinate neighborhood N of q satisfies N\E⊂U.

Take a smooth transverse path γ(t)=(t,v0), 0<t≤ε, in such a blowup chart, tending to q as t↓0. It lies entirely in U. Since g_X is smooth positive near p and π is a smooth map, the pullback tensor πg_X has bounded coefficients on N, including across E. In particular |γ'(t)|_{πg_X}≤C, and the path has finite length, at most Cε. Its points γ(t_k), for any t_k↓0, form a Cauchy sequence in the intrinsic Riemannian distance of U: join two points by their intervening segment of γ. They have no limit in U, since they converge in the manifold topology to q∉U, and a smooth positive Riemannian distance induces the manifold topology at every point of U. Equivalently, this is finite intrinsic distance to an unavoidable missing boundary component. Thus U is incomplete.

This works for every dense Zariski open regular positive locus, not merely for the natural one U0. The example is compact, satisfies every geometric-smoothing compatibility identity including dV_X=ω² and its exact discrepancy volume identity, has a genuinely nonintegrable prescribed pole with smooth positive coefficient, and uses the actual full-mass relative E1 solution. It disproves the proposed completeness implication in the geometric-compatible branch. No assertion is made about the metric of the actual time-dependent maximal flow.
