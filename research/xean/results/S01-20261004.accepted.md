## w7-1/n1

Work on the standard Hopf threefold in the problem. Write D=dd^c, V=∫_X ω³ (=6 under the stated normalization), and M(f)=∫_X(ω+Df)³ for a smooth real function f. The supplied differential identities are Dω²=0 and Dω=−α². All wedge products below are of smooth forms, so Stokes applies without a singular-product issue.

1. Quadratic mass identity reconstructed by Stokes.

First, ∫ω²∧Df=∫f Dω²=0. Also ∫(Df)³=0, since (Df)³=D(f(Df)²). Consequently

  M(f)=V+3Q(f),    Q(f):=∫ω∧(Df)².

Since D(f Df)=(Df)²,

  Q(f)=∫f Df∧Dω.

With d^c=(i/(4π))(bar∂−∂), one has D(f²/2)=df∧d^c f+f Df. The D(f²/2) term integrates to zero against the d-closed form Dω. It follows that

  Q(f)=−∫df∧d^c f∧Dω=∫df∧d^c f∧α²≥0.

This also proves exactly, rather than asymptotically,

  M(λf)−V=λ²(M(f)−V)

for every real λ. These identities hold for every smooth f, not just a special radial profile. No fiberwise formula is needed here.

2. A positive carrier for the nonlinear defect.

Suppose 0<t<1 and Df≥−tω. Set H=Df+tω≥0, and put m=M(f)−V=3Q(f). Expanding and using the identities above gives

  ∫ω²∧H=tV,
  ∫ω∧H²=m/3+t²V,
  ∫H³=tm+t³V.

In particular all three are masses of positive forms. The final formula is sometimes especially useful: the pure cubic positive term has mass tending to zero when t→0 and m stays bounded, despite the nonzero Monge–Ampère defect.

Define the positive top-degree measure ν(f,t)=3ω∧H². Its mass is m+3t²V. The exact expansion is

  (ω+Df)³=(1−t)³ω³+3(1−t)²ω²∧H+(1−t)ν(f,t)+H³.

Every term displayed on the right is positive. Subtracting ω³+ν(f,t) and using their masses therefore yields the quantitative signed-measure estimate

  ||(ω+Df)³−ω³−ν(f,t)||TV
  ≤[1−(1−t)³]V+3(1−t)²tV+t(m+3t²V)+(tm+t³V)
  ≤6tV+2tm+4t³V.

Thus, if t_j→0, Df_j≥−t_jω, and m_j=M(f_j)−V is bounded and nonnegative, then

  (ω+Df_j)³−ω³−3ω∧(Df_j+t_jω)² →0 in total variation.

In particular, for an original fixed-mass sequence f_j=s_j u_j with excess mass m=b−V, weak convergence of its Monge–Ampère measures to ω³+σ is equivalent to weak convergence of the positive quadratic carriers 3ω∧(Df_j+s_jω)² to σ. This is a full local weak-measure identification, not just an integrated mass equality.

3. Scope and remaining gap.

The reduction uses special threefold identities. It does not follow from linear current convergence and it is not asserted on arbitrary Hermitian manifolds or in arbitrary dimension. It does not by itself tell us the supports or pluripolar charging of a carrier limit. Subsequent notes use it to build actual sequences whose carrier limits are prescribed. The only geometric premises used here are the differential identities supplied in the problem; the nonlinear calculation itself has been reconstructed.

## w7-1/n2

THEOREM (a prescribed diffuse defect on the standard Hopf threefold). Let X and ω be the standard Hopf threefold and metric in the problem. Assume the supplied existence of smooth normalized ω-psh potentials of arbitrarily large mass. For every b>V=∫ω³ and every positive ε_j→0, there are smooth U_j with sup_X U_j=0 and ω+dd^c U_j≥0, and parameters 0<r_j<ε_j, such that

  ∫(ω+dd^c(r_j U_j))³=b,
  (ω+dd^c(r_j U_j))³ → (b/V)ω³ weakly.

They are honest shrinking sequences, hence retain the capacity and fixed-level properties stated in the question. Their defect is the prescribed measure σ=((b−V)/V)ω³. In particular its support is all of X and it charges no pluripolar sets.

This theorem supplies realizable examples, not a classification of arbitrary shrinking sequences. It uses arbitrary large-mass smooth potentials, not a presumed special Hopf profile.

PROOF.

A. An explicit invariant averaging distribution.

The compact group G=U(3)×(R/Z) acts on X by

  (A,t)[z]=[e^t A z].

Changing t by an integer changes only the deck representative. The action is holomorphic and transitive: one can first match norms by real scaling and then match unit vectors by a unitary matrix. It preserves ω, because unitary maps leave s=log|z|² unchanged and real scaling adds a constant to s, leaving α=dd^c s and β=ds∧d^c s unchanged.

One may use the usual invariant probability h on this group. To avoid an unverified group-measure premise, it can be constructed as follows. Take a 3×3 complex Gaussian matrix Z with density proportional to exp(−Tr(Z*Z)) and let A=Z(Z*Z)^(−1/2), defined almost surely. Left and right unitary invariance of the Gaussian law, and equivariance of this polar expression, make the law of A left and right invariant. Independently take t uniform modulo 1. This supplies the required left and right invariant probability on G.

For every smooth q, the function ∫q∘g dh(g) is constant, by transitivity and right invariance. Therefore averaging commutes with dd^c and

  ∫g^*(dd^c q+sω) dh(g)=sω.

For every finite positive measure η, averaging its translates gives

  ∫g^*η dh(g)=(η(X)/V)ω³.

For completeness, on testing against a continuous φ, the inner average of φ∘g^(−1) is independent of the point by transitivity; integrating it against the invariant probability ω³/V identifies its constant as V^(−1)∫φω³. This proves the displayed top-degree averaging identity directly.

B. Start with one arbitrarily small fixed-mass shrink.

Put m=b−V>0. For any desired upper bound on s, select a smooth normalized ω-psh u with sufficiently large M(u). By note n1,

  s=√(m/(M(u)−V)),   f=su

gives M(f)=V+m=b, and s can be as small as desired. Let

  H=dd^c f+sω≥0,    ν=3ω∧H².

Note n1 gives ν(X)=m+3s²V and ∫ω²H=sV.

Take N independent group elements g_1,...,g_N with distribution h. Define

  W=N^(−1/2)∑_{l=1}^N f∘g_l,
  τ=s√N,
  K=dd^c W+τω=N^(−1/2)∑_{l=1}^N g_l^*H≥0.

The quadratic carrier of W decomposes into two positive measures:

  3ω∧K²=D_N+C_N,
  D_N=(1/N)∑_{l=1}^N g_l^*ν,
  C_N=(6/N)∑_{l<k}ω∧g_l^*H∧g_k^*H.

This is an averaging of actual potentials, not a formal averaging of their Monge–Ampère measures.

C. The mixed terms really vanish, and the empirical measures can be chosen deterministically.

Independence, Fubini, and the form-average identity give

  E[C_N(X)]=3(N−1)s²V.

Indeed each independent mixed integral has expectation ∫ω∧(sω)²=s²V. Positivity permits a Markov estimate on the entire off-diagonal measure.

The variables Y_l(φ)=∫φ g_l^*ν have absolute value at most (m+3s²V)||φ||∞. For ||φ||∞≤1 their empirical average has variance at most (m+3s²V)²/N, and its mean is

  ((m+3s²V)/V)∫φω³.

Here variance follows directly by independence; no limit theorem is needed.

Choose a countable uniformly dense collection of real continuous test functions φ_k in the unit ball of C(X). Choose δ_j↓0, with δ_j<m/10. At step j choose N_j→∞ large enough that, for s≤1,

  j(m+3V)²/(N_j δ_j²)<1/4.

Then choose the large-mass potential and its s_j so small that

  τ_j=s_j√N_j<min(ε_j/4,1/j,1/4),
  3Vτ_j²<m/2,
  3(N_j−1)s_j²V<δ_j/4.

The probability that any of the first j empirical test errors exceeds δ_j is less than 1/4 by the variance bound and a union bound. The probability that C_N(X)>δ_j is less than 1/4 by Markov. Hence there is a deterministic tuple of smooth translates for which both conclusions hold. Fix such a tuple at each j.

Its diagonal measures satisfy

  D_j → (m/V)ω³ weakly,

and its off-diagonal measures satisfy ||C_j||TV=C_j(X)≤δ_j→0. Thus

  3ω∧K_j² → (m/V)ω³ weakly.

This proves the desired complete local quadratic measure limit, using all continuous tests rather than only the mass.

D. Exact fixed mass, not just an asymptotically fixed mass.

Since K_j=dd^c W_j+τ_jω, the identities in n1 give

  M(W_j)−V=3∫ωK_j²−3τ_j²V
            =m+C_j(X)−3(N_j−1)s_j²V=:d_j.

Thus d_j→m. Our parameter choice ensures d_j≥m−3Vτ_j²>m/2 at every step. Define

  λ_j=√(m/d_j).

Then λ_j→1, λ_j≤√2, and M(λ_jW_j)=V+m=b exactly.

Let a_j=(1/N_j)∑u_j∘g_l and c_j=sup_X a_j. Set

  U_j=a_j−c_j,
  r_j=λ_jτ_j,
  w_j=r_jU_j.

The averaged a_j, and hence U_j, is a smooth genuine ω-psh function; sup U_j=0. Also 0<r_j<ε_j. The function w_j differs from λ_jW_j only by a constant. Consequently its mass is exactly b and

  ω+dd^c w_j≥(1−r_j)ω≥(1−ε_j)ω.

Its positive carrier is

  dd^c w_j+r_jω=λ_jK_j,
  3ω∧(dd^c w_j+r_jω)²=λ_j²(D_j+C_j) → (m/V)ω³.

Applying the total-variation reduction of n1 with t=r_j and fixed excess mass m now proves

  (ω+dd^c w_j)³ → ω³+(m/V)ω³=(b/V)ω³

against every continuous test function. This is the complete local weak Monge–Ampère limit.

E. Retained shrinking properties and support/pluripolarity.

The final sequence has exactly the form stipulated in the problem: smooth normalized genuine ω-psh U_j, parameters r_j<ε_j, and exact fixed mass b. Thus the shrinking capacity conclusion supplied in the problem applies directly: w_j→0 in capacity. Likewise its supplied fixed shallow/deep and coefficient-current conclusions apply. No differently normalized potential class or nonlinear averaging assertion has been substituted.

The defect σ=(m/V)ω³ is a positive smooth volume measure, with support X because ω is Hermitian. A pluripolar set has Lebesgue measure zero: locally it is contained in the −∞ locus of a non-identically−∞ psh function, and such a psh function is locally integrable. Smooth volume therefore vanishes on every pluripolar set.

Accordingly defects need not have proper support and need not charge pluripolar sets. In particular a universal requirement that defects be supported on a pluripolar set, or that every defect have a nonzero pluripolar part, is false. Nothing in this proof shows that every defect is diffuse, and the possibility of pluripolar-charging defects remains unresolved.

Premise accounting: existence of arbitrarily large-mass smooth potentials and the shrinking capacity statement are used as background explicitly supplied by the task. The nonlinear reduction, averaging distribution, positive mixed-term estimates, deterministic selection, exact mass adjustment, and full weak limit are proved above. No source-dependent assertion about the defect of a particular initial Hopf profile is used.

## w7-1/n4

GENERAL NECESSARY CURRENT AND SUPPORT RESTRICTIONS.

Let (X,ω) and an honest shrinking sequence be exactly as in the problem, in arbitrary complex dimension n. Write D=dd^c, w_j=s_j u_j, and

  H_j=Dw_j+s_jω=s_j(ω+Du_j)≥0.

Thus ω+Dw_j=(1−s_j)ω+H_j. We work after discarding finitely many indices so that s_j<1/2. Suppose the measures have weak limit ω^n+σ and total mass b, as stipulated.

1. Uniform bounds on every positive power.

The pointwise positive expansion

  (ω+Dw_j)^n=Σ_{k=0}^n binom(n,k)(1−s_j)^{n−k} ω^{n−k}∧H_j^k

and total mass b imply, for every k,

  ∫ω^{n−k}∧H_j^k ≤ 2^{n−k}b/binom(n,k).

These bounds control all coefficients of the positive currents H_j^k in finite coordinate charts: relative to a fixed positive metric, the diagonal coefficients are bounded by the trace, and positivity bounds off-diagonal coefficients by the diagonal ones. Thus a common subsequence has weak current limits K_k≥0 for all k. This compactness is simply compactness of bounded coefficient measures, applied in finitely many charts and then diagonally.

The first power actually tends to zero. One can use the linear/Gauduchon trace property supplied in the problem. More explicitly, for a smooth Gauduchon metric γ used for that trace, Dγ^{n−1}=0 and Stokes gives

  ∫γ^{n−1}∧H_j=s_j∫γ^{n−1}∧ω.

Metric comparison controls the ω-trace by this trace. Therefore H_j has current mass O(s_j), and K_1=0. This uses the available smooth Gauduchon trace, not convergence of any nonlinear product.

2. All limiting powers are d-closed.

Since dH_j=s_j dω,

  d(H_j^k)=k s_j dω∧H_j^{k−1}.

Testing against a fixed smooth form, the right side is bounded by a constant times s_j∫ω^{n−k+1}H_j^{k−1}. The preceding positive mass bounds show it tends to zero. Consequently dK_k=0.

3. Their closed-form pairings vanish; the top power vanishes altogether.

Let η be any smooth real d-closed form of degree 2n−2k. Exactness of Dw_j gives

  ∫η∧(Dw_j)^k=0.

Indeed (Dw_j)^k=d(d^c w_j∧(Dw_j)^{k−1}), and η is closed. Substitute Dw_j=H_j−s_jω and expand:

  ∫η∧H_j^k
  =−Σ_{r=0}^{k−1} binom(k,r)(−s_j)^{k−r}
       ∫η∧ω^{k−r}∧H_j^r.

The absolute value of each integral in this sum is bounded by a constant depending on η and ω times ∫ω^{n−r}H_j^r. Hence ∫ηH_j^k=O(s_j). Passing to the limit,

  ∫η∧K_k=0

for every smooth d-closed complementary real form η. These are precise zero de Rham pairings; no cohomological representation theorem is needed for the assertion. In the usual current-cohomology terminology the K_k are cohomologically trivial closed currents.

For k=n take η=1. The same computation gives ∫H_j^n=O(s_j). Since H_j^n is a positive measure, it tends to zero in total variation. Therefore K_n=0. This rules out a pure top-power defect even though intermediate products can be nonzero.

4. Identification of the general defect.

Pass to the common subsequence in the positive expansion. The k=0 term tends to ω^n, the first and last powers vanish, and multiplication by fixed smooth forms is continuous on currents. Hence

  σ=Σ_{k=2}^{n−1} binom(n,k) ω^{n−k}∧K_k.

Every summand is positive. The total mass of this sum is b−V_ω. For n=3 this simplifies to

  σ=3ω∧K_2,

where K_2 is positive, d-closed, and has all zero closed-form pairings. For n≤2 the sum is empty, so a positive fixed excess mass cannot occur in an honest shrinking construction of the stipulated type.

5. A concrete general support restriction.

Let θ be any GLOBAL smooth d-closed semipositive real (1,1)-form on X. For every k in the defect sum, η=θ^{n−k} is a smooth closed complementary form, so

  ∫θ^{n−k}∧K_k=0.

The integrand is a positive measure and therefore vanishes identically. Where θ is positive definite, its relevant power dominates a positive constant times ω^{n−k} on every relatively compact subregion. Thus K_k vanishes on that open set. It follows that

  supp σ ⊂ {x∈X : θ_x is not positive definite}={θ^n=0}.

This holds simultaneously for every global smooth closed semipositive θ. In particular a globally positive closed θ forces σ=0. The global hypothesis is essential: the argument does not apply merely because a coordinate neighborhood carries a local Kähler form.

These are necessary restrictions forced by the general shrinking construction, not merely by a named profile. They do not assert that every positive closed zero-pairing current, or every measure supported in the indicated degeneracy sets, can be realized. No external nonlinear-continuity theorem has been used: all products before taking limits are smooth, and the limits are controlled by positivity, Stokes, and the supplied linear trace property.

## w7-1/n5

NECESSARY FIBER STRUCTURE FOR ALL HOPF DEFECTS.

Let π:X→P² be [z]↦[z]. Let α=π^*ω_FS and ω=α+β as in the problem. The fibers are the elliptic curve E=C/(Z+2πiZ), with local holomorphic coordinate w=log z_0. The transition functions in w between base charts are translations depending holomorphically on the base; thus there is a canonical probability Haar measure h_p on every fiber π^{-1}(p).

For a positive finite measure μ on P² define its Haar lift Lμ by

  ∫_X φ d(Lμ)=∫_{P²} [∫_{π^{-1}(p)}φ dh_p] dμ(p)

for continuous φ. This is well-defined because changes of local trivialization translate the elliptic fiber and preserve Haar probability. The bracketed fiber average is continuous in p, as follows from any local trivialization and uniform continuity on the compact fiber. Thus L preserves weak convergence of finite base measures.

THEOREM. Every defect σ of an honest smooth shrinking sequence on this Hopf threefold has the form

  σ=Lμ,     μ=π_*σ≥0,     μ(P²)=b−6.

The corresponding quadratic-carrier current converges, along the sequence on which σ is the limit, to

  (dd^c w_j+s_jω)² → (1/6)π^*μ.

In particular supp σ=π^{-1}(supp μ), and σ has no point atoms.

PROOF.

1. A closed carrier annihilated by α.

Put H_j=dd^c w_j+s_jω. Note n4 gives, after extracting a subsequence, a positive d-closed (2,2)-current K=lim H_j² with

  σ=3ω∧K.

Its pairing with the smooth closed form α is zero. Since α≥0 and K≥0, this gives the measure identity

  α∧K=0.

For clarity, this also follows directly from the Hopf identities without the general pairing argument: α is closed, ∫α(Dw_j)²=0, and

  ∫αω Dw_j=∫w_j D(αω)=−∫w_j α³=0.

Hence ∫αH_j²=s_j²∫αω²→0.

2. Positivity forces K to be horizontal.

Use a product chart U×E over a base coordinate chart U⊂P², and local holomorphic coordinates x_1,x_2 on U and w on E. On relatively compact base subcharts, α dominates a positive constant times i dx_1∧dbar x_1+i dx_2∧dbar x_2.

A positive (2,2)-current in three complex dimensions has a positive Hermitian matrix of measure coefficients indexed by the three holomorphic two-forms

  dx_1∧dx_2,  dx_1∧dw,  dx_2∧dw.

One can make this statement entirely measure-theoretic: take the sum of diagonal coefficient measures as a dominating measure and use the Radon–Nikodym densities; their matrix is positive semidefinite almost everywhere. The identity αK=0 forces the two diagonal entries containing dw to vanish. Positivity then forces every cross entry involving them to vanish as well, by the elementary positive-matrix inequality |a_ij|²≤a_ii a_jj.

Therefore K has only a horizontal dx_1∧dx_2∧dbar x_1∧dbar x_2 coefficient. Its scalar coefficient is a positive distribution/measure on U×E.

3. Closedness forces Haar constancy along the fiber.

The equation dK=0 says that both real vertical distributional derivatives of this horizontal coefficient vanish. Horizontal derivatives give no condition because it already has top degree in the base directions. On E with real periods 1 and 2π, test the coefficient against a base test function times a Fourier mode exp(2πik Re w+iℓ Im w). Either vertical derivative shows that every nonconstant Fourier coefficient is zero. Density of trigonometric polynomials among continuous functions on E then shows that the coefficient is a product of a base measure with constant fiber area. This proves locally, and hence globally by compatibility of the translation transition functions, that

  K=π^*τ

for a positive top-degree base current, equivalently a positive finite measure τ on P². Here pullback by the submersion is defined locally by this horizontal product construction; no pullback through a singular map is being used.

4. Normalization and identification of σ.

In a local base chart, s=w+bar w+log(1+|x|²). With dd^c=(i/(2π))∂bar∂ and d^c=(i/(4π))(bar∂−∂), the vertical restriction of β is

  β|_E=(1/π) d(Re w)∧d(Im w).

Since the real periods are 1 and 2π, ∫_Eβ=2. Consequently, in the notation of the lift,

  Lμ=(1/2)β∧π^*μ.

Horizontal components of β disappear in this wedge. Also α∧π^*τ=0 because the base has complex dimension two. Therefore

  σ=3ω∧K=3β∧π^*τ=L(6τ).

Set μ=6τ. Its mass is σ(X)=b−6 and its pushforward is π_*σ, because h_p is a probability measure.

Every subsequential current limit of H_j² must have this same τ=μ/6 once σ is fixed. The coefficient mass bounds from n4 give precompactness, so uniqueness of all subsequential limits proves the stated full current convergence H_j²→(1/6)π^*μ.

5. Supports and atomlessness.

The lift of a base measure is locally its product with a full-support atomless Haar probability on E. This immediately implies

  supp Lμ=π^{-1}(supp μ),    (Lμ)({x})=0 for every x∈X.

These are restrictions on every honest shrinking sequence on this Hopf threefold. They were not inferred from the total mass identity or merely from linear current convergence. The essential additional reasoning is positivity plus closedness of the nonlinear current limit. Sufficiency of the lift condition is not proved in this note; subsequent notes give actual smooth admissible realization constructions.

## w7-1/n6

THEOREM (prescribed pluripolar fiber defect).

Let m=b−6>0. For every p∈P² and every positive ε_j→0, there are smooth normalized genuine ω-psh U_j and 0<r_j<ε_j with

  M(r_jU_j)=b,
  (ω+dd^c(r_jU_j))³ → ω³+m h_p,

where h_p is the probability Haar measure on the elliptic fiber E_p=π^{-1}(p). The full excess mass m is concentrated on a globally ω-pluripolar set E_p.

PROOF.

1. Obtain a source current limit, not merely a source volume limit.

Use the honest diffuse realization from n2, with excess mass m. Denote its functions by f_k=s_k u_k and its positive carriers by H_k=dd^c f_k+s_kω. Its defect is (m/6)ω³=L(mω_FS²). Here ∫_{P²}ω_FS²=1: one can derive this normalization directly from V=6, ω³=3α²β, and ∫_Eβ=2 as computed in n5.

By the necessity/uniqueness theorem n5, the entire source carrier current sequence satisfies

  H_k² → (m/6)α².

Also H_k has mass O(s_k), by its Gauduchon trace. This stronger current statement is indispensable for the transformation argument.

2. Transform by a fixed linear automorphism and repair the lower curvature bound.

Every A∈GL(3,C) commutes with the deck dilation and defines a holomorphic automorphism of X. Let a be its projective automorphism of P². Choose a finite constant C_A≥1 such that A^*ω≤C_Aω, and put R_A=C_Aω−A^*ω≥0.

The smooth function f_k^A=f_k∘A satisfies dd^c f_k^A≥−C_As_kω. Its positive carrier for the parameter t_k=C_As_k is

  H_k^A=dd^c f_k^A+t_kω=A^*H_k+s_kR_A≥0.

For fixed A, the difference

  (H_k^A)²−A^*(H_k²)=2s_k A^*H_k∧R_A+s_k²R_A²

is positive and has current mass O_A(s_k²). The estimate uses mass(A^*H_k)=O_A(s_k), which follows by fixed-metric comparison from mass(H_k)=O(s_k). Therefore

  (H_k^A)² → (m/6)A^*α²=(m/6)π^*(a^*ω_FS²).

Multiplying by the fixed ω gives the complete carrier-measure limit

  3ω∧(H_k^A)² → (m/2)β∧π^*(a^*ω_FS²)
                   =L(m a^*ω_FS²).

It has mass m, because a is a diffeomorphism and ∫a^*ω_FS²=1.

3. Exact mass and normalized admissibility after transformation.

Let d_k^A=M(f_k^A)−6. By the exact identities in n1,

  d_k^A=3∫ω(H_k^A)²−3t_k²V →m.

In particular it is positive for all sufficiently late k. Set λ_k^A=√(m/d_k^A)→1. The mass identity gives

  M(λ_k^Af_k^A)=6+m=b

exactly. The underlying function u_k^A=(u_k∘A)/C_A is smooth, has supremum zero, and satisfies

  ω+dd^c u_k^A≥ω−C_A^(−1)A^*ω≥0.

Thus λ_k^Af_k^A=r_k^A u_k^A with r_k^A=λ_k^AC_As_k→0. Its carrier is λ_k^AH_k^A, so n1 proves the full weak measure limit

  (ω+dd^c(λ_k^Af_k^A))³ → ω³+L(m a^*ω_FS²).

All potentials here are actual smooth admissible ones. No formal pushforward assertion for nonlinear Monge–Ampère measures is used.

4. Degeneration to any prescribed fiber.

First take p_0=[1:0:0]. Let R_j→∞ and let A_j=diag(R_j^(−1),1,1), with induced projective map a_j. Since a_j is a diffeomorphism,

  a_j^*ω_FS²=(a_j^(−1))_*ω_FS².

For every q=[q_0:q_1:q_2] with q_0≠0, a_j^(−1)(q)=[R_jq_0:q_1:q_2]→p_0. The exceptional hyperplane {q_0=0} has zero smooth volume. Dominated convergence applied to each continuous test function therefore proves

  a_j^*ω_FS² → δ_{p_0}.

The fiber-average characterization in n5 shows that L is weakly continuous. Hence

  L(m a_j^*ω_FS²) → m h_{p_0}.

For each fixed A_j, step 3 supplies an entire honest sequence converging to its indicated defect. Choose one sufficiently late actual member so that its shrinking parameter is <ε_j and its Monge–Ampère measure is within 1/j of ω³+L(m a_j^*ω_FS²) on the first j functions in a fixed dense collection of continuous tests. It has exact mass b already. These deterministic diagonal choices prove

  (ω+dd^c w_j)³ → ω³+m h_{p_0}

against every continuous test. They retain smooth normalized ω-psh underlying potentials and exact parameters 0<r_j<ε_j. A fixed unitary transformation gives the same construction for any p.

The capacity and fixed-level conclusions in the problem apply because the final sequence is an honest shrinking sequence of precisely its specified class.

5. The supporting fiber is genuinely globally ω-pluripolar.

For p_0=[1:0:0], define

  ψ([z])=log((|z_1|²+|z_2|²)/|z|²).

This is well-defined under the deck action, has the canonical upper representative, is locally a genuine psh logarithm minus the smooth s, and satisfies dd^cψ≥−α. Thus ω+dd^cψ≥β≥0. Its −∞ locus is exactly E_{p_0}. It is a nontrivial genuine ω-psh function, so E_{p_0} is even globally ω-pluripolar. Rotate by a unitary map for any other p. 1. Positive carriers of the source sequence.

  B_{k,j}=∫g^*H_k dρ_j(g).

  E[∫ω∧g_l^*H_k∧g_a^*H_k]

  W=N^(−1/2)∑ f_k∘g_l,

  3ωK²=D+C,

  E[C(X)]≤3(N−1)C_j²s_k²V.

The empirical diagonal D has expected measure A_{ρ_j}ν_k. For every real test φ of sup norm ≤1 its variance is at most (m+3s_k²V)²/N, independently of the density bound C_j.

PROOF OF REALIZATION.

Necessity is n5. We prove sufficiency while retaining actual smooth potentials, exact fixed mass, genuine ω-psh admissibility, prescribed shrinking bounds, and the full weak measure limit.

  μ=mΣ_{a=1}^N θ_a δ_{p_a},   θ_a≥0,  Σθ_a=1.

This gives finitely atomic μ_j with μ_j(P²)=m and μ_j→μ weakly. For each j use the just-proved honest realization of Lμ_j. PROOF OF THE SUPPORT CLAIM.

By n5, supp Lμ=π^{-1}(supp μ), so every possible support is fiber saturated. Conversely, every nonempty closed F in the compact metric space P² is the support of a positive measure of mass m: choose a countable dense set {p_l}⊂F and positive weights with sum m, for example m2^(−l). In particular a measure supported on a single point of X, or on a proper non-Haar subset of a fiber, cannot occur, even though an entire pluripolar fiber can carry all defect mass.

PROOF OF THE PLURIPOLARITY EQUIVALENCE.

We use the usual local definition of pluripolarity. For measure statements one can take measurable pluripolar sets; the argument also applies to outer measure zero by using their local −∞ supersets.

If B⊂P² is pluripolar, π^{-1}(B) is pluripolar: locally pull back a nontrivial psh function with value −∞ on B through the holomorphic submersion π. It remains nontrivial because a submersion has open image. Also

  (Lμ)(π^{-1}(B))=μ(B).

Consequently if Lμ charges no pluripolar set, neither does μ.

Conversely suppose μ charges no pluripolar set on P², and let P⊂X be pluripolar. Cover X by countably many relatively compact local product charts U×D in which P is contained in the −∞ locus of a non-identically−∞ genuine psh function h(x,w). Shrinking product charts still cover X, so we can work with a fixed smaller fiber disc D' in each chart.

The function h is locally integrable and locally bounded above. For each fixed x, its restriction h(x,·) is either a nontrivial subharmonic function or identically −∞. In the former case its −∞ set has fiber area zero. Thus positive area of P on that fiber disc can occur only for base points x where h(x,·) is identically −∞ on D'.

That exceptional set of base points is pluripolar. To see this without a global projection theorem, define

  F(x)=(1/area(D'))∫_{D'}h(x,w)dA(w).

This is a psh function on the smaller base chart, possibly −∞ at some points, and it is not identically −∞. Indeed joint local integrability and Fubini give finiteness for almost every x. Upper semicontinuity follows by reverse Fatou, using a common upper bound on the relatively compact product chart; the psh submean inequalities follow by integrating the inequalities for h(·,w). Thus F is a genuine nontrivial base psh function. If h(x,·) is identically −∞ on D', then F(x)=−∞. The exceptional parameters are therefore contained in a base pluripolar set.

Outside the countable union of these exceptional base sets, every local piece of P has fiber Haar measure zero. Since μ gives that union measure zero, the defining fiber integral for Lμ and Fubini show (Lμ)(P)=0. This proves the converse and hence (iii).

At θ=0 the defect is full-support and charges no pluripolar set; at θ=1 it is entirely carried by a pluripolar fiber. All are non-atomic as point measures.
