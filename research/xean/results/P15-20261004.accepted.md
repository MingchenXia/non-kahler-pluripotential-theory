## w7-1/n1

SETUP AND THEOREM.
Let G be a connected complex Lie group of complex dimension n≥2 and Γ a discrete subgroup acting by left translations, such that X=Γ\G is compact. Fix a left-invariant holomorphic coframe α1,…,αn, which descends to X. For a Hermitian matrix A define
  θA=(i/(2π))Σj,k Ajk αj∧barαk.
The harmless choice of transpose in the coefficient convention does not change positivity, determinant, or any trace formula below. Let h0=θI, H=∫X h0^n, and μ=h0^n/H. Thus μ is a smooth positive probability volume. Let W be a real linear subspace of Herm(n) containing at least one positive-definite matrix, and set V={θA:A∈W}.
Then:
(a) V∩dd^c C∞(X,R)={0}; the specified cone of backgrounds admitting a smooth positive representative is C={θA:A∈W,A>0}.
(b) Every positive θA is balanced, hence Gauduchon.
(c) With the question's dd^c=(i/(2π))∂bar∂, its exact variational volume is
  λμ(θA)=(H det A)^(1/n),  Vμ(θA)=H det A.
(d) The unique minimizing Gauduchon metric normalized by Dμ(γ)=1 is γA=θA/λμ(θA).
(e) The Hessian metric is exactly
  gA(U,Z)=(1/n)tr(A⁻¹UA⁻¹Z),  U,Z∈W.
(f) A background θA has a positive-current representative θA+dd^c u with u a real distribution if and only if A≥0. For such A the invariant θA is itself a smooth semipositive representative. The zero background has no nonzero positive-current representative. If the Lie algebra of G is nonabelian, every positive θA is genuinely nonclosed, and has no d-closed representative obtained by adding dd^c.

1. DIVERGENCE AND BALANCEDNESS, PROVED FROM COMPACTNESS.
Let Zj be the dual invariant holomorphic frame. Write Ω=α1∧⋯∧αn. A positive constant multiple of Ω∧barΩ is h0^n. For any invariant holomorphic vector field Z, the Lie derivative of Ω is
  LZ Ω=−tr(ad Z)Ω.
This follows by evaluating the derivative on an invariant frame and summing the diagonal entries of the bracket action. Also LZ barΩ=0: contraction with barΩ is zero, and d barΩ=0. Hence the divergence of Z with respect to h0^n is the constant −tr(ad Z). Cartan's identity and Stokes on the compact X give
  0=∫X LZ(h0^n)=−tr(ad Z)H.
Consequently tr(ad Z)=0 and ∫X Zf μ=0 for every smooth complex-valued f. Conjugate vector fields have the same zero-divergence property.
Since dΩ=0, Cartan's identity now yields d(ιZ Ω)=0. The invariant (n−1,0) forms obtained by omitting one αj form a basis and are all closed. Their conjugates are closed as well. Every θA^(n−1) is a constant linear combination of wedges of these forms and their conjugates. Therefore
  d θA^(n−1)=0.
This proves balancedness, and in particular dd^c θA^(n−1)=0. No classification theorem for complex parallelizable manifolds is being used: the compact quotients above are the explicitly selected family.

2. THE SPECIFIED CONE AND THE ABSENCE OF dd^c DIRECTIONS.
On a complex Lie group [Zj,barZk]=0, and dαj has type (2,0). Therefore the coefficient matrix of dd^c u in the above coframe is (Zj barZk u)j,k. Each coefficient has integral zero against μ, because ∫Zj f μ=0. Thus the mean coefficient matrix of θA+dd^c u is A.
If this form is smoothly positive, then for each nonzero constant vector v, its coefficient quadratic form on v is everywhere strictly positive. Its integral is the corresponding quadratic form of A, so A>0. Conversely A>0 makes θA itself positive. If θA=dd^c u, taking mean coefficients gives A=0, so θA=0. This proves both the cone description and V∩dd^c C∞={0}.
For currents the same argument is distributional: the formal adjoints of Zj and barZk annihilate constants, so the average matrix of dd^c u is still zero. The coefficient matrix of a positive (1,1) current is a positive matrix of measures in the global frame. Its mean is therefore semipositive. Necessity of A≥0 follows, and sufficiency is provided by θA itself. If A=0, the trace measure of a positive representative has zero total mass and hence vanishes. Positivity and polarization then force the entire current to vanish.
As W contains a positive A+, its ordinary coefficient closure is precisely W∩{A≥0}: each such A is approached by A+εA+. Thus this current-representability cone is also the coefficient closure, but it will not be the metric completion of the unit-volume slice.

3. EXACT VARIATIONAL FORMULA AND THE MINIMIZERS.
The determinant identity gives θA^n=(det A)h0^n=Qμ, where Q=H det A. For any smooth positive Hermitian γ, pointwise arithmetic-geometric mean gives
  (θA∧γ^(n−1))/γ^n=(1/n)trγ θA ≥ (θA^n/γ^n)^(1/n).
Integrating yields
  ∫θA∧γ^(n−1) ≥ Q^(1/n) ∫(μ/γ^n)^(1/n)γ^n
                         =Q^(1/n)Dμ(γ).
This applies, in particular, to every competitor in the question. The balanced metric γ=θA is an admissible competitor and satisfies
  Dμ(θA)=Q^((n−1)/n),
so its quotient is Q^(1/n). Therefore λμ(θA)=Q^(1/n), exactly.
If a Gauduchon γ attains the infimum, the continuous nonnegative arithmetic-geometric-mean deficit has zero integral. Pointwise equality implies γ=fθA for a smooth positive f. Put q=f^(n−1). Since θA^(n−1) is closed, the Gauduchon equation becomes
  dd^c q∧θA^(n−1)=0.
For d^c=(i/(4π))(bar∂−∂), multiplication by q and integration by parts give
  0=∫q dd^c q∧θA^(n−1)=−∫dq∧d^c q∧θA^(n−1).
The last integrand is nonnegative and vanishes only where dq=0. Thus q, and hence f, is constant on connected X. Finally Dμ(cθA)=c^(n−1)λμ(θA)^(n−1). Therefore Dμ=1 singles out exactly γA=θA/λμ(θA). In particular, on λμ=1 the unique normalized minimizer is θA itself. This is an explicit solution of the minimizing-metric problem, not a formal Hessian computation.

4. THE HESSIAN FORMULA AND GENUINE NONCLOSEDNESS.
The proved formula is log λμ=(log H+log det A)/n. Differentiating det(A+tU) using det(I+tA⁻¹U) gives D log det[U]=tr(A⁻¹U). Differentiating A⁻¹ gives D(A⁻¹)[Z]=−A⁻¹ZA⁻¹. Consequently
  −D²log λμ[U,Z]=(1/n)tr(A⁻¹UA⁻¹Z).
It is real, symmetric, and strictly positive for U≠0, since for U=Z it is (1/n) times the squared Frobenius norm of the Hermitian matrix A⁻¹/²UA⁻¹/². Restriction to W remains positive.
Finally ∂θA=(i/(2π))Σj,k Ajk dαj∧barαk. If A is invertible and this vanishes, independence of the barαk and invertibility of A imply dαj=0 for every j. This says the Lie algebra is abelian. Therefore on a nonabelian example every positive θA is nonclosed. Since d(dd^c u)=0, adding a smooth dd^c potential cannot change this fact.

LIMITATIONS.
The invariant probability μ is part of the sufficient hypotheses. There is no claim here for arbitrary μ on these quotients or for arbitrary finite-dimensional cones on arbitrary compact complex manifolds. No Monge–Ampère existence theorem has been imported.

## w7-1/n2

FIXED-CONE METRIC THEOREM.
Take the family and exact metric proved in n1. In matrix notation let W⊂Herm(n) contain a positive matrix, fix H>0, and write
  C_W={A∈W:A>0},
  λ(A)=(H det A)^(1/n),
  S_W={A∈C_W:λ(A)=1}.
The metric is gA(U,Z)=tr(A⁻¹UA⁻¹Z)/n, restricted to the indicated manifold. All distances below are intrinsic path distances. The theorem is that S_W is complete and proper, and its completion adds no points. This holds for every such W, not merely the full matrix space. The proof also supplies explicit estimates on the minimizing metrics in n1.

1. ELEMENTARY FULL-MATRIX DISTANCE FORMULA.
For the full cone Herm+(n),
  d_full(A,B)=(1/√n)||log(A⁻¹/²BA⁻¹/²)||F.                 (1)
Here log denotes the Hermitian matrix logarithm and ||·||F the Frobenius norm. Congruence by a fixed invertible matrix is an isometry, by the trace formula for g. It is enough to prove (1) with A=I and B=C>0.
The path exp(t log C) has constant speed ||log C||F/√n and gives the upper bound.
For completeness, here is a direct lower-bound argument, so no symmetric-space distance theorem is being imported. Along a piecewise C¹ positive matrix path M(t), the ordered eigenvalues σi(t) are Lipschitz on each smooth piece and stay uniformly positive. Lipschitz continuity follows from −εI≤M(t)−M(s)≤εI and the elementary ordered-eigenvalue variational principle, with ε=||M(t)−M(s)||op. Thus fi=log σi are absolutely continuous. At almost every t,
  Σi (fi'(t))² ≤ tr((M⁻¹/²M'M⁻¹/²)²).                 (2)
To check (2), diagonalize M(t). Within an eigenspace of eigenvalue σ, the first-order eigenvalue variations are the eigenvalues of the compression of M'(t) to that eigenspace. This perturbation rule can be seen directly: conjugate M(t+s) by exp(sK), with K skew-Hermitian chosen to eliminate the off-block first-order terms between distinct eigenvalues. The resulting matrix is M(t)+s times its block-diagonal compression of M'(t)+o(s); the ordered-eigenvalue bound just used controls the o(s) eigenvalue errors. Diagonalize each compression. Its contribution to the squared norm on the right of (2) is exactly the sum of (σi'/σi)² in that block; the remaining off-block entries contribute nonnegative squares. This proves (2), including repeated eigenvalues at the almost-everywhere differentiability points.
For a path from I to C, Minkowski's inequality in Euclidean R^n now gives
  length ≥ (1/√n)∫||f'||2 ≥ (1/√n)||f(1)−f(0)||2
         = (1/√n)||log C||F.
This proves (1).
If det A=det B, the attaining curve
  M(t)=A¹/²(A⁻¹/²BA⁻¹/²)^tA¹/²                         (3)
has constant determinant. Therefore (1) is also the intrinsic distance on a full constant-determinant hypersurface. No assertion is made that (3) belongs to an arbitrary proper subspace W.

2. ESTIMATES INSIDE THE SPECIFIED SUBSPACE W.
For A,B∈S_W every W-path is also a full-matrix path. Hence
  d_W(A,B) ≥ (1/√n)||log(A⁻¹/²BA⁻¹/²)||F.              (4)
This use of an ambient lower bound cannot shorten the intrinsic distance under discussion. For an upper bound we construct a path in the specified W. Put U=B−A,
  C(t)=A+tU,
  N(t)=C(t)/λ(C(t)).
Positive matrices form a convex cone, W is linear, and homogeneity of λ implies N(t)∈S_W. Its endpoints are A and B. Set K=C⁻¹/²UC⁻¹/². Direct differentiation, using d log λ(C)/dt=tr(C⁻¹U)/n, gives
  gN(N',N')=(1/n)tr(K²)−(1/n²)(tr K)²
            ≤ ||U||F²/(n m²),
where m=min{λmin(A),λmin(B)}>0. Indeed C(t)≥mI throughout. Consequently
  d_W(A,B) ≤ ||A−B||F/(√n min{λmin(A),λmin(B)}).        (5)
This is a wholly intrinsic upper bound along the normalized chord, not along a curve in an enlarged cone.
From (4), with d=d_W(A,B), each relative eigenvalue lies in [e^(−√n d),e^(√n d)]. Equivalently,
  e^(−√n d)A ≤ B ≤ e^(√n d)A.                         (6)
The same conclusion follows along a finite-length path with its length replacing d.

3. COMPLETE CAUCHY CLASSIFICATION AND PROPERNESS.
Let (Aj) be intrinsic Cauchy in S_W. Relative to one fixed tail element, (6) gives uniform scalar bounds mI≤Aj≤MI for all sufficiently large j. For two tail elements, (6) also implies
  ||Aj−Ak||op ≤ M(e^(√n d_W(Aj,Ak))−1).
Thus Aj is a Cauchy sequence of matrix coefficients. Its limit A lies in W because W is a closed finite-dimensional linear space; A≥mI; and continuity of determinant gives H det A=1. Hence A∈S_W. Estimate (5) then gives d_W(Aj,A)→0.
Conversely, if Aj converges in coefficients to A∈S_W, estimate (5) gives intrinsic convergence. Thus the complete, exact Cauchy criterion is:
  a sequence is Cauchy if and only if its coefficient matrices converge to a positive-definite matrix in S_W.
No eigenvalue-zero, unbounded-matrix, or other point at infinity is a Cauchy boundary point.
The same inequalities show properness. A bounded distance ball about A* is contained in the matrix order interval e^(−√n R)A*≤A≤e^(√n R)A*. The coefficient and intrinsic topologies agree by (4)–(5), so a closed distance ball is a closed subset of a compact matrix order interval with fixed determinant and W-membership. It is compact. This is a direct proof, not an application of Hopf–Rinow or an imported metric-completion statement.
In particular, if λmin(Aj)→0 or λmax(Aj)→∞ on S_W, then d_W(A*,Aj)→∞. More generally an escape from every coefficient-compact subset forces distance to infinity. Distances to a fixed reference are bounded exactly when the relative eigenvalues are uniformly bounded above and away from zero; sufficiency also follows from (5).

4. MINIMIZING-GAUDUCHON ESTIMATES AND CURRENT BOUNDARIES.
By n1, on S_W the unique Dμ=1 minimizing metric is γA=θA. The matrix order estimate (6) is therefore the pointwise tensor estimate, at every x∈X,
  e^(−√n d_W(A,B))γA ≤ γB ≤ e^(√n d_W(A,B))γA.        (7)
Finite path length forces the normalized minimizing metrics to remain uniformly positive and uniformly bounded. A Cauchy sequence of these metrics converges in C∞, because they have constant coefficients in a fixed smooth coframe. Its limit is exactly the smooth positive minimizer belonging to its limit A∈S_W.
Thus every point of the completion has the canonical smooth current representative θA; there are no additional Cauchy-boundary currents. Arbitrary potentials giving other representatives are not coordinates of the specified cone and are not new completion points.
One can also rigorously compare with the positive-current coefficient boundary. By n1, its points are precisely A≥0 in W and have canonical smooth semipositive current representatives θA. For A_j∈C_W converging to a nonzero singular A0≥0, λ(A_j)→0 and the normalized minimizing matrices
  Âj=A_j/λ(A_j)∈S_W
have λmax(Âj)→∞. Hence their intrinsic distance tends to infinity. Quantitatively, for fixed A*∈S_W, generalized eigenvalue comparison and (4) give, for all sufficiently large j,
  d_W(A*,Âj) ≥ (1/√n)log(λmax(A_j)/(λ(A_j)λmax(A*)))
              = |log det A_j|/(n√n)+O(1).             (8)
Here the O(1) is bounded because A_j→A0≠0. Thus a nonzero semipositive current representative at the coefficient boundary is explicitly excluded from the unit-volume completion by blowup of the minimizing Gauduchon metric, not merely by nomenclature such as 'nef'.
At the zero matrix, normalization may remain interior, for example A_j=t_jA* with t_j→0. This is radial collapse, not a new unit-volume point: its normalized limit is the already-existing A*. There is no zero-current point of S_W. The unnormalized cone itself has infinite distance to every determinant-zero boundary. Indeed
  |d log λ(A(t))/dt|=|(1/n)tr(A⁻¹A')|≤sqrt(gA(A',A')),
so distance dominates the absolute change of log λ. This also proves infinite distance for volume tending to infinity.

SCOPE.
This is a complete intrinsic classification for the fixed linear invariant cones under n1's sufficient hypotheses. Upper bounds and completeness use curves staying in W throughout. It does not classify arbitrary nonclosed cones, arbitrary μ, or finite-distance nef boundaries arising in other geometries.

## w7-1/n3

COMPLETE SOLUTION FOR A SPECIFIED GENUINELY NONCLOSED FAMILY.
The task admits the following sufficient-condition completion theorem, with a concrete non-Kähler, genuinely nonclosed family. The theorem deliberately does not claim a classification of arbitrary finite-dimensional nonclosed cones.

I. EXPLICIT GEOMETRY AND THE SPECIFIED CONE.
Let G be C³ with multiplication
  (z1,z2,z3)·(w1,w2,w3)=(z1+w1,z2+w2,z3+w3+z1w2),
and let Γ consist of triples in Z[i]³. This is a subgroup: the group law and inverse preserve Gaussian integer coordinates. Γ is discrete. Its left action is free and properly discontinuous: for a compact K⊂G, any γ with γK∩K≠∅ belongs to the compact KK⁻¹, which contains only finitely many Γ-elements.
The quotient X=Γ\G is compact. In fact, every coset has a representative with the real and imaginary parts of all three coordinates in [0,1]. To see this, choose γ1,γ2∈Z[i] to reduce z1,z2 modulo the lattice, then choose γ3 to reduce z3+γ1z2. Away from the boundary the representative is unique: two representatives in the half-open unit cube first force γ1=γ2=0 and then γ3=0. Thus a unit real six-cube is a measurable fundamental domain. X is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension three.
The left-invariant holomorphic forms
  α1=dz1,  α2=dz2,  α3=dz3−z1dz2
are Γ-invariant and descend to a global coframe on X. They satisfy
  dα1=dα2=0,  dα3=−α1∧α2.
For every Hermitian 3×3 matrix A put
  θA=(i/(2π))Σj,k Ajk αj∧barαk.
Take the fully specified real nine-dimensional space V={θA:A∈Herm(3)}. Set h0=θI and choose μ=h0³/H, H=∫Xh0³. The auxiliary smooth positive form h in the question may be h0; nothing in λ depends on that auxiliary choice. Since α1∧α2∧α3=dz1∧dz2∧dz3, the volume density h0³ in the coordinate fundamental domain is (6/π³) times ordinary real Lebesgue volume. Hence
  H=6/π³.
This also explicitly specifies μ.
Applying the proved averaging argument in n1, V has no nonzero smooth dd^c direction, and the question's positive-representative cone is precisely
  C={θA:A>0}.
This is not merely a positive cone inserted into a larger space of representatives: smooth dd^c changes cannot enlarge it. Moreover every element of C is nonclosed. Directly, the coefficient of α1∧α2∧barα3 in ∂θA is −(i/(2π))A33, and A33>0. Thus dθA≠0. As d(dd^c u)=0, none of these backgrounds has a d-closed representative in its dd^c class.

II. EXACT VOLUME, HESSIAN, AND MINIMIZING METRICS.
All needed analytic arguments are proved in n1 rather than imported. In particular every θA with A>0 is balanced; it is an admissible Gauduchon competitor. Its top form is (H det A)μ. Pointwise arithmetic-geometric mean against arbitrary Gauduchon competitors proves exactly
  Vμ(θA)=(6/π³)det A,
  λμ(θA)=((6/π³)det A)^(1/3).
There is no appeal to a formal Hessian or a Monge–Ampère existence assertion. Actual differentiation of this established determinant formula gives
  gA(U,Z)=(1/3)tr(A⁻¹UA⁻¹Z).
This is a smooth positive definite metric on V's positive cone.
The unique minimizing Gauduchon metric after fixing Dμ(γ)=1 is
  γA=θA/((6/π³)det A)^(1/3).
Indeed equality in arithmetic-geometric mean requires a conformal multiple of θA, and its Gauduchon condition plus balancedness forces that multiple to be constant by the energy argument of n1. This supplies explicit minimizing metrics at every point, not just minimizing sequences.
The unit-volume hypersurface is
  S={θA:A>0, det A=π³/6}.
On S, γA=θA.

III. COMPLETE INTRINSIC COMPLETION AND ALL CAUCHY POINTS.
The self-contained spectral length proof in n2 yields the exact intrinsic distance on this specified full cone's unit-volume slice:
  dS(θA,θB)=(1/√3)||log(A⁻¹/²BA⁻¹/²)||F.
The attaining curve is θM(t), with
  M(t)=A¹/²(A⁻¹/²BA⁻¹/²)^tA¹/².
It has determinant π³/6 throughout and lies inside the specified V and S. Thus the displayed formula is not an extrinsic shortcut through an enlarged cone.
Every intrinsic Cauchy sequence has coefficient matrices converging to a positive-definite A with det A=π³/6, and conversely every such coefficient convergence is intrinsic convergence. This is proved by the quantitative lower and normalized-chord upper bounds in n2. Consequently
  the metric completion of S is S itself;
  there are no Cauchy boundary points, at finite coefficients or at infinity.
In fact closed bounded intrinsic balls are compact. All completion points have the canonical smooth positive current representative θA, which is also their unique Dμ=1 minimizing Gauduchon metric. Thus this is a complete classification of the completion, not just a list of some excluded coefficient-boundary points.
For a scalar reference A*=aI, a=(π³/6)^(1/3), the distance is particularly explicit:
  dS(θA*,θA)²=(1/3)Σi=1³ (log(λi(A)/a))².
The sum of these three logarithms is zero on S. Hence any eigenvalue tending to zero or infinity forces infinite distance. There are no additional asymptotic Cauchy directions.

IV. CURRENT REPRESENTATIVES AND FINITE/INFINITE DISTANCE ESTIMATES.
Positive-current representability of a coefficient background θA, even permitting distributional potentials, is precisely A≥0, by the distributional averaging proof of n1. Its canonical representative is the smooth semipositive current θA. The zero background has only the zero positive-current representative. Thus the entire positive-current coefficient closure is identified explicitly. It must not be confused with the metric completion.
On S, the unique normalized minimizing metrics obey at every point of X
  e^(−√3 dS(A,B))γA ≤ γB ≤ e^(√3 dS(A,B))γA.
Therefore finite length gives uniform lower and upper positivity bounds on these minimizing metrics. A Cauchy sequence of them converges smoothly to a positive invariant metric. Conversely every minimizing-metric eigenvalue collapse or explosion on S occurs at infinite distance, quantitatively detected by the logarithmic distance above.
More concretely, suppose Aj>0 converges to a nonzero singular semipositive A0. The unnormalized coefficient boundary has the current θA0, but λμ(θAj)→0. Its unit-volume normalized minimizing metrics correspond to
  Âj=Aj/λμ(θAj),
and their largest eigenvalue tends to infinity. The rigorous estimate from n2 is
  dS(A*,Âj) ≥ |log det Aj|/(3√3)+O(1) →∞.
All nonzero semipositive coefficient boundary currents are therefore excluded from the completion even after attempting unit-volume normalization. Approaching the zero coefficient matrix can be pure radial scaling; its normalization may converge to an existing point of S, but it never gives a zero-current point of the completion. On the unnormalized cone, distance dominates the change in log λ, so volume tending to zero or infinity has infinite length as well.
This distinguishes finite from infinite distance directly in terms of the actual minimizing Gauduchon metrics: finite Cauchy behavior is exactly smooth convergence to a uniformly positive invariant minimizer; every true unit-volume degeneracy is infinitely distant.

V. A MORE GENERAL SUFFICIENT-CONDITION CLASSIFICATION, WITH THE CONE KEPT FIXED.
The same conclusions hold on every compact quotient Γ\G in n1, with the normalized invariant μ, and every separately specified real linear subspace W⊂Herm(n) containing a positive matrix. Its prescribed space is V_W={θA:A∈W}; its prescribed cone is exactly W∩{A>0}; its volume is H det A; and its unit-volume completion adds no points.
For proper W the full-matrix formula is only an intrinsic lower bound, not necessarily an equality. Intrinsic upper estimates instead use the normalized straight chord entirely inside W:
  d_{S_W}(A,B) ≤ ||A−B||F/(√n min{λmin A,λmin B}).
Together with the full-matrix lower estimate, this proves the complete Cauchy classification and properness without ever enlarging the specified cone in an upper-bound argument. On nonabelian G all its positive invariant backgrounds are genuinely nonclosed.
The full Iwasawa cone above is the fully explicit instance, with real cone dimension nine and unit-volume dimension eight. It is different from a closed projective product cone or a blowup-surface cone, and provides the requested nonclosed family. The conclusion in this family is that no positive-current coefficient boundary is a finite-distance unit-volume boundary; it does not assert the same for general cones, where finite-distance boundary behavior may occur.
No external metric-geometry theorem or source premise is required: the distance formula, properness, and completeness were independently proved in n2 by spectral variation, matrix inequalities, and curves inside the fixed cone.
