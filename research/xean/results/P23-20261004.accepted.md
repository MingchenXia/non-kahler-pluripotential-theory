## w7-1/n1

THEOREM: AN ELEMENTARY NONNORMAL, NON-CONE SUFFICIENT-CONDITION FAMILY

This is a scoped dimension-one result. Its geometric hypotheses are explicit and do not include existence, uniqueness, or continuity of the desired solution.

Geometric hypotheses:
(1) X is a connected compact reduced complex curve, locally irreducible, and there is a finite bijective normalization map π:E→X, where E=C/Λ is a complex torus. The map is biholomorphic away from a finite set. (Equivalently, the normalization of the unibranch curve is this torus.)
(2) h is any smooth Hermitian background in the local-embedding sense in the question. No closed ambient extension is required.

Let dA be flat Euclidean area on E, let V=∫E dA, and put β0=dA/V, so ∫Eβ0=1. The smooth pullback β=π*h can be written β=bβ0 with b smooth and nonnegative. It is positive on the inverse image of X_reg, but it is NOT assumed positive everywhere. In particular, it can vanish at a cusp. Put H=∫Eβ=∫E bβ0>0. In real dimension two every smooth 2-form is closed; this is the reason the argument works for arbitrary h in this dimension.

Singularity hypotheses and the actual class:
Choose distinct points q_1,...,q_k of E and positive numbers a_1,...,a_k, with A=Σ_j a_j<H. The empty list, A=0, is allowed. The reference ψ below is a genuine h-potential. The specified class consists of ALL genuine h-potentials w with w≤ψ+C and ψ≤w+C for some finite C, interpreted by these inequalities at common −∞ values. No smoothness or continuity of w is assumed.

Measure hypotheses:
Choose a nonnegative f∈L^p(E,β0) for a finite p>1, with F=∫E fβ0>0. The prescribed measure is ν=π_*(fβ0). This is finite and non-pluripolar. The condition is on the normalization-side density, not on a density with respect to the possibly degenerating h-volume on X.

Conclusions:
Every potential in the specified class has finite extended non-pluripolar mass M=H−A. There is a solution of
 MA_ext,h(u)=cν,   sup_X u=0,
with c=M/F>0, in the specified ordinary type. It is unique among all genuine potentials in that type, even if a competing solution is initially rough and has its own unknown positive proportionality constant. Unnormalized solutions differ by constants, with equality of canonical extended values at common poles. The solution is continuous as a real function away from its finite pole set, and it is continuous into R∪{−∞}, with the order topology, at its poles. Its residual relative to the specified reference, initially defined away from the poles, extends to a continuous real-valued function on all of X. No subtraction of −∞ from −∞ is used. The equation, class, and continuity statements are independent of resolutions and common refinements.

Here is a complete elementary construction and proof.

1. Normalization of dd^c and a periodic Green function.
In a flat coordinate z=x+iy,
 dd^c v=(Δv/(4π)) dA.
Indeed, ∂bar∂ has the usual scalar coefficient Δ/4 and i dz∧dbar z=2dA. The divergence theorem gives Δ log|z|²=4πδ0, hence, with exactly the normalization in the question,
 dd^c log|z|²=δ0.
There is no factor 2 missing in the pole weights below.

We construct, rather than invoke an elliptic existence theorem, a real distribution G on E with
 dd^c G=δ0−β0,   ∫E Gβ0=0,
and G(z)=log|z|²+smooth near 0, smooth elsewhere. Choose a smooth cutoff supported in a small coordinate disk, equal to 1 near 0, and let L be the cutoff times log|z|², extended by zero. Then
 dd^c L=δ0+qβ0,
where q is smooth and ∫qβ0=−1: pair this distributional equality with the constant function 1. Thus q+1 has mean zero. If ξ ranges over the dual lattice and −Δ e_ξ=λ_ξe_ξ, λ_ξ>0 for ξ≠0, solve
 dd^c S=−(q+1)β0
by the Fourier coefficients S_ξ=(4π/V)(q+1)_ξ/λ_ξ, ξ≠0. Repeated integration by parts shows the coefficients of q+1 decay faster than every power, so this series and every derivative converge uniformly. Set G=L+S and subtract its mean. This proves all the claimed properties. In particular G∈L^r(E,β0) for every finite r, because a logarithm has this integrability.

For r∈L^p and the group convolution K=G*(rβ0), distributional differentiation gives
 dd^c K=(r−∫E rβ0)β0.
The calculation follows directly by pairing with a smooth test function and using dd^c_zG(z−t)=δ_t−β0. Fubini is legitimate since G is integrable and the test-function convolution is smooth. Smooth mean-zero r gives smooth K, either from the Fourier construction or by multiplying the rapidly decaying Fourier coefficients.

For r∈L^p, p>1, K is continuous and bounded. With p'=p/(p−1), Hölder gives
 |K(z+s)−K(z)|≤||r||_p ||G(·+s)−G||_{p'}.
Translations are continuous in L^{p'}: approximate G in that norm by smooth functions and use their uniform translation continuity. Also ||K||_∞≤||G||_{p'}||r||_p. These observations independently give the precise continuity assertion needed here; no continuity theorem on singular spaces is invoked.

2. Explicit genuine reference and solution upstairs.
Define the smooth function
 Φ=G*((H−b)β0).
The density has mean zero, and
 dd^cΦ=(H−b)β0.
Define the canonical extended-valued function
 Ψ(z)=Φ(z)+Σ_j a_jG(z−q_j).
It is finite and smooth away from Q={q_j}, equals −∞ at each q_j, and near q_j has the form a_j log|ζ|² plus a smooth function. Directly,
 β+dd^cΨ=Mβ0+Σ_j a_jδ_{q_j},   M=H−A>0.
Thus Ψ is genuinely β-subharmonic; its local sums with smooth primitives of β are genuine subharmonic representatives. A smooth local primitive exists by solving the scalar smooth Laplace equation in a coordinate disk, for example by convolving a compactly supported smooth extension of its density with log|z|². Positivity of the displayed measure gives subharmonicity by local smoothing; the logarithmic and smooth expressions fix the canonical upper values.

Set c=M/F and r=cf−M, which has mean zero. Put
 v=G*(rβ0),   U=Ψ+v.
The preceding estimates make v a bounded continuous real function. Moreover
 β+dd^c U=cfβ0+Σ_j a_jδ_{q_j}.
It is a positive measure, so U is genuinely β-subharmonic with its canonical values, and U is in the ordinary type of Ψ. It tends to −∞ at Q. Its supremum S is finite: it is upper semicontinuous on the compact torus, not identically −∞, and its positive singularities are bounded above. Subtract S for normalization.

3. Descent is proved, not assumed.
A finite bijective proper map between the present Hausdorff compact spaces is a homeomorphism. Thus the only possible descended functions are ψ=Ψ∘π^{-1} and u=(U−S)∘π^{-1}; their fibers are singleton fibers and their canonical values agree exactly, including the value −∞ at a pole.

Every holomorphic disk f:D→X has a holomorphic lift F:D→E. For a nonconstant disk, f^{-1}(X_sing) is discrete. On its complement the lift is holomorphic because π is biholomorphic there. The continuous lift π^{-1}∘f stays in a bounded coordinate neighborhood at each omitted point; the usual one-variable removable-singularity argument for its coordinate function therefore extends it holomorphically. Constant disks have constant lifts. A nonconstant disk cannot have image in the finite singular set.

On a sufficiently small disk take a local smooth primitive p of β on the image of F. Then p∘F is a smooth potential for f*h, since F*β=f*h. The function
 (u∘f)+(p∘F)=(U−S+p)∘F
is subharmonic, or identically −∞, by holomorphic composition of a subharmonic function. This composition property follows, for example, by decreasing smooth subharmonic regularizations and the smooth chain rule Δ(v∘F)=|F'|²(Δv)∘F. Any other smooth primitive for f*h differs by a harmonic function. Therefore the exact disk tests in the question hold. A constant disk in a pole has identically −∞ restriction; no distributional derivative is assigned to that restriction. If a nonconstant lifted disk meets q_j with order m, the local logarithmic coefficient is m a_j, as follows from log|ζ(F(t))|²=m log|t|²+smooth. This also records the disk ramification factor explicitly.

The homeomorphism and the explicit logarithmic behavior prove the canonical upper-value statements downstairs. They also prove real continuity off π(Q) and extended continuity to −∞ at π(Q). The residual v−S descends to a continuous REAL function on all of X. This function, and not an undefined subtraction at the poles, is the asserted residual.

4. Non-pluripolar measure and finite mass of the entire type.
In dimension one, for a β-subharmonic function W which is locally bounded away from Q and has value −∞ precisely at Q, put T_W=β+dd^cW. The non-pluripolar measure is
 <β+dd^cW>=1_{E\Q}T_W.
This follows directly from the truncation definition: on each relatively compact set avoiding Q a sufficiently low truncation equals W, so the linear product is T_W; the pole set is removed by the indicator in that definition. No higher-dimensional continuity or comparison theorem is used.

For any genuine downstairs w in the ordinary type, W=w∘π is locally β-subharmonic by applying the disk tests to normalization coordinate disks. The type inequalities imply that W is finite and locally bounded on E\Q and, near q_j,
 W=a_j log|ζ|²+O(1),
with W(q_j)=−∞. It is locally integrable and T_W is a positive distribution, hence a positive Radon measure. Its total mass is H, since the distribution dd^cW pairs to zero with 1 on the compact torus.

We verify the atom weights, without an assumption about a competing solution's regularity. If s=W+p is subharmonic near q_j, where dd^cp=β, its circular mean m(t) satisfies, for almost every t,
 m'(t)=2 T_W(B_t)/t.
This is the divergence theorem for smooth approximants; local convergence of their Laplacians gives the distributional version. If τ=T_W({q_j}), then T_W(B_t)→τ and integrating the identity shows
 lim_{t→0} m(t)/log t²=τ.
But W=a_j log|ζ|²+O(1), and p is smooth, so the same limit is a_j. Consequently T_W({q_j})=a_j. The coefficient is invariant under a change of normalization coordinate, since the difference of the two logarithms is smooth. It follows that EVERY genuine potential in the entire ordinary type has
 ∫E<β+dd^cW>=H−Σ_j a_j=M<∞.

For the constructed solution, removal of these atoms gives <β+dd^cU>=cfβ0. Hence, with the original normalization unchanged,
 MA_ext,h(u)=π_*(cfβ0)=cν,
and its total mass is cν(X)=cF=M.

The measure ν is non-pluripolar. A nontrivial local quasi-subharmonic representative pulls back to a nontrivial local quasi-subharmonic function on E; its −∞ set has flat area zero, because such a function is locally integrable. Thus local pluripolar sets have zero β0-area and zero fβ0-measure. The homeomorphism transfers this fact to ν on X.

5. Uniqueness in the ENTIRE ordinary class.
Let w be any genuine potential in the specified class satisfying MA_ext,h(w)=dν for some d>0. Its extended mass is M, so dF=M and d=c. Alternatively this follows from the atom calculation and total distributional mass H. Since π is a Borel homeomorphism, the equation implies
 T_W=cfβ0+Σ_j a_jδ_{q_j}=T_U.
Thus dd^c(W−U)=0 as distributions on E. The difference is an L^1 distribution; all its nonzero Fourier coefficients vanish by the flat Laplacian equation. It is therefore a constant distribution. The corresponding local subharmonic representatives agree everywhere, not merely almost everywhere: a genuine subharmonic value is the limit of its shrinking disk averages, so its almost-everywhere class determines its canonical values. Hence W=U+K with genuine extended values; in particular both sides are −∞ at Q. The same equality holds downstairs. A supremum normalization fixes K. This proves uniqueness among rough potentials too, rather than only among the explicitly constructed solutions.

6. Resolution independence and the precise measure transformation.
Any resolution ρ:Y→X in dimension one factors holomorphically through E: the continuous map π^{-1}∘ρ is holomorphic by the same local disk lifting/removability argument. The resulting map r:Y→E is a nonconstant proper degree-one map of smooth compact curves. Fibers are finite and the sum of local multiplicities is the degree; degree one makes every local multiplicity one and every fiber a singleton. Thus r is biholomorphic. The same applies to every common refinement. There are no additional exceptional-fiber values or measure terms to lose. Consequently both the class and the computed measure are unchanged on all such resolutions and refinements.

The normalization map in this theorem has generic degree ONE; its possible vanishing differential at a cusp does not change that degree. The formula ν=π_*(fβ0) has no extra degree factor. On X_reg, if β=bβ0, then ν has density (f/b)∘π^{-1} relative to h. Here b contains exactly the derivative/Jacobian factor of the pulled-back h. At singular points this density formula is not used; both fβ0 and its pushforward assign those finitely many points zero mass. The pullback β was only assumed semipositive, and its degeneration was explicitly compensated by Φ in the construction, rather than silently discarded.

Scope and source audit: the theorem requires n=1, torus normalization, finite logarithmic ordinary type, A<H, and the stated L^p condition. It does not address arbitrary higher-dimensional unibranch singularities or arbitrary singularity types. No external Monge–Ampère existence/uniqueness/continuity theorem, uniformization theorem, or log-terminal result is invoked. The solvability and continuity arguments are the explicit cutoff/Fourier/convolution arguments above; the uniqueness argument is linear distribution theory. The one-dimensional non-pluripolar computation is the direct truncation computation. The next note constructs and checks a compact non-cone example satisfying all the geometric hypotheses.

## w7-1/n2

EXPLICIT COMPACT NON-CONE EXAMPLE AND COMPLETE SCOPED SOLUTION PACKAGE

This note uses the fully proved sufficient-condition theorem in n1. It supplies an explicit compact example, checking rather than assuming its geometry, positivity properties, and normalization factors. Together the notes give a complete sufficient-condition answer scoped to dimension one.

1. Explicit torus functions, with the needed facts reconstructed.
Let Λ=Z+iZ and E=C/Λ, with β0=dx∧dy of total mass 1. Define
 x(z)=1/z²+Σ_{λ∈Λ\{0}}[(z−λ)^{-2}−λ^{-2}],
 y(z)=x'(z).
The series converges normally on compact subsets away from Λ: its summands are O(|λ|^{-3}) there, and this series converges on a two-dimensional lattice. Pairing λ and −λ proves x is even. The differentiated series for y is absolutely normally convergent and reindexing makes y periodic. Therefore x(z+ω)−x(z) is constant; for ω=1 or i, evaluation at z=−ω/2 and evenness make that constant zero. Thus x and y are meromorphic functions on E. At 0,
 x=z^{-2}+a_2 z²+a_4 z⁴+...,   y=−2z^{-3}+2a_2z+4a_4z³+... .
There are no other poles on E.

The elliptic function y²−4x³+g_2x+g_3 has no pole and has zero constant Laurent coefficient if g_2=20a_2 and g_3=28a_4. It is constant by the maximum principle on the compact torus and that constant is zero. Hence
 y²=4x³−g_2x−g_3.
For the square lattice g_3=0 also follows by the rotation of the defining lattice sums, but its value is immaterial here.

The meromorphic function x has degree two. Indeed the argument principle on a translated fundamental parallelogram gives two zeros, counted with multiplicity, of x−c for every finite c: opposite boundary integrals cancel, and its only pole has order two. This also proves the following elementary facts used in checking the embedding. A fiber of x consists of z and −z, counted with multiplicity. If x'(z)=0, its local multiplicity is exactly two and z=−z modulo Λ: otherwise z and −z would give at least four zeros. At such a point x''(z)≠0. Thus (x,y) is injective on E\{0}: equality of x gives w=z or −z, and equality of the odd function y either gives w=z or y=0, in which case −z=z. The map (x,y) is immersive there: if y≠0 use dx, and if y=0 use dy=x''dz. These arguments avoid reliance on a uniformization theorem or on an unchecked nonsingularity assertion for a cubic model.

2. The projective curve and its cusp.
Define
 π:E→P^4,   z↦[1:x(z):y(z):x(z)²:x(z)³]
away from 0, and set π(0)=[0:0:0:0:1]. The latter extension is holomorphic. In the last affine chart the coordinates are
 [s³,s²,t,s,1],   s=1/x=z²+O(z⁶),   t=y/x³=−2z³+O(z⁷).
The relation above becomes
 t²=s³(4−g_2s²−g_3s³).
The factor in parentheses is a nonvanishing analytic unit near s=0. Replacing t by t divided by its analytic square root makes the relation exactly t'^2=s³. If desired, take a normalization coordinate ζ satisfying s=ζ²; the parametrization is then t'=±ζ³. Hence the local image is the ordinary reduced irreducible cusp with local ring C{ζ²,ζ³}. Its normalization is C{ζ}, finite over the cusp ring (generated as module by 1 and ζ).

Away from 0, the first projective coordinate is nonzero, and the already proved properties of (x,y) make π injective and immersive. No such point maps to π(0). Therefore π is globally injective. Its image X is a compact reduced analytic curve: locally it is a smooth graph away from π(0), and the explicit analytic cusp above at π(0). Compactness and injectivity exclude unwanted additional local branches. Equivalently it is the projective closure of the affine algebraic graph given by y²=4x³−g_2x−g_3, the other coordinates x²,x³, so it is projective.

Thus X is connected, compact, reduced, of pure dimension one, locally irreducible and unibranch. It has exactly one singular point p=π(0). The map π is proper, finite, bijective and birational, with the local normalization just checked, so it is its normalization. In particular π is a homeomorphism. Its differential vanishes at 0; it is not an immersion there.

This curve is not a projective cone of the usual type mentioned in the question. A reduced irreducible projective cone of dimension one has a zero-dimensional reduced irreducible base, hence a single point, and is a projective line. Our curve has a cusp and its normalization is the genus-one torus E, not P^1. More intrinsically, no change of projective embedding turns this curve into such a cone. The example is also not an application of a log-terminal continuity theorem: the cusp is nonnormal, whereas that terminology requires normality.

3. Background, total mass, and the actual degeneracy.
Take h to be the restriction of the Fubini–Study form on P^4, using exactly
 dd^c=(i/(2π))∂bar∂,
so its affine local potential is log(Σ|Z_j|²) after choosing a nonzero homogeneous coordinate. This is smooth and strictly positive in the ambient projective coordinate charts, as required by the local-embedding definition of h.

Set β=π*h=bβ0. It is smooth and positive off 0, but not positive at 0. In the last chart s=z²+..., t=−2z³+..., so the derivatives of all affine coordinate functions vanish at 0. Strict ambient positivity, with the leading derivative ds=2z dz+..., gives constants C_1,C_2>0 such that
 C_1|z|²≤b(z)≤C_2|z|²
for z near 0. In particular b(0)=0. This degeneracy is part of the example, not overlooked by a positivity assertion.

The total background mass is H=6 with the stated dd^c convention. To check it directly, on E\{0} write
 L(z)=log(1+|x|²+|y|²+|x|⁴+|x|⁶).
Then dd^cL=β there. The last-chart formula and x=z^{-2}+... show
 L=−6 log|z|²+smooth
near 0; the smooth term is the last-chart Fubini–Study potential plus a smooth change of local trivialization. Thus globally as distributions
 dd^cL=β−6δ0.
Pairing with 1 on E gives ∫Eβ=6. This independently checks the curvature normalization and the integer 6; it is not inferred from an unspecified convention for degree. The full map π has generic degree one, even though x alone has degree two.

4. Concrete nonconstant data and a genuine singularity type.
Apply n1 with one pole q=0 of weight a=1. Let G be its explicitly constructed mean-zero Green function and put
 Φ=G*((6−b)β0),   Ψ=Φ+G.
Then
 β+dd^cΨ=5β0+δ0.
Descend Ψ to the genuine reference ψ on X. Its actual ordinary singularity type is weight-one logarithmic type at the cusp: on the normalization it is log|z|²+smooth, with canonical value −∞ at 0. It has no other poles. The class is exactly all genuine h-potentials satisfying the two bounded-shift inequalities against ψ; rough potentials are included.

Choose
 f(z)=1+(1/2)cos(2π Re z),   ν=π_*(fβ0).
This is an everywhere positive smooth normalization density, with F=1. The measure downstairs has no atom at the cusp and is non-pluripolar. Near the cusp its density relative to h on the regular locus is f/b, which grows like |z|^{-2}; no bounded-density hypothesis with respect to h is being smuggled in.

The proportionality constant required by the original equation is
 c=(H−a)/F=5.
The correction in n1 is particularly explicit. Since β0=dx∧dy on this unit square torus,
 dd^c cos(2πx)=−π cos(2πx)β0.
Consequently
 v(z)=−(5/(2π))cos(2π Re z)
satisfies dd^cv=(5f−5)β0 and has mean zero; it is exactly the Green convolution correction. Therefore
 U=Ψ+v,   β+dd^cU=5fβ0+δ0.
Let S=sup_E U and set u=(U−S)∘π^{-1}. By the fully proved descent and measure arguments of n1,
 sup_Xu=0,   MA_ext,h(u)=5ν,
and its extended mass is 5, not 6. The omitted mass is the pole atom of weight one; this number uses dd^c log|z|²=δ0, not a different logarithmic convention.

All genuine potentials in this entire ordinary type have mass 5. Any genuine solution in this class, even with an initially unknown positive c and without a continuity assumption, has c=5 and equals u up to a constant. Supremum normalization makes it u. Thus this is not merely uniqueness among smooth normalization-side solutions.

The potential u is real-continuous on X\{p}. It tends to −∞ at p and is continuous as an extended real-valued function there. The residual, defined away from p by u−ψ, is the descended continuous real function v−S on all of X. We never define −∞−(−∞). Ambient smoothness downstairs at the cusp is not claimed. The construction and uniqueness force the same continuity for every solution in the type.

For an everywhere finite variant, take the empty pole list in n1 on this SAME singular curve. Then ψ=Φ is bounded and the specified ordinary type is the full bounded class. With the same f one obtains c=6 and the correction v=−(3/π)cos(2π Re z); the potential itself, not just a residual, is real-continuous everywhere downstairs. Thus the example family covers both bounded potentials and a nontrivial pole type.

5. Complete-result statement and limitations.
The sufficient hypotheses are the geometric torus-normalization assumptions, arbitrary smooth local-embedding Hermitian h in dimension one, a finite logarithmic reference with total weight A<H, and prescribed ν=π_*(fβ0) with f≥0 in L^p, p>1 and positive integral. They yield finite mass of the whole type, existence, constant-difference uniqueness in the whole genuine type, canonical descent, and precisely stated continuity. The explicit nonnormal projective cusp above checks that this family is nonempty and lies beyond the projective-cone and log-terminal backgrounds. It also displays a pullback background with genuine degeneracy, which the proof compensates by a constructed smooth correction rather than treating it as positive.

All relevant measure transformations, background mass, lost pole mass, degree one, and local Jacobian behavior were checked above and in n1. All resolutions and common refinements are biholomorphic to the normalization, as proved in n1, so the statement has the required independence. No existence, uniqueness, descent, positivity of the degenerate pullback, or continuity conclusion is an assumption. No external nonlinear PDE or singular-space regularity theorem is imported; the elementary Green/Fourier proof and the elliptic-function example have been reconstructed. This completes the exact requested sufficient-condition package in the explicitly stated one-dimensional scope. It is not a classification of arbitrary unibranch singularities and makes no claim for n>1 or for arbitrary prescribed singularity classes.
