## Additional Hermitian structures

Gauduchon metrics supply conserved linear traces, positivity duality, smooth equation adjoints, a prescribed-volume equation with its nonlinear estimates reconstructed, weak determinant duality, finite variational volumes and finite-dimensional metric geometry. Chapters 4, 9, 15 and 27 give the proofs. Chapter 11 gives the cofactor identity, proves C2 uniqueness for continuous densities positive on a dense set on arbitrary Hermitian surfaces, and treats C1,1 extrema and connectedness, threefold torsion signs, the density sublevel threshold, and Guan–Li bounded comparison. These constructions do not restore conservation of nonlinear mass.

The equation chapters prove smooth and weak Hermitian solvability, minimal ordinary-type Lp equations for arbitrary smooth big backgrounds without BMP/PVP, bounded and prescribed ordinary-type equations for Lp densities in the stated semipositive regimes, stability, Hölder regularity in the strict Hermitian setting, and general non-pluripolar equations with any reference satisfying the smaller-background model condition, in a semipositive big background under positive lower volume. Relative domination is proved without assuming finite mass of the original measures. BMP is not a standing assumption. For arbitrary smooth big backgrounds, both general NPP measure equations are reconstructed for any model reference with positive lower volume under BMP, together with unrestricted weighted addition and the ordinary-type Lp refinement. The relative projection is based on an explicit cross-class rooftop. Metric PVP is automatic from those hypotheses, so this is an equivalent formulation of the known regime. The remaining extension beyond BMP and other proof boundaries are listed in `proof-obligations.md`.

Chapter 11 includes complete projective nonuniqueness constructions: an open zero-density band, two hypersurfaces with infinite-order or exactly quadratic density zeros, and a prescribed real analytic density. The last example works in every dimension at least three and proves sharpness of the square-root sublevel exponent. The two smooth hypersurface extensions are also given in the standalone note. Chapter 13 proves selection of the explicit symmetric C1,1 solution by the whole normalized exponential family for the original symmetric data, including loss of C2 regularity at positive exponent. The selection statement is not extended to the modified densities.

The source of the new uniqueness criteria and analytic-density construction is archived with its checksum and inclusion map in the research records. Its results are incorporated directly with proofs; no formal citation to the user-supplied manuscripts appears in the book.

## Connections to non-Kähler geometry

Chapter 32 relates the developed tools to nef-to-big and Fujiki-class questions, the positivity of Gauduchon variational volume, Hermitian null loci and Chern–Ricci flow, degeneracy and selection of equation solutions, coexistence of balanced and pluriclosed metrics, and transcendental Morse inequalities. Each connection distinguishes the known theorem from the additional estimate or implication being sought.

Chapters 29–31 add ordinary closed Bott–Chern loci, the full supplied Nakamaye proof, and LCK geometry. The ordinary class, the quotient of nonclosed backgrounds by ddc-potentials, and the twisted Bott–Chern class are distinct data. The first has no big class on a strict LCK manifold; the latter two still support different equation problems. The same Hopf background is Vaisman and has unbounded ordinary-potential mass. The new LCK chapter proves Gauduchon harmonicity, Lee-degree identities, the closed-bigness obstruction, and the Chern–Ricci-flat Gauduchon rigidity theorem. Section31.8 also reconstructs Lee–Gauduchon strict duality and bimeromorphic invariance on arbitrary compact complex manifolds, using classical analytic weak factorization. Its Calabi–Eckmann example has zero cone but no strongly Gauduchon metric; the cone does not test every positive de Rham-exact Bott–Chern class. These results require no BMP. The chapter retains the external transverse existence theorem in its exact invariant-volume regime. The supplied general Nakamaye proof is fully adapted in Chapter 30. Its weak nef-difference criterion is proved through the smooth equation and Gauduchon duality. Two local auxiliary lemmas have general Hermitian reference metrics; the main numerical theorem remains in its closed Kähler regime.
