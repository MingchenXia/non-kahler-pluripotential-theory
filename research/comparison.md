Sections 15.6.4 and 29.12.3 develop convergence for every smooth obstacle. The resulting uniformity is covariant under smooth gauges and positive scaling, and its analytic closure is exactly ordinary/ideal envelope equality for all smooth obstacles. On curves this closure equals the zero-obstacle closure. The new pseudometric has complete canonical ideal loci, while the whole ordinary space remains incomplete even on a fixed positive-mass model family. On ordinary curve models its topology is strictly between the zero-obstacle topology and polar total variation. All-obstacle convergence restores smooth-gauge covariance but remains nonseparated on ordinary classes and incomplete on general curve models. Its analytic closure requires equality at every smooth obstacle. This is equivalent to zero-obstacle equality on curves and, in higher dimension, under bounded mass with positive lower volume of the ideal representative. Relative-envelope membership suffices in any smooth background. Compact holomorphic submersions with finite positive weighted prime divisors now give exact reduction of both obstacle envelopes to the base and zero mass for every relevant ordinary representative, including vertical components. Singularities, ramification and complete fibers in the pole set are allowed; the induced obstacle may be infinite. For a positive curve base, zero-obstacle equality is equivalent to atomic polar curvature and to all-obstacle equality. A specified Hopf-threefold product has all-obstacle equality and zero model volumes while the ambient Hermitian background fails bounded mass; its base potential satisfies the proved relative-envelope condition. Relative-envelope membership is exactly that of the base, including the ideal references, even when complete fibers lie in the pole divisor; every relevant ordinary representative still has zero mass. This follows from ideal cancellation and direct model comparison. Both continuous-obstacle envelope identities and the full all-obstacle equivalence now allow infinite fiber minima. Truncated divisor potentials, ideal-limit identification and Hartogs control prove the converse; see [the whole-fiber criterion](editorial/whole-fiber-all-obstacles-275.md). A connected Kahler-surface example shows that disconnected fibers can obstruct both envelope identities; see [the explicit counterexample](editorial/disconnected-fiber-obstruction-277.md). The [component-cover reduction](editorial/component-cover-reduction-278.md) restores both continuous-obstacle identities and the all-obstacle equivalence on the finite covering base, while preserving zero masses and singularity volumes. The explicit weighted ideal-product formula still requires relative simple normal crossings; a smooth branched graph violates it at weight 3/4 even though its envelope reduction holds. The unrestricted higher-dimensional zero-lower-volume and no-BMP implications remain unresolved here. Polar total variation remains the stronger complete curve-model topology. See [the all-obstacle review](editorial/all-obstacle-topology-228.md), [the relative-envelope refinement](editorial/obstacle-projection-229.md), [the product criterion](editorial/zero-volume-product-obstacles-267.md), and [the fibration extension](editorial/fibration-zero-volume-obstacles-268.md), and [the exact envelope equivalences](editorial/fibration-envelope-equivalences-269.md), and [the weighted-divisor extension](editorial/weighted-fibration-divisors-271.md).
