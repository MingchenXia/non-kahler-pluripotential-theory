## From local potentials to nonlinear mass

The polar order and variation-capacity estimates support the later curve topology. The Holder trace-mass and path-distance theorem is the foundation for the distinction between finite-distance singularities and continuous potentials with complete ends.

Chapters 2-4 explain what closedness used to provide. The mass polynomial yields the smooth/bounded mass-invariance criterion. The raw Monge-Ampere one-form has a local primitive only under the proved closedness condition; a Gauduchon surface can conserve mass without supplying that primitive. Gauduchon traces, positive-current duality and adjoint weights provide the corresponding linear tools.

## Sections, modifications and metric geometry

Chapter 24 constructs compatible ideal-envelope systems and their decreasing retraction. Recovery means preservation of each finite slice's multiplier ideals.
