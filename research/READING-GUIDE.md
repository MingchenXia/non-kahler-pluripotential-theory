## Foundations

Chapter 1 develops the elementary calculus, isolated-puncture extension in dimension at least two with a fixed smooth background, periodic subharmonic rigidity, smooth conformal-current regularity, ordered analytic-support rigidity, normalized compactness, exponential integrability, local products, bounded smooth-background products and their pullback by holomorphic submersions, Hermitian capacity and its volume/sublevel estimates, regularization, multiplier ideals, divisorial estimates, global smooth-residual potentials and canonical divisorial ordinary types, obstacle envelopes, the minimum principle and pointwise Legendre duality, pullback and descent, convergence against a fixed non-pluripolar measure, bounded-carrier integrability and coordinate smoothing of measure data. The analytic regularization with a prescribed multiplier-ideal type is kept separate from the decreasing sequence with smooth resolved remainders. The Chern regularization estimates retain their different boundedness and background hypotheses.

Chapter 2 proves the smooth and bounded mass-invariance criterion directly after its mass polynomial. Chapters 2–4 explain torsion, mass variation, the obstruction to a primitive of the raw Monge–Ampère one-form, and Gauduchon traces and positivity. Chapter 3 defines the radial energy once for all smooth backgrounds and computes its closed and nonclosed first variations. A Gauduchon surface has invariant smooth mass, but this alone does not give the raw energy primitive. Persistence under potential perturbation in higher dimensions requires the precise mass-invariance identities.

Chapter 5 identifies normalized potentials with their finite Gauduchon traces. Its linear inverse L1 estimate and square-root forward estimate also apply to unbounded potentials. These linear estimates do not control nonlinear Monge–Ampère mass.

Chapter 6 extends the first-chapter bounded products to non-pluripolar measures, then proves the unbounded density and linear-trace inequalities. Chapter 7 develops finite weighted measures, their capacity continuity and the uniform-tail criterion for undamped convergence. Chapter 8 gives the Hopf counterexample. Infinite determinant integrals can coexist with coefficient-L1 convergence of currents and total-variation convergence of their traces. The explicit Hopf geometry also has a sharp fiber-average coercivity estimate; it is not a general mass hypothesis.

## Equations and singularity types

Chapters 9–11 develop smooth and continuous equations, their scalar normalization and potential uniqueness or nonuniqueness. Keep a unique scalar, a unique potential and a selected approximation limit distinct. For a smooth nonnegative density with connected dense positive set, Section 11.6 proves uniqueness of bounded normalized solutions by level energies and concentrating test potentials. Its continuity input follows from the proper equation in Chapter 10. Positivity almost everywhere alone remains insufficient.

Chapter 12 applies the first-chapter envelope tools to contact sets, domination, extended volumes and relative envelope classes. General stretching and finite rooftop arguments retain arbitrary smooth backgrounds; model positivity and the stronger comparison statements retain their own assumptions.

Chapter 13 develops prescribed-singularity equations without a blanket metric mass hypothesis. It includes weighted lower semicontinuity for extended measures, measure representations, equations in a fixed ordinary type, and minimal-type Lp equations for arbitrary smooth big backgrounds. Global Lp data, p>1, supply the local Holder input for interior smoothness on the strict-barrier locus. Smooth density on an arbitrary open set alone does not supply that integrability. The local boundary examples explain this distinction. The chapter also treats individual-density thresholds and exponential approximations.

Chapter 14 studies normalization functionals, weak supporting probabilities and entropy duality. Its selection theorem identifies the whole vanishing-exponent limit for fixed nonzero Lp data, p > 1, in ordinary minimal type on a smooth big background. These functionals need not separate singularity types in a closed background. Chapter 15 develops ideal projection, analytic-envelope equality, the divisorial envelope identity, mass dependence and the ideal L1 metric before its dyadic approximation criterion. The curve counterexamples show that this metric does not control non-pluripolar mass. The scalar-distance comparison is made in a Kähler background. The general Hermitian topology on ordinary singularity types remains open.

Chapter 16 constructs Perron segments with explicit endpoint barriers and compares the smooth product equation with the torsion terms in the L2 action. The general strip convexity, minimum and gluing principles are used from Chapter 1. Chapter 17 proves the envelope correspondence between test curves and Perron rays, using the general pointwise Legendre results from Chapter 1, and computes a logarithmic-pole example. Chapter 18 constructs intrinsic trace data from restricted multiplier ideals, using the general analytic-restriction and modification lemmas in Chapter 1. On a curve, Green potentials realize these data and the source singularity topology is equivalent to total variation of the atomic measures. Trace envelopes give positive finite-mass models in a sufficiently large background; curve fibrations give exact realizations. The arbitrary higher-dimensional exact-realization and background-comparison problems remain open.

## Geometry and section spaces

Chapter 19 develops weighted section spaces and kernels, Gauduchon and restricted-trace obstructions, a bounded section algebra and the exact curve growth formula. Its Hopf and infinite-mass examples separate section growth from Hermitian mass. Chapter 20 uses Gauduchon bounds and flag valuations to count graded sections by intrinsic lattice volume, including degree periodicity and bodies of smaller dimension. Point blowups supply flags on every compact complex manifold; full-dimensional growth implies a projective modification and bounded mass. Chapter 21 identifies weighted sections and Hermitian measures on the Hopf fibration with their base counterparts. The converse positivity argument makes its mass bound apply to every fiber-constant Hermitian potential. It derives the reduced Lp equation and the logarithmic gradient simplex. Chapter 22 constructs finite-mass divisorial trace currents on every compact modification and their Bott–Chern classes. Constant-trace smooth representatives give continuity of positive-current pullback; exceptional divisors measure the change in the canonical ordinary types. Chapter 23 uses affine classes with a prescribed exterior derivative to retain the torsion of a nonclosed background. Analytic singularities have nef Cartier realizations; the general approximation changes the derivative by a vanishing smooth error. One residual family can contain both finite and infinite raw masses. Chapter 24 constructs compatible ideal-envelope systems and their canonical decreasing retraction. Recovery is equivalent to preservation of every slice's multiplier ideals. A ruled surface exhibits an extra limiting divisorial pole as the residual volume tends to zero; raw Hermitian mass also fails smooth-gauge invariance. Linear compatibility on modifications does not imply continuity of nonlinear mass or realization of every proposed trace system.

Chapter 25 applies the Chapter 2 mass-invariance criterion to density independence and entropy, and characterizes smooth mass extrema on threefolds. It develops Gauduchon variational volume through the normalized smooth equation, its Hessian and derivative, and a compact dual problem over currents. Uniform determinant tails control integrable-density attainment; the general unbounded trace-density inequality is used from Chapter 6. Smooth positive attainment, semipositive attainment away from an analytic set and integrable-current attainment have different regularity consequences. The closed-core nef subtraction theorem gives two fixed initial tests and a finite improper cost, including a nonclosed blowup example. Elliptic-product maximizing sequences have the sharp critical determinant norm while all higher moments diverge. Upper bounded mass and positive lower mass are independent; the elliptic example has finite upper mass and zero lower mass.

Chapter 26 studies partial kernels and quotient equilibrium. General compact-obstacle bounds, smooth-center ideals, logarithmic-pole constructions and modification calculus are used from Chapter 1. Birational transfer uses the canonical Jacobian correction and retains a Kähler modification for convergence; submersion transfer uses fiber probabilities and the base-dimensional normalization. The Hopf example gives a nonzero limit that loses the original singularity type. The chapter includes exact one-dimensional growing-order models, compact-curve localization in the stated growth range, and the supplied horizontal-center estimates. For horizontal poles over projective space it proves the exact domination envelope, a lower bound in every degree, local uniform and global L1 convergence of partial potentials, and total-variation convergence of kernel measures with the base-dimensional normalization. Arbitrary selected-space approximation and the general Fubini–Study lower bound remain open.

Chapter 27 connects Gauduchon trace compactness with closed Bott–Chern classes, divisorial constraints at the maximal subsolution coefficient and singular loci. The elliptic example identifies the closed cones and section spaces and computes boundary volume and minimizing-test degeneration. Ample-locus regularity uses global Lp data and the strict analytic barrier. The blowup metric uses the first-chapter puncture extension; the normal-length criterion and torus profiles give the sharp logarithmic completeness exponent. The integrable-pole examples retain their distinction from a prescribed nonintegrable pole and from flow-compatible data. Chapter 28 relates the Lee equation to ordinary and twisted potentials, separates the coupled LCK volume problem from the invariant-volume Vaisman theorem, and proves the Chern–Ricci-flat Gauduchon rigidity statement. Its Lee–Gauduchon cone is defined on any compact complex manifold; the chapter proves duality and bimeromorphic invariance, characterizes strongly Gauduchon metrics by positive exact currents and de Rham mass bounds, and distinguishes these conditions on the Calabi–Eckmann quotient. The temporarily hidden Nakamaye chapter remains excluded.

## Theory under mass bounds and historical sources

Chapters 29–31 form the separate part under mass assumptions. Chapter 29 proves comparison and addition for finite singularity volumes and identifies relative full mass with the envelope condition under positive lower reference volume. It proves that positive lower volume gives a smaller-background competitor when the background is semipositive, with an explicit mixed-product bound, and gives the conditional volume criterion for equality of ordinary and ideal envelopes. Chapter 30 passes from domination and common envelopes to general finite non-pluripolar measure equations in semipositive big backgrounds with the smaller-background reference condition and positive lower mass. The exponential potential is unique; at exponent zero only the scalar is unique, as the final projective examples show. Chapter 31 constructs general-measure equations in arbitrary smooth big backgrounds under bounded mass and positive lower model-reference volume. Minimal-class moment estimates and smoothed-data continuity precede weighted addition and projection to the prescribed model. A separate relative moment estimate recovers ordinary reference type for Lp data. A condition on one density or reference is not a uniform metric mass property.

Chapter 32 formulates the remaining research problems through missing compactness, attainment, positivity and singularity-preservation estimates. Fixed-Lp-density selection is already proved; bounded uniqueness and the general integrable-density selection problem retain their separate scopes. The root-space sum norm and root norm have different completeness hypotheses. Primitive elliptic bundles give exact upper mass and sharp separated-potential estimates without settling their unrestricted lower mass. Chapter 33 connects the historical sources through their mathematical problems and methods, with exact locators and comparisons of hypotheses. Its prescribed-singularity discussion separates ordinary-type equations, semipositive general measures and big backgrounds under mass bounds. The appendix develops fiberwise descent, resolution-independent envelopes, compactness and extended measures on unibranch compact spaces. Section A.6 constructs equations on projective cones in a logarithmic ordinary class, including nonclosed deformations, and in the closed bounded class. Their mass identities and all-resolution comparison retain their exact scopes; the full solution descends even when its smooth residual does not. Smooth disk tests and modification-capacity convergence are proved in Chapter 1; equations beyond these cone regimes and intrinsic singular traces remain separate.
