## From local potentials to nonlinear mass

The polar order and variation-capacity estimates support the later curve topology. The Holder trace-mass and path-distance theorem is the foundation for the distinction between finite-distance singularities and continuous potentials with complete ends.

The raw Monge-Ampere one-form has a local primitive only under the proved closedness condition; a Gauduchon surface can conserve mass without supplying that primitive. ## The separate part under mass assumptions

Chapter 29 develops singularity volumes, relative full mass, finite-deficit stretching and model convergence under bounded mass. Lower-volume continuity for decreasing models includes zero limits; the corresponding upper-volume and Lelong conclusions retain positive lower volume. Exact trace realization and envelope-system recovery follow from these estimates in their stated regimes.

The model comparison separates normalized models at positive lower volume and controls L1 and capacity convergence, but can fail the triangle inequality. A complete compatible metric additionally controls reciprocal lower volume. Analytic approximation characterizes ideal models in this regime, with smooth-gauge and two-sided background transport. On curves, polar total variation instead gives a complete geodesic metric including zero mass, an exact analytic defect and a nearest ideal-model retraction. Smooth real backgrounds of nonnegative area and uniform collapse at zero area are covered. Weak-current, capacity and metric convergence still differ.

Chapter 30 constructs both general finite-measure equations in semipositive big backgrounds with positive lower mass and a smaller-background model reference. An envelope-class solution need not have bounded difference from that reference. In a Hermitian background with positive lower mass, Corollary 30.7.1 identifies the zero-exponent scalar with the individual L1 threshold and proves subsequential capacity convergence for normalized exponential solutions. Upper bounded mass is not needed for that conclusion.

Chapter 31 treats arbitrary smooth big backgrounds under bounded mass and positive lower model-reference volume. Moment estimates and smoothing lead to projection and weighted addition; higher moments recover ordinary type for Lp data. Varying models retain a common positive lower-volume bound. Positive exponents give whole-potential stability; exponent zero gives scalar/measure convergence and equations for the potential clusters.
