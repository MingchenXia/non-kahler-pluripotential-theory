# Reading guide — checkpoint103

The book separates the general Hermitian theory from the substantial developments requiring uniform mass properties. Chapter numbers here refer to the reorganized manuscript. Earlier audit records retain the numbering of their own checkpoints.

## General foundations and singularities

Chapters 1–8 develop the nonclosed background, torsion, Gauduchon traces, compactness, extended non-pluripolar products, finite damped measures and the Hopf example. Upper bounded mass and positive lower mass are independent properties. The general framework allows infinite total Monge–Ampère mass and vanishing lower mass.

Chapter 3 derives the primitive obstruction and the general radial torsion-defect formula. A Gauduchon surface has invariant smooth mass, but this alone does not give a primitive of the raw one-form. The closed-energy identity uses only closedness of the smooth real background.

Chapter 4 now distinguishes Gauduchon surfaces from higher dimensions under potential perturbations. Propositions 4.5.2 and 4.6.1 give the exact persistence/mass criterion and its bounded adjoint extension; Example 4.5.1 supplies a three-torus obstruction. General weak adjoints remain a separate question.

Chapter 5 gives finite Gauduchon trace coordinates on the full normalized potential space. Proposition 5.2.2 supplies a linear inverse L1 estimate and square-root forward trace estimate; Example 5.2.1 proves optimality of both exponents. These estimates also apply to unbounded potentials.

Read the trace estimates together with the infinite-mass witness. Corollary 6.8.2 also gives smooth strictly positive currents converging in coefficient L1 to the background, with all fixed exponential weights converging, and freely prescribed larger masses when BMP fails. Even a fixed finite target mass need not converge to the background volume. Corollary 7.6.2 shows that the same sequence converges in capacity to zero. Its shallow restrictions converge in whole total variation, while all fixed deep levels retain the same positive weak defect along one subsequence. This shows why the sequence-level tail condition in Chapter 7 cannot simply be dropped. Even coefficient-L1 convergence of full currents and total-variation convergence of their Gauduchon traces can coexist with infinite determinant integrals. Linear control does not supply nonlinear integrability. Finite damping supplies a different, explicitly controlled family of measures without BMP.

On the Hopf metric in Chapter 8, Proposition 8.7.1 supplies a complementary positive statement: bounded mass minimizers are precisely base pullbacks, and the mass excess controls the squared L2 distance to the fiber average with sharp constant 2/3. This is a property of that explicit geometry, not a general mass or coercivity assumption.

Section 4.4 distinguishes the compact weak closure of smooth Gauduchon tests from all normalized positive dd^c-closed currents. The first gives the pseudoeffective/big threshold and its stability; the second tests smooth positivity and nefness in Chapter 26. The exceptional curve in Chapter 28 shows why the two current families cannot be identified. Smooth attainment of the linear threshold is equivalent to smooth dd^c-exactness at the threshold.

Chapter 12 constructs envelopes, defines extended upper and lower singularity volumes, and develops the relative envelope class. Stretching, nonempty finite rooftops and equality of ordinary singularity envelopes work for any smooth background and genuine reference. The difference-envelope and mass-positivity arguments retain the stated semipositive model setting, without a uniform positive lower mass. The individual positivity conclusion remains valid when the uniform lower bound vanishes. The chapter ends with infinite mass inside the relative envelope class. Finite-volume comparisons are in Chapter 30.

Chapter 15 develops multiplier-ideal envelopes and the relation between ordinary and ideal envelopes. Dyadic analytic regularizations give a specific monotone convergence criterion without BMP or bigness. Rational scaling gives analytic density in the elementary ideal metric on a fixed Hermitian background. It does not give the desired topology on arbitrary ordinary types. On a curve, even fixed positive masses fail to restore mass continuity for that elementary metric.

Chapters 16–25 retain interpolation, test curves, analytic approximation, restriction, section spaces, convex bodies, divisorial classes and envelope systems. In Chapter 19, the source singularity metric on positive-mass curve ideal envelopes is quantitatively equivalent to total variation of atomic data. A fixed positive lower mass gives completeness and analytic density, but not total boundedness. This is a curve result and does not construct a general Hermitian singularity metric.

The curve-fibration construction realizes exact compact-model divisor orders. Products preserve these traces and all multiplier ideals while allowing finite or infinite mass. A canonical ordinary type on one fixed model does not recover arbitrary data on every higher model.

## Equations and Gauduchon duality without a uniform mass hypothesis

Chapters 9–11 treat smooth and continuous equations, normalization, uniqueness criteria and explicit failures of potential uniqueness. Keep the distinctions between a unique scalar, a unique potential and a selected approximation limit. The zero set of the density alone does not decide uniqueness.

Chapter 13 also proves weighted lower semicontinuity for extended measures in any smooth background, with capacity-convergent potentials and bounded nonnegative quasi-continuous weights. This result allows infinite mass. Turning L1 convergence into capacity convergence or obtaining weak convergence of the unweighted measures still requires the stated barrier, domination or tail estimates.

Chapter 13 reconstructs minimal ordinary-type Lp equations in arbitrary smooth big backgrounds, without either mass property. The proof uses a strict singular barrier, resolution and the Gauduchon trace. Prescribing a more general ordinary type in a semipositive big background requires the stated smaller-background condition.

Section 13.19 proves stability and fixed-reference compactness above a strict barrier; Section 13.20 uses compactness to shorten the normalized existence proof. Section 13.21 reconstructs local Holder continuity for minimal-singularity solutions with global Lp density, p>1. The smooth nonclosed regularization error is derived from Demailly’s classical expansion. Section 13.22 proves interior smoothness where the barrier is smooth and the density is smooth positive. Dang26 is credited for the Holder theorem, whose proof is now supplied in the book. Subsection 13.21.1 proves normalized exponential compactness, scalar and density convergence, with whole-family potential convergence conditional on invariant-class uniqueness. Neither metric mass property is used. Local smoothness of the density alone does not supply global Lp integrability; nonminimal regularity and behavior across poles remain separate.

The two measure-representation sections in Chapter 13 require no mass property. One represents every finite non-pluripolar measure as an integrable density relative to a bounded potential’s measure. The other retains a prescribed ordinary type and allows the reference measure to have infinite mass. The density is not asserted bounded. Neither representation by itself solves the equation.

For a Hermitian background, Chapter 13 also characterizes positive lower mass by universal normalized solvability and constructs obstructing positive L1 densities when it vanishes. This characterization belongs to the general theory because it describes both possibilities. For one density, the attained subsolution threshold and its exact Gauduchon formula replace a uniform assumption. A positive threshold alone is not yet known here to give an exact zero-exponent equation in general.

Chapter 14 develops normalization functionals and weak adjoint probabilities from ordinary-type equations. It imposes neither uniform mass property. Its entropy comparisons, reference monotonicity and dual formulas specify their exact reference hypotheses. A pseudometric on normalization functionals need not separate singularity types.

Chapter 26 studies the Gauduchon variational volume and attainment. Distinguish the following inputs:

| Hypothesis on the individual data or maximizing tests | Conclusion established in the book |
|---|---|
| Lp density, p > 1 | Continuous exact equations and compactness of normalized exponential approximations; potential uniqueness requires a further argument. |
| Uniform root tails | Compact current attainment and quantitative scalar/measure errors; no general capacity conclusion follows from tails alone. |
| Smooth strictly positive dual maximizer, in dimension at least two | Smooth exact threshold equation, uniqueness modulo constants and whole-family uniform exponential convergence. |
| Smooth semipositive maximizer, positive off a proper analytic set | Exact equation, local smoothness and uniqueness modulo constants for genuine threshold subsolutions; normalized exponential L1 convergence. |
| Uniform first moments along one maximizing sequence, plus a genuine smaller-background envelope for the greatest threshold subsolution | Exact equations and capacity convergence along normalized L1-convergent exponential subsequences. |

Uniform integrability alone gives an integrable maximizing current and supporting probabilities; it does not give the smoothness or envelope assumption in the last two rows. These are conditions on the selected data and tests, not global bounded-mass hypotheses.

Section 9.9 specializes the smooth equation to the fixed Hopf metric. It identifies the normalization defect with vertical energy, characterizes its zero set by base pullbacks, and computes its background Hessian using the Gauduchon adjoint. The smooth result does not assert a nonsmooth equality case or global convexity.

## Theory under uniform mass assumptions

Part XII contains three separate chapters. The upper and lower assumptions are not interchangeable.

**Chapter 30, Volumes under bounded mass.** Read volume comparison and addition before the equality of the envelope and volume full mass classes. The reverse implication uses positive lower reference volume. The final section gives the conditional ideal-envelope volume characterization, retaining its relative-class and positive-volume hypotheses.

**Chapter 31, Relative equations under positive lower mass.** The background is semipositive and big; the reference satisfies the smaller-background model condition; v_-(theta) is positive. There is no upper mass bound. Finite-measure domination is followed by extended domination, relative compactness, moving measures, minimal-reference existence and prescribed-reference existence. The positive-exponent solution and the zero-exponent scalar are unique; the normalized zero-exponent potential need not be. Positive lower mass identifies the exact individual-density threshold and gives capacity convergence along normalized L1-convergent exponential subsequences.

**Chapter 32, Big backgrounds under bounded mass.** The background can be non-semipositive. BMP and positive lower model-reference volume supply a finite positive mass interval. Read relative rooftops and domination, higher moments and minimal-reference equations, weighted limits and addition, then cross-class projection and the general model-reference construction. The Lp refinement preserves the ordinary type. Metric positive lower mass follows from these hypotheses; omitting it as a separate assumption does not enlarge the regime. The last section compares all three domination regimes, each under its own listed assumptions.

## Geometry and the remaining questions

Chapter 27 treats partial kernels. Birational transformation identities do not require a mass bound. A Kähler modification and positive closed-curvature mass permit the source equilibrium theorem to be applied; that geometric setting implies BMP. Submersion and Hopf examples instead retain their base and intrinsic-growth hypotheses. Kernel-measure convergence alone does not prove a lower potential bound or recover the original ideal type.

Sections 27.12–27.13 treat growing vanishing orders. The exact degenerate entire-function model precedes compact-curve localization for q_m=o(m^(1/(2r+1))), with global semipositivity and positivity away from the chosen point for the lower bound. Problem 33.12.3 asks about larger ranges and higher weight terms. Transfer to non-Kähler total spaces and higher dimension remains separate.

Chapter 28 develops ordinary Bott–Chern loci and elliptic-bundle examples. It keeps the classical and numerical restricted-volume definitions in their stated regimes. A direct Hermitian-error supremum is infinite even in the zero class on the Hopf example. The former Chapter 30, *A transcendental Nakamaye theorem*, is temporarily excluded, with its sources and history preserved; its proof is not an active book dependency.

Subsection 28.3.1 applies the complete regularity chain to every big closed class on a compact Kähler manifold. Theorem 28.3.2 proves that the full-mass solution for a smooth strictly positive global volume form is smooth on the ample locus, with no nefness assumption. Read it after the non-Kähler-locus theorem and Sections 13.19, 13.21 and 13.22. Dang26 Theorem 3.1 is reconstructed internally, including its scalar/local-equation version; classical BEGZ existence and minimality remain standard inputs. The incomplete-metric example in Section 28.8 shows why local smoothness is distinct from completeness.

Chapter 29 treats LCK metrics, Lee and anti-Lee forms, Gauduchon identities, rigidity and the Lee–Gauduchon cone. The cone can vanish even when a positive de Rham-exact current obstructs a strongly Gauduchon metric. The checked strongly Gauduchon criterion remains active; its complete-proof review is recorded separately. No content from the supplied private Popovici working reference is integrated.

Chapter 33 connects the analytic theory with bigness, nefness, the Fujiki class, balanced and pluriclosed metrics, null loci and geometric flows. The primitive elliptic-bundle calculation and K3 test are explicit cases; unrestricted lower mass in that K3 example remains unresolved. Other open directions include individual-measure exact equations when lower mass vanishes, ordinary-type topology, higher-dimensional trace realization and lower partial Bergman asymptotics.

The appendix states the extension to unibranch compact complex spaces and its limitations. The companion nonuniqueness note remains unchanged. A completed bounded audit or a successful build is not whole-book mathematical certification.

Section 28.8 integrates the supplied incomplete divisorial metric: a unique minimal-singularity finite-energy solution in a big non-nef class can be incomplete on every open subset of its ample locus. Chapter 33 records the limitation for geometric applications. Its bounded proof review and release records are linked in STATUS.md.
