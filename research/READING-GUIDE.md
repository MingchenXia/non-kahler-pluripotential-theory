# Reading guide — checkpoint121

The chapter numbers below refer to the current book. Earlier review records keep the numbering of their own checkpoints. General psh tools are collected in Chapter 1. Paper sources and exact locators are collected in final Chapter 33; book citations may remain beside the mathematics.

## Foundations

Chapter 1 develops the elementary calculus, normalized compactness, exponential integrability, local products, bounded smooth-background products, Hermitian capacity and its volume/sublevel estimates, regularization, multiplier ideals, divisorial estimates, obstacle envelopes, the minimum principle, pullback and descent, and convergence against a fixed non-pluripolar measure. The analytic regularization with a prescribed multiplier-ideal type is kept separate from the decreasing sequence with smooth resolved remainders. The Chern regularization estimates retain their different boundedness and background hypotheses.

Chapters 2–4 explain torsion, mass variation, the obstruction to a primitive of the raw Monge–Ampère one-form, and Gauduchon traces and positivity. A Gauduchon surface has invariant smooth mass, but this alone does not give the raw energy primitive. Persistence under potential perturbation in higher dimensions requires the precise mass-invariance identities.

Chapter 5 identifies normalized potentials with their finite Gauduchon traces. Its linear inverse L1 estimate and square-root forward estimate also apply to unbounded potentials. These linear estimates do not control nonlinear Monge–Ampère mass.

Chapter 6 extends the first-chapter bounded products to non-pluripolar measures. Chapter 7 develops finite weighted measures, their capacity continuity and the uniform-tail criterion for undamped convergence. Chapter 8 gives the Hopf counterexample. Infinite determinant integrals can coexist with coefficient-L1 convergence of currents and total-variation convergence of their traces. The explicit Hopf geometry also has a sharp fiber-average coercivity estimate; it is not a general mass hypothesis.

## Equations and singularity types

Chapters 9–11 develop smooth and continuous equations, their scalar normalization and potential uniqueness or nonuniqueness. Keep a unique scalar, a unique potential and a selected approximation limit distinct. Positivity almost everywhere is weaker than a fixed positive lower density bound.

Chapter 12 applies the first-chapter envelope tools to contact sets, domination, extended volumes and relative envelope classes. General stretching and finite rooftop arguments retain arbitrary smooth backgrounds; model positivity and the stronger comparison statements retain their own assumptions.

Chapter 13 develops prescribed-singularity equations without a blanket metric mass hypothesis. It includes weighted lower semicontinuity for extended measures, measure representations, equations in a fixed ordinary type, and minimal-type Lp equations for arbitrary smooth big backgrounds. Global Lp data, p>1, supply the local Holder input for interior smoothness on the strict-barrier locus. Smooth density on an arbitrary open set alone does not supply that integrability. The local boundary examples explain this distinction. The chapter also treats individual-density thresholds and exponential approximations.

Chapter 14 studies normalization functionals, weak supporting probabilities and entropy duality. Its selection theorem identifies the whole vanishing-exponent limit for fixed nonzero Lp data, p > 1, in ordinary minimal type on a smooth big background. These functionals need not separate singularity types in a closed background. Chapter 15 develops ideal projection, analytic-envelope equality, mass dependence and the ideal L1 metric before its dyadic approximation criterion. The curve counterexamples show that this metric does not control non-pluripolar mass. The scalar-distance comparison is made in a Kähler background. The general Hermitian topology on ordinary singularity types remains open.

Chapters 16–18 cover Perron interpolation, test curves and intrinsic trace data. The minimum principle and analytic regularization are used from Chapter 1. Restriction of analytic approximants opens Chapter 18. Positive-mass curve ideal envelopes have a trace description by atomic measures; the higher-dimensional realization problem remains separate.

## Geometry and section spaces

Chapters 19–24 develop holomorphic sections, flags, quotient models, divisorial currents, nonclosed background classes and envelope systems. Linear compatibility on modifications does not imply continuity of nonlinear mass or realization of every proposed trace system.

Chapter 25 develops Gauduchon variational volume and its dual formulations. Smooth positive attainment, semipositive attainment away from an analytic set and integrable-current attainment have different regularity consequences. Upper bounded mass and positive lower mass are independent; the elliptic example has finite upper mass and zero lower mass.

Chapter 26 studies partial kernels and quotient equilibrium. Birational transfer uses the canonical Jacobian correction; the relevant general ideal identities are in Chapter 1. The chapter includes exact one-dimensional growing-order models, compact-curve localization in the stated growth range, and the supplied horizontal-center estimates. Arbitrary selected-space approximation remains open.

Chapters 27–28 connect the theory with closed Bott–Chern classes and locally conformally Kahler geometry. The first includes minimal multiplicities, ample-locus regularity, incomplete big-class metrics and the supplied divisorial normal-length criterion. Its pole and flow hypotheses remain distinct. The LCK chapter treats ordinary/twisted positivity, rigidity and the Lee–Gauduchon cone. The temporarily hidden Nakamaye chapter remains excluded.

## Theory under mass bounds and historical sources

Chapters 29–31 form the separate part under mass assumptions. Chapter 29 develops finite singularity volumes under bounded mass. Chapter 30 treats semipositive big backgrounds with the smaller-background reference condition and positive lower mass. Chapter 31 treats arbitrary smooth big backgrounds under bounded mass and positive lower model-reference volume. A condition on one density or reference is not a uniform metric mass property.

Chapter 32 records the remaining research problems and their exact boundaries. Chapter 33 gives the historical sources, checked locators and comparisons of hypotheses for the mathematical body. The appendix treats compact complex spaces; smooth-space psh tools are referenced from Chapter 1 rather than defined again.
