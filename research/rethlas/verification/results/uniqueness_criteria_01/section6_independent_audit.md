## Sequential deductions

1. **Definition and bounded products.** The manuscript defines omega-PSH as the actual upper semicontinuous quasi-psh representative with omega+dd^c a positive as a current. Locally a smooth closed positive form Omega dominating omega makes a+its potential bounded psh. Expanding omega+dd^c a=(Omega+dd^c a)-(Omega-omega) defines the Hermitian products. DK §2 explicitly gives this expansion and bounded monotone continuity. Locality is explicitly used in its comparison proposition. No local potential of omega is asserted.

2. **The differentiation formula.** Since omega has even degree, d^c(omega^k)=k omega^{k-1} wedge d^c omega and a further d gives exactly the manuscript's positive sign before k(k-1) omega^{k-2} wedge d omega wedge d^c omega. Thus dd^c omega=dd^c(omega^2)=0 is equivalent to dd^c omega=0 and d omega wedge d^c omega=0, and these imply all powers vanish. Conversely, when n>=3 the k=1,2 conditions are in the listed range. In n=2 all degree-six equations vanish and the only nontrivial condition is dd^c omega=0. In n=1 even dd^c omega vanishes by degree.

3. **Smooth versus bounded equivalence.** Chiose's PSH(X,g) is explicitly C-infinity, so its Theorem 0.2 supplies precisely the smooth equivalence claimed, not a silently enlarged bounded theorem. DK (3.1) is explicitly invariance for every bounded omega-PSH potential; Proposition 3.1 supplies bounded ordinary comparison from this condition. Immediately before Proposition 3.2 DK states that its Guan–Li zero conditions imply (3.1), and Proposition 3.2 supplies bounded comparison directly under the more general signed conditions. DK orders its torsion product as d^c omega wedge d omega in that proposition; the manuscript uses only its zero case, so changing this order creates no sign problem. Bounded comparison implies equality of all masses by applying comparison after sufficiently large constant shifts, and comparing with zero. Restriction to smooth tests closes the converse through Chiose.

4. **Exact existence interface.** DK Theorem 5.2 assumes (3.1), mass normalization, the capacity domination alpha t/h(t^{-1/n}), and an Orlicz space with psi(x)/x increasing to infinity. It concludes a continuous omega-PSH solution, without assuming continuous or positive-everywhere density. Corollary 2.4 supplies the stated exponential capacity domination for nonnegative L^p density, p>1. With psi(t)=t^p and h(y)=exp(a_0 y), all hypotheses hold; the integral of y^{-1}exp(-a_0 y/n) on [1,infinity) is finite. In particular indicator truncation preserves L^p, so the later auxiliary density is covered. No uniqueness is imported from this existence theorem.

5. **Capacity quantifiers.** DK states Corollary 2.4 for compact sets K. The manuscript writes E; extension to Borel E follows by inner regularity of G omega^n and monotonicity of capacity, since t exp(-a t^{-1/n}) is increasing. This is a routine measure-theoretic specialization and does not require continuity of G.

6. **Normalization interface.** DK and Nguyen use dd^c_0=(i/pi)partial bar-partial=2 dd^c in the manuscript, so their potential is u/2. Chiose uses i partial bar-partial=2 pi dd^c, so its potential is u/(2 pi). In each case positivity, actual solution forms, top powers, constant shifts, convex mixtures, and strict sublevel sets are preserved. The unit-oscillation capacity depends on the operator normalization; it is comparable rather than literally unchanged. For k=2, cap_1 <= cap_2 <= 2^n cap_1: the first inclusion uses u/2 and the second bound follows from omega+dd^c u <= 2(omega+dd^c(u/2)) for 0<=u<=2. The exponential capacity bound therefore transfers with changed positive constants, which the manuscript leaves unspecified. There is no resulting normalization defect.

7. **Exact mixed interface.** Nguyen Lemma 1.9 explicitly assumes nonnegative L^1 densities and u,v in PSH(omega) intersection L-infinity. It states each mixed wedge lower bound and the convex-mixture consequence used verbatim by the manuscript. Its proof explicitly recognizes a nonclosed Hermitian background. It is not a theorem restricted to continuous potentials, strictly positive densities, or a local-potential background.

8. **Domination perturbation.** After making b<=0, b_t=b+t|b|-epsilon satisfies b-epsilon<=b_t<b when t||b||_infinity<epsilon. Hence the two strict-sublevel inclusions hold. Its solution form is (1-t)omega_b+t omega, whose nth power dominates t^n omega^n by positivity of bounded mixed products. Comparison on {a<b_t} yields zero smooth volume of {a<b-2epsilon}. A countable sequence epsilon down to zero gives a>=b almost everywhere. The actual quasi-psh representatives have local-ball-average limits at every point, so the inequality holds everywhere. The proof does not assume that {a<b} is open.

9. **Centering and choosing S.** Boundedness allows a constant shift making sup(u-v)=s and inf(u-v)=-s, s>0 when the difference is nonconstant; extrema need not be attained. The disjoint strict-sign sets have combined mu-mass at most V, so interchanging u,v makes mu(S)<=V/2. The constant b_0=(V-2mu(S))/V is nonnegative and the density 2F 1_S+b_0 has total mass exactly V and belongs to L^p. All required properties of the auxiliary rho are supplied by the checked existence interface.

10. **Amplification and comparison.** Choose C_0>=sup(rho-u), possible since both functions are bounded. Then q_delta<=u, so E_delta is contained in S. On S the auxiliary density is at least 2F. Nguyen's inequality gives MA(q_delta)>=((1-delta)+delta 2^{1/n})^n mu, with amplification strictly greater than one. Ordinary comparison on E_delta forces mu(E_delta)=0 because mu is finite. Domination with a=v, b=q_delta is now applicable and gives v>=q_delta everywhere. Letting delta tend to zero gives v>=u, contradicting the centered positive supremum. No division by F occurs, and open sets where F=0 cause no problem.

11. **LPT attribution.** The cited paper's proof of Theorem 3.7 uses an auxiliary density twice the restriction to a sublevel set plus a constant, followed by mixing, ordinary comparison, and domination. Remark 3.8 explicitly says the proof applies to the Hermitian setting when the comparison principle holds. The manuscript reconstructs the equal-density specialization with its own checked Hermitian existence and mixed interfaces. It does not claim that LPT proves equal-density uniqueness on arbitrary Hermitian backgrounds.

12. **Normalization and continuity of all solutions.** For I=integral f omega^n>0, c=V/I makes cf have mass V. Existence yields a continuous bounded solution, normalized by its supremum. Any competing bounded solution has c'I=V by the already established bounded mass identity; hence c'=c. Equal-density uniqueness identifies the normalized potentials. It follows that every bounded solution for this density is continuous.
