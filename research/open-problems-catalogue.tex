\section{Conventions and the main invariants}
Unless otherwise specified, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, $h>0$ is a Hermitian form, and $\mathrm dV$ and $\mu$ are smooth strictly positive probability volume forms. We use
\[\dc=\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi}(\bar\partial-\partial),\qquad
 \ddc=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial.\]
A background $\theta$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, which need not be closed; $\PSH(X,\theta)$ consists of genuine quasi-psh functions $u$ with $\theta+\ddc u\geq0$. The measure $\MA_\theta(u)$ is the extended non-pluripolar product obtained on finite levels. It has no mass on pluripolar sets, but its total mass need not be finite. Ordinary type equivalence $u\sim v$ means bounded difference; $u\preceq v$ means $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C$.

\field{Mass properties}
\[v_-(h)=\inf_{u\in\PSH(X,h)\cap L^\infty}\int_X(h+\ddc u)^n,
 \qquad
 v_+(h)=\sup_{u\in\PSH(X,h)\cap L^\infty}\int_X(h+\ddc u)^n.\]
BMP means $v_+(h)<\infty$; positive lower mass means $v_-(h)>0$. These properties are independent of the choice of Hermitian metric, though their numerical values are not. Neither property follows from the other. The standard Hopf threefold has positive lower mass and infinite upper mass; a different balanced elliptic threefold has finite upper mass and zero lower mass.

\field{Multiplier ideals and envelopes}
The ideal $\mathcal I(tu)$ consists of holomorphic germs $g$ with $|g|^2\mathrm e^{-tu}$ locally integrable. Write $u\preceq_{\mathcal I}v$ when $\mathcal I(tu)\subseteq\mathcal I(tv)$ for every real $t>0$; equality at every weight defines ideal equivalence. For a smooth obstacle $F$, put
\[
\begin{split}
P_{\theta,F}[u]&=\bigl(\sup\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):v\leq F,\ v\preceq u\}\bigr)^*,\\
P_{\theta,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}&=\bigl(\sup\{v\in\PSH(X,\theta):v\leq F,\ v\preceq_{\mathcal I}u\}\bigr)^*.
\end{split}
\]
The star denotes upper semicontinuous regularization. Omitting $F$ means obstacle zero. An ordinary model is a normalized fixed point of $P_\theta[\cdot]$; an ideal model is a fixed point of $P_\theta[\cdot]_{\mathcal I}$. For an ordinary type, its lower and upper singularity volumes are
\[\underline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)=\inf_{v\sim u}\MA_\theta(v)(X),\qquad
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_\theta(u)=\sup_{v\sim u}\MA_\theta(v)(X),\]
with competitors $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$. These are not generally equal to the mass of one chosen representative. For a reference $q$, the relative envelope class $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta,q)$ consists of $u\preceq q$ for which, for every $b\geq1$, there is $v\in\PSH(X,\theta)$ with $b^{-1}v+(1-b^{-1})q\leq u$. This convex-inequality definition avoids subtracting two infinite values at common poles.

\field{Fixed-density Gauduchon volume}
A Hermitian $\gamma$ is Gauduchon if $\ddc(\gamma^{n-1})=0$. For a pseudoeffective background define
\[D_\mu(\gamma)=\int_X(\mu/\gamma^n)^{1/n}\gamma^n,\quad
\lambda_\mu(\theta)=\inf_{\gamma\text{ Gauduchon}}
\frac{\int_X\theta\wedge\gamma^{n-1}}{D_\mu(\gamma)},\quad
\mathcal V_\mu(\theta)=\lambda_\mu(\theta)^n.\]
In dimension one the quotient is $\int_X\theta$. Pseudoeffective means the background has a positive current representative; big means one representative dominates $\delta h$ with $\delta>0$. This definition of bigness also applies to nonclosed backgrounds. The fixed-density volume is finite, gauge invariant, and positively homogeneous, but differs from upper singularity volume and from $v_-(h)$.

\field{Determinant densities of current tests}
For $n\geq2$, normalize positive $\ddc$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-currents by $\int_Xh\wedge T=1$. Let $\sigma_T=h\wedge T$. In an $h$-unitary frame define $P_T\geq0$ by
\[\beta\wedge T=\frac1n\operatorname{tr}(B_\beta P_T)\,\mathrm d\sigma_T,
\qquad\operatorname{tr}P_T=n\quad\sigma_T\text{-a.e.}\]
For a real continuous $F$, set
\[\Gamma_F(T)=\left(\frac{\mathrm e^F\mathrm dV}{h^n}\right)^{1/n}
(\det P_T)^{1/n}\sigma_T.\]
The manuscript proves that these weighted determinant measures are absolutely continuous; write $\Gamma_0(T)=k_T\mathrm dV$. For smooth $T=\gamma^{n-1}$ the total mass of $\Gamma_F(T)$ is the Gauduchon denominator with volume $\mathrm e^F\mathrm dV$. Supporting probabilities are normalized versions of these measures when the current maximizes the relevant dual problem.

\subsection{P01: A complete topology beyond positive lower volume}
\label{P01}
\noindent\textbf{Status:} open in the stated generality. \textbf{Book locator:} Problem 15.6.1, printed p.~590.
\field{Setup and scope} An ordinary model is a normalized fixed point $p=P_\theta[p]$; it is an ideal model if also $p=P_\theta[p]_{\mathcal I}$. The desired topology must distinguish ordinary models and remember multiplier ideals on every modification. Zero lower volume and infinite upper mass are part of the requested domain.
\field{Full problem statement} Extend the complete topology of Book, Theorem~29.11.1 to ordinary models of zero lower volume and beyond bounded mass, retaining analytic closure exactly at the ideal models. Recover the curve topology of Book, Corollary~29.12.1 and the background compatibility of Book, Section~29.9, Section~29.10, including higher-dimensional limits that lose bigness or positive lower volume.
\field{State of the art} Under BMP, the positive-lower-volume models admit a complete metric inducing the upper-volume comparison topology; analytic models have closure exactly the ideal models in that regime (Book, Theorem~29.11.1, Theorem~29.8.2). On a curve, all ordinary models are described by positive polar curvature measures with total-variation distance, including zero non-pluripolar mass. Separated curve products and specified auxiliary products extend this description to some higher-dimensional loci. The existing general envelope uniformities characterize analytic closure but can identify distinct ordinary classes and have incomplete curve families. Book, Theorem~29.14.1 also constructs a complete joint space of smooth curve backgrounds and their models, including zero area. Its analytic closure is exactly the ideal locus, and Book, Corollary~29.14.1 gives smooth-gauge isometries and scaling compatibility. Completeness uses the $C^\infty$ topology on the backgrounds. In dimension at least two, Book, Proposition~15.6.19 gives distinct analytic ideal models with isolated logarithmic poles whose first curvature currents have zero restriction to their pole sets. Their all-weight multiplier ideals still differ. Thus the first polar curvature current alone cannot extend the curve metric. Book, Corollary~15.6.10 recovers the logarithmic coefficient on the exceptional divisor of the blowup at the pole, with an exact polar-trace variation formula. However, Book, Corollary~15.6.11 proves that every prescribed countable family of modifications misses some pairwise inequivalent analytic ideal models. A detector using only such a fixed family cannot separate the required space. Book, Proposition~15.6.20 gives a Gauduchon-normalized polar-trace pseudometric bounded by two, separating analytic ordinary models and recovering the complete fixed-background curve topology. Book, Proposition~1.25.1 proves that bounded ordinary equivalence preserves the coefficientwise polar curvature on the common pole set. Book, Corollary~15.6.12 consequently proves that the comparison descends to ordinary classes and that smooth-gauge transport preserves it after normalized ordinary-model projection, including analytic and ideal models. Book, Proposition~1.25.3 proves that the coefficientwise polar curvature is a closed positive current for arbitrary pole sets. Its local proof uses the bounded cutoff and energy argument for complete pluripolar restrictions; the set need not be closed. Book, Corollary~15.6.13 therefore shows that the scalar traces determine the full polar currents on every modification, using Book, Lemma~1.25.3. These currents need not identify general ordinary models. Book, Proposition~15.6.23 gives an obstruction to arbitrary auxiliary choices: on $\mathbb P^2$, fixed Gauduchon forms can make analytic ideal models with a fixed point-pole coefficient $a\in(0,1)$ converge to zero for the polar comparison, while their upper-volume distance to zero is $a^2$. All these models have lower volume $1-a^2>0$, so this failure already occurs in a common positive-volume sector. Compatibility therefore needs an additional condition on the auxiliary forms. Book, Proposition~15.6.21 proves completeness of the ambient measure-coordinate space. Book, Proposition~1.25.2 proves exact pushforward compatibility of polar curvature under modifications for arbitrary pole sets. For comparison-Cauchy sequences of ordinary models, Book, Proposition~15.6.22 constructs unique compatible weak limits of the full closed polar currents. Their traces converge in total variation, and all currents are carried by the pullback pole set of one genuine potential. This common carrier does not realize the current family as that potential's polar curvature. Simultaneous realization by an ordinary model remains a further problem. Book, Proposition~15.6.24 gives a stronger obstruction on every connected compact K\"ahler manifold of dimension at least two: a nonzero ordinary model of positive lower volume has zero polar current on every modification and trivial multiplier ideals at every weight. Its non-pluripolar volume is strictly below that of the zero model. Thus every choice of Gauduchon forms identifies these two distinct ordinary models. The polar-comparison closure of analytic models also contains this nonideal model. This first polar comparison therefore fails the required separation and ideal analytic-closure properties even when all modifications are used. Book, Corollary~15.6.14 further gives a complete ordered interval of such models, with volumes $A-Mt^n$ and distances $M|s^n-t^n|$, collapsed by every first polar coordinate. The interval can lie in any prescribed positive lower-volume cutoff $\delta<A=\int_X\omega^n$; only its zero endpoint is ideal. Book, Proposition~15.6.25 extends the mass calculation for the same displayed cutoff to an arbitrary Hermitian background, for sufficiently small $t>0$: $\int_X\mathrm{MA}_\omega(tH)=A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_jt^j-Mt^n$, with finite torsion coefficients $c_j$ obtained from bounded truncations. They vanish when $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for every $1\leq q<n$. Book, Corollary~15.6.15 proves invariance over the entire bounded-equivalence class when $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, and therefore extends the exact model volumes, ordered distances and first-polar obstruction to that Hermitian regime. In particular it applies to every compact complex surface with a Gauduchon form. The proof uses the full local product at the Hausdorff-zero-dimensional pole set and its bounded-perturbation invariant top residual mass (Book, Theorem~1.6.3 and Lemma~1.6.5), together with bounded global mass invariance. Book, Proposition~15.6.26 computes every bounded-equivalent representative's mass in an arbitrary Hermitian background as $A+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_j(v)-Mt^n$, with finite coefficients given by bounded truncations. Each such mass lies between $v_-(\omega)-Mt^n$ and $v_+(\omega)-Mt^n$, the latter interpreted in the extended real sense. Book, Corollary~15.6.16 proves that, on a surface, constancy over this entire class is equivalent to the Gauduchon condition; otherwise sufficiently small smooth bounded perturbations change the mass by $2\varepsilon\int_Xf\ddc\omega$. The same singularity type therefore need not fix mass. The exact singularity volumes require optimization over the representative-dependent coefficients. Book, Proposition~15.6.27 proves that, when $0<v_-(\omega)\leq v_+(\omega)<+\infty$, sufficiently small diffuse ordinary models have lower volume at least $v_-(\omega)-Mt^n>0$, upper volume at most $v_+(\omega)-Mt^n$, and distance from zero at least $Mt^n$. Their first polar currents on all modifications and all-weight multiplier ideals are still zero and trivial, respectively. Hence this comparison again fails separation and exact ideal analytic closure. Every Hermitian metric on every compact complex surface satisfies the required two mass properties. For any $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$ the models can lie in the common $\delta$ cutoff. Book, Theorem~29.3.3 proves that ordinary projection preserves the full residual measure on any compact Hausdorff-zero-dimensional pole set, under bounded mass and positive lower singularity volume. Its bounded-equivalent rooftops converge in the full truncation measure. Book, Corollary~15.6.17 uses this to distinguish every diffuse parameter and gives a complete ordered interval for every Hermitian background with both mass properties, throughout each strict range $0\leq t\leq t_0<1/C$. Its lower volume is at least $(1-Ct)^nv_-(\omega)$. For either lower or upper volume, $[(1-Ct)/(1-Cs)]^nV(s)\leq V(t)\leq V(s)$ for $s<t$. The upper-volume coordinate is strictly decreasing and Lipschitz; its differences give the restricted metric, making the interval isometric to $[0,v_+(\omega)-U(t_0)]$. All first polar coordinates vanish on this whole interval. The length need not equal $Mt_0^n$ outside the invariant-mass regime; no exact general torsion volume formula is asserted. Book, Proposition~15.6.28 puts equal residual totals $r$ in two disjoint regions. Both models lie in any prescribed sector $0<\delta<v_-(\omega)$ for sufficiently small $r$, with trivial ideals and zero first polar data on all modifications. Their maximum is zero, and their distance is at least $2r$. If $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, both lower and upper volumes of both models equal $A-r$, and their distance equals $2r$. Thus adding one base residual total, or in this regime both scalar volumes, cannot repair first-polar separation; the residual measures have different spatial supports. The full topology problem remains open.

Book, Lemma~15.6.9 computes the anisotropic separable maximum exactly: for positive weights $a_j$, its bounded truncation measure is $(\prod_j a_j)\bigotimes_j\ddc\max(g,-L/a_j)$, and its full residual measure is $(\prod_j a_j)\sigma^{\otimes n}$. In particular the earlier isotropic mass $M$ equals $1$ in the book normalization. Book, Proposition~15.6.29 gives distinct models in every prescribed positive sector $0<\delta<A$, for $\ddc(\omega^q)=0$, $1\leq q<n$, with the same complete base residual measure $t^n\sigma^{\otimes n}$, both scalar volumes $A-t^n$, all trivial multiplier ideals and zero first polar currents on every modification. Their comparison distance is $t^n>0$. The weights $a=(2,1/2,1,\ldots,1)$ and $b=(1,1,\ldots,1)$ have equal products; their coordinatewise minima have product $1/2$, which determines the volume of the maximum and hence the distance. Thus even the whole base residual measure does not repair separation. This does not identify the full measures off the carrier or full residual products on every modification, and does not solve the full topology problem. For a fixed compact box $[\alpha,\beta]^n$ of positive weights, the same proposition now gives one uniform cutoff curvature bound and the exact metric $t^n(\prod_j a_j+\prod_j b_j-2\prod_j\min(a_j,b_j))$. This is the scaled Lebesgue measure of the symmetric difference of coordinate rectangles. Its topology is the Euclidean weight topology, with explicit two-sided bounds; constant-product fibers are complete and homeomorphic to convex polytopes of real dimension $n-1$, sharing the entire base residual and both scalar volumes.
\field{Remaining gap} A replacement for finite upper-volume deficits must separate zero-volume models, preserve polar data under Cauchy limits, and handle backgrounds losing positivity. The all-obstacle topology repairs smooth-gauge dependence but does not solve completeness. This is the earlier formulation of P17; the two labels do not represent two independent conjectures.
\field{Sources} \cite{SGPT,XL26}; Chapters 15 and 29, especially Book, Problem~32.7.1.
\subsection{P02: Geometric restrictions on nef degeneration orders}
\label{P02}
\noindent\textbf{Status:} open in the stated generality. \textbf{Book locator:} Problem 25.18.1, printed p.~823.
\field{Setup and scope} For a nef background $\theta$ and a Hermitian $h$, define
\[a_-(\theta)=\liminf_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{\log\mathcal V_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h)}{\log\epsilon},\qquad
 a_+(\theta)=\limsup_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{\log\mathcal V_\mu(\theta+\epsilon h)}{\log\epsilon}.\]
The question concerns existence of a common order, its integrality, and equality after pullback or subtraction of an effective divisorial current with nef residual.
\field{Full problem statement} Which geometric conditions on a nonclosed nef background force a common degeneration order, or an integral one? When is equality attained in the modification and residual order comparisons?
\field{State of the art} Both orders lie in $[0,n]$ and are independent of $\mu$ and $h$, of positive scaling, and of smooth gauge. For closed nef backgrounds on a K\"ahler manifold they equal $n-\operatorname{nd}(\theta)$. Nonclosed examples on products of elliptic curves realize unequal orders, irrational common orders, and zero volume with decay slower than every positive power. A modification can strictly increase the orders; effective residual subtraction also gives a one-sided comparison. Explicit elliptic threefolds have integral orders determined by the rank of their boundary forms. The flat-torus criterion separates current rigidity from rank: a nonzero semipositive boundary class is rigid exactly when its kernel leaves are dense, equivalently when a fixed positive perturbation collapses in diameter (Book, Theorem~27.16.1, printed p.~1000). Irrational and rational kernel slopes give the two alternatives (Book, Example~27.16.1, printed p.~1001). Arbitrary approaching flat K\"ahler forms can collapse at a nonrigid limiting class (Book, Example~27.16.2, printed p.~1001). These metric distinctions do not supply the nonclosed degeneration-order conditions asked for here. A Kummer resolution also has a nonzero smooth semipositive, nonrigid irrational boundary class with square zero (Book, Proposition~27.17.1, printed p.~1003). A global periodic $\ddc$ primitive identifies the representatives' Bott--Chern classes, and two explicitly translated torus periods detect the irrational ray. The resolved surface contains sixteen exceptional curves (Book, Lemma~27.17.1, printed p.~1002); the example does not address the curve-free regime of P11.\par Book, Lemma~25.18.3, printed p.~807 proves a full-Gauduchon-infimum comparison when $\theta\leq CB$ and $\theta+\ddc\varphi\geq cB$, with $\varphi\in L^1$, $c,C>0$, and $B$ closed semipositive on a compact K\"ahler manifold. Constant rank $R$ gives two-sided variational-volume bounds by $\epsilon^{n-R}$ for arbitrary smooth positive probability and Hermitian perturbation. Theorem~25.18.1, printed p.~809 constructs such a comparison for products of projective spaces and their linear-center blowups. The fixed cone has nonnegative parameters in $\theta=\sum_i((a_i+b_if_i)H_i+(c_i+d_ig_i)Q_i)$, with strictly positive fixed smooth $f_i,g_i$, omitted $Q_i$ terms on plain factors, and fixed pullback Fubini--Study generators. These functions may depend on every product coordinate. Each $H_i$-active block contributes $m_i$ to $R$, each $Q_i$-only blown block contributes $m_i-\ell_i-1$, and an inactive block contributes zero. All zero parameters and the zero background are included. An exceptional-divisor logarithm supplies the lower current bound; no restricted invariant/product test or Monge--Amp\`ere mass extremum replaces the variational infimum.\par Proposition~25.18.10, printed p.~810 preserves this order under every specified product linear blowup and, more generally, every modification with compact K\"ahler manifold source. In the latter case the book also proves two-sided power bounds, using the closed-background intersection polynomial after pulling back the actual nonzero divisor sections. The upstairs probability is independently smooth and positive; a degenerate pullback probability is not used in the smooth dual formula.\par Lemma~25.18.4, printed p.~811 determines the exact numerical range of the specified finite effective real sums of zero divisors of sections of integer tensor powers of the $H_i,Q_i$ bundles, with their fixed metrics: $\delta_i\geq0$ on plain factors and $\delta_i\geq0$, $\delta_i+\rho_i\geq0$ on blown factors. Theorem~25.18.2, printed p.~812 assumes the sufficient representation-preserving inequalities $a_i\geq\delta_i$, $c_i\geq\rho_i$. The residual remains in the same nef cone, its order gap is $\sum_i(R_i(\theta)-R_i(\beta))\geq0$, and equality holds exactly when every initially $H_i$-active block retains $a_i-\delta_i+b_i>0$ and every initially $Q_i$-only block retains $c_i-\rho_i+d_i>0$. Example~25.18.1, printed p.~812 gives genuinely nonclosed positive $\theta_t=tH+gQ$ and residual $(t-s)H+(g+s)Q$ after removal of $sE$, $0\leq s\leq t$. Equality holds for $s<t$, while $s=t$ gives jump $\ell+1$, realizing every integer from one to the dimension minus one. The chosen boundary current has actual divisorial mass, although perturbative minimal divisorial multiplicities remain zero.\par Book, Proposition~25.18.11, printed p.~813 removes the constant-rank requirement in this comparison. For a smooth closed semipositive $B$, put $R=\max_X\operatorname{rank}B$. The full quotient comparison and closed intersection polynomial give the same two-sided $\epsilon^{n-R}$ bounds: $B^{R+1}=0$, and the top nonzero mixed coefficient is positive on a nonempty open set. The target background is still nef, the ambient manifold is K\"ahler, and the lower comparison still requires its global $L^1$ potential. Corollary~25.18.2, printed p.~814 applies to any finite collection of smooth closed semipositive generators $B_j$ with fixed strictly positive smooth $f_j$ and parameters $t_j\geq0$: $\theta=\sum_jt_jf_jB_j$. Its order is $n-\max_X\operatorname{rank}(\sum_{t_j>0}B_j)$, including the empty active set. It preserves this order and the power bounds under every modification with compact K\"ahler manifold source, using only smooth pulled-back comparisons. The actual rank of the sum accounts for overlapping generators; constants are not asserted uniform across parameter faces. Example~25.18.2, printed p.~814 gives nonclosed $(f\circ p_2)p_1^*(b\omega_1)$ on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^q$, with $f>0$ nonconstant, $b\geq0$ nonzero and zero on an open set, and exact order $q$. Thus the closed generators themselves may have open zero-rank regions.\par A smooth compact connected anticanonical curve $D$ with $D^2=0$ in a complex surface has a unitary flat divisor line on a neighborhood (Book, Theorem~25.18.3, printed p.~817). The complete local proof uses a holonomy-independent flat elliptic inverse and a weighted fiberwise contraction; no arithmetic condition on the degree-zero normal line is imposed. A concave logarithmic cutoff gives a smooth closed semipositive divisor curvature $B$, supported in an annulus, of rank at most one (Book, Proposition~25.18.12, printed p.~819). On a compact K"ahler surface its maximum rank is exactly one, because $\int_SB\wedge\kappa=\int_D\kappa>0$. Every fixed smooth $f>0$ therefore gives a nef background $fB$ with two-sided variational-volume bounds by $\epsilon$ for arbitrary Hermitian perturbation and smooth positive probability; choices with $\mathrm df\wedge B\ne0$ are nonclosed (Book, Corollary~25.18.3, printed p.~820). For the closed perturbation, $\mathcal V_\mu(B+\epsilon\kappa)=2\epsilon\int_D\kappa+\epsilon^2\int_S\kappa^2$. Nine successive blowups along the strict transforms of a smooth plane cubic, including infinitely near centers, give these anticanonical square-zero curves (Book, Proposition~25.18.13, printed p.~820). The anticanonical line is semiample exactly when its elliptic normal line is torsion; exact order $m$ gives the first moving, basepoint-free system $|mD|$. For a non-torsion normal line every $|kD|$ is the singleton $\{kD\}$, yet the smooth rank-one semipositive curvature and nonclosed order-one multiples still exist. Thus the comparison class includes geometric generators that have no generated positive tensor power.\par The flat neighborhood also controls the divisor complement. For a smooth compact connected divisor in a complex manifold of dimension at least two, a flat divisor line gives compact Levi-flat circle levels. A torsion normal line of exact order $m$ gives a proper local holomorphic function with divisor $mD$ and connected compact nonzero fibers (Book, Proposition~25.18.14, printed p.~822). A non-torsion normal line has dense Levi leaves; maximum modulus forces every holomorphic function on a punctured tube to be constant. Thus every compact-hole complement that is connected has only constant holomorphic functions, which extend to the full divisor complement (Book, Theorem~25.18.4, printed p.~822). For either holonomy regime the complement is not Stein; in particular the non-torsion nine-point cubic example has the Hartogs property and only constant global holomorphic functions (Book, Corollary~25.18.4, printed p.~823). These complement results retain the flat-neighborhood hypothesis and do not supply a comparator for a general nonclosed nef background.
\field{Remaining gap} No unrestricted common-order or integrality assertion survives the examples. Find hypotheses on geometry, torsion, or the degenerating positive representatives that force the closed-type behavior, and characterize equality in the comparison inequalities. These results fix strictly positive coefficient functions and specified closed semipositive generators or global current comparisons. Their divisor inequalities are sufficient for the displayed residual representation and do not characterize every nef residual or alternative cone representation. Fixed functions that vanish and modifications with non-Kahler source remain outside the proved regime. \field{Full problem statement} Let $\pi:X\to B$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion of connected compact complex manifolds with connected fibers, where $B$ is K\"ahler of dimension $q\geq1$. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega$ on $X$, a holomorphic line bundle $A$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ on $B$, and $\varphi\in\PSH(X,\omega)$. Set $\theta=c_1(A,h)$. For positive integers $m$, identify
\[
 V_m=H^0\bigl(X,\pi^*A^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\varphi)\bigr)
          \subseteq H^0(B,A^m)
\]
by holomorphic descent. Find geometric or analytic conditions that produce $\psi\in\PSH(B,\theta)$ of positive non-pluripolar mass such that, for all sufficiently large $m$,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 W_m=H^0\bigl(B,A^m\otimes\mathcal I(m\psi)\bigr)\subseteq V_m,\\
 \dim V_m-\dim W_m=o(m^q).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Determine which features of the singularities of $\varphi$ along the fibers suffice for this approximation.
\field{State of the art} When the selecting data descend, the base equilibrium theorem gives a precise limit. Isolated point constraints upstairs yield base vanishing conditions and lower-order codimension. The isotropic section-over-divisor theorem also gives an explicit divisorial reference and a sharp $O(m^{q-1})$ error.

A more general sufficient criterion is now proved in Book, Theorem~26.10.2, printed p.~877. Let $D$ be a nonempty smooth connected reduced divisor, choose a smooth metric $H_D$ with curvature $\eta$ on $\mathcal O_B(D)$ and its defining section $s_D$, and suppose that $c>0$, $u\in C^\infty(B,\mathbb R)$ satisfy
\[
 \beta=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}u-c\eta>0,
 \qquad \varphi\geq c\log\|\pi^*s_D\|_{H_D}^2-C
 \quad\hbox{on }X.
\]
Put $f=\dim X-q$. In just one submersion chart over an open part of $D$, suppose
\[
 \pi(z,t,w,v)=(z,t),\quad D=\{z=0\},\quad
 \varphi\leq\frac cp\log\left(|z|^{2p}+\sum_{j=1}^r|w_j|^{2b_j}\right)+C',
\]
where $1\leq r\leq f$ and $p,b_j$ are positive integers. With $a=p\sum_jb_j^{-1}$, the reference $\psi=u+c\log\|s_D\|_{H_D}^2$ has non-pluripolar mass $\int_B\beta^q\in(0,\infty)$. For every $m\geq1$, putting $L_m=\lfloor mc\rfloor$ and $K_m=\max\{\lfloor mc-a\rfloor,0\}$ gives
\[
 W_m=H^0(B,A^m(-L_mD))\subseteq V_m
                  \subseteq H^0(B,A^m(-K_mD)),
 \qquad \dim V_m-\dim W_m=O(m^{q-1}).
\]
For $q=1$ the defect is at most $\lceil a\rceil$. The strict local integrability endpoint, including logarithmic divergence at equality, is Book, Lemma~26.10.2, printed p.~876. For exact anisotropic poles along a compact smooth submanifold projecting submersively onto $D$, with the selector locally bounded elsewhere, Book, Corollary~26.10.2, printed p.~878, gives $V_m=H^0(B,A^m(-K_mD))$. Product models allow positive-dimensional pole sets inside fibers. Book, Example~26.10.2, printed p.~879, takes $p=2,b_1=3,c=1/2$ and attains the error order: for $m\geq2$ the defect is $\binom{\lceil m/2\rceil+q}{q-1}\sim2^{1-q}m^{q-1}/(q-1)!$, and the reference mass is $2^{-q}$. These results answer the sufficient-condition request in this regime, with non-descended selecting weights.
The criterion also extends to distinct smooth connected divisors $D_1,\ldots,D_N$ with simple-normal-crossing union: Book, Theorem~26.10.3, printed p.~880. Choose metrics $H_i$, sections $s_i$ and curvatures $\eta_i$. Require $c_i>0$, a smooth $u$, a positive residual $\beta=\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}u-\sum_i c_i\eta_i$, and the global lower bound $\varphi\geq\sum_i c_i\log\|\pi^*s_i\|_{H_i}^2-C$. For each $D_i$ require one anisotropic upper witness of the preceding form, with $c,p,b_j$ replaced by $c_i,p_i,b_{ij}$, over an open subset away from the other divisors. Put $a_i=p_i\sum_j b_{ij}^{-1}$, $L_{m,i}=\lfloor mc_i\rfloor$ and $K_{m,i}=\max\{\lfloor mc_i-a_i\rfloor,0\}$. Then $\psi=u+\sum_i c_i\log\|s_i\|_{H_i}^2$ has mass $\int_B\beta^q>0$ and, for every $m\geq1$,
\[
 H^0\left(B,A^m\left(-\sum_iL_{m,i}D_i\right)\right)=W_m
 \subseteq V_m\subseteq
 H^0\left(B,A^m\left(-\sum_iK_{m,i}D_i\right)\right).
\]
The defect is $O(m^{q-1})$ and is at most $\sum_i\lceil a_i\rceil$ when $q=1$. The uniform mixed-twist estimate is Book, Lemma~26.10.3, printed p.~880. Book, Example~26.10.3, printed p.~882, treats $B=(\mathbb P^1)^q$, $X=B\times(\mathbb P^1)^q$, $A=\mathcal O_B(1,\ldots,1)$ and the sum of the $p=2,b=3,c=1/2$ weights in distinct base--fiber pairs. The exact spaces have external degrees $m-K_m$ and $m-L_m$, respectively. Their defect is zero for $m=1$ and $(\lceil m/2\rceil+2)^q-(\lceil m/2\rceil+1)^q$ for $m\geq2$; its leading term is $q2^{1-q}m^{q-1}$ for $q\geq2$, with constant defect one for $q=1$. The positive reference mass is $q!/2^q$.

A criterion on a specified smooth modification is proved in Book, Theorem~26.10.4, printed p.~883. Let $\rho:Y\to B$ be a smooth compact modification, form $X'=X\times_BY$ with projections $\pi':X'\to Y$ and $\mu:X'\to X$, and place the preceding simple-normal-crossing divisor data on $Y$. Require the positive residual $\beta=\rho^*\theta+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}u-\sum_i c_i\eta_i$, the global lower bound for $\varphi'=\varphi\circ\mu$, and one anisotropic upper witness for $\pi'$ over each divisor away from the others. The potential $\psi'=u+\sum_i c_i\log\|s_i\|^2$ descends uniquely to $\psi\in\PSH(B,\theta)$ with $\mathrm{MA}_\theta(\psi)=\rho_*(\beta^q)$ and positive finite mass. Let $R_\rho$ be the relative canonical bundle, $J_\rho$ its Jacobian section, $M_m=\rho^*A^m\otimes R_\rho$, and $T_m(s)=J_\rho\rho^*s$. Then
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 T_m(W_m)=H^0\left(Y,M_m\left(-\sum_iL_{m,i}D_i\right)\right),\\
 T_m(W_m)\subseteq T_m(V_m)\subseteq
 H^0\left(Y,M_m\left(-\sum_iK_{m,i}D_i\right)\right).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The defect is $O(m^{q-1})$, with the same sum-of-gaps bound on curves. The orders are those of $T_m(s)$, including the Jacobian; resolution alone does not supply the required selector bounds or positive residual.

Book, Example~26.10.4, printed p.~884, takes $q\geq2$, $B=\mathbb P^q$, $A=\mathcal O_B(1)$ and $X=B\times\mathbb P^1$. For $p=[1:0:\cdots:0]$ and a fiber point $o$, put $R=\sum_{j=1}^q|Z_j|^2/\sum_{j=0}^q|Z_j|^2$, $T=\|s_o\|^2$ and $\varphi=\tfrac14\log(R^2+T^3)$. This selector is $(\omega_B+\omega_F)$-psh. The base reference $\psi=\tfrac12\log R$ has mass $1-2^{-q}>0$. On the blowup of $p$ its residual is half the sum of the pullback base form and the resolved-projection form; the Jacobian order is $q-1$. With $\mathfrak m_p$ the point ideal, the exact selected spaces are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 V_m=H^0(B,A^m\otimes\mathfrak m_p^{k_m}),\quad
 W_m=H^0(B,A^m\otimes\mathfrak m_p^{\ell_m}),\\
 k_m=\max\{\lfloor m/2-2/3\rfloor-q+1,0\},\quad
 \ell_m=\max\{\lfloor m/2\rfloor-q+1,0\}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The defect is zero for $m<2q$ and $\binom{\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1}{q-1}\sim2^{1-q}m^{q-1}/(q-1)!$ for $m\geq2q$. The base pole has codimension $q$ and the selector does not descend up to bounded difference.

In all three band criteria, strict residual positivity can be replaced by
\[
 \beta\geq0,\qquad \int_M\beta^q>0,
 \qquad M=B\ \hbox{or, in the modified-base case, }M=Y,
\]
with every other hypothesis retained: Book, Corollary~26.10.3, printed p.~886. The same weakening applies to the exact anisotropic-series corollary. The reference current remains positive and its non-pluripolar measure remains $\beta^q$ on the chosen smooth model, or its pushforward on the base. The integrability thresholds and restriction estimates use no strict curvature positivity. Semipositivity alone does not ensure positive mass.

Book, Example~26.10.5, printed p.~886, exhibits this larger regime. Let $q\geq2$, let $\rho:B\to\mathbb P^q$ be the blowup of one point with exceptional divisor $E$, and let $\tau:B\to\mathbb P^{q-1}$ resolve the projection from that point. Write $\alpha=\rho^*\omega_{\mathbb P^q}$, $\gamma=\tau^*\omega_{\mathbb P^{q-1}}$ and $\kappa=\alpha+\gamma>0$. Equip $\mathcal O_B(E)$ with the quotient metric of curvature $\eta=\alpha-\gamma$. For an integer $k\geq1$, set $A=\rho^*\mathcal O(k)\otimes\mathcal O_B(E)$ with curvature $\theta=k\alpha+\eta$. On $X=B\times\mathbb P^1$, for a marked fiber point $o$, put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 R=\|\pi^*s_E\|^2,\quad T=\|s_o\|^2,\quad
 \varphi=\tfrac12\log(R^2+T^3),\quad
 \omega=\pi^*\kappa+2\omega_{\mathbb P^1},\\
 \psi=\log\|s_E\|^2.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The selector is $\omega$-psh, is finite on most fibers over $E$ and has a proper pole there; hence it does not descend up to bounded difference. The base reference is $\theta$-psh, with semipositive residual $k\alpha$ and mass $k^q>0$. Its integral on every contracted exceptional line is zero, even after adding any smooth $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm c}u$, so this residual has no strictly positive smooth gauge. Despite the different local thresholds $L_m=m$ and $K_m=m-1$, Hartogs extension and the exceptional fixed divisor give, for every $m\geq1$,
\[
 V_m=W_m=H^0(B,A^m)=s_E^m\rho^*H^0(\mathbb P^q,\mathcal O(km)),
 \qquad \dim V_m=\binom{km+q}{q}.
\]
Thus the actual section defect is zero in this non-descended positive-mass model.

The exact exceptional-divisor calculation extends to every positive selecting coefficient: Book, Proposition~26.10.1, printed p.~888. Retain the blowup $\rho:B\to\mathbb P^q$ of a point $x$, $q\geq2$, its divisor metric and $X=B\times\mathbb P^1$. For integers $k,e\geq1$, take $A_e=\rho^*\mathcal O(k)\otimes\mathcal O_B(eE)$ with curvature $\theta=k\alpha+e\eta$. For positive integers $p,b$, a real $c>0$ and $a=p/b$, set
\[
 \varphi_c=\frac cp\log(R^p+T^b),\qquad
 \omega_c=c\pi^*\kappa+(cb/p+1)\omega_{\mathbb P^1}.
\]
This selector is $\omega_c$-psh. For every $m\geq1$ the exact space is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 V_m(c)=s_E^{em}\rho^*H^0(\mathbb P^q,\mathcal O(km)\otimes\mathfrak m_x^{v_m}),\\
 v_m=\max\{\lfloor m(c-e)-a\rfloor,0\}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
For $0<c\leq e+k$, put $d=\max\{c,e\}$, $\delta=d-e$ and $\psi_d=d\log\|s_E\|^2$. The residual is $(k-\delta)\alpha+\delta\gamma\geq0$; its non-pluripolar mass is $k^q-\delta^q$. The reference space is
\[
 W_m(c)=s_E^{em}\rho^*H^0(\mathbb P^q,\mathcal O(km)\otimes\mathfrak m_x^{\ell_m})
 \subseteq V_m(c),\qquad \ell_m=\lfloor m\delta\rfloor.
\]
When $0<c\leq e$, both spaces equal the whole $H^0(B,A_e^m)$. For $e<c<e+k$, the reference mass is positive and
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \dim V_m(c)-\dim W_m(c)
  =\binom{\ell_m+q-1}{q}-\binom{v_m+q-1}{q}=O(m^{q-1}),\\
 \dim V_m(c)=\frac{k^q-(c-e)^q}{q!}m^q+O(m^{q-1}).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The binomial count is zero for point order zero. At $c=e+k$, this displayed reference has zero mass and the selected spaces have leading dimension $(\lceil a\rceil+1)k^{q-1}m^{q-1}/(q-1)!$. For $c>e+k$, $V_m(c)=0$ exactly when $m(c-e-k)\geq a+1$, including equality. These are complete series computations for this fixed family and the mass of the constructed reference, not a necessity theorem for arbitrary selecting potentials or other possible references.

The positive-volume semipositive regime also has a nonzero sharp defect. On the blowup of two coordinate points $x_1,x_2\in\mathbb P^q$, $q\geq2$, use the two resolved projections with forms $\gamma_i$ and their exceptional divisors $E_i$. Take $A=\rho^*\mathcal O(2)\otimes\mathcal O(E_1+E_2)$, $\psi=2\log\|s_1\|^2+2\log\|s_2\|^2$ and the selector $\varphi=\sum_i\log(R_i^2+T^3)$ on $B\times\mathbb P^1$. Here $R_i=\|\pi^*s_i\|^2$ and $T=\|s_o\|^2$, using the quotient divisor metrics and the normalized Fubini--Study metric of the marked fiber point $o$. The residual $\beta=\gamma_1+\gamma_2$ has mass $2^q-2>0$ but integrates to zero on the strict transform of the joining line, obstructing every strictly positive smooth gauge. For each $m\geq1$ the selected and reference spaces, after the fixed factor $s_1^ms_2^m$, are degree-$2m$ projective sections with point orders $m-1,m-1$ and $m,m$, respectively. Direct simultaneous-jet interpolation gives
\[
 \dim V_m-\dim W_m=2\binom{m+q-2}{q-1}
 \sim\frac{2}{(q-1)!}m^{q-1},
\]
and both spaces have leading dimension $(2^q-2)m^q/q!$. The proof establishes jet independence under the explicit condition that the sum of orders is at most the degree plus one; it does not assume independent conditions for arbitrary multiple-point orders (Book, Example~26.10.6, printed p.~891; Lemma~26.10.4, printed p.~890). Thus positive-volume semipositivity does not force the defect to vanish or improve its general order.

\field{Remaining gap} Determine which more general fiberwise singularities admit a positive-mass base reference and whether useful necessary conditions can be given. The verified criteria require a global logarithmic lower bound and a smooth semipositive residual with positive top integral; a local integrability comparison alone does not construct the required reference. Arbitrary selectors, coefficients varying along a horizontal locus and unrestricted base ideal constraints remain outside the verified criteria. The direct multiple-divisor proof uses simple normal crossings. \subsection{P22: Moving Gaussian transitions for growing vanishing jets}
\label{P22}
\noindent\textbf{Status:} resolved in the original compact-curve scope. \textbf{Book locator:} Problem 32.9.3, printed p.~1176.
\field{Setup and scope} Here the selecting ideal itself varies with the tensor power. The full statement specifies the local weight, local volume normalization, orthonormal kernel, moving observation point, and limiting Gaussian distribution. All these normalizations are essential to the proposed law.
\field{Full problem statement} Let $C$ be a connected compact Riemann surface, let $x\in C$, and let $(L,h)$ be a smooth Hermitian holomorphic line bundle whose curvature is semipositive and strictly positive on $C\setminus\{x\}$. Fix an integer $r\geq1$. Suppose that in a local coordinate $z$ centered at $x$ and a holomorphic frame $e$ one has
\[
 |e|_h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\phi},\qquad
 \phi(z)=|z|^{2r}+O(|z|^{2r+1}).
\]
Let $\nu$ be a smooth positive volume form with local expression $a(z)\,\mathrm{d}A$ and $a(0)=1$, where $\mathrm{d}A$ is Euclidean area. For positive integers $m$, choose integers $q_m\to\infty$ with $q_m=o(m)$. Give
\[
 V_m=H^0(C,L^m\otimes\mathfrak m_x^{q_m})
\]
the norm $\|s\|^2=\int_C|s|_{h^m}^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu$, where $\mathfrak m_x$ is the ideal sheaf of $x$. Write $B_m=\sum_j|s_{m,j}|_{h^m}^2$ for an orthonormal basis $(s_{m,j})$ of $V_m$, with value zero when $V_m=0$. Determine the growth ranges of $q_m$ for which, for each fixed real $\tau$, every sequence of points $x_m$ in the coordinate neighborhood satisfying
\[
 m|z(x_m)|^{2r}=q_m/r+\tau\sqrt{q_m/r}
\]
obeys
\[
 \frac{\pi B_m(x_m)}{mr^2|z(x_m)|^{2r-2}}
 \longrightarrow
 \Phi(\tau):=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}
                         \mathrm{e}^{-s^2/2}\,\mathrm{d}s.
\]
When this transition law fails, determine how further terms of the local weight change its center and scale.
\field{State of the art} The optimal universal uncorrected range is
\[
 q_m\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad q_m=o(m^{1/(r+1)}).
\]
This holds for every fixed integer $r\geq1$, every permitted smooth metric and volume, and every sequence of observation directions. Every excluded sublinear sequence admits a compact example violating the law along a subsequence. For a fixed weight, let $R_m=(q_m/(rm))^{1/(2r)}$ and write the actual curvature quadrature boundary as $\Gamma_{q_m/m}=\{R_m c_{R_m}(\theta)\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta}\}$. The exact all-direction criterion for the uncorrected law is $\sqrt{q_m}\|c_{R_m}-1\|_\infty\to0$ (Book, Theorem~26.13.3, printed p.~917).

For every $q_m\to\infty$, $q_m=o(m)$, the corrected center is $\Gamma_{q_m/m}$. Its enclosed domain $D_t$ satisfies
\[
 \int_{D_t}\rho(z)f(z)\,\mathrm dA=\pi t f(0),\qquad
 \rho=\phi_{z\bar z},\qquad t=q_m/m,
\]
for every $f$ holomorphic near $\overline{D_t}$. If $d_t$ is signed Euclidean distance, positive outside, and $p_t$ is the nearest boundary point, then uniformly in direction and each fixed band $|d_t|\leq M R_m/\sqrt{q_m}$,
\[
 \frac{\pi a(z)B_m(z)}{m\rho(z)}
 -\Phi\bigl(2\sqrt{m\rho(p_t(z))}\,d_t(z)\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]
At $d_t=o(R_m)$ and scaled distance tending to $-\infty$ or $+\infty$, the normalized density tends to $0$ or $1$. Thus the normal transition length is $1/(2\sqrt{m\rho})$ (Book, Theorem~26.13.2, printed p.~913).

The finite effective boundary coefficients give further corrections. If $\phi=|z|^{2r}+P_{2r+1}+O(|z|^{2r+2})$, $P$ is that homogeneous term restricted to the circle and $\Lambda\mathrm e^{\mathrm i n\theta}=|n|\mathrm e^{\mathrm i n\theta}$, then
\[
 c_R=1+Rb_1+O(R^2),\qquad
 b_1=-\frac{((2r+1)-\Lambda)P}{4r^2}.
\]
For $q_m=O(m^{1/(r+1)})$ the raw Gaussian argument acquires the shift $\sqrt{q_m}R_m((2r+1)-\Lambda)P/(2r^{3/2})$. Using coefficients through $R^J$ gives a valid center correction whenever $q_m=o(m^{(J+1)/(r+J+1)})$. If the first nonzero effective coefficient is $b_j$, the exact fixed-weight uncorrected range is $q_m=o(m^{j/(r+j)})$. A smooth Taylor-flat radial perturbation proves that no fixed finite Taylor jet determines the center to the transition width for every sublinear sequence. These results use the actual smooth curvature and full compact global sections, not only a local polynomial model.
\field{Remaining gap} The original compact-curve question is resolved. General higher-dimensional coupled ideals and general fiber-selected transfers remain open directions in S14; the explicit radial product class is settled there. The resolution does not assert an exhaustive literature search or priority.

\subsection{P23: Equations in prescribed types on compact unibranch Hermitian spaces}
\label{P23}
\noindent\textbf{Status:} open in the stated generality. \textbf{Book locator:} Problem 32.10.1, printed p.~1177.
\field{Setup and scope} Let $X$ be a compact reduced locally irreducible (unibranch) complex space with a smooth Hermitian background in the local-embedding sense. Potentials are defined by holomorphic disk tests. On a resolution $\pi:Y\to X$, extended measures are computed upstairs and pushed forward; existence in a specified type also requires the upstairs potential to descend. The target is a geometric and measure criterion valid beyond explicit projective cones.
\field{Full problem statement} Beyond the projective-cone constructions of Book, Section~A.6, determine geometric and measure conditions on compact unibranch Hermitian spaces that give existence and uniqueness in specified singularity classes. Identify estimates ensuring finite mass, descent and continuity, and their behavior under common refinements.
\field{State of the art} The appendix proves disk-test descent under modifications and compatibility of ordinary envelopes, constructs resolution-independent non-pluripolar products, and gives explicit equations on projective cones under stated inequalities on their data. Dang provides continuity for bounded solutions under additional hypotheses, notably a log-terminal setting. Pan constructs a Gauduchon metric on the regular locus uniformly comparable to the prescribed metric, with conformal uniqueness up to scale.

The bounded potential descends, including for a nonclosed ambient background, and solves (Book, equation~13.52) on the singular space. When the pullback background is closed, Book, Theorem~13.18.2 proves uniqueness among bounded normalized solutions and convergence of the entire smooth perturbation family. These arguments do not establish continuity at a node or settle the general prescribed-class problem.\par For compact unibranch curves with finite bijective torus normalization, Book, Lemma~A.7.1, printed p.~1224 constructs a periodic Green function directly. Theorem~A.7.1, printed p.~1225 proves an equation in every prescribed finite logarithmic ordinary type of total pole weight $A<H$, for nonzero nonnegative normalization-side $L^p$ data with $p>1$. Here $H=\int_E\pi^*h$, $\beta_0$ is flat area probability on the normalization $E$, and $f\geq0$ is its data density. The proportionality constant is $(H-A)/\int f\beta_0$, the entire genuine type has mass $H-A$, and uniqueness includes initially rough competitors. The local-embedding background may pull back degenerately. Example~A.7.1, printed p.~1227 supplies a nonnormal projective cusp with genus-one normalization, background mass six and a weight-one pole of nonpluripolar mass five. This is a dimension-one sufficient condition; all curve backgrounds are closed.\par Book, Theorem~A.7.2, printed p.~1230 gives an exact endpoint for normalization-side densities with local form $C_0|z|^{-2}(\log(1/|z|))^{-\gamma}$, where $C_0>0$ and the density is smoothly and positively extended away from the chosen point. Within this family, a positive-proportionality equation has a solution in the prescribed ordinary logarithmic type exactly for $\gamma>2$; it is unique in that entire type and has a continuous residual with no positive H\"older exponent upstairs. No ordinary-type solution exists, even with an unspecified positive proportionality constant. Fix an integer $s\geq n+3$ before choosing its sufficiently small nonzero real perturbation. A quantitative contraction solves full-variable normalized densities in a small $H^s$ neighborhood of the positive reference density, with a $C^2$ normalization-side potential and solution form at least one quarter of the flat reference (Book, Theorem~A.8.1, printed p.~1233). \[
 \frac{\pi^q a(t)B_m(z)}{m^q\prod_jg_j(t_j)}\longrightarrow\Phi(\tau),
 \qquad a(t)=\prod_j(1+t_j)^{-2}.
\]
This includes every sublinear order with the actual radial center. Exact global monomials give $\dim V_m=\prod_j((r_j+1)m+1)-\binom{k_m+q-1}{q}$ and $m^{-q}B_m\nu_Y\to c_1(L,h)^q/q!$ in total variation (Book, Theorem~26.15.1, printed p.~923). The homogeneous center has the sharp smaller range $k_m=o(m^{2/(r_*+2)})$, $r_*:=\max_jr_j$; positive first remainders produce a nonzero critical Gaussian shift and supercritical limit one (Book, Proposition~26.15.1, printed p.~925).

For a compact holomorphic submersion $\pi:X\to Y$ with connected fibers of dimension $f\geq1$, a pullback bundle and metric and a matched smooth volume $\pi_*\nu_X=\nu_Y$, the moving isolated selector $\varphi_m=(k_m+q+f-1)\chi_p/m$ has $\mathcal I(m\varphi_m)=\mathfrak m_p^{k_m}$. Its pole and ideal do not descend, but selected sections and their smooth norms identify exactly with $V_m$. Thus $B_{X,m}=B_m\circ\pi$, the growth exponent is $q$, and the lifted density converges in total variation. Matched volumes exist on every such submersion; arbitrary volumes instead transform the base norm, for which this local theorem has not been proved (Book, Theorem~26.15.2, printed p.~926). This moving selector does not close the fixed-selector problem P21.

At a fixed zero of curvature, an explicit smooth semipositive metric on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(1)$ already gives the unselected kernel scale $B_m(p)=3m/(\pi^2\rho_0\log m)(1+O((\log m)^{-1}))$ for a smooth positive two-torus-invariant probability volume with local density $\rho_0$ (Book, Proposition~26.14.1, printed p.~921). The leading constant is $3/2$ for Fubini--Study probability volume. The metric is the exact model of \cite[Example I.15, pp.~9--10]{ChiangKernel26}; the manuscript proves the norm asymptotic directly by elementary integrals. No growing vanishing ideal or moving-scale limit is included in this example.
\field{Remaining gap} Extend beyond the specified separable radial class: mixed or angular remainders, observation faces with some zero energy fractions, other compact bases or ideals, and general fiber-selected singularities. Control global section production and the transformed norm at the moving scale in each new regime. The proved sufficient conditions do not imply a universal Gaussian expression or an ambient-dimensional Monge--Amp\`ere limit.

\subsection{S16: Automatic envelope genuineness in semipositive dual attainment}
\label{S16}
\noindent\textbf{Status:} unresolved question in the manuscript.
\field{Full problem description} Suppose a positive-threshold $L^1$ density has a smooth semipositive maximizing Gauduchon current, strictly positive off a proper analytic set $Z$, and let $\Phi_f$ be the greatest normalized threshold subsolution. Find conditions making $P_{\delta h}(\Phi_f)$ genuine for some $0<\delta<1$, and determine regularity across $Z$. More generally, ask whether moment or entropy control forces this envelope condition.
\field{State of the art} The semipositive maximizer gives a unique exact global threshold solution, smoothness off $Z$, a fixed lower barrier and whole-family normalized $L^1$ convergence of exponential solutions. A genuine smaller-background envelope upgrades to capacity convergence; $L^p$ data, $p>1$, give continuity and uniform convergence. For rough maximizing currents, first moments plus this envelope condition yield an exact cluster equation. Entropy bounds supply moments and uniform integrability but do not supply the envelope. Book, Theorem~25.9.3, printed p.~747 now proves genuine obstacle envelopes at every smaller scale and zero Lelong numbers for all full-mass potentials when $\ddc(h^j)=0$ for $1\leq j<n$, and gives the exact density threshold $V/\int_Xf\,\mathrm{d}V$. Book, Proposition~25.9.6, printed p.~748 gives explicit all-scale competitors from a quantitative sublinear singularity bound. Book, Theorem~25.9.4, printed p.~748 proves smooth extension across $Z$ under attainment, zero Lelong numbers and a globally smooth semipositive root of complex rank at least two everywhere. Book, Example~25.9.2, printed p.~749 and Book, Example~25.9.3, printed p.~750 verify actual attained Hopf and ramified quadric instances. These are restricted sufficient conditions; a smooth maximizing current alone does not supply a smooth root with the required rank. Book, Proposition~25.9.7, printed p.~751 computes an explicit unbounded full-mass point-pole threshold solution with genuine smaller-background envelopes at every scale, an $L^1$ density in no $L^p$ for $p>1$, and no smooth semipositive maximizing test. Book, Corollary~25.9.6, printed p.~753 gives its exact finite-entropy and weighted-integrability parameter ranges. These are properties of the single equation density; the example does not satisfy the smooth semipositive attainment hypothesis and does not settle the original uniform probability question.
\field{Remaining gap} Determine whether general moment or entropy control forces a genuine smaller-background envelope in the attained setting, or identify its failure there. Invariant bounded mass and a quantified sublinear lower singularity bound now give sufficient conditions. The across-set theorem also requires a globally smooth root of rank at least two and zero Lelong numbers; remove or replace those hypotheses only with an additional proof. Capacity convergence under a genuine envelope and exact solvability in the semipositive attained regime remain distinct from these intrinsic-condition questions.

\subsection{S17: Critical determinant bounds restricted to actual adjoint maximizers}
\label{S17}
\noindent\textbf{Status:} unresolved question in the manuscript.
\field{Full problem description} When $v_-(h)=0$, determine whether the critical $L^{n/(n-1)}$ estimate can hold for the determinant-weighted probabilities of exact maximizing currents representing adjoints, uniformly as $F$ ranges over a specified bounded set. Identify geometric or density hypotheses for such a restricted estimate.
\field{State of the art} All normalized Gauduchon tests can violate the endpoint, while their subcritical estimates hold universally. Adjoint densities inherit those subcritical bounds; positive lower mass supplies the endpoint. For one fixed smooth strictly positive density the exact maximizer is smooth and unique. Near-maximizing sequences can have divergent higher moments even on K\"ahler manifolds.
 Theorem 25.8.3, printed p.~733, now gives uniform $L^\infty$ bounds for both $k_{T_F}$ and $q_F=\mathrm d\nu_F/\mathrm dV$ on every fixed smooth positive background of dimension $n\geq3$, including $v_-(h)=0$, for a fixed $0<\alpha<1$, a smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm dV$, and smooth $F$ in an explicit small $C^{2,\alpha}$ ball around $\log(h^n/(V_h\mathrm dV))$. Lemma 25.8.1, printed p.~732, gives the two controlled solution and factor constructions; Corollary 25.8.2, printed p.~734, gives probability-normalized families of arbitrary finite dimension and a path of distinct data and exact currents. The exact bounds are $C_\nu=3^{n+1}aH_{\max}/4$ and $C_k=3^naH_{\max}^{(n-1)/n}/2$, where $\int\chi_0h^n=1$, $a=\max\chi_0$ and $H_{\max}=\max(h^n/\mathrm dV)$; at the critical exponent, the probability norm is at most $C_\nu^{1/n}$. Primary elliptic locators are explained in Section 33.13, printed p.~1200.
\field{Remaining gap} The small smooth $C^{2,\alpha}$ family supplies one sufficient density hypothesis. Endpoint bounds for larger families or for merely continuous densities bounded in $C^0$ or $L^\infty$ remain open. The theorem concerns exact maximizers; it gives no uniform higher-moment bound for all tests or near-maximizers. Keep $k_T$ separate from the normalized $F$-weighted probability density. It does not resolve P16 at arbitrary continuous data.

\subsection{S19: Optimal multiweight ideal distance when the number of weights exceeds the dimension}
\label{S19}
\noindent\textbf{Status:} unresolved for $n\geq2$ and $r>n$; the planar case is settled in the manuscript.
\field{Full problem description} Fix integers $n\geq2$, $r>n$, and real exponents $p_1,\ldots,p_r>1$. Determine the smallest universal constant $C(n;p_1,\ldots,p_r)$ such that the following inequality holds on every pseudoconvex domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$: for genuine psh $\psi$, genuine negative psh $\phi_i$, and holomorphic $f$ with $0<I=\int_\Omega|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi-\sum_i\phi_i}\,\mathrm dV<+\infty$,
\[
 \inf\left\{\int_\Omega|g|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi}\,\mathrm dV:
       g\in\mathcal O(\Omega),\quad
       f-g\in\sum_{i=1}^r\Gamma(\Omega,\mathcal I(p_i\phi_i+\psi))\right\}
       \leq C(n;p_1,\ldots,p_r)I.
\]
Global ideal membership means local integrability at every germ; it does not require a finite global norm for the subtracted sections. Establish both the universal upper estimate and examples attaining it or approaching it.
\field{State of the art} Book Theorem~1.16.2, printed p.~63, proves the upper constant $\prod_i(1-1/p_i)$ for every $r$, with equality models when $r\leq n$. Theorem~1.16.3, printed pp.~66--67, determines the optimal constant $\max\{1-\sum_i1/p_i,0\}$ for every $r$ in dimension one; when the reciprocal sum is at least one, $g=0$ is admissible. Takakura's exact preprint \cite[v1, Theorem~1.2 and Example~3.2, pp.~1--2 and 7]{TakDivision26} states the dimension-dependent bound $1-\frac{\sum_i1/p_i}{\min\{n,r\}}$ under $\sum_i1/p_i<\min\{n,r\}$ and asks about optimality. Its general higher-rank proof is not an adopted input of the manuscript; the book reconstructs scalar division and the stated product and planar estimates directly.
Book Example~1.16.1, printed pp.~67--68, constructs unit-ball hyperplane weights with equal exponents $p$, for every $n\geq2$, $r>n$ and $r/n<p\leq r$. The reciprocal sum is in $[1,n)$, the input integral is finite, and the distance equals $\pi^n/n!>0$. It gives a positive lower bound $\pi^n/(n!I)$ for the corresponding universal constant and excludes extending the planar zero regime to higher dimensions. It does not determine the optimal constant.
\field{Remaining gap} For $n\geq2$ and $r>n$, the known product estimate has no equality argument using $r$ distinct coordinate directions. Determine how the common zero sets and the interaction of multiplier ideals affect the optimal constant. The planar principal-ideal calculation does not extend to arbitrary higher-dimensional ideals. This is a local quantitative problem, distinct from global $\mathcal I$-goodness in P01/P06.

\field{Source location} \path{book/author/section_psh_planar_ideal_distance.tex}; Subsection~1.16.2, printed pp.~64--67, especially its final paragraph. The hyperplane model is \path{book/author/section_psh_hyperplane_ideal_distance.tex}, Subsection~1.16.3, printed pp.~67--68; the corresponding source discussion is in the final paragraph of Section~33.1 (printed p.~1182).

\section{Relationships and settled boundaries}
The catalogue preserves 23 formal entries and 19 supplemental entries. P08 and P12 are resolved negatively, P22 is resolved in its original compact-curve scope, and S13 in its original two separate regimes; 20 formal entries and 18 supplemental entries remain open, giving 38 open directions. Overlapping entries are not independent conjectures. The main dependencies are:
\begin{itemize}
\item P01 and P17 are versions of one complete-topology program; S09 isolates its all-obstacle analytic-closure issue, also relevant to P06.
\item P08 refutes the unrestricted bigness implication, and P12 shows that an actual flow endpoint does not restore it. The closed-intersection-to-root-volume and closed-bigness bridges in P09 remain separate requirements.
\item P07 concerns exact equations at a positive individual threshold. S03 concerns exponential solvability at zero threshold; S04 concerns root tails; S05 concerns selection; S15 concerns arbitrary non-pluripolar measures. These assertions are not interchangeable.
\item P10 concerns all normalized Gauduchon tests. S17 concerns exact adjoint maximizers. P16 concerns uniqueness of the induced probability, not uniqueness of every maximizing current.
\item P20 concerns general kernel lower bounds and measures; P21 provides a specific base-series construction; P22 resolves growing jets on a curve under its original hypotheses; S14 asks for higher-dimensional and general fibration extensions.
\item S13 disproves completeness both under mere nefness and bigness and for exact smoothing-compatible auxiliary data. Simultaneous nefness and compatibility, and actual evolving flows, require separate study. Conditional smooth convergence off a big endpoint's null locus in P12 does not by itself prove completeness.
\end{itemize}
The following broader assertions are already refuted and are included only as boundaries on the open questions: universal BMP; positivity of a fixed-density volume as a uniform lower-mass bound; zero-volume upper-volume equality as a criterion for ideal-envelope equality; general uniqueness for zero-exponent equations with nonnegative density; completeness for all big divisorial exponential equations; and smooth residual Hessians determined solely by density vanishing order. The catalogue does not reopen these assertions.
